## 1 · THE DIRECT ANSWER

> **Nobody has ever measured the age at which a WOREE child is diagnosed.** Three cohorts totalling 83 reported
> individuals — Piard 2019 (n = 20), Oliver 2023 (n = 13 plus 62 from the literature), Gao 2025 (n = 50) —
> report age at seizure onset, age at last follow-up and age at death, and **not one of them reports age at
> molecular diagnosis.** The PubMed census is `WWOX AND "age at diagnosis"` → **`total_count: 0`**, translation
> expanded, positive control `WWOX AND (epileptic encephalopathy OR WOREE)` → **`total_count: 72`**. The
> quantity on which the whole of filter 2 turns is **not in the literature**, and this file's first result is
> that absence.
>
> **What is measured is the age at presentation, and it is very early.** Seizures begin at a **median of 5 weeks**
> (Oliver cohort, n = 13, range 1 day–10 months, mean 9 weeks), a **median of 8.6 weeks** in the 62 literature
> cases (mean 8 weeks, range 1 day–6 months), and a **mean of 1.6 months** in Piard's 20 (range day 1–7 months).
> Diagnosis cannot precede presentation and in practice follows it by an unmeasured interval, so the honest
> statement is a **one-sided bound: any post-diagnosis intervention acts at or after ≈1–2 months of postnatal
> life, with no measured upper bound.**
>
> **At that age the human brain is not developmentally quiet, and this is measured, not assumed.** In the first
> six months of life the human subventricular zone still carries **vimentin⁺/GFAP⁺ cells with elongated radial
> glial processes** lining the ventricular wall and a **dense network of DCX⁺ tangentially migrating immature
> neurons**; DCX⁺ cells fall **25-fold across the first six months**; the rostral migratory stream is
> uninterrupted at 1 day, 1 week, 1, 3 and 6 months and gone by 2 years; and a **medial migratory stream to the
> ventromedial prefrontal cortex is present at 4–6 months and absent at 8–18 months** (Sanai 2011, human
> autopsy and surgical material, read in full here). **Radial glia and neuronal migration — the two processes
> `DL-MECH-011` and the portfolio treat as prenatal and closed — are documented still running in the human at
> exactly the age WOREE children present.**
>
> 🔴 **And the WWOX limb of that cell is empty. Nobody has ever looked at WWOX in postnatal human
> subventricular zone, in the rostral migratory stream, or in the medial migratory stream** — and the mouse
> cannot be asked, because Sanai's own control experiment found **no medial migratory stream in mouse at P4, P8,
> P16 or P20.**
>
> **The intersection asked for in Task 4 is therefore NOT empty — but it is narrower than the portfolio needs,
> and only one cell in it has both limbs measured in human postnatal tissue: myelination.** That is the one
> process for which a WWOX-specific human longitudinal measurement exists (serial MRI: *"delayed myelination"*
> from **day 19 of life**, and progression on repeat imaging in 7 of 13 patients) and for which the human
> process demonstrably runs for decades. It is also the cell whose direct lever this repository has **already
> examined and downgraded** (`N-13`, `DL-MECH-031`: the myelin defect is neuron-driven, not
> oligodendrocyte-autonomous; `DL-MECH-039` removes the "protect myelin → protect development" final link).
> **So the honest verdict is not "empty" and not "open": it is that the one occupied cell is occupied by a
> process the repository already reads as downstream, and the one cell that would decide the portfolio's fate —
> WWOX in the postnatal human progenitor compartment — has never been measured by anyone.**
>
> **On the translation (Task 3): the honest answer is that it is too uncertain to support a number, and the
> prior question invalidates it anyway.** `P1–P5` is **not a measured window**. It is **the set of ages that
> were tested**, and the primary says so in the repository's own locator file: *"Therapeutic rescue beyond this
> early postnatal window was not explored, as Wwox-null mice rapidly deteriorate"* and *"the inability to assess
> later intervention likely reflects a combination of model-specific biological constraints and technical
> limitations, **rather than a definitive boundary for therapeutic responsiveness**"*. Translating `P1–P5` into
> human gestational weeks answers a question about an experiment's design, not about biology.

## 6 · TASK 3 — MOUSE-TO-HUMAN WINDOW TRANSLATION

### 6.1 🔴 The prior question that invalidates the translation before it starts

`DL-MECH-011` and `superior_node_search_20260922.md` §4 both treat **P1–P5** as a window that **closes**.
**The repository's own locator file for the primary says it is not.**

> "Therapeutic rescue beyond this early postnatal window was not explored, as Wwox-null mice rapidly deteriorate
> with progressive neurological dysfunction and early lethality"

> "the inability to assess later intervention likely reflects a combination of model-specific biological
> constraints and technical limitations, rather than a definitive boundary for therapeutic responsiveness"

`surface: body` · PMID 42422765, Discussion · quoted from
`fulltext_dossiers/PMID42422765_partial_locators.md`, 🔴 **held from a prior session, not re-fetched in this
act** (`get_copyright_status` on this PMID was recorded as `is_open_access: false` in that same file).

And the same file's audit of Supplementary Figure S8 records that S8 **contains no P0 arm**, that only **P1 and
P5** carry P300 survival curves, and that **P4 is absent entirely**.

> ⇒ **`P1–P5` is the set of ages that were tested. It is not a measured window, and no upper bound has ever been
> established in any WWOX model.** Translating it into human time therefore describes an experiment's design.
> **This must be said before any number, because a translated number inherits the error of the thing translated.**

### 6.2 The published methods, and what each one gives

Every statement below is quoted from **PMID 23583307, read in full in this act**. Each is that review's
**attribution to an earlier source I did not open**, and is labelled as such.

| Method | What it says | Anchor it gives for early postnatal rodent |
|---|---|---|
| **Brain growth spurt** (Dobbing & Sands; via PMID 23583307 §2) | the brain growth spurt *"peak[s] in humans around birth and postnatal day (pnd) 7 in rats"*; *"This likely founded the widespread 'rule of thumb' usage of 7-day-old rat pups to investigate perinatal scenarios"* | **rat P7 ≈ human birth** ⇒ **rat P1–P5 is PRE-TERM** |
| **Oligodendrocyte-lineage staging** (Back and colleagues; via PMID 23583307 §5) | *"estimating that white matter development and axonal outgrowth in the rodent CNS at pnd 1–3 corresponds to 23–32 weeks gestation in human infants, while pnd 7 is analogous to 32–36 weeks"*; *"a pnd 7 rodent thus represents a preterm infant—as such, some researchers now consider rodents at pnd 10 to be more comparable to a term infant, at least in terms of white matter development"* | **rodent P1–P3 ≈ human 23–32 gestational weeks** |
| **Brain weight** (via PMID 23583307 §2) | rat cortex ~90% adult weight by pnd 20; human plateau at 2–3 years ⇒ *"based on brain weights alone, pnd 20 in rats appears to correspond to a 2–3 year old human child"* | calibrates the far end |
| **Single-biochemical-marker matching** (via PMID 23583307 §4) | GAD at human 40 weeks gestation is 16.2% of adult; *"A similar calculation to determine 16.2% of the adult rat levels corresponds to 7.4 days of age in the rat."* For choline acetyltransferase the same human timepoint is *"strikingly later, at pnd 20.4 in the rat cortex"* | **human term = rat P7.4 or rat P20.4** |

### 6.3 The error bars, quantified

1. 🔴 **Process-dependence, measured: one human age maps to two rodent ages a factor of 2.8 apart.** Human term
   is rat **P7.4** on GAD and rat **P20.4** on ChAT — a **13-rodent-day spread for the same human timepoint**,
   with both numbers from the same review paragraph. The review draws the conclusion itself: *"equating ages
   based upon a single milestone or event may lead to misinterpretation."*
2. 🔴 **The interval that is offered is already 9 weeks wide before anything else is added:** "rodent P1–P3 ≈
   **23–32** weeks gestation."
3. 🔴 **Species: every anchor I could source is rat or unqualified "rodent". I found no mouse-specific anchor in
   this act.** `DL-MECH-011`'s window is a **mouse** result. Applying a rat calibration to a mouse is a further
   unquantified step, and the two species do not even share a neural-tube date (PMID 23583307 §2: gd 10.5–11 in
   rats, **gd 9–9.5 in mice**).
4. 🔴 **The one formal model disclaims the range that matters.** Postnatal rodent days are exactly the
   peri/postnatal region its own authors *"concede the difficulty of extending"* to. Workman 2013 claims the
   extension; **its body is not retrievable here**, so I cannot report its residual error and I will not quote
   `0.9929` as an error bar — a correlation on an ordered event scale across 18 species is **not** a confidence
   interval in days for one species pair.
5. 🔴 **Region and process heterogeneity inside one brain.** PMID 23583307 §2: *"these early studies do not
   account for the considerable heterogeneity between different brain regions, which likely mature at different
   rates."*

### 6.4 The verdict on Task 3, in the form the brief asks for

> 🔴 **The translation is too uncertain to support a clinical inference, and I will not give a number.**
>
> **What survives is a direction, and it is unanimous across every index I could source:** mouse/rat **P1–P5
> falls before human term birth.** On the oligodendrocyte-lineage index it is roughly **23–32 gestational
> weeks**; on the brain-growth-spurt index it is **before the perinatal peak**; on the ChAT index it is far
> pre-term. **No index I could source places rodent P1–P5 after human birth.**
>
> **The consequence for the portfolio, stated plainly.** If the P1–P5 result were a real window, its human
> equivalent would close **at or before birth** — that is, **weeks before the median WOREE presentation at 5
> weeks, and an unmeasured interval before a diagnosis nobody has dated.** But §6.1 says it is not a window; it
> is a tested range with no upper bound. **So the correct statement is neither "the window is closed" nor "the
> window is open" — it is: the experiment that would locate the upper bound has never been run, and the mouse
> model in which the window was defined cannot run it, because it dies.** That is the finding.
>
> ⚠️ **And one thing is settled and is not uncertain:** whatever the translation, `DL-MECH-011`'s own chain
> sentence — *"P1–P5 murino ≈ perinatale/tardo-gestazionale umano"* — carries **no method and no source** in the
> ledger. On the only index that gives a number, the correct reading is **earlier** than "perinatal" (see §7,
