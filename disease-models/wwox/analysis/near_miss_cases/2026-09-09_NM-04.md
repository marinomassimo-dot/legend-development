# NM-04 · An identifier taken from an external search while it sat inside the open artefact

**Actor:** `scientist-a` · **Wave:** `AQEILAN-FT-A-001` wave 4 · **Date:** 2026-09-09
**Source:** PMID 31428585 (Chang 2019, *Front Oncol* editorial), reference 1
**Caught by:** both blind auditors, independently
**Primary record:** `disease-models/wwox/research/deepdive_manifests/PMID31428585.json` `entries[6]`
and `verbatim_locators.audit_note` · receipts `FTR-20260909-31428585-01` and `-02` ·
commits `a387bf1` (landed), `ef02e94` (corrected)

---

## 1 · What almost happened

The reading's `entries[6]` records the family-level denominator the editorial gives for WW-domain
proteins, attributed to its reference 1 — Huang and Chang 2018, *Exp Biol Med* 243:137–47. The locator
named that reference as **`29581896`**. (The wrong identifier is written bare throughout these
files, without a `PMID` prefix, following the retrospective's own convention: it is an error being
quoted, not a citation, and prefixing it would make the unread-premise check read a documented
mistake as a load-bearing premise.)

**The deposit being read carries the answer in its own markup:** `<ext-link ext-link-type="pmid">`
**29310447**`</ext-link>`. `29581896` is a different paper entirely — a Chang-group FRET microscopy
paper in *Cell Death Discov* 2018.

The method failure is the substance, and the actor states it without softening:

> *"I ran an external author search and took its first hit, when the identifier was inside the
> artefact I already had open."*

The wrong value did not merely sit in a manifest. It was **persisted to an append-only ledger** in
receipt `FTR-20260909-31428585-01`, and the manifest's `multihop.queued[]` and
`multihop.gene_direct_refs_in_source[]` both carried it as this reading's declared multi-hop debt. Had
it stood, the corpus would have carried a reading debt pointing at the wrong paper — and 29310447 is
not an idle reference: it is **the reference that would settle what Figure 2A of the wave's other
paper (PMID 26499798) marks and does not name**, and it has no receipt in this corpus. The debt would
have been discharged, eventually, against a paper that could not answer the question it was queued for.

This is one of three instances of one failure in that wave, and the actor grouped them itself:
identifying Figure 2A's markers as known phosphorylation sites when the article names only one
phosphosite (caught by both auditors); writing two field-density counts from expectation — Zfra as 2
when it is 13, Alzheimer as 36 when it is 32 — and building a false sentence on one (caught by the
actor itself, re-measuring before shipping); and this. Its own generalisation:

> **"These are one failure, not three: an assumption asserted with the confidence of a measurement,
> in a place too small to look at twice."**

## 2 · What actually caught it

**Two independent blind auditors, both of whom did the one thing the reader had not: looked in the
artefact.** From the manifest's `audit_note`:

> *"ONE FACTUAL ERROR, caught by both: entry 6 gave ref 1's PMID as 29581896 where the deposit's own
> markup says 29310447, and 29581896 is a different paper entirely. Both auditors checked the deposit
> and both were right."*

Three further properties of this audit are worth keeping, because they are what an expensive control
buys:

- **Zero `NOT_IN_SOURCE` quotes** across 17 triples — every quote was a verbatim substring of the
  deposit. The reading's *quotation* discipline was sound; what failed was a value asserted *about* a
  reference, which quotation discipline does not touch.
- **The audit's most valuable output was an omission, not an error,** and both auditors found it
  independently: no triple covered *"Furthermore, blocking the downregulation of WWOX in the middle
  aged individuals would likely to prevent the development of AD"* — a **second** forward-looking
  clinical claim, weaker than the one the reading was built on because it carries no attribution at
  all where the first at least names a personal communication. The reading's headline changed shape
  from one unsupported clinical claim to two. *A reading is judged not only by whether its
  propositions are true, but by whether the document contains something worse that it walked past* —
  and nothing in this repository can look for that.
- **The audit ran at all only because the reading was ordered to survive.** A session rate limit had
  already killed two auditors mid-run. The actor landed the *reading* first — manifest, receipt,
  dossier — and wrote **no commit candidate** until verdicts existed, *"on the reasoning that a landed
  reading with no commit candidate touches no claim."* Nothing had to be reconstructed on resume. This
  is a procedural repair with a measured before/after: wave 2 lost an audit to a session boundary and
  shipped ten locators marked `[UNAUDITED]` with the candidate HELD; wave 4 lost a harder failure and
  lost nothing.

**The correction was made lawfully.** The bad PMID was in an append-only ledger and was fixed by a
linked `receipt_correction` — `FTR-20260909-31428585-02`, `reread_reason: receipt_correction`,
`prior_receipt: FTR-20260909-31428585-01` — carrying coverage, depth, analysis time, source locator and
fingerprint over unchanged, with only the evidence basis and outputs differing. The JSONL was never
hand-edited. Its own text says so: *"THIS IS A METADATA CORRECTION AND NOT A SECOND READING."*

## 3 · Why the gates let it through

Every gate passed on the manifest and the receipt carrying the wrong identifier: `deepdive_manifest.py
--verify-artifacts --require-current-schema` PASS with 0 gaps · `fulltext_receipts.py verify` OK, 152
chained, tail anchored · `legend_lint.py` PASS · `session_self_eval.py` PASS with neither wave-4 PMID
in any declared gap.

The blindness here is the sharpest illustration in the sweep of the recurring shape, because the
correct value was **inside a file the validator had already parsed**.

- **`multihop` is checked for shape and for debt-completeness, never for correctness.**
  `deepdive_manifest.py` asserts that `gene_direct_refs_in_source` is a list; that if it is non-empty,
  the references were either resolved or queued (unrecorded reading debt is refused); and that
  `references_enumerated` is a non-negative integer, on the reasoning that *"an integer is cheap to
  state and hard to fake."* `29581896` is a perfectly well-formed member of a list. Every assertion
  the validator makes about `multihop` was true of the wrong PMID.
- **The snippet-verification branch does not look at identifiers.** It matches the locator's *quote*
  against the declared artefact's text. `entries[6]`'s quote was verbatim and matched — the auditors
  confirmed zero `NOT_IN_SOURCE` across all 17 triples. The wrong number lives in the **proposition**,
  which is the reader's prose about the quote, and nothing in the validator reads a proposition as an
  assertion about the world.
- **`retraction_check` is per-PMID and asserts an outcome string.** It cannot notice that the PMID it
  was handed is not the paper the source cites.
- **The receipt writer's checks are identity, ordering and chaining.** It refuses `analysis_at` later
  than `event_at`, refuses a `first_read` naming a `prior_receipt`, refuses conflicting identifiers
  *for the same study*, and hashes each record onto the chain. All of that ran and all of it passed:
  the receipt's own `study_id` was correct throughout. The wrong PMID was a value inside
  `evidence_basis` prose and inside the manifest, not a study identity.
- **No gate cross-references a proposition against the artefact's structured markup.** The deposit's
  `<ext-link ext-link-type="pmid">` elements were parsed by `_xml_surfaces` on the way to building the
  body text used for quote matching. The correct answer passed through the validator's own hands and
  out again, because nothing asked the question.

## 4 · A verification that would have caught it

**This one is buildable, is already built, and should be wired in.** It is the strongest
cost-to-value case in the six.

### The check — `locator_identifier_provenance.py`, shipped by the same actor in the same wave

The tool was written in direct response to this defect. For every locator entry it extracts the
identifier-shaped tokens asserted in the **proposition** (PMID, PMCID, DOI) and asks one question:

> *does this identifier occur in ANY artefact this manifest declares?*

sorting each into `IN_ARTEFACT`, `DECLARED_EXTERNAL` (the proposition says in words that the value came
from elsewhere — a ledger, a registry, a DOI-to-PMID resolution) or `UNDECLARED_EXTERNAL` (neither).

**The property it asserts is deliberately the weak one**, and its own docstring is explicit about why:

> *"It is deliberately NOT a check that the identifier is CORRECT. Nothing local can know that. It
> checks the weaker, cheaper and — on the evidence of the failure above — sufficient property: whether
> the reading could have got the value from what it declares it read."*

Against NM-04 that is exactly sufficient. `29581896` occurs nowhere in the deposit; `29310447` occurs
in its markup. The locator would have landed in `UNDECLARED_EXTERNAL` and been a question before it
was a ledger entry.

### The wiring, and why it must be a WARN and not a block

The retrospective's § 9.4 proposal is the right shape: wire it into `deepdive_manifest.py` as a **WARN
with a required declaration** — an identifier asserted in a proposition must either occur in a declared
artefact, or carry an explicit `external_provenance` field naming the command or index that produced
it and the date. **The declaration is the point: a value that must be declared external is a value
someone can challenge.**

**False-positive risk: known, quantified, and structural.** The tool's own first corpus measurement:
**80 manifests · 26 measurable · 37 in-artefact · 4 declared external · 15 undeclared external · 16
source-identity unverified** — offered by its author as a review queue and *"explicitly not as 16
errors."* The third bucket is not an error set: a manifest may legitimately name a corpus PMID in a
proposition where the sentence makes provenance obvious to a human. Blocking on it would refuse
correct readings, which is why the tool's default exit code is 0 and `--strict` is opt-in. The right
target is *undeclared external = 0 on manifests minted after the change*, with the pre-existing 15
worked down as their papers are next touched, and **not retro-edited**.

One more caution, recorded because the tool itself recorded it: it **caught two defects in itself
before landing**, one of which was that it *would have missed the error it exists for*. A check written
in the heat of the defect it addresses deserves its own adversarial pass.

### The independent confirmation to watch

Machine checks cannot confirm this one; the auditors can. The measurable is: **blind auditors report
zero factual-identifier errors of the 29581896 class.** Baseline this sweep: **1**, found by both
auditors independently.

### What stays a human rule

The actor's own target, which should be adopted verbatim and is not mechanisable:

> *Before a locator carries an identifier, a count or a residue identity, the value must come from the
> artefact or from a command run in this session — never from recall, and never from the first hit of
> a search.*

The check above enforces the *identifier* limb only. The count limb was caught in this very wave by
the actor itself, re-measuring before shipping; the residue-identity limb was caught by both auditors.
Neither has a cheap machine form, and proposing one would be pretending.
