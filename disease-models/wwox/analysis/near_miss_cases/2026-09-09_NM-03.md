# NM-03 · A committed cross-panel bridge that fails on cell line

**Actor:** `scientist-a` · **Wave:** `AQEILAN-FT-A-001` wave 3 · **Date:** 2026-09-09
**Source:** PMID 15070730 (Aqeilan 2004, *PNAS*), Supporting Figures 5 and 6
**Caught by:** a blind auditor — `legend-locator-audit`, run because R4 was satisfied
**Primary record:** `disease-models/wwox/research/deepdive_manifests/PMID15070730.json` `entries[29]` ·
`disease-models/wwox/research/session_evaluations/2026-09-09_scientist-a_aqeilan_wave3.md` ·
commit `8583553`

> Unlike NM-01 and NM-02 this one **landed**. It was written into a manifest and committed before it
> was withdrawn. It is a near-error rather than an error because the withdrawal happened before the
> commit candidate reached any claim — and because the withdrawal is recorded in place rather than
> deleted.

---

## 1 · What almost happened

Figure 5 of this paper is the only graded-dose titration of Wwox against p73 localisation. Its citing
sentence asserts a dose-to-expression step that the panel itself does not display: the panel carries
**no dose labels of any kind**, no merged channel and no cell count.

The reader found what looked like the missing step in Figure 6 of the same supplement — a labelled
dose axis, with plasmid dose visibly raising Wwox protein — and wrote it up as a **cross-panel rescue
within one supplement**: Fig 6 supplies the step Fig 5 asserts and does not show. It was built,
written into `entries[29]` of the manifest, and committed.

**It fails on cell line.** Figure 5 is NIH 3T3 — the body says so in a sentence the reader had itself
captured as a locator two hours earlier: *"we transfected NIH 3T3 with equal amounts of HA-p73β and
different quantities of Myc-Wwox."* Figure 6 belongs to the SAOS-2 p21/transactivation series, and
`entries[24]` and `entries[30]` of the same manifest carry the SAOS-2 sentences verbatim. Two
different cell lines: **Figure 6 cannot establish the dose-to-expression step for Figure 5's
experiment.**

Two subsidiary quantities went with it. The reported *"roughly 1.6-fold protein for 2-fold plasmid"*
is wrong — the per-level folds are approximately 4.0 / 1.3 / 1.5, and the 0 ng pair exceeds 2× — and
*"monotonically"* is vacuous over two dose points.

What survives is much narrower and is recorded as such: Figure 6 carries a labelled dose axis that
Figure 5 lacks, and *within the SAOS-2 series* plasmid dose does raise Wwox protein. **The gap in
Figure 5's sentence is not closed anywhere in this paper.**

## 2 · What actually caught it

Two independent blind auditors, given only (proposition, quote, anchor) triples and the artefact —
never the dossier, never the reader's name, never the conclusions. They corrected **7 of 13 triples**.
The withdrawal is preserved in the manifest rather than deleted, and its wording is the record:

> *"WITHDRAWN IN FULL BY THE BLIND AUDIT — AND IT WAS THE MOST ATTRACTIVE FINDING IN THIS READING,
> WHICH IS WHY IT IS RECORDED RATHER THAN DELETED. […] IT FAILS ON CELL LINE, and I verified this
> against the body myself rather than on the auditor's word."*

Two things in that sentence are worth teaching separately. The auditor found it; the reader then
**re-derived it from the body** rather than accepting a verdict — which is the correct handling of an
audit finding and the inverse of NM-05's failure. And the withdrawn finding stays in the record, so a
later reader meets the refutation rather than the absence.

The actor's own diagnosis names the mechanism, and it is not carelessness:

> *"`partial` on the dimension 'did I check my most attractive finding as hard as my least attractive
> one' — I did not, and the pattern is that an appealing cross-panel rescue got less scrutiny
> precisely because it was appealing."*

The target it set from this — *"the finding I most want to be true is the one to re-derive from the
source before writing it"* — was met in wave 4, where the Figure 2B genotype work survived two
independent recomputations from the pixels. Wave 4's errors then came from somewhere else entirely
(NM-04), which is why wave 4's generalisation supersedes this one.

The audit ran at all only because **R4 was satisfied**: this reading proposed a NARROW on `CLAIM 023`,
a `consolidated baseline` claim. The actor also recorded, in the manifest's own `skills_considered`,
that the audit *should be run by someone else* and said so rather than self-auditing — *"a blind
auditor who does not know I wrote it is the point."*

## 3 · Why the gates let it through

The manifest carrying the wrong bridge passed everything: `deepdive_manifest.py --verify-artifacts
--require-current-schema` PASS with 0 gaps, 38 locators · `regenerate_adjudications.py verify` 9/9
digests and 9/9 needles for the wave's other paper · `fulltext_receipts.py verify` OK, 144 chained ·
`legend_lint.py` PASS after every commit · `session_self_eval.py` PASS.

The validator had in fact been *strict* with this reading — it rejected the first locator design four
times, for needle collisions, a relation outside the enum, a panel pointing at another panel and two
needles under 30 characters. Every one of those is a defect in the **shape of a declaration**. The
cell-line error is not.

Where each gate stops, specifically:

- **`entries[29]` declares `surface: figure`,** so the quote-verification branch never runs on it —
  the same structural blindness as NM-01. Its snippet, *"Western blotting was performed under standard
  conditions"*, is a methods sentence used as a textual anchor fixing *which experiment* the panel
  belongs to. The validator does not read it as evidence, and neither should a reader; it is not what
  the proposition rests on.
- **`panel_text_relation: panel_only`.** The coupled relations (`text_contradicted_by_panel`,
  `panel_qualifies_text`) are the ones the validator polices hard: they must name a target
  `entries[N]`, that target must exist, must not be the entry itself, must sit on a text surface, and
  must carry a resolvable needle. A locator declaring `panel_only` asserts a relation to **no other
  entry**, so none of that machinery engages. And that declaration was honest — the bridge genuinely
  was a claim about a panel, not a claim about a sentence.
- **The artefact digest matched.** `files/figures/PMID15070730/pnas_101_13_4401__00805Fig6.jpg` is the
  right file, correctly fingerprinted, correctly declared. The digest asserts *which pixels*; it has
  no concept of *which experiment those pixels come from*.
- **Nothing joins two locators by experimental context.** The manifest contains, at the time of the
  commit, both the NIH 3T3 sentence and the SAOS-2 sentences, as separate verified locators. The
  contradiction was fully present *inside a single machine-checked file* and no gate could see it,
  because no gate models "these two panels are the same experiment" — that is a fact about biology and
  method, not about the manifest's structure.

There is one further, harder blindness worth naming, because it bounds any fix: **Figure 6's cell line
is never stated in the retrieved artefact.** `entries[30]` records this explicitly — the
supporting-figure legends are absent (the caption stubs returned HTTP 403), so the SAOS-2 attribution
is the *body's*, not the pixels'. A checker demanding that each bridged panel declare a cell line
found in the source would have had nothing to find on one side of this very bridge.

## 4 · A verification that would have caught it

**The honest answer for the substance of this defect is: a human rule and a widened audit trigger, not
a machine.** No local check can assert that two panels share an experimental system. What *is*
buildable is a declaration that makes the assumption visible, and it should be built with its limits
stated.

### (a) Buildable, as a WARN with a required declaration — never a block

**Property asserted.** A figure locator whose proposition **bridges two panels** — i.e. asserts that
one panel supplies something another panel or its citing sentence lacks — must declare the
experimental system of each side, and the validator asks the weak, checkable question:
*does each declared system occur verbatim in some artefact this manifest declares?*

This is deliberately the same weak question that `locator_identifier_provenance.py` asks about
identifiers, and for the same stated reason: it *"is deliberately NOT a check that the identifier is
CORRECT. Nothing local can know that."* Here likewise, it cannot know that NIH 3T3 and SAOS-2 are the
wrong pair. It can force the pair to be **written down**, and a bridge whose two declared systems are
different strings is then a question anyone — reader, auditor or later session — can ask in one glance.

**False-positive risk: substantial, and it must not block.** Two sources of noise are already visible
in this very manifest. First, a legitimate bridge in a paper whose figure legends are unavailable has
no in-artefact string to declare — exactly PMID 15070730's Figure 6 — so the check must accept an
explicit `context_unstated` declaration and count it, rather than refuse the reading. Second, deciding
which propositions "bridge two panels" is a prose judgement; any regex will over- and under-fire. Both
argue for a review surface that reports a count, on the model of
`manifest_flag_drift.py`, whose docstring records the governing lesson: *"Overreaching there is how an
advisory tool earns the habit of being ignored."*

**Value: real but modest.** It would have surfaced this case, because both cell lines *were* verbatim
in declared artefacts. It would not have caught a bridge across two panels whose systems the paper
never names — and the corpus contains those.

### (b) Not worth building — a cell-line or model-system checker

A tool that extracts model systems from a paper and reasons about whether two panels are comparable is
a large piece of natural-language machinery, would need per-paper calibration, and would be wrong in
the direction that matters (a confident, plausible verdict about experimental comparability). Its cost
plainly exceeds its value against a defect the existing blind audit caught in one pass.

### (c) The control that actually worked, and the cheapest way to get more of it

**`legend-locator-audit`, blind, on triples.** It caught this in a corpus where nothing else could,
and in the same sweep it caught NM-04 twice over and found an *omission* no gate can look for. Its
limitation is not accuracy but **reach**: it runs only when R4 triggers on claim status, so it saw
this actor's readings and never looked at the other two lots at all.

The proportional response is the one the retrospective proposes in § 9.1 — **extend the R4 floor so
that a reading which contradicts, corrects or replaces a persisted verbatim locator is audited blind,
whatever claim status is involved** — and it is worth stating that this widening would *not* have
caught NM-03, which corrected nothing. It would have caught NM-01. The two together are the argument
for the trigger being about **what a reading does to the record**, not only about what it does to a
claim.

### What stays a human rule

*The finding you most want to be true is the one to re-derive from the source before writing it.* That
is the actor's own target, met in the following wave, and it is not mechanisable. The measurable proxy
already exists and is worth watching instead: the share of high-severity incidents that were
self-caught — **3 of 6 in this sweep**.
