# NM-01 · A false correction drafted against a correct persisted locator

**Actor:** `scientist-b` · **Wave:** `AQEILAN-FT-B-001` wave 1 · **Date:** 2026-09-09
**Source:** PMID 29724996 (Abu-Remaileh 2018), Figure 6A
**Caught by:** the reader's own judgement — a procedural rule applied in the right order
**Primary record:** `learning/scientist-b/SLR-scientist-b-0002.md` · receipt `FTR-20260909-29724996-01` ·
manifest `disease-models/wwox/research/deepdive_manifests/PMID29724996.json` `entries[21]`

> This is a record about a piece of work, not about an actor. The near-error is instructive
> *because* the reader was competent, well-motivated and inside its own lot: every property that
> normally makes a mistake survivable was absent, and the only thing between it and the ledger was
> the order in which one rule was applied.

---

## 1 · What almost happened

`PMID29724996.json` `entries[21]` is a figure attestation persisted on 2026-08-10 and carried
forward. It asserts, of Figure 6A:

> *"The high-fat-diet result is solid and its numbers are legible in the panel: incidence 11 of 12
> in the knockout against 4 of 18 in control."*

Resuming that paper's declared supplementary gap in wave 1, the reader re-opened Figure 6A **at the
667 px PMC rendering** and read the stacked bars as roughly **14 versus 1, of about 15** — a flat
contradiction of the persisted numbers — and *began drafting a correction to `entries[21]`.*

At 400 dpi the panel resolves differently. The bars are stacked **by genotype within each outcome
category**, not by outcome within each genotype: tumour-free = control 14 + cKO 1; liver-tumours =
control 4 + cKO 11. Control is therefore 4/18 and cKO 11/12 — **exactly what the existing locator
said**. The 14-versus-1 reading was the tumour-free bar alone, mistaken for the whole experiment.

Had the edit landed, it would have replaced a correct canonical figure locator with a false one, in
a manifest that supports `CLAIM 025`, and the false version would have been the one a later reader
inherited. The current `entries[21]` snippet carries the corrected arithmetic explicitly, so the
resolution is legible in the record rather than only in the diagnosis.

**What makes this the sweep's most instructive near-error** is the combination of properties: it
would have been an *edit to existing correct work*, made by the actor best placed to make it, argued
from pixels rather than from memory, and — because it touched no `consolidated baseline` claim — it
would have gone in with no blind auditor anywhere in the loop.

## 2 · What actually caught it

The reader, before writing the edit. From `SLR-scientist-b-0002.md`, graded against its own first
pass:

> **"I was about to file a false correction against a correct locator.** Reading Fig 6A at the
> 667 px PMC rendering I made the counts ~14 vs ~1 of ~15 and began drafting a correction to
> `entries[21]`, which asserts 11/12 vs 4/18. At 400 dpi the bars resolve as stacked **by genotype
> within each outcome category** and the existing locator is **exactly right**. What caught it was
> the rule that a figure is inspected at original resolution — applied *before* contradicting an
> existing locator, not after. **Grade for my own first pass here: `failed`.** The near-miss is the
> most important thing in this batch, because the wrong edit would have been confident,
> well-argued, and would have passed every gate this repository owns."

The mechanism is recorded in the receipt rather than only in the prose: the PMC assets are 667–800 px
wide, but the bar charts are **vector art in the publisher PDF**, so rasterising the page at 400 dpi
resolves them arbitrarily crisply. The instrument was available; the question was whether it would be
reached for before or after the contradiction was written.

Note the ordering, because it is the transferable part. The rule ("a figure is inspected at original
resolution") is not new and was not in dispute. What saved the record is that it was applied *as a
precondition for contradicting persisted work*, not as a later verification step.

## 3 · Why the gates let it through

Four gates ran on this reading and all four returned green — before and after the near-error, since
the near-error never became a defect. Each is listed with what it actually asserts.

| Gate | What it asserts | Why it is blind here |
|---|---|---|
| `deepdive_manifest.py --verify-artifacts --require-current-schema` | PASS, 0 gaps, 38 locators | see below |
| `fulltext_receipts.py verify` | the ledger hash chain is intact and the tail is anchored — 134 chained | says nothing about any locator's content |
| `legend_lint.py .` | PASS — structural integrity of the canonical state, IDs, statuses, wikilinks | the manifest is well-formed either way |
| `session_self_eval.py` | every complete read has landed and every declared output resolves | a landed wrong locator resolves as well as a landed right one |

The strict validator is the one that would have had to catch it, and the reason it cannot is
specific and worth stating precisely rather than as "no gate covered it".

**The snippet-verification branch never runs on a figure locator.** In `deepdive_manifest.py`, quote
matching is gated on `surface in TEXT_SURFACES`, and `TEXT_SURFACES = {body, table, supplement}`.
`entries[21]` declares `surface: figure`, and its snippet is a bracketed attestation that opens
`[figure attestation — pixels cannot be quote-matched]`. There is no string in the artefact for the
validator to compare it against, by construction: the evidence is pixels, and the attestation is the
reader's prose *about* those pixels.

What the validator does assert about `entries[21]`, all of which would have remained true of the
wrong version:

- `surface` is a member of the enum — `figure` is, whatever the panel says;
- `artifact` is declared in `source_artifacts` and its SHA-256 matches the file on disk. **This is
  the crucial one.** The digest asserts *which file was inspected*; it asserts nothing about *at what
  fidelity it was inspected*. The 667 px PMC JPEG and the 400 dpi rasterisation are readings of the
  same declared artefact, and the digest is identical for both;
- `anchor` is a non-empty string naming a place in the source — "Figure 6, panel A, stacked bar
  segments read at original resolution" is a well-formed anchor whether or not the reader was at
  original resolution;
- `panel_text_relation` is in the enum, and — being `text_confirmed_by_panel`, a coupled relation —
  names a target entry that exists, sits on a text surface, and carries a resolvable needle. All of
  that is a check on the *shape of the assertion*, not on whether the panel confirms the text.

So the defect class here is outside anything the validator can reach: it refuses **malformed
declarations about pixels** and is structurally blind to **a wrong reading of the pixels**.

Two further controls were absent for reasons that were, at the time, correct:

- **`legend-locator-audit` (R4) did not trigger.** The review floor was checked mechanically against
  the claim registry and correctly not met: this reading corroborated `CLAIM 025` in direction and
  narrowed its scope with `Status` unchanged, and the three consolidated baselines in scope are human
  clinical claims a hepatic paper does not reach. The trigger is written around *claim status*, and
  the near-error was not about claim status — it was about a persisted locator.
- **Nothing compares a manifest against its own committed predecessor at the locator level.** The
  repository does own a git-history comparator — `manifest_flag_drift.py` — but it watches
  *declared-effort flags* (`multihop.performed` and its siblings), not locator propositions. A
  rewritten `entries[21]` would have landed as an ordinary manifest revision, and no gate would have
  registered that a persisted assertion had been reversed.

## 4 · A verification that would have caught it

Two checks are available here and they are not equally worth building. Both are declaration checks;
neither can assert that a panel reading is *right*, and neither should pretend to.

### (a) Worth building — declare the fidelity a figure reading was taken at

**Property asserted.** A figure-surface locator whose proposition asserts a **count, a ratio or an
ordering** must declare the pixel dimensions of the surface it was read at, and the validator
compares that declaration against the actual dimensions of the declared artefact, refusing a
mismatch.

This does not assert that the resolution was *sufficient* — no machine can know what a given panel
requires. It asserts something weaker and cheaper: that the record says at what fidelity the reading
was taken, and that the number is not fiction. The whole of NM-01 is invisible in the artefact record
today, because 667 px and 400 dpi are indistinguishable once the digest matches. Making the fidelity
a declared field turns a silent property into a challengeable one, and makes "read at 667 px, then
contradicted a locator" a thing a later reader can see.

**False-positive risk: low, and of a benign kind.** The check fires when a declared dimension does not
match the artefact, which is either a typo or a genuine mismatch — both worth a look. The real cost is
not false positives but the boundary question of which propositions count as asserting a count; a
conservative regex over digits and ratio forms will miss some and should, because this is a WARN
surface and not a block. The design precedent is `locator_identifier_provenance.py`, which asks the
weaker checkable question rather than the strong uncheckable one.

### (b) Buildable, but only in its declaration limb — flag a locator that reverses a persisted one

**Property asserted.** When a manifest already committed is edited, any entry whose proposition or
snippet changes must carry an explicit declaration — of the form "this corrects what was written on
`<date>`" — and, per the retrospective's § 9.1 proposal, a sibling `audit_note`.

The declaration limb is genuinely buildable and cheap: `manifest_flag_drift.py` already reads a
manifest's own git history and already solved the hard design question here. Extending its diff to
locator entries is a bounded change.

**Do not build it as a prose grep.** The retrospective's § 9.1 sketches a script that greps manifests
for correction language and asserts a sibling `audit_note`. On this corpus that would swamp itself:
`PMID15070730.json` alone carries several entries whose propositions narrate corrections in exactly
that vocabulary (`entries[27]`, `entries[31]`, `entries[32]`) — all of them correct records of audits
that *did* run. The git-diff form has no such ambiguity, because it compares two revisions rather than
reading intent out of prose. `manifest_flag_drift.py`'s own docstring records that two earlier
note-detecting designs both returned PASS on the case the tool existed to catch, and that "no string
test can decide whether a note is ABOUT a flag" — the same conclusion applies here and should be
inherited rather than re-learned.

**The expensive half is the audit, not the flag, and it stays human.** Requiring a blind audit of
every reversed locator is affordable only because reversals are rare — three in this sweep, of which
one was audited. Scope the audit to the changed triples and it is bounded; make it unconditional over
all triples and it will be routed around.

### What stays a human rule

*Inspect a figure at original resolution before contradicting an existing locator.* No check can
enforce the ordering of two acts inside one reader's session. What the two checks above can do is make
the ordering **visible afterwards** — which is the most a repository can ask of itself here, and is
worth saying plainly rather than proposing a machine that would only appear to solve it.
