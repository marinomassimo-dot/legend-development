# NM-02 · A safety-critical screen called with inverted arguments, returning CLEAN on an unscreened filename

**Actor:** `scientist-c` · **Wave:** `AQEILAN-FT-C-001` wave 3 · **Date:** 2026-09-09
**Source:** PMID 16223882 (Fabbri/Croce 2005, *PNAS*) — the PDF text layer
**Caught by:** the reader's own judgement — refusing to believe a green result
**Primary record:** `disease-models/wwox/research/session_evaluations/2026-09-09_scientist-c_aqeilan_wave3.md` ·
receipt `FTR-20260909-16223882-01` · commit `91357fe`

---

## 1 · What almost happened

Rule 5c's worst failure class is a locator that *verifies* against a corrupted surface: the quote
matches, the badge of verification is earned, and the characters are ones no author wrote. The screen
that exists to prevent it is `_refuse_suspect_surface` in `framework/scripts/deepdive_manifest.py`,
whose signature is `_refuse_suspect_surface(path: Path, *parts: str)`.

Screening the PMID 16223882 PDF text layer, the reader called it as:

```
_refuse_suspect_surface(text, path_string)
```

— the arguments the wrong way round. **It returned normally.** `path` is not touched before the loop,
so the loop screened the *filename*, which of course holds no control characters, and the silent
return was read as CLEAN.

The surface actually being asked about carries **191 C0 control characters across 30,273 extracted
characters, and zero occurrences of** `< > ≤ ≥ ± × µ α β γ` — in a paper that prints `P < 0.05`,
`β-actin`, `25 µg` and `5 × 10⁶`. The correct call refuses it, and the receipt now records exactly
that refusal.

Had the green been trusted, the reader would have declared a SUSPECT surface as an `article_text`
artefact and anchored **twelve text locators** to characters no author wrote. In the actor's own
words, it *"would have worn the badge of having been checked."*

**It also retro-invalidated a result already reported.** The same inverted call had produced the
earlier "supplement text: CLEAN" verdict for PMID 41562193. That paper is nonetheless sound — but not
because the check worked. The authoritative screen also runs inside `deepdive_manifest.py` at write
time, so the manifest passed strict validation with the supplement TXT declared. It survived by
**defence in depth, not by the check the actor ran.**

## 2 · What actually caught it

The reader, by disbelieving a verdict it had not earned:

> **"Nothing in the repository would have caught this.** I caught it only because I had separately
> counted the raw characters, saw `<`, `±`, `×`, `β`, `μ` all at zero in a paper that prints
> `P < 0.05` and `β-actin`, and refused to believe a screen that called that clean. Had I trusted
> the green, I would have declared a SUSPECT surface as `article_text` and anchored twelve text
> locators to characters no author wrote — the exact false positive rule 5c calls the worst class
> this system can produce, **and it would have worn the badge of having been checked.**"

And the lesson the actor drew, which is the durable sentence and is not "be careful":

> **"my check asserted nothing and I reported it as though it had."**

The catch was *cross-measurement*: one instrument (a raw character census) disagreeing with another
(the screen's verdict). That is the same habit that caught this actor's Figure 2 segmentation error
and its Figure S1 baseline error in the same wave — arithmetic refusing to reconcile — and it is more
transferable than care.

## 3 · Why the gates let it through

Every gate ran and every gate passed, on both papers of the wave:
`deepdive_manifest.py` strict PASS with 0 gaps on both manifests · `regenerate_adjudications.py verify`
12 of 12 · `fulltext_receipts.py verify` OK, 145 chained · `legend_lint.py` PASS · `growth_anchors.py`
PASS with two ratchets improved · `session_self_eval.py` PASS.

The blindness here is of a different kind from NM-01's, and sharper. It is not that a validator
declines to inspect content. It is that **the screen's contract has no way to express "I screened
nothing."**

- **`_refuse_suspect_surface` signals by exception.** It raises `ValueError` on a suspect surface and
  returns `None` otherwise. A boolean-by-absence verdict cannot distinguish *"I examined 30,273
  characters and found them clean"* from *"I examined a 41-character filename."* Both are `None`.
  The screen's own failure mode is a **silent pass**, which is the one failure mode a caller has no
  way to notice.
- **Nothing typed the call site.** Before the repair, the function accepted whatever it was handed:
  `path` was used only for `path.name` inside the raised message, so an inverted call reached the
  message-formatting path only when it was going to refuse anyway. The inversion was therefore
  *invisible in exactly the case that mattered* — the clean case.
- **The strict validator did not cover this call.** The authoritative screen runs inside
  `deepdive_manifest.py` at write time and is well-formed there. What the actor ran was a *separate,
  hand-made call* to the same function during acquisition, to decide whether to declare the surface at
  all. That call is outside every gate: it precedes the manifest, and no artefact records that it
  happened. The validator would have refused the surface later — but by then twelve locators would
  have been built against it, and the reader would have been arguing with the validator rather than
  agreeing with it.
- **`fulltext_receipts.py verify` asserts chain integrity, not content.** A receipt declaring a
  SUSPECT surface as `article_text` chains and anchors exactly as well as an honest one.

The general shape, stated concretely rather than as a slogan: the repository's checks refuse
**malformed declarations** (a snippet under 30 characters, a timestamp out of order, a field name off
the enum) and are blind to **wrong content**. This case is the extreme point of that pattern, because
here the wrong content was *a verdict about content* — the check itself became the thing that asserted
something it had not established.

## 4 · A verification that would have caught it

This is the near-error with the best machine answer in the sweep, and part of it is already built.

### (a) Already shipped — the argument-shape guard

The wave's micro-upgrade was `framework/scripts/test_suspect_surface_call_shape.py` (9/9 PASS) plus an
argument-shape guard inside `_refuse_suspect_surface`: a non-`PathLike` first argument raises a
`TypeError` naming the inversion, and so does a part that is merely the artefact's own path or
filename — the mirror image of the same mistake.

It is **mutation-tested rather than asserted**: disabling the `PathLike` check turns exactly the two
inversion cases red and leaves the other seven green. Test 8 exists so the guard is not later deleted
for the usual reason — a real document that *mentions* its own filename still passes, because the
guard compares the whole stripped part and never a substring.

**False-positive risk: effectively zero**, and this is a property of the design rather than a hope.
The guard inspects the *shape of the call*, never the text. Every in-tree caller passes a `Path`; a
caller that does not has swapped its arguments. There is no document content that can trip it.

### (b) Worth building — and it is the single highest-value check the sweep produced

The guard closes the inversion case. It does not close the class. The class is **a screen that returns
an uninformative pass**, and it is the class that also produced NM-05 at the opposite polarity — a
refusal accepted without asking what it refused for.

**Property asserted.** A verdict is a **record, not a boolean**. Every screen in `framework/scripts/`
returns a structure carrying `screened: {digest, bytes}` — the SHA-256 and byte length of what it
actually examined — and, on a refusal, **which signature fired**. A verdict over a zero-length or
non-artefact input is an error, never a pass. `INSUFFICIENT_DATA`, naming the missing input, becomes
the repository-wide convention rather than one tool's practice.

Under that contract, NM-02 is not a near-error but an impossible state: a verdict reporting
`bytes: 41` on a surface the reader knows to be 30 KB is self-evidently a verdict about the wrong
thing, and it is *visible in the record* rather than only in the reader's suspicion.

**Baseline, measured.** Of the seven screens in scope — `text_surface_intrusion_check.py`,
`_refuse_suspect_surface`, `erratum_scope_check.py`, `oa_status_dissent.py`, `manifest_flag_drift.py`,
`locator_identifier_provenance.py`, `genre_discriminator.py` — **1 of 7** (`genre_discriminator`)
refuses to return an uninformative pass, and **0 of 7** report the digest of what they screened.

**False-positive risk: near zero, and the residual cost is not false positives but churn.** The
contract adds no new opinion about any document; it only makes each tool state its own scope. The real
cost is seven call-site migrations and the test updates behind them. The mutation test that proves it
is the shape `test_suspect_surface_call_shape.py` already demonstrates for one tool: handing each
screen a path where it expects content, or an empty string, must turn exactly the new cases red and
leave every other case green.

**Judgement: build this one.** It is the only proposal in this file set that covers two of the six
near-errors, it carries no document-content heuristic and therefore no false-positive surface, and one
seventh of it is already shipped and mutation-tested.
