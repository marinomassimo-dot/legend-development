# NM-05 · A REFUSED verdict nearly accepted without asking what it refused for

**Actor:** `scientist-b` · **Wave:** `AQEILAN-FT-B-001` wave 6 · **Date:** 2026-09-09
**Source:** PMID 25245215 (Aqeilan 2014, *CMLS* 71(23) chapter 8) — the PDF text layer
**Caught by:** the coordinator's mid-task instruction
**Primary record:** `disease-models/wwox/research/session_evaluations/2026-09-09_scientist-b-wave6.md`
Part 2 and Part 3 · commit `1e12c82`

> This is NM-02's failure at the opposite polarity, and it happened to a reader who had shipped a
> whole tool about that failure one wave earlier. That is what makes it worth writing down: knowing
> the rule is not the control.

---

## 1 · What almost happened

The PMID 25245215 PDF text layer was **REFUSED** by the validator's suspect-surface screen. Refused is
the *right outcome*: the surface was excluded, the reading proceeded on clean HTML, and nothing was
harmed. The reader could have stopped there, and nearly did.

Running the determination out shows the refusal fired for **two incidental reasons** — 8 harmless
front-matter `·` keyword separators (a C0 control), and suspicion-by-absence — while
`PRINTABLE_SUBSTITUTIONS`, the signature set that exists to catch actual character corruption, matched
the real defect **with nothing**. The counterfactual, run rather than argued:

```
as extracted                        REFUSED  (C0 control — a keyword separator)
C0 stripped                         REFUSED  (suspicion by absence)
C0 stripped + statistics reworded   ACCEPTED  ← Wwox?/-, Wwox?/?, Wwox?/mic all intact
```

The surface that survives both incidental signatures is one in which the extraction has welded `?` to
`/` where the page prints a superscript allele sign:

```
Wwox +/−  →  Wwox?/-      Wwox +/+  →  Wwox?/?      Wwox +/− mice  →  Wwox?/mice
```

**Wild-type and heterozygote become typographically indistinguishable** — the exact distinction rule
5d names as load-bearing, in a corpus whose central evidence includes heterozygote tumour incidence.

Nothing bad happened to *this* paper. What almost happened is worse than a single wrong locator: **a
screen would have been left standing with a known blind spot and a green record of having worked.**
Both incidental signatures are removable by ordinary variation — another paper from the same producer
without a `·`-separated keyword block, or with its statistics phrased differently, presents the
genotype corruption with nothing left to refuse it. The near-error is the *unbuilt* fix, and its blast
radius is every future paper from that production chain.

## 2 · What actually caught it

Not the reader, and the record says so plainly. **The coordinator's mid-task instruction:**

> *finish the determination from the validator's own code, and if the answer is that the validator
> screens less than rule 5d requires, that is a finding to report, not a gap to route around.*

The reader's own diagnosis of why it needed saying is the sharpest sentence in the sweep on this class:

> **"A green result for the wrong reason is the failure mode I shipped a whole tool about in wave 5"**
> (`genre_discriminator`'s design rule: *a screen whose failure mode is a silent pass is worse than no
> screen*). **"I had the rule and still nearly accepted a refusal without asking what it was refusing
> *for*."**

And the generalisation, which is the one worth propagating repository-wide:

> **"'The gate said no' is not a finding; 'the gate said no because X' is."**

**The response was proportionate and was calibrated before it shipped**, which is the part to imitate.
A fifth signature was added to `PRINTABLE_SUBSTITUTIONS` in `deepdive_manifest.py`, catching `?` welded
to `/` where the page prints a superscript allele sign. It was screened over **all 45 local
text-bearing artifacts** in `files/fulltext/` — 25 structured XML/HTML/TXT surfaces and 20 PDFs via
`pdftotext` — returning **9 hits, all 9 in the one PDF, 0 in the other 44**. Both halves were tested,
per the module's own convention: one test that the substitution is caught and survives a "repair", one
that the pattern does **not** fire on ordinary prose (a question mark ending a sentence before a
slashed pair, a correctly-rendered `Wwox+/-`, an ordinary `positive/negative`). Suite 92 → **94 tests,
OK**. Regression proven twice over: 55 corpus manifests non-PASS before and after, none implicating the
new pattern, established by grep **and** by re-running against `git stash`-ed code. The previously-
`ACCEPTED` counterfactual is now `REFUSED` **for the genotype reason**.

## 3 · Why the gates let it through

Here the gate did not merely fail to fire. **The gate fired, correctly, and its firing is what
concealed the defect.**

- **`_refuse_suspect_surface` refuses by exception and reports one reason — the first one it reaches.**
  It raises on the first signature that matches. Once the C0 separator matched, the screen was done:
  it had a true statement to make about the surface, and it made it. Nothing in its contract obliges
  it to enumerate *every* signature it could fire on, still less to report which ones it checked and
  found nothing. A refusal is therefore a **verdict without a scope**, and a caller cannot tell a
  refusal that caught the load-bearing corruption from one that caught a keyword separator.
- **Suspicion-by-absence compounds it.** The second refusal branch fires when a paper uses statistical
  language and prints none of `< > ≤ ≥ ± × µ α β γ`. That branch is deliberately coarse — it exists to
  say *"adjudicate against the rendered page before declaring this surface"* — and it is satisfied by
  a great many defective surfaces including ones whose actual defect it never looked for. A coarse
  true refusal masks a specific missing one.
- **`PRINTABLE_SUBSTITUTIONS` is a hand-built list of known corruptions,** and can only assert about
  the corruptions someone has already met. It is precisely the class of check that is *complete by
  accretion*: it grows one measured defect at a time, and every un-met corruption is a silent gap by
  construction. That is not a flaw in the design — a general "is this text corrupted" oracle does not
  exist — but it means **the number of signatures that fired is the only evidence the caller has about
  the screen's coverage, and today that number is not reported.**
- **Every downstream gate was green and correctly so.** The manifest passed strict with 0 gaps (21
  locators / 5 artifacts), `fulltext_receipts.py verify` returned OK at 154 chained, `legend_lint.py`
  PASS, `growth_anchors.py` PASS, `genre_discriminator.py --self-test` 24/24. All true. None of them
  has any view into *why* a surface was excluded — only that the reading proceeded on a declared clean
  one, which it did.

The general shape again, made concrete: the validator refuses a **malformed declaration** — here, a
declaration that a corrupted surface is `article_text` — and is blind to **whether the refusal was for
the reason that matters**. The defect is not in what the gate asserted; it is in everything the gate
declined to say.

## 4 · A verification that would have caught it

### The check — the same verdict contract NM-02 argues for

**Property asserted.** A screen's refusal is a **record**, not an exception with a message. It carries
`screened: {digest, bytes}` — what was actually examined — and **which signature fired**, plus the set
of signature families evaluated. A verdict over a zero-length or non-artefact input is an error, never
a pass.

Under that contract, this near-error is visible at a glance: a refusal reporting
`signature: C0_CONTROL, matched_span: keyword separator` on a paper whose distinguishing feature is a
genotype notation is self-evidently a refusal about the wrong thing. The reader would not have needed a
coordinator to ask the question, because the answer would have been printed next to the verdict.

**False-positive risk: none of the usual kind.** The change adds no new opinion about any document; it
only makes each screen state its own scope. Its cost is call-site migration across the seven screens
(baseline: **0 of 7** report the digest of what they screened; **1 of 7** refuses to return an
uninformative pass) and the tests behind them.

**This is the same build as NM-02's, and that is the argument for it.** One contract change closes two
of the sweep's six near-errors — a silent pass and an unexamined refusal — which no other proposal in
this file set does.

### A second, cheaper check worth having alongside

**Property asserted.** When `PRINTABLE_SUBSTITUTIONS` (or any signature set) changes, a calibration
run over every local text-bearing artefact is required, and its counts are recorded with the change.

This is a codification of what the actor already did by hand — 45 artefacts, 9 hits, all 9 in one PDF,
0 in the other 44 — and the value is that it makes the *before* number exist. Without it, a later
session cannot tell a signature that earns its place from one that fires everywhere.

**False-positive risk: not applicable**; it is a measurement obligation, not a verdict. Its cost is one
corpus run per signature change, which is minutes.

### What stays a human rule

*"The gate said no" is not a finding; "the gate said no because X" is.* And its harder corollary,
which this case demonstrates and which no machine can supply: **the reader was already carrying the
right rule, in its own words, from one wave earlier, and still needed a second party to make it
operate.** The verdict contract does not remove that need — it lowers the cost of meeting it from an
investigation to a glance, which is the realistic ambition.

One organisational note belongs here rather than in a proposal, because it is a control the repository
has and does not name: **this is the sweep's only near-error caught by the coordinator**, and it was
caught by an instruction to *finish a determination* rather than by a review of a finished product.
Mid-task direction reached a defect that no end-of-task review would have seen, because by then the
refusal would have been a green line in a table.
