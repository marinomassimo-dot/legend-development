## 2 · The three findings that change what LEGEND holds

### 2.1 The antibody/genotype problem is structural, and it is dated

Both foundational localisation claims are **older than the first Wwox-null animal**. Chang's
mitochondrial claim is `11058590` (2000); Aldaz's Golgi claim is `11719429` (2001) and `14526170`
(2003); Chang's immuno-EM is `15664696` (2005). The first full `Wwox` knockout mouse lines — the only
material that can validate a WWOX antibody by absence of signal — are described in `24932569` as
generated later, and `25012504` (2014) is the first paper in this set to use `Wwox`-deficient cells
as the experimental subject.

**Consequence:** neither lab *could* have run the decisive control at the time it made its claim, and
**neither has gone back and run it since**, in anything read or indexed here. Both sides' compartment
assignments rest on either (a) a GFP tag that can itself mislocalise a 46 kDa protein, or (b) an
antibody never shown to go dark in null tissue. Asking *"would this experiment produce this result
even if the claim were false?"* — for row 1a the answer is **yes on both sides**. That is why 1a is
`UNRESOLVED` and not a split decision.

One asymmetry survives it: `15664696` is the only experiment in the dispute that **tested the rival
hypothesis directly and reported its negative** (*"little or no WOX1-positive particles were found in
the Golgi apparatus"*). Aldaz never ran the reciprocal test on mitochondria under stress.

### 2.2 🔴 Registry correction — `PAPER 053` mis-targets Aldaz's artefact argument

`PAPER 053` records that Aldaz *"attribuisce il ruolo pro-apoptotico riportato dal lab Chang ad
artefatto da vettori adenovirali."* The full text does not say that. The sentence is:

> *"Furthermore, WWOX overexpression has been reported to induce apoptosis in lung (), prostate ()
> and breast () cancer lines among others; in most of these cases the delivery of WWOX was
> [via] adenoviral vectors."* — `24932569`

The adenoviral attribution is attached to **the lung / prostate / breast overexpression papers**,
which are a separate literature. Chang's own cited apoptosis work — `11058590`, `12514174`,
`16219768` — uses **stable and transient transfection, antisense mRNA, a dominant-negative construct
and siRNA**; adenoviral delivery appears nowhere in their abstracts or MeSH. Aldaz's separate,
earlier sentence — *"Some authors originally proposed that WWOX behaves as a pro-apoptotic molecule
()"* — carries a **different** reference marker.

⚠️ **The identity of those reference markers cannot be resolved here** (§ 0.3), so this is stated as
what the *text* attaches to what, not as a verified reference mapping. But the direction is clear and
it matters: the registry note credits Aldaz with a cleaner kill on Chang than the review actually
claims, and it obscures the real division — **sufficiency vs necessity** (row 2).

### 2.3 Aldaz's own "agreeing" citation reports a conditional pro-apoptotic phenotype

`24932569` writes *"In agreement with Gourley et al (), we hypothesize that WWOX is not
pro-apoptotic when overexpressed in cancer cells…"*. Gourley's abstract (`19458077`) says:

> *"whereas there is no WWOX-dependent difference in cell death in adherent cells, WWOX-transfected
> cells in suspension culture display a proapoptotic phenotype."*

Gourley is not a flat negative. It is a **context-dependent** result: no apoptosis when adherent,
apoptosis when detached. Aldaz's citation is accurate **only for the adherent condition**, which is
the condition he names. The honest reading of the whole set is that WWOX's pro-apoptotic output is
**conditional on cellular state** — which is the same shape as the resolution of rows 1 and 4, arrived
at from a third laboratory.

### 2.4 A mitochondrial *phenotype* does not require mitochondrial *residence*

`25012504` (Aqeilan, third party, genotype-controlled): *"WWOX deficiency is associated with enhanced
glycolysis and diminished mitochondrial respiration"* — and the mechanism offered is
**transcriptional**, via HIF1α, through the **WW1 domain**, not through anything mitochondrial. This
is the cleanest demonstration in the corpus that **WWOX loss can produce a mitochondrial functional
deficit without WWOX ever being in a mitochondrion.** Any future argument that infers Chang's
localisation from Chang's mitochondrial phenotypes must clear this alternative first. It does not
refute row 1b — `15664696` is a direct localisation measurement, not an inference from phenotype —
but it disqualifies the phenotype-based version of the argument.

## 3 · What would settle each unresolved dispute

One decisive experiment per row. Each is specified so that a null result is as informative as a
positive one.

| Row | Decisive experiment |
|---|---|
| **1a** — steady-state localisation | **Genotype-controlled antibody validation followed by fractionation, at endogenous level, in one system, with both labs' antibodies run side by side.** Take `Wwox+/+` and `Wwox−/−` MEFs from the existing lines. Step 1: immunoblot and immunofluoresce both antibodies on both genotypes — any signal surviving in the null is artefact, and the experiment stops there for that reagent. Step 2: on the validated reagent only, run subcellular fractionation with Golgi (GM130/TGN46), mitochondrial (TOM20/VDAC) and nuclear (lamin B) markers, unstimulated. **The null result is the informative one:** if the Golgi/perinuclear assignment survives in validated endogenous fractionation, row 1b becomes a pure stress-relocation story and 1a is closed for Aldaz. No tagged construct may appear anywhere in this experiment — that is the whole point |
| **1b** — stress relocation | **Reciprocal immuno-EM under the rival's condition.** Repeat the `15664696` immunogold protocol, with the `Wwox−/−` genotype as the specificity control that 2005 could not have run, quantifying gold particles per organelle over **Golgi, mitochondria and nucleus simultaneously** in the same sections, before and after a defined stress. Ambiguity here is entirely about particle assignment, so blinded counting by compartment marker is the whole experiment |
| **2** — pro-apoptotic role | **Already effectively settled as posed — do not re-run it.** The remaining question is not sufficiency vs necessity but **whether necessity holds in neurons**: repeat the `16219768` siRNA design (endogenous knockdown → stress challenge → survival) in **primary post-mitotic neurons**, not L929 fibroblasts, with the stressor relevant to the disease rather than TNF/staurosporine. If WWOX knockdown *protects* neurons from stress, the neuroprotective framing of the whole Chang corpus inverts, and that is worth knowing before any of it is built on |
| **3** — SDR function | **Test the alleles instead of the deletions.** Both labs compared full-length WWOX to large exonic deletions, which destroy the fold and cannot distinguish "the SDR targets the protein" from "an unfolded protein mislocalises". Express **single disease-relevant SDR missense alleles** at near-endogenous level in `Wwox−/−` cells, and score, in one assay: (i) compartment occupancy against Golgi and mitochondrial markers, (ii) whether the mutant reaches the nucleus as the deletion isoforms do, (iii) retention of a known SDR-tail binding partner. **A missense allele that folds but mislocalises proves the targeting claim; one that localises normally kills it.** Because Aldaz's `14526170` frames SDR-less isoforms as *"possibly functioning as dominant negative inhibitors of full length WWOX"*, this experiment must be run **with and without** co-expressed wild-type WWOX — otherwise a dominant-negative allele and a loss-of-function allele are indistinguishable |
| **4** — physiological vs stress role | **No experiment needed — the dispute is dissolved, not unresolved.** Both roles are separately evidenced. What is worth doing is the integration: measure the constitutive scaffold function (a sequestration readout) and the stress-activation readout (Tyr33 phosphorylation) **in the same cells across a stress time course**, to establish whether stress activation *suspends* the scaffold function or runs in parallel with it |
| **5** — haploinsufficiency | **No dispute to settle; a reporting fix.** Any future use of the Aldaz sentence must carry its endpoints. If a CNS haploinsufficiency claim is wanted at Aldaz's standard of rigour, the experiment is aged-cognition phenotyping of `Wwox+/−` on the Aldaz-lab genetic background with blinded scoring — i.e. Chang's endpoint on Aldaz's animals, which no one has run |

pro-apoptotic" — turns out to be two labs answering two different questions with two different
experimental directions, and both answers stand. The genuinely open question is narrower and duller
than the controversy: **where is endogenous, untagged WWOX at rest, measured with a reagent validated
against null tissue?** Nobody has answered it, in 26 years.

## 5 · Epistemic notes and limits of this packet

1. **Nothing here is medical advice, and no therapy is proposed.** No commit candidate is created.
2. **No figure panel was inspected.** Every verdict above is anchored to prose, per `D-14`.
3. **Eight of the ten papers were read at abstract depth.** The method-class assignments they carry
   are drawn from abstract text and MeSH indexing and are reliable *as method-class assignments*;
   they are not a substitute for full-text reading, and rows **1a**, **1b** and **3** should be
   re-adjudicated if those full texts are ever acquired.
4. **This packet does not rank the laboratories.** It ranks experiments. On row 1b Chang's method is
   better; on row 2 Aldaz's negative is sound and Chang's positive is not; on rows 1a and 3 both are
   insufficient. Any summary that reduces this to "Chang wins" or "Aldaz wins" misreports it.
5. **Direction of correction to Wave 1.** Wave 1 concluded, from `27551439` alone, *"Aldaz better
   supported on method"* on the apoptosis dispute. That conclusion was correct **for that paper** and
   is superseded for the corpus: `16219768` supplies the endogenous loss-of-function experiment that
   `27551439` lacks, and the artefact argument cannot reach it. Row 2 is the amended verdict.
