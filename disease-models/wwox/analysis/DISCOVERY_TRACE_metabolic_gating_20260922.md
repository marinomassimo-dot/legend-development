## 1 · OBSERVATION

Two facts from this session sit next to each other and have not been put together:

1. **Survival is decided early.** Untreated `Wwox`-null mice die at *"approximately three weeks"*;
   the LD arm *"declines from ~20 d"* and reaches **0%** by ~80 d; HD plateaus ≈80% to 300 days.
2. **The only formally tested, arm-separating, survivor-conditioning-free endpoint inside that window
   is BLOOD GLUCOSE at P20** — `*` WT-vs-LD, **`**` LD-vs-HD**, **`ns` WT-vs-HD**. So at P20 the two
   doses are statistically separated **and HD is indistinguishable from wild type**.

Meanwhile **every** WWOX protein measurement is at **P30 or later**, i.e. after the window, in the
animals that survived it — and across LD→HD protein shows only *"a trend"*, with significance in
**1 of 4** regions and none on mRNA.

> **So the variable that separates the arms inside the lethal window is metabolic, not the protein
> abundance everyone has been measuring.**

## 2 · DIVERGE — five mechanistically distinct readings of that pairing

| | mechanism | distinct because |
|---|---|---|
| **D1** | **Glucose is the PROXIMATE CAUSE of death.** Hypoglycaemia is severe enough to kill; the dose difference matters only insofar as it corrects glucose. | makes glucose **causal** and protein merely upstream |
| **D2** | **Glucose is a BIOMARKER of adequate CNS rescue**, not a cause — it reads out how much functional WWOX reached a glucose-regulating population, and death is by another route (seizures, respiratory, growth failure). | makes glucose **diagnostic**, not causal |
| **D3** | **Glucose is one limb of a shared upstream cause** — e.g. a hypothalamic/brainstem population whose failure produces hypoglycaemia *and* the lethal event independently. | no causal arrow between glucose and death at all |
| **D4** | **Glucose is an artefact of feeding/competition** — sicker pups suckle less, so hypoglycaemia is a *consequence* of decline, reversing the arrow. | inverts D1's direction entirely |
| **D5** | **Glucose separates the arms only because it is the most sensitive assay available at P20**, and other early variables would separate them too if measured. | denies glucose any special mechanistic status |

## 3 · CONNECT — outside domains that inform this

- **Congenital hyperinsulinism / neonatal hypoglycaemia**: a field whose entire question is whether
  hypoglycaemia is a treatable proximate cause of neurological injury, with established management
  and a long argument about glucose thresholds. Transferable: the **distinction between a glucose
  number and a glucose-mediated injury**, and the assay conventions for establishing it.
- **Central glucose sensing** (hypothalamic glucose-sensing neurons, counter-regulatory response):
  supplies the candidate anatomy for D1/D3 — and note that **hypothalamus was never assayed for WWOX
  at any dose**.
- **Mitochondrial and metabolic epilepsies** (e.g. GLUT1 deficiency): the canonical precedent in
  which a **metabolic intervention modifies a DEE** without touching the causative gene. Transferable:
  the *architecture* of a metabolic adjunct. **NOT transferable:** any specific therapy, dose or
  disease conclusion.
- **Neonatal mouse husbandry / pup competition**: supplies D4's falsifier and the reason weight must
  be read alongside glucose.

## 4 · HYPOTHESIS under test

> **H1 (= D1):** Survival in this model is **gated by a centrally-controlled metabolic variable**, such
> that the dose difference acts on survival **largely through glucose**, and correcting the metabolic
> variable would improve survival **partly independently of full CNS WWOX restoration.**

⚠️ **This is the therapeutically interesting reading, which is exactly why it must be attacked
hardest.** D2, D3 and D4 all predict that a metabolic intervention would **not** rescue survival.

## 6 · DISCRIMINATOR

**The clean discriminator does not exist in the literature and would be one arm:** a
glucose-supplementation (or scheduled-feeding) arm in untreated `Wwox`-null pups, with survival as
the endpoint. **H1 predicts improved survival; D2, D3 and D4 predict none.**

**Within existing data**, the partial discriminator is: does survival across **all** arms track
**glucose correction** more closely than it tracks **regional WWOX protein**? And does **weight**
separate the arms on the same schedule as glucose (H1/D1) or lag it (D4)?

### 7.3 🎯 And the DIVERGE step beat my own chosen hypothesis — which is the method working

According to PubMed, Ludes-Meyers et al. 2009, *PLoS One* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0007775),
PMC2777388) reports that `Wwox`-KO mice show, **concurrently**:

> *"significant **hypocapnia** suggesting a state of **metabolic acidosis**"* · *"impaired
> hematopoiesis, **leukopenia**, and **splenic atrophy**"* · *"**Hypoglycemia and hypocalcemia**"* ·
> *"bone metabolic defects … reduced bone volume … a defect in **mineralization**"*

— and proposes that *"impaired hematopoiesis can also be a contributing factor to metabolic acidosis
and death."*

> 🔴 **So hypoglycaemia is ONE limb of a multi-system metabolic derangement, not the proximate cause.
> That is `D3`, which I did NOT choose — and it is now better supported than `D1`, which I did.**

**A second, independent line cuts the same way**, and it was already in the repository: the rat
`lde/lde` is recorded as **uremic WITHOUT being hypoglycaemic**. A model with the neurological
phenotype and no hypoglycaemia shows glucose is **not necessary** for that phenotype.

⚠️ **Consequence for `P3`:** its "not tested" is now *less* interesting, not more — under `D3` a
glucose-only intervention is **predicted to fail**, so its absence is unsurprising rather than a gap.

### 7.4 🎯 What SURVIVES, and it is a question nobody appears to have asked

If death is driven substantially by **acidosis, impaired haematopoiesis, hypocalcaemia and bone
mineralisation failure** — all **peripheral** — then:

> **How does a NEURON-SPECIFIC vector (`hSynI`, CNS-only) rescue a death with large peripheral
> components?**

The repository answers this for **glucose** (central control, recorded). It does not answer it for
acidosis, calcium, haematopoiesis or bone. **And those endpoints appear never to have been measured in
treated animals.** Counted across the TX-007 analyses and the claim registry:

| term | count |
|---|---|
| `hypocapnia` · `hypocalc` · `calcium` · `leukopenia` · `h(a)ematopoi` · `bone volume` · `mineralis/z` | **0 each** |
| `acidosis` · `splenic` | **1 each** |

⇒ **The gene therapy is evaluated on survival, growth, glucose, seizures, myelin, gliosis and
behaviour — and NOT on the other derangements the same field named as contributors to death.**

**Two readings, and they are cheap to separate:** either neuronal WWOX restoration **corrects** the
peripheral derangements (which would be a striking and unreported central-control result), or it
**rescues survival despite them** (which would mean they were never the lethal limb, and would
narrow the field's own "succumb to metabolic defects" framing).
