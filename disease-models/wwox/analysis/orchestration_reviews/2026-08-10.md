## 3. Proposals

**First: freeze the manifest contract for the duration of a reading round.**
Contract changes to `deepdive_manifest.py` land only at a round boundary — after the round's branches
are merged, never while they are open. A manifest records the validator commit it was written against;
a new required field applies to manifests authored after it, and older ones are `unknown_legacy` until
their next touch.
*Why first:* the intervention hierarchy puts *eliminate unnecessary work* above *add validation*, and
this is the only proposal that removes work rather than adding it. It cost ~1000 re-authored lines today
and is currently blocking 21 locator entries on three branches.
*Falsifiable success criterion:* in the next round, **zero** commits whose message or diff is a
manifest realignment to a field introduced mid-round; strict-validation blocks attributable to
post-authoring fields = 0.
*Trade-off:* a defect found at 16:00 in the contract waits until the round closes. Correctness
improvements are delayed by up to one round, and the round must therefore be short.

**Second: `coverage.supplementary: read` must be backed by an artifact.** At least one fingerprinted
`supplement_text`/supplement artifact **and** either one `surface: supplement` locator or an explicit
named waiver; otherwise the slot may only be `not_read` / `not_present` / `unavailable`. Enforced where
`require_work_manifest` already enforces the body.
*Falsifiable criterion:* re-running today's 7 readings under the rule, 7 of 7 either produce a
supplement locator/waiver or downgrade the slot honestly; corpus `surface: supplement` count rises
above 1.
*Trade-off:* readings get slower, and some "supplementary: read" claims will be revealed as
optimistic and must be re-opened — which is the point, and which is retroactive debt.

**Third: one paper, one actor, per round** — a written assignment before dispatch, checked against
open branches. *Criterion:* zero files modified on two branches in one round. *Trade-off:* the
Orchestrator serialises what it might have parallelised, and loses the accidental second-reader effect
that produced `385dd28` ("A second harness reads PMID 30755385, and finds a hole three reviews had
missed"). If second readings are wanted, they should be *assigned* as second readings, not collided into.

*Hypothesis:* contradiction yield per panel is a property of the paper, not the reader.

### 6.1 How much supplementary evidence exists under the wrong label — floor, estimate, ceiling

Union of all seven branches, one version per paper (the one with the most locator entries). **192**
`surface: figure` locators in the corpus. Three *independent* detectors, each declared:

| detector | what it is | count | share |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1 — floor** | an unambiguous token (`supplement`/`suppl`/`mmc\d`) in the artifact's `kind` or `path`. Cannot produce a false positive. | **7** | 3.6% |
| **D2** | naming conventions actually used in this corpus (`S_fig_*`, `S_p\d*`, `S\d+_caption`, `File0\d\d`, `_esm`, `si_`) | 18 | 9.4% |
| **D3** | **the locator's own prose** — `anchor`/`proposition`/`note` says "supplement". Independent of every filename. | 29 | 15.1% |
| **D2 ∪ D3 — best estimate** | | **29** | 15.1% |
| **ceiling** | every figure locator not positively identifiable as a *main* figure (`fig\d`, `gr\d`, `figs/fig…`) | **103** | 53.6% |

`surface: supplement` locators in the entire corpus, all branches, all time: **1**.

**The interval is [7 … 29 … 103] against a representable count of 1 — a 15× spread between floor and
ceiling. That spread is the measurement.** The state cannot say whether 4% or 54% of its figure evidence
came from a supplement, and no probe can close the gap, because closing it requires a fact the schema
does not hold.

**The sharpest single result is that D2 ⊆ D3 exactly**: every path-convention hit is also a prose hit,
and prose finds eleven more. **The readers wrote the fact down every time — in free text, inside an
`anchor` field, where no command will ever read it.** The information was never lost. It was never given
a field.

### 6.4 The `actor` gap, and the minimal forward-only fix

Confirmed by the Orchestrator and worse than round 1 stated it. `d372a54` (08:35, PMID 24871327) and
`ab816cd` (11:06, PMID 34747138) are **A's**, and `lettore`'s first divergent commit is `d8e21a1` at
**13:16** — so both predate the branch's existence. For those hours **not even the topology substitutes
for attribution**, and the only record is the Orchestrator's transcript, outside the repository.

**Minimal fix, and its whole point is that no human types it.** At `append_receipt`, *derive* `actor`
from the worktree/branch the write is happening in, and cache it in the receipt — the same discipline
CLAUDE.md already requires for facts about external databases, and the same reason: at five hundred
manifests a hand-declared field is a field nobody checks. Forward-only; the chain is hashed and
rewriting it is the gesture the chain exists to prevent.

Three constraints the fix must satisfy:

1. **A value that means *unattributable*, and it must be the value for everything before 13:16 today.**
   Not a plausible reconstruction — the only source for that stretch is somebody's memory, and a
   plausible attribution recorded as fact is worse than a blank.
2. **Derived, not typed.** If an actor can type it, an actor will type it wrong, and the cost of
   updating it will fall below the cost of complying with it.
3. **Say out loud that branch ≠ person.** Today one branch was one actor. The day two actors share a
   worktree the derivation is silently wrong, so the field records *what it derived from*, not only its
   value. This is the same shape as the `surface` defect: do not put two facts in one field.

*Trade-off:* a branch-derived actor is a proxy and will not satisfy the DisMech export, whose curator
wants a person or a group, not a worktree name. It closes the internal question — *which actor produced
this reading* — and leaves the external one open. That is worth stating now rather than discovering it
at export.

### 6.6 The proposal

**Split `surface` into two fields, and do it in the same change as the enum values already queued.**

`provenance` ∈ {`body`, `supplement`, `adjudicated_page`} — *where the material came from*.
`readout` ∈ {`characters`, `pixels`} — *how it was read*, and the only one validation consults when it
decides whether an exact match is required.

`surface` becomes derivable from the pair for every existing value, so legacy manifests map without a
rewrite: `body`→(body, characters), `figure`→(body|supplement **unknown**, pixels), `table`→(body,
characters), `supplement`→(supplement, characters), `abstract`→(body, characters). The one honest
migration is that today's 192 `figure` locators get `provenance: unknown` — which is **true**, and is the
first time the state says so.

*Falsifiable success criterion:* re-run §6.1 after the change. The interval [7 … 29 … 103] must collapse
to a single number, and that number must be ≥ 29, because the readers' prose already establishes 29 as a
floor once the fact has a field. If it comes back below 29, the migration dropped evidence and must be
reverted.

*Trade-off, stated:* every reader pays a second field on every locator, and 192 existing locators carry
`provenance: unknown` until someone touches them — a visible debt where there is currently a silent one.
And it is a contract change, so by my own first proposal it **must land at a round boundary, not during
one**. Those two proposals constrain each other, and that is the correct order: freeze first, then change.

**Why this and not the four enum values.** The enum work (`panel_qualifies_text`, `schematic`, the
Gemini-drawn-figure case) repairs the vocabulary for *relations between* surfaces. This repairs the
identification *of* a surface, which is what makes any of those relations retrievable. Ship them
together; if only one ships, ship this.
