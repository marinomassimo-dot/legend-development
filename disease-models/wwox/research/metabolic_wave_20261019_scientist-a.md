## End-of-lot synthesis (selection questions 1-5)

Fifteen papers read in full (13 `complete_fulltext_read`, 2 `partial_fulltext_read`; the partial two owe only a 44 MB supplementary video with no decoder and patient photographs and four videos deliberately not opened). Only two of the fifteen (Liu S 2024; Richards 2015) have WWOX as a subject; four human or mouse genetics papers carry WWOX as a locus; nine are non-WWOX lipid comparators with WWOX text-absent (one has WWOX protein as a single unchanged proteome row). The product of the lot is the negative: no paper measures a lipid, sterol, oxysterol, sphingolipid or fatty-acid readout in a Wwox-null, P47T or WWOX-deficient tissue.

1. **Does Wwox-null (or P47T) brain show altered very-long-chain sphingolipid or cholesterol content, and could it drive the recorded hypomyelination?** Not answered by any paper; no such measurement exists in the lot or in the repository's registry. What the lot supplies is the design: chain-length-resolved lipidomics (C16-C18 against C22-C26 species of ceramide, hexosylceramide, sulfatide, sphingomyelin), purified myelin versus whole tissue, sterol profile (cholesterol, desmosterol, lathosterol, 7-dehydrocholesterol, lanosterol), and a cell-of-origin split. The comparators set expectations: a very-long-species deficit in oligodendrocytes alone produces hypomyelination (CerS2, Sun 2026 and Teo 2023), but CLAIM 003 records the WWOX myelin defect as non-cell-autonomous after neuronal deletion and CLAIM 005's boundary records astrocyte and oligodendrocyte Wwox deletions as negative, so a CerS2-like oligodendrocyte lipid lesion is not the recorded WWOX mechanism. Cholesterol was unchanged where measured (Teo 2023 myelin; Eberhart 2026 brain), consistent with the Peeples and Kondo premise of tightly held brain cholesterol. The lipid arm of RL-003 cannot be closed on this lot (no negative measurement exists); it remains unmeasured, with a prescribed assay.
2. **Is the HDL-C association carried by heterozygous loss; does any biallelic loss-of-function lipid phenotype exist?** No. All three genetics papers (Saez 2010, Leduc 2011, Liu 2022) are common-variant or strain-QTL data; the lead 2008 HDL variant did not replicate in Saez 2010; the Leduc mouse case rests on one non-conserved missense in a 184-gene interval with no expression difference; the Liu 2022 lipid-by-genotype results are implausible for common SNPs and its deposited file does not reproduce the joint analyses. Peripheral lipid genetics also cannot speak to brain sterol (brain synthesis is local). Lipid biomarkers do not belong in a WOREE panel on this evidence; the human lipid anchor stays at ESPANSIONE.
3. **Does WWOX loss alter oleate/MUFA pools, and does that matter outside tumour macrophages?** Oleate rises about 1.3-fold in conditioned medium of WWOX-low HCC cells (Liu S 2024), nothing outside tumour cells, the macrophage-active dose is not stated, and the SCD5 arm cannot be tested in a mouse (no rodent orthologue in the database used). DL-MECH-015's source note is corrected (the claimed prior reading has no receipt).
4. **Is the cholesterol-turnover route (CH24H/soticlestat) reachable in a WWOX-LF model?** Open for lack of a measurement (Kondo 2025, sponsor-authored, model-dependent efficacy, mechanism undetermined by its own statement). The assay is a sterol and oxysterol profile plus CH24H expression in Wwox-null and P47T brain. A caution rather than a lead: statins are a named sterol-pathway inhibitor in a developing-brain context (Peeples 2026), and DL-THER-080 (simvastatin seed) carries no such caution; a line is proposed.
5. **Is the SDR domain a lipid-elongation or steroid enzyme; does an SDR-dead hypomyelination differ from a null in lipid terms?** No support (Richards 2015: SDR substrate unknown in vivo, steroids the only named class; no lipid phenotype for an SDR-lacking allele). The NME2-binding span mapped by Liu S 2024 covers the SDR region, so SDR-domain function in the lipid arm may be binding rather than catalysis; IPOTESI.

Cross-paper facts worth a future reader's time:
- Chain-length shift (very-long to C16-C18) is the common lesion across CerS2, ELOVL1 and Pex2 papers; net amount of HexCer rises in the CerS2 papers but total GalCer falls in the ELOVL1 mouse. The fly paper (Ziegler 2025) adds that C16 species and long-chain bases are each harmful and separable only by rescue.
- Seizures: the ELOVL1 mouse has two startle-epilepsy deaths of 22; the human recessive ELOVL1 series records none; the ELOVL family review's conclusion states human ELOVL1 seizures against its own Table 1 (do-not-cite note proposed). No lipid-lesion comparator in this lot reproduces a WOREE-type epilepsy.
- Glial-inflammation link: Sun 2026 (GFAP up 3.2-fold) and Teo 2023 (an expanded microglial subset) find glial responses to a very-long-species deficit; the Sahu 2026 review proposes S1P from excess VLCFA as the inflammatory trigger; opposite lesions, both said to inflame. A pointer line for DL-MECH-012 is proposed as an IPOTESI.
- One WWOX datum from the comparator lot: WWOX protein unchanged in Pex2-null spinal cord, brainstem and cerebellum (log2 fold change +0.14, -0.06, -0.26).
