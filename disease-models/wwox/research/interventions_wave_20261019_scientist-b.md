## 2 · What the lot, read as a set, says (INTERNAL_EDGES the Orchestrator did not declare)

1. **The class label "adrenergic" has no direction.** A beta2 agonist infused into rat piriform cortex cuts bicuculline seizures (Biagioni); alpha2 agonists lengthen spike-wave discharges in absence rats, and very high doses reverse it (Sitnikova); a three-drug blockade lengthens each acute kainic acid seizure yet lowers mortality and later seizure counts in adult mice (Sun); alpha blockade after hypoxia-ischaemia in preterm fetal sheep leaves seizures unchanged but lowers lactate and glucose, while its harm to injury is cited from earlier work (Dhillon); the Alzheimer review records protective and harmful evidence for alpha1, alpha2, beta1 and beta2 (Li). Propranolol in Sun blocks the receptor Biagioni stimulates; atipamezole in Sun blocks the receptors Sitnikova shows are pro-absence when stimulated. No two systems agree and none is WWOX.
2. **A short-term surrogate moves without the outcome that matters.** Return of circulation after epinephrine (OR about 3.4) without a neurological gain (Williams); restored pressure with respiration 29 to 40 percent low and lactate 4.8 against 0.9 mmol/L (Oulehri); a paediatric protocol whose primary outcome is time to return of circulation among those who achieve it (Idrees); mortality lower yet each seizure longer (Sun).
3. **Source defects cluster in the strongest-sounding sentences.** The beta2 abstract says seizures are fully prevented (3 of 8 still seized); Sun's Discussion says the blockade shortened seizures (Table 1 says 115 to 204 s, longer); the fetal-sheep abstract reports 90 percent sensitivity and 80 percent specificity where its Figure 10 counts give 80 and 90; a unit slip makes seizure burden "172 seizures per hour"; the cardiac-arrest review calls an observational study placebo-controlled; the SIG report's HPA mechanism rests on a review and a preprint and one of its citations points to a potassium channel paper; the hypertension paper cites reference 23 as Japanese where its own Table 1 says 34.
4. **Counter-regulation is a blueprint without a datum.** Holzen gives the panel (epinephrine, glucagon, growth hormone, cortisol; epinephrine blunted after antecedent hypoglycaemia in diabetic adults) and Conlon shows a ketogenesis-defect toddler normoglycaemic in a febrile crisis with none of it measured. A glucose value alone cannot separate energy failure from counter-regulatory failure in the Wwox-null mouse.
5. **The only noradrenaline-pathway number in the lot is one nobody discussed.** In Sugiyama Table 2, CSF MHPG is 27.7 (below 35 to 65) in one adult and 40.7 (inside) in the other; the text treats only HVA and 5-HIAA.
6. **One WWOX-direct thread, small and indirect.** A gene-level hypertension scan flags WWOX with lower expression in cases and a mouse-knockout annotation that lists hypoglycaemia and abnormal adrenal and pituitary morphology (a database lookup, not a measurement); no SNP reaches significance and it replicates only within Han Chinese.
