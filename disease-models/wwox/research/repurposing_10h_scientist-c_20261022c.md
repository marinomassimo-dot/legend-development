## 2 · First-pass observations (each paper ends with its negative)

**39148107 (sirolimus pooled safety; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** Two Chinese prospective cohorts, TSC and LAM, mTOR-inhibitor-naive, sirolimus 1 mg per square metre daily titrated to a trough of 5 to 10 ng/ml (maximum 2 mg); 1738 patients (1521 children, median age 2.0 years, IQR 0.8 to 5.6; 217 adult women), median follow-up 25 months, 380 followed over 4 years. Stomatitis 920 of 1738 (52.9 percent), upper respiratory infections 6.6 percent, two grade 3 or higher events, one death of unknown cause at two years, seven discontinuations for adverse effects (six recurrent infections). Growth stayed within normal curves; in the paediatric group dose was the only factor associated with Z-scores above or below 2 SD (ORs of 7 to 30 for higher), which the authors read as a dose-dependent effect and, in the Discussion, as a possible dose-response reversal above a threshold. **Checks across surfaces:** the Results print 1783 patients but Fig 1 and Table 3 give 1823 screened, 85 ineligible, 1738 in the safety set (1521 plus 217 is 1738; 1783 is a transposition). Printed menstrual irregularity is "112 of 217 (48.5 percent)" but 112/217 is 51.6 percent (the table also prints 112 with 48.5). The text cites Fig 2 for the transient rise in RBC, WBC, HGB and PLT, but Fig 2 is the height, weight and BMI percentile figure and its row labels "Weight" and "Height" are swapped relative to the axis scales (the "Weight" panels run 40 to 180, i.e. cm); the blood-count curves are Fig 3. In Fig 3 the children's RBC and HGB curves settle above their starting values rather than "returning to baseline" (read by eye, my inference). The odds ratios use "dosage" with no printed unit. **Negative:** no control group (the authors say so); a pooled safety set, so the infants (5 days to 2 years) are not separated in the printed results; Table S2, Figure S1 not read; growth conclusions rest on regression with an unstated dose unit.

**33405376 (palbociclib, paediatric brain tumours; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** Ages 4 to 21; the Discussion says children under 4 were excluded and no PK exists for them. MTD 75 mg per square metre in both strata (Stratum I 2 of 12 with a DLT; Stratum II 1 of 7); at 95, 2 of 4 evaluable had grade 4 neutropenia. Higher steady-state Cmax and AUC0-24h tracked grade 3 or 4 neutropenia (OR 7.49, 95 percent CI 1.50 to 37.46, and 1.16, 1.04 to 1.30). **Dose standard (CLAIM 011):** the authors say Cmax and AUC rose "in proportional manner" from 50 to 95 mg per square metre; from Table 6, Day 1 AUC means 1327, 1872, 2375 give ratios 1.41 (for a 1.5-fold dose) and 1.27 (for 1.27), Day 21 AUC means 1517, 2219, 2294 give 1.46 and 1.03 (for 1.27) with N = 6, 17 and 2, so the 95 level on Day 21 rests on two patients and the statement holds on Day 1 and not on Day 21 (my recomputation). The text says repeated-dose PK in 27 patients (6, 18, 3) while Table 6 shows 6, 17, 2 (25). **Other surfaces:** the Stratum II adverse-event table lists "Seizure" in 3 patients (grade 3 by column position); the text never mentions it, and these are children with brain tumours, so attribution is not possible. **Negative:** no figure or supplement was lawfully available; no infant; PK from 34 patients, large variability; myelosuppression is the safety signal, not a neural one.

**33547201 (ribociclib plus everolimus, recurrent tumours, with a tumour/CSF cohort; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** Ages 3.9 to 20.4. Dose level 2 (ribociclib 170, everolimus 1.2) gave DLTs in 2 of 3 (grade 3 hypertension in a patient also on dexamethasone; grade 3 ALT elevation); RP2D 120 and 1.2. Ribociclib in resected tissue 19 to 23 hours after the last 350 mg per square metre dose: tumour-to-plasma ratio mean 19.8 (2.22 to 53.4). **Recomputed from the printed table (my inference):** ratios 5.26, 14.6, 2.22, 53.4, 23.3 reproduce the mean 19.8 and the median is 14.6; CSF-to-plasma ratios for the four CSF samples are about 0.07, 0.14, 0.12 and 0.43 (mean about 0.19). The Abstract says the DLT was a grade 4 ALT; the Results and Table 2 say grade 3. Everolimus exposure with ribociclib was about 2.5-fold (this trial) against 1.9 ± 0.9-fold in 35611273. **Negative:** concentrations are total, not unbound; tissue is recurrent tumour with a disrupted barrier (one sample is spinal cord, 2.5 mg), five patients; Ki-67 "5 percent against 40 percent" compares recurrent with archival tissue, not paired pre and post treatment; a CDK4/6 inhibitor, not CDK7/9.

**35611273 (ribociclib plus everolimus after radiotherapy; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** Enrolled ages 2 to 15, median 6.5 (the Abstract prints median 8 and range 2 to 18); RP2D ribociclib 170 and everolimus 1.5 mg per square metre, MTD not reached; one DLT (grade 3 lung infection) in 6; grade 3 or 4 neutropenia 33 percent, leucopenia 17, lymphopenia 11; one patient left with grade 4 cardiac toxicity (conduction disorder, prolonged QTc, ventricular tachycardia) and recovered; no thrombocytopenia of grade 3 or 4. The authors' mucositis conclusion has no control arm. Combination RP2Ds are 49 percent (ribociclib 170 of 350) and 30 percent (everolimus 1.5 of 5) of the single-agent RP2Ds quoted (my arithmetic). The attributable-event table lists headache (6 patients) and one intracranial haemorrhage; it lists only attributable events, so absence of seizure from it is not evidence of no seizures. **Negative:** 19 patients, a tumour population on dexamethasone mouthwash and anticonvulsants, Fig S1 unread.

**39771507 (palbociclib population PK/PD; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** Same trial as 33405376 (an internal edge, not an independent cohort); ages 4.9 to 21.6; clearance fell with AST (48.2 to 24.1 L/h/m2 from AST 10 to 100). The Discussion says "a 2.5-fold increase in AST gave a 2-fold lower clearance", which does not match the Results (a 10-fold AST range gave a 2-fold clearance change). Simulated grade 3 or higher neutropenia 19 percent at 50, 49 at 75 and 69 at 95 mg per square metre; 75 against 35 percent in the heavily and lightly pretreated strata. Weighting those stratum figures by the trial's strata (21 and 14) gives about 51 percent against the printed 49 (my arithmetic, a small gap). **Negative:** model-based, no external validation (the authors say so), six patients behind the "not cumulative" statement.

**38042867 (everolimus, TSC, real-world; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** 64 patients, 0.9 to 54 years; 95 percent had an adverse effect, 36 percent grade 3 or 4 (35 in the Discussion), stomatitis 63 percent, pneumonia the commonest grade 3 or 4 (13 percent); patients with grade 3 or 4 effects were younger (median 12 against 23 years); half needed dose modification; 34 percent had a hospitalisation; one cerebral oedema led to discontinuation; one patient developed immunodeficiency with the authors' attribution "probable". Five patients started before age 2: seizure reduction of at least 50 percent in 2, at least 30 percent in 1, no change in 1, an increase in 1. **Two printed inconsistencies:** the Results print a median treatment duration of 15 months (range 0 to 28, mean 13, SD 7.9, 95 percent CI 6.9 to 19.1) while Table 1 prints median follow-up 31 (0 to 106) months; and the CI 6.9 to 19.1 cannot belong to a mean of 13 with SD 7.9 in 64 patients (about 11.1 to 14.9 by my arithmetic). **Negative:** most patients also changed other anti-seizure drugs or had surgery (the authors say so), a mean treatment time of about a year, Additional file 1 unread.

**39409184 (Foxg1 c.946del mouse; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** Heterozygous mice are microcephalic (cortex and callosum about 15 percent smaller), more sensitive to PTZ, and show spontaneous spike trains and altered theta-gamma coupling on hippocampal telemetric EEG (n = 6 and 5); cortical RNA-seq flags a myelin cluster, only Plp1 protein is corroborated, and O4 and Olig2 labelling rise while CNPase does not. A single intracerebroventricular Plp1 antisense oligonucleotide at P0 (30 micrograms) lowered Plp1 and kept grip strength from differing from wild type at 3 months. **Other surfaces looked at:** Fig 3 legend gives n = 11 wild type and 8 mutant, but the text's 63.6 and 12.5 percent can only be 7 of 11 and 1 of 8, i.e. they fit wild type and mutant the other way round; the same legend says seizure severity was comparable (Fig 3G ns, means about 3.2 against 4.1) while the Fig 3E latency curve shows mutants reaching higher stages faster, so one of text and legend has the n labels or the arms swapped (not resolvable from the paper). Fig 7B (n = 3 per group) shows Plp1 in treated mutants at about 0.33, below the wild-type control about 0.68, so "restored to wild-type levels" is an over-suppression in the figure; Fig 7C compares treated mutants with wild type only ("ns", n = 6 against 10), not with untreated mutants, so "no longer different" is an absence of significance. **EEG and myelin are never re-measured after the ASO: it is not an EEG-plus-myelin pairing after an intervention.** **Negative:** no seizure, EEG or myelin-histology readout after the ASO; motor tests not repeated.

**41144763 (Olig2-Slc35a2 knockout; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** No drug. Six of eight knockouts had spontaneous seizures on 20 to 24 hours of video-EEG against none of eight controls; 45 mg/kg PTZ killed 8 of 10 knockouts (Fig 3A bar chart) against 1 of 11 controls; CNPase area coverage is lower in corpus callosum, striatum, anterior commissure and lateral septum (n = 3 per group and timepoint; area coverage by thresholding, no EM or g-ratio), with fewer Olig2-positive cells in callosum and gray-white junction. **Printed inconsistency:** the text prints F(1, 17) = 13 with p = .0022, then p < .0001, then p = .0003 for three different comparisons; F(1, 17) = 13 corresponds to p of about 0.0022 (my numerical integration), so the other two cannot share that F. Slc35a2 protein falls to about 0.36 of control in males and not significantly in females. **Negative:** hypomyelination in an oligodendrocyte-specific, constitutive knockout shows that myelin deficiency can come with seizures, not that myelin loss causes the seizures in a neuronal-gene DEE; the knockout is X-linked and sex-mosaic; sample sizes 5 to 8; no treatment arm.

**35589394 (neonatal hyperoxia and GSK3 beta; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** The GSK3 inhibitor SB216763 (15 mg/kg subcutaneously, P6 to P8, a dose the paper used) given before 48 hours of 80 to 85 percent oxygen at P6 prevented the loss of Sox2 progenitors, interneurons and inhibitory currents at P60; deletion of Gsk3b in Gad2 cells (tamoxifen at P4) restored memory, deletion in POMC cells did not. This is Wnt-raising pharmacology, the opposite direction from Wnt suppression. **Timing:** the inhibitor was given only before the insult; there is no later-start arm, so the paper tests a protective timing, not a window. **No SB-alone group** appears in the text or in the Fig 8 and Fig 9 legends or bars (control, hyperoxia, hyperoxia plus SB), so tolerance of the drug in a normal neonate is not tested. In Fig 9J the cumulative curve for EPSC inter-event interval in treated mice lies well beyond control (fewer EPSCs than control), while the text says SB "reversed" the rise; that is an overshoot in the panel (read by eye, my inference). **Negative:** no seizure, EEG or excitability measure beyond patch clamp; n = 4 per group in Fig 8; mouse P6 is not a human age.

**34358226 (Wnt and epileptogenesis; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** In adult (7-week) mice after pilocarpine status epilepticus, inhibitory GSK3 phosphorylation and beta-catenin rise slightly by day 3, peak at day 5 and return to control by day 7, with a Warburg-like shift and mTOR activation. A porcupine inhibitor and a tankyrase inhibitor are reported to have "failed to affect basal or induced hippocampal Wnt signalling" (no data, no dose; the stated likely reason, no barrier penetration, is not measured). Germline loss of the Wnt repressor HBP1 gave spontaneous seizures (observed by the animal facility, no count), no survival beyond an hour at 220 mg/kg pilocarpine, no survival beyond a day at 150, and, at 135, the most grade 5 seizures with heterozygotes between (gene-dosage pattern). **Other surfaces:** Fig 7J survival is n = 5 per group and the legend says unpaired t-test while the panel prints P = 0.0054; the text says beta-catenin is "additionally" higher in knockouts than heterozygotes, and in Fig 7B the star is on the heterozygote bar and the knockout bar carries a large error bar (read by eye). **This is the opposite direction to the adult kainate paper read in R3 (Wnt activation reduced remodelling) and it shows seizure sensitisation by constitutive Wnt activation: direction matters, and the HBP1 result is a germline, lifelong activation, not a timed one.** **Negative:** no Wnt suppression experiment succeeded; the "therapeutic window" is a time-limited rise in a marker (day 5) in adults, proposed by the authors and untested (their own last paragraph).

**28919853 (CDK5 and PV cells; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** Adult male rats; osmotic pumps into the lateral ventricle (roscovitine 100 micromolar, apocynin 150, Mdivi-1 50) lowered mitochondrial fission and PV-cell loss after lithium-pilocarpine status epilepticus (n = 7); CDK5-Y15 phosphorylation was 81.7 percent of PV cells at 6 hours against 19.6 in controls. The authors state without data that these concentrations did not change seizure latency or severity. **Negative:** ventricular infusion bypasses the barrier; no EEG after the drug; PV counts are the neural readout; CDK5 is not the CDK7/9 class; same group and method as 31331032 (previous relay), so not independent.

## 3 · Answers to the three open parts, at the level the papers allow

**Q-a, a measured window of tolerated or harmful Wnt suppression (still none; two informative papers).** No paper read measures Wnt suppression in a developing brain with a seizure or EEG endpoint. 34358226 times a transient rise of Wnt signalling at day 5 after status epilepticus in adults and shows constitutive activation sensitises to seizures; its attempts at pharmacological suppression "failed" without data. 35589394 raises Wnt signalling in neonates by GSK3 inhibition before an injury and protects interneurons, with no SB-alone arm and no later start. The nearest candidate, a Wnt/beta-catenin inhibitor in young rats with a seizure-susceptibility test (38070690), is parked behind a closed-access wall. A zero from a title/abstract query licenses only "no paper is indexed"; my query for neonatal or postnatal Wnt with seizure, EEG or epilepsy plus inhibitor or knockout returned 12 records and for critical period or window 4, none of them a suppression-window series. The measurement owed is unchanged: a dose-by-timing series of Wnt/CK1 modulation in developing cortex with progenitor, myelin and electrographic endpoints.

**Q-b, EEG paired with myelin after a drug (no second paper found).** The one found remains 35501379, and it is the only one found in the files searched (the blind audit searched 832 full-text XML files on disk for EEG together with g-ratio; I added title/abstract queries and eleven readings). New on the other side of the question: 41144763 pairs video-EEG with myelin in a genetic model, without a drug, and shows hypomyelination from an oligodendrocyte-specific knockout with spontaneous seizures; 39409184 pairs EEG and an oligodendrocyte phenotype in a DEE-gene mouse and gives an antisense oligonucleotide, but re-measures neither. Together: more myelin followed seizures in absence-epilepsy models (35501379) and less myelin came with seizures in a conditional knockout (41144763); a card for a pro-myelination drug cannot assume the direction in a hyperexcitable network.

**Q-c, paediatric and infant record (literature observations, not recommendations).**
- *mTOR class:* sirolimus, 1521 children with median age 2.0 years (5 days to 69 years overall), median follow-up 25 months: stomatitis in over half, infections the usual cause of discontinuation, no growth deviation, no control group; everolimus in TSC across 0.9 to 54 years: nearly all had an adverse effect, a third grade 3 or 4, younger patients had more grade 3 or 4 events, five treated before age 2 (one with an increase in seizures).
- *CDK class:* palbociclib and ribociclib in children are myelosuppressive (neutropenia dose- and exposure-related), with a grade 4 cardiac event and QTc prolongation on ribociclib plus everolimus, grade 3 hypertension and ALT elevation at the upper dose; **no infant exposure**: the youngest enrolled were 3.9 years (33547201), 4 (33405376, eligibility floor) and 2 (35611273 combination); no CDK7/9-class drug has a paediatric record in anything read.
- *Brain exposure of a CDK-class drug in children:* the only measure is ribociclib in resected tumour and CSF of five patients (total concentrations, ratio of tumour to plasma 2.2 to 53; CSF about 7 to 43 percent of plasma by my arithmetic).
- *Monitoring that the records themselves imply* (as what was monitored): weekly blood counts, QTc/ECG, echocardiography, lipids, liver enzymes, mouth care; a statement of what these trials did, not a monitoring plan.
