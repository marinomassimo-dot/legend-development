## 3. What each paper measures, supports, and does not support

### Tier 1

**Wainberg 2023 (PMID 37328865).** Summary-statistics pleiotropy analysis of three published GWAS (ADRD 487,511 participants with public statistics, PD 1,474,097, ALS 138,086 European). It asks whether each genome-wide-significant lead variant for one disorder is also significant in another after Bonferroni correction for the number of leads tested. **Measures:** the ADRD lead variant `rs450674` lies about 11 kb downstream of **MAF** (not within or beside WWOX) and is associated with ADRD (OR 1.04, p 3.2e-8) and with PD (OR 1.04, p 2.1e-4), the PD value being only just under the Bonferroni threshold (3.1e-4); colocalisation gives 80.5% for a shared causal variant (80.4% in the Discussion text; the discrepancy is stated, not resolved). **Does not support:** a WWOX effect. The authors assign WWOX only because "besides MAF, the only other coding gene within 500 kilobases is the oxidoreductase WWOX", then argue relevance from three papers (Tau phosphorylation on WWOX knockdown, a knockout-mouse tau aggregation statement, and the MPP+ toxin study), conceding that a knockout "may not be a realistic model" for a common-variant effect and that "causal gene candidates should not be treated as definitive". No eQTL or expression colocalisation was run, no dopaminergic neuron, dopamine or catecholamine measurement is made. The abstract and Discussion say "near ... WWOX" while Table 2 names MAF as the nearest gene. **Changes in the model:** nothing in the DEE model; it adds a population-genetics sentence for the heterozygous/common-variant end, at hypothesis strength.

**Sathe 2025 (PMID 39570186).** Single-centre retrospective cohort of infantile epileptic spasms (108 vigabatrin-treated children, 17 with VABAM, 11 symptomatic). **Measures:** all 11 symptomatic cases had simultaneous hormonal therapy (ACTH or prednisolone) against 40 of 91 children without VABAM (Fisher p = 0.001); peak dose was not associated with any VABAM class; asymptomatic cases showed no hormonal difference in an underpowered comparison (4 of 6 vs 17 of 39). **Does not support:** causation. The authors say so, name disease severity as a rival explanation (more refractory children get both more drug and combination therapy), note that the radiologist was not blinded to exposure, and that some cases were scanned because VABAM was suspected. No WWOX patient. **Changes:** it puts a co-factor on the table for `CLAIM 001`, but see section 4 for why that cannot yet be tested against the WWOX reports.

**Ng 2023 (PMID 37443770).** Narrative review of dopamine transporter deficiency syndrome by the group that characterised it; no new data (stated); authors hold a gene-therapy patent. **Measures (as reported):** classical DTDS gives raised CSF HVA with normal 5-HIAA and an HVA:5-HIAA ratio of 5.0-13.0 (normal 1.0-4.0), MRI normal or with mild delayed myelination, absent or reduced DaTscan uptake, limited or no levodopa response, mean age of death 10.4 years. **Does not support:** anything about WWOX. Seizures are not among the features quoted for classical DTDS. **Changes:** a reference signature for the discriminator panel in section 5.

### Tier 2

**Ferraro 2022 (PMID 35053314).** Meta-analysis of nine public bulk microarray datasets of substantia nigra (70 control, 88 PD) with computational cell-type deconvolution. Correcting for neuron and oligodendrocyte proportions cut differentially expressed genes from 1050 to 93, and **none of the PD-causing or GWAS-proximal genes was among them**. WWOX enters only as one of a list of "central proteins" in a protein-interaction network built from the partners of the 93 genes, cited in one sentence to two references; no WWOX expression value is reported, and the queue entry `FT-167` (rated method-only transfer) does not mention WWOX. **Does not support:** WWOX deregulation in PD nigra. Network centrality in a database-built interactome is a property of how many experimentally reported interactors a protein has, not of its expression; WWOX is a WW-domain protein with many reported partners (my inference, not tested here). **Changes:** nothing; it corrects the selection file's description ("WWOX among genes retained as neurodegeneration players") to "WWOX named as a network-central protein; not differentially expressed".

**Al Sari 2025 (PMID 40291937).** Narrative review (keyword search of four databases to January 2024, no appraisal) of DTDS and PKDYS2. It repeats the DTDS CSF signature (ratio above 4) and states PKDYS2 has **normal** CSF HVA and 5-HIAA, EEG normal in both, and that oculogyric crises are sometimes misdiagnosed as epilepsy. It is internally inconsistent: the text says DTDS patients "are not treated with anticholinergics and benzodiazepines" while its Table 2 lists anticholinergic and GABAergic agents against DTDS. It also disagrees with Ng 2023 and Naranjo-Lobo 2026 on the CSF in vesicular-transport disease (see section 5).

**Naranjo-Lobo 2026 (PMID 42483444).** Targeted narrative review of pediatric parkinsonism (PubMed, 10 years to December 2025; AI language-editing disclosed). Age-stratified tables of CSF/imaging/treatment response; epilepsy is listed among common associations. WWOX is not mentioned. Useful for the work-up order: CSF monoamines, pterins and urine studies first in infants.

**Sugiyama 2025 (PMID 40183510).** Two adult women with Dravet syndrome (SCN1A; one variant of uncertain significance), parkinsonism around 30 years, DAT SPECT normal, **CSF HVA 36.6 and 79.3 and 5-HIAA 20.4 and 52.2 nmol/L against cited intervals of 145-324 and 67-140**. n = 2, adults, polypharmacy, **no CSF from Dravet patients without parkinsonism**, and the authors themselves say CSF monoamine metabolites "can be reduced secondarily because of epilepsy, convulsions, and other factors". **Supports:** that a low CSF panel was seen in two adults with a DEE-associated parkinsonism. **Does not support:** a causal reduction of dopamine synthesis (the authors' hypothesis), nor a template that transfers to a neonate.

**Bian 2024 (PMID 38330420).** Narrative review without methods of CRH, ACTH and glucocorticoids in epilepsy; machine-translation help acknowledged. Background only: ACTH acts on the adrenal cortex (cholesterol to pregnenolone to cortisol), the HPA axis is described as "largely under GABAergic control", and seizures and the HPA axis are described as circular. No WWOX.

**Abdygalyk 2025 (PMID 41470225).** Structured narrative review of infantile spasms (236 references). WWOX appears in **one prose sentence** ("Autosomal recessive defects in WWOX") and **one table row** ("WWOX-related encephalopathy with spasms"), both resting on one cited case report (PMID 30361190, two children, complete read in this repository). It proposes that ACTH acts partly by raising neurosteroid synthesis, and reports that **ACTH non-responders show a low DHEA to androstenedione ratio**, while conceding that "direct measurements of neurosteroids or steroidogenic enzymes in human IS tissue are not provided". It describes the GABA-A developmental switch as possibly delayed but states "the supplied corpus does not provide direct NKCC1 measurements in IS". Phrases such as "supplied corpus" suggest the text was assembled from a restricted source set; I take its claims as the review's statements, not measurements.

**Bagyinszky 2026 (PMID 42782811).** Narrative review of ERBB4 in the HPA axis and neurodegeneration. It calls the proposed causal bridge between ERBB4 and HPA dysregulation "a promising but largely speculative working hypothesis", resting on constitutive knockout and acute-stress rodent models. Reported rodent facts that touch this group: ErbB4-deficient mice have regionally different dopamine (down in prefrontal cortex, hippocampus and nucleus accumbens, up in dorsal striatum), loss of ErbB4 raises noradrenergic firing and norepinephrine, and ERBB4 is expressed in the adrenal cortex. **WWOX is not mentioned**, so any WWOX connection runs only through the WWOX-ErbB4 partnership recorded elsewhere, which this paper does not test.

**Stevering 2025 (PMID 39641935).** Registry cohort of 80 vigabatrin-treated TSC infants, 23 untreated TSC infants and 29 non-TSC epilepsy controls, blinded MRI review, a pre-treatment scan for every treated child. **Measures:** VABAM in 35.5% of treated children; **VABAM-like abnormalities in 7 of 52 children never given vigabatrin (13.5%)**; vigabatrin independently associated with VABAM (RR 3.57), TSC and refractory epilepsy not; complete resolution in all 13 treated children with a later scan; no dose or hormonal-therapy association with the MRI finding (hormonal RR 0.94); symptoms not significantly associated with VABAM (RR 2.76, CI 0.68-8.77); **hormonal or steroid therapy associated with the symptoms (RR 9.00) in only eight exposed children**, without higher MRI severity. **Does not support:** a WWOX statement; the treated group is TSC, an mTORopathy, and no non-TSC vigabatrin-treated group exists.

**Alammary 2026 (PMID 41573381).** Three siblings, one with exome, biochemistry and imaging; the two deceased siblings confirmed homozygous for the same ABAT nonsense variant by targeted testing only. GABA values for the index infant were retrieved from stored profiles in mixed units (12 and 15 umol/L in plasma, later 74 nmol/mL serum, against a reference of <4 nmol/mL, with a markedly raised urinary value); CSF GABA failed three times and the MRS GABA peak was not possible. **Relevance:** GABA-transaminase deficiency is the genetic counterpart of vigabatrin's target. The day-48 MRI showed new symmetrical restricted diffusion in cortex, subcortical white matter, corpus callosum, internal capsule and corticospinal tracts. I note, as `IPOTESI` and not as a finding of the paper, that a restricted-diffusion white-matter pattern appears both in this genetic loss of GABA-T and in VABAM; the paper does not compare them and the two imaging sets were not read side by side. No WWOX.

**Dumitrescu 2026 (PMID 41977367).** Conceptual narrative review with no methods section, a "unifying" chloride-microdomain hypothesis, and the universal sentence "Epilepsy, regardless of type, occurs by the exact same mechanisms". It states that direct characterisation of chloride microdomains in human brain "remains technically challenging". A string search of the rendered text found no mention of bumetanide, NEMO or any trial. **It measures nothing that could settle whether GABA is depolarising in WWOX loss of function.** WWOX is not mentioned.

### Tier 3

**Nikkhah 2026 (PMID 42643745)** and **Almutair 2026 (PMID 42245405)** are small SLC18A2 (VMAT2) case reports: one boy with a canonical splice-acceptor variant (likely pathogenic on PVS1, PM2, PP4 with SpliceAI, no RNA study, CSF discussed but not done) and two siblings with a splice-donor variant (likely pathogenic on PVS1, PM2, PP1, in-silico only, CSF planned but not possible). Common pattern: normal MRI; **oculogyric crises first treated as seizures, EEG during events without epileptiform activity**; levodopa worsened or gave no benefit; pramipexole partial and variably tolerated; same homozygous variant, different severity and tolerability in the two siblings. Both rely on a statement that CSF HVA/5-HIAA is usually normal in this disorder. No WWOX.

**Shi 2020 (PMID 32546684).** Review of NRG1-ErbB4 signalling in cortical interneurons and a proposed gene-therapy model for adult schizophrenia. ErbB4 sits on PV+ and VIP+ interneurons and on dopamine-neuron axons; the NRG1 EGF-domain peptide is reported to raise activity-dependent GABA release and to suppress seizure activity. The proposed therapeutic direction is to **reduce** ErbB4 signalling in PV+ cells to reopen plasticity, which is the opposite of "more ErbB4 is better". No WWOX; no tested therapy.

**Li 2026 (PMID 41914335).** Review of serotonin in SUDEP with a stated database search. Most evidence is from audiogenic-seizure DBA mice; the table grades 5-HT2A and 5-HT4 evidence as conflicting with low reproducibility; human data are limited. Developmental or intellectual disability is listed as a SUDEP risk factor. No WWOX, no serotonin measurement in any WWOX system.

## 4. The five questions the selection posed

1. **Neurotransmitter-disorder mimic with a measurable CSF signature, or structural/hypomyelinating?** Not answerable from the literature in this group, and nothing in it is WWOX data. The group does supply what a panel would have to be read against: DTDS raised HVA with a raised HVA:5-HIAA ratio; dopamine-synthesis defects reduced HVA with or without reduced 5-HIAA and an abnormal pterin profile; **VMAT2 deficiency CSF described as normal by two sources (stated in Al Sari 2025, Nikkhah 2026 and Almutair 2026) but as "urinary metabolites elevated while free monoamines are low" by Naranjo-Lobo 2026**; and the one DEE-with-parkinsonism comparator (Sugiyama 2025) has low CSF values obtained in adults on antiseizure drugs without a Dravet-without-parkinsonism control. A normal CSF panel would therefore **not** exclude a vesicular storage disorder, and a low one in a child with seizures would **not** by itself prove a primary synthesis defect. The existing record of the WWOX neonatal parkinsonism-like case (`PAPER 145`) states CSF neurotransmitters were never measured and no dopaminergic drug was tried; nothing read here changes that.
2. **Dopaminergic vulnerability or general neurodegeneration signal at the WWOX locus?** The locus evidence is a common-variant association (OR 1.04) whose gene assignment is by proximity argument, and the substantia nigra expression meta-analysis does not show WWOX as differentially expressed. The data fit a general neurodegeneration signal at least as well as a dopaminergic one; neither paper contains a dopaminergic readout for WWOX.
3. **Does VABAM track with ACTH/steroid therapy, and is WWOX-null steroidogenesis relevant?** Two non-WWOX cohorts (both partial reads) do not disagree *(sentence corrected at `BATCH_20261019_003`, Mirror B1; the earlier wording said they disagree on the MRI finding itself, which the figures do not show: both tie hormonal therapy to symptoms and neither to the MRI finding alone)*: in infantile spasms, symptomatic VABAM was always accompanied by hormonal therapy (11 of 11) while the TSC cohort found no hormonal association with the MRI finding (RR 0.94) but a hormonal association with symptoms in eight exposed children. Neither isolates a mechanism. **For WWOX the test cannot be run from this repository:** the VABAM anchor of `CLAIM 001` (PMID 41442931, `PAPER 003`) has no receipt in the ledger and no lawful body (it is listed `EVIDENCE_BLOCKED`), so whether the two WWOX children received hormonal therapy is unknown here. The selection file's phrase "(Choi 2026, read)" is therefore wrong as written: the source is registered but unread. The repository's Wwox-null steroidogenesis paper (`PAPER 076`) is a gonadal/pituitary endocrine phenotype confounded by systemic illness; its dossier carries no adrenal, ACTH, cortisol or corticosterone term (checked by string search of the dossier, not by re-reading the source). **The adrenal axis of Wwox-null animals is, on this evidence, unmeasured.** Any link from WWOX to ACTH responsiveness is `IPOTESI` with no datum under it.
4. **Separating accounts of GABAergic vulnerability with chloride homeostasis and GABA-T biology.** Not possible from these sources. Dumitrescu 2026 measures nothing and Abdygalyk 2025 says no direct NKCC1 measurement exists in infantile spasms. Alammary 2026 shows what genetic GABA-T loss does (severe neonatal encephalopathy, raised GABA in blood and urine), not what WWOX loss does. The working-model line that depolarising GABA is **unmeasured** in a WWOX system is confirmed unchanged by this group.
5. **Is ErbB4 the missing bridge between interneuron loss and the stress/adrenal axis?** Bagyinszky 2026 calls the ErbB4-HPA bridge "largely speculative" even for ErbB4 itself, and does not mention WWOX. The WWOX-ErbB4 partnership is recorded elsewhere (a binding-biophysics paper and a blocked nuclear-translocation paper); a third source on it (Aqeilan 2007, breast cancer cohort, where Wwox expression was associated with membranous ErbB4) could not be obtained. A bridge from interneuron loss to the stress axis through WWOX-ErbB4 is therefore two unmeasured links in series.

## 7. Candidate leads (all `IPOTESI`; none is a claim)

- **L1. Retrieve the WWOX VABAM anchor and read its treatment histories for co-administered hormonal therapy.** Falsifier of "hormonal co-therapy is irrelevant to WWOX VABAM": both WWOX children exposed to hormonal therapy. Falsifier of "co-therapy is relevant": neither exposed. This is a reading, not an experiment, and it is blocked on acquisition.
- **L2. ACTH-responsiveness and neurosteroid readouts in Wwox-null mice** (adrenal and brain DHEA, androstenedione, pregnenolone). Premise chain: ACTH efficacy in infantile spasms is proposed to run partly through neurosteroidogenesis (Abdygalyk 2025, a review); a gonadal steroidogenesis defect exists in Wwox-null mice (`PAPER 076`), confounded by systemic illness. The adrenal and neurosteroid readouts are not in any source read. The cheapest first step is a literature check, not an animal experiment.
- **L3. A single CSF panel (HVA, 5-HIAA, ratio, pterins, 3-O-methyldopa) and a record of event EEG in a WWOX child with hypokinetic-rigid features**, read against the table in section 5 and logged with the drug and seizure state at sampling. The owed measurement already exists as an unmeasured item in `PAPER 145`; this adds the comparators and the confounder list.
- **L4 (method).** The two SLC18A2 case reports classify a canonical splice-site variant as likely pathogenic on a predicted loss of function plus absence from population databases, without an RNA study. For a WWOX canonical splice-acceptor allele the same evidential step exists; a patient or cell RNA readout would move it from predicted to observed. This is a methodological observation about evidence strength, not a statement about any individual.
