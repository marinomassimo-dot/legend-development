## 2. What the figures add (DATO, one line per paper, only what bears on the three leads)

- **40834072.** Published Figure 1B: after the low convulsant dose the knockout group has 3 of 10 reaching death against 0 of 11, and the number past the spike-wave stage is 5 of 11 wild type against 8 of 10 knockout (the preprint text says 4 of 11 and 6 of 10: preprint and journal figure differ). Supplementary S6: the blocker lengthened the latency to spike-wave in both genotypes (paired, baseline session against drug session, no repeated vehicle session shown). Reciprocal-interaction and grooming results are in the manifest. No interaction statistic is printed.
- **40827489.** The supplement gives the interaction terms the main text omits: ethosuximide reduced seizure number in both genotypes without interaction (F = 0.84, P = 0.44), shortened the average seizure duration with a significant interaction (F = 5.65, P = 0.0089; effect in the heterozygous rats, none in wild type), and reduced total duration in both (interaction F = 1.28, P = 0.29). Selection of wild-type animals for the drug experiments required baseline seizures (four seizure-free wild-type rats excluded), and in the sleep and seizure recordings nine wild-type and four heterozygous rats from one founder were excluded for high seizure rates. Reticular-nucleus infusion: four animals, no significant effect (P = 0.11); one animal's seizure number rose above its saline value.
- **32719346.** The wild-type and two other autism-model controls were compared before and after ethosuximide in the same animals with no vehicle-injected arm (the supplement confirms before-and-after paired points, n = 13 and 10 wild type). After ethosuximide the knockout thalamic neurons still show lower sIPSC amplitude and frequency than wild type and the rebound burst number falls in both genotypes: the drug acts on the burst in both genotypes, so a burst-lowering effect is not knockout-specific in that slice assay.
- **35243344.** In situ hybridisation shows no visible difference in Cav3.1, 3.2 and 3.3 transcripts between model and control; the quantitative PCR compares PV-ArchT mice on normal chow against PV-ArchT mice on doxycycline chow (the same genotype with the transgene repressed), three animals each, Cav3.1 about 0.72 and 0.82 of control in cortex and thalamus, no star; the T-type current in reticular neurons is lower (first-relay reading). Spike-wave discharge number rises with age from about 1 per hour at P60 to about 54 at P150 and is abolished by transgene repression at the neonatal stage.
- **24953239.** Rebound-burst threshold after a 1000 ms hyperpolarisation is lower for the epileptic strain (about minus 57 against minus 33 pA in adults); Ih tail density is larger (about minus 8.8 against minus 6.2 pA/pF at minus 110 mV in adults) and HCN1 and HCN3 protein are higher; with Ih blocked the burst thresholds converge (about plus 59 against plus 62 pA). The T-type current is smaller (first-relay reading), so the burst-firing difference in relay neurons follows Ih, not the T-type channel.
- **42536722.** Cav3.1 deletion in the digenic mouse removes the rebound spikes and the T-type current and removes the spike-wave discharges, and the social-novelty deficit remains (P = 0.042 against control for the triple mutant); ethosuximide suppresses the discharges (total duration per 30 minutes from about 100 s to near zero, 12 mice) and leaves social novelty lower than control (star). Activating parvalbumin cells with a designer receptor and low-dose clozapine abolishes the discharges and brings social novelty to control level (P = 0.56 after, 1.9e-4 before), and clozapine alone in receptor-negative mice does not.
- **37275776.** The heterozygous missense mouse has a lower electroconvulsive threshold in both sexes while the heterozygous deletion line does not differ from wild type; baclofen-evoked currents are larger in heterozygous hippocampal neurons and inhibitory cortical neurons; the oocyte data show that the Gbeta variant is a gain of function at low expression and a loss at high expression for GIRK1/2, so the sign of the variant's effect depends on dose of expression. This is the clearest example in the lot of a missense and a null allele of one gene behaving differently, which bears on any assumption that a Wwox hypomorph and a Wwox null have the same T-type or drug phenotype.

## 3. What changed for DL-MECH-170, 171 and 172

- **DL-MECH-172.** The supplements add two things the main texts hid: a significant genotype-by-treatment interaction exists for one endpoint (average seizure duration) in the Grin2b rat and not for the others, and in the Neuroligin 2 slice assay the drug acts on bursts in both genotypes. Together with the Cntnap2 and GluA1 designs this says the direction and the existence of an interaction depend on the endpoint within one experiment; a single pooled endpoint cannot carry the lead. The test fixed in the previous note (a vehicle arm per genotype, an interaction term, a sodium-channel comparator, an endpoint stratified by state or time) is unchanged and the Grin2b supplement is the only study in the lot that printed all three interaction terms.
- **DL-MECH-171.** Four models now have a T-type measurement against a seizure phenotype: the GAERS strain (smaller T-current in relay neurons, absence seizures), the PV-ArchT mouse (smaller T-current in reticular cells, absence seizures, unchanged transcripts), the Cntnap2 null (larger T-current in reticular cells, no spontaneous seizures) and the digenic mouse (T-current needed for the discharges). The direction of the T-current is not predicted by the seizure phenotype, and transcripts did not track current in the PV-ArchT model; this adds weight to the previous note's requirement that a Wwox test measure current in thalamic cell types and does not rest on mRNA.
- **DL-MECH-170.** No new Wwox data. The Gnb1 and Grin2b papers show a non-T-type target (GIRK channels) and a baseline floor in wild type as alternatives for an ethosuximide effect that appears only in a mutant.
