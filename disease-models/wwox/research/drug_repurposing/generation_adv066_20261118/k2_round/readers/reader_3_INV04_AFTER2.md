(1) First three actions: (a) run a pilot in Wwox-null and wild-type pups to fix the variability of the 60-minute summary, the assay age and a stimulus that lifts wild type off the floor; (b) state the assay age against the age windows of the panel agents; (c) set the interaction margin before any data are collected.

(2) Arms, controls, measurements: the same provoked-seizure design with all genotypes treated, a vehicle per genotype, and an interaction term. Wild-type pups at the assay age and at two or more older ages up to about P28. Reference agents with flat, rising and (if an NMDAR arm is included) falling age profiles as an assay-sensitivity panel, reported per age. Seizure type recorded per animal, plus a motor and ataxia control. An exposure check at each age. A selective T-type blocker beside ethosuximide, with an engagement control if possible. Optionally a Cav3 current or expression measurement, and video-EEG on a subset of knockouts.

(3) More promising: on provoked-seizure suppression in a Wwox-null system, lithium and ethosuximide are the only measured candidates, so they are more promising than every other one. That is a DATO from one experiment in one laboratory. Lithium looks like a general effect and ethosuximide is the only open selectivity question. On development, AAV9 gene addition is clearly more promising, with limits. On acute rescue, ketamine is more promising only by general paediatric evidence (transfer, abstract level). Nothing is more promising on rescue in a WWOX system, on spontaneous seizures or on harms. Withdrawn: the Kasahara 2019 mouse inference. Unresolved: the mouse age floor for ethosuximide, the test age in Cheng 2020, any T-type current in a WWOX system, and the contested T-type pharmacology of ethosuximide.

(4) Cannot be concluded: that ethosuximide is knockout-specific, because a difference in significance is not an interaction and no genotype-by-treatment test exists. That T-type block is involved. That lithium is better than ethosuximide, because the arms used different cohorts. That the effect size can power the study (about 180 to 320 animals per cell). That it works in spontaneous seizures or in children. That any agent is safe or recommended.

(5) The decision that needs someone else: the assay age and stimulus. The material says these are the laboratory's to choose, and the paper does not state the age.

(6) Competing explanations, using the interaction ratio (knockout effect ratio over wild-type effect ratio, log scale), with a valid panel and wild type above the floor:
- E1, knockout-specific: knockout effect ratio at most 0.7 with an interval excluding 1, wild type null (interval inside 0.85 to 1.15), and interaction ratio at most 0.7 or at least 1.43.
- E2, age-limited effect with a wild-type floor: wild type null at the assay age but an effect at an older age. No numeric margin is given.
- E3, general proportional effect: interaction ratio between 0.7 and 1.43.
- E4, T-type route: the selective blocker reproduces the ethosuximide pattern in the knockout. That makes the route plausible, not proven. No numeric margin is given.
- A failed panel is an assay failure, not a negative result.
