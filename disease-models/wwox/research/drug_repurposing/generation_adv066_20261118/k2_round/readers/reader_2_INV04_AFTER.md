(1) First actions. (a) Run a pilot in Wwox-null and wild-type pups. It fixes the variability of the 60-minute summary on the analysis scale, the assay age, and a stimulus that gives wild type a measurable response above the floor. (b) State the assay age against the age windows of the panel agents, and build the wild-type age series. (c) Fix the margins and the analysis before any data: effect ratios per genotype and the interaction ratio.

(2) Design. The provoked-seizure assay uses +/+, +/- and -/- pups with a vehicle per genotype in the same session. Wild-type pups are also tested at two or more older ages, up to about P28. An assay-sensitivity panel of reference agents with flat, rising and (if an NMDAR arm is included) falling age profiles is reported per age. A dose or exposure check is made at each age. Seizure type is recorded per animal, with a motor and ataxia control before the stimulus. A selective T-type blocker arm sits beside ethosuximide, age-matched and genotype-crossed, with an engagement control if possible. Cav3 current or expression is measured in thalamic and reticular neurons. Video-EEG goes on a knockout subset.

(3) Which are more promising, and what is withdrawn or unresolved.
- Provoked-seizure endpoint in a WWOX system: lithium and ethosuximide, because they are the only ones measured. Lithium is probably a general effect. Ethosuximide holds the only open selectivity question and has the weaker mechanistic rationale. This is one experiment from one laboratory.
- Acute rescue: ketamine is more promising on general paediatric evidence only (transfer, abstract level). Nothing is more promising in a WWOX system.
- Spasm control: vigabatrin, by evidence level in a related seizure type. Harm columns are not ranked.
- Development: AAV9 gene addition, clearly, with limits.
- Withdrawn: the Kasahara 2019 mouse diazepam/bumetanide inference.
- Unresolved:
  - the test age in Cheng 2020;
  - the mouse ontogeny of ethosuximide;
  - any T-type current in a WWOX system;
  - E_GABA.

(4) Not concludable. Knockout-specific action of ethosuximide: significance differed between genotypes, but no interaction test exists. A T-type mechanism. Lithium being better than ethosuximide, since the arms used different cohorts. Any dose, use or efficacy. Any ordering for spontaneous seizures. The published effect size cannot power the study.

(5) The decision I could not take without asking: the assay age and stimulus, which the material says are the laboratory's. I would also need the laboratory's own criterion for "selective" for the T-type blocker.

(6) Outcome patterns by explanation.
- E1, knockout-selective: knockout effect ratio at most 0.7 with an interval excluding 1; wild type null (interval inside 0.85 to 1.15); interaction ratio at most 0.7 or at least 1.43; panel valid; wild type above the floor.
- E2, age-limited: wild-type null at the assay age, an effect at an older age, panel valid at both ages. It is weakened if the same effect appears at every wild-type age. If the panel fails at the assay age, move the age and do not read the null.
- E3, general proportional effect: similar ratios across genotypes, interaction ratio between 0.7 and 1.43, panel valid.
- E4, T-type: the selective blocker reproduces the ethosuximide pattern in the knockout. This would make a T-type route plausible, not proven. A null without an engagement control is uninterpretable.
- Sizing: the material gives about 180 to 320 animals per cell at the paper's stimulus, so the interaction is exploratory unless the pilot finds low variability.
