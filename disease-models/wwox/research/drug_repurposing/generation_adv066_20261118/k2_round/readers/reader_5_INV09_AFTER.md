(1) First actions: (a) fix the lithium regimen as Cheng 2020 states it (repeated pretreatment within an hour) and pre-specify the genotype-by-treatment term; (b) run the provoked-seizure pilot shared with INV-04 (variability, assay age, non-floor stimulus) to size the interaction; (c) measure the baseline first: pS535 (human 540) with total GSK3 and replicate lanes in WWOX-deficient cells against corrected controls, and repeat the single-lane p-Ser9 blot with replicate lanes.

(2) Arms, controls and measurements: lithium; a chemically unrelated second GSK-3 inhibitor with published selectivity and brain exposure; a general non-GSK-3 anticonvulsant comparator; vehicle per genotype; an inositol add-back arm (not GSK-3-free). Measurements: p-Ser9, total GSK-3beta and a kinase or beta-catenin readout in treated and vehicle animals; E_GABA by perforated patch at matched maturity. For the cell work: GSK3A/B knock-down, S535A and catalytically dead epsilon, ISRIB with an engagement control, and a stated null (the GSK3 route is not engaged). Isogenic isoform knockout per compound is needed. A wild-type pup age series is also needed.

(3) Promising and withdrawn: the material ranks nothing overall. Lithium is called general. It has the most paediatric exposure of the GSK-3 agents (label from age 7, bipolar; no infant treatment data), at label and abstract level. The KCC2 branch is more promising than an NKCC1 blocker on development, at evidence level only. Ethosuximide is proposed to run first in vivo (design, not efficacy). Withdrawn: Kasahara 2019 P11 datum and any claim that it bears on lithium or GSK-3 (D09). Unresolved: the sign of GSK-3 inhibition for myelination; opposing beta-catenin signs; the age of the p-Ser9 blot (day 14 against day 20); E_GABA, never measured in any WWOX system.

(4) Cannot be concluded: that the lithium effect is GSK-3-mediated; that it is larger in the knockout (82 against 54 percent, not shown to exceed noise); that GSK-3 is more active in Wwox-null brain (inferred from one blot, no kinase assay); that any agent is beneficial or safe in infants; that the GSK3-eIF2B route is engaged.

(5) The decision I could not take alone: which second GSK-3 inhibitor and which general comparator to use, and the lithium regimen if it departs from Cheng's. The material names classes and criteria but no choice, and the pilot needs its own assay age, which is the laboratory's choice.

(6) Separating patterns:
- L1 (general effect): lithium effect in all genotypes with interaction ratio between 0.7 and 1.43, the second inhibitor effective, the comparator effective.
- L2 (GSK-3-specific, knockout-enhanced): interaction outside 0.7 to 1.43, reproduced by the second GSK-3 inhibitor and not by the comparator, with a p-Ser9 or kinase change.
- L3 (regimen artefact): the sign changes with the regimen; the same sign under the replication regimen.
- Lithium acting through other targets (inositol depletion): the material gives no numeric pattern; it only notes that the add-back arm is not GSK-3-free.
- The GSK3-eIF2B explanations H1 to H5 have qualitative patterns only: H1 fails if pS535 is not raised at baseline, if the effect also appears in corrected cells or is unchanged by S535A, or if it disappears when phospho-eIF2 alpha is lowered; H2 fails if alpha also acts or neither isoform changes pS535; H3 fails with no change under S535A. The material gives no numeric margins for these.
- Sample size: about 36 to 109 animals per arm for a ratio of 0.7, and about 180 to 320 per cell for the interaction, so the interaction is exploratory.
