A dose check at each age. A selective T-type blocker beside ethosuximide. Provoked-seizure design, 60-minute summary.

(3) More promising: the material ranks no candidate or class as more promising, and gives no endpoint or evidence level for such a ranking. The carded T-type thread is only the subject of the question. Withdrawn: nothing is stated. Unresolved: whether the suppression is genotype-specific; the mouse age floor (the rat floor is an inference); whether any T-type current exists in WWOX systems (none has been measured, and the held transcript sets do not sample thalamus); and the contested cell pharmacology of ethosuximide.

(4) Cannot be concluded: that the suppression is specific to WWOX loss. The paper reports no genotype-by-treatment test, does not state pup age, and its wild-type pups sit near the floor. Nothing can be concluded about a T-type mechanism. Variability is only a bracket (coefficient of variation about 0.6 to 1.2, read from means and error bars). The estimated 180 to 320 animals per cell is approximate.

(5) Decision needing someone else: whether to commit to a design needing roughly 180 to 320 animals per cell, or to run the pilot first. The material says the pilot comes first, but the resources and approval for the main study are not stated.

(6) Competing explanations, as the material names them: (i) genotype-specific suppression and (ii) a similar effect in wild type, which would move the thread out of its group. The separating pattern is the interaction ratio (knockout effect ratio over wild-type effect ratio, log scale). Genotype-specific: at most 0.7 or at least 1.43. Not genotype-specific: between 0.7 and 1.43. The material names no other competing explanation, such as a non-T-type mechanism, and gives no margin for one.
