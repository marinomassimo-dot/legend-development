(1) First actions: (a) Measure the baseline in WWOX-deficient human neurons or oligodendrocyte-lineage cells, stage matched, against corrected isogenic controls. Read eIF2B epsilon pSer535 (human Ser540), total GSK3, phospho-eIF2 alpha and new-protein labelling, with replicate lanes. (b) State the null (H5, GSK3 route not engaged) in writing before measuring. (c) Run the isoform knock-down (GSK3B against GSK3A).

(2) Arms, controls and measurements:
- GSK3B and GSK3A knock-down, with double depletion.
- Epsilon variants: wild type, Ser-to-Ala, a phospho-mimetic (unvalidated), and catalytically dead (E572A). The dead form needs its own control in tissue with intact Gsk3b.
- An ATP-site GSK3 inhibitor plus a second inhibitor of another class. Lithium is only a labelled non-specific comparator.
- A lithium arm with inositol add-back, with the second need's readout measured in the same experiment.
- ISRIB as a discriminating probe only, with an engagement control and exposure check.
- Readouts: pS535, exchange activity, translation, and phospho-eIF2 alpha lowered as a manipulation.
- For the seizure family: a general anticonvulsant comparator that does not act on this kinase, and a wild-type pup age series. Single and repeated lithium pretreatment are design variables.

(3) More promising, withdrawn and unresolved:
- The material names no candidate as more promising and gives no endpoint ranking or evidence grade. It promotes no repurposing candidate (the abstention stands). GSK3 inhibitors are experimental tools only.
- Documented: LiCl, SB-415286 and SB-216763 lower Ser535 phosphorylation (CHO cells, abstract level). Genetic Gsk3b loss does the same in mouse neurons.
- Unresolved: the thread's retention, because lithium's pharmacology opposes a second need. Also unresolved are the isoform, whether the GSK3 route is engaged at all, and whether ISRIB works on a phospho-epsilon lesion (no source tests it).
- Nothing is explicitly withdrawn.

(4) Cannot be concluded:

- Direction of effect, since the package asserts none.
- Whether the retinal beta-isoform result transfers.
- Whether the opposing push on the second need is tolerable.
- Any genotype-specific seizure effect (one experiment, effect in all three genotypes).
- Any selectivity in live cells: biochemical selectivity does not carry over (4-6-fold).

(5) Decision needing someone else: whether lithium stays in the selection given its opposition to the second need. The material states the question but gives no criterion for it. Its reopen-or-abandon fields are "not stated".

(6) Outcome patterns for competing explanations:
- H1 (GSK3 route engaged) is refuted if pS535 is not raised at baseline, if pS535 falls but exchange and translation do not move, or if the effect also appears in corrected cells or is unchanged by S535A.
- H5 (not engaged, ISR from another input): the null of H1 and H3. It is supported by no baseline pS535 rise or no S535A effect.
- ISR route: the effect disappears when phospho-eIF2 alpha is lowered.
- H2 (beta isoform): the beta knock-down reproduces the compound effect and the alpha knock-down does not. It is refuted if alpha also acts or neither isoform changes pS535.
- H3 (the node carries the defect): S535A restores translation and E572A removes it. It is refuted if S535A changes nothing, or if the Ala form alone raises control translation.
- H4 (ISRIB probe): little correction predicts a GSK3-site lesion independent of phospho-eIF2. Some correction predicts a stability component. Equal correction with or without phospho-eIF2 alpha means an undescribed route.
- Lithium general effect versus knockout-specific: the effect appears in all genotypes, so it is general. A genotype effect needs a non-GSK3 anticonvulsant comparator.
- The material gives no numeric margin for any of these.
