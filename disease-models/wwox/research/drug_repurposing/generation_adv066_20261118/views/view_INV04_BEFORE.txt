INVESTIGATION INV-04: Ethosuximide (and the carded T-type thread)
Question: Is the carded suppression specific to WWOX loss, or does the same agent act equally in wild type?
Decisive uncertainty: Whether an interaction term survives; a wild-type effect of the same size would move this thread out of its group without any new compound being involved.
Next test: The same provoked-seizure design with both genotypes treated and an interaction term, powered on the published effect size. [IT57; ADV-051, ADV-054, ADV-059, ADV-061] The published effect size cannot be used to power it: the paper reports no genotype-by-treatment test, does not state the age of the pups, and its wild-type pups sit near the floor of the scale; from its curves the variability of the 60-minute summary is a bracket (a coefficient of variation of about 0.6 to 1.2 in knockouts, read from means and error bars, not a value), which puts the interaction contrast at about 180 to 320 animals per cell at the paper's stimulus, so a pilot comes first and fixes the variability, the assay age and a stimulus that gives wild type a measurable response. The design also needs: wild-type pups at the assay age and at two or more older ages; reference agents with different age profiles as an assay-sensitivity panel (the rat age floor for this agent is an inference, unresolved for the mouse); seizure type recorded per animal and a motor control; a dose check at each age; and a selective T-type blocker beside this agent, because no T-type current has been measured in any WWOX system (the held transcript sets do not sample the thalamus) and the cell pharmacology of this agent is contested. The margin for the same size is the interaction ratio (the knockout effect ratio over the wild-type effect ratio, on the log scale): genotype-specific if at most 0.7 or at least 1.43, not genotype-specific between 0.7 and 1.43, fixed before data (in vivo brief, section 2.7).
