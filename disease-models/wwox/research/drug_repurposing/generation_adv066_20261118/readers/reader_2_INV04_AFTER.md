**(1) First three actions**
- Run a pilot in knockout and wild-type pups. It fixes the variability of the 60-minute summary, the assay age, and a stimulus that gives wild type a response above the floor.
- Fix the interaction margin before data: genotype-specific if the ratio is at most 0.7 or at least 1.43.
- Set up a wild-type age series with the reference panel and a dose/exposure check at each age.

**(2) Arms, controls, measurements**
- Genotype-crossed design (+/+, +/-, -/-), with a vehicle per genotype, ethosuximide, and an interaction term.
- Wild-type pups at the assay age and at two or more older ages up to about P28.
- Reference agents with flat, rising and falling age profiles as an assay-sensitivity panel, reported per age.
- Seizure type recorded per animal, and a motor/ataxia control.
- A selective T-type blocker in the same layout, with an engagement control if possible.
- Cav3 current or expression in Wwox-null thalamic neurons.
- Optional video-EEG in a knockout subset.
- Lithium as the contrast case.
- Analysis: ratios per genotype and age, with a bootstrap over litters.

**(3) More promising / withdrawn / unresolved**
- Provoked-seizure suppression: lithium and ethosuximide over every other candidate, because only they are measured (one experiment, one laboratory). Lithium is promising for a robust but probably general effect. Ethosuximide is promising for the open selectivity question, with the weaker mechanistic rationale.
- Acute rescue: ketamine by general paediatric evidence (transfer, abstract level). No candidate is more promising in a WWOX system.
- Chronic control: vigabatrin for spasm control (a related seizure type; WWOX data bear only "reported").
- Development: AAV9 gene addition, clearly.
- Withdrawn: the Kasahara 2019 mouse inference.
- Unresolved: Cheng's test age; the mouse ethosuximide age floor; any T-type current in WWOX; the T-type mechanism of ethosuximide itself.

**(4) Cannot be concluded**
- That suppression is knockout-specific, because only a difference in significance exists and no interaction test.
- That lithium beats ethosuximide, because the cohorts differ.
- T-type mediation.
- Sample size from the published effect size.
- That a young-age null is real.
- Harm rankings, or any effect on spontaneous seizures or in children.

**(5) Decision needing someone else**
The assay age and stimulus. The material assigns them to the laboratory, and the pilot depends on them.
