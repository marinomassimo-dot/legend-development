## COMPARABLE?
- **Tazemetostat, EZH2 enzyme potency across documents: not one number.** IC50 values for the enzyme run from 0.34 nM (2020, AlphaLISA) through 1.0 to 1.2 nM and 2.2 to 4.3 nM to 22 nM (2023) and 170 nM (2024): about a 500-fold range across documents, with different substrates (nucleosome, H3 peptide, SAM), pre-incubation times and PRC2 constructs. The inhibition constant 2.5 nM (Ki) appears in five documents (2020 to 2023) as a quotation of the 2015 value (document CHEMBL3414551): **one origin, not five confirmations**. Within one document, wild-type EZH2 and the Y641 and A677G mutants are comparable: 1.2 (wild type), 1.7 (Y641F), 2.2 (Y641N), 2.5 (Y641S), 4.3 (Y641C) and 1.9 nM (A677G) in CHEMBL5634131 (2023): a 3.6-fold spread, all within the same assay.
- **EZH2 against EZH1: comparable only within a document.** CHEMBL5634131: EZH2 1.2 nM against EZH1 93 nM (about 78-fold); CHEMBL5331057: 4 against 392 nM (about 98-fold); CHEMBL5584353: 170 against 1,170 nM (about 7-fold). The three documents disagree by more than tenfold on the ratio, so no single selectivity figure is given.
- **Cell readouts are a different quantity from enzyme inhibition.** Reduction of H3K27me3 in cells (EC50 or IC50): 5 to 64 nM in eight lymphoma lines (2021, one document), 20 nM in HeLa (72 h, 2015), 200 nM in G401 (2016, 2021 quotation). Antiproliferative IC50 is above 3 microM in six wild-type EZH2 lymphoma lines (2025) and 17.8 microM in one EZH2-overexpressing line (2020); one 2024 row gives 1.07 microM in MINO cells. The exposure time of those growth assays was not read for every row.
- **TMP195, class IIa HDAC enzymes: comparable within one document, and the two documents differ by 1.5- to 7-fold (corrected 2026-11-25, HE: HDAC5 216 against 148 nM; first written as 2- to 7-fold).** 2021 (CHEMBL4732198): HDAC4 354, HDAC5 216, HDAC7 27, HDAC9 16 nM; class I and HDAC8 weak (HDAC1 45,000 nM; HDAC8 2,100 nM). 2022 (CHEMBL5154665, a different laboratory's assay set): HDAC4 128, HDAC5 148, HDAC7 7.7, HDAC9 2.4 nM and all of HDAC1, 2, 3, 6, 8, 10, 11 above 5,000 nM (a "greater than" ceiling, not a measured value). The order of potency inside each document is the same (HDAC9 and HDAC7 more potent than HDAC4 and HDAC5); absolute values are not interchangeable.
- **TMP195 in cells is weaker than on the recombinant enzymes (about 6- to 370-fold, depending on the isoform).** Class IIa activity in four cell lines (THP-1, PANC-1, L3.6pl, HT-29) is 0.9 to 1.0 microM in one 2022 document against 2.4 to 148 nM on recombinant enzymes in the same document; a 2021 THP-1 row gives 3.0 microM. Class I and IIb in cells: above 5 microM (above 30 microM in THP-1, 2021).
- **10058-F4: no comparison is possible on the rows.** Five rows only, all cell growth after 96 h (one 2025 document): 44.7 microM (MDA-MB-231), 39.8 microM (MDA-MB-468), a row without a value (MCF7), 35.1 microM (P493-6 cells expressing MYC) and 59.5 microM (P493-6 with tetracycline, MYC repressed). The MYC-expressing cells are 1.7-fold more sensitive than the MYC-repressed cells: a weak dependence on MYC, one document, no second one. **No row on MYC, MAX or MYC-MAX dimerisation at all.**

## Tazemetostat (CHEMBL3414621)
- **Potency (163 rows; 60 on EZH2 or EZH1 by gene symbol):** as above. Also EED rows (2.0 nM enzyme, 40 nM cells; 2017 document), G9a rows (2.9 and 15 nM; two 2025 documents) and PROTAC-document rows (EZH2 degradation IC50 above 10 nM): tazemetostat's role in those documents (comparator, series member or tested agent) was **not read, so none of these rows is used**. Rows on other target families are antiproliferative only. `NOT_ASSESSED` for selectivity outside the methyltransferases: no kinase or receptor panel row was found by these routes.
- **Exposure (PubMed, title and abstract read):** the 2015 Int J Cancer study (PMID 25868794) tested EPZ-6438 (tazemetostat) in wild-type and transporter-knockout mice: **P-glycoprotein (ABCB1) limits its brain accumulation, and the efflux was completely abrogated by elacridar** (a P-gp inhibitor); BCRP is not the limiting transporter for this compound. The abstract gives no concentration figure for brain. A 2026 bioRxiv preprint (PMID 42124576, abstract read, **not peer reviewed, full text not read**) reports tazemetostat combined with elacridar in a mouse social-behaviour model; its effect on brain concentration is not read here. A 2020 paper on convection-enhanced delivery of an EZH2 inhibitor for glioma (PMID 32674144, title read) (title read only; what it showed about exposure is not read). "Tazemetostat cerebrospinal fluid": 0 records. **Not found by these routes:** a measured brain or CSF concentration of tazemetostat given alone.
- **Label (openFDA SPL, newest 2026-04-29, TAZVERIK):** epithelioid sarcoma in adults and pediatric patients aged 16 years and older; relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma in adults (accelerated approval). Safety and effectiveness in patients younger than 16 years **not established**; a 13-week juvenile rat study (post-natal day 7 to 97) reports T-cell lymphoblastic lymphoma from about 2.8 times the adult human exposure, increased trabecular bone, increased body weight and distended testicles; secondary malignancies in adults (warning), embryo-fetal toxicity (animal skeletal abnormalities). No dose is given here. Label text read in part.
- **Trials:** ClinicalTrials.gov API v2 intervention search "tazemetostat" returns 61 studies (status fields and results not read; the count is a pointer, not a finding).

## What changes for SCI's broad unit C and IT's compound-level units
1. **Tazemetostat has the most complete public data of the three** (enzyme potency in many documents, cellular H3K27me3 readout, a label, a mouse brain-penetration study). Its brain exposure is limited by an efflux transporter (P-gp) alone, which is a published mechanism for a missing exposure, not a measurement of the exposure; a pediatric label exists only from age 16 and the juvenile rat study flags findings at young ages.
2. **TMP195 has a clear class IIa selectivity profile on recombinant enzymes in two documents but no exposure, no label and no brain cell data.** Its class IIa potency in cells is about 6- to 370-fold weaker than on the recombinant enzymes, depending on the isoform.
3. **10058-F4 has no on-target measurement in ChEMBL, a cell potency of 35 to 60 microM, and a mouse study with a one-hour half-life and low tumour exposure.** Any comparison with the other broad agents would rest on literature figures not read here.
4. Compound-level identity for IT: all three have a ChEMBL id and a structure key above; none has a conflict between the sources read. The "label_conflict" field of the identity tool was empty for tazemetostat.
