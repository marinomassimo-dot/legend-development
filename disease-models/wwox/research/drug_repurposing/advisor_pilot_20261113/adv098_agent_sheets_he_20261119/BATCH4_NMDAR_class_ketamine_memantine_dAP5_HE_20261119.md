## COMPARABLE? (which pairs can be set side by side, and the condition that decides each number)
- **Pore blockers against each other (ketamine, memantine): comparable only within one document and one holding potential.** The single document that records both agents on GluN2A and GluN2B in one patch-clamp protocol (CHEMBL6083260, 2024, human receptors in HEK293 cells, L-glutamate-induced response) reads, at -60 mV: ketamine 500 nM (GluN2A) and 510 nM (GluN2B), memantine 840 nM and 780 nM; at -40 mV: ketamine 53.3 microM and 15.2 microM, memantine 28.1 microM and 25.6 microM. **The holding potential, not the receptor, drives the 30- to 100-fold difference** (a pore block that weakens as the membrane is depolarised). At -60 mV the two agents differ by less than 2-fold; at -40 mV the order of the GluN2A values reverses (ketamine weaker). So no order between ketamine and memantine is read: it flips with the potential. This also settles my batch 1 remark that "the assay decides" the memantine value in that document: the assay difference there is the holding potential (-60 against -40 mV).
- **d-AP5 against the pore blockers: not comparable as one potency.** d-AP5 is a competitive antagonist at the glutamate site; its Ki or IC50 depends on the agonist concentration used (not in the ChEMBL text), and the Mg2+ and voltage conditions that shape pore-blocker values do not act on it in the same way. The two quantities are different and **no order is read**; d-AP5 is a tool.
- **Mg2+ per subunit (SCI's request): not found by these routes.** No row gives an IC50 per GluN2 subunit with a stated Mg2+ concentration. The only text that names Mg2+ is the [3H]MK-801 or [3H]TCP binding rows ("in the presence of Mg2+"), and those do not resolve the GluN2 subunit. The patch-clamp texts name the holding potential but not the Mg2+ concentration. A Mg2+-matched per-subunit IC50 for ketamine or d-AP5 has to come from the primary papers (SCI's delegate).
- **Repeated identical values are one origin.** For ketamine, a Ki of 180 nM appears on about 25 rows with different brain preparations and species in one 2020 document (CHEMBL4680282); it is one transcribed value, not 25 measurements. For d-AP5, the same numbers (280, 460, 1640, 3710 nM) appear as an EC50 in CHEMBL1133464 and as a Ki in CHEMBL1143579: the same origin.

## d-AP5 (declared identity CHEMBL84612 = 2-amino-5-phosphonopentanoic acid, D configuration read from the SMILES; ChEMBL stores it without a name)
- **Targets and potency (21 rows, 18 on the NMDA receptor or its binding site):** per-subunit competitive antagonist values, rat recombinant GluN1a/GluN2x in Xenopus oocytes (CHEMBL4024770): Ki 0.39 microM (GluN2A), 2.8 microM (GluN2B), 5.9 microM (GluN2C), 21 microM (GluN2D). Another document (CHEMBL1143579, currents evoked by glutamate and glycine): Ki 280 nM (GluN2A), 460 nM (GluN2B), 1640 nM (GluN2C), 3710 nM (GluN2D). **Both documents agree on the subunit order (2A strongest, 2D weakest) and differ 6-fold on GluN2B (corrected 2026-11-25, HE: 2,800 against 460 nM; first written as 7-fold); within each document the subunits are comparable**; across documents the magnitude is not. Binding: IC50 177 nM ([3H]CGS-19755 displacement), 880 nM and Ki 3.83 microM (rat cortical membranes, different radioligands); a cortical wedge assay IC50 3.7 microM.

## What changes for SCI's exposure-effect choice (batch 4)
1. Any potency for a pore blocker needs the **holding potential stated** before it is used; at the model's depolarised states a value read at -60 mV can be 30 to 100 times too low.
2. The ketamine against memantine order flips with the potential: a mapping that places one above the other from a single IC50 is not supported.
3. d-AP5 sits on a different axis (competitive, agonist-dependent): in the circuit model the identical J_N perturbation (see the normalisation note) hides that difference; the data do not hide it, and they do not give a Mg2+-matched per-subunit value for either agent.
