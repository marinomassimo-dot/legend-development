## COMPARABLE?
- **Ganaxolone and gaboxadol: not comparable.** Ganaxolone is read as a positive allosteric modulator (potentiation of the GABA response, EC50); gaboxadol as a direct agonist (activation EC50 or binding). A modulator's EC50 and an agonist's EC50 are different quantities, and each depends on the GABA concentration used, which the ChEMBL text rarely gives. No order is read.
- **Ganaxolone, alpha1beta2gamma2 against alpha4beta3delta (the synaptic-against-extrasynaptic question):** EC50 266 nM at alpha1beta2gamma2 (LTK cells) and 103 nM at alpha4beta3delta (CHO cells) in one document (CHEMBL3425450, 2015). Two cell lines in one paper: a 2.6-fold difference is an indication, not an identified contrast; the GABA concentration used, the efficacy (maximal potentiation) and the temperature are not in the text. Alpha1beta2gamma2 across documents: 260 nM (oocytes, 1997), 266 nM (LTK cells, 2015), 200 nM (oocytes, 2018): consistent within a factor of 1.3 across three assay systems. Alpha2beta1gamma2 100 nM against alpha1beta2gamma2 200 nM in the same oocyte document (CHEMBL5260830, 2018): within one document, a 2-fold difference.
- **Gaboxadol on delta-containing receptors: no value.** The only functional human GABA-A value found is EC50 14 microM (14.1 and 14.0 microM, two rows) at alpha1beta2gamma2S (FLIPR, TSA201 cells, 2013); the only other human functional rows are two GABA-rho1 antagonist rows (Ki 100 microM, FMP, HEK293, 2007); no alpha4beta3delta or alpha4beta2delta row exists among the retrieved IC50, Ki, Kd and EC50 rows under this id (corrected 2026-11-26, HE, ADV-293; see the dated line at the end). The delta preference usually attributed to the compound is **not found by these routes** in measured rows. So "extrasynaptic versus synaptic" cannot be read for gaboxadol from these sources.
- **Species and system:** most gaboxadol binding values are rat brain membranes (IC50 31.5 to 130 nM and Ki 160 nM, [3H]muscimol or [3H]GABA displacement, 1983 to 2013) (corrected 2026-11-26, HE, ADV-293; see the dated line at the end), which measure the high-affinity site binding, not function.

## Ganaxolone (ChEMBL CHEMBL1568698, NAME_EXACT)
- **Targets and potency:** 8 rows. EC50 (potentiation) 266, 260 and 200 nM at alpha1beta2gamma2 (three systems), 103 nM at alpha4beta3delta (CHO), 100 nM at alpha2beta1gamma2 (oocytes); [35S]TBPS or [3H]TBPS displacement IC50 80 and 42 nM in rat cortical membranes; Ki 790 nM (unspecified receptor preparation, 2024). The ChEMBL symbols of the 266 and 103 nM rows list all six alpha and the three beta subunits with no gamma or delta symbol (a ChEMBL target-group mapping); the 260 and 200 nM rows carry GABRA1, GABRB2, GABRG2 and the 100 nM row GABRA2, GABRB1, GABRG2; so gene-level attribution is not read from the symbols (corrected 2026-11-26, HE, ADV-293; see the dated line at the end).
- **Selectivity / off-targets:** no row outside GABA-A: the three rows counted apart from the five human subunit rows are the two rat cortical-membrane TBPS binding rows (80 and 42 nM) and the unspecified-origin binding Ki row (790 nM) (corrected 2026-11-25 and 2026-11-26, HE, ADV-293; see the dated lines at the end); `NOT_ASSESSED`. Not found: efficacy (maximal potentiation) per subunit, activity at other receptors, any neurosteroid-site or tonic-current measurement in these rows.
- **Rodent exposure (PubMed, titles read):** a rat pharmacokinetic and intravenous study exists (PMID 30552296, 2019, ganaxolone blocks diazepam-resistant lithium-pilocarpine status epilepticus in rats) and a 2001 LC-MS/MS method paper "for the determination of pharmacokinetics" (PMID 11232855, title truncated; the animal species not read). Brain concentrations: not confirmed by titles. First search "ganaxolone brain concentration plasma rat mice" 0 records (a long AND query: not an absence); "ganaxolone pharmacokinetics rat" 5 records; a general "neonatal OR juvenile rodent" query was unusable (11,333 records, no ganaxolone paper among the first five). **Not found by these routes:** a juvenile exposure and a free brain concentration.
- **Related, titles only:** "Ganaxolone enhances microglial clearance activity and promotes remyelination in focal demyelination in the corpus callosum" (PMID 31332963, 2020): a myelination-relevant record that bears on the broad objective too, not read.
- **Label (openFDA SPL, 1 label, effective 2026-09-04):** indicated for seizures associated with CDKL5 deficiency disorder in patients 2 years of age and older; pediatric section states safety and effectiveness established from age 2 years for that indication. No dose given here.
- **Not measured or not found:** efficacy per subunit at one GABA concentration; any measured tonic-current effect; juvenile exposure.

## Gaboxadol (= THIP; ChEMBL CHEMBL312443, NAME_EXACT; "THIP" returned only unrelated partial matches and was not used)
- **Targets and potency:** 19 rows; 5 on neither GABA-A nor GABA-rho (two SARS-CoV-2 cytotoxicity rows, one GABA-B, two GABA uptake: no activity up to limits of 20 to 500 microM) and two on GABA-rho1 (formerly GABA-C), Ki 100 microM (corrected 2026-11-25, HE; see the dated line at the end). GABA-A binding IC50 31.5 to 130 nM and Ki 160 nM (rat brain membranes); functional: EC50 14 microM at human alpha1beta2gamma2S (FLIPR, TSA201); EC50 335 microM for chloride uptake into mouse brain synaptoneurosomes (2002); EC50 55 microM for inhibition of the CA1 population spike in rat hippocampal slice (1991).
- **Selectivity / off-targets:** GABA-B IC50 above 100 microM (rat cortex), GABA uptake IC50 above 300 to 500 microM, GABA-C rho1 Ki 100 microM (HEK293): the high-concentration non-GABA-A rows are listed as read.
- **Rodent exposure (PubMed):** "Plasma and CNS concentrations of Gaboxadol in rats following subcutaneous administration" (PMID 17362924, 2007, title read; adult rat, abstract not read). A mouse brain-concentration search returned 26 records, all pharmacology papers by title (not read). **Not found by these routes:** neonatal or juvenile exposure (the query "gaboxadol neonatal rat seizures" returned 0 records; a query shape, not an absence; SCI's delegate reports THIP action in P6 to P9 neonatal slices, which is a slice effect, not an exposure).
- **Label (openFDA):** NO_MATCH_FOUND for "gaboxadol": not found by this route, not a statement about development status.
- **Not measured or not found:** any delta-containing receptor value; juvenile exposure. A free (extracellular) CNS concentration in adult rats exists: PMID 17362924 (dynamic-no-net-flux and ultraslow microdialysis after subcutaneous dosing, peak CNS 0.7 to 3 microM at 2.5, 5 and 10 mg/kg, half-life 28 min; abstract read 2026-11-26; the abstract does not give an age) (corrected 2026-11-26, HE, ADV-293; see the dated line at the end).

## What changes for SCI's exposure-effect choice (batch 3)
1. For the synaptic against extrasynaptic question the data separate the two agents unequally: ganaxolone has one cross-cell-line pair (103 against 266 nM, an indication), gaboxadol has none on delta receptors.
2. Neither agent has a juvenile exposure by these routes (gaboxadol has an adult-rat microdialysis CNS concentration, PMID 17362924, line above) (corrected 2026-11-26, HE, ADV-293; see the dated line at the end); ganaxolone has a label from 2 years of age for a defined seizure disorder.
3. Gaboxadol's functional potency (14 microM) sits about 50 to 140 times above ganaxolone's modulation EC50 (corrected 2026-11-25, HE; see the dated line at the end) under different readouts: this difference describes the readouts as much as the agents and is not read as potency order.
