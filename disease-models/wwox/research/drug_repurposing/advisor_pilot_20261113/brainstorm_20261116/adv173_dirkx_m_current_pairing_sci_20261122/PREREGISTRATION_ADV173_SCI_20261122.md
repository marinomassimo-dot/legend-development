## Where the existing text already stands (read first, as the assignment asks)
The slice panel and ADV-117 read an opener's SUPPRESSION of S-KO bursts as the ordering criterion; they ask for the end-of-recording resting potential and an XE991 reversal, and list a Kv7 function measurement as one possible observation. They do NOT (as I read them before the paper) require (a) a baseline Kv7/M-current measurement in the same cells or slices that carry the burst readout, nor (b) an opener-treated control-slice arm that would show whether quieting happens without any channel defect, nor (c) a rule that quieting is not correction. The brief's pup design has no Kv7 content. So the question is live for the slice package, closed for the pup brief.

## The two interpretations (fixed)
* **A (deficit predicts rescue):** an early, reversible M-current (Kv7.2/7.3) deficit is present in the disease preparation before the network phenotype; an opener's network effect follows that deficit in direction and size, across variants, ages or timings (early better than late), and is absent or reversed where no deficit exists.
* **B (acute quieting, divergent later):** an opener lowers firing or network activity acutely in the disease preparation AND in controls or in lines without a deficit (a non-specific brake), and longer exposure leaves network maturation or synchrony different from untreated or control networks even after the intrinsic current recovers.
The two make different predictions only with a treated contrast in the same preparation: A predicts no or small network effect in a control or no-deficit line; B predicts a similar acute effect there.

## Discriminating observation, readings and rules (fixed)
Discriminator: in ONE preparation and one age window, a table with (1) baseline Kv7/M-current or Kv7 current density per line or variant; (2) the network metric before treatment by line; (3) the network response to the opener, acute and week-long, early and late, in each line AND in an opener-treated control.
* **P-pair (surviving delta):** the paper shows, for the same lines and an overlapping age window, the Kv7 current and the network response, with an opener-treated control or no-deficit line, and the response is non-specific (quieting in the control or no-deficit line) OR diverges from the intrinsic recovery (network differences persisting after the current recovers). Then ONE optional readout for the slice package: a paired baseline Kv7 (XE991-sensitive) current in the cells or slices that carry the burst readout plus an opener-treated control-slice arm, with the written rule "a suppression that also occurs in control slices is not read as correction or as WWOX-specific".
* **P-logic (weak, optional):** the dissociation is shown only across different cells, wells, ages or lines (single-cell current and network wells from different cultures), so the pairing is supported as a design logic but not as a measured dissociation. Carry the same optional line, labelled weak.
* **P-closed (documented closure):** (i) no opener-treated control or no-deficit line exists, so the quieting-versus-correction contrast cannot be drawn from the paper, AND no paired current and network measure exists; or (ii) the data are not comparable to the slice package (different species, system, age, channel gene, or a gain-of-function line); then CLOSE naming exactly what is missing, with no clause. The text of the panel and ADV-117 is then left as it is.

## Rejecting observation (named in advance)
No opener-treated control or no-deficit comparison in the same preparation, or a Kv7 current measured only in a different age or cell set from the network readout with no overlap, would send the result to P-closed or P-logic; an opener that changes the network in controls as much as in the disease line would support B; a response that tracks the deficit across lines would support A and keep the pairing demand (but would be a stronger reason, not a different clause).
