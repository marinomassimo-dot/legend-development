H1. transfer · Raising neuronal chloride extrusion capacity (KCC2-type cotransporter expression or activity) lowers intracellular chloride, so GABA-A currents hyperpolarise more and the distance to burst threshold grows. · Evidence: in other epilepsy and developmental models, KCC2 loss or late maturation weakens inhibition and enhancement restores it. No Wwox-specific measurement known. · Test: gramicidin-perforated-patch EGABA in Wwox-null versus control principal cells at matched age. Depolarised EGABA means a chloride lever is real. Normal EGABA closes it and points to conductance, not driving force. · Ignored: a phasic deficit is usually read as fewer receptors, but the same conductance gives less effect if driving force is lost, so a chloride gap can mimic a receptor gap.

H2. transfer · Reducing chloride import (NKCC1-type cotransporter activity) in a delayed-maturation state would shift GABA toward hyperpolarising and lower burst propensity. · Evidence: neonatal seizure literature supports this, with mixed clinical-model results. Wwox-null chloride status is unmeasured. · Test: compare EGABA at the ages when null mice start to seize with the NKCC1:KCC2 ratio (Western blot or protein ratio) in the same tissue. A high ratio with depolarised EGABA supports a developmental-delay reading. Normal ratio argues against it. · Ignored: the null phenotype appears in a developmental window when GABA polarity is still changing, so timing alone may explain part of the deficit.

H3. assumption · Boosting availability of endogenous neuroactive-steroid-type positive modulators of delta-containing receptors would raise tonic and graded inhibition without changing receptor number. · Evidence: Wwox-null mice are reported to have steroid and metabolic abnormalities (my recollection, not verified here). Link to Gabrd function is none known. · Test: measure tonic current and its response to a delta-selective positive modulator in null slices. A response scaled to Gabrd level means the lever has a ceiling set by receptor number. A supranormal response means a modulator-supply deficit. · Ignored: the receptor can be present but under-modulated, so Gabrd mRNA or protein per neuron cannot give the functional deficit alone.

H4. assumption · Restoring activity- or growth-factor-dependent transcription of Gabrd (immediate-early and neurotrophin-linked regulators) would increase delta-subunit supply. · Evidence: Gabrd expression is known to vary with activity, hormonal state and neurotrophin signalling in other systems. Wwox-null data: lower Gabrd per neuron in hippocampus only (as stated). · Test: single-neuron or single-nucleus Gabrd readout after a defined period of altered network activity in null slices or culture. Recovery means the deficit is secondary to activity. No recovery means a transcription-level defect. · Ignored: lower Gabrd could be a consequence of the disease (a response to chronic seizures) rather than a cause.

H5. assumption · Improving assembly and ER export of delta-containing receptors (partner alpha subunit supply, chaperone and folding capacity) would increase surface delta receptors independent of mRNA. · Evidence: WWOX loss is linked to ER stress and altered proteostasis in other cells (transfer, partial). Receptor assembly in Wwox-null: none known. · Test: surface versus total delta and alpha4 protein by surface biotinylation or immunogold in null and control. Low surface with normal total means a trafficking lever. Both low means supply. · Ignored: mRNA-level readouts do not see assembly or surface expression.

H6. transfer · Slowing GABA clearance by extrasynaptic transporter blockade (astrocytic and neuronal GAT classes) would raise ambient GABA and tonic drive onto the remaining delta receptors. · Evidence: in other systems this raises tonic current and has been used for anticonvulsant effect, but with possible paradoxical effects. Null: none known. · Test: tonic-current change after transporter-class block in null versus control slices. A smaller change means that receptors, not GABA, limit the system. A bigger change means ambient GABA is low. · Ignored: the usual reading attributes tonic lowering to receptors, but astrocytic GABA handling decides what the receptors see.

H7. transfer · Enhancing fast-spiking capacity of PV interneurons (potassium channel subtype class that speeds repolarisation) would sustain high-frequency output and hence graded inhibition. · Evidence: in other models, fast-spiking channel dysfunction in PV cells is a recognised cause of seizures. Wwox-null PV output: unmeasured, as the question states. · Test: PV firing frequency-current curve and spike-failure rate under sustained drive in null versus control. Early spike failure means a PV-intrinsic lever. Normal firing with weak IPSCs means the lesion is downstream (synapse or postsynaptic). · Ignored: a phasic deficit may be postsynaptic, so PV output should be tested by presynaptic firing before any change is tied to the receptor side.

H8. transfer · Re-timing or stabilising perineuronal-net and extracellular-matrix structure around PV cells would change their excitability and synaptic strength. · Evidence: net remodelling alters PV function and seizure threshold in other models. Null: none known. · Test: net density (a lectin-based marker) around PV cells in null versus control at the age of onset. Reduced or delayed nets support a maturation lever. Normal nets close it. · Ignored: structural extracellular components regulate inhibitory output and are rarely in the receptor-centred picture.

H9. transfer · Lowering intrinsic excitability of principal cells (persistent sodium or HCN-type current classes) would widen the distance to burst threshold without touching inhibition. · Evidence: a standard mechanism in other epilepsy models. Null: none known. · Test: rheobase and persistent-current size in null versus control principal cells. Raised persistent current or lowered rheobase means a distance-to-threshold lever independent of GABA. Normal values mean the gap is all inhibitory. · Ignored: distance to burst threshold has two sides (inhibition and intrinsic excitability), and a graded-inhibition framing leaves out the second.

H10. transfer · Neuromodulatory recruitment of interneurons (cholinergic, serotonergic or noradrenergic receptor classes) could raise interneuron firing and graded output. · Evidence: neuromodulators shift inhibition-to-excitation balance in cortex and hippocampus in other systems. Null: none known. · Test: IPSC rate and amplitude in null principal cells after receptor-class activation, compared with control. Normal gain means the interneuron pool is recruitable and the deficit is a drive problem. Blunted gain means a receptor-side or PV ceiling. · Ignored: whether inhibition is lowered because it is under-driven versus under-responsive.

H11. WWOX observation · Correcting systemic hypoglycaemia (reported in Wwox-null mice, to my recollection) would raise interneuron energy supply, lessen metabolic-sensor channel opening, and restore phasic output. · Evidence: hypoglycaemia in Wwox knockouts is reported (my recollection, unverified here); a direct effect on inhibitory output is none known. · Test: clamp glucose (in vivo or in slice) and measure IPSCs and burst latency in null. Recovery means a metabolic confound explains a share of the phasic deficit. No recovery means the deficit is intrinsic. · Ignored: fast-spiking cells are among the most energy-demanding neurons, and a systemic metabolic defect can masquerade as a circuit defect.

H12. WWOX observation · Hypomyelination or poor myelination of PV axons (hypomyelination is reported in Wwox-deficient rodents, to my recollection) would slow or fail conduction and weaken phasic output. · Evidence: myelin deficits in Wwox mutants are reported; PV-axon-specific myelination is none known. · Test: measure IPSC latency and jitter evoked from PV-cell stimulation in null versus control. Longer latency or higher jitter supports a conduction lever. Normal values close it. · Ignored: white-matter and glial defects feed back into inhibitory timing, which a neuron-centred reading omits.

H13. assumption · Restoring the postsynaptic scaffold of inhibitory synapses (gephyrin-type clustering and its regulatory kinases) would raise receptor clustering and phasic IPSC size. · Evidence: WWOX is a WW-domain adaptor protein that binds PPxY partners, so a scaffold role is plausible; a specific link is none known. · Test: count and size of perisomatic scaffold puncta opposed to PV terminals in null versus control. Fewer or smaller puncta with normal presynaptic markers means a postsynaptic lever. Normal puncta means function, not structure, is lowered. · Ignored: a synapse can be present anatomically and weak functionally.

H14. transfer · Limiting bicarbonate-linked depolarisation and activity-driven chloride loading (carbonic-anhydrase class) would preserve hyperpolarising GABA during bursts. · Evidence: collapse of inhibition during intense activity is described in other systems, with bicarbonate and chloride accumulation as drivers. Null: none known. · Test: EGABA before and after a high-frequency train in null versus control, with and without the enzyme-class block. A faster collapse in null, relieved by block, supports the lever. Equal collapse closes it. · Ignored: inhibition measured at rest may hide a failure that appears only during sustained activity, which is when bursts begin.

H15. transfer · Preventing excess microglial or complement-linked removal of inhibitory synapses would preserve phasic input. · Evidence: microglial pruning of inhibitory terminals is described in other seizure and development models. Null: none known. · Test: count VGAT-positive puncta inside microglial lysosomes in null versus control hippocampus around the time of onset. A higher count means a glial lever. A normal count closes it. · Ignored: non-neuronal cells can erase inhibition while neurons look intact, and Gabrd per neuron would not reveal this.
