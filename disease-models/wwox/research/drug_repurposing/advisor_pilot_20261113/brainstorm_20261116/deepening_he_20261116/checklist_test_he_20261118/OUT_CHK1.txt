Closest downstream components that set the signal: cGMP phosphodiesterases (PDE5, PDE3, PDE2, PDE1, PDE10), the efflux pump MRP4/ABCC4, PKG2 (PRKG2), CFTR and NHE3.

STEP C. The defect is a gain of function: too much cGMP from GC-C. Entries that would correct it fall in two groups.
(a) Entries that lower input or catalytic output of the enzyme itself: ligand withdrawal, the ATP-KHD potentiation, the phosphorylation state, a dominant-negative or dilution effect, glycosylation/chaperone/trafficking, pH-dependent ligand selectivity, and Zn/Li/Mg-type metal effects (the last transferred by analogy from AC, IPOTESI).
(b) Entries that remove cGMP after it is made: PDE5/PDE2 hydrolysis and MRP4 efflux.
Entries that act downstream of cGMP or on the epithelium without changing cGMP could normalise the consequences without moving the second messenger: PKG2, CFTR/NHE3, the cAMP arm, cytokines, Notch/Atoh1 and microbiota/bile acids.
Entries that would not correct it: NO/CO, sGC stimulators, ATP raising, Mn2+, Gs-type activators, and any agent that raises cGMP or substitutes cations to raise activity.
An epistemic caution: I recall that GC-C loss, not gain, impairs the barrier in mice. A leaky barrier with cGMP excess is therefore either a direct overshoot of cGMP signalling or secondary to cytokines, secretory-cell loss or luminal fluid and bile load. The hypotheses below are built to separate these.
