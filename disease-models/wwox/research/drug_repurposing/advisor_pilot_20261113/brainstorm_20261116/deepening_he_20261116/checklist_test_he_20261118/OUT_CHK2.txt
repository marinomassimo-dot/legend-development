Several checklist rows are low-confidence or inferred from a neighbouring family, and I mark them as such.

FAMILY: The defective molecule is a membrane receptor guanylyl cyclase, GC-C (GUCY2C). Its ligands are guanylin, uroguanylin and the E. coli heat-stable enterotoxin STa, and its product is cGMP. The wider family is the particulate receptor guanylyl cyclases (GC-A/NPR1, GC-B/NPR2, GC-D, GC-E/F, GC-G). The closest relatives are:
1. Soluble guanylyl cyclase (sGC), which has the same class III nucleotidyl-cyclase catalytic fold but a heme H-NOX regulatory domain.
2. Transmembrane and soluble adenylyl cyclases (tmAC, sAC), which share the class III cyclase mechanism and fold.
3. Protein kinases and pseudokinases, because GC-C carries an ATP-binding kinase-homology domain (KHD) as its regulatory domain.

STEP C
The defect is a gain of function. A corrective lever must lower cGMP, or interrupt a step downstream of cGMP that damages the epithelium. Entries that would move in the corrective direction:
- Less ligand, or tuning the pH-dependent ligand potency.
- Interference with ATP or the phospho-state at the KHD.
- Fewer mutant receptors.
- More cGMP hydrolysis (PDE gain) or more export (MRP4 gain).
- Blockade of PKG2, CFTR or the cAMP cross-talk branch.
- Removal of the NO/sGC contribution.
- Bypass of the differentiation block (Atoh1 gain or Notch inhibition).
- Removal of secondary injury (microbiota, bile acids, cytokines).
Entries that would worsen the defect when applied to the mutant receptor:
- Agonists such as STa, linaclotide and plecanatide.
- PDE5 inhibitors and MRP4 inhibitors.
- NO donors and sGC stimulators.
- Mn2+ and ATP excess.
Entries that are not usable for the correction:
- Entries with no member data (most vitamins, metals and metabolites in the checklist).
- Entries known only for another family, where transfer to GC-C is IPOTESI. Examples are Cl-, GCAP/Ca2+ and bicarbonate.
Residual caveat: the barrier may be U-shaped in cGMP. As I recall, loss of GC-C signalling in mice also gives a leaky barrier. Over-correction is therefore possible, and I could not rank the effect sizes.

H1. Statement: the mutant receptor is hypersensitive to normal guanylin-peptide ligand, so removing ligand normalises cGMP and the epithelium.
Evidence: GC-C gain-of-function variants cause familial diarrhoea and an IBD-like phenotype in humans (recall of the case reports). Ligand dependence of the mutants is unknown to me.
Usual reading assumes: a gain-of-function variant is constitutive. This one changes that to a shifted ligand sensitivity.
Rejection: cGMP stays high in a Guca2a/Guca2b double-null background.
Discriminating manipulation: cross the mutant to Guca2a/Guca2b double knockout. Nearest rival is H3 (intrinsic ATP-dependent activation).
Predicts: cGMP normal and barrier normal if ligand-dependent. If constitutive, cGMP and barrier phenotype persist.

H2. Statement: the dominant ligand is pH-matched to the lumen, so selective loss of the acid-preferring uroguanylin or the alkaline-preferring guanylin lowers net drive in one gut segment.
Evidence: pH-dependent potency of the two peptides (recall). Segmental rescue is none known.
Usual reading assumes: the two peptides are interchangeable. This one changes that to segment-specific drive.
Rejection: both single knockouts leave cGMP unchanged in the segment where it is high.
Discriminating manipulation: Guca2a-only versus Guca2b-only deletion in the mutant. Nearest rival is H1.
Predicts: segment-specific fall in cGMP and a segment-specific barrier improvement. The unaffected segment stays abnormal.

H3. Statement: the variant makes receptor activity depend on ATP binding at the kinase-homology domain, so disabling ATP binding cuts cGMP.
Evidence: ATP potentiates ligand-stimulated GC-C and GC-A through the KHD (recall). Not tested for the variant.
Usual reading assumes: the variant sits in the catalytic domain and acts independently. This one changes that to dependence on KHD allostery.
Rejection: an ATP-binding-deficient KHD in cis leaves cGMP unchanged.
Discriminating manipulation: ATP-site-disabled knock-in in cis with the variant. Nearest rival is H4.
Predicts: cGMP falls under ligand-saturating and ligand-free conditions, and the barrier recovers.

H4. Statement: the variant keeps the KHD in a hyper-phosphorylated state, so a phospho-null KHD or phosphatase gain lowers cGMP.
Evidence: KHD phosphorylation is needed for ligand activation (recall, low confidence). Not tested.
Usual reading assumes: activity is set by the ligand. This one changes that to a phospho-state set point.
Rejection: phospho-null KHD leaves cGMP unchanged and ligand response intact.
Discriminating manipulation: phosphosite alanine knock-in in cis with the variant. Nearest rival is H3, which keeps the phospho-state and removes ATP binding.
Predicts: cGMP falls with preserved ATP binding. The barrier improves in step with cGMP.

H5. Statement: the mutant receptor acts partly in trans through oligomers with the wild-type allele, so lowering mutant dose lowers cGMP more than proportionally.
Evidence: none known for this variant.
Usual reading assumes: one mutant allele contributes additively. This one changes that to a shared-complex effect.
Rejection: cGMP in mutant/null mice is half that of mutant/wild-type.
Discriminating manipulation: compare mutant/wild-type with mutant/null (hemizygous), or allele-specific silencing. Nearest rival is H1.
Predicts: cGMP below half in mutant/null if cooperative. Barrier follows cGMP.

H6. Statement: raising epithelial PDE5 or PDE2 capacity removes the excess cGMP.
Evidence: PKG feedback activates PDE5. Gain of PDE in this model is none known.
Usual reading assumes: the receptor is the only control point. This one changes that to degradation capacity.
Rejection: Pde5a/Pde2a overexpression leaves cGMP and barrier abnormal.
Discriminating manipulation: epithelial Pde5a or Pde2a gain in the mutant. Nearest rival is H7 (export).
Predicts: tissue cGMP falls, and so does the effluxed cGMP. Barrier recovers if cGMP-driven.

H7. Statement: raising MRP4/ABCC4 export removes cGMP from the cell.
Evidence: MRP4 exports cGMP (recall). Not tested for this model.
Usual reading assumes: intracellular cGMP reflects synthesis alone. This one changes that to include export.
Rejection: Abcc4 loss does not raise intracellular cGMP in the mutant.
Discriminating manipulation: Abcc4 deletion versus gain in the mutant. Nearest rival is H6.
Predicts: Abcc4 loss raises intracellular cGMP and lowers extracellular cGMP, and worsens the barrier. PDE-based rescue should be unaffected by it.

H8. Statement: the epithelial damage runs through PKG2, so deleting it normalises differentiation and barrier without lowering cGMP.
Evidence: PKG2 is the effector that phosphorylates CFTR. Its role in differentiation here is none known.
Usual reading assumes: the damage is a consequence of the cGMP level. This one changes that to a PKG2-specific effector.
Rejection: Prkg2 deletion leaves the barrier and secretory cells abnormal.
Discriminating manipulation: epithelial Prkg2 deletion in the mutant. Nearest rival is H12 (lineage bypass).
Predicts: cGMP unchanged or higher; differentiation, barrier and cytokines improve.

H9. Statement: the barrier injury is secondary to CFTR-driven fluid secretion, not to cGMP-driven junction signalling.
Evidence: none known for this model.
Usual reading assumes: cGMP acts on junctions directly. This one changes that to a flux effect.
Rejection: Cftr deletion leaves the barrier abnormal.
Discriminating manipulation: Cftr deletion or a CFTR-gating inhibitor class in the mutant. Nearest rival is H15 (bile-acid injury).
Predicts: cGMP unchanged; luminal fluid falls, and the barrier improves only if flux-driven.

H10. Statement: cGMP spills over into cAMP by inhibiting PDE3, and the cAMP arm is what harms the epithelium.
Evidence: cGMP inhibits PDE3 (established pharmacology). Its role here is none known.
Usual reading assumes: the cGMP-PKG axis is the only axis. This one changes that to cAMP crosstalk.
Rejection: epithelial PKA-catalytic deletion or PDE3 gain leaves the barrier abnormal.
Discriminating manipulation: epithelial PKA loss in the mutant. Nearest rival is H8.
Predicts: cGMP unchanged, cAMP falls, barrier improves.

H11. Statement: inflammation-induced NO drives sGC and adds to epithelial cGMP.
Evidence: NO strongly activates sGC; iNOS is raised in colitis (recall). Not tested here.
Usual reading assumes: all cGMP comes from the receptor. This one changes that to include sGC.
Rejection: Nos2 loss or sGC beta-1 deletion does not change cGMP.
Discriminating manipulation: Nos2 deletion or epithelial Gucy1b1 deletion in the mutant. Nearest rival is H14 (cytokine loop).
Predicts: partial fall in cGMP, and a barrier gain if NO damage contributes.

H12. Statement: the secretory-lineage defect can be bypassed, which would restore mucus and antimicrobial defence.
Evidence: none known for this model.
Usual reading assumes: the lineage block and the cGMP signal must be fixed together. This one changes that to a bypass that leaves the signal unchanged.
Rejection: Atoh1 gain (or Notch inhibition) fails to restore goblet and Paneth cells.
Discriminating manipulation: epithelial Atoh1 overexpression in the mutant. Nearest rival is H8, which removes the signal rather than bypassing it.
Predicts: cGMP unchanged, lineage rescued, barrier improved only if the leak follows secretory deficiency.

H13. Statement: the leak and the cytokines are driven by microbial encroachment through a thin mucus layer.
Evidence: none known for this model.
Usual reading assumes: the cytokines are epithelial-intrinsic. This one changes that to microbe-driven.
Rejection: germ-free mutant mice keep their leak and cytokines.
Discriminating manipulation: germ-free or antibiotic-treated mutant mice. Nearest rival is H14.
Predicts: cGMP unchanged, barrier and cytokines normalise.

H14. Statement: a cytokine feed-forward loop (TNF, IL-1) sustains the damage once started.
Evidence: cytokines lower guanylin-peptide expression in colitis (recall). Not tested here.
Usual reading assumes: the cytokines are an outcome only. This one changes that to a driver.
Rejection: TNF/IL-1 blockade does not change the barrier.
Discriminating manipulation: Tnf or Il1b deletion, or a blocking-antibody class, in the mutant. Nearest rival is H13.
Predicts: cGMP at receptor level unchanged, barrier improves.

H15. Statement: bile-acid load from the extra luminal flux damages the epithelium.
Evidence: none known for this model.
Usual reading assumes: fluid loss is harmless to the epithelium. This one changes that to a bile-acid injury.
Rejection: Cyp7a1 deletion or a sequestrant class leaves the barrier abnormal.
Discriminating manipulation: Cyp7a1 deletion in the mutant. Nearest rival is H9, which blocks the fluid itself.
Predicts: cGMP unchanged, fluid output unchanged, barrier improves.

Not selected, for lack of recalled evidence or for being redundant with the above: Cl- binding to the extracellular domain (known for GC-A, not GC-C), IMPDH and GTP-pool depletion, and NHE3 restoration.
