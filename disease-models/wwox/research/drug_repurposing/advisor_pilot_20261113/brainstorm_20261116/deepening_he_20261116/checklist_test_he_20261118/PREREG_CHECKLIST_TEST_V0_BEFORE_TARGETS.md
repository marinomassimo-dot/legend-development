# Stage-1-only checklist test: preregistration v0, written BEFORE any target is chosen and before any run (HE; 2026-11-18; non-canonical; not medical advice)

Authority: ADV's host decision (message 2026-11-17, after KDS closed): GO for the small test (option b of the decision page), cap 250,000 tokens including sourcing (an ESCALATION line before any overrun), at most two delegates at once, stop on any usage-limit notice, delegates read back (model and effort) every time, the full 1.35M v4 test stays closed, the operator can stop it with one word. Not an improvement claim of any kind: a stage-1 cue test.
Frozen BEFORE any target: `CHECKLIST_ITEMS_FIXED.txt` (77 items, sha256 a4d70b5e272e01d8...; generic categories of a physiology and biochemistry text; the same for every target) and `CHK_S1_TEMPLATE.txt` (sha256 7492a446f9b3f81f...; the family-first stage-1 template of the FF step plus one step A2 that makes the model go through the items one at a time). The only text that changes between targets is the question.

## What is tested
The diagnosis so far: the model knows a modulator when asked about it and does not volunteer it from the defect (T7, zinc and a receptor guanylyl cyclase: 0 of 4 uncued runs, 0 of 2 family-first runs; named in the one run where it was in the input). Hypothesis: a step that makes the model answer item by item ("does this item modulate any member of the defective family or its closest relatives, with what direction") makes it state a modulator it would not list from the defect. Not tested: whether the step improves final hypotheses, stage 2, or anything for a lever that is a drug or a gene (not an item of the list).

## Targets (not yet chosen)
Chosen by the standing target-selection rule (`engine_eval/ENGINE_EVAL_DESIGN.md`): contamination counts at the cut for PMID, first author and distinctive terms written beside the claim; publication date AND corpus presence stated; a memory probe (a tool-free delegate asked directly) when within two months of the model cutoff; atoms from the whole abstract and the open full text; strict and loose rules registered before any run; question-leak audit (`third_set/LEAK_AUDIT_PROCEDURE.md`); one target at least with a ONE-outcome headline. Additional criteria set now for this test: published after 2026-06-01; not T1 to T9; the lever is ONE specific ion, cofactor, condition or endogenous small molecule that is an item of (or a member named by an item of) the fixed list, acting on a defective molecule with identifiable related families; I state in the freeze for each target whether its atom is a list item (then class-level credit is void and only the specific member with its direction counts). Selection stops at two passing targets or at about 100,000 tokens of sourcing, whichever comes first; if fewer than two pass, I report that with the yield and run only what passes, or decline, and I do not lower the audit.
Targets are never used to change the checklist or the template; a target found unsuitable is dropped with the reason.

## Runs
Per passing target, two runs with the composed prompt (template plus the audited question), each a fresh tool-free delegate (`legend-deepdive`, sonnet, effort high asked; model and effort read back from each transcript; path audit that no file was opened), at most two at once. Expected cost: about 35,000 tokens per run, 4 runs about 140,000. No stage 2.

## Scoring (registered now; terms for each atom registered at the freeze of its target, before its runs)
STRICT: the atom's specific member is named as a modulator in the CHECKLIST table with the right direction for the defective family or a related family AND is carried into at least one of the 15 hypotheses as a lever in the corrective direction. LOOSE: named as a modulator with its direction anywhere (CHECKLIST table or MODULATORS table). Mechanical presence by preregistered term lists, plus a read of the sentence; a second, blind read by a tool-free delegate on the shuffled outputs only if budget allows. Reported per target and run. Also reported: the number of checklist items answered 'yes' per run (noise), and whether the atom item was answered 'not known'.
A target is 'reached' if at least one of its two runs is STRICT; 'reached loosely' if LOOSE.

## Expectations (committed now)
P(at least one of the four runs names the atom, LOOSE) = 0.30 (ADV's and my number; history 0 of 4 uncued, 0 of 2 family-first). P(STRICT in at least one run) = 0.20. P(both targets reached STRICT) = 0.06. P(the atom item answered 'not known' in every run of a target) = 0.55. P(the model marks 25 or more of the 77 items 'yes' in a run, i.e. noise) = 0.60. The expected result is 'the checklist does not help'; written before the run so that a null is not read as a surprise.
SCI's hashed forecast (number of the four runs she expects to name the atom, her own words) is requested before the runs and scored by her.

## Decision rule and what the result would change
Reached STRICT on at least one target whose atom is NOT a repeat of zinc: the item-by-item step is a candidate stage-1 step worth a second, larger test (more targets, with stage 2) and goes to the operator's budget decision; reached on none: the stage-1 cue branch (enumeration, family-first, checklist) is closed for this generator and the remaining gap is retrieval of a member not in view, to be handled upstream (a different generator input, not a prompt); reached LOOSE only: the model states the modulator but does not carry it into hypotheses, which moves the next change to the STEP C and D wording. One test, four runs: nothing here is a rate.
Limits: two targets chosen by me from PubMed with the audits that reduce but do not remove selection bias; the list is itself a lever class named in the prompt; one model and one delegate type; atoms that are drugs or genes are outside the list's scope by design.
