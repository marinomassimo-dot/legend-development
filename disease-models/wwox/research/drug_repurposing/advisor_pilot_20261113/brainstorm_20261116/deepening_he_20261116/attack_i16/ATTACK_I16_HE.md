## Answer first
1. **Re-read against the library-wide shift, the K+ markers are smaller than the raw ratios say, and the neocortex-against-hippocampus asymmetry disappears for the pump and GLT-1.** SCI's ratios (`regional_astro.tsv`) are raw knockout over control TPM. The IT24 analysis of the same tables removes a library-wide median shift (`mouse_p17_summary.json`: cortex S −0.202 log2, hippocampus S −0.389 log2); the centring reproduces the IT24 row for Gfap in the hippocampus (raw 4.34, +2.51 centred). Centred: cortex Atp1a2 −0.51, Kcnj10 −0.53, Slc1a2 −0.44 (0.70 to 0.74 of control, not 0.60 to 0.64); Gja1 0.00, Aqp4 −0.10, Slc1a3 +0.08. Hippocampus: Atp1a2 −0.53, Slc1a2 −0.40, Gja1 −0.42, Slc12a2 −0.76, but Kcnj10 +0.60 and Aqp4 +0.77. So for Atp1a2 and Slc1a2 the hippocampus is as deficient as the neocortex (−0.53 against −0.51; −0.40 against −0.44), and Gfap is higher there (+2.51 against +1.05, 4.3-fold against 1.8-fold): by the dossier's own markers the region that does not burst is not better protected. **The one asymmetric marker is Kir4.1 (Kcnj10: cortex −0.53, hippocampus +0.60), S line only, and not reproduced in P47T (−0.06).** D1 step 5 ("hippocampus differs, consistent with no bursting") therefore rests on one marker in one line. HELD (SCI's table, IT24 summary), arithmetic by me.
2. **The cortical fall is astrocyte-class-wide, not clearance-specific.** Among the descriptive rows of the cortex S contrast (|centred log2| ≥ 0.585, animal-separated, TPM ≥ 1: absence from them is not "unchanged"), 101 of 113 astrocyte-enriched genes (class table GSE52564: astro/neuron FPKM ≥ 3, larger ≥ 5; 1,232 such genes) fall, median −0.81, against 45 down and 35 up among 80 neuron-enriched genes (a filter that treats both classes alike). The K+ trio is three of many falling astrocyte genes, and the same cortex shows Gfap up. The hippocampal S contrast falls for both classes (113 of 147 astrocyte-enriched, 145 of 185 neuron-enriched), so that region's markers are not astrocyte-specific at all. Descriptive; the counts are of filtered rows, not rates.
3. **Best rival:** an astrocyte state or maturation change that lowers many astrocyte genes at once and happens to include Kir4.1, the pump and GLT-1, rather than a loss of K+ clearance capacity. It is also what SCI's own seed 2 (maturation lag) and the held Gfap rise would predict. **Separating experiment:** the Ba2+-sensitive astrocytic current and the extracellular K+ transient (SCI's D5b) measured in the same slices as a panel of astrocyte transcripts or proteins that are not clearance genes (for example Aldh1l1, Glul, S100b, Slc1a3, Aqp4). Outcomes: clearance lower and graded (WT > HT > KO) while the non-clearance panel is flat: buffering specifically; clearance equal to wild type while the transcripts fall: a class-wide state change, I16 killed for incidence; both lower and the blocker does not shift the I15 threshold: present, not sufficient.
4. **The "neuron-specific" wording is itself a test:** SCI's pool shows Wwox-positive astrocyte-like nuclei 28.1% to 3.3% in the S line (one pool). If the Synapsin-Cre also removes Wwox in astrocytes at this age, a cell-autonomous astroglial deficit is possible and the cheapest check is a recombination reporter or Wwox protein in astrocytes of the S line; if not, the astroglial change is secondary and the rival in point 3 gains weight.
5. **Model reading, accepted:** K+ clearance is a weak knob (R1 fails; R2 and R3 fail): consistent with points 1 to 3; the idea has no model-level support for the incidence gradient, and the data-level support is weaker than the raw ratios suggest.
