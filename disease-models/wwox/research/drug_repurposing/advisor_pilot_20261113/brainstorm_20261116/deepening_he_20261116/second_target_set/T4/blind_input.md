# Sixty hypotheses (statement and discriminating manipulation only)

## Z01
Statement: A ligand-type stabiliser of the mutant subunit (the pharmacological-chaperone class), or global proteostasis capacity, raises the functional mutant pool when the lesion is folding or turnover. The ligand class stabilises by occlusion, which can raise abundance while abolishing function.
Discriminating manipulation (vs Ric-8 gain): A direct small-molecule folding-stabiliser class applied with Ric-8 level held constant, compared with neuronal Ric-8 gain on the same mutant. - Motor and seizures: a partial benefit to both appears only in low-abundance mutants. An occluding stabiliser raises abundance but leaves motor and seizures unchanged. Ric-8 gain predicts a similar direction with a lesser seizure effect.

## Z02
Statement: Raising wild-type GNAO1 dosage in neurons, by gene addition without removing the mutant, dilutes or outcompetes the mutant subunit and restores enough total functional Gαo, provided the mutant is not a strong dominant-negative.
Discriminating manipulation: Neuron-restricted wild-type addition versus allele-selective knockdown (H1). Predicted motor: modest improvement in loss-of-function-like alleles, little or none in dominant-negative alleles. Predicted seizures: improve in loss-of-function-like alleles, with no change or a worsening in dominant-negative or gain-of-function-like alleles. In WWOX the equivalent rescue repaired seizures (SWD/ECoG) and motor function together (mechanism_intervention_map.md:174-176), so the corpus precedent leans toward both improving.

## Z03
Statement: If the mutant has slow GDP release or a nucleotide-binding defect, raising the guanine-nucleotide-exchange input (receptor-driven flux or a Ric-8A-type chaperone/GEF) restores activation.
Discriminating manipulation vs H4: a Gi/o-coupled receptor agonist class, or Ric-8A gain-of-function, in the knock-in. Prediction under H5: the Gαo-dependent readout rises, motor improves and seizures fall (the exchange-limited subset only). Under H4 the same drive worsens motor function and may lower the seizure threshold, because GTP-locked signalling is amplified.

## Z04
Statement: The seizure arm comes from Gαo dysfunction in GABAergic interneurons (PV/SST), so rescuing the subunit there raises seizure threshold but leaves the motor phenotype unchanged.
Discriminating manipulation: PV/SST-restricted rescue versus excitatory-neuron-restricted rescue (nearest rival, same circuit). Predicted seizures: decrease with the interneuron-restricted arm. Predicted motor: unchanged. If excitatory-neuron rescue also lowers seizures, the interneuron specificity is not supported.

## Z05
Statement: A mutant alpha that is stable but has altered nucleotide handling (slow GDP release, faster GTP hydrolysis, or impaired receptor coupling) can be restored by a ligand or genetic change that acts on the subunit's activation cycle (guanine-nucleotide exchange or GTPase tuning), without changing its abundance.
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H1): Over-express an accessory GEF-class protein, or the cognate receptor, in the mutant neuron. This raises the switch rate without changing protein level. Predicted: the receptor-evoked effector deficit is corrected and motor performance improves, since a lever that fits only the cycle is function-specific. Seizure effect is smaller or absent if the epileptic arm is carried by a different compartment (see H5).

## Z06
Statement: Removing the mutant transcript while leaving the wild-type allele restores function if the mutant acts dominantly (gain-of-function or dominant-negative) over a wild-type allele that is already sufficient.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H2, wild-type addition): Allele-specific mutant knockdown with no wild-type added. - Motor and seizures: if the mutant is dominant, both improve. If it is pure loss of function, motor and seizures are unchanged or move toward the knockout state, because the dose falls.

## Z07
Statement: Seizures arise mainly from GABAergic interneuron dysfunction, so restoring the subunit in interneurons controls seizures while leaving movement.
Discriminating manipulation (vs excitatory-neuron restoration, e.g. CaMKIIα- or Emx1-driven): Interneuron-restricted restoration. - Seizures: fewer, only if the interneuron arm is causal. Motor function: unchanged. Opposite predictions for excitatory-neuron restoration.

## Z08
Statement: Raising wild-type subunit dose in neurons, by gene addition or transcriptional up-regulation, restores function when the mutant is hypomorphic rather than interfering, and rescue is threshold-like rather than graded.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): Add wild-type subunit and leave the mutant in place. - Motor and seizures: both improve only above a threshold, and only if the mutant is a loss-of-function type. There is no benefit, or worsening, if the mutant is dominant-negative.

## Z09
Statement: The movement phenotype originates in the Gαo-dependent signalling of indirect-pathway (D2) striatal projection neurons, so restoring mutant-subunit function only in that cell type is sufficient for motor rescue.
Discriminating manipulation: Cre-restricted wild-type or mutant-corrected expression in D2 versus D1 striatal neurons (nearest rival: cholinergic interneuron or M4 route, a rival). Predicted motor: improves with D2-restricted rescue only. Predicted seizures: unchanged, because the striatum is not the main generator.

## Z10
Statement: The deficit is an ongoing physiological loss of signalling, not a developmental scar, so acute re-engagement of the existing mutant pathway (receptor-ligand tuning at the same GPCR) gives immediate benefit in adults.
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H9): In adult mutants, acutely engage or antagonise the cognate GPCR (agonist/antagonist class, mechanism level) with a washout, and read the slice effector current and open-field/rotarod, plus EEG, in the same animals. Predicted: if H10 is right, motor function and seizure burden shift reversibly within the assay window and return on washout. If H9 is right, there is no durable change.

## Z11
Statement: The movement phenotype and the seizure phenotype come from different cell types, and restoring function of the mutant subunit in the movement-circuit population (striatal projection neurons, for example) rescues motor function without rescuing seizures.
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H5, interneuron-driven seizures): Restore function by a conditional Cre in striatal or cerebellar projection populations only. Predicted: motor function improves; seizure count and electrographic activity stay unchanged.

## Z12
Statement: The syndrome has a developmental window, and restoring subunit function early (neonatal) improves motor development and seizures, whereas late restoration relieves only the acute seizure and movement features, not the developmental trajectory.
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H10, an ongoing-function deficit): A reversible genetic switch (tamoxifen-class or doxycycline-class control, genetic) turning the corrected subunit on at three ages, with a seizure-matched anti-seizure comparator arm (per E-3). Predicted: early restoration improves motor and development, late restoration improves seizures and mild motor features; the comparator arm shows seizure control without motor or developmental gain.

## Z13
Statement: Bypassing Gαo by activating GIRK channels (the Gβγ output) restores neuronal inhibition.
Discriminator vs H8: Overexpress GIRK2 in forebrain inhibitory interneurons. If H7 holds, seizures fall, with a smaller change in motor function. If H8 holds, GIRK activation does nothing for presynaptic release.

## Z14
Statement: Enhancing Ric-8B-assisted folding and stabilisation rescues a mutant subunit that is intrinsically active but lost to misfolding, and only the active-but-unstable variants respond.
Discriminating manipulation: Ric-8B gain versus enhanced clearance of the mutant (proteasomal or autophagic routes), the nearest rival. Predicted motor: improves only for unstable variants. Predicted seizures: improve only in loss-of-function-type unstable variants. Clearance would instead help dominant-negative alleles and harm unstable ones.

## Z15
Statement: A developmental window exists in which restoring the mutant subunit rescues both motor function and seizures, and later restoration rescues only the part that is a functional signalling defect.
Discriminating manipulation: Timed (tamoxifen- or doxycycline-inducible) restoration at P0 versus in adulthood, compared in the same allele; the nearest rival is the "acute-only" reading of H5. Predicted motor: improves at both ages, more at P0 if circuit structure is part of the phenotype. Predicted seizures: improve at P0, with a smaller or absent improvement in adulthood.

## Z16
Statement: The motor phenotype comes from Gαo loss of function in striatal cholinergic interneurons or indirect-pathway neurons, while seizures arise from cortical or hippocampal excitatory neurons, so rescuing each cell type separates the two phenotypes.
Discriminating manipulation: ChAT/Drd2-Cre rescue versus Emx1-Cre (pallial excitatory) rescue. Prediction: the striatal rescue improves motor function and does not reduce seizures, while the Emx1 rescue reduces seizures with little motor benefit.

## Z17
Statement: Loss of Gαo-mediated inhibition of adenylyl cyclase raises cAMP/PKA tone in striatal indirect-pathway neurons, driving the hyperkinetic phenotype, and lowering that tone (AC5 or PKA inhibition, or a cAMP-degradation class) bypasses the mutant.
Discriminating manipulation vs H6/H7 (channel effectors): conditional Adcy5 deletion or PKA inhibition restricted to indirect-pathway spiny neurons. Prediction under H8: motor improves clearly and seizures are unchanged, because the manipulation does not reach cortical interneurons. Channel-level manipulations (H6, H7) improve seizures. This dissociation is the discriminator.

## Z18
Statement: If the mutant is hydrolysis-deficient (locked GTP-bound, signalling too long or toward the wrong effector), increasing GTPase-accelerating activity (RGS or RGS7–Gβ5-type complexes) normalizes signal duration.
Discriminating manipulation vs H5 (GEF/receptor drive): neuron-restricted RGS gain-of-function. Prediction under H4: motor improves (hyperkinetic output falls) and seizures fall. If the mutant is instead exchange-deficient (H5), the same manipulation is neutral or worsens motor function, because more GAP shortens already weak signalling.

## Z19
Statement: In a heterozygous patient-class genotype, raising output of the wild-type Gαo allele (neuron-restricted gene addition or transcriptional activation of the endogenous allele) restores net signalling by outcompeting a loss-of-function or weakly dominant-negative mutant subunit.
Discriminating manipulation vs H2 (nearest rival, mutant-allele silencing): pan-Gnao1 knockdown (not allele-selective) in the heterozygote. Prediction if H1 is right: motor function worsens and seizures worsen or appear, because total dose falls. If H2 is right (toxic gain), pan-knockdown is neutral or mildly improves. Under H1, motor and seizures both improve with wild-type-allele upregulation.

## Z20
Statement: The decisive effector lesion is postsynaptic. Loss of Gβγ-driven GIRK hyperpolarization raises excitability in cortical and striatal neurons, and a GIRK-activating class restores it.
Discriminating manipulation vs H6: GIRK-activator class in the knock-in, versus Cav2 blockade. Prediction under H7: seizure threshold rises sharply and motor function improves modestly. Under H6 the reverse holds: motor improves, with seizures less affected.

## Z21
Statement: The restorable function is neuron-intrinsic. Subunit restoration in neurons alone is sufficient for motor and seizure endpoints, while glial or oligodendrocyte restoration adds nothing for these two endpoints.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H6-H8, a subtype-specific arm): Pan-neuronal restoration (synapsin-driven) versus pan-neuronal plus astrocyte (GFAP-driven) versus astrocyte-only. - Motor and seizures: pan-neuronal restoration gives most of the benefit for both. Astrocyte-only gives none, or a minor seizure effect.

## Z22
Statement: The mutant is misfolded or unstable and is cleared, so enhancing the Ric-8B (GEF/chaperone) pathway or applying a chemical-chaperone or proteostasis class restores the amount of functional mutant.
Discriminating manipulation: Ric-8B (or proteostasis) upregulation versus membrane-lipidation enhancement (H4). Prediction: for a destabilised mutant, motor function and seizures both improve. For a lipidation-deficient mutant, neither improves.

## Z23
Statement: In gain-of-function variants (slow GTP hydrolysis, constitutive GTP-bound state), increasing GTPase-accelerating activity (RGS, R7-family RGS7/Gβ5 or RGS4/RGS9-2) restores normal signal termination.
Discriminating manipulation: RGS overexpression versus allele-specific knockdown (H1) on a gain-of-function variant. Prediction: motor function improves, and seizures change little because gain-of-function variants are less epileptogenic.

## Z24
Statement: Rescuability is gated by a developmental window. Restoring or stabilising the subunit in the early postnatal window rescues motor circuitry and seizures, while the same restoration after circuit assembly rescues seizures and network state but only part of motor function.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H2, same lever at a fixed timepoint): The same restoration lever given at P0-P5-equivalent versus after weaning (inducible Cre, or a timed vector). - Seizures: reduced at both times. Motor: complete rescue early, only partial late. Caveat: held seizure phenotypes are rat-specific (claim_registry_current.md:682, CLAIM 037) and are explicitly absent in mouse, so a seizure endpoint needs a model that seizes.

## Z25
Statement: Limiting presynaptic Cav2 (N-type and P/Q-type) calcium entry restores function, because a failed Gαo brake leaves release excessive.
Discriminator vs H7: Block Cav2.2 selectively in presynaptic terminals (conditional deletion) while leaving GIRK untouched. If H8 holds, seizures and chorea-like motor signs both improve. If H7 holds, only the postsynaptic-inhibition-related component improves.

## Z26
Statement: Paralogous Gαi/Gαz subunits can partially take over the dysfunctional Gαo role at shared GPCRs, so raising Gαi expression compensates.
Discriminating manipulation: Gαi2/Gαi1 overexpression versus wild-type Gαo supplementation (H2). Prediction: partial rescue of motor function and weaker seizure benefit, because Gαo-specific roles in the presynapse remain. It fails if a dominant-negative mutant sequesters the shared Gβγ or receptors (H1).

## Z27
Statement: Mutant Gαo traps Gβγ or receptors in non-productive heterotrimers, so supplying free Gβγ, or displacing the mutant from the heterotrimer, restores effector coupling without repairing the subunit itself.
Discriminating manipulation: Neuronal Gβ1γ2 gain versus wild-type Gαo augmentation (H2). Predicted motor: smaller improvement than with H2. Predicted seizures: improvement, because effector-driven channel modulation is the most direct seizure-relevant readout. A worsening would signal that excess free Gβγ has its own effect on excitability.

## Z28
Statement: Seizures and the movement disorder come from different cells. Restoring Gαo function in inhibitory interneurons rescues seizures, and restoring it in striatal spiny neurons (or cerebellar Purkinje cells) rescues movement, so the two phenotypes are dissociable.
Discriminating manipulation vs H8 (cAMP axis, not cell-type): interneuron-restricted (Dlx-Cre) restoration versus spiny-neuron-restricted (Drd2-Cre) restoration. Prediction under H9: interneuron restoration reduces seizures with unchanged motor function, and spiny-neuron restoration improves motor function with seizures unchanged. Note the folder's species caution (meta_gaba_paradox_current.md, provenance correction): seizures were a rat phenotype and absent in Wwox-null mice, so seizure readouts in a mouse model may be unavailable and the seizure arm may not be testable there.

## Z29
Statement: Striatal cholinergic interneurons lose Gαo-coupled muscarinic autoreceptor braking, so acetylcholine is in excess. Restoring subunit function there, or restoring the braking from outside the cell, corrects dystonia and chorea.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H6): Cholinergic-interneuron-restricted restoration (ChAT-Cre), or an M4-selective positive allosteric modulator class. - Motor: improvement, larger than with D2-restricted restoration only if the cholinergic arm is the driver. Seizures: unchanged.

## Z30
Statement: The phenotype includes a downstream cAMP/PKA over-activation that is mutant-subunit-specific, so correcting the effector side (adenylyl cyclase or PKA tone) without touching the subunit compensates for the lost Gαo/i-type brake.
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H2): Lower adenylyl-cyclase or PKA tone (small-molecule class, mechanism level) in mutant versus wild-type neurons, with the cAMP-reporter readout. Predicted: motor function improves in mutants disproportionately to wild type (a true interaction); seizures improve only if the epileptogenic arm is also a cAMP arm; otherwise it is a general anticonvulsant effect and not a rescue, as the folder found for lithium.

## Z31
Statement: Loss of Gαo inhibition of adenylyl cyclase (AC5) and PKA is the effector bottleneck for movement, so reducing AC5-PKA output bypasses the mutant subunit.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H6, GIRK/Gβγ arm: opening GIRK channels, a Gβγ effector, with no change in AC5-PKA output): An AC5-inhibitor class, or AC5 knockdown restricted to striatal neurons. - Motor: improvement. Seizures: a variable effect, with no seizure benefit if the seizure arm runs through Gβγ-gated channels.

## Z32
Statement: Raising wild-type Gαo (gene addition or upregulation of the normal allele) restores function by out-competing a mutant that sequesters Gβγ, receptors or GAP/GEF partners.
Discriminator vs H2: Add wild-type Gαo in forebrain GABAergic neurons without lowering the mutant. Predicts partial seizure rescue and little motor rescue if the dominant-negative effect is in the striatum. If the lever is true, both outcomes improve with dose-matched wild-type, and removing the mutant (H2) adds nothing beyond that.

## Z33
Statement: Amplifying upstream Gi/o-coupled receptors (M4 muscarinic, D2, GABA-B, α2 classes) pushes residual mutant signalling over threshold.
Discriminator vs H7: Apply an M4-class potentiator in cholinergic-receptor-expressing medium spiny neurons only. If H6 holds, motor function improves and seizures are unchanged unless the receptor is in cortex. If H7 holds, a direct effector agonist works while the receptor class does not.

## Z34
Statement: Gβγ release and effector coupling (GIRK, N-type/P-type Ca channels, GRK2-class interactions) is the limiting arm: the mutant subunit holds Gβγ too tightly or fails to release it, so tuning Gβγ-effector coupling restores signalling.
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H7, alpha-arm cAMP): Sequester free βγ (a βγ-binding scavenger, genetic or peptide class) in mutant neurons. Predicted: if H8 is right, effector currents and seizures change (excitability and GIRK-dependent brake), with a smaller motor effect; the cAMP-lowering manipulation of H7 should instead move motor more than seizures.

## Z35
Statement: The excitatory-projection compartment (cortical pyramidal or hippocampal principal neurons) bears the seizure phenotype, since mutant-subunit loss raises their excitability (reduced Gi/o-type brake on cAMP and channel gating), so restoring the subunit there fixes seizures and leaves motor function.
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H5): Restore the subunit in excitatory principal neurons only. Predicted: seizure/burst endpoints fall; motor function unchanged. If interneuron-only gives the opposite split, H5 and H6 both survive as separate compartments.

## Z36
Statement: Seizures arise chiefly from lost inhibitory (interneuron) function of the mutant subunit, so restoring the subunit specifically in inhibitory interneurons relieves seizures more than motor signs.
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H4): Restore (or delete) the subunit in GABAergic interneurons only, with whole-cell inhibitory-current readout (not marker counts). Predicted: seizure threshold and spike burden improve (or worsen on deletion); motor function changes little.

## Z37
Statement: Raising GTPase-accelerating activity (RGS family, including RGS7/9 with Gβ5) in the affected neurons shortens the GTP-bound lifetime of a slowly hydrolysing mutant and re-times its signal.
Discriminating manipulation: Neuron-type-restricted RGS (RGS9-2 or RGS7/Gβ5) gain versus the same step applied to a fast-hydrolysis allele, which is the nearest rival and acts as an internal control. Predicted motor: improvement where the variant has slow GTP hydrolysis and striatal signalling is prolonged, with worsening in a fast-hydrolysis allele. Predicted seizures: little change unless RGS is raised in forebrain neurons, and then a possible improvement in gain-of-function-like alleles.

## Z38
Statement: Loss of Gαo-released Gβγ inhibition of presynaptic Cav2 channels and of GIRK activation causes hyperexcitability, so reducing presynaptic release (Cav2.2/α2δ class) or boosting GIRK/Kir3 compensates.
Discriminating manipulation: α2δ ligand/Cav2.2 reduction (or GIRK activation) versus Adcy5 lowering (H8). Prediction: seizures decrease, with only a partial effect on motor function.

## Z39
Statement: Selective lowering of the mutant allele's product (allele-specific knockdown) restores function because a toxic gain of function or dominant-negative effect is the main driver.
Discriminator vs H1: Compare mutant-allele knockdown with a non-selective total Gαo knockdown in the same cell type. If H2 holds, selective knockdown rescues motor function and seizures while total knockdown worsens both. If H1 holds, total knockdown is no worse than selective knockdown. A gain-of-function mutant would be rescued for motor function and seizures by the selective approach.

## Z40
Statement: For mutants with slow GTP hydrolysis (locked active state), increasing RGS-type GTPase-accelerating activity restores timely deactivation. For under-active mutants it makes things worse.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H5, effector bypass): Neuronal RGS overexpression, with Gβγ and effectors untouched. - Motor and seizures: if the mutant is over-active, less tonic hyperkinesia and fewer seizures. If the mutant is under-active, both worsen. The sign of the response is itself the readout that identifies the allele class.

## Z41
Statement: The decisive effector lesion is presynaptic. A mutant Gαo/Gβγ fails to inhibit N-type and P/Q-type Cav2 channels, so presynaptic Ca²⁺ entry and release are raised, and reducing it with a Cav2 modulator class (α2δ ligand or N-type blocker) compensates.
Discriminating manipulation vs H7 (GIRK): Cav2.2 conditional knockdown or blockade in striatal and cortical presynaptic terminals, with a GIRK-activator arm. Prediction under H6: motor improves and seizures fall. If the defect is somatodendritic GIRK (H7), Cav2 blockade lowers release but does not recover resting hyperpolarization, so seizures persist and motor improves little.

## Z42
Statement: If the mutant subunit is a dominant-negative or sequestering allele that competes with wild-type alpha for Gβγ or receptor, then lowering mutant protein selectively (allele-specific knockdown) works better than adding more wild-type.
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H1, which raises protein of an allele): Allele-specific knockdown (genetic, ASO class) in the mutant neuron, against wild-type gene addition. Predicted: if H3 is right, knockdown improves both, but the extra wild-type copy alone does not. Motor function improves (more so in movement-circuit neurons) and seizure frequency falls only if the dominant-negative effect acts in the epileptogenic compartment.

## Z43
Statement: Loss of Gαo inhibition of adenylyl cyclase raises striatal cAMP/PKA, so lowering adenylyl cyclase 5 (ADCY5) activity restores the balance.
Discriminating manipulation: Adcy5 knockdown or an AC5-inhibitor class versus GIRK/Cav modulation (H9). Prediction: motor function improves, and seizures are unchanged because the cAMP arm is mainly striatal.

## Z44
Statement: If the mutant alpha is a loss-of-function allele that is partly misfolded or unstable but retains some activity, then raising its folded abundance (proteostasis or chaperone class, or genetically enhancing the stability of the same subunit) restores signalling in proportion to the rescued protein.
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H2, direct allosteric rescue of the existing protein): Boost the folding-capacity branch (HSP70/90-class induction) in variant neurons while the receptor and effector inputs are held clamped. Predicted: effector output and the protein pool rise together, so motor and seizure scores improve only if the allele is unstable. If it is stable but inert, protein rises, function does not, and neither motor function nor seizures change.

## Z45
Statement: The motor phenotype arises mainly in indirect-pathway (D2) striatal projection neurons, and restoring the subunit there corrects movement with little change in seizures.
Discriminating manipulation (vs D1-pathway restoration): D2-neuron-restricted (Cre-dependent) restoration versus D1-neuron-restricted restoration. - Motor: improvement with D2 and little with D1. Seizures: unchanged with either.

## Z46
Statement: Dampening the striatal indirect-pathway cAMP/PKA cascade (adenylyl cyclase 5 or PDE-class control) compensates for lost Gαo inhibition of cAMP.
Discriminator vs H10: Delete AC5 only in D2-receptor-expressing medium spiny neurons. If H9 holds, hyperkinetic motor signs improve and seizure frequency is unchanged. If H10 holds, deleting AC5 changes nothing.

## Z47
Statement: Silencing only the mutant GNAO1 allele, with the wild-type allele left intact, lowers the dominant-negative burden of the mutant subunit and restores heterotrimer signalling, while silencing both alleles removes the remaining wild-type signal.
Discriminating manipulation: Allele-selective versus pan-allele knockdown in the same cell population, against H2 (wild-type gene addition). Predicted motor: improves with selective knockdown, and pan-allele knockdown gives no motor gain. Predicted seizures: improve or stay unchanged with selective knockdown, and worsen with pan-allele knockdown.

## Z48
Statement: The mutant Gαo acts as a dominant negative (it traps Gβγ, GPCRs or RGS partners), so silencing the mutant allele while leaving wild-type Gαo in place restores Gαo/Gβγ signalling.
Discriminating manipulation: allele-specific knockdown of the mutant (ASO/siRNA class) versus adding wild-type Gαo (H2). Prediction: knockdown improves both motor function and seizures, and wild-type addition does not.

## Z49
Statement: The mutant fails to be lipidated (N-myristoylation or palmitoylation) or to localise correctly at the plasma membrane, so shifting the palmitoylation balance restores membrane coupling.
Discriminating manipulation: depalmitoylase-inhibitor or zDHHC-boost class versus Ric-8B (H3). Prediction: motor function improves and seizures improve only in mislocalised variants. Nothing changes in variants that localise normally.

## Z50
Statement: Part of the phenotype is developmental miswiring that only early restoration prevents. Restoring Gαo function after onset reverses the signalling-dependent part (acute motor and excitability) but not the structural part.
Discriminating manipulation vs H1 (dose, timing-agnostic): inducible (tamoxifen) restoration at post-onset age versus neonatal restoration, with a matched anticonvulsant comparator arm. Prediction under H10: motor function recovers substantially in the late arm (acute signalling-dependent) but cognitive and developmental readouts and the seizure threshold stay partly impaired. Under H1 both arms recover equally. The ASM-matched comparator is needed because the lithium case in /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/therapeutic_canonical_repair_package.md (Candidate B) shows an effect present in wild-type animals too is a general anticonvulsant effect, not a rescue.

## Z51
Statement: The mutant subunit is misfolded or unstable and degraded, so a proteostasis or pharmacological-chaperone class that increases mutant protein level and folded fraction restores signalling.
Discriminating manipulation vs H1 (more protein of any allele): a proteasome- or lysosome-route block applied to knock-in neurons, with the Gαo-dependent coupling readout measured. Prediction: if H3 holds, the effect scales with mutant fraction and motor and seizures improve only in alleles with a folding lesion. If H1 holds, wild-type-allele upregulation helps regardless of folding class. If stable-but-inert, motor and seizures are unchanged despite higher protein.

## Z52
Statement: In a mutant-G-protein DEE, the seizure state is partly an NMDAR-dependent network burst that can be suppressed without repairing the subunit, whereas the motor phenotype needs the subunit function itself; suppressing the state therefore treats the seizures and not the movement disorder.
Discriminating manipulation: NMDAR antagonist class against restoration of the subunit in the same preparation (nearest rival: H5, effector-level bypass). Predicted seizures: decrease with NMDAR block, as with H5. Predicted motor: unchanged, whereas subunit restoration (H2) changes motor. If the NMDAR block also improves motor scores, the motor phenotype is network-state driven too and this hypothesis fails.

## Z53
Statement: Reducing acetylcholine release from striatal cholinergic interneurons (ChAT-cell manipulation, muscarinic antagonism at class level) restores motor control because mutant Gαo disinhibits them.
Discriminator vs H9: Silence cholinergic interneurons chemogenetically while leaving medium spiny neurons alone. If H10 holds, motor signs improve and seizures are unchanged. If H9 holds, silencing them has no motor benefit.

## Z54
Statement: A mutant Gαo that is dysfunctional but still captures Gβγ, receptors or RGS acts as a toxic dominant gain, so selective removal of the mutant allele (allele-selective ASO or RNAi) beats any attempt to repair it.
Discriminating manipulation vs H1: allele-selective knockdown of the mutant. Prediction under H2: motor improves, because dystonia and chorea-type output is gain-driven, and seizures decrease. Under H1 the same manipulation is neutral or worsens both.

## Z55
Statement: Boosting folding, palmitoylation or membrane targeting (Ric-8-class chaperones, depalmitoylation control) restores function in mislocalised or unstable mutants.
Discriminator vs H3: Raise Ric-8 chaperone activity (genetic overexpression) in the affected neurons. If H4 holds, it improves both motor function and seizures for the trafficking-defective subset. If H3 holds, it has no effect or worsens output in GTP-locked mutants.

## Z56
Statement: Accelerating GTP hydrolysis (RGS-family GAP activity, e.g. RGS17/19 class) restores function in mutants that are stuck in the GTP-bound state.
Discriminator vs H4: Overexpress or delete an RGS GAP in striatal medium spiny neurons only. If H3 holds, motor function improves in mutants that are GTP-locked, and seizures are unchanged or improved only if the same mutation lowers cortical thresholds. If instead the defect is trafficking (H4), the RGS manipulation has no effect.

## Z57
Statement: Raising a Gαi paralog (Gαi1/2/3) restores inhibitory signalling by taking over the functions Gαo can no longer perform.
Discriminator vs H1: Overexpress a Gαi paralog in an otherwise untreated mutant neuron population. If H5 holds, it improves motor function and seizures even though no wild-type Gαo is added. If H1 holds, it is not enough.

## Z58
Statement: The pathology is functional haploinsufficiency, so raising wild-type Gαo dosage above the threshold (gene supplementation or transcriptional upregulation of the normal allele) restores signalling.
Discriminating manipulation: neuronal wild-type Gαo overexpression on the mutant background versus allele-specific knockdown (H1). Prediction: motor and seizures both improve if H2 is true, and neither improves if the mutant is dominant negative.

## Z59
Statement: Residual functional mutant Gαo can be pushed past the signalling threshold by amplifying the upstream receptor drive (GPCR positive allosteric modulators for Gi/o-coupled receptors such as D2, GABA-B, α2, or opioid receptors) or the GEF step (Ric-8A).
Discriminating manipulation: Gi/o-GPCR PAM class versus Ric-8A boost. Prediction: for hypomorphs, motor function improves and seizures decrease. For null-like or dominant-negative variants, receptor amplification worsens symptoms by increasing sequestration (H1).

## Z60
Statement: Directly activating the Gβγ-gated inward-rectifier (GIRK) effector bypasses the faulty subunit signal and restores inhibitory hyperpolarisation downstream of the defect.
Discriminating manipulation: GIRK-activator class against Cav2.2 reduction (nearest rival, same pathway at a different effector). Predicted motor: modest or none, because the motor circuit is not driven by this channel alone and activation can sedate. Predicted seizures: decrease. If Cav2.2 reduction (the rival) works and GIRK activation does not, the presynaptic arm and not the postsynaptic arm carries the seizure phenotype.
