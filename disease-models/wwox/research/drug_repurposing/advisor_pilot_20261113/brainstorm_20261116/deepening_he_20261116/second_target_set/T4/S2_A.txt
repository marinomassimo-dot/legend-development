H1. Origin: S1-1 (INSIDE as allele-level logic; dominance premise outside)
Statement: Removing the mutant transcript while leaving the wild-type allele restores function if the mutant acts dominantly (gain-of-function or dominant-negative) over a wild-type allele that is already sufficient.
Corpus test: NOT STATED for any G-protein. The nearest held logic is "Lever assignment is per allele" (/tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/mechanism_intervention_map.md:325). CLAIM 032, "WWOX haploinsufficiency is not deleterious" (/tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/registries/claim_registry_current.md:585-594), is an analogy only: one sufficient allele equals a healthy carrier. The held oligonucleotide discussion covers splice-masking only and does not cover silencing (map line 359).
Usual vs this: The usual reading treats a missense DEE subunit as lost function, to be replaced. This reading says the surplus of mutant protein is the lesion, so the wild-type dose is not limiting.
Single rejecting observation: In neurons or mice carrying mutant plus wild type, mutant-specific knockdown leaves the Gαo effector readout (GIRK current, cAMP inhibition) unchanged or worse.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H2, wild-type addition): Allele-specific mutant knockdown with no wild-type added.
- Motor and seizures: if the mutant is dominant, both improve. If it is pure loss of function, motor and seizures are unchanged or move toward the knockout state, because the dose falls.

H2. Origin: S1-2 (INSIDE)
Statement: Raising wild-type subunit dose in neurons, by gene addition or transcriptional up-regulation, restores function when the mutant is hypomorphic rather than interfering, and rescue is threshold-like rather than graded.
Corpus test: SUPPORT by mechanism class (WWOX). Neuron-restricted gene addition rescued survival, behaviour, ataxia and spike-wave discharges in the Wwox-null mouse (/tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/mechanism_intervention_map.md:397-415, R-01). Rescue was a threshold: the low dose failed and the high dose succeeded (working_model_current.md:87, 131). Held CONTRADICTION in the opposite direction: "'more WWOX = better' is not a safe default" (working_model_current.md:71, CLAIM 028). The held dCas9/CRISPRa option for hypomorphic states is "not actionable now" (working_model_current.md:229).
Usual vs this: The usual reading is "more wild-type is better, and graded". This one predicts a minimum effective dose below which nothing is rescued, and no benefit or harm if the mutant interferes.
Single rejecting observation: Wild-type overexpression on the mutant background leaves the readout unchanged, which is a dominant-negative signature, or improvement is smoothly graded with no dose threshold.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): Add wild-type subunit and leave the mutant in place.
- Motor and seizures: both improve only above a threshold, and only if the mutant is a loss-of-function type. There is no benefit, or worsening, if the mutant is dominant-negative.

H3. Origin: S1-3 (INSIDE; S1-4 Ric-8 gain is the named rival)
Statement: A ligand-type stabiliser of the mutant subunit (the pharmacological-chaperone class), or global proteostasis capacity, raises the functional mutant pool when the lesion is folding or turnover. The ligand class stabilises by occlusion, which can raise abundance while abolishing function.
Corpus test: SUPPORT by mechanism class (WWOX). HYP-20260709-08 (/tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/research/therapeutic_hypotheses_ledger_current.md:271-299) requires first separating synthesis, insolubility and turnover. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/proteostasis_rationale.md retracted three of four rationale steps, including the ΔΔG "recoverability" band, which has no demonstrated predictive value (lines 31-47). Ric-8, the endogenous Gα chaperone, is NOT STATED (0 hits).
Usual vs this: The usual reading takes higher mutant abundance as higher function. This one tests abundance and nucleotide-cycle function together, and treats a ligand that clamps the pocket as a possible failure.
Single rejecting observation: Mutant protein level and nucleotide-binding competence are already normal in neurons, so stabilisation adds nothing. Or stabilisation raises protein while the effector readout falls (stable but inert).
Discriminating manipulation (vs Ric-8 gain): A direct small-molecule folding-stabiliser class applied with Ric-8 level held constant, compared with neuronal Ric-8 gain on the same mutant.
- Motor and seizures: a partial benefit to both appears only in low-abundance mutants. An occluding stabiliser raises abundance but leaves motor and seizures unchanged. Ric-8 gain predicts a similar direction with a lesser seizure effect.
