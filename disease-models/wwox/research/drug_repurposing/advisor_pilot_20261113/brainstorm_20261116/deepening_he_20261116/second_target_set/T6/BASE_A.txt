H1. Statement: Phage-encoded single-strand-annealing and exonuclease recombination functions (Red/RecET/Erf-like) repair the cut by homologous recombination with an uncut sister copy in the replicating concatemer, so the cleavage site is restored to wild type. · Evidence: Lambda Red and related annealing proteins are well-established recombinases. Phage-encoded repair of nuclease cuts is plausible, but I know no direct demonstration that it explains tolerance of multiple RM and CRISPR systems. · Usual reading assumes: survival under a nuclease needs either an inhibitor or a mutated site. This one says the recombinase is a general anti-cleavage tool and the site stays wild type. · Rejection: if the site in survivors is mutated, or if survival is unchanged when the phage recombinase is inactivated and host recombination is intact, H1 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H2, host-driven repair): delete the phage recombinase pair and keep Chi-free or Chi-rich sites constant. Prediction: survival under all nucleases drops, by more under high cleavage load. The few survivors carry cleavage-site mutations or are uncut genomes, whereas wild-type phage shows a wild-type repaired site.

H2. Statement: The host RecBCD/RecA pathway repairs the phage break, and the phage relies on Chi-like sequences or on RecBCD modulation to steer degradation toward recombination instead of loss of the genome. · Evidence: RecBCD degrades linear phage DNA, and Chi attenuates that. Lambda's Gam inhibits RecBCD. Whether Chi density in phage genomes tracks anti-nuclease tolerance is unknown to me. · Usual reading assumes: host RecBCD is a pure antiviral (it degrades broken ends). This one makes it a repair resource when its activity is tuned. · Rejection: if survival after cleavage is the same in a host with RecBCD or RecA lost, host repair is not needed and H2 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): add or remove Chi sites flanking the cleavage site in the phage, with its own recombinase left intact. Prediction: survival rises with Chi added and falls with Chi removed, with no change in the repaired sequence (wild type). H1 predicts no Chi dependence.

H3. Statement: A phage Ku/ligase (NHEJ-like) module joins the broken ends directly, tolerating cleavage at the cost of small deletions at the site. · Evidence: Ku-like proteins have been reported in some mycobacteriophages. Their function in repairing nuclease cuts is not established, and most phages lack Ku. Evidence against it as a general mechanism: it is absent from most phages. · Usual reading assumes: bacterial NHEJ is rare and phages do not use it. This one says a phage module supplies end joining. · Rejection: if cleavage-site junctions in survivors show no microhomology or small-indel signature, or survival is unchanged without the Ku/ligase gene, H3 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (rival H8, mutational escape by host polymerases): delete the phage ku/ligase gene. Prediction: survival falls under cuts in essential regions. In wild type the repaired site carries microhomology-flanked 1 to 20 bp deletions that are not found in the deletion mutant, which shows only point-mutational escape.

H4. Statement: Direct repeats flanking cleavage sites let single-strand annealing rejoin the ends and delete the intervening sequence, so surviving phages carry a single repeat copy and lose the site. · Evidence: Annealing proteins can mediate repeat-based deletions. Repeat-flanked site loss in nuclease-attacked phages is not something I know to be shown. · Usual reading assumes: a repaired site is either wild type or point-mutated. This one predicts a deletion with a defined repeat junction. · Rejection: if survivors carry intact sites, or junctions do not fall on the repeats, H4 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (rival H1, sister-copy repair): insert or remove a direct repeat pair around the site, with the recombinase intact. Prediction: survival rises with repeats and the product is a deletion with one repeat retained. Without repeats the survivor site is wild type (H1) or the survival is lower.

H5. Statement: A broken end invades an intact homologous copy and primes recombination-dependent replication (T4-like, UvsX/UvsY-driven), so the cut becomes a replication start instead of a lethal lesion. · Evidence: T4 recombination-dependent replication is well documented. Its role in nuclease tolerance is not shown. · Usual reading assumes: a break is a loss and repair merely restores a sequence. This one treats it as productive, with long-tract copying from the template. · Rejection: if survival is unchanged when the recombinase-dependent replication mode is inactivated but the origin-dependent replication mode remains, H5 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): inactivate the strand-invasion recombinase while leaving the phage annealing proteins intact. Prediction: survival falls under cuts, and a neutral marker several kb from the cut shows conversion to the template allele (long tracts) in wild type, whereas H1 predicts short tracts at the site only.

H6. Statement: Phage proteins that cap or bind broken DNA ends (gp2, Gam-like, terminal protein) shield them from degradation long enough for rejoining, with no recombination needed. · Evidence: T4 gp2 protects ends from RecBCD, and lambda Gam does similarly. Whether this extends to RM and CRISPR cuts I do not know. · Usual reading assumes: the end-protection protein is for replication or against host nucleases only. This one makes it general DSB tolerance. · Rejection: if survival is unchanged without the end-binding protein, H6 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): delete the end-capping gene while keeping recombination intact. Prediction: survival falls even though the recombination machinery is intact. The repaired site is wild type or identical to the template. H1 predicts survival is independent of end protection.

H7. Statement: No repair occurs: cut genomes are lost and the infection is carried by uncut copies, so tolerance is a matter of copy number, partial cleavage and redundancy. · Evidence: Cutting is rarely complete, and concatemers or many replicated copies give redundancy. I know of no direct test. · Usual reading assumes: tolerating a break means repairing it. This one says the surviving genomes were never cut. · Rejection: if repaired or mutated sites appear in a large share of survivors, or recombination-gene deletions reduce survival, H7 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): add a second cleavage site in the genome. Prediction: survival falls multiplicatively, since the cutting probability per genome rises, and the surviving sites are wild type, whereas with repair H1 predicts little decline.

H8. Statement: Error-prone repair, by host translesion polymerases or the SOS response, produces indels or point changes at the cut, so tolerance is escape by site mutation. · Evidence: Escape mutants with protospacer or recognition-site mutations are well documented. Whether the repair route is host SOS or phage error-prone polymerase is not established. · Usual reading assumes: escapes pre-exist as mutants. This one says the cut itself generates them. · Rejection: if survivors' sites are wild type, or mutation frequency does not rise at the cut, H8 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (rival H3): place the cleavage site in a non-coding versus an essential coding region. Prediction: survival is far higher with the non-coding site, because indels there are tolerated, and the sequences carry indels or substitutions. H3 predicts the pattern depends on the Ku gene, not on site position.

H9. Statement: Phage-encoded DNA modification (glucosylated hydroxymethyl-cytosine, methylation) reduces the cutting rate below the repair capacity, so repair of the remaining cuts suffices. · Evidence: T4-type modification protects against many restriction enzymes and partly against some CRISPR nucleases. That modification limits the cut load and does not repair it is plausible. · Usual reading assumes: modification is itself an anti-nuclease inhibitor with a complete effect. This one says it works with repair as a combination. · Rejection: if removing the modification abolishes survival with repair intact, and survival returns after raising repair, H9 is not needed. If survival is unchanged by modification, it is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (rival H7): delete the modification gene and keep repair genes. Prediction: survival falls steeply (threshold), and surviving sites are wild type. If instead the survivors still tolerate, H9 is rejected. H7 predicts no threshold behaviour.

H10. Statement: Multiplicity reactivation: cut genomes from co-infecting phages recombine, giving an intact genome from fragments of different parents. · Evidence: Multiplicity reactivation after UV damage is classic. Its role in nuclease tolerance is not known to me. · Usual reading assumes: each infecting genome survives or dies independently. This one says survival rises with multiplicity of infection. · Rejection: if survival at low multiplicity equals survival at high, H10 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (rival H1, intra-genome sister repair): make the phage replication-defective so there is a single copy per cell, and co-infect with a second genome carrying neutral markers near the cut. Prediction: survival is near zero at multiplicity 1 and rises with co-infection. The repaired site carries the other parent's markers (inter-genome exchange). H1 predicts survival at multiplicity 1 with replication intact and markers from the same parent.

Basis: written from general knowledge, no tools or files used. Evidence lines are hedged where I am unsure.
