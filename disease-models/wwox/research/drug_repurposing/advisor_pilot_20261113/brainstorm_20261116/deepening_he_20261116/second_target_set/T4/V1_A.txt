GNAO1 returned no matches in the corpus seed .tsv or .jsonl files. So the G-protein biology below (Gαo/Gαi, Gβγ, RGS, Cav2, GIRK, adenylyl cyclase) is general recall, marked IPOTESI or ESPANSIONE. What the folder supplies is the WWOX programme's reasoning discipline, and I use it as "held evidence". The disciplines I borrow:
- mechanism → lever triage per allele
- stability versus function tension
- MARKER_TO_FUNCTION_GATE
- the lithium specificity failure
- the ASM-matched comparator
- window and dose-threshold logic
- the species discordance of seizures
- neuron-restricted rescue

Every prediction is mechanism level. None is a recommendation, and none is medical advice.

H1. Statement: In a heterozygous patient-class genotype, raising output of the wild-type Gαo allele (neuron-restricted gene addition or transcriptional activation of the endogenous allele) restores net signalling by outcompeting a loss-of-function or weakly dominant-negative mutant subunit. · Held evidence: no G-protein data. By analogy, /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/mechanism_intervention_map.md (Chain A and R-01) records that neuronal gene addition is genotype-agnostic and that rescue is a dose threshold, not a gradient (low dose fails). The same file (R-01) also records "restore toward physiological, NOT above it", and /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/research/discovery_ledger_current.md (DL-MOL-004 is cited there) holds upregulation of the endogenous allele as a distinct lever. That "neither too little nor too much" statement partly contradicts unconditional dose-raising. I keep H1 because a heterozygote has a fixed deficit that a bounded increase toward the physiological ratio can close. · Usual reading vs this: the usual reading treats a dysfunctional-not-absent subunit as needing repair of the mutant protein itself. This reading says the wild-type/mutant ratio, not the mutant's biochemistry, is the lever. · Rejecting observation: in neurons from the heterozygous knock-in, raise wild-type Gαo 1.5–2× from the wild-type allele only, then measure a Gαo-dependent readout (GPCR-mediated inhibition of presynaptic Cav2 currents or GIRK currents). Rejected if the readout does not move or worsens, which would mean the mutant sequesters shared Gβγ, RGS or receptor partners and extra wild-type protein is captured too. · Discriminating manipulation vs H2 (nearest rival, mutant-allele silencing): pan-Gnao1 knockdown (not allele-selective) in the heterozygote. Prediction if H1 is right: motor function worsens and seizures worsen or appear, because total dose falls. If H2 is right (toxic gain), pan-knockdown is neutral or mildly improves. Under H1, motor and seizures both improve with wild-type-allele upregulation.

H2. Statement: A mutant Gαo that is dysfunctional but still captures Gβγ, receptors or RGS acts as a toxic dominant gain, so selective removal of the mutant allele (allele-selective ASO or RNAi) beats any attempt to repair it. · Held evidence: no G-protein data. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/variant_triage_rescuability.md separates "made but unstable", "stable but inert" and null alleles, each with its own lever, and states lever assignment is per allele. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/registries/claim_registry_current.md (CLAIM 032, cited in the map) records that haploinsufficiency is tolerated in WWOX. That is the bridge: if one copy is tolerated, a null-like heterozygote is better than a toxic mutant. · Usual reading vs this: the usual reading treats "not absent" as meaning the allele still contributes. This reading says a non-absent, non-functional subunit is worse than an absent one. · Rejecting observation: in knock-in neurons, compare mutant-allele knockdown against a Gnao1 heterozygous null. Rejected if the knockdown phenotype is worse than the null, which would mean the mutant retains partial useful function. · Discriminating manipulation vs H1: allele-selective knockdown of the mutant. Prediction under H2: motor improves, because dystonia and chorea-type output is gain-driven, and seizures decrease. Under H1 the same manipulation is neutral or worsens both.

H3. Statement: The mutant subunit is misfolded or unstable and degraded, so a proteostasis or pharmacological-chaperone class that increases mutant protein level and folded fraction restores signalling. · Held evidence: no G-protein data. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/variant_triage_rescuability.md ("stable but inert is a demonstrated WWOX phenotype; function readout is mandatory"). /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/mechanism_intervention_map.md (Chain F): "stability is bought with occlusion", and the "recoverability band" had no demonstrated predictive value. That directly contradicts assuming ΔΔG predicts rescuability, so H3 is held only as a conditional hypothesis. · Usual reading vs this: the usual reading assumes more protein means more function. This reading makes protein level a testable intermediate, with function measured separately. · Rejecting observation: a chaperone or turnover-block class raises mutant protein ≥2× in knock-in neurons, but the Gαo-dependent coupling readout does not change. That is the stable-but-inert state. · Discriminating manipulation vs H1 (more protein of any allele): a proteasome- or lysosome-route block applied to knock-in neurons, with the Gαo-dependent coupling readout measured. Prediction: if H3 holds, the effect scales with mutant fraction and motor and seizures improve only in alleles with a folding lesion. If H1 holds, wild-type-allele upregulation helps regardless of folding class. If stable-but-inert, motor and seizures are unchanged despite higher protein.

H4. Statement: If the mutant is hydrolysis-deficient (locked GTP-bound, signalling too long or toward the wrong effector), increasing GTPase-accelerating activity (RGS or RGS7–Gβ5-type complexes) normalizes signal duration. · Held evidence: none in folder. By analogy, /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/mechanism_intervention_map.md §0 hard rule 2: "Direction before candidate — no intervention enters without its axis's sign resolved". The sign of the GTP-state defect would have to be measured first. · Usual reading vs this: the usual reading groups all mutants as "less signalling". This reading says some are "too much, too long". · Rejecting observation: in knock-in neurons, a GTP-cycling assay (GTPase turnover, or an effector-recruitment biosensor kinetic) shows normal hydrolysis and normal exchange. H4 is then rejected for that allele. · Discriminating manipulation vs H5 (GEF/receptor drive): neuron-restricted RGS gain-of-function. Prediction under H4: motor improves (hyperkinetic output falls) and seizures fall. If the mutant is instead exchange-deficient (H5), the same manipulation is neutral or worsens motor function, because more GAP shortens already weak signalling.

H5. Statement: If the mutant has slow GDP release or a nucleotide-binding defect, raising the guanine-nucleotide-exchange input (receptor-driven flux or a Ric-8A-type chaperone/GEF) restores activation. · Held evidence: none in folder; mechanism-level only. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/meta/meta_gaba_paradox_current.md (Hypothesis 4, "timing matters", and Interpretive risks) is the held discipline: a modulation's effect depends on the unmeasured state of the receptor-side system. · Usual reading vs this: the usual reading targets the mutant protein. This reading targets its activators. · Rejecting observation: in mutant-expressing neurons, a Gi/o-coupled receptor agonist class produces the same or a larger downstream signal as in control. H5 is rejected for that allele. · Discriminating manipulation vs H4: a Gi/o-coupled receptor agonist class, or Ric-8A gain-of-function, in the knock-in. Prediction under H5: the Gαo-dependent readout rises, motor improves and seizures fall (the exchange-limited subset only). Under H4 the same drive worsens motor function and may lower the seizure threshold, because GTP-locked signalling is amplified.

H6. Statement: The decisive effector lesion is presynaptic. A mutant Gαo/Gβγ fails to inhibit N-type and P/Q-type Cav2 channels, so presynaptic Ca²⁺ entry and release are raised, and reducing it with a Cav2 modulator class (α2δ ligand or N-type blocker) compensates. · Held evidence: none in folder. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/therapeutic_canonical_repair_package.md (§6, E-1) supplies the design lesson: a silenced preparation is not evidence for a specific target, so the occlusion term and an unaffected-control comparator are required. · Usual reading vs this: the usual reading puts the dysfunction at the receptor-G protein step. This reading puts it at the effector, and says a downstream channel manipulation bypasses the mutant. · Rejecting observation: in knock-in neurons, a G protein–coupled receptor inhibition of Cav2.2 current is normal, so no Gαo/Gβγ→Cav2 defect exists. · Discriminating manipulation vs H7 (GIRK): Cav2.2 conditional knockdown or blockade in striatal and cortical presynaptic terminals, with a GIRK-activator arm. Prediction under H6: motor improves and seizures fall. If the defect is somatodendritic GIRK (H7), Cav2 blockade lowers release but does not recover resting hyperpolarization, so seizures persist and motor improves little.

H7. Statement: The decisive effector lesion is postsynaptic. Loss of Gβγ-driven GIRK hyperpolarization raises excitability in cortical and striatal neurons, and a GIRK-activating class restores it. · Held evidence: none in folder. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/meta/meta_gaba_paradox_current.md ("marker ≠ function") applies: GIRK expression would not equal GIRK-mediated inhibition, so current must be measured. · Usual reading vs this: the usual reading treats the movement disorder as one signalling failure. This reading says excitability and presynaptic release are separable. · Rejecting observation: the GPCR-evoked, Ba²⁺-sensitive GIRK current in knock-in neurons is the same size as in controls. · Discriminating manipulation vs H6: GIRK-activator class in the knock-in, versus Cav2 blockade. Prediction under H7: seizure threshold rises sharply and motor function improves modestly. Under H6 the reverse holds: motor improves, with seizures less affected.

H8. Statement: Loss of Gαo-mediated inhibition of adenylyl cyclase raises cAMP/PKA tone in striatal indirect-pathway neurons, driving the hyperkinetic phenotype, and lowering that tone (AC5 or PKA inhibition, or a cAMP-degradation class) bypasses the mutant. · Held evidence: none in folder. The folder's rule that a node must be engageable and its direction resolved (/tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/mechanism_intervention_map.md §2) gates this one: direction in the striatum is unmeasured. · Usual reading vs this: the usual reading expects the same signalling failure to produce both epilepsy and the movement disorder. This reading puts the movement disorder in the cAMP axis and does not claim it explains seizures. · Rejecting observation: striatal cAMP and PKA substrate phosphorylation in knock-in mice are not above wild type. · Discriminating manipulation vs H6/H7 (channel effectors): conditional Adcy5 deletion or PKA inhibition restricted to indirect-pathway spiny neurons. Prediction under H8: motor improves clearly and seizures are unchanged, because the manipulation does not reach cortical interneurons. Channel-level manipulations (H6, H7) improve seizures. This dissociation is the discriminator.

H9. Statement: Seizures and the movement disorder come from different cells. Restoring Gαo function in inhibitory interneurons rescues seizures, and restoring it in striatal spiny neurons (or cerebellar Purkinje cells) rescues movement, so the two phenotypes are dissociable. · Held evidence: no G-protein data. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/registries/claim_registry_current.md (CLAIM 003 and CLAIM 004: neuron-restricted rescue is sufficient for non-cell-autonomous phenotypes, while oligodendrocyte-restricted drivers do nothing) shows cell-type restriction can discriminate loci. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/meta/meta_gaba_paradox_current.md shows the interneuron marker/function problem and the systemic-null confounder, which a conditional design avoids. · Usual reading vs this: the usual reading assumes one lesion with a spectrum of expression. This reading assumes two circuit loci. · Rejecting observation: restricting restoration to GABAergic neurons (Dlx-Cre) fails to reduce seizures, or restricting it to striatal spiny neurons also rescues seizures. · Discriminating manipulation vs H8 (cAMP axis, not cell-type): interneuron-restricted (Dlx-Cre) restoration versus spiny-neuron-restricted (Drd2-Cre) restoration. Prediction under H9: interneuron restoration reduces seizures with unchanged motor function, and spiny-neuron restoration improves motor function with seizures unchanged. Note the folder's species caution (meta_gaba_paradox_current.md, provenance correction): seizures were a rat phenotype and absent in Wwox-null mice, so seizure readouts in a mouse model may be unavailable and the seizure arm may not be testable there.

H10. Statement: Part of the phenotype is developmental miswiring that only early restoration prevents. Restoring Gαo function after onset reverses the signalling-dependent part (acute motor and excitability) but not the structural part. · Held evidence: /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/mechanism_intervention_map.md (Chain A boundary 3: efficacy window P0–P5, neuronal rescue does not repair the prenatal defect) and /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/therapeutic_canonical_repair_package.md (E-3: delayed gene therapy against an ASM-matched comparator separates symptomatic seizure control from developmental modification; DISEASE_MODIFICATION_EVIDENCE_STATUS: ABSENT for every WWOX intervention). The designs transfer; the G-protein data do not exist here. · Usual reading vs this: the usual reading treats a signalling defect as acutely reversible at any age. This reading says part is irreversible past a window. · Rejecting observation: post-onset (adult) restoration of the wild-type/corrected allele fully restores motor function and seizure threshold to wild-type levels with no residual deficit. · Discriminating manipulation vs H1 (dose, timing-agnostic): inducible (tamoxifen) restoration at post-onset age versus neonatal restoration, with a matched anticonvulsant comparator arm. Prediction under H10: motor function recovers substantially in the late arm (acute signalling-dependent) but cognitive and developmental readouts and the seizure threshold stay partly impaired. Under H1 both arms recover equally. The ASM-matched comparator is needed because the lithium case in /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/therapeutic_canonical_repair_package.md (Candidate B) shows an effect present in wild-type animals too is a general anticonvulsant effect, not a rescue.
