Held methodological items I used:
(a) PMID38499540 dossier §3.1: a null measured "at the floor of the assay" (HDR 0.03–0.83 % of events) is not evidence of no change. Focus counts are surrogates, and only a direct repair-outcome readout counts. Applied below: read the repaired site by sequencing, and check that survival is above assay floor before calling a null.
(b) Same dossier §6: repair-pathway choice is cell-cycle gated, so pathway readouts do not transfer across cell states. Applied below: phage replication state (pre- vs post-replication) is a variable.

H1. Statement: the phage survives by homologous repair of the cut site from an uncut sister or co-infecting genome copy, using a phage- or host-encoded strand-exchange function, so the cut is restored to the parental sequence. · Held evidence: none on phage; /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/research/fulltext_dossiers/PMID38499540.md §2 shows only that in mammalian cells RAD51-marked HDR needs S/G2 (ESPANSIONE). · Usual reading vs this one: the usual reading says the phage needs one inhibitor per system, or that it dodges cutting. This one says cutting is tolerated because a second copy is present, and survival scales with copy number or multiplicity of infection (MOI), not with a per-system inhibitor. · Rejection observation: at MOI of about 1 (one genome per cell, measured by single-cell or limiting-dilution infection), survival under nuclease attack is the same as at high MOI, and the cleaved site is not restored from another copy. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): supply a second, marked-but-site-containing genome copy in trans, or equivalently delete the phage recombinase (RecA analogue, e.g. UvsX or Redα/β class). Prediction: survival falls to the single-copy level in the recombinase-null phage, and the surviving repaired sites are parental sequence, or a donor-marker conversion tract, with no indels.

H2. Statement: the phage survives by end-joining of the broken ends with a phage- or host-encoded Ku/ligase-type system that seals the break at the cost of a scar. · Held evidence: none on phage; PMID38499540.md §2 reports only mammalian 53BP1 (an NHEJ surrogate) and notes the paper's caption inverts its own panels (§3). That is, the held dossier's own NHEJ reading is unreliable (ESPANSIONE). · Usual reading vs this one: the usual reading says a phage genome cannot survive a DSB without a recombination partner. This one says a single-genome phage can survive, with a mutated site. · Rejection observation: survivors from single-genome infections show a restored parental site and no microhomology-bearing deletions or insertions at the cut site. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): infect at single-genome multiplicity and delete the phage ligase/Ku-like gene. Prediction: survival is lost only in the ligase-null phage, and survivors carry indel or microhomology-deletion scars at the site (vs parental sequence in H1).

H3. Statement: a phage end-capping protein (Gam- or gp2-like) blocks host exonuclease degradation of the free ends, which keeps the break repairable and gives time for any downstream repair. · Held evidence: none held. · Usual reading vs this one: the usual reading treats such proteins as inhibitors of one specific host nuclease. This one treats them as generic protectors of every nuclease-induced end. · Rejection observation: a phage lacking the capping protein but carrying an intact recombinase survives nuclease attack as well as wild type. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): delete the capping gene and keep the recombinase. Prediction: survival falls under both RM and CRISPR attack, and if any survivors exist the site shows end resection (deletions spreading from the cut), not a clean parental restoration.

H4. Statement: the phage uses the host RecBCD (or an analogue) as a repair enzyme, stopping it at phage-encoded Chi-like sequences, so cutting is converted into a recombination substrate and the repaired site is parental. · Held evidence: none held. · Usual reading vs this one: the usual reading treats RecBCD as an anti-phage nuclease. This one makes it a repair resource for the phage. · Rejection observation: deleting the phage Chi-like sites does not change survival under nuclease attack, in a RecBCD-proficient host. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H3): remove or add Chi-like sites near the cut site, in a host with intact RecBCD. Prediction: removing the Chi sites lowers survival and shifts repair toward degradation; adding them raises survival. Surviving sites are parental sequence in both cases.

H5. Statement: the phage repairs the cut through single-strand annealing between direct repeats flanking the site, independent of RecA, which gives a deletion of one repeat copy. · Held evidence: none held. · Usual reading vs this one: the usual reading assumes recombination needs strand invasion. This one allows annealing alone. · Rejection observation: in a recA-null host, a phage with intact flanking repeats and a phage annealing protein survives no better than the same phage without repeats. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): flank the site with direct repeats, or remove them, in a recA-null host. Prediction: survival is repeat-dependent and recA-independent. The repaired site shows loss of one repeat unit plus the region between the repeats (a deletion), which differs from H1's parental restoration.

H6. Statement: the phage tolerates cutting by dilution: a concatemeric or headful-packaged genome carries redundant copies, so a cut removes one copy but packaging still picks up an intact one. · Held evidence: none held. · Usual reading vs this one: the usual reading counts a cut as lethal to the genome. This one says it is lethal only to one copy of many. · Rejection observation: survival under attack is unchanged when the number of genome copies at the time of cutting is reduced. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): delay or block phage DNA replication (a temperature-sensitive replication mutant) until after the challenge. Prediction: survival falls roughly in proportion to the copy-number loss, with no repair needed, so the surviving sites are the uncut parental sequence (no repaired site) and no scars appear.

H7. Statement: the phage tolerates RM cuts by simple religation of cohesive ends, giving futile cut-and-reseal cycles that restore the site to its exact parental sequence. · Held evidence: none held. · Usual reading vs this one: the usual reading says a cut is a one-way commitment. This one makes it a reversible equilibrium. · Rejection observation: survivors lack ligase-dependent exact restoration, and the proportion of re-sealed sites does not depend on ligase level. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): use a host strain with a ligase thermosensitive allele, or delete the phage ligase, and compare an overhang-generating RM cut with a blunt cut. Prediction: survival falls without the ligase, and the surviving site is exactly parental (no scar), unlike H2.

H8. Statement: phage-induced SOS-type repair of the host supplies the repair factors, and the phage borrows them. · Held evidence: none held. · Usual reading vs this one: the usual reading treats the host SOS response as a stress response unrelated to phage survival. This one says it is the repair supply line. · Rejection observation: in a non-inducible lexA host (SOS blocked), phage survival under nuclease attack is unchanged. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): infect a lexA(Ind-) host. Prediction: survival falls and is not recovered by phage recombinase alone. Surviving sites show host-error-prone substitutions or indels where the SOS polymerases act.

H9. Statement: phage survival under nuclease attack depends on error-prone repair that destroys the recognition site (a PAM/seed substitution or a site-internal indel), which produces escape mutants rather than repair. · Held evidence: none held. · Usual reading vs this one: the usual reading counts a repaired site as a restored site. This one says tolerance often means the site is lost, so survival is selection among variants. · Rejection observation: survivors of attack retain a cleavable parental site. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): replace the phage polymerase with a high-fidelity (antimutator) allele. Prediction: fewer survivors appear under attack, and the survivors that remain carry the same classes of escape mutation at a lower rate. Site sequence in survivors is mutated at the PAM or seed (unlike H1 and H7).

H10. Statement: the phage places its genome in a nuclease-excluding compartment (a nucleus-like shell), so most genomes are never cut at all. · Held evidence: none held; this is an avoidance mechanism, not a tolerance mechanism, and I include it as the null rival to H1–H9. · Usual reading vs this one: the usual reading says tolerating cuts requires a repair system. This one says it requires no repair, because cuts do not occur in the compartment. · Rejection observation: a shell-deficient phage that is otherwise equal survives the same as wild type. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H6): delete the shell protein gene (e.g. the major shell protein). Prediction: survival falls, and surviving sites are uncut parental sequence (no repair signature at all).
