All ten are mechanism-level hypotheses from memory, with no tools used. "Surface" means plasma-membrane or synaptic subunit abundance. "Current" means GABA-evoked current per cell.

H1. Statement: Blocking ER-associated degradation of the misfolded mutant subunit (Hrd1/Sel1L–VCP/p97–proteasome axis) increases the mutant pool available for assembly and export, so surface expression and current rise. · Evidence: Reported for GABRA1 A322D and GABRG2 variants, where ERAD inhibition raises total and sometimes surface subunit. Proteasome inhibition raises total protein but often leaves it ER-trapped. Raised surface is not consistent across variants. · Usual reading assumes: Degradation is a downstream consequence of misfolding, so blocking it only accumulates junk. This one holds that, for folding-competent but slowly folding mutants, ERAD removes protein before it can fold. · Rejection: Hrd1/Sel1L knockdown or VCP inhibition raises total mutant protein but surface (biotinylation or surface imaging) is unchanged and EndoH-sensitive only. · Discriminating manipulation: Versus H2 (more folding help), use Hrd1/Sel1L loss of function, or a p97 inhibitor class, in cells with chaperone capacity held constant. Predicts total up, surface up only modestly and only if H2-type folding is intact, and current up in proportion to surface. If the rival H2 is right, surface stays flat and current is unchanged.

H2. Statement: Remodelling the cytosolic and ER chaperone network (more Hsp70/BiP-type folding, less Hsp90/Hsp40-driven degradation triage) shifts mutant subunits from degradation to productive folding and assembly, raising surface and current. · Evidence: BiP, calnexin and Hsp70/Hsp90 handling of GABA-A subunits is documented. Chemical chaperones such as 4-phenylbutyrate raised surface levels of some mutant subunits in heterologous cells. Evidence in neurons or in vivo is thin. · Usual reading assumes: Chaperones are generic and non-specific. This one treats the Hsp70/Hsp90 balance as a variant-specific lever. · Rejection: Raising Hsp70 activity or a chemical chaperone leaves surface unchanged in the mutant while rescuing a control misfolded subunit. · Discriminating manipulation: Versus H1, use Hsp90 inhibition (class), which promotes client degradation, against Hsp70 co-chaperone activation. Predicts that Hsp70 activation raises surface and current, while Hsp90 inhibition lowers both. If H1 is right, Hsp90 inhibition has no net effect once ERAD is blocked.

H3. Statement: Selective activation of the ATF6 arm of the unfolded protein response raises ER folding and export capacity, rescuing mutant surface expression without general proteostasis stress. · Evidence: ATF6-activating compounds reported to raise surface GABA-A receptors containing destabilised α1. This is from memory and needs checking. Against: chronic ATF6 or UPR activation can be toxic. · Usual reading assumes: UPR activation is a stress response that worsens disease. This one treats the adaptive ATF6 arm as a therapeutic arm. · Rejection: ATF6 activation raises BiP and other targets but not mutant surface, or PERK/IRE1 co-activation appears and counteracts it. · Discriminating manipulation: Versus H2, activate ATF6 with BiP or ATF6 targets knocked down, or use an ATF6-selective class against an IRE1/PERK class. Predicts surface and current up with ATF6-selective activation, and no gain or a loss with PERK/IRE1 activation. H2 alone would not depend on ATF6 transcription.

H4. Statement: A ligand that binds the orthosteric or an allosteric site stabilises the folded pentamer (a pharmacological chaperone), raising assembly and surface expression, while function depends on whether the ligand leaves the receptor. · Evidence: Pharmacological chaperoning is well established for other channels and receptors. For GABA-A, chronic agonist/modulator exposure alters assembly in some reports; none known as a rescue of DEE variants. · Usual reading assumes: A ligand modulates function only. This one separates folding stabilisation (surface) from gating (current). · Rejection: Applying a competitive ligand class raises neither assembly (native gel or co-IP) nor surface for the mutant. · Discriminating manipulation: Versus H2 and H3 (non-specific), compare an agonist-site ligand class with a non-binding positive modulator class. Predicts that the agonist-site class raises surface but evoked current is blocked or desensitised until washout. The modulator class raises current without raising surface. A general chaperone raises surface and current together.

H5. Statement: Reducing Ca2+ entry through L-type Ca2+ channels, or reshaping ER Ca2+ handling, modulates ER-chaperone activity and ER exit and raises mutant surface expression. · Evidence: One group reported that Ca2+ channel blocker classes raised surface GABA-A receptors in misfolding models. Mechanism and neuronal relevance are unproven. · Usual reading assumes: Ca2+ channel block acts on excitability only. This one makes it a proteostasis lever. · Rejection: Surface is unchanged after blocking L-type channels or buffering cytosolic Ca2+, even when excitability is matched. · Discriminating manipulation: Versus H3, block L-type channels (class) with ATF6 held fixed. A genetic L-type channel knockdown and an ER Ca2+ store depletion class discriminate. Predicts surface and current up with L-type block, and a smaller effect or none when ER Ca2+ is depleted. H3 predicts the same surface rise with no Ca2+ dependence.

H6. Statement: The shortfall is partner-limited assembly, so raising the supply of co-assembling subunits or assembly factors (for example the β/γ partner, or LHFPL4/Shisa/NACHO-like helpers) restores pentamer formation and surface expression. · Evidence: Subunit stoichiometry controls ER exit, and partner co-expression increases surface of other subunit mutants. Specific helper factors for GABA-A are only partly known. · Usual reading assumes: The mutant protein is the problem, so the lever is on the mutant. This one says the partner's abundance sets the surface outcome. · Rejection: Overexpressing the partner subunit raises total and WT surface but not mutant-containing receptors (shown by tagging the mutant). · Discriminating manipulation: Versus H1/H2, overexpress the partner subunit alone, then do a subunit-specific knockdown. Predicts mutant-containing surface and current rise with partner excess, and fall with partner knockdown. A folding lever (H2) would be partner-independent.

H7. Statement: Function is restored by upregulating a paralogous subunit (for example another β or α) that substitutes for the lost subunit, in the cell type where loss matters most (inhibitory interneurons rather than pyramidal cells). · Evidence: Compensation by other subunits is documented in knockout models. The cell-type specificity is plausible but untested for these variants. · Usual reading assumes: Rescue means restoring the mutant protein. This one restores current without restoring the mutant subunit's surface. · Rejection: Paralog upregulation in the affected cell type does not raise GABA-evoked current. · Discriminating manipulation: Versus H1–H6, cell-type-specific viral or CRISPRa upregulation of the paralog, with the mutant allele silenced. Predicts mutant-subunit surface unchanged and current up. Any H1–H6 lever predicts mutant surface up.

H8. Statement: The mutant reaches the plasma membrane but is lost by accelerated endocytosis and lysosomal degradation, driven by altered phosphorylation or ubiquitination (PKC/PKA at the β3 intracellular loop, BDNF–TrkB signalling, AP2/clathrin). Blocking this restores surface and current. · Evidence: Phosphorylation of β-subunit loop sites and AP2 binding control GABA-A endocytosis. BDNF–TrkB lowers surface GABA-A receptors. Evidence specific to DEE variants is none known. · Usual reading assumes: Surface loss comes from failed ER export. This one assumes post-ER loss. · Rejection: Glycosylation maturity (EndoH resistance) shows no mutant at the plasma membrane to rescue, and dynamin or clathrin block leaves surface unchanged. · Discriminating manipulation: Versus H1–H3, block clathrin/dynamin-dependent endocytosis (class) or a TrkB-signalling class. Predicts surface and current up if the mutant is post-ER. Surface stays flat if it is ER-retained, where H1–H3 apply.

H9. Statement: Reduced palmitoylation (ZDHHC3/GODZ) impairs Golgi-to-surface delivery and synaptic retention (with gephyrin/neuroligin-2 clustering), and boosting palmitoylation raises synaptic surface expression and current. · Evidence: GODZ palmitoylates γ2 and some α subunits, and affects synaptic clustering. Evidence in DEE variants is none known. · Usual reading assumes: Palmitoylation is background modification. This one makes it the limiting step. · Rejection: ZDHHC3 overexpression leaves surface, and especially synaptic surface, unchanged. · Discriminating manipulation: Versus H8, ZDHHC3 overexpression against inhibition of the depalmitoylase class (ABHD17-type). Predicts synaptic surface and synaptic current up with more palmitoylation, and a loss with ZDHHC3 knockdown. H8 predicts no palmitoylation dependence.

H10. Statement: A dosage shortfall of total (WT plus mutant) subunit can be offset by raising transcription of the gene, which pushes more folded receptors to the surface, unless the mutant is dominant-negative. · Evidence: Haploinsufficiency is the working model for some GABRB3/GABRG2 variants, and CRISPRa or HDAC-inhibitor class approaches raise expression in related disorders. None known for these variants. · Usual reading assumes: A missense variant has a fixed per-molecule defect. This one says allele dosage changes the outcome. · Rejection: Raising transcription raises total protein, but the extra is mutant-containing and trapped in the ER, with no gain in surface or current. · Discriminating manipulation: Versus H6, CRISPRa of the gene against allele-selective silencing of the mutant allele. Predicts CRISPRa raises surface and current only if loss of function is haploinsufficient. Under dominant-negative action, mutant silencing raises current and CRISPRa lowers or does not change it.
