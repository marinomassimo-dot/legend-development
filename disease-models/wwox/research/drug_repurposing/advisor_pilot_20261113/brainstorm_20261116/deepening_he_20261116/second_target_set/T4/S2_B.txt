WWOX is a recessive loss-of-function gene where losing one allele is benign (registries/claim_registry_current.md:593, CLAIM 032, "haploinsufficiency … not deleterious"), and lever assignment is made per allele (analysis/mechanism_intervention_map.md:325-327 and 355-379, Chains F/G). The corpus carries no data on poisonous alleles, and it says a comparator model is not a read-across bench (mechanism_intervention_map.md:62, MECHANISM_TRANSFER_FIREWALL). · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading treats a "dysfunctional" subunit as a loss to be topped up. This one treats the mutant as an active poison, so removing it comes before adding anything, and an allele-selective tool outranks a dose-matched one. · Single observation that would reject it: In a heterozygous knock-in with wild-type transcript and protein measured unchanged, selective mutant knockdown does not improve the effector readout (GIRK current or cAMP inhibition), or it lowers the seizure threshold as much as pan-allele knockdown does. · Discriminating manipulation: Allele-selective versus pan-allele knockdown in the same cell population, against H2 (wild-type gene addition). Predicted motor: improves with selective knockdown, and pan-allele knockdown gives no motor gain. Predicted seizures: improve or stay unchanged with selective knockdown, and worsen with pan-allele knockdown.

H2. Origin S1-2 · Statement: Raising wild-type GNAO1 dosage in neurons, by gene addition without removing the mutant, dilutes or outcompetes the mutant subunit and restores enough total functional Gαo, provided the mutant is not a strong dominant-negative. · Corpus test: INSIDE class, SUPPORT by analogy only. The corpus holds gene addition as its lead lever. AAV9-hSynI-WWOX is T1 in the Wwox-null mouse, with an explicit "neither too little nor too much WWOX" safety limit (analysis/mechanism_intervention_map.md:412-414, R-01). Dose behaves as a threshold: the low dose does not rescue survival (mechanism_intervention_map.md:184-187). A partial, mosaic correction may suffice because haploinsufficiency is tolerated (claim_registry_current.md:593-594, CLAIM 032). Nothing is held for G-proteins, so this is support for the design logic, not for the G-protein claim. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading assumes a mutation must be removed to be cured. This one assumes the supply of wild-type subunit is the limiting step, so more wild-type works despite the mutant. It carries a built-in prediction that fails if the mutant sequesters Gβγ or receptors (H4). · Single observation that would reject it: Wild-type overexpression in a heterozygous knock-in gives no improvement of the effector readout, or it worsens the phenotype (overexpression toxicity), while the mutant protein level is unchanged. · Discriminating manipulation: Neuron-restricted wild-type addition versus allele-selective knockdown (H1). Predicted motor: modest improvement in loss-of-function-like alleles, little or none in dominant-negative alleles. Predicted seizures: improve in loss-of-function-like alleles, with no change or a worsening in dominant-negative or gain-of-function-like alleles. In WWOX the equivalent rescue repaired seizures (SWD/ECoG) and motor function together (mechanism_intervention_map.md:174-176), so the corpus precedent leans toward both improving.

This one places it at the heterotrimer level, a partner shortage, so overexpressing a different member of the complex becomes a lever. · Single observation that would reject it: In cells expressing the mutant, Gβ1γ2 overexpression does not restore GIRK or Cav2.2 modulation, or the mutant fails to co-immunoprecipitate Gβγ at higher affinity than wild type. · Discriminating manipulation: Neuronal Gβ1γ2 gain versus wild-type Gαo augmentation (H2). Predicted motor: smaller improvement than with H2. Predicted seizures: improvement, because effector-driven channel modulation is the most direct seizure-relevant readout. H5. H6. Origin S1-11 · Statement: The movement phenotype originates in the Gαo-dependent signalling of indirect-pathway (D2) striatal projection neurons, so restoring mutant-subunit function only in that cell type is sufficient for motor rescue. · Corpus test: NOT STATED for striatum (D2, striatal, cholinergic, chorea return 0 hits in the md grep, apart from one myelin-quantification mention at fulltext_dossiers/PMID42422765_partial_locators.md:141). The method is held as precedent: neuron-restricted manipulation separates cell types in WWOX. The corpus also records a motor phenotype with no structural explanation: rat ataxic gait is 95% penetrant and "not cerebellar" (claim_registry_current.md:717-726, CLAIM 039). That shows a motor phenotype and its anatomical origin can be dissociated, which is the premise of this hypothesis. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading assumes widespread dysfunction with a diffuse motor consequence. Predicted motor: improves with D2-restricted rescue only. Predicted seizures: unchanged, because the striatum is not the main generator.

H10. Origin ENGINE · Statement: In a mutant-G-protein DEE, the seizure state is partly an NMDAR-dependent network burst that can be suppressed without repairing the subunit, whereas the motor phenotype needs the subunit function itself; suppressing the state therefore treats the seizures and not the movement disorder. · Corpus test: SUPPORT by analogy, held evidence. In a WWOX slice, neuron-specific loss gives spontaneous bursting that depends on NMDAR activity and gap junctions (claim_registry_current.md:382-391, CLAIM 021), and d-APV abolishes the burst, which is T1 for the tool compound (analysis/therapeutic_translation_second_pass.md:395-406). The gap-junction half is downgraded (second_pass.md:408-420: carbenoxolone does not wash out and blocks NMDAR). The limiting caveat is held too: the recordings are P13-P17 in animals that die at 3-4 weeks, and chronic NMDAR block removes a signal needed for activity-dependent maturation (second_pass.md:398). Mice show no epilepsy, so seizures in the WWOX record are a rat phenotype (meta_gaba_paradox_current.md:19-20), and ataxia is more penetrant than seizures there (claim_registry_current.md:724). Nothing is held for G-proteins. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading assumes that repairing the signalling defect is the only route to fewer seizures. This one says a network state can be treated independently, at the cost of leaving the motor lesion untouched. · Single observation that would reject it: NMDAR blockade abolishes the burst in the mutant slice but seizures in vivo are unchanged, or the burst persists in d-APV, which would put the seizure origin upstream of the NMDAR. · Discriminating manipulation: NMDAR antagonist class against restoration of the subunit in the same preparation (nearest rival: H5, effector-level bypass). Predicted seizures: decrease with NMDAR block, as with H5. Predicted motor: unchanged, whereas subunit restoration (H2) changes motor. If the NMDAR block also improves motor scores, the motor phenotype is network-state driven too and this hypothesis fails.
