# Sixty hypotheses (statement and discriminating manipulation only)

## Z01
Statement: Resection followed by annealing at short direct repeats flanking the site deletes the cut segment, so tolerance is a repeat-dependent deletion.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H1, which needs no repeats): Make silent changes that add or erase a 6-8 nt direct repeat flanking the site, in a host with no end-joining machinery. Survival: it rises with the repeat and falls when the repeat is erased. Repaired site: a deletion with its junction at the introduced repeat and one repeat copy kept. With H1, survival would not follow repeats and junctions would show 0-4 nt microhomology scatter.

## Z02
Statement: Phage-encoded resection plus an annealase joins the two cut ends across direct repeats in the same genome, so survival rises with the presence of flanking repeats and the site is repaired as a deletion between repeats.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): Insert direct repeats (tens of bp) on both sides of the target site and keep the annealase gene. Survival: it rises at low MOI, where no sibling donor exists. A recombinase-donor route (H1) predicts no gain at MOI ~1 and no dependence on the repeats. Site: the readout is a deletion between the repeats leaving one repeat copy. H1 predicts a scarless or gene-converted site.

## Z03
Statement: The phage tolerates cuts by RecA-dependent recombination using an uncut co-infecting sister genome as template, so survival rises with multiplicity of infection and the repaired site copies the donor allele
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H8, replication outrunning the nuclease): infect at multiplicity well below 1 and compare with a high-multiplicity infection, with a silent marker in the sister genome that lies 200 bp from the cut. H5 predicts survival at low multiplicity collapses and the marker is co-converted into the repaired site. H8 predicts survival at low multiplicity is unchanged and no marker conversion occurs. The caution from the held reporter dossier applies: measure the repaired sequence itself, not a surrogate.

## Z04
Statement: Rolling-circle/concatemeric replication produces many redundant genome copies, so some copies stay uncut and the lineage persists even though individual copies are cut.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): Block late replication with a conditional initiator or replication-helicase mutant and keep the recombinase. Survival: it falls sharply at the same nuclease dose. If H1 carries the load, survival falls less. Site: survivors keep the original, uncut site (sequence unchanged) under H7, or are site-lost escapes after the block. H1 predicts repaired products, not an unchanged site.

## Z05
Statement: the phage survives by end-joining of the broken ends with a phage- or host-encoded Ku/ligase-type system that seals the break at the cost of a scar.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): infect at single-genome multiplicity and delete the phage ligase/Ku-like gene. Prediction: survival is lost only in the ligase-null phage, and survivors carry indel or microhomology-deletion scars at the site (vs parental sequence in H1).

## Z06
Statement: A phage-encoded Ku/LigD-type end-joining module religates cut genome ends, so survival comes from joining after the cut and the repaired site carries small indels.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H3, microhomology alt-EJ, and a host-only route): Delete the phage joiner gene in a host with no end-joining of its own, and add it back in trans to a phage lacking it. Survival: EOP drops by orders of magnitude under attack, with no change in unattacked growth, and is restored by complementation. Repaired site: short indels, mostly 0 to 4 nt of microhomology, at the junction, and the site is no longer cleavable. If H3 were right, the same deletion would have no effect and junctions would still follow repeats.

## Z07
Statement: The extent of end resection before commitment sets the repair route, so short resection gives end-joining and indels while long resection gives annealing or recombination and a wild-type or deleted site.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H1, direct joining): Delete the phage's own 5'-3' exonuclease while keeping its annealing protein and ligase intact. Survival: EOP falls at high attack intensity and the annealing route is lost. Repaired site: indels with 0-4 nt microhomology replace the repeat-bounded deletions and wild-type tracts. H1 predicts survival depends on the joiner, not on the exonuclease.

## Z08
Statement: A phage end-protecting protein (Gam or gp2 type) shields cut ends from host exonuclease, so the ends stay intact long enough for later repair.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H1, joining without end-protection): Delete the protector gene in a RecBCD-proficient host, then repeat in a recBCD-null host. Survival: EOP falls only in the proficient host and returns in the null host, which separates end-degradation from joining. Repaired site: wild type or a small deletion with no extended end loss, versus H1, where the deletion of the joiner leaves ends intact but unjoined and survivors vanish in both hosts.

## Z09
Statement: Co-infecting phage genomes cross-rescue each other by recombination, so a cut genome is restored from a cut-resistant partner and survival per genome rises with MOI.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H7, copy-number buffering): Co-infect the nuclease-sensitive phage with a helper phage whose target site carries silent changes, at fixed total MOI, with recombination functional. Survival: sensitive progeny rise with the helper fraction. If H7 holds, the helper adds copies but does not rescue the sensitive genome. Site: under H6, surviving sensitive progeny carry the helper's silent allele at the site (a recombinant). Under H7 the sensitive allele stays and the helper allele is not transferred.

## Z10
Statement: the phage places its genome in a nuclease-excluding compartment (a nucleus-like shell), so most genomes are never cut at all.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H6): delete the shell protein gene (e.g. the major shell protein). Prediction: survival falls, and surviving sites are uncut parental sequence (no repair signature at all).

## Z11
Statement: the phage uses the host RecBCD (or an analogue) as a repair enzyme, stopping it at phage-encoded Chi-like sequences, so cutting is converted into a recombination substrate and the repaired site is parental.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H3): remove or add Chi-like sites near the cut site, in a host with intact RecBCD. Prediction: removing the Chi sites lowers survival and shifts repair toward degradation; adding them raises survival. Surviving sites are parental sequence in both cases.

## Z12
Statement: phage survival under nuclease attack depends on error-prone repair that destroys the recognition site (a PAM/seed substitution or a site-internal indel), which produces escape mutants rather than repair.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): replace the phage polymerase with a high-fidelity (antimutator) allele. Prediction: fewer survivors appear under attack, and the survivors that remain carry the same classes of escape mutation at a lower rate. Site sequence in survivors is mutated at the PAM or seed (unlike H1 and H7).

## Z13
Statement: The infected cell is flooded with genome copies faster than the nuclease can cut them, so survival is a titration or kinetic race that needs no repair.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): delay early replication, for example with a temperature-sensitive or weakened replication initiator, with recombinase intact. Prediction: survival falls sharply. Survivors have a site sequence unchanged from wild type, because uncleaved genomes were never repaired, and no conversion markers are seen. Under H1 survival would fall only if recombination were also hit.

## Z14
Statement: A phage terminal protein or an end-binding protein (Gam-type) caps the cut ends against degradation, which keeps the end intact long enough for rejoining or recombination.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): Delete the terminal/end-binding protein and leave the recombinase intact. Survival: it falls at all cut loads, even at high MOI, because the ends are degraded before any donor can be used. Under H1 survival would fall mainly at low MOI. Site: survivors show deletions that extend beyond the target on one or both sides (exonucleolytic loss). A recombinase-null phage keeps intact ends and shows a failed rejoining instead.

## Z15
Statement: Headful packaging of circularly permuted, terminally redundant genomes lets a cut genome be reassembled from overlapping ends, so packaging acts as a repair step and a broken molecule can still yield a complete genome.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H7): Swap the headful terminase for a fixed-end (cos-type) system and keep replication and recombination. Survival: it falls even when copy number is unchanged, because there are no redundant overlaps. Under H7 survival would not change. Site: survivors carry large deletions/truncations (or uncut sites) rather than point repairs, because the cut genome cannot be reassembled.

## Z16
Statement: Phage proteins that cap or bind broken DNA ends (gp2, Gam-like, terminal protein) shield them from degradation long enough for rejoining, with no recombination needed.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): delete the end-capping gene while keeping recombination intact. Prediction: survival falls even though the recombination machinery is intact. The repaired site is wild type or identical to the template. H1 predicts survival is independent of end protection.

## Z17
Statement: The phage tolerates cuts by amplification, since replication produces genome copies faster than the nuclease can cut them, so the population of intact copies exceeds the cut-site burden without any repair
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H5): delay the onset of phage DNA replication with a conditional (temperature-sensitive or inducible) initiator, keeping the nuclease active. H8 predicts survival falls sharply with the delay, with no change in the repaired-site sequence class (wild-type, uncut). H5 predicts that survival is less sensitive to replication delay and that the repaired site is templated.

## Z18
Statement: The phage tolerates cuts by single-strand annealing mediated by its own RecT/Redβ-type annealase, which joins resected ends across flanking repeats and so deletes one repeat copy and the sequence between
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H5, RecA-dependent homologous recombination with a co-infecting genome): delete the phage annealase and infect at one high multiplicity in a RecA-proficient host. H4 predicts survival falls (SSA is gone) while a single-copy cut still kills. H5 predicts survival is retained at high multiplicity because RecA uses a sister genome. The repaired site under H4 is a repeat-flanked deletion; under H5 it is a copy of the sister allele.

## Z19
Statement: The phage tolerates nuclease cuts by direct, scarless re-ligation of the compatible ends by a phage-encoded or host DNA ligase, so cutting and sealing cycle futilely and a fraction of genomes escapes
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H2, mutagenic NHEJ): delete or inactivate the phage ligase in a host whose own ligase cannot seal these ends (or use a ligase-dead allele). H1 predicts survival falls and the surviving repaired sites remain 100% wild-type sequence, with no indels. H2 predicts that survivors carry indels and that survival falls as well, so the deciding readout is the sequence class (wild-type versus indel) in the survivors that remain.

## Z20
Statement: The phage tolerates cuts by break-induced replication, where the broken end invades a homologous region and primes DNA synthesis (recombination-dependent replication), giving a long conversion tract and genome copy number gain
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H5, short-tract homologous recombination): inactivate the phage helicase loader or primosome factor that is needed only for recombination-primed replication, while leaving origin-driven replication intact. H7 predicts survival falls and the surviving repaired sites show long (kb-scale) conversion tracts only in the remaining survivors. H5 predicts survival and the short conversion tract (hundreds of bp) are unchanged.

## Z21
Statement: Two or more parallel repair routes each suffice, so deleting the phage's own module alone leaves survival unchanged while deleting it together with the host backup collapses it.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H1, a single required module): Delete the phage joiner in a wild-type host (expect no change in EOP), then in a host also lacking the backup route (expect collapse). Survival: single deletion neutral, double deletion falls by orders of magnitude. Repaired site: single deletion shifts the spectrum between indels (joiner route) and wild type or donor tracts (recombination route), while H1 predicts survivors vanish with the single deletion.

## Z22
Statement: A phage Ku/ligase (NHEJ-like) module joins the broken ends directly, tolerating cleavage at the cost of small deletions at the site.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H8, mutational escape by host polymerases): delete the phage ku/ligase gene. Prediction: survival falls under cuts in essential regions. In wild type the repaired site carries microhomology-flanked 1 to 20 bp deletions that are not found in the deletion mutant, which shows only point-mutational escape.

## Z23
Statement: A broken end invades an intact homologous copy and primes recombination-dependent replication (T4-like, UvsX/UvsY-driven), so the cut becomes a replication start instead of a lethal lesion.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): inactivate the strand-invasion recombinase while leaving the phage annealing proteins intact. Prediction: survival falls under cuts, and a neutral marker several kb from the cut shows conversion to the template allele (long tracts) in wild type, whereas H1 predicts short tracts at the site only.

## Z24
Statement: Repair by homology is gated by whether a replicated template exists at the time of the cut, so a cut during the single-copy early window is lethal and a cut after replication onset is repaired.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H7, which asks only whether a template exists): Place the phage's replication initiator under a late promoter so the single-copy window is extended, with recA left wild type. Survival: EOP falls, in contrast to H7 where only recA loss matters, and the effect is largest when the nuclease acts early. Repaired site: survivors are wild type from a later-made copy, and at an early-cut stage the site is end-joined with indels.

## Z25
Statement: The genome is physically sequestered from the nuclease, for example by a protein shell or nucleus-like compartment, so no double-strand breaks form (not tolerance, but an alternative to it).
Discriminating manipulation (rival H7): delete or truncate the shell protein gene (for example chmA). Prediction: survival falls and cleaved genome fragments are seen. A truncated mutant should be rescued by high MOI, with the cleavage site sequence unchanged in survivors, since no repair is involved. H7 would not predict loss from the deletion.

## Z26
Statement: No repair occurs: cut genomes are lost and the infection is carried by uncut copies, so tolerance is a matter of copy number, partial cleavage and redundancy.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): add a second cleavage site in the genome. Prediction: survival falls multiplicatively, since the cutting probability per genome rises, and the surviving sites are wild type, whereas with repair H1 predicts little decline.

## Z27
Statement: A phage 5'-to-3' exonuclease sets the amount of end resection, which channels a cut away from mutagenic end joining toward annealing/recombination, so more resection raises scarless and repeat-deletion repair and less resection raises indels.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1, which deletes the whole recombinase): Make the exonuclease catalytically dead and leave the annealase and strand-exchange activity intact. Survival: it falls modestly at low cut number and strongly at high cut number, less than for a full recombinase deletion. Site: the repeat-deletion and scarless classes drop and the indel class rises. Separate prediction (from DL-METH-084): over-supplying the exonuclease is not the mirror image, because too much resection can itself remove the site, so survival is non-monotonic.

## Z28
Statement: The phage tolerates cuts because its genome is a concatemer packaged by a headful mechanism, so a cut simply creates a new packaging start and pieces are packaged as permuted, terminally redundant genomes
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H8): replace the headful packaging signal and terminase with a fixed-end (cos-type) system and keep replication unchanged. H9 predicts survival falls and the permuted-end packaging at the cut vanishes. H8 predicts survival is unchanged. The cleavage site itself is not repaired in either; the sequence readout is the end position of packaged genomes.

## Z29
Statement: RecA-dependent recombination with an intact sibling genome in the same cell restores the original sequence by gene conversion.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H3, repeat-dependent deletion): Compare recA-null and wild-type hosts at constant phage replication, and in the wild-type host add marked SNPs near the cut on the donor copy. Survival: EOP falls in recA-null only. Repaired site: unchanged wild type, still cleavable, with donor-derived SNP tracts around the site in wild-type survivors. H3 predicts deletions at repeats and no donor tract.

## Z30
Statement: the phage tolerates RM cuts by simple religation of cohesive ends, giving futile cut-and-reseal cycles that restore the site to its exact parental sequence.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): use a host strain with a ligase thermosensitive allele, or delete the phage ligase, and compare an overhang-generating RM cut with a blunt cut. Prediction: survival falls without the ligase, and the surviving site is exactly parental (no scar), unlike H2.

## Z31
Statement: A protein shell around the replicating genome excludes nucleases, so no break ever forms and nothing is repaired.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H1, repair after cutting): Disrupt the shell protein gene. Survival: EOP under attack falls sharply and cleavage becomes visible in the genome. Repaired site: in the intact shelled phage the site is uncut and unchanged, with no repair signature, while every repair hypothesis predicts a signature in survivors. Add a gam/joiner gene to the shell-less phage to test whether survival is recovered by repair.

## Z32
Statement: Cleaved genomes are repaired by RecA/UvsX-type homologous recombination, using an intact sister genome (or concatemer copy) from the same infected cell as the template, so survival rises with the number of co-replicating genome copies.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H2, single-strand annealing): delete the phage strand-exchange recombinase (uvsX-like) while keeping the annealing protein. Prediction: survival falls strongly, and the effect is greater at low MOI. Survivors that remain carry the intact, re-cleavable wild-type site, with gene-conversion tracts copying the sister's sequence.

## Z33
Statement: Error-prone repair or replication through the break creates point or indel mutations at the cleavage site, so tolerance is the heritable escape of a mutant subpopulation.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): swap in an antimutator, high-fidelity polymerase allele, or a mutator allele. Prediction: survival tracks mutation rate, falling with the antimutator and rising with the mutator. Survivors carry site mutations and resist re-challenge. H1 survivors would be wild type and stay sensitive.

## Z34
Statement: phage-induced SOS-type repair of the host supplies the repair factors, and the phage borrows them.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): infect a lexA(Ind-) host. Prediction: survival falls and is not recovered by phage recombinase alone. Surviving sites show host-error-prone substitutions or indels where the SOS polymerases act.

## Z35
Statement: the phage tolerates cutting by dilution: a concatemeric or headful-packaged genome carries redundant copies, so a cut removes one copy but packaging still picks up an intact one.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): delay or block phage DNA replication (a temperature-sensitive replication mutant) until after the challenge. Prediction: survival falls roughly in proportion to the copy-number loss, with no repair needed, so the surviving sites are the uncut parental sequence (no repaired site) and no scars appear.

## Z36
Statement: the phage repairs the cut through single-strand annealing between direct repeats flanking the site, independent of RecA, which gives a deletion of one repeat copy.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): flank the site with direct repeats, or remove them, in a recA-null host. Prediction: survival is repeat-dependent and recA-independent. The repaired site shows loss of one repeat unit plus the region between the repeats (a deletion), which differs from H1's parental restoration.

## Z37
Statement: A phage Ku/ligase pair seals the ends directly (end joining), so survivors carry small indels at the site and survival depends on that module at high cut load.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): Delete the Ku or ligase gene and leave the recombinase intact. Survival: it falls only when the break has no homologous partner (low MOI, single copy). H1 predicts no change when siblings are present. Site: small indels with 0–few nt microhomology vanish, and survivors are escapes or deletions flanked by long homology.

## Z38
Statement: Phage-encoded DNA modification (glucosylated hydroxymethyl-cytosine, methylation) reduces the cutting rate below the repair capacity, so repair of the remaining cuts suffices.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H7): delete the modification gene and keep repair genes. Prediction: survival falls steeply (threshold), and surviving sites are wild type. If instead the survivors still tolerate, H9 is rejected. H7 predicts no threshold behaviour.

## Z39
Statement: Cleaved genomes are repaired by single-strand annealing between direct repeats flanking the cut, through a Redbeta/RecT-type annealing protein after end resection, so the repair removes the intervening sequence.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): place the cleavage site between engineered direct repeats, or remove the existing ones. Prediction: with repeats, survival rises independently of MOI. Without repeats, survival falls to the H1 level. The repaired site is deleted along with one repeat copy and is no longer cleavable.

## Z40
Statement: Concatemeric rolling-circle replication makes many copies of the site per genome, so cutting some copies is harmless and the packaging machine takes the uncut ones.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H5, repair by homology): Run two arms. Arm 1 blocks recombination (recA-null host, annealing-protein gene deleted) and keeps concatemer formation. Arm 2 forces monomeric replication with recombination intact. Survival: arm 1 keeps survival (redundancy model) and arm 2 loses it. H5 predicts the reverse. Repaired site: unchanged wild type in arm 1, which stays cleavable.

## Z41
Statement: The phage genome carries Chi-like sites so that the host RecBCD, once it meets them, switches from degrading the broken end to loading RecA and recombining, so tolerance depends on Chi position relative to the cut
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H5): ablate by synonymous recoding the Chi-like sites on the side of the cut from which RecBCD enters, leaving RecA and the genome copy number untouched. H6 predicts survival falls even at high multiplicity, and a repaired-site product copied from a sister drops sharply. H5 predicts survival at high multiplicity is unchanged by the ablation.

## Z42
Statement: a phage end-capping protein (Gam- or gp2-like) blocks host exonuclease degradation of the free ends, which keeps the break repairable and gives time for any downstream repair.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): delete the capping gene and keep the recombinase. Prediction: survival falls under both RM and CRISPR attack, and if any survivors exist the site shows end resection (deletions spreading from the cut), not a clean parental restoration.

## Z43
Statement: Error-prone repair, by host translesion polymerases or the SOS response, produces indels or point changes at the cut, so tolerance is escape by site mutation.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H3): place the cleavage site in a non-coding versus an essential coding region. Prediction: survival is far higher with the non-coding site, because indels there are tolerated, and the sequences carry indels or substitutions. H3 predicts the pattern depends on the Ku gene, not on site position.

## Z44
Statement: the phage survives by homologous repair of the cut site from an uncut sister or co-infecting genome copy, using a phage- or host-encoded strand-exchange function, so the cut is restored to the parental sequence.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): supply a second, marked-but-site-containing genome copy in trans, or equivalently delete the phage recombinase (RecA analogue, e.g. UvsX or Redα/β class). Prediction: survival falls to the single-copy level in the recombinase-null phage, and the surviving repaired sites are parental sequence, or a donor-marker conversion tract, with no indels.

## Z45
Statement: Fast replication and high copy number deplete a limited pool of nuclease, so survivors are cells in which the enzyme runs out before every genome is cut.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H4, redundancy): Slow phage replication with a temperature-sensitive polymerase while holding the nuclease constant. Survival: it collapses at the restrictive temperature. Repaired site: survivors keep the original site, with no repair signature, since the cell was never fully cut. H4 predicts survival depends on concatemer structure, not rate, so raising polymerase activity would not matter.

## Z46
Statement: A broken end invades a homologous genome copy and primes replication (break-induced or recombination-dependent replication), so the break is bypassed by copying rather than patched.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): inactivate the helicase loader (gp59-like) while leaving strand exchange intact. Prediction: survival falls, with local patching still possible. Survivors under H9 show long conversion tracts, with distal markers from the template copied along. H1 survivors would show short tracts.

## Z47
Statement: A phage strand-exchange or annealing recombinase (Red-beta/UvsX/RecT-type) repairs the cut by templated recombination from a sibling genome in the same cell, which raises survival with cut number and restores the original site sequence.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H3, end-joining): Delete the recombinase gene and leave the ligase/Ku-type module intact. Survival: it falls with rising cut number and rising nuclease dose if H1 holds. If H3 carries the load, survival stays. Site: under H1, donor-templated or scarless products and gene-conversion tracts disappear and survivors are only indels or escapes. Under H3, the indel spectrum persists unchanged.

## Z48
Statement: Phage-encoded single-strand-annealing and exonuclease recombination functions (Red/RecET/Erf-like) repair the cut by homologous recombination with an uncut sister copy in the replicating concatemer, so the cleavage site is restored to wild type.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H2, host-driven repair): delete the phage recombinase pair and keep Chi-free or Chi-rich sites constant. Prediction: survival under all nucleases drops, by more under high cleavage load. The few survivors carry cleavage-site mutations or are uncut genomes, whereas wild-type phage shows a wild-type repaired site.

## Z49
Statement: Direct repeats flanking cleavage sites let single-strand annealing rejoin the ends and delete the intervening sequence, so surviving phages carry a single repeat copy and lose the site.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1, sister-copy repair): insert or remove a direct repeat pair around the site, with the recombinase intact. Prediction: survival rises with repeats and the product is a deletion with one repeat retained. Without repeats the survivor site is wild type (H1) or the survival is lower.

## Z50
Statement: The phage tolerates multiple nuclease systems by physically excluding them, via a protein shell or nucleus-like compartment around its replicating DNA, so most genomes never receive a DSB and the few that do are repaired by routes H1–H7
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H8): delete or inactivate the shell protein gene while keeping replication and repair genes intact. H10 predicts survival falls to the level of the most sensitive nuclease system and the cut-site fraction in the genome rises, with survivors showing the same repaired-site classes as the intact phage, only fewer. H8 predicts that the shell deletion does not change the cut fraction, only the survival.

## Z51
Statement: The phage tolerates cuts through host-driven microhomology-mediated end joining (host polymerase and ligase), giving deletions flanked by short microhomologies
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H2): recode the 2–10 bp microhomologies flanking the cut site synonymously while leaving all repair genes in place. H3 predicts survival falls and the survivor spectrum shifts to other junctions, or collapses to only rare events. H2 predicts survival is unaffected and the indel spectrum is only mildly altered.

## Z52
Statement: Apparent tolerance is selection of rare pre-existing site variants (point, PAM or seed changes), not active repair, so survivors are jackpot-distributed escapes that carry no repair signature.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1/H3): Single-plaque-purify the stock, then run a fluctuation test across many parallel small cultures and sequence the survivors' cut site. Survival: if H10 holds, survivor counts are jackpot-distributed (variance far above the mean) and do not rise with cut number. If repair carries the load, survival is uniform across cultures and rises with MOI or repair-gene dose. Site: H10 survivors carry only point or PAM/seed changes, and none carry indels or microhomology deletions. Repair predicts indels, microhomology deletions or donor-templated sequences.

## Z53
Statement: The phage repairs by homologous recombination with host chromosomal or prophage sequences that share homology with it, so the template comes from the host.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): move the cleavage site into a segment that has no homology to the host, and in a second construct add a marked homologous cassette to the host or prophage. Prediction: at MOI of about 1, survival falls for the non-homologous site and rises again with the cassette. The repaired site carries the cassette's marker. Under H1 the host cassette would not matter and the marker would never transfer.

## Z54
Statement: The phage tolerates cuts through a Ku/LigD-like end-joining system that ligates ends after limited end processing, so survivors carry small indels at the cut
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H3, alt-EJ using host factors): delete the phage ku (and ligD) alone, in a host that has no Ku/LigD of its own. H2 predicts survival falls and the survivors that remain are templated or wild-type with no short indels. H3 predicts survival and the indel/microhomology spectrum are unchanged, because the host supplies the end-joining.

## Z55
Statement: A phage-encoded end-binding protein (Gam- or gp2-like) shields the broken ends from RecBCD and other exonucleases, so the ends stay intact long enough for other repair to act.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): delete the end-protector alone while leaving the recombinase intact. Prediction: survival falls in a recBCD+ host and recovers in a recBCD-null host. The repaired site sequence is unchanged in kind, with wild-type or converted sequence and fewer survivors. Under H1 the same deletion would not matter.

## Z56
Statement: Phage-encoded Ku and ligase (LigD-like) join the ends by non-homologous end joining, which tolerates the break at the price of small indels.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): delete the phage Ku gene in a phage that is otherwise recombination-proficient. Prediction: survival falls even at high MOI. Survivors under H3 carry short deletions or microhomology-flanked indels at the cut. H1 survivors would be wild type.

## Z57
Statement: A phage or host ligase directly re-seals cleaved ends, and where ends are cohesive this restores the identical sequence, creating a cut and re-seal cycle that lets a fraction of genomes get through.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H3, non-homologous end joining): delete the phage ligase gene and test an enzyme-matched pair of cohesive-end and blunt-end sites. Prediction: survival falls with the deletion, and more for cohesive-end sites. The repaired site is the exact wild-type sequence, with no indels, and so stays re-cleavable. Under H3 the repaired sites would carry indels.

## Z58
Statement: The host RecBCD/RecA pathway repairs the phage break, and the phage relies on Chi-like sequences or on RecBCD modulation to steer degradation toward recombination instead of loss of the genome.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1): add or remove Chi sites flanking the cleavage site in the phage, with its own recombinase left intact. Prediction: survival rises with Chi added and falls with Chi removed, with no change in the repaired sequence (wild type). H1 predicts no Chi dependence.

## Z59
Statement: Multiplicity reactivation: cut genomes from co-infecting phages recombine, giving an intact genome from fragments of different parents.
Discriminating manipulation (rival H1, intra-genome sister repair): make the phage replication-defective so there is a single copy per cell, and co-infect with a second genome carrying neutral markers near the cut. Prediction: survival is near zero at multiplicity 1 and rises with co-infection. The repaired site carries the other parent's markers (inter-genome exchange). H1 predicts survival at multiplicity 1 with replication intact and markers from the same parent.

## Z60

Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): Delete the helicase/loader that primes replication from the invading end and leave strand exchange intact. Survival: it falls at high cut number only.
