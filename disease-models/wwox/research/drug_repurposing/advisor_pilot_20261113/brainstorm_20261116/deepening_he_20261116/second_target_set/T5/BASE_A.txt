H1. Statement: Raising ER folding capacity (BiP/Hsp70 chaperone cycle, driven by the ATF6 arm of the UPR) increases the folded, assembly-competent fraction of the mutant subunit, so more of it reaches the surface and the current rises. · Evidence: Partial support. Work from the Mu lab (on GABRA1 D219N and related variants) reports that enhancing ER proteostasis (BiP activation, ATF6 activation) raises surface levels of mutant GABA-A subunits. Chemical chaperones such as 4-phenylbutyrate partly rescue GABRA1 A322D and GABRG2 truncation variants in cell models. Evidence in patient-derived neurons is limited, and I cannot cite specifics for the GABRB3 or GABRG2 DEE variants. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading treats a missense variant as a fixed folding defect that the cell clears. This one treats the defect as a kinetic partition between folding and degradation that the chaperone network can shift. · Rejection: Selective ATF6 activation (or BiP overexpression) in cells expressing the mutant subunit leaves total and surface mutant protein unchanged by surface biotinylation or flow cytometry (<10% change, wild-type control unaffected). · Discriminating manipulation (vs. H2, ERAD block): Genetic ATF6 activation or BiP overexpression, with the degradation machinery left intact. It predicts surface expression up and an increase in GABA-evoked current in proportion to the surface rise. If the ERAD block (H2) alone gave the same result, folding would not be the limiting step.

H2. Statement: Degradation of the mutant subunit by the SEL1L-HRD1 ERAD pathway (with p97 extraction and the proteasome) is the dominant loss route, so reducing it gives more mutant protein a chance to assemble and exit the ER. · Evidence: For GABRA1 and GABRG2 variants, proteasome inhibitors and ERAD-factor manipulation raise total mutant protein, but this often yields retained, non-surface protein. Whether it raises the surface fraction is mixed. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading assumes that more protein means more function. This one holds that ERAD removal buys time for slow folding, but that release is not guaranteed. · Rejection: HRD1 or SEL1L knockdown raises total mutant protein but leaves surface protein flat and current flat. That rejects "rescue", and it supports H1 or a defect in ER exit instead. · Discriminating manipulation (vs. H1): HRD1 or SEL1L knockout, or a dominant-negative p97, with the folding machinery unchanged. It predicts total protein up and surface expression up only modestly. It predicts GABA-evoked current up less than surface expression if the rescued protein is gating-defective. A current lower than the surface rise predicts is the signature that separates trafficking from function.

H3. Statement: ER calcium handling (class: L-type Ca2+ channel blockers, SERCA activation) lowers ERAD flux and raises ER chaperone function, increasing surface mutant subunit through a calcium-dependent route rather than a direct folding route. · Evidence: Mu-lab reports that verapamil (an L-type calcium channel blocker) reduces ERAD of mutant GABRA1 and raises its surface level and current in cells. I do not know of replication in DEE patient neurons. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading takes calcium channel blockers as irrelevant to receptor biogenesis. This one makes ER calcium a regulator of proteostasis. · Rejection: Clamping cytosolic and ER Ca2+ (e.g., an ER-targeted indicator and a SERCA-activation manipulation) leaves surface expression unchanged, while the blocker class still raises it. That points to an off-target route. · Discriminating manipulation (vs. H2): Genetic SERCA2 overexpression or an ER-Ca2+ clamp in HRD1-deficient cells. If the effect needs an intact ERAD pathway, it is upstream of ERAD. It predicts surface expression up and GABA-evoked current up in the ERAD-intact cells only; in the ERAD-deficient cells it predicts no additional gain.

H4. Statement: A membrane-permeant ligand that binds the assembled interface (a positive allosteric modulator class, or a pharmacological chaperone) stabilises the folded or assembling subunit in the ER, raising surface expression and also potentiating channel gating. · Evidence: Partial and indirect. Pharmacological chaperones are established for other ion channels and transporters. For GABA-A receptors, some reports link benzodiazepine-site or other modulator ligands to altered surface levels. I cannot name a ligand shown to rescue a DEE missense variant. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading treats a PAM as acting only on gating at the surface. This one has it act earlier, in the ER. · Rejection: A membrane-impermeant analogue of the same class (same binding site) raises current acutely but not surface expression after washout, and the permeant version also does not raise surface expression. That makes the effect purely gating. · Discriminating manipulation (vs. H1): Binding-site-dead mutation in cis, plus permeant versus impermeant ligand of the same class. It predicts surface expression up with the permeant ligand only (not with the impermeant one), and current up more than surface expression (surface rise plus potentiation). A current gain with no surface gain means gating modulation, not chaperoning.

H5. Statement: The mutant protein is limited by the availability of partner subunits and assembly factors (alpha/beta/gamma stoichiometry; LHFPL4/GARLH4-type auxiliary proteins), so raising these raises surface assembly. · Evidence: Partner subunits and auxiliary proteins govern surface assembly and synaptic targeting (LHFPL4 and neuroligin-2 affect clustering). Whether they limit mutant-subunit rescue is not shown to my knowledge. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading puts the defect in the mutant subunit alone. This one puts part of it in a limiting assembly pool. · Rejection: Co-expressing or overexpressing the partner subunits or auxiliary proteins in the same cell type leaves mutant surface expression unchanged. · Discriminating manipulation (vs. H1): Co-expression of partner subunit(s) at a fixed mutant dose, in HEK cells, then repeated in iPSC-derived neurons. It predicts surface expression up and GABA-evoked current up if the mutant is assembly-competent. If it does not rise, folding is the block.

H6. Statement: Post-ER trafficking is the block: palmitoylation (ZDHHC3/GODZ) and Golgi export (e.g. BIG2/ARF-dependent) are limiting, so raising them forwards more mutant-containing receptors to the surface. · Evidence: GODZ palmitoylation of the gamma2 subunit controls GABA-A receptor Golgi-to-surface trafficking and synaptic clustering. For missense DEE variants, none known. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading puts the defect before the Golgi. This one puts a share of it after. · Rejection: Mutant protein shows endoglycosidase-H-sensitive (ER-form) glycans only, and ZDHHC3 overexpression does not change this. That rejects a Golgi-stage block. · Discriminating manipulation (vs. H2): ZDHHC3 overexpression versus palmitoylation-site mutation, with the ERAD pathway unaffected. It predicts surface expression up and current up only where the mutant already has endoglycosidase-H-resistant forms. No effect if the mutant is ER-retained.

H7. Statement: Increased endocytosis and lysosomal turnover (clathrin/AP2, with phosphorylation-dependent beta-subunit internalisation) remove mutant receptors that did reach the surface, so inhibiting internalisation raises their surface residency. · Evidence: AP2 binding to the beta subunit is regulated by phosphorylation (PKA/PKC sites in the beta3 intracellular loop), and this controls surface lifetime of GABA-A receptors. Whether mutant-subunit turnover is accelerated is not shown to my knowledge. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading treats surface loss as an ER problem. This one treats it as increased post-surface removal. · Rejection: Surface-protein half-life (biotin pulse-chase) for the mutant is equal to wild-type, so removal is not faster. · Discriminating manipulation (vs. H2): Phospho-null beta-subunit (S408/409A-type) or a dynamin-class endocytosis block, versus a proteasome block. It predicts surface expression up only if mutant receptors reach the surface, and GABA-evoked current up in proportion. If surface half-life is normal, no change is predicted.

H8. Statement: The mutation affects synaptic anchoring and lateral diffusion (gephyrin, neuroligin-2, calcineurin-dependent gamma2 S327 dephosphorylation) rather than total surface protein, so the functional deficit is at synapses while whole-cell surface expression is near normal. · Evidence: Gephyrin and neuroligin-2 anchor GABA-A receptors, and calcineurin controls their lateral mobility. Applied to DEE missense variants, none known. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading says "loss of surface protein". This one says the loss is of synaptic, not total, surface protein. · Rejection: Whole-cell surface biotinylation shows a clear drop (>30%) for the mutant. That shows total surface loss. · Discriminating manipulation (vs. H7): Gephyrin knockdown (or a calcineurin-inhibitor class) in neurons. It predicts total surface expression unchanged, synaptic clusters and mIPSC amplitude changed, and whole-cell GABA-evoked current from exogenous agonist unchanged (or tonic current changed).

H9. Statement: Transcriptional up-regulation of the wild-type allele (HDAC inhibition or targeted gene activation on that allele) compensates for the mutant allele's loss and restores total surface receptor. · Evidence: Some evidence that HDAC-inhibitor classes change GABA-A subunit transcript levels in neuronal models. Evidence in DEE variant models: none known. Variant effects on the wild-type allele are also unclear. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading seeks to rescue the mutant protein. This one rescues the wild-type protein by dosage. · Rejection: The wild-type allele's transcript rises, but surface protein does not (a post-transcriptional limit), so the compensation fails. · Discriminating manipulation (vs. H10): Allele-specific activation of the wild-type allele (CRISPRa) in heterozygous iPSC neurons. It predicts surface expression up and GABA-evoked current up with the wild-type-assembled receptors. If the mutant exerts dominant-negative effects (H10), the gain is smaller than the dosage predicts.

H10. Statement: The mutant subunit acts as a dominant-negative in the ER by co-assembling with wild-type subunits and dragging them into degradation, so silencing only the mutant allele frees wild-type protein and raises surface expression above the level expected from haploinsufficiency alone. · Evidence: Several GABRG2 and GABRA1 variants reduce co-expressed wild-type subunit surface levels in cell models, which is consistent with a dominant-negative effect, but it differs among variants. For specific DEE missense variants: partial. · What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading counts the loss as one allele (50%). This one says the loss exceeds 50% because the mutant poisons the wild-type partner. · Rejection: Co-expressed wild-type subunit surface level in the presence of the mutant equals wild-type alone (no dominant-negative). Mutant silencing then produces only dosage-proportional change. · Discriminating manipulation (vs. H9): Allele-specific silencing (siRNA or ASO) of the mutant transcript in heterozygous neurons. It predicts surface expression up above 50% of normal and GABA-evoked current up, more than haploinsufficiency would predict. If it rises only by dosage, H9 fits better.
