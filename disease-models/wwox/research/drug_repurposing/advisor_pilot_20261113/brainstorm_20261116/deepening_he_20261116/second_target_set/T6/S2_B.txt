H1. Origin: S1-2
- Statement: A phage strand-exchange or annealing recombinase (Red-beta/UvsX/RecT-type) repairs the cut by templated recombination from a sibling genome in the same cell, which raises survival with cut number and restores the original site sequence.
- Corpus test: not stated for phage. The phage terms hit 0 times. The held analogue is support by analogy only: HDR is a measured repair route for a DSB (PMID38499540.md:128-135; discovery_ledger_current.md:399-400). Two held cautions apply. In the same laboratory's data, gain and loss of a repair factor did not move HDR symmetrically (discovery_ledger_current.md:2235-2238, DL-METH-084). An HDR readout at 0.03–0.83% of events is a floor and cannot show "no change" (PMID38499540.md:162-165).
- What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading assumes that a cleaved phage genome is lost and the phage needs one inhibitor per nuclease. This one says the sibling copies in the cell are the repair donor, so one repair module covers many nucleases.
- Rejection observation: A recombinase-null phage keeps wild-type survival at high cut number and has the same distribution of repaired-site sequences.
- Discriminating manipulation (vs H3, end-joining): Delete the recombinase gene and leave the ligase/Ku-type module intact. Survival: it falls with rising cut number and rising nuclease dose if H1 holds. If H3 carries the load, survival stays. Site: under H1, donor-templated or scarless products and gene-conversion tracts disappear and survivors are only indels or escapes. Under H3, the indel spectrum persists unchanged.

H2. Origin: S1-6
- Statement: Phage-encoded resection plus an annealase joins the two cut ends across direct repeats in the same genome, so survival rises with the presence of flanking repeats and the site is repaired as a deletion between repeats.
- Corpus test: not stated for phage. SSA is held only as a listed readout of the eukaryotic pathway: "WWOX loss increases HDR/SSA and RPA32/RAD51, reduces NHEJ/alt-NHEJ" (discovery_ledger_current.md:399). This is thin support by analogy, with no experimental detail.
- What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading assumes repair needs an intact template from a sibling. This one says repeats on the same molecule suffice, so a single-copy infection can be repaired.
- Rejection observation: Phage with repeats flanking the site and phage without them survive equally, and no survivor carries a deletion with a single repeat copy.
- Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): Insert direct repeats (tens of bp) on both sides of the target site and keep the annealase gene. Survival: it rises at low MOI, where no sibling donor exists. A recombinase-donor route (H1) predicts no gain at MOI ~1 and no dependence on the repeats. Site: the readout is a deletion between the repeats leaving one repeat copy. H1 predicts a scarless or gene-converted site.

H4. Origin: ENGINE (held circuit DL-MECH-024)
- Statement: A phage 5'-to-3' exonuclease sets the amount of end resection, which channels a cut away from mutagenic end joining toward annealing/recombination, so more resection raises scarless and repeat-deletion repair and less resection raises indels.
- Corpus test: support by analogy only; the phage version is not stated. discovery_ledger_current.md:398-401: WWOX-WW1 binds BRCA1 and limits end resection and HDR; WWOX loss raises HDR/SSA and RPA32/RAD51 and lowers NHEJ/alt-NHEJ (belief "medio-alto in the cellular system"). - What the usual reading assumes and this one changes: The usual reading treats repair-route choice as set by which repair enzymes exist. This one says the extent of resection decides the route, so the exonuclease is a switch rather than an extra route.
- Rejection observation: A nuclease-dead exonuclease with intact annealase and ligase leaves both survival and the repaired-site spectrum unchanged.
- Discriminating manipulation (vs H1, which deletes the whole recombinase): Make the exonuclease catalytically dead and leave the annealase and strand-exchange activity intact. Survival: it falls modestly at low cut number and strongly at high cut number, less than for a full recombinase deletion. Site: the repeat-deletion and scarless classes drop and the indel class rises. Separate prediction (from DL-METH-084): over-supplying the exonuclease is not the mirror image, because too much resection can itself remove the site, so survival is non-monotonic.
