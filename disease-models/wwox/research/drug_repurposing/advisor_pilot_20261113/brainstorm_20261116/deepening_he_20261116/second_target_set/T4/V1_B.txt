PRELIMINARY NOTE (read first). The folder holds NO record on any heterotrimeric G-protein alpha subunit. A Grep for GNAO1, GNAI, GNAQ, GNAS, G-protein, Galpha, heterotrimeric, GPCR, adenylyl and cAMP over disease-models/ returned only incidental hits. These were an unrelated breast-cancer "leucine-rich repeated-containing G protein-coupled receptor" title in batch_queue.md, and some corpus-seed TSV/JSONL titles. So all ten hypotheses below are ESPANSIONE: they apply general G-alpha biology, labelled as such, to the question. The folder supplies only (a) analogue WWOX material, used as method and caution, and (b) several rules that constrain how a lever may be claimed. Those rules are: mechanism-to-lever triage, "stable but inert", marker is not function, systemic-null confounding, seizure control is not disease modification, and genotype-by-treatment interaction. Nothing in the folder contradicts any H directly. Where a WWOX file warns against a move, I say so. "Held evidence" lines therefore mostly give the analogue or the caution, not G-alpha data. Dose and drug recommendations are deliberately absent. Everything is mechanism-level and IPOTESI.

H1. Statement: If the mutant alpha is a loss-of-function allele that is partly misfolded or unstable but retains some activity, then raising its folded abundance (proteostasis or chaperone class, or genetically enhancing the stability of the same subunit) restores signalling in proportion to the rescued protein. · Held evidence: /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/proteostasis_rationale.md and /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/variant_triage_rescuability.md. These give the class-level logic that missense with a folding defect points to a chaperone lever. They also record that the ΔΔG band was retracted and that "stable but inert" is a demonstrated phenotype, so abundance alone cannot be taken as success. This logic is held only for WWOX, not for G-alpha. · Usual reading assumes / this one changes: The usual reading assumes the mutant protein is present and merely inactive, or is simply a gain of function. This reading says that abundance is the limiting step for at least some alleles. The folder warns that the cause of loss (synthesis, insolubility or turnover) must be separated and measured, not assumed. · Single observation that rejects it: In variant-expressing neurons, a cycloheximide chase plus a soluble/insoluble split shows the mutant's half-life and soluble fraction equal to wild type. Or raising mutant abundance by a proteostasis-class manipulation raises protein but not receptor-evoked effector output (cAMP or GIRK/Cav current). That result rejects it. · Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H2, direct allosteric rescue of the existing protein): Boost the folding-capacity branch (HSP70/90-class induction) in variant neurons while the receptor and effector inputs are held clamped. Predicted: effector output and the protein pool rise together, so motor and seizure scores improve only if the allele is unstable. If it is stable but inert, protein rises, function does not, and neither motor function nor seizures change.

H2. Statement: A mutant alpha that is stable but has altered nucleotide handling (slow GDP release, faster GTP hydrolysis, or impaired receptor coupling) can be restored by a ligand or genetic change that acts on the subunit's activation cycle (guanine-nucleotide exchange or GTPase tuning), without changing its abundance. · Held evidence: /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/proteostasis_rationale.md: "Stabilizing ≠ restoring function … A stability readout without a function readout cannot support a GO". This is the only held analogue, and it is a caution. No G-alpha nucleotide data are held. · Usual reading assumes / this one changes: The usual reading assumes the lesion is in abundance or in the effector interface. This reading places it in the switch cycle itself, so the lever sits at the nucleotide pocket or at the receptor or GEF interface. · Single observation that rejects it: A purified or in-cell biosensor assay of GDP release, GTP binding and hydrolysis shows the mutant's nucleotide kinetics equal to wild type, while a deficit remains downstream. That result rejects it. · Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H1): Over-express an accessory GEF-class protein, or the cognate receptor, in the mutant neuron. This raises the switch rate without changing protein level. Predicted: the receptor-evoked effector deficit is corrected and motor performance improves, since a lever that fits only the cycle is function-specific. Seizure effect is smaller or absent if the epileptic arm is carried by a different compartment (see H5).

H3. Statement: If the mutant subunit is a dominant-negative or sequestering allele that competes with wild-type alpha for Gβγ or receptor, then lowering mutant protein selectively (allele-specific knockdown) works better than adding more wild-type. · Held evidence: The folder holds no dominant-negative G-alpha data. Mechanism-to-lever triage is in /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/variant_triage_rescuability.md. It says that gene addition suits null alleles, whereas missense and splice alleles each need their own lever, and "lever assignment is an allele-level operation". · Usual reading assumes / this one changes: The usual reading assumes that a dysfunctional-not-absent subunit is rescued by "adding back" function. This reading says that for a competing allele, adding wild type is insufficient and removing the mutant is the active step. · Single observation that rejects it: Het knockout of the gene, or an allele-specific silencer that removes the mutant message, fails to improve the effector readout and the phenotype in mutant neurons. Or the mutant/wild-type titration shows the effect scales with wild-type dose, not with mutant removal. That result rejects it. · Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H1, which raises protein of an allele): Allele-specific knockdown (genetic, ASO class) in the mutant neuron, against wild-type gene addition. Predicted: if H3 is right, knockdown improves both, but the extra wild-type copy alone does not. Motor function improves (more so in movement-circuit neurons) and seizure frequency falls only if the dominant-negative effect acts in the epileptogenic compartment.

H4. Statement: The movement phenotype and the seizure phenotype come from different cell types, and restoring function of the mutant subunit in the movement-circuit population (striatal projection neurons, for example) rescues motor function without rescuing seizures. · Held evidence: /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/therapeutic_repair_candidates.md (§4): "SEIZURE_CONTROL ≠ DISEASE_MODIFICATION" and "Every intervention with a filled SEIZURE_CONTROL cell has an empty DEVELOPMENTAL_TRAJECTORY cell". The same file's §3 shows that AAV9-WWOX gave motor rescue (S4A, WT-vs-HD ns) alongside separately measured SWD rescue, but cerebellum was under-transduced and never measured as an endpoint. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/registries/claim_registry_current.md CLAIM 039: ataxia is the most penetrant rat phenotype and "not cerebellar". · Usual reading assumes / this one changes: The usual reading assumes one subunit lesion gives one syndrome, so seizures and movement disorder go up and down together. This reading uses the folder's lesson that endpoints must be measured separately. Seizure and motor are separate columns and can come apart. · Single observation that rejects it: Cell-type-restricted rescue of the wild-type or the corrected subunit (conditional Cre, striatal versus forebrain-excitatory versus interneuron) rescues both phenotypes from the same single population. That result rejects it. · Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H5, interneuron-driven seizures): Restore function by a conditional Cre in striatal or cerebellar projection populations only. Predicted: motor function improves; seizure count and electrographic activity stay unchanged.

H5. Statement: Seizures arise chiefly from lost inhibitory (interneuron) function of the mutant subunit, so restoring the subunit specifically in inhibitory interneurons relieves seizures more than motor signs. · Held evidence: /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/meta/meta_gaba_paradox_current.md. It holds the "marker ≠ function" rule (CLAIM 028), reduced spontaneous inhibition in layer 2/3 neurons with neuron-specific loss (CLAIM 021), and the caution that PV/NPY markers were reduced but cell loss was not shown. It also records that the systemic mouse null had no seizures (rat-only phenotype), so the species of the seizure phenotype matters. This is analogue-only for G-alpha. · Usual reading assumes / this one changes: The usual reading assumes excitatory-neuron loss, or the same cell type, drives all features. This one assigns seizures to the inhibitory compartment, and requires a functional test, not a marker count. · Single observation that rejects it: A conditional interneuron-only rescue (or interneuron-only deletion of the same subunit) changes neither seizure threshold nor electrographic burden, while excitatory-only manipulation does. That result rejects it. · Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H4): Restore (or delete) the subunit in GABAergic interneurons only, with whole-cell inhibitory-current readout (not marker counts). Predicted: seizure threshold and spike burden improve (or worsen on deletion); motor function changes little.

H6. Statement: The excitatory-projection compartment (cortical pyramidal or hippocampal principal neurons) bears the seizure phenotype, since mutant-subunit loss raises their excitability (reduced Gi/o-type brake on cAMP and channel gating), so restoring the subunit there fixes seizures and leaves motor function. · Held evidence: /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/therapeutic_repair_candidates.md §1 Candidate C: d-APV abolishes bursting in the Wwox S-KO slice, with a washout overshoot of about 1.85× baseline. This shows an excitatory-drive-dependent burst phenotype, in a different protein system. The same file warns that CBX effects are not attributable to a single target. Analogue only. · Usual reading assumes / this one changes: The usual reading assumes the seizure is a consequence of network miswiring. This reading localises it to the cell-autonomous excitability of principal neurons. · Single observation that rejects it: Principal-neuron-only rescue of the subunit leaves burst frequency in the slice, or SWD (spike-wave discharges) in vivo, unchanged. Or a Gi/o-type GIRK-class current deficit is absent in those neurons. That result rejects it. · Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H5): Restore the subunit in excitatory principal neurons only. Predicted: seizure/burst endpoints fall; motor function unchanged. If interneuron-only gives the opposite split, H5 and H6 both survive as separate compartments.

H7. Statement: The phenotype includes a downstream cAMP/PKA over-activation that is mutant-subunit-specific, so correcting the effector side (adenylyl cyclase or PKA tone) without touching the subunit compensates for the lost Gαo/i-type brake. · Held evidence: Nothing on cAMP or PKA is held. The only analogue is /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/therapeutic_repair_candidates.md Candidate B (lithium): a general anticonvulsant effect is not a rescue of the mutant pathway, and "a drug effect is WWOX-specific only if the genotype × treatment interaction is non-null". Applied here: a bypass lever must pass the same interaction test. · Usual reading assumes / this one changes: The usual reading assumes you must repair the subunit. This one says a downstream bypass can restore the output without it, and that this is distinguishable by an interaction test. · Single observation that rejects it: The effector-side manipulation produces the same effect size in wild-type and mutant animals (no genotype × treatment interaction), or fails to change the cAMP/PKA readout in the mutant. That result rejects it. · Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H2): Lower adenylyl-cyclase or PKA tone (small-molecule class, mechanism level) in mutant versus wild-type neurons, with the cAMP-reporter readout. Predicted: motor function improves in mutants disproportionately to wild type (a true interaction); seizures improve only if the epileptogenic arm is also a cAMP arm; otherwise it is a general anticonvulsant effect and not a rescue, as the folder found for lithium.

H8. Statement: Gβγ release and effector coupling (GIRK, N-type/P-type Ca channels, GRK2-class interactions) is the limiting arm: the mutant subunit holds Gβγ too tightly or fails to release it, so tuning Gβγ-effector coupling restores signalling. · Held evidence: No Gβγ data are held. /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/meta/meta_gaba_paradox_current.md supplies only the lesson that a GABA-linked inhibitory result needs a functional circuit readout, not a marker. Pure ESPANSIONE. · Usual reading assumes / this one changes: The usual reading assumes the alpha-arm (cAMP) is the effector. This one says the βγ-arm is. · Single observation that rejects it: In variant neurons, receptor-evoked GIRK/Cav current is normal or restored while the phenotype persists. Or a Gβγ-sequestering (scavenger-class) manipulation changes nothing in the mutant. That result rejects it. · Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H7, alpha-arm cAMP): Sequester free βγ (a βγ-binding scavenger, genetic or peptide class) in mutant neurons. Predicted: if H8 is right, effector currents and seizures change (excitability and GIRK-dependent brake), with a smaller motor effect; the cAMP-lowering manipulation of H7 should instead move motor more than seizures.

H9. Statement: The syndrome has a developmental window, and restoring subunit function early (neonatal) improves motor development and seizures, whereas late restoration relieves only the acute seizure and movement features, not the developmental trajectory. · Held evidence: /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/therapeutic_repair_candidates.md §3 and E-3: AAV9 dosing was P0–P5 only, post-neonatal efficacy was never measured, and E-3 prescribes delayed dosing with a seizure-matched comparator arm. CLAIM 031 (/tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/registries/claim_registry_current.md) states seizure control did not rescue development in two human cases. · Usual reading assumes / this one changes: The usual reading assumes function restored at any age is equivalent. This reading assumes a critical period, so timing is a lever in its own right. · Single observation that rejects it: Delayed (post-onset) restoration of the subunit gives the same motor and developmental result as neonatal restoration. That result rejects it. · Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H10, an ongoing-function deficit): A reversible genetic switch (tamoxifen-class or doxycycline-class control, genetic) turning the corrected subunit on at three ages, with a seizure-matched anti-seizure comparator arm (per E-3). Predicted: early restoration improves motor and development, late restoration improves seizures and mild motor features; the comparator arm shows seizure control without motor or developmental gain.

H10. Statement: The deficit is an ongoing physiological loss of signalling, not a developmental scar, so acute re-engagement of the existing mutant pathway (receptor-ligand tuning at the same GPCR) gives immediate benefit in adults. · Held evidence: /tmp/legend_cut_1867a26a8/disease-models/wwox/analysis/therapeutic_repair_candidates.md §5 E-1 shows an ex vivo preparation where pharmacology acts in minutes (d-APV). It does not apply to G-alpha, but shows the type of acute assay. CLAIM 031 argues against it for the developmental axis: development was not rescued by seizure control. · Usual reading assumes / this one changes: The usual reading assumes the damage is structural and irreversible once the window has passed. This reading says the circuit is intact but under-driven. This hypothesis therefore competes with H9. · Single observation that rejects it: An acute manipulation of the receptor input in adult mutant animals changes neither the slice signalling nor the behaviour, while neonatal restoration does (H9 holds). That result rejects it. · Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival (H9): In adult mutants, acutely engage or antagonise the cognate GPCR (agonist/antagonist class, mechanism level) with a washout, and read the slice effector current and open-field/rotarod, plus EEG, in the same animals. Predicted: if H10 is right, motor function and seizure burden shift reversibly within the assay window and return on washout. If H9 is right, there is no durable change.
