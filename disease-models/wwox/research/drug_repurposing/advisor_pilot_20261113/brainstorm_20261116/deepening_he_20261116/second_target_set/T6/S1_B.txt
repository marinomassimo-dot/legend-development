Shared readout for all blocks: "repair outcome" means sequencing the cleavage site in surviving progeny. Predicted classes are scarless (templated, wild-type sequence restored), small indel or microhomology deletion, large deletion between repeats, point or PAM/seed mutation (escape by site loss), and uncut site.

H1. Statement · A phage-encoded Ku/ligase pair (mycobacteriophage-type NHEJ) seals the broken ends directly, so survival rises with cut number and the repaired site carries small indels. · Evidence: Mycobacteriophage Corndog has Ku, and Omega-type phage ligases show end-joining in vitro; no demonstration of defence tolerance known. · Usual reading: phages avoid breaks or block the nuclease. This one: the break occurs and is mended by a phage-borne NHEJ module. · Rejection: a Ku/ligase-null phage survives nuclease attack as well as wild type, with an unchanged indel spectrum. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2, recombination): delete the Ku or ligase gene while leaving the recombinase intact. Prediction: survival falls and indel repair vanishes if H1 holds. Repaired sites become scarless or are lost (escape) under H2, so no change at the site if H2 operates.

H2. Statement · A phage recombinase (Red-beta/Exo, UvsX, RecT-like) repairs the break by templated homologous recombination from a sibling genome, which restores the wild-type site. · Evidence: T4 recombination-dependent replication and lambda Red-driven repair are well established; their role in defence tolerance is only inferred from recombineering with Cas9 counterselection. · Usual reading: a cleaved genome is lost. This one: sibling copies in the same cell act as the repair donor. · Rejection: a recombinase-null phage keeps wild-type survival and the same repair spectrum. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): delete the recombinase. Prediction: survival falls at high cut load, and the repaired site loses its scarless (wild-type) outcome. In an H1 world the indel spectrum persists.

H3. Statement · A phage RecBCD inhibitor (Gam-like) stops end degradation, which leaves breaks open long enough for other repair to act. · Evidence: Gam binds RecBCD (lambda); it is not shown for defence tolerance. · Usual reading: Gam is a dedicated anti-nuclease factor. This one: it is a general end-protector whose effect is on break dwell time. · Rejection: a recBCD-deficient host gives no survival difference between gam+ and gam-null phage. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): delete gam in a recBCD host. Prediction: survival is unchanged if H3's action is only through RecBCD and the repair route is separate. The site sequence is unchanged versus wild-type phage.

H4. Statement · Concatemeric/rolling-circle replication gives many redundant genome copies, so the DSB-free copies maintain the population even if individual copies are cut. · Evidence: T7 and lambda concatemer packaging are known; the buffering effect is not tested as far as I know. · Usual reading: one cut kills one genome. This one: copy number dilutes the cut. · Rejection: survival does not rise with replicated copy number once the nuclease dose is held fixed. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): block late replication with an initiator mutant while keeping the recombinase. Prediction: survival falls sharply; surviving sites show high escape mutation, not templated repair.

H5. Statement · Terminal redundancy and headful packaging let a cut genome be reassembled from overlapping ends, so packaging acts as a repair step. · Evidence: T4 and P22 headful packaging with circular permutation; not shown for defence tolerance. · Usual reading: packaging is passive. This one: packaging selects for intact genomes. · Rejection: a phage with no terminal redundancy (cos-type) survives equally. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H4): change terminase to a cos-type or fixed-end system. Prediction: survival falls; the repaired site shows large deletions or truncations rather than point repair.

H6. Statement · Single-strand annealing between repeats in the phage genome joins the broken ends after phage-encoded resection. · Evidence: phage annealases (RecT, Beta) promote SSA in vitro; role in defence not shown. · Usual reading: repair needs a full intact template. This one: repeats within the same molecule are enough. · Rejection: removing flanking repeats around the site leaves survival unchanged. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): insert or remove direct repeats flanking the site. Prediction: with repeats, survival is high and the site shows a deletion between the repeats; without them, survival returns to the H2 level and sequence is scarless.

H7. Statement · The phage genome is mostly kept in a protein-enclosed compartment (a nucleus-like shell, as in jumbo phages) that physically excludes nucleases and so reduces the break rate. · Evidence: jumbo phages Phi KZ and relatives resist Cas9, Cas12 and RM; this is a documented case for compartmentalisation. · Usual reading: immunity requires an inhibitor. This one: exclusion explains broad tolerance. · Rejection: nuclease delivered into the compartment (by tagging with a shell-entry signal) does not reduce survival. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H4): knock out the shell protein. Prediction: survival collapses and the site is rarely repaired (cuts lethal). Compartmentalisation predicts no repaired sites to read.

H8. Statement · Hypermodified bases (glucosyl-hydroxymethylcytosine) prevent cutting and so avoid the DSB at all, a different route from tolerating it. · Evidence: T4 hmC glycosylation blocks many RM and Cas9 sites; strong. · Usual reading: modification is a dedicated defence. This one: it should be compared as a rival to tolerance, not equated. · Rejection: unmodified phage DNA is cut at the same rate and survival is equal. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1/H2): delete the glucosyltransferase. Prediction: survival falls and the sites are cut and repaired (indel or scarless), proving tolerance operates; the sequence reveals repair.

H9. Statement · Collective repair at high MOI: coinfecting phages cross-complement and provide donors, so survival rises with MOI. · Evidence: multiplicity reactivation is classical (T4, lambda, UV-damaged phages). · Usual reading: single-hit kinetics. This one: multi-hit repair. · Rejection: survival per phage is identical at MOI 0.1 and 10. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H4): hold copy number fixed but lower MOI to 0.1 by dilution. Prediction: survival per phage falls; the sequence at low MOI shifts from scarless to escape mutants.

H10. Statement · Host RecA/RecBCD-dependent homologous repair is co-opted by the phage (host repair, not phage genes). · Evidence: DSB repair of Cas9 cuts in phage uses host recombination in some cases; limited. · Usual reading: phage supplies its own repair. This one: the host does. · Rejection: a recA-deficient host shows no survival loss. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): use a recA host with the phage recombinase intact. Prediction: if host-dependent, survival falls; if H2, survival stays and the site remains scarless.

H11. Statement · Microhomology-mediated end joining by a phage or host polymerase (Pol theta-like) repairs the cut, leaving deletions at microhomologies. · Evidence: escape deletions with microhomology reported in Cas9-selected phage mutants; the mechanism is not established. · Usual reading: escapes are pre-existing mutants. This one: they are repair products. · Rejection: escapers are present before nuclease exposure at the same frequency, and have no microhomology signature. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): remove the ligase, keep the polymerase. Prediction: deletions with microhomology persist (H11); indels vanish for H1.

H12. Statement · Pre-existing sequence variants (target-site drift) provide survivors, so apparent tolerance is selection. · Evidence: a classic Luria-Delbruck-like explanation for CRISPR escape; well known. · Usual reading: tolerance is an active process. This one: it is selection of rare pre-existing variants. · Rejection: fluctuation test shows escapers arise after exposure and at a rate above the mutation rate. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2/H1): purify the phage stock by single-plaque passage and run a fluctuation test. Prediction: if H12 holds, escapers are rare, jackpot-distributed and carry point or PAM/seed changes; if the others hold, survival is uniform and repair sequences are scarless or indel.

H13. Statement · Partial or slow cleavage (sub-saturating nuclease) leaves nicks that a phage ligase simply seals, so few DSBs form. · Evidence: Cas9 nickase variants are repaired easily; relevance to wild-type nuclease is indirect. · Usual reading: cuts equal DSBs. This one: much of the damage is nicking. · Rejection: all cleavage events are shown to be double-strand by end-mapping. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): delete the ligase, keep Ku. Prediction: survival falls for nick-type attack (RM), not for DSB-type; sequence at the site is unchanged (nick sealed).

H14. Statement · Protective end-capping by a phage terminal protein or circularisation prevents degradation of cut ends so they can be rejoined later. · Evidence: phi29 terminal protein and lambda cos circularisation; no tolerance data. · Usual reading: ends matter only for replication. This one: ends matter for repair after a cut. · Rejection: terminal protein deletion leaves survival unchanged. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H3): delete the terminal protein. Prediction: survival falls, and repair sites show end degradation (deletions extending beyond the site) in survivors.

H15. Statement · Abortive-infection-like delay: phage-encoded general DNA-binding or nucleoid-remodelling proteins coat the genome and lower the break rate. · Evidence: Dps/HU-like phage proteins, Ocr-like DNA mimics (but Ocr is a dedicated inhibitor); coating is speculative here. · Usual reading: a dedicated inhibitor per nuclease. This one: one nonspecific coat covers many. · Rejection: removing the coat protein does not change the cut rate, as measured by DSB-end mapping. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H7): delete the coat protein while preserving the shell. Prediction: survival falls and more cut events are mapped; repaired sites are mostly scarless if repair routes remain intact.
