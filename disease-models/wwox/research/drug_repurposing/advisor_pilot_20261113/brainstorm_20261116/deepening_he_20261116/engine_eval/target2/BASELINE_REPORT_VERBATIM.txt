H1. Statement: The social deficit is a fixed developmental wiring defect (critical-period miswiring of prefrontal-limbic projections) that was set before any seizure or drug exposure and cannot be undone by later seizure control. · Evidence: indirect only. In several neurodevelopmental models, restoring the gene in adults rescues seizures but not social behaviour (Shank3-type and Scn1a-type timing studies). None known specifically for WWOX. · Usual reading assumes: the social deficit is downstream of the seizures, or is reversible once the circuit is "quiet". This reading changes that: the deficit is already built and has a closed window. · Rejection: if adult-onset restoration of the gene (inducible Cre-ERT2 re-expression after P60) rescues social interaction, H1 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2, ongoing interneuron dysfunction): tamoxifen-timed re-expression in the same forebrain population, early postnatal vs adult. Predicts seizures reduced in both timings (or unaffected, if they are not the target) and social deficit rescued only with the early timing. Under H2 the adult timing would also rescue.

H2. Statement: Reduced parvalbumin-interneuron output in medial prefrontal cortex lowers the gamma/E-I balance needed for social processing, and an ASM that dampens pyramidal firing or sodium-channel-dependent bursting does not repair that interneuron hypofunction. · Evidence: interneuron hypofunction and E/I imbalance are well documented in epilepsy-plus-autism models (Scn1a, Shank3, Cntnap2). For WWOX, GABAergic involvement is suggested but I am not certain of the direct data. · Usual reading assumes: seizure control normalises E/I balance. This reading changes that: ASM changes ictal threshold but not the interneuron-dependent computation. · Rejection: if mPFC PV-cell recordings show normal firing and gamma power in the mutant, H2 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): chemogenetic activation (hM3Dq) of mPFC PV cells in adult mutants. Predicts social deficit improved acutely (reversible), seizures unchanged if the focus is hippocampal or subcortical. Under H1 it would produce no rescue.

H3. Statement: Hypothalamic oxytocin (PVN) signalling to the prefrontal cortex, amygdala and VTA is reduced, which removes social salience but is irrelevant to seizure threshold. · Evidence: oxytocin deficits rescue social behaviour in Cntnap2, Shank3 and Magel2 models. None known for WWOX. · Usual reading assumes: a cortical or hippocampal cause for both phenotypes. This reading changes that: the social deficit is neuromodulatory and hypothalamic. · Rejection: if PVN oxytocin cell number, activity and CSF/brain oxytocin are normal in the mutant, H3 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H4, VTA dopamine): chemogenetic activation (hM3Dq) of PVN oxytocin neurons (Oxt-Cre) in adult mutants. Predicts seizures unchanged and social deficit improved. If the effect needs VTA dopamine neurons (blocked by hM4Di there), H4 is favoured instead.

H4. Statement: Hypofunction of the VTA-to-nucleus-accumbens dopamine reward pathway lowers social motivation, independent of the cortical network that generates seizures. · Evidence: VTA dopamine hypoactivity and impaired social reward appear in several ASD models (Shank3, Fmr1, Ube3a). For WWOX, indirect evidence only (WWOX expression in dopaminergic regions reported, behavioural link unknown). · Usual reading assumes: social deficit equals cortical cognitive failure. This reading changes that: the deficit is one of motivation or reward, not comprehension. · Rejection: if dopamine release in the nucleus accumbens during social contact is normal in the mutant (fiber photometry), H4 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H3): hM3Dq in Dat-Cre VTA neurons with the oxytocin pathway left intact. Predicts social deficit improved and seizures unchanged. Placing the manipulation in the VTA alone separates it from H3 because H3 requires the PVN-to-VTA input.

H5. Statement: Cerebellar Purkinje-cell dysfunction (crus I/II) disrupts the cerebello-thalamo-prefrontal loop required for social behaviour, while seizures arise elsewhere. · Evidence: Purkinje-cell loss or dysfunction causes social deficits in Tsc1, Shank2 and Fmr1 models. WWOX loss causes ataxia and cerebellar involvement is reported in WWOX-related disease, so cerebellar dysfunction is plausible, but a social link is not shown. · Usual reading assumes: social and cognitive deficits are cortical. This reading changes that: the circuit is cerebellar. · Rejection: if crus I/II Purkinje firing is normal in the mutant and Purkinje-specific rescue gives no social effect, H5 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1/H2, cortical): Pcp2-Cre-restricted rescue (or chemogenetic activation of crus I/II Purkinje cells). Predicts seizures unchanged and social deficit improved if H5 holds. Cortical rescue then fails to repair social behaviour.

H6. Statement: Persistent interictal discharges and abnormal sharp-wave ripples in hippocampal CA2/CA3 disrupt social-memory encoding even when overt seizures are suppressed, so ASM reduces convulsions but not subclinical epileptiform activity. · Evidence: interictal spikes impair memory in rodent epilepsy models, and CA2 pyramidal neurons are needed for social memory. Many ASMs reduce seizures without removing spikes. None known for WWOX. · Usual reading assumes: seizure freedom means the network is controlled. This reading changes that: the harm comes from interictal activity, not from convulsions. · Rejection: if interictal spikes and ripples are absent or normal on ASM, yet the social deficit persists, H6 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H1, structural): closed-loop optogenetic or hM4Di silencing of CA2/CA3 at spike onset. Predicts seizures reduced or unchanged, depending on the focus, and social memory improved. Under H1 silencing would change nothing.

H7. Statement: The ASM itself (sedation, reduced plasticity, altered synaptic transmission) adds to or masks social and cognitive function, so seizure control comes with a drug-induced behavioural cost that hides any benefit. · Evidence: cognitive and behavioural side effects are documented for sodium-channel and GABAergic ASMs. None known specifically here. · Usual reading assumes: the deficit is purely disease-intrinsic and the drug is neutral. This reading changes that: the drug is a contributor. · Rejection: if drug-free seizure suppression gives the same persistent social deficit, and the social deficit is unchanged by drug withdrawal in seizure-free animals, H7 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H6): seizure control without the drug, with hM4Di in the seizure-focus population (for example dentate granule cells). Predicts seizures suppressed and social deficit improved if H7 holds. Under H6 social improvement requires spike removal in the specific CA2/CA3 network, not just seizure control.

H8. Statement: Oligodendrocyte or myelination deficits slow long-range prefrontal-limbic conduction, causing a social deficit unrelated to seizures. · Evidence: hypomyelination is reported in some WWOX-null contexts and is a feature of other developmental encephalopathies, but I do not know the strength of the data. · Usual reading assumes: neurons are the only relevant cell type. This reading changes that: the lesion is in white matter. · Rejection: if myelin thickness, g-ratio and callosal conduction velocity are normal in the mutant, H8 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H9, microglial): oligodendrocyte-lineage-specific (Olig2- or Pdgfra-CreER) re-expression or deletion of the gene. Predicts seizures unchanged and social deficit changing in the same direction as myelination. Microglial manipulation would leave myelin unchanged.

H9. Statement: Microglial over-pruning of synapses during development reduces prefrontal excitatory connectivity and lowers social behaviour, independent of ictogenic circuitry. · Evidence: aberrant microglial pruning is implicated in Fmr1, Mecp2 and 16p11.2 models. None known for WWOX. · Usual reading assumes: the lesion is neuronal and cell-autonomous. This reading changes that: the lesion is a non-neuronal pruning error. · Rejection: if spine density, synaptic engulfment (CD68/PSD95) and microglial phagocytosis are normal in the mutant, H9 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H8): microglia-restricted (Cx3cr1-CreER) deletion of the gene in wild type, or rescue in the mutant, at the pruning window. Predicts seizures unchanged and social deficit following the microglial genotype. Oligodendrocyte-specific rescue would not change spine density.

H10. Statement: Thalamic (mediodorsal or reticular) dysfunction affects prefrontal gating of social cues and is separable from the cortical-hippocampal circuit that generates seizures. · Evidence: thalamic reticular and mediodorsal circuits are linked to autism-like phenotypes (Ptchd1, Cntnap2) and to absence-type seizures. None known for WWOX. · Usual reading assumes: seizure focus and social circuit are the same. This reading changes that: the two are anatomically distinct, and ASM acts on the seizure focus only. · Rejection: if mediodorsal-thalamus-to-mPFC activity during social contact is normal in the mutant, H10 is rejected. · Discriminating manipulation (vs H2, mPFC interneurons): hM3Dq or hM4Di on mediodorsal thalamus neurons projecting to mPFC (retrograde Cre). Predicts seizures unchanged and social deficit modulated. mPFC PV-cell activation (H2) would act locally and not depend on thalamic input.
