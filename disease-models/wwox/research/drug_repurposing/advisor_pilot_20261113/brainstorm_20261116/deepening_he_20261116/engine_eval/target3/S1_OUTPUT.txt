All 15 blocks come from my own knowledge, with no files read or searched. Evidence lines are hedged where my recall of WWOX-specific data is uncertain. Mechanism level only. Each block runs: Statement · Evidence · Usual reading vs this one · Rejecting observation · Discriminating manipulation (WM = white-matter integrity, COG = cognitive behaviour).

H1. Statement: An oligodendrocyte-progenitor-cell (OPC)-intrinsic differentiation block (excess Wnt/β-catenin, Notch or Id2/4 tone) keeps OPCs from maturing, and releasing it restores both myelin and cognition. · Evidence: OPC-stage blocks are well documented in other hypomyelinating and demyelinating models; for WWOX-DEE it is none known beyond reports of reduced mature oligodendrocytes in Wwox-deficient mice. · Usual reading assumes mTOR hyperactivity is the main lesion in oligodendrocytes; this one says the lesion is a stage-specific maturation arrest that mTOR inhibition cannot reach. · Rejection: OPC density is normal and the proportion of CC1+ mature cells is normal, with the myelin deficit still present. · Discriminator: OPC-specific (Pdgfra-CreER) re-expression or pathway correction vs neuron-specific correction. Predicts WM rises and COG partly rises if the cause is intrinsic; if COG stays low with WM corrected, H2 or H15 is favoured.

H2. Statement: Reduced neuronal-activity-dependent signalling to OPCs (synaptic input, secreted factors) under-drives myelination, and restoring activity drive restores both myelin and cognition. · Evidence: activity-dependent myelination is shown in other systems (optogenetic and chemogenetic work); for WWOX-DEE none known. · Usual reading assumes myelination defects are glial and cell-autonomous; this one makes them a downstream consequence of the neuron's own state. · Rejection: chemogenetic neuronal activation fails to raise OPC proliferation or myelin in the mutant, while it does so in the control. · Discriminator: chronic chemogenetic activation of excitatory cortical neurons vs OPC-intrinsic rescue (H1). Predicts a WM increase in circuits that are activated and a COG gain that tracks the activated circuit, with no effect on non-activated tracts.

H3. Statement: Excess cholinergic (M1 muscarinic) or other GPCR-mediated brakes on OPC differentiation, so that blocking the brake restores maturation and cognition. · Evidence: muscarinic antagonism promotes OPC differentiation in demyelination models; none known for WWOX-DEE. · Usual reading assumes the block is internal to the OPC; this one says it is an extrinsic, reversible receptor signal. · Rejection: OPC-specific M1 deletion in the mutant gives no gain in mature oligodendrocytes. · Discriminator: OPC-specific Chrm1 deletion or antagonist-class treatment vs the nearest rival H1, which needs an intrinsic fix. Predicts a modest WM gain and a small COG gain only if myelination is rate-limiting.

H4. Statement: Astrocyte-derived cholesterol and lipid supply is insufficient for myelin membrane synthesis, and restoring the supply restores both. · Evidence: cholesterol is rate-limiting for myelination in animal models, and astrocytic lipid supply is known in other leukodystrophies; none known for WWOX-DEE. · Usual reading assumes oligodendrocytes make their own lipid; this one assigns the bottleneck to astrocytes. · Rejection: myelin lipid composition is normal or the lipid deficit is not present in oligodendrocytes. · Discriminator: astrocyte-specific SREBP/SCAP restoration vs oligodendrocyte-specific restoration. Predicts WM recovery, thicker myelin, and COG gain that is partial.

H5. Statement: The metabolic coupling between oligodendrocytes and axons (MCT1/lactate shuttle) is impaired, so axons degenerate and conduction fails, and restoring the coupling restores conduction and cognition. · Evidence: MCT1 loss causes axon degeneration in other models; none known for WWOX-DEE. · Usual reading assumes the main defect is myelin quantity; this one says it is the support function of myelinating cells for axons. · Rejection: axonal energy markers and axon counts are normal in the mutant. · Discriminator: oligodendrocyte-specific MCT1 re-expression vs lipid supply (H4). Predicts axon preservation, little change in myelin thickness, and a COG gain via conduction.

H6. Statement: Microglia fail to clear myelin debris or to support OPC maturation (TREM2/CSF1R axis), and normalising microglial function restores myelin and cognition. · Evidence: microglial support for myelination is shown in other models; for WWOX-DEE none known. · Usual reading assumes microglia are bystanders; this one makes them rate-limiting. · Rejection: microglial depletion or TREM2 loss does not change myelin in the mutant. · Discriminator: transient microglial depletion/repopulation or TREM2 manipulation vs OPC-intrinsic rescue. Predicts a WM gain if a debris-clearance defect exists, with a COG gain smaller than in H2.

H7. Statement: Parvalbumin interneuron myelination is the part of the deficit that causes cognitive impairment, so the cognitive deficit tracks interneuron axons and not total white matter. · Evidence: PV interneuron axon myelination is documented in cortex, and a link to cognition is described in other models; none known for WWOX-DEE. · Usual reading assumes the global WM change explains cognition; this one localises the cognitive effect to a small circuit. · Rejection: restoring myelin in PV axons alone leaves cognition at the mutant level. · Discriminator: PV-axon-targeted myelin restoration vs global OPC rescue (H1). Predicts a small global WM change but a large COG gain.

H8. Statement: Seizures, not the primary lesion, cause myelin loss, and controlling seizures raises myelination and cognition. · Evidence: seizures drive OPC proliferation and myelin changes in other models, and WWOX-DEE has prominent epilepsy; the direction of causation is unproven. · Usual reading assumes the myelin defect is primary; this one makes it secondary to hyperexcitability. · Rejection: seizure suppression leaves WM unchanged. · Discriminator: antiseizure mechanism (e.g. Kv7 opener or Na channel blocker class) vs myelin-directed intervention. Predicts a WM gain only if seizure burden falls, with a COG gain proportional to that burden.

H9. Statement: Thyroid-hormone (THRA) signalling to OPCs is too low locally, and restoring it drives OPC terminal differentiation. · Evidence: T3 promotes OPC differentiation in other models; none known for WWOX-DEE. · Usual reading assumes the relevant signal is growth-factor driven; this one makes it nuclear-receptor driven. · Rejection: local T3 and deiodinase levels are normal. · Discriminator: OPC-specific THRA activation vs H3. Predicts a WM gain and a small COG gain.

H10. Statement: ERK/MAPK signalling is the limiting driver of myelin thickness and compensates incompletely for mTOR inhibition. · Evidence: Mek1/ERK activity controls myelin thickness in other models; none known for WWOX-DEE. · Usual reading assumes PI3K–Akt–mTOR is the main driver; this one says ERK is. · Rejection: phospho-ERK is normal and raising it does not change myelin. · Discriminator: oligodendrocyte-specific ERK activation vs mTOR inhibition. Predicts thicker myelin, and COG gain only if the conduction deficit is real.

H11. Statement: Epigenetic repression (HDAC-dependent chromatin state) blocks the myelin gene programme. · Evidence: HDAC1/2 and HDAC3 are needed for oligodendrocyte maturation in other models; none known for WWOX-DEE. · Usual reading assumes signalling controls maturation; this one says chromatin state does. · Rejection: histone acetylation at myelin genes is normal. · Discriminator: HDAC-inhibitor-class treatment or oligodendrocyte-specific deletion vs signalling rescue. Predicts a WM gain with a possibly broad, off-target COG effect.

H12. Statement: An integrated stress response / ER stress in oligodendrocytes reduces myelin protein synthesis, and relieving it restores myelin. · Evidence: ISR modulation protects oligodendrocytes in other models; none known for WWOX-DEE. · Usual reading assumes lipid or progenitor limits; this one says protein-synthesis stress limits. · Rejection: eIF2α phosphorylation and CHOP are not elevated. · Discriminator: eIF2B activator class or PERK modulation vs mTOR inhibition. Predicts a WM gain with COG gain if mTOR inhibition is blocked by this arm.

H13. Statement: Axon-to-glia neuregulin–ErbB signalling is reduced and limits myelination. · Evidence: neuregulin signalling controls myelination and thickness in other models; none known for WWOX-DEE. · Usual reading assumes the glial side is at fault; this one makes it axonal. · Rejection: axonal NRG1 expression is normal. · Discriminator: neuronal NRG1 restoration vs OPC ErbB activation. Predicts WM gain and a modest COG gain.

H14. Statement: Vascular or hypoxic (HIF) stress in white matter blocks OPC maturation, and fixing perfusion restores it. · Evidence: HIF stabilisation impairs OPC maturation in other models; none known for WWOX-DEE. · Usual reading assumes a cell-autonomous block; this one makes it environmental. · Rejection: perfusion and oxygen are normal. · Discriminator: vascular or HIF modulation vs OPC rescue. Predicts a WM gain with a smaller COG gain.

H15. Statement: Cognition is restored independently of myelin by correcting neuronal synaptic plasticity, so the WM and COG deficits are parallel and not serial. · Evidence: WWOX is expressed in neurons and a synaptic role is reported in Wwox-deficient mice; no rescue data known. · Usual reading assumes the myelin deficit explains the cognitive deficit; this one says it does not. · Rejection: myelin restoration alone raises cognition to control level. · Discriminator: neuron-specific rescue vs OPC-specific rescue. Predicts COG recovery with WM unchanged. This is the opposite of H1 (WM gain with COG gain only if myelination is rate-limiting).
