# Thirty hypotheses on restoring myelination and cognition where mTOR inhibition gives limited benefit (statement and discriminating manipulation only)

## Y01
Statement: mTORC1 signalling is not hyperactive but hypoactive or mis-timed in OPCs/neurons of the WWOX-deficient brain, so inhibition cannot repair myelination, while restoring mTORC1/4E-BP translational tone in the OL lineage would (IPOTESI; the mTOR-OL link is ESPANSIONE).
Discriminating manipulation vs H2 · Pdgfra-CreER Tsc1 deletion (constitutive mTORC1 gain) vs Rptor deletion in OPCs of the null. Prediction: if H8 holds, gain raises myelin thickness and g-ratio normalises and loss worsens; if the problem is H2 (differentiation), mTORC1 gain does not alter the maturation block. Cognition: improves only if white matter was limiting.

## Y02
Statement: A deficit of oligodendrocyte metabolic support (glycolysis, lactate export through MCT1, HIF1 handling) starves axons and halts myelin maintenance, so restoring the metabolic coupling restores both.
Discriminating manipulation: Oligodendrocyte-specific MCT1 or HIF1a/glycolytic gene manipulation (a genetic rescue of lactate export), against a lineage differentiation push (H1).
  - WM prediction: Axonal integrity and myelin maintenance improve, mainly in axonal health markers (fewer degenerating axons) rather than in OPC numbers.
  - Cog prediction: Improved performance, with possible exercise- or energy-demand dependence.

## Y03
Statement: Pathological network state in L2/3 pyramidal neurons (NMDAR- and gap-junction-dependent bursting) disrupts activity-dependent myelination; normalising the burst state, with WWOX still absent, would restore myelin and cognition (IPOTESI).
Discriminating manipulation vs H1 (rescue by signalling restoration) · Chemogenetic silencing of excitatory neurons (or a mechanism-level NMDAR antagonist class) during P3–P21 in the null. Prediction: myelination recovers only partially, correlating with burst frequency, while axon-intrinsic markers (MAP2, neurite) stay abnormal. Cognition: partial, if at all.

## Y04
Statement: A second, oligodendroglia-autonomous WWOX function (SOX10 / differentiation arm, emerging under stress or remyelination) supplies the residual gap; restoring it in the OL lineage on top of neuronal rescue closes the gap and adds a small cognitive gain (IPOTESI).
Discriminating manipulation vs H1 · Olig2/Pdgfra-CreER Wwox deletion layered on neuron-rescued animals, with and without cuprizone. Prediction: white matter is normal at baseline and fails to remyelinate under cuprizone (maturation block: PDGFRα+ up, CC1+ down). Cognition: unchanged at baseline, impaired only after a demyelinating insult.

## Y05
Statement: The signal that sets myelin thickness is an axon-intrinsic deficit, such as reduced neuronal NRG1 type III (and downstream axonal caliber or ensheathment cues), so neuron-restricted restoration of axonal instructive cues restores myelin and cognition.
Discriminating manipulation: Neuron-restricted rescue (axonal NRG1 type III), versus an OPC-intrinsic rescue (H1).
  - WM prediction: A thickness increase on axons that carry the transgene, and little change in OPC number.
  - Cog prediction: Improvement, specifically in myelin-dependent timing and learning tasks.

## Y06
Statement: The myelin deficit is a consequence of the seizure burden, so seizure control, not myelin-directed therapy, restores white matter and cognition.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): seizure suppression through a channel mechanism that does not alter bursting-independent signalling, versus gene addition, with matched seizure suppression in both.
- Prediction if H7 is true: white matter and learning rise in proportion to seizure reduction in both arms.
- Prediction if the primary-lesion reading (H1/H2) is right: only gene addition raises FA and cognition, as E-D1 states (:715).

## Y07
Statement: An extrinsic M1-muscarinic brake on OPC differentiation is raised in the mutant, so releasing it from OPCs (not from neurons) restores myelin, with a smaller cognitive effect than H1 or H2.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): OPC-specific Chrm1 deletion in the Syn-Cre null, versus OPC-specific Wnt suppression.
- Prediction: H3 gives a modest FA and CC1⁺ gain with a small cognitive gain.
- If the nearest rival (H1) is right, Chrm1 deletion fails.
- THL:262-264 predicts both OPC-only manipulations will be modest.

## Y08
Statement: WWOX loss de-represses Wnt/β-catenin, and Wnt hyperactivation in the OPC lineage arrests differentiation; lowering β-catenin tone in OPCs restores myelin and (secondarily) cognition (INFERENZA for the Wnt sign; ESPANSIONE for the OPC arrest).
Discriminating manipulation vs H6 (GSK3β) · Olig2/Pdgfra-CreER Ctnnb1 (β-catenin) lowering in the null background. Prediction: CC1+ rises and g-ratio improves without any change in neuronal MAP2/neurite markers; cognition improves only to the extent that white matter was limiting.

## Y09
Statement: Loss of the WWOX docking brake on GSK3β (Axin-like motif 388–407, L404) in neurons impairs axonal maturation (tau/microtubule/neurite), so axons are not myelination-competent; this is independent of Wnt (INFERENZA).
Discriminating manipulation vs H5 · Neuron-restricted L404A-WWOX vs WW1-mutant WWOX re-expression in the null (WW-scaffold carries Wnt/HIF; the L404 site carries GSK3β). Prediction: L404A fails to restore MAP2 and myelin if H6 holds; WW1-mutant fails if H5 or H7 holds. Cognition follows the same pattern (IPOTESI).

## Y10
Statement: WWOX loss derepresses Wnt/β-catenin signalling (through Dvl), which holds OPCs in a progenitor state, independently of mTOR.
Discriminating manipulation: Inhibit the Wnt pathway in the OPC lineage (a tankyrase or porcupine-class inhibitor, or lineage-restricted Dvl/β-catenin reduction), in combination with versus without mTOR inhibition.
  - WM prediction: Additive or synergistic WM gains with mTOR inhibition.
  - Cog prediction: Additional cognitive gain beyond mTOR inhibition alone.
  - If there is no additive effect, Wnt is not an independent arm.

## Y11
Statement: Aberrant, NMDAR-dependent bursting and desynchronised network activity (not low activity) is the signal that fails to drive OPC maturation, so normalising network state restores myelin and cognition together.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): suppress bursting in the Syn-Cre null without touching OPCs. Examples are a sodium-channel-blocker class or an NMDAR-antagonist class given in the myelination window, or a non-pharmacological route such as a neuronal Kv7 opener.
- Prediction if H2 is true: CC1⁺, g-ratio and FA rise, and learning improves, with the OPC-intrinsic Wnt readouts unchanged.
- If H1 is true: bursting is suppressed and white matter stays low.
- Caution on safety: NMDAR blockade in a developing brain removes a maturation signal (MIM:434).

## Y12
Statement: Astrocyte-derived lipid and cholesterol supply (SREBP/SCAP pathway) is insufficient for myelin membrane synthesis, so restoring astrocytic lipid output restores myelin and cognition.
Discriminating manipulation: Astrocyte-specific SREBP/SCAP gain or loss, versus oligodendrocyte-specific mTORC1 gain. Cholesterol supplementation would be a mechanism-level contrast.
  - WM prediction: Astrocyte-targeted rescue increases myelin thickness.
  - Cog prediction: Cognition improves in step.
  - If astrocytic rescue fails but an oligodendrocyte-intrinsic push works, the lipid hypothesis is rejected.

## Y13
Statement: A prenatal progenitor/radial-glia and migration defect creates a mis-wired callosal and cortical scaffold, so postnatal myelin and cognition are limited by structure that neuron-restricted post-mitotic rescue cannot repair; only progenitor-stage restoration restores both (INFERENZA).
Discriminating manipulation vs H1 · Timed, progenitor-stage vs post-mitotic WWOX restoration. Prediction: progenitor-stage restoration gives a larger callosal fibre number, myelinated fraction and cognitive gain; post-mitotic restoration gives the P0-rescue pattern (myelin partly restored, cortical layering and callosal complement not).

## Y14
Statement: Loss of functional inhibition (PV-interneuron deficit, reduced spontaneous IPSCs) makes the circuit non-myelination-competent. Restoring the inhibitory arm restores white matter in PV-connected tracts and cognition more than excitatory restoration does (IPOTESI; the biology of PV-axon myelination is ESPANSIONE).
Discriminating manipulation vs H3 · Pvalb/Gad2-Cre-dependent WWOX restoration vs Emx1/CaMKII-Cre-dependent restoration in the null. Prediction: PV-restricted restoration repairs sIPSCs, partly repairs callosal and cortical myelin and gives the larger cognitive gain; excitatory-restricted restoration gives the opposite.

## Y15
Statement: A reactive glial/inflammatory state (IL-1β/NLRP3, NF-κB, microglia) suppresses OPC maturation in specific regions; resolving it without restoring WWOX frees myelination (IPOTESI).
Discriminating manipulation vs H1 · Nlrp3 or microglia-specific Il1b deletion in the null, with neurons still WWOX-null. Prediction: partial white-matter gain (smaller than neuron rescue) in regions with gliosis, none in cortex of lde-type regions; cognition: little or none.

## Y16
Statement: Seizure and hyperexcitability burden suppresses activity-dependent myelination and degrades myelin, so controlling network excitability raises both WM and cognition.
Discriminating manipulation: Suppress neuronal firing with a sodium-channel-blocker-class or chemogenetic approach, with OPC differentiation left untouched. Compare against an OPC-intrinsic differentiation push (H1).
  - WM prediction: WM improves with excitability control, with no OPC-differentiation change measured intrinsically.
  - Cog prediction: Cognition improves.
  - If a differentiation push (for example a muscarinic M1 antagonist class) improves WM but not seizures, H1 or H10 is favoured over H2.

## Y17
Statement: Cognitive impairment arises mainly from synaptic and neuronal dysfunction (for example GluN2B/NMDAR-dependent signalling or ER stress), with hypomyelination a parallel effect, so rescuing the neuron restores cognition and myelination only partially or secondarily.
Discriminating manipulation: Repair myelin alone (an OPC differentiation push, for example through a muscarinic M1 antagonist class or a lineage-specific Myrf gain) with neurons untouched, versus neuron-restricted rescue.
  - WM prediction: Myelin repair alone raises WM.
  - Cog prediction: Cognition improves little, while neuron-restricted rescue improves cognition with only a modest WM change. That dissociation is the signature of H10.
  - If myelin repair alone restores cognition, H10 is rejected in favour of H1.

## Y18
Statement: Oligodendrocyte-to-axon metabolic coupling (MCT1 lactate shuttle) is impaired, so axons are energetically unsupported and conduction fails even where myelin is present; restoring the coupling rescues conduction and cognition with little change in myelin thickness.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1 and H3): oligodendrocyte-specific MCT1 re-expression in the Syn-Cre null.
- Prediction if H4 is true: axon preservation and conduction velocity recover, FA recovers modestly, myelin thickness and CC1⁺ do not change, and cognitive gain tracks conduction.
- If H1 is true, restoring MCT1 leaves CC1⁺ low.

## Y19
Statement: mTORC1 signalling may not be hyperactive in this disorder. The limited benefit of inhibition is then expected, and the lever is pathway restoration or other routes, not inhibition.
Discriminating manipulation (vs the TSC-like reading where mTORC1 is high): OPC- or neuron-restricted mTORC1 activation (e.g. Tsc1 deletion) in the Syn-Cre null, against inhibition.
- Prediction if H9 is true: activation raises CC1⁺ and g-ratio and FA, with a cognitive gain only if the neuron is also corrected.
- If the TSC-like reading is true: activation worsens both, and inhibition helps.

## Y20
Statement: Microglia are rate-limiting for OPC maturation or myelin remodelling (TREM2/CSF1R axis), so normalising microglial support restores myelin, with cognition smaller than in H2.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1/H2): transient CSF1R-class depletion with repopulation, or microglial Trem2 manipulation, in the Syn-Cre null, against restoration of neuronal WWOX alone.
- Prediction if H5 is true: myelin gain without a change in neuronal bursting.
- If H2 is true: bursting persists and microglial manipulation alone changes neither.
- Cognition: a small gain at best.

## Y21
Statement: Neuron-derived axonal signalling (non-cell-autonomous) is the main driver, so restoring WWOX in neurons alone raises OPC-to-oligodendrocyte maturation and myelination, and cognition improves through the restored circuit (INFERENZA).
Discriminating manipulation vs nearest rival H2 (oligodendroglial-autonomous) · Cell-type-restricted re-expression in a fully null animal (Syn-Cre-dependent vs Olig2/CNP-Cre-dependent WWOX), at matched transduction. Prediction: neuron-only gives g-ratio normalisation and a large rise in myelinated axons, with the unmyelinated-axon gap remaining; Olig2-only gives little. White matter: callosal myelin largely restored. Cognition (IPOTESI): improves in proportion to the myelinated fraction and to network normalisation, not to g-ratio alone.

## Y22
Statement: DNA-damage-response and oxidative stress signalling (p53-dependent), driven by loss of WWOX's role in genome maintenance, causes OPC senescence or apoptosis and depletes the myelinating pool.
Discriminating manipulation: OPC-restricted p53 reduction or a senolytic-class manipulation, compared with a differentiation push (H1).
  - WM prediction: OPC density and myelination recover, with fewer senescence markers.
  - Cog prediction: Improved performance.
  - A differentiation push in a depleted pool would fail to restore WM, which would separate H9 from H1.

## Y23
Statement: Axon-to-glia neuregulin–ErbB signalling is reduced in the mutant and limits myelination, which would place the myelin deficit in the axonal signal that the OPC receives.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): neuron-specific NRG1 restoration in the Syn-Cre null, versus OPC-specific ErbB activation.
- Prediction if H6 is true: myelin thickness (g-ratio) recovers and FA rises, with a modest cognitive gain.
- If H1 is true: OPC ErbB activation alone fails to unblock the OPCs, because they do not receive an axonal signal.
- A gain from neuronal NRG1 but not from OPC-side Wnt suppression favours H6 over H1.

## Y24
Statement: Cognitive impairment arises from neuron-intrinsic synaptic and excitability change that runs in parallel with the myelin deficit, so cognition can be restored without changing myelin (and the reverse).
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): neuron-specific WWOX restoration after the myelination window, in the Syn-Cre null, versus OPC-restricted Wnt suppression, with myelin quantified against wild type.
- Prediction if H8 is true: cognitive recovery with FA and g-ratio still reduced, and the OPC arm recovers myelin with no cognitive gain.
- If H1 is true: the reverse.

## Y25
Statement: Dysregulated microglia (excess pruning of axons or myelin, or failure to clear myelin debris) damage myelin and synapses, so resetting the microglial state restores both.
Discriminating manipulation: CSF1R-inhibitor-class microglial depletion followed by repopulation, versus oligodendrocyte-selective manipulation.
  - WM prediction: Depletion and repopulation improves WM (fewer myelin debris, more intact sheaths) if H7 holds.
  - Cog prediction: Cognition improves and synaptic density normalises.

## Y26
Statement: Loss of WWOX function inside the oligodendrocyte-precursor (OPC) lineage blocks OPC-to-myelinating-oligodendrocyte differentiation cell-autonomously, so lineage-restricted rescue restores both myelin and cognition.
Discriminating manipulation: Re-express the gene only in the OPC lineage, versus only in neurons (Syn1-Cre, the rival H2/H10).
  - WM prediction: OPC-only re-expression restores WM, and neuron-only re-expression does not.
  - Cog prediction: OPC-only re-expression partly restores cognition (the part that depends on myelin).

## Y27
Statement: Reduced ErbB4/NRG1 signalling in parvalbumin interneurons causes E/I imbalance and impaired circuit-driven myelination, so restoring interneuron ErbB4 signalling raises both WM and cognition.
Discriminating manipulation: Selectively activate or restore the ErbB4 arm in PV cells, versus restoring signalling in excitatory neurons (the rival H8).
  - WM prediction: Increased myelination of PV axons and local cortical WM.
  - Cog prediction: Improved working-memory and learning measures, with reduced network hypersynchrony.

## Y28
Statement: Neuronal WWOX loss de-represses Wnt/β-catenin tone, which holds OPCs in the progenitor state (PDGFRα⁺ high, CC1⁺ low), so releasing the Wnt brake in OPCs restores myelination, and cognition follows only if myelination is rate-limiting.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H2, neuron-derived signal): OPC-restricted Wnt-pathway suppression (Axin2 stabilisation or a dominant-negative β-catenin, driven by a Pdgfra-CreER line) in the neuron-specific Wwox-null.
- Prediction: if H1 is true, CC1⁺ mature cells, g-ratio and diffusion anisotropy (FA) rise without any change in neuronal bursting.
- Cognitive behaviour: improves only in proportion to the myelin gain, and not at all if the network state alone drives the deficit.
- If H2 is true instead, white matter stays low and the bursting stays.

## Y29
Statement: A cell-autonomous oligodendroglial WWOX function limits differentiation and remyelination under stress, and accounts for the incomplete neuronal rescue, so rescuing the oligodendrocyte side adds a separate, additive myelin gain.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1/H3): oligodendrocyte-specific Wwox restoration (OPC/OL-targeted vector) in the neuron-restored background, versus neuronal restoration alone.
- Prediction if H10 is true: the combination gives a further drop in unmyelinated-axon count and a further FA gain, with a cognitive gain only if conduction is the limiting step.
- If the neuron is the only driver, the combination adds nothing.

## Y30
Statement: HIF1α-PDK1-PDH block starves axons and oligodendrocytes of oxidative support (Warburg-like state), so the energy cost of myelination cannot be met; relieving the block restores myelin and cognition (IPOTESI).
Discriminating manipulation vs H8-type systemic metabolic confound · Neuron-restricted deletion of Hif1a or Pdk1 (or a PDH-activator class) in the null with glycaemia clamped normal. Prediction: if H7 is right, white matter recovers with normal glycaemia; if the lesion is systemic hypoglycaemia, only the clamp matters. Cognition (IPOTESI): improved only in the H7-true case.
