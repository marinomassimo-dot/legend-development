STEP 2. THE TEN FINAL HYPOTHESES

H1. Origin: S1-1 (the Wnt arm only; Notch/Id are dropped as unstated)
Statement: Neuronal WWOX loss de-represses Wnt/β-catenin tone, which holds OPCs in the progenitor state (PDGFRα⁺ high, CC1⁺ low), so releasing the Wnt brake in OPCs restores myelination, and cognition follows only if myelination is rate-limiting.
Corpus test: SUPPORT for the premises, NOT STATED for the OPC link.
- WWOX-KO cerebral organoids show Wnt chronically activated (nuclear β-catenin ~1.7×; WNT/LEF1/AXIN2 up), partly recovered by W-AAV (DL:691; DL-MOL-003 at DL:77-84).
- In the neuronal-rescue paper, the numbers run PDGFRα⁺ WT ~53 / KO ~87 / rescued ~70 and CC1⁺ WT ~170 / KO ~77 / rescued ~135 (CR:96). That is the "maturation block" pattern. The ledger candidate states it as a signature (CC-20260826-CLAIM003-01.md:115), and CLAIM 003 records "OPCs present" (CR:75).
- No study measures Wnt in OPCs. The rat study counted no precursor marker (PMID31340538.md:80-82; DL:1798).
Usual reading assumes: the myelin block is an oligodendrocyte-intrinsic defect. This one changes: Wnt is a neuron-dependent tone that arrests OPCs, and WWOX scaffold anti-Wnt function (DVL2 sequestration) is the lever.
Rejecting observation: in neuron-specific Wwox-null tissue, OPC β-catenin/AXIN2 readouts are normal and the PDGFRα⁺/CC1⁺ ratio is unchanged. The ratio is already measured and "present OPCs" is already stated, so rejection would have to come from the OPC pathway readout.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H2, neuron-derived signal): OPC-restricted Wnt-pathway suppression (Axin2 stabilisation or a dominant-negative β-catenin, driven by a Pdgfra-CreER line) in the neuron-specific Wwox-null.
- Prediction: if H1 is true, CC1⁺ mature cells, g-ratio and diffusion anisotropy (FA) rise without any change in neuronal bursting.
- Cognitive behaviour: improves only in proportion to the myelin gain, and not at all if the network state alone drives the deficit.
- If H2 is true instead, white matter stays low and the bursting stays.

H2. Origin: S1-2, direction reversed
Statement: Aberrant, NMDAR-dependent bursting and desynchronised network activity (not low activity) is the signal that fails to drive OPC maturation, so normalising network state restores myelin and cognition together.
Corpus test: CONTRADICTS S1's direction (reduced drive); the myelin link is NOT STATED.
- Neuron-specific loss gives increased excitatory drive, reduced spontaneous inhibition, bursting that is NMDAR- and gap-junction-dependent, and altered phase-amplitude coupling (CR:390).
- d-APV abolishes the burst; carbenoxolone is not clean (MIM:31-33).
- "Sequential or parallel?" is the open question (meta_network_myelin_glia_current.md:129).
- A candidate mediator is listed in DL:297 (NS-017) but never measured.
Usual reading assumes: hyperexcitability and hypomyelination are two outputs of the lesion. This one changes: bursting is upstream and the myelin deficit is its consequence.
Rejecting observation: acute or chronic NMDAR blockade abolishes bursting in the mutant but the myelin deficit persists (g-ratio and CC1⁺ unchanged).
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): suppress bursting in the Syn-Cre null without touching OPCs. Examples are a sodium-channel-blocker class or an NMDAR-antagonist class given in the myelination window, or a non-pharmacological route such as a neuronal Kv7 opener.
- Prediction if H2 is true: CC1⁺, g-ratio and FA rise, and learning improves, with the OPC-intrinsic Wnt readouts unchanged.
- If H1 is true: bursting is suppressed and white matter stays low.
- Caution on safety: NMDAR blockade in a developing brain removes a maturation signal (MIM:434).

H3. Origin: S1-3 (OUTSIDE, kept)
Statement: An extrinsic M1-muscarinic brake on OPC differentiation is raised in the mutant, so releasing it from OPCs (not from neurons) restores myelin, with a smaller cognitive effect than H1 or H2.
Corpus test: NOT STATED, with a nearby partial CONTRADICT of OPC-only levers.
- Muscarinic, Chrm1 and cholinergic give 0 real hits.
- The corpus does contain a pro-myelinating anticholinergic-type candidate, clemastine (THL:257), and it parks that route: "an oligodendrocyte-autonomous pro-myelinator is not the right lever… even if all OPCs are present, pushing them alone would yield little because they do not receive the axonal signal" (THL:262-264).
- That passage is an argument from neuronal-rescue data, not a test of an M1 manipulation. Silence on M1 itself is therefore "not stated".
Usual reading assumes: the block is a cell-intrinsic maturation arrest. This one changes: the block is a reversible extrinsic receptor signal.
Rejecting observation: OPC-specific Chrm1 deletion in the mutant leaves CC1⁺ and g-ratio at mutant level, while the same deletion raises them in a control demyelination model.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): OPC-specific Chrm1 deletion in the Syn-Cre null, versus OPC-specific Wnt suppression.
- Prediction: H3 gives a modest FA and CC1⁺ gain with a small cognitive gain.
- If the nearest rival (H1) is right, Chrm1 deletion fails.
- THL:262-264 predicts both OPC-only manipulations will be modest.

H4. Origin: S1-5, with S1-4 folded in as the supply-side rival (OUTSIDE, kept)
Statement: Oligodendrocyte-to-axon metabolic coupling (MCT1 lactate shuttle) is impaired, so axons are energetically unsupported and conduction fails even where myelin is present; restoring the coupling rescues conduction and cognition with little change in myelin thickness.
Corpus test: NOT STATED.
- MCT1, lactate shuttle and axon degeneration give 0 hits.
- The corpus holds only "reduced myelinated axons → conduction deficit" (meta_network_myelin_glia_current.md:41) and an unmeasured lipid-homeostasis route to myelin biogenesis labelled INFERENZA (DL:1755-1762).
- Silence is not contradiction, but note the human lactate data: normal blood lactate four times and "extremely low" cerebral lactate by MRS (MIM:312-313), which complicates any lactate-based reading.
Usual reading assumes: the deficit is quantity of myelin. This one changes: the deficit is the support function of myelinating cells for axons, so white-matter integrity can fall with normal-looking myelin.
Rejecting observation: axon counts, axonal mitochondrial and energy markers and ex vivo compound action potential are normal in the mutant, where the compound action potential is in fact reported impaired in neuronal-deletion mice (CC-20260826-CLAIM003-01.md:118).
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1 and H3): oligodendrocyte-specific MCT1 re-expression in the Syn-Cre null.
- Prediction if H4 is true: axon preservation and conduction velocity recover, FA recovers modestly, myelin thickness and CC1⁺ do not change, and cognitive gain tracks conduction.
- If H1 is true, restoring MCT1 leaves CC1⁺ low.

H5. Origin: S1-6 (OUTSIDE, kept)
Statement: Microglia are rate-limiting for OPC maturation or myelin remodelling (TREM2/CSF1R axis), so normalising microglial support restores myelin, with cognition smaller than in H2.
Corpus test: NOT STATED, with a tension that weakens it.
- TREM2, CSF1R and microglial depletion give 0 hits.
- Wwox is not detected in microglia in the rat, and cortical Iba1 is reduced there (PMID31340538.md:55-58, :83-84; DL:539).
- Gliosis falls after neuron-restricted rescue, so it is read as downstream (meta_network_myelin_glia_current.md:43).
- The glial sign is region- and species-dependent (PMID31340538.md:85-88).
- These facts show microglia respond to the neuron, but do not test whether they are also rate-limiting.
Usual reading assumes: microglia are bystanders. This one changes: they are a permissive stage between neuron and OPC.
Rejecting observation: transient microglial depletion and repopulation, or TREM2 loss, in the Syn-Cre null leaves CC1⁺ and g-ratio unchanged.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1/H2): transient CSF1R-class depletion with repopulation, or microglial Trem2 manipulation, in the Syn-Cre null, against restoration of neuronal WWOX alone.
- Prediction if H5 is true: myelin gain without a change in neuronal bursting.
- If H2 is true: bursting persists and microglial manipulation alone changes neither.
- Cognition: a small gain at best.

H6. Origin: S1-13 (OUTSIDE, kept)
Statement: Axon-to-glia neuregulin–ErbB signalling is reduced in the mutant and limits myelination, which would place the myelin deficit in the axonal signal that the OPC receives.
Corpus test: NOT STATED for myelination.
- NRG1 gives 0 hits.
- WWOX–ErbB4 binding exists as structural and cancer biology: WWOX/YAP compete for ErbB-4 (corpus_seed_pubmed_20260806.tsv:46), and native ErbB4 PY1PY2 binds predominantly via WW1 (analysis/dismech_export_spec.md:842-850).
- The corpus does say the neuron→OPC mediator is unknown and druggable in principle (DL:297, NS-017).
- WWOX–ErbB4 binding is a support for the node, not for the myelin claim.
Usual reading assumes: the OPC side is at fault. This one changes: the axon-side ligand or its receptor interaction with WWOX is the failing signal, which would account for the neuron-restricted rescue working.
Rejecting observation: axonal NRG1 and OPC ErbB activation are normal in the mutant, and exogenous pathway activation does not change myelin.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): neuron-specific NRG1 restoration in the Syn-Cre null, versus OPC-specific ErbB activation.
- Prediction if H6 is true: myelin thickness (g-ratio) recovers and FA rises, with a modest cognitive gain.
- If H1 is true: OPC ErbB activation alone fails to unblock the OPCs, because they do not receive an axonal signal.
- A gain from neuronal NRG1 but not from OPC-side Wnt suppression favours H6 over H1.

H7. Origin: S1-8
Statement: The myelin deficit is a consequence of the seizure burden, so seizure control, not myelin-directed therapy, restores white matter and cognition.
Corpus test: CONTRADICTS in the rat; NOT STATED in the neuronal-deletion mouse.
- The rat lde/lde shows MBP/CNP reductions from PND5-10 and mature oligodendrocytes reduced from PND15 (PMID31340538.md:75-79), while the earliest seizure onset is day 16 (CR:116; CR:691 gives 16–63 days). The myelin deficit begins before seizures.
- The Wwox-null mouse shows no epilepsy per PMID 19500159 (CR:116, CLAIM 037), yet has hypomyelination.
- The experiment that would decide, a seizure-matched comparator, has not been done (E-D1 in analysis/therapeutic_translation_second_pass.md:705-718; analysis/commit_candidates AAV9-ENDPOINT-SPLIT at CC-20260826-AAV9-ENDPOINT-SPLIT-01.md:274-275: cognition not measured in any model).
Usual reading assumes: the lesion is primary. This one says seizures cause the loss, but the rat timing says seizure onset is too late to start it. The version that survives is amplification: seizures worsen, but do not initiate, the deficit.
Rejecting observation: Wwox-null or rat tissue shows the same MBP/CC1⁺ deficit before any seizure, and seizure suppression with a matched antiseizure arm does not change white matter (the design E-D1 sets up). The rat already supplies the first half.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H2): seizure suppression through a channel mechanism that does not alter bursting-independent signalling, versus gene addition, with matched seizure suppression in both.
- Prediction if H7 is true: white matter and learning rise in proportion to seizure reduction in both arms.
- Prediction if the primary-lesion reading (H1/H2) is right: only gene addition raises FA and cognition, as E-D1 states (:715).

H8. Origin: S1-15
Statement: Cognitive impairment arises from neuron-intrinsic synaptic and excitability change that runs in parallel with the myelin deficit, so cognition can be restored without changing myelin (and the reverse).
Corpus test: NOT STATED (cognition has never been measured); the structure is held.
- Neuron-specific loss changes both synaptic and intrinsic physiology (CR:390).
- The corpus lists "sequential or parallel" as open (meta_network_myelin_glia_current.md:129).
- CLAIM 003/011 say cognition and developmental trajectory are not measured in any WWOX model (CC-20260826-AAV9-ENDPOINT-SPLIT-01.md:274-275).
- E-D1 is the pre-specified design that would settle it (second_pass:705-718).
Usual reading assumes: hypomyelination explains the cognitive deficit. This one changes: cognition and myelin have separate neuronal causes.
Rejecting observation: restoring myelin alone (OPC-side manipulation with the neuron untouched) raises cognition to control level.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1): neuron-specific WWOX restoration after the myelination window, in the Syn-Cre null, versus OPC-restricted Wnt suppression, with myelin quantified against wild type.
- Prediction if H8 is true: cognitive recovery with FA and g-ratio still reduced, and the OPC arm recovers myelin with no cognitive gain.
- If H1 is true: the reverse.

H9. Origin: ENGINE, built on the stated premise
Statement: mTORC1 signalling may not be hyperactive in this disorder. The limited benefit of inhibition is then expected, and the lever is pathway restoration or other routes, not inhibition.
Corpus test: SUPPORT (weak, transcriptomic), with the mTOR direction formally INSUFFICIENT.
- The only datum is "mTOR/EIF4EBP1 down" and "autophagy down" in WWOX-KO organoids at n=2 vs 4, raw P<0.01, no locator (DL:691).
- That datum was retired from "wrong direction" to "never measured" (CC-20260826-MTOR-DIRECTION-01.md:36-38, :59-62; MIM:31, :112-120).
- mTOR directional statements in the canonical layer: 0 (CC-20260826-MTOR-DIRECTION-01.md:96).
- The WWOX–autophagy registry stubs point in opposite directions and are unread (same file, :74-79).
Usual reading assumes: the mTOR pathway is over-active and inhibition is the logical first lever. This one changes: the sign is unknown, and if the pathway is low, the inhibition result is a symptom of a wrong-direction trial.
Rejecting observation: phospho-S6 and 4E-BP1 phosphorylation, measured at protein level in WWOX-deficient neurons and OPCs, are raised.
Discriminating manipulation (vs the TSC-like reading where mTORC1 is high): OPC- or neuron-restricted mTORC1 activation (e.g. Tsc1 deletion) in the Syn-Cre null, against inhibition.
- Prediction if H9 is true: activation raises CC1⁺ and g-ratio and FA, with a cognitive gain only if the neuron is also corrected.
- If the TSC-like reading is true: activation worsens both, and inhibition helps.

H10. Origin: ENGINE (the second, stress-dependent oligodendrocyte mechanism)
Statement: A cell-autonomous oligodendroglial WWOX function limits differentiation and remyelination under stress, and accounts for the incomplete neuronal rescue, so rescuing the oligodendrocyte side adds a separate, additive myelin gain.
Corpus test: SUPPORT-qualified (IPOTESI, single unrefereed preprint).
- The neuronal-rescue paper's residual deficit: unmyelinated axons ~26 in WT vs ~52 in treated; g-ratio normalises (CR:96).
- Olig2-Cre; Wwox-flox shows no gross phenotype, and no myelin endpoint was measured on it (CC-20260826-CLAIM003-01.md:19-31 and :42-48).
- Under cuprizone, the review reports defective differentiation and remyelination (CC-20260826-CLAIM003-01.md:55-61, citing a preprint labelled IPOTESI).
- The oligodendrocyte-promoter vector was less effective than the neuronal one, but AAV9 has poor oligodendrocyte tropism (DL-MECH-010 at DL:192-195; PMID42422765_partial_locators.md:105).
- THL:262-264 parks a pure oligodendrocyte-autonomous pro-myelinator.
Usual reading assumes: myelin failure is entirely non-cell-autonomous. This one changes: it has two components, an additive stress-gated oligodendrocyte one and the neuronal one.
Rejecting observation: myelin endpoints (g-ratio, unmyelinated axon counts, CC1⁺) in the existing Olig2-Cre; Wwox-flox line are normal at baseline and after cuprizone.
Discriminating manipulation (vs H1/H3): oligodendrocyte-specific Wwox restoration (OPC/OL-targeted vector) in the neuron-restored background, versus neuronal restoration alone.
- Prediction if H10 is true: the combination gives a further drop in unmyelinated-axon count and a further FA gain, with a cognitive gain only if conduction is the limiting step.
- If the neuron is the only driver, the combination adds nothing.
