## 3 · Atoms (full text where open)
A1 Dissociation (the question GIVES it, so it does not discriminate): ethosuximide abolished spike-wave discharges but "did not rescue the social deficits"; deletion of Cacna1g (Cav3.1) abolished thalamocortical bursting and seizures but "fails to rescue social deficits" (Fig. 2, 3).
A2 Cell type: heterozygous loss of Cacna1a in PV interneurons on a stargazer background produced seizures and social deficits; "PV-specific deletion of Cacna1a alone is sufficient to induce social deficits but produces rare absence seizures" (Fig. 4): PV-interneuron hypofunction as the cause of the social deficit.
**A3 (the discriminating atom):** chemogenetic activation of PV interneurons, cortical and thalamic together (global; the paper did not test them separately), abolished seizures AND rescued the social deficit (Fig. 5). That is a COMMON cell-type lever on both outcomes, where the held reading (and the question) suggests two mechanisms needing two treatments.
A4 (discussion, offered as hypothesis by the authors): seizures and social deficit are "at least partially dissociable at the circuit level", possibly through "intracortical oscillatory mechanisms independent of thalamic drive".
Credit rule: A2 is credited when a hypothesis names PV or fast-spiking interneuron hypofunction as the cause of the deficit; A3 only when a hypothesis PREDICTS that raising that interneuron activity (chemogenetic or equivalent) rescues BOTH outcomes; "restoring inhibition" in general is not A3. Three outcomes per atom: support / contradict / not stated; a hypothesis that predicts the opposite (activation worsens, or rescues seizures only) is scored contradict.
