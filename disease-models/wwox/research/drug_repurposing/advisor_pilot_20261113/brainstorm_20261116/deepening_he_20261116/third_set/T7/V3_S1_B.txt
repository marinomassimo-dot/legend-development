Setting: GC-C (GUCY2C) gain of function with raised cGMP in enterocytes, impaired secretory (goblet/Paneth/enteroendocrine) differentiation, leaky barrier and raised cytokines in mice. Everything below is from my own knowledge, with no look-ups. Evidence lines flag where I am unsure.

H1. Excess cGMP is cleared too slowly because the dominant intestinal cGMP phosphodiesterase is limiting; raising PDE activity (PDE5-type or PDE9-type, class level) lowers cGMP and restores the epithelium. · Evidence: PDE5 inhibition raises intestinal cGMP and fluid secretion, and PDE5/PDE9 expression is reported in epithelium; no known test in a GC-C gain-of-function (GOF) model. · The usual reading treats the cyclase as the only source of the excess; this one treats a degradation deficit as co-causal and tunable. · Rejects it: if enterocyte cGMP-PDE hydrolytic activity (cGMP-hydrolysing activity in lysate with class-selective blockers) is unchanged and an equal PDE gain does not change steady-state cGMP, the hypothesis fails. · Discriminator: epithelium-specific PDE overexpression (genetic gain of a cGMP-hydrolysing PDE) against H2 (efflux). Predicts cGMP down in cell lysate, with extracellular cGMP also down, and barrier improved; H2 would instead lower intracellular cGMP while extracellular cGMP rises.

H2. Export of cGMP through MRP4 (ABCC4) is a safety valve; its loss deepens the intracellular cGMP load, and its gain would relieve it. · Evidence: MRP4 exports cGMP and cAMP and is expressed in gut epithelium; MRP4 loss gives higher cGMP in several tissues; none known in GC-C GOF. · The usual reading counts intracellular cGMP as the signal; this one says the extracellular cGMP pool is a separate paracrine signal and a separate load. · Rejects it: if Abcc4 deletion on the GOF background leaves intracellular cGMP and barrier unchanged, it fails. · Discriminator: Abcc4 epithelial deletion on the GOF background, against H1. Predicts intracellular cGMP up and extracellular cGMP down, with barrier worse; H1 predicts both pools move together.

H3. Overproduction depends on ligand (guanylin/uroguanylin) acting on a sensitised receptor; reducing ligand drive normalises cGMP. · Evidence: GOF variants in the kinase-like or catalytic domains often raise ligand-induced response, but some are partly ligand-independent; the mouse-model dependence is unknown to me. · The usual reading sees constitutive activity; this one says the GOF is a gain of ligand sensitivity. · Rejects it: if deleting the ligand genes (Guca2a/Guca2b) leaves cGMP and barrier unchanged, it fails. · Discriminator: ligand-gene deletion on the GOF background, against H4 (constitutive activity). Predicts cGMP down toward wild-type and barrier restored; H4 predicts no change.

H4. The variant is ligand-independent constitutive activity (loss of ATP/kinase-like-domain autoinhibition), so only reducing receptor protein or catalytic output helps. · Evidence: the kinase-homology domain autoinhibits the cyclase and its mutations can release it; no direct evidence for this variant that I know of. · The usual reading attributes excess to ligand; this one says the ligand is irrelevant. · Rejects it: if ligand deletion lowers cGMP by more than 50 percent, it fails. · Discriminator: a heterozygous Gucy2c null allele on the GOF background (halved receptor dose). Predicts cGMP down by roughly half at baseline, barrier improved; H3 predicts similar effects, so combine it with the ligand deletion to separate them.

H5. Receptor desensitisation (phosphorylation of the kinase-like domain, ATP-dependent) is defective and restoring it would normalise cGMP. · Evidence: GC-C is phosphorylated and ATP modulates its cyclase activity; whether the variant is desensitisation-defective is not known to me. · The usual reading treats the output as a fixed gain; this one says the output is a loss of feedback. · Rejects it: if a phospho-site mutant of the receptor that blocks desensitisation does not worsen cGMP, desensitisation is not engaged. · Discriminator: phospho-site knock-in on the wild-type background, against H4. Predicts cGMP raised only transiently after ligand, then fall-off blunted, with barrier mildly worse.

H6. Excess cGMP signals mainly through PKG2 (PRKG2), so that kinase is the barrier-damaging effector. · Evidence: PKG2 mediates GC-C effects on CFTR and NHE3; its role in differentiation and tight junctions is not established to my knowledge. · The usual reading takes cGMP itself as the damaging variable; this one says its kinase effector is. · Rejects it: if epithelial Prkg2 deletion leaves the secretory-cell defect and leak intact, it fails. · Discriminator: epithelial Prkg2 deletion on the GOF background, against H7 (PKA cross-activation). Predicts cGMP unchanged or slightly up (feedback lost) and barrier normalised.

H7. Excess cGMP inhibits PDE3, raising cAMP and activating PKA, and cAMP-PKA is the real damaging effector. · Evidence: cGMP-inhibited PDE3 cross-talk is well described in other tissues; not shown in this epithelium to my knowledge. · The usual reading takes cGMP-PKG as the axis; this one says the harm travels through cAMP. · Rejects it: if cAMP is unchanged in the GOF epithelium, it fails. · Discriminator: PDE3-resistant or PDE3-overexpressing epithelium, against H6. Predicts cGMP unchanged but cAMP down and barrier restored; H6 would leave barrier unchanged.

H8. Excess cGMP drives fluid and chloride secretion through CFTR and NHE3 inhibition, and the resulting luminal flow and osmotic stress, not cGMP signalling to the nucleus, damages the barrier. · Evidence: GC-C stimulation raises CFTR-dependent secretion; it is well established that this causes diarrhoea. · The usual reading separates secretion from differentiation; this one says the secretory load is the cause of the barrier and the cytokine phenotype. · Rejects it: if barrier damage persists when the secretory output (CFTR) is blocked, it fails. · Discriminator: Cftr deletion on the GOF background. Predicts cGMP unchanged and barrier restored; the differentiation defect, if intact, falsifies the secretory-only route.

H9. cGMP directly restrains the Notch/Atoh1 or Wnt programme that gives secretory cell fate, so differentiation fails before the barrier does. · Evidence: GC-C loss alters proliferation and Wnt in some reports; a direct cGMP link to Atoh1 is not known to me. · The usual reading says differentiation defects follow from inflammation; this one says they are primary. · Rejects it: if enforced Atoh1 expression restores secretory cells but the leak persists, differentiation is not the driver of the leak. · Discriminator: Atoh1 re-expression in the GOF epithelium, against H10. Predicts cGMP unchanged, goblet and Paneth cells restored, and barrier improved only if differentiation is causal.

H10. The leak is a direct tight-junction effect of cGMP-PKG (claudin and ZO-1 redistribution), independent of cell fate. · Evidence: GC-C-null mice also have a leaky barrier, so the protein matters in both directions; the mechanism is unknown to me. · The usual reading ties barrier to secretory cells (mucus); this one decouples them. · Rejects it: if barrier is normal in the GOF mice at an age when goblet cells are already abnormal, or the reverse, the coupling is not direct. · Discriminator: junctional proteins (a claudin-2 knockout) on the GOF background. Predicts cGMP unchanged, barrier improved, secretory cells unchanged.

H11. Cytokines (TNF, IFN-gamma, IL-1 beta) are secondary and amplify the defect; neutralising them breaks a loop. · Evidence: cytokines disrupt junctions and secretory cells in general; the loop in this model is not known to me. · The usual reading treats cytokines as read-outs; this one says they are a feed-forward amplifier. · Rejects it: if anti-TNF or equivalent genetic loss lowers cytokines but barrier and cGMP do not move, the loop is not causal. · Discriminator: Tnfrsf1a deletion on the GOF background. Predicts cGMP unchanged (or modestly lower if cytokines up-regulate the receptor) and barrier partly restored.

H12. Microbiota or bile acids drive the inflammation in the setting of the abnormal mucus layer. · Evidence: general colitis models need microbiota; none known here. · The usual reading is epithelium-intrinsic; this one says the cause is luminal. · Rejects it: if germ-free GOF mice show the same barrier defect and cytokines, it fails. · Discriminator: germ-free rederivation of the GOF line. Predicts cGMP unchanged and barrier improved and cytokines down only if microbiota matter.

H13. Receptor trafficking or folding (glycosylation, HSP90 chaperoning, apical retention) amplifies the output, so lowering surface receptor dose normalises it. · Evidence: GC-C needs glycosylation and HSP90 for surface expression in the literature I recall; the variant link is unknown. · The usual reading takes surface receptor as fixed; this one makes it a lever. · Rejects it: if surface receptor level (surface biotinylation) is unchanged in the GOF cells, it fails. · Discriminator: a chaperone or trafficking-defective receptor allele. Predicts cGMP down and barrier improved.

H14. Epithelial regeneration (IL-22/STAT3, stem-cell response) is the bottleneck, so boosting repair compensates without touching cGMP. · Evidence: IL-22 supports secretory-cell and barrier recovery in colitis; no link here known. · The usual reading says normalise the signal; this one says repair the tissue. · Rejects it: if IL-22 pathway activation does not change barrier. · Discriminator: epithelial Stat3 gain on the GOF background. Predicts cGMP unchanged and barrier restored.

H15. The harm requires a long-term adaptive response: chronic cGMP lowers receptor or ligand expression (guanylin loss, as in colitis and tumours), so GC-C signalling is inappropriately unbalanced rather than too high. · Evidence: guanylin and uroguanylin loss occurs in colitis and cancer; the opposite direction here is untested. · The usual reading says too much signal; this one says unbalanced regional signalling (loss of the crypt-to-surface gradient). · Rejects it: if ligand mRNA and protein are unchanged along the crypt axis in the GOF mice. · Discriminator: restoring the gradient with a ligand transgene limited to the surface. Predicts cGMP unchanged on the whole-tissue level but redistributed, and barrier improved.
