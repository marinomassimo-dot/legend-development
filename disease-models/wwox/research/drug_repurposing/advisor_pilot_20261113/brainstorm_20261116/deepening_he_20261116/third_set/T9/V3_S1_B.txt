Note on the evidence lines: the carrier is not named, so I draw on the SLC6 family (DAT/SLC6A3 in dopamine transporter deficiency syndrome, GAT-1/SLC6A1, SERT). I give these from memory and they need checking. Predictions are stated for a knock-in with a misfolding, surface-depleted carrier and hyperactivity that comes from loss of reuptake.

H1. Statement: A ligand that binds and stabilises the folding intermediate in the ER (a pharmacological chaperone) raises surface carrier and lowers hyperactivity. · Evidence: In heterologous cells, some DAT ligands (bupropion, ibogaine-type) were reported to rescue DAT-deficiency variants. GAT-1 variants gave similar results with chemical chaperones. In knock-in mice: none known. · Usual reading assumes the ligand acts only as a blocker, so rescue would be paradoxical. This reading says that binding at the folding stage outweighs inhibition at the surface. · Rejection: the rescued surface carrier is transport-dead because the ligand is still bound, or surface carrier rises without any change in activity. · Discriminator: a binding-site-dead but still ER-exiting mutant of the carrier, compared with a ligand-binding class that does not stabilise folding. Prediction if H1 is true: the stabilising class raises surface carrier and lowers activity, and the non-stabilising class does neither. Nearest rival is H2, a non-specific chaperone.

H2. Statement: A generic chemical chaperone (osmolyte or chemical-chaperone class) improves folding in a non-site-specific way and raises surface carrier. · Evidence: Rescue of misfolded GAT-1 and other SLC6 variants in cells with a butyrate-type chaperone has been reported. Brain exposure in vivo is questionable. · Usual reading: rescue needs a ligand-specific fit. This reading: a general increase in folding capacity is enough. · Rejection: no surface gain in cultured knock-in neurons, or a gain that equals the gain seen with a wild-type carrier (a non-specific expression effect). · Discriminator: block ligand-dependent rescue by mutating the substrate site, and test the generic chaperone on that variant. Prediction if H2 is true: the generic chaperone still raises surface carrier and still lowers activity. A ligand-specific chaperone (H1) would fail there. Nearest rival is H1.

H3. Statement: Reducing Hsp70/Hsc70 holding or Hsp90 client-retention activity lets the mutant leave the ER and raises surface carrier, which lowers hyperactivity. · Evidence: Hsp90 and Hsp70 cycles control triage of misfolded membrane proteins (CFTR is the model). Retention can be rescued by partial inhibition. For SLC6 carriers: none known. · Usual reading: more chaperone gives better folding. This reading: chaperone retention is the barrier. · Rejection: neuronal knock-down of the relevant co-chaperone leaves surface carrier flat. · Discriminator: neuron-specific heterozygous knock-down of the retention co-chaperone (an Hsp90 cochaperone), compared with a general proteostasis boost. Prediction if H3 is true: surface carrier rises and activity falls. The effect is lost in the double with a ubiquitin-ligase knockout. Nearest rival is H4.

H4. Statement: Attenuating ER-associated degradation (partial loss of the Hrd1/Sel1L or p97 pathway) allows more mutant to escape and reach the surface. · Evidence: Partial inhibition of ERAD raised surface CFTR and some channels. Complete loss is toxic, so a graded partial effect is needed. · Usual reading: the misfolded protein is destroyed and nothing can be done. This reading: some of it is correctly folded but is being degraded by a stringent quality control. · Rejection: the ER pool rises but surface carrier does not (it only accumulates). · Discriminator: neuron-selective haploinsufficiency of Sel1L, compared with an export enhancer (H6). Prediction if H4 is true: surface carrier rises only if the fold is near-native. Activity falls modestly. Total ER carrier rises, whereas an export enhancer would not raise the ER pool. Nearest rival is H6.

H5. Statement: Chronic UPR/ISR activation by the retained protein causes network dysfunction independently of transport loss, and dampening it (an ISR inhibitor class, such as eIF2B activation) lowers hyperactivity without restoring the carrier. · Evidence: ISR modulation is protective in several misfolding neuropathies. For SLC6 knock-in mice: none known. · Usual reading: the hyperactivity is a pure loss of function. This reading: part of it is gain-of-toxicity from ER stress. · Rejection: markers of ISR (eIF2α phosphorylation, ATF4 target genes) are normal in the knock-in brain. · Discriminator: a PERK or eIF2α-phosphorylation knock-in that cannot signal, crossed with the carrier variant. Prediction if H5 is true: activity falls and surface carrier is unchanged. If there is no ISR, the cross is silent. Nearest rival is H1.

H6. Statement: Increasing COPII export capacity (an ER exit adaptor or SAR1/Sec24 isoform) pushes more of the mutant to the surface. · Evidence: ER exit of SLC6 carriers needs Sec24 isoforms and a C-terminal ER export motif. For rescue of misfolded variants: none known. · Usual reading: export is passive for folded protein. This reading: export is rate-limiting. · Rejection: overexpression of the exit adaptor leaves surface carrier flat for the variant but raises it for the wild type. · Discriminator: neuronal Sec24 isoform overexpression, compared with Sel1L haploinsufficiency (H4). Prediction if H6 is true: surface carrier rises with no rise in the ER pool, and activity falls. Nearest rival is H4.

H7. Statement: The residual surface carrier is lost faster by regulated endocytosis (PKC or Nedd4-2 ubiquitination), and preventing it raises surface carrier. · Evidence: PKC-driven internalisation of DAT and GAT-1 is established in cells. A misfolded variant could be turned over faster. In mice: none known. · Usual reading: loss is only at the ER. This reading: loss at the surface is also significant. · Rejection: a half-life measured by surface biotinylation chase is equal in knock-in and wild type neurons. · Discriminator: knock-out of the internalisation signal (a lysine-to-arginine mutation at the ubiquitination site) in cis on the mutant. Prediction if H7 is true: surface carrier and transport rise and activity falls. This is opposite to H6 in a pulse-chase design, where only the ER-exit rate changes. Nearest rival is H6.

H8. Statement: Raising membrane cholesterol or palmitoylation (a ZDHHC-dependent lipid raft clustering class) stabilises the mutant at the surface. · Evidence: Cholesterol and palmitoylation regulate DAT and SERT surface stability. Not shown for misfolded variants. · Usual reading: lipid environment is only modulatory. This reading: it sets stability of the partly folded protein. · Rejection: the stabilising lipid class does not change the half-life of surface carrier. · Discriminator: a palmitoylation-site-dead allele on the mutant. Prediction if H8 is true: the surface level falls further and activity increases. The rescue class has no effect in the mutant. Nearest rival is H7.

H9. Statement: Delivering wild-type carrier (AAV, neuron-restricted) adds surface carrier and normalises activity. · Evidence: AAV gene therapy for DAT deficiency has been reported in a knockout mouse (rescue of survival and movement). It is not tested against knock-in missense variants. · Usual reading: the mutant has no role once wild-type is supplied. This reading: the mutant may be dominant-negative and block the wild type. · Rejection: wild-type expression in a knock-in does not raise surface carrier above the mutant-only level (the mutant traps it). · Discriminator: apply the same virus to the knockout and to the knock-in. Prediction if H9 is true: activity falls and surface carrier rises equally in both. A shortfall in the knock-in indicates H10. Nearest rival is H10.

H10. Statement: The mutant co-assembles with wild type into a trapped oligomer, so lowering the mutant allele (allele-specific knock-down) raises surface wild-type carrier. · Evidence: SLC6 carriers are oligomeric, and dominant-negative DAT variants were reported in cells. In mice: none known. · Usual reading: the effect is haploinsufficiency. This reading: the mutant poisons the wild-type partner. · Rejection: heterozygous knock-in and heterozygous null have equal activity and equal surface carrier. · Discriminator: knock-in/knock-out compound versus knock-in/wild-type. Prediction if H10 is true: the heterozygote is more affected than the null heterozygote, and allele-specific silencing raises surface carrier and lowers activity. Nearest rival is H9.

H11. Statement: Hyperactivity arises from the increased post-synaptic signalling (postsynaptic receptor hyperstimulation) and is corrected by receptor blockade whatever the carrier does. · Evidence: Dopamine D1/D2 antagonists reduce hyperactivity in DAT knockout mice. Psychostimulants have a paradoxical sedating effect in them. · Usual reading: the only way is to restore the carrier. This reading: the downstream receptor is a sufficient lever. · Rejection: a receptor antagonist class does not reduce activity in the knock-in. · Discriminator: conditional deletion of the downstream receptor in striatal neurons. Prediction if H11 is true: activity falls and surface carrier is unchanged. Nearest rival is H12.

H12. Statement: Reducing transmitter synthesis or release (partial inhibition of the rate-limiting enzyme, or a presynaptic autoreceptor agonist class) lowers extracellular tone and activity. · Evidence: Partial tyrosine hydroxylase loss reduces hyperdopaminergia in DAT-knockout mice (a reported rescue; I am not certain of the details). · Usual reading: only reuptake sets tone. This reading: supply sets it. · Rejection: heterozygous Th loss in the knock-in leaves activity unchanged. · Discriminator: Th heterozygous allele crossed in versus a postsynaptic receptor deletion (H11). Prediction if H12 is true: activity falls, surface carrier is unchanged, and extracellular transmitter falls, whereas H11 leaves transmitter high. Nearest rival is H11.

H13. Statement: Upregulating an alternative clearance route (a paralogous or low-affinity carrier on glia or neurons, e.g. NET, PMAT, OCT3 or GAT-3) compensates for the missing uptake. · Evidence: NET clears dopamine in prefrontal cortex in DAT-deficient settings. GAT-3 compensates in some GAT-1 models. Not tested here. · Usual reading: the carrier is non-redundant. This reading: redundancy is partial and can be recruited. · Rejection: overexpression of the paralog does not change extracellular transmitter or activity. · Discriminator: a glial-specific overexpression of the paralog. Prediction if H13 is true: activity falls and surface mutant carrier is unchanged. Nearest rival is H12.

H14. Statement: Raising global inhibitory or arousal-circuit tone (noradrenergic α2, GABA-A positive modulation or serotonergic classes) reduces the behaviour at the circuit level. · Evidence: In DAT-deficient mice, serotonergic agents were reported to decrease hyperactivity (I am not certain). · Usual reading: the lever must be on the transmitter in question. This reading: any circuit that sets locomotor gain will work. · Rejection: the effect is equal in the wild-type mouse (non-specific sedation). · Discriminator: a locomotor-specific test (compare with a wild-type littermate treated identically). Prediction if H14 is true: the same fractional fall in wild type and knock-in, and no change in surface carrier. Nearest rival is H11.

H15. Statement: The hyperactivity reflects developmental circuit remodelling, so restoring carrier early rescues it and restoring it in adulthood does only partially. · Evidence: In a DAT deficiency model, adult rescue was partial (reported, not firm). · Usual reading: the phenotype is acute and reversible. This reading: it is partly fixed. · Rejection: adult-onset restoration (conditional Cre-inducible wild-type allele) normalises activity fully and as fast as embryonic restoration. · Discriminator: tamoxifen-timed restoration at postnatal day 5 versus week 12. Prediction if H15 is true: surface carrier is equally restored but activity is normalised only after the early switch. Nearest rival is H9.
