FAMILY: The defective molecule is most likely guanylyl cyclase C (GUCY2C, GC-C), a single-pass receptor guanylyl cyclase of the particulate/receptor GC class. It has an extracellular ligand domain, a kinase-homology (pseudokinase) domain, a hinge/dimerisation domain and a C-terminal class III cyclase domain, and its second messenger is cGMP. Closest related families:
1. Other receptor GCs: GC-A/NPR-A, GC-B/NPR-B, GC-D, GC-E/F (retinal), GC-G.
2. Soluble guanylyl cyclase (sGC, heme-NO/CO sensor), which shares the class III cyclase fold.
3. Adenylyl cyclases (transmembrane AC1-9 and soluble AC10), which also have the class III cyclase fold.
A further related family is the protein kinase / pseudokinase fold, which matches the kinase-homology regulatory domain. Components of the same pathway are PDEs, MRP4/ABCC4 efflux, cGKII (PRKG2), CFTR and NHE3. I list their modulators because they are the likeliest levers.

STEP C: The defect is a gain of function: too much cGMP from GC-C. Entries that correct it fall into five groups.
- (a) Reduce synthesis. These are fewer activating ligands (Guca2a/Guca2b), loss of the ATP/KHD amplification, lower receptor abundance or surface expression, and re-establishing desensitisation by phosphorylation control. Inhibitors of other GCs, such as ODQ-type or sGC blockers, would reduce only the sGC and NO-derived pool and not GC-C itself.
- (b) Raise removal. These are PDE5/PDE9/PDE1 activity and MRP4 efflux. Raising these lowers cGMP, but PDE3 and PDE2 changes also move cAMP.
- (c) Block the effectors, namely cGKII, CFTR and NHE3. This corrects the phenotype without lowering cGMP.
- (d) Correct the epithelial consequences directly. These are Atoh1/Notch and Wnt control of secretory differentiation, ER stress, microbiota/TNF/IL-signalling, and bile-acid load.
- (e) Entries that would not help or would worsen the defect: ligands and ligand mimetics (linaclotide, plecanatide, STa), PDE5/PDE inhibitors (sildenafil, IBMX), MRP4 inhibitors (probenecid, MK571), sGC stimulators or activators, NO donors, ATP-competitive effects that increase KHD signalling, and acid luminal pH (it favours uroguanylin potency). Conditions that raise GC-A/B, sGC or AC activity are irrelevant or harmful except as controls.

Evidence I recall: a GUCY2C gain-of-function variant (S839I, in the kinase-homology domain) causes familial diarrhoea with early-onset IBD-like disease in a Norwegian family. I am not certain of the mouse model details given in the question, and I am not citing any specific paper for the mouse phenotype.

· Usual reading: the variant simply boosts ligand response. This one says the variant bypasses the need for ligand.
