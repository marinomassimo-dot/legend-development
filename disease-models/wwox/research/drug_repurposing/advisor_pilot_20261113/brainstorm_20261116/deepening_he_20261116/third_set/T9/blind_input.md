# Sixty hypotheses (statement and discriminating manipulation only)

## Z01
Statement: The mutant protein retains the wild-type subunit in oligomers (dominant-negative), so silencing or editing the mutant allele frees wild-type carrier and raises surface carrier above the 50 percent expected in a heterozygote.
Discriminating manipulation (versus adding a wild-type copy, the nearest rival): allele-specific silencing of the mutant in the heterozygote, compared with addition of one wild-type copy. Prediction if dominant-negative holds: silencing raises surface above 50 percent of wild type and lowers locomotion more than copy addition. If it is simple haploinsufficiency, both give the same gain.

## Z02
Statement: The carrier that does reach the surface cycles poorly, so raising per-carrier turnover lowers locomotion with no change in surface level.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H1, which is surface-number): a positive modulator class acting on the cycling step, with no change in folding. Surface carrier is unchanged and locomotion falls for H6. For H1 the same class does nothing, because the number of carriers is what limits.

## Z03
Statement: A site-specific ligand that binds and stabilises the folding intermediate raises surface carrier and lowers hyperactivity, but it can bind the carrier's own transport site and so rescue abundance while abolishing function.
Discriminator (nearest rival: generic chemical chaperone/proteostasis class): a substrate-site-dead but fold-competent mutation in cis on the variant. Site-specific ligand: surface rescue lost, no locomotor change. Generic class: surface carrier still rises and activity still falls, if the carrier retains function.

## Z04
Statement: The sign of chaperone-balance manipulation depends on which chaperone arm is limiting. If triage (HSC70-type delivery to degradation) dominates, reducing it raises surface carrier and lowers locomotion. If folding assistance dominates, the opposite holds.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H1): conditional loss of the HSPA8-class chaperone in carrier-expressing neurons. If triage-limited: surface rises, locomotion falls, and the effect does not depend on the ligand pocket. If folding-limited: surface falls and locomotion rises. H1 predicts no dependence on this arm.

## Z05
Statement: Neuron-restricted delivery of wild-type carrier adds functional surface carrier and lowers hyperactivity, with an efficacy ceiling set by whether the variant traps it and by the age of delivery.
Discriminator (nearest rival H8): the same neuron-restricted vector in the knock-in versus the null of the carrier, at the same age. H7 (no trapping) predicts equal surface gain and equal fall in activity in both. A shortfall in the knock-in indicates H8. Overexpression above wild type may push activity below normal.

## Z06
Statement: The mutant subunit co-assembles with wild-type carrier and retains it, so a heterozygote has less than half the surface level. Removing the mutant allele (allele-selective silencing or editing) frees wild-type carrier, raises surface, and lowers hyperactivity.
Discriminating manipulation (vs nearest rival H10): Allele-selective knockdown or editing of the mutant in the heterozygous knock-in (genetic). - If poisoned: wild-type carrier surface rises, total falls, locomotion falls. - If not poisoned: total and surface fall, locomotion does not improve. - Wild-type addition (H10) works in both cases unless dominance is strong.

## Z07
Statement: A partly redundant clearance route (a paralogous or low-affinity carrier, neuronal or glial) can be recruited to substitute for the lost surface carrier, lowering hyperactivity with the variant carrier's surface level unchanged.
Discriminator (nearest rival H2): paralog-null cross on the knock-in background. H3 predicts activity rises further, extracellular clearance falls, and surface variant carrier is unchanged. The benefit of paralog gain is lost in the paralog-null. The H2 effect (reduced supply) persists in the paralog-null.

## Z08
Statement: Chronic unfolded-protein-response/integrated-stress activation by the retained variant causes network dysfunction independently of the missing transport, so dampening it lowers hyperactivity without restoring the carrier.
Discriminator (nearest rival H5): a signalling-dead PERK/eIF2a-phosphorylation allele crossed to the knock-in. H9 predicts activity falls and surface carrier unchanged. The cross is silent if ISR markers are normal. H5 predicts surface carrier rises.

## Z09
Statement: Reducing synthesis of the transmitter the carrier would clear lowers the extracellular load and normalises locomotion with no change in the carrier.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H8): dopamine-neuron-restricted tyrosine hydroxylase haploinsufficiency on the knock-in. Extracellular transmitter falls, locomotion falls, surface carrier is unchanged. H8 leaves transmitter high. If the carrier clears an inhibitory transmitter, the locomotor sign flips.

## Z10
Statement: Hyperactivity is driven by excess extracellular transmitter. A partial reduction in transmitter supply (rate-limiting synthesis or vesicular loading) lowers drive without touching the carrier.
Discriminating manipulation (vs nearest rival H8): Heterozygous deletion of the rate-limiting synthetic enzyme (or vesicular transporter) in carrier neurons (genetic). - Locomotion falls, surface is unchanged, and extracellular transmitter falls. - Locomotion and surface do not separate H7 from H8. The separating readout is extracellular transmitter: H8 leaves it high. - A chaperone (H1/H2) would change surface instead.

## Z11
Statement: ERAD removes the mutant before export, so relaxing ERAD raises the ER and surface pool and lowers locomotion. This is the opposite of the lysosome-first route that the folder holds for WWOX.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H3): conditional deletion of the ERAD E3 ligase in carrier neurons, read as surface versus total carrier. If surface rises, H4 holds and locomotion falls. If only total rises, H3 (export-limited) holds and locomotion is unchanged.

## Z12
Statement: Hyperactivity is fixed by a developmental adaptation, so restoration of surface carrier normalises locomotion only if it is started early.
Discriminating manipulation (versus H9, titration): a Cre-inducible restored allele switched on neonatally versus in adulthood at matched surface level. Prediction: surface up in both; locomotion down only with early induction. If H9 holds, locomotion tracks the level reached, not the timing.

## Z13
Statement: Part of the hyperactivity is a toxic gain from ER stress caused by retained misfolded carrier, so lowering the terminal UPR/ISR arm (CHOP/PERK-ISR) lowers locomotion without changing surface carrier.
Discriminating manipulation (versus H1): neuron-specific deletion of the terminal-UPR effector CHOP in the knock-in. Prediction if H2 holds: surface unchanged, locomotion down. Prediction if the phenotype is pure loss of function (H1 territory): both unchanged. A knock-in more hyperactive than the null favours H2.

## Z14
Statement: Hyperactivity is carried by an NMDAR-dependent network-burst state downstream of the carrier lesion. Lowering NMDAR-dependent drive (a low-affinity open-channel antagonist class) lowers locomotion without changing carrier surface level.
Discriminating manipulation (vs nearest rival H9): Conditional deletion of GluN1 or GluN2B in forebrain excitatory neurons of the knock-in (genetic). - Locomotion falls more in the knock-in than in the wild type, and surface carrier is unchanged. - NKCC1 deletion (H9) acts only through the chloride gradient. The separating readout is E_GABA: unchanged under GluN deletion, shifted under NKCC1 deletion.

## Z15
Statement: Surface loss is partly an export-checkpoint shortfall. Strengthening COPII cargo recognition (Sec24 isoform gain or export-motif strengthening) raises surface carrier and lowers hyperactivity, but only if the exported protein is functional.
Discriminating manipulation (vs nearest rival H2): Sec24-isoform or SAR1 gain in carrier neurons (genetic), compared with BiP/calnexin gain. - Surface and locomotion track export capacity. Locomotion falls with gain and rises with loss, and the gain still works when folding capacity is lowered. - Chaperone gain (H2) does not help an export-limited pool. - If surface rises but uptake and locomotion do not change, the corpus's "stable but inert" outcome (research/discovery_ledger_current.md:1027) applies.

## Z16
Statement: Variant carrier that reaches the surface keeps reduced per-molecule transport, so potentiating the transport cycle of the surface-resident fraction normalises uptake and activity without raising surface carrier number.
Discriminator (nearest rival H5, abundance rescue): a positive allosteric potentiator class of the transport cycle, applied in the knock-in and in a line with an ER-retention signal in cis. H4 predicts surface carrier unchanged by surface biotinylation, activity falls in the knock-in, and no effect in the ER-retained control. H5 predicts surface carrier rises.

## Z17
Statement: A ligand-site or kinetic stabiliser binds the folded state of the missense carrier in the ER, restores export, raises surface carrier and lowers hyperactivity, but only if the stabiliser does not occlude the function it rescues.
Discriminating manipulation (versus H2, a non-ligand route): run the ligand class on a knock-in that carries, in cis, a pocket-dead but fold-neutral substitution. If the effect is lost, a binding-dependent chaperone (H1) holds. The folder's own design is a parallel negative-control variant (therapeutic_hypotheses_ledger_current.md:335-339). Prediction if H1 holds: surface rescue lost and locomotion unchanged in the pocket-dead line, surface up and locomotion down in the intact mutant. If surface still rises, the lever is non-specific (H2 favoured).

## Z18
Statement: Hyperactivity is set by transmitter supply as well as by clearance, so partial reduction of transmitter synthesis or release lowers extracellular tone and activity without touching the carrier.
Discriminator (nearest rival H10): cross in a heterozygous allele of the rate-limiting synthesis enzyme, versus conditional deletion of the postsynaptic receptor. H2 predicts activity falls, extracellular transmitter falls, and surface carrier is unchanged. H10 predicts activity falls with transmitter still high and surface carrier unchanged.

## Z19

Above physiological level the treated animal can exceed wild type in open-field activity, and late induction can leave locomotion partly high.
Discriminating manipulation (vs nearest rival H6): A recombinase-switchable, dose-graded wild-type allele in the knock-in, induced at early postnatal versus adult age (genetic). - Surface rises with dose at both ages. - Locomotion normalises at intermediate dose, can overshoot at supraphysiological dose, and normalises less after adult induction. - Allele silencing (H6) predicts a fall in total carrier and no overshoot. ## Z20
Statement: Part of the hyperactivity is a developmental adaptation, so restoring surface carrier early normalises locomotion and restoring it in the adult does so only partly.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H10, level rather than timing): a Cre-revertible wild-type allele restored early versus in the adult, with surface level matched by measurement. Surface is equal by design. Locomotion is normal only with early restoration for H7. For H10, locomotion depends on surface level alone and not on timing.

## Z21
Statement: ER export, not folding or degradation, limits the marginally folded mutant, so raising cargo-selective COPII capacity raises surface carrier and lowers locomotion.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H4): conditional gain of the cargo-selective Sec24 isoform on the knock-in. Surface rises and locomotion falls for H3. For H4 surface does not move, because the mutant is cleared before it reaches the export step.

## Z22
Statement: Restoring surface carrier to above the wild-type set point overshoots and itself produces hyperactivity, so locomotion normalises only in a window of restored level, bounded below by a threshold under 100% of wild type.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H7, timing): graded restoration at fixed timing with per-animal surface quantification. Locomotion reaches wild type at a surface level near wild type and exceeds it at supra-wild-type surface levels. For H7 it depends on timing and not on level.

## Z23
Statement: The variant is cleared by a degradation route that has not been identified (ERAD/proteasome, ER-phagy, or post-ER lysosome). Blocking the route that is actually operative raises surface carrier and lowers hyperactivity only if a fold-competent fraction exists.
Discriminating manipulation (vs nearest rival H2): Conditional Hrd1/Sel1L deletion in carrier neurons (genetic). - If ERAD-dominant, surface rises modestly and locomotion falls. - If clearance is post-ER lysosomal, total rises but surface and locomotion are unchanged. - H2 predicts total up without a surface gain when folding is the limit.

## Z24
Statement: The mutant protein traps wild-type carrier (oligomer trapping or ER stress), so removing the mutant allele's product raises wild-type surface carrier and lowers locomotion.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival: pure loss of function, which includes H6): mutant-allele-specific knockdown in the heterozygote, compared with the heterozygous null. For H5, wild-type surface rises and locomotion falls. For pure loss of function nothing changes.

## Z25
Statement: The small surface pool is removed by ubiquitin-dependent internalisation, so blocking removal (E3-ligase or dynamin class, or ubiquitin-acceptor-site mutation) lengthens surface residence and lowers hyperactivity.
Discriminating manipulation (vs nearest rival H4): Nedd4-2 conditional deletion, or lysine-to-arginine mutation of the carrier's ubiquitin acceptor sites in cis (genetic). - Surface half-life lengthens with unchanged biosynthesis. Surface rises modestly and locomotion falls partly. - If the ER block is complete, nothing changes. - H4 changes surface by raising delivery and is lost when export is blocked.

## Z26
Statement: A ligand that binds the carrier's own pocket acts as a folding scaffold, so ER exit and surface density rise and locomotion falls.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H2, a non-ligand folding lever): put in cis a second-site change that deletes ligand binding but leaves misfolding unchanged. Locomotion stays high and surface stays at the knock-in baseline for H1, while H2 is unaffected.

## Z27
Statement: Hyperactivity is carried by the striatal-projection output of the excess transmitter (D1-pathway cAMP/PKA signalling), so removing that output normalises locomotion with surface carrier untouched.
Discriminating manipulation (versus H5, supply reduction): conditional Drd1 deletion in striatal projection neurons of the knock-in. Prediction: locomotion down, surface unchanged, extracellular transmitter unchanged or high. H5 gives the same locomotion and surface effect but lowers extracellular transmitter, so transmitter level is the discriminator.

## Z28
Statement: Reducing the postsynaptic response, for example by removing D1 signalling in direct-pathway striatal neurons, normalises locomotion while transmitter stays high and surface carrier stays low.
Discriminating manipulation (nearest rival H9, input reduction): direct-pathway-restricted D1 deletion on the knock-in. Locomotion falls, extracellular transmitter stays high, and surface carrier is unchanged. H9 predicts transmitter falls.

## Z29
Statement: Raising ER folding capacity (adaptive UPR output or chemical chaperones) shifts the kinetic partition of the variant toward export, so surface carrier rises and hyperactivity falls. The sign depends on which chaperone is manipulated.
Discriminating manipulation (vs nearest rival H3): ER chaperone gain (BiP or calnexin overexpression) in carrier neurons. Surface rises and locomotion falls even in a Sel1L/Hrd1-null background, because the effect acts on folding, not on dwell time. A loss-of-function allele of the same chaperone lowers surface and raises locomotion further. H3 predicts no folding gain from chaperone gain when ERAD is already blocked.

## Z30
Statement: The variant co-assembles with wild-type carrier into a trapped oligomer, so allele-specific lowering of the variant raises wild-type surface carrier and lowers hyperactivity.
Discriminator (nearest rival H7): knock-in/null compound versus knock-in/wild-type. H8 predicts the knock-in/wild-type heterozygote is more affected, and allele-specific silencing of the variant raises surface carrier (wild-type-derived) and lowers activity. A null/wild-type heterozygote is near normal.

## Z31
Statement: Lipid post-translational stabilisation (DHHC-mediated palmitoylation, cholesterol or PIP2 occupancy) lengthens residence of the mutant at the surface, so raising it increases surface carrier and lowers locomotion without changing folding.
Discriminating manipulation (versus H8, route-specific clearance): cysteine-to-alanine substitution at the palmitoylation sites in cis in the knock-in. Prediction if H4 holds: surface falls further and locomotion rises further. Gain of the palmitoyl-transferase gives surface up and locomotion down. If H8 holds, the cysteine mutant shows no change in the post-arrival half-life that the route block would change.

## Z32
Statement: Restoration is effective only within a narrow expression band. Supraphysiological gene addition confounds the locomotor readout, so locomotion should be read against the restored level, not against presence or absence of the rescue.
Discriminating manipulation (versus H7, timing): graded neuron-selective gene addition (an intermediate-expression arm) versus endogenous-locus allele correction. Prediction: surface rises monotonically with the added dose; locomotion falls to a wild-type plateau at partial restoration and does not go lower. Endogenous correction reaches the wild-type plateau without overshoot.

## Z33
Statement: The loss of mutant carrier at the surface is set by one dominant clearance route (ER-associated proteasomal versus post-ER endolysosomal or chaperone-mediated). Blocking that route raises carrier mass, and the sign of any chaperone-pathway manipulation depends on which route is dominant.
Discriminating manipulation (versus H1, which changes fold): in knock-in neurons, a lysosomal-flux blocker class versus a proteasome-flux blocker class, each with a chase and an EndoH readout. - If post-ER endolysosomal clearance dominates: lysosomal block raises surface, proteasome block does not. - If ER-associated clearance dominates: proteasome block raises ER-retained mass, surface unchanged. - Locomotion falls only where the rescued carrier is functional (the H1 caveat).

## Z36
Statement: ER export capacity (COPII cargo adaptor or Sec24 isoform dosage) is rate-limiting for the partly folded mutant, so raising it increases the post-ER pool and surface carrier and lowers locomotion.

## Z37
Statement: Carrier loss lowers inhibitory efficacy through a raised intracellular chloride load. Reducing NKCC1-dependent chloride loading (NKCC1-blocker class) restores inhibition and lowers locomotion without changing carrier surface. If the carrier is itself a KCC2-type chloride cotransporter, surface level and phosphorylation state (Ser940/Thr1007) are the lever.
Discriminating manipulation (vs nearest rival H8): Conditional Slc12a2 (NKCC1) deletion in knock-in forebrain neurons (genetic). - Locomotion falls only if E_GABA is shifted, and carrier surface is unchanged. - GluN deletion (H8) lowers locomotion with E_GABA unchanged. - A Ser940-phosphomimetic KCC2 allele would raise KCC2 surface, which separates this lever from carrier-level rescue.

## Z38

The chaperone effect is lost: locomotion stays high and surface stays low. A general proteostasis agent (H2) still raises surface and lowers locomotion on the double mutant.
