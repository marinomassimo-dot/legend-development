**What I know that is specific and reliable**

- Dopamine transporter deficiency syndrome (DTDS, also called infantile parkinsonism-dystonia) is caused by recessive loss-of-function variants in SLC6A3, which encodes DAT. Cerebrospinal fluid HVA is high and the HVA/5-HIAA ratio is raised, and the clinical course is a progressive hyperkinetic-to-hypokinetic movement disorder.
- Many pathogenic DAT missense variants cause misfolding, reduced surface expression and retention in the endoplasmic reticulum. Some variants also have reduced uptake per transporter. This is the rationale for pharmacological chaperones. The mechanism reasoning is mine. I know no clinical evidence for it.
- Knock-in mouse work exists for the DAT missense variants A313V (mouse) and the human A314V. I know of a DAT-deficiency-type knock-in line (the A313V line from the Sorkina/Sorkin and Gether-type groups). Its general features are reduced DAT protein and surface levels, reduced dopamine uptake, and a hyperdopaminergic or dysregulated dopaminergic phenotype. I am not certain of the exact behavioural and neurochemical details, and I will not state them as fact.
- Mechanism level only: with reduced DAT clearance, extracellular dopamine is prolonged, and tonic dopaminergic signalling and receptor regulation are altered. Amphetamine is a DAT substrate. It acts by reversing transport and by promoting dopamine efflux. That mechanism depends on a functioning DAT at the membrane. For this reason I would expect a poor or altered response in a transporter-deficient animal. I give this as inference from the mechanism, not as a recalled result.
- Alpha-methyl-para-tyrosine (AMPT, metyrosine) inhibits tyrosine hydroxylase and depletes catecholamines. Mechanistically it reduces dopamine synthesis and so it is a plausible way to lower dopamine load in a hyperdopaminergic state. It is used clinically in pheochromocytoma.
- Noribogaine is the active metabolite of ibogaine. It binds the serotonin transporter, which is where it has been characterised. Ibogaine and noribogaine stabilise an inward-facing transporter conformation. Work from the Sitte laboratory showed that ibogaine and noribogaine can act as pharmacological chaperones for a set of misfolded DAT variants (mutations at the C-terminal region or others, in the context of DTDS and other folding-deficient variants). They rescued surface expression and function in heterologous cells. I recall this as cell-culture work, not mouse work. The effect I remember is partial rescue of function in some variants.
- Related background: the same group (Sitte and colleagues) also did earlier work on rescuing misfolded SERT and DAT variants with ibogaine-type compounds, including in a Drosophila model. Their related DAT-variant rescue work also covered some compounds beyond noribogaine, and I cannot reliably name them.

**What I do not know**

- I know of no study giving AMPT to A313V or A314V DAT knock-in mice. I do not know of a published AMPT treatment trial in any DTDS mouse model.
- I know of no study giving amphetamine to A313V or A314V knock-in mice as a treatment. I cannot say whether amphetamine was tested in them, or what the result would be.
- I know of no study of noribogaine, or any other pharmacological chaperone, in a DTDS knock-in mouse in vivo. I do not know its pharmacokinetics or brain exposure in such mice, or whether any rescue has been shown at the whole-animal level.
- I do not know whether noribogaine rescues the A314V variant specifically, or what the dose-response, durability and safety are.
- I do not know of any human data (case report or trial) on chaperones or AMPT in DTDS. The treatment landscape I recall is dopamine agonists, levodopa and anticholinergics. They have limited benefit, and the recently reported AAV-based gene therapy approach is separate from the question here.
