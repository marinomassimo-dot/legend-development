H1. Origin S1-1 (INSIDE)
- Statement: A ligand-site or kinetic stabiliser binds the folded state of the missense carrier in the ER, restores export, raises surface carrier and lowers hyperactivity, but only if the stabiliser does not occlude the function it rescues.
- Corpus test: SUPPORT, conditional.
  - analysis/proteostasis_rationale.md:11 and :53-57 give the rationale and the three chaperone families, "nothing demonstrated for WWOX".
  - proteostasis_rationale.md:96 says stabilising does not mean restoring function.
  - dismissal_ledger_current.md:70 (D-04) says stability and function are in tension, "stability is bought with occlusion".
  - discovery_ledger_current.md:1027 describes P282A as stable but inert.
  - therapeutic_hypotheses_ledger_current.md:331 raises a doubt: a buried proline cannot be removed by a chaperone.
- Usual reading assumes: more surface protein means more function. This reading changes that. Surface level is a necessary readout but not the lever's success criterion, and a stable-but-inert carrier would raise surface while locomotion stays high.
- Rejection observation: a ligand class that raises surface carrier of the mutant, with transport function measured in the same cells, leaves locomotion at knock-in level.
- Discriminating manipulation (versus H2, a non-ligand route): run the ligand class on a knock-in that carries, in cis, a pocket-dead but fold-neutral substitution. If the effect is lost, a binding-dependent chaperone (H1) holds. The folder's own design is a parallel negative-control variant (therapeutic_hypotheses_ledger_current.md:335-339). Prediction if H1 holds: surface rescue lost and locomotion unchanged in the pocket-dead line, surface up and locomotion down in the intact mutant. If surface still rises, the lever is non-specific (H2 favoured).
