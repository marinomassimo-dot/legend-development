Notes. The carrier is treated generically, as a Na+-coupled SLC6-type transporter. The dopamine transporter (DAT/SLC6A3) is the model case in the literature on misfolding variants and hyperactive knock-in mice, so the examples lean that way. "Surface" means the carrier's plasma-membrane level on the relevant neurons, e.g. by surface biotinylation, a tagged knock-in, or imaging. "Activity" means locomotor activity in the open field. Everything below is from memory, with no source checked in this run, and is mechanism level only.

H1. Statement: A ligand that binds the folding-competent state acts as a pharmacological chaperone, which raises ER exit and surface carrier and lowers hyperactivity. · Evidence: Pharmacological rescue of misfolded DAT and other SLC6 variants in cells (ibogaine-type and similar ligands, and 4-phenylbutyrate-like agents) is known. Its benefit in knock-in mice is not established as far as I know, and it can be confounded by transport inhibition. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading treats any ligand as blocking the carrier and so worsening function. Here the binding is folding-stabilising and transient, and it leaves a net gain of functional carrier. · Rejection: The cell-surface level fails to rise after ligand exposure and washout, in neurons or slices from the knock-in mouse. · Discriminator: Put a pocket-disrupting second-site substitution on the misfolded variant, which keeps misfolding but abolishes ligand binding (genetic). Prediction: the chaperone effect disappears, so locomotion stays high and surface stays low. A general proteostasis modulator (H2) would still work on this double mutant.

H2. Statement: Boosting ER folding capacity (chaperone induction, chemical chaperone, or unfolded-protein-response tuning toward adaptive output) raises surface carrier and lowers hyperactivity. · Evidence: This is the generic rescue strategy for trafficking-deficient membrane proteins, for example in CFTR and GABA-transporter variants. In vivo evidence for the carrier here: none known. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading says the misfolded protein is lost for good. Here it is a kinetic partitioning problem that can be shifted without ligand binding. · Rejection: The surface level is unchanged after the folding capacity is raised. · Discriminator: Overexpress or knock out a single ER chaperone, with a dominant-negative or null allele of the relevant Hsp70/Hsp40 or calnexin node (genetic). Prediction: a gain raises surface and lowers locomotion, a loss lowers surface and raises locomotion further. A ligand-site chaperone (H1) would not depend on this node.

H3. Statement: Reducing ER-associated degradation (ERAD) of the variant lengthens its ER dwell time and lets more of it fold or exit, so surface carrier rises and hyperactivity falls. · Evidence: Rescue of misfolded transporters or channels by ERAD inhibition is known for several proteins. Unknown for this carrier. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading calls degradation the proper disposal of junk. Here degradation is excessive and removes a partly functional species. · Rejection: A neuron-specific loss of an E3-ligase/retrotranslocation component raises total carrier but not surface carrier, and locomotion is unchanged. · Discriminator: Hrd1/Sel1L conditional deletion in the carrier-expressing neurons (genetic), against export-motif strengthening (H4). Prediction: surface rises and activity falls only if the ER pool is fold-competent. Otherwise total protein rises while surface and locomotion stay unchanged, which would favour H4 or H1.

H4. Statement: Strengthening ER exit (COPII cargo recognition) raises surface carrier regardless of the variant's folding state, and so lowers hyperactivity. · Evidence: ER export motifs and Sec24 isoforms control the surface level of transporters, such as GAT1 and DAT. Not tested for this variant. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading puts the defect in folding. Here the bottleneck is the ER-export checkpoint, and the folded fraction is under-exported. · Rejection: Added export capacity (Sec24 isoform or an export-motif change) leaves surface carrier unchanged. · Discriminator: Introduce a Sec24-isoform or SAR1 gain or loss specifically in carrier neurons (genetic). Prediction: surface and locomotion track export capacity, up with gain and down with loss, but a folding chaperone (H1/H2) would not change export-limited surface unless folding was limiting.

H5. Statement: Reducing endocytic or ubiquitin-dependent removal from the plasma membrane (Nedd4-2 and dynamin-dependent internalisation) increases the residence time of the small surface pool, so surface rises and locomotion falls. · Evidence: Regulated DAT internalisation (PKC, Nedd4-2) is established for wild-type carrier. A mutant-specific effect: none known. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading says the problem is delivery. Here it is removal, and the surface pool that exists is too short-lived. · Rejection: A block of internalisation does not raise surface carrier in knock-in neurons, because there is little escaped protein to retain. · Discriminator: Knock out Nedd4-2 (or mutate the carrier's ubiquitination sites) in carrier neurons (genetic), against H4. Prediction: surface rises only modestly and locomotion drops only partly. Only a surface-residence defect would respond. If the ER block is complete, nothing changes.

H6. Statement: Restoring normal carrier dosage by adding a wild-type copy (gene replacement) restores clearance and lowers hyperactivity. · Evidence: Gene or protein replacement is a known principle. For this carrier in the knock-in: none known. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading assumes the missense allele is simply loss-of-function. Here replacement works only if the mutant does not poison the wild-type. · Rejection: A wild-type transgene in the knock-in background fails to normalise locomotion, which suggests dominance or poisoning. · Discriminator: Express a wild-type allele in the knock-in (heterozygous and homozygous) and compare with allele-specific silencing of the mutant (H11). Prediction: if there is no poisoning, locomotion falls and surface wild-type carrier rises in both. If the mutant is dominant-negative, only silencing works.

H7. Statement: Hyperactivity is a downstream effect of loss of neurotransmitter clearance, so an alternate clearance route (a second transporter such as NET or OCT3/PMAT, or glial uptake) should compensate without touching the mutant carrier. · Evidence: In DAT knockouts, compensation by other uptake routes is partial. For misfolding knock-ins: none known. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading demands rescue of the mutant protein. Here the carrier is bypassed. · Rejection: Added alternative uptake capacity fails to lower locomotion. · Discriminator: Overexpress the alternative transporter, or knock it out in the knock-in (genetic). Prediction: overexpression lowers locomotion and leaves the mutant carrier's surface level unchanged. Knockout raises locomotion and also leaves surface unchanged. A chaperone (H1) would change surface instead.

H8. Statement: Blocking the postsynaptic receptor class that reads the excess transmitter (for DAT, the D1- or D2-type receptors) removes the signalling excess and so the hyperactivity. · Evidence: Dopamine receptor antagonism lowers the locomotion of DAT-knockout mice in studies I recall. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading treats hyperactivity as a carrier problem. Here it is a receptor-stimulation problem. · Rejection: Receptor-class blockade does not lower locomotion in the knock-in mouse. · Discriminator: Delete the receptor in the striatal neurons that carry it (genetic, conditional), against H9, where the amount of transmitter is reduced. Prediction: locomotion falls and surface carrier is unchanged. If the receptor is dispensable and the drive is presynaptic, locomotion is unchanged.

H9. Statement: Cutting transmitter synthesis or vesicular loading (for example, a partial reduction in the rate-limiting synthetic enzyme or in the vesicular transporter) lowers extracellular transmitter, so activity falls. · Evidence: In DAT-knockout mice, partial synthesis reduction gives some normalisation, by memory. Not tested for the knock-in. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading says to restore uptake. Here supply is lowered instead. · Rejection: Halving synthesis does not reduce locomotion. · Discriminator: Heterozygous deletion of the synthetic enzyme or vesicular transporter in the knock-in (genetic). Prediction: locomotion falls and surface carrier is unchanged. This separates it from H8, where only receptor signalling changes and transmitter stays high.

H10. Statement: Raising serotonergic tone (a class that boosts 5-HT signalling) quiets hyperactivity through a paradoxical serotonin route. · Evidence: In DAT-knockout mice, psychostimulants and serotonergic agents lower locomotion paradoxically. This is a known finding, and its mechanism is debated. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading says stimulating compounds should increase locomotion. Here they reduce it by acting through a different monoamine system. · Rejection: Serotonin depletion fails to abolish the paradoxical lowering. · Discriminator: Delete the serotonin-synthesising enzyme in the central neurons (Tph2) in the knock-in. Prediction: baseline locomotion is similar or higher and the paradoxical lowering is lost. Surface carrier is unchanged. This separates it from H8/H9, which don't need serotonin.

H11. Statement: The mutant carrier oligomerises with the wild-type subunit and traps it in the ER, so removing the mutant allele (allele-specific silencing) frees the wild-type and raises surface carrier. · Evidence: SLC6 transporters oligomerise, and dominant-negative effects of DAT variants on the wild type are shown in cells. In the knock-in mouse: none known. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading says heterozygous loss is half-dosage. Here the loss is more than half, because of poisoning. · Rejection: Silencing the mutant allele in a heterozygote does not raise the wild-type's surface level. · Discriminator: Allele-selective knockdown in the heterozygous knock-in, against wild-type addition (H6). Prediction: surface of the wild-type carrier rises and locomotion falls, with total carrier lower. If there is no poisoning, silencing lowers total carrier and locomotion does not improve, so it fails where H6 works.

H12. Statement: Chronic ER stress (the integrated stress response) in the carrier neurons drives the hyperactivity apart from loss of uptake, and dampening it normalises behaviour. · Evidence: Misfolding disease models show ISR activation and rescue by ISR inhibitors in other proteinopathies. For this carrier: none known. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading says the phenotype comes from missing carrier. Here part of it comes from toxic gain from retained protein. · Rejection: ISR suppression leaves locomotion unchanged. · Discriminator: Neuron-specific deletion of a stress-pathway effector (for example the PERK arm or its downstream factor), against H2. Prediction: locomotion falls with unchanged surface carrier. Folding capacity (H2) would instead raise surface.

H13. Statement: Strengthening the carrier's trafficking partners (syntaxin-1, Rit2, or other interacting proteins) raises delivery or stability of the mutant at the surface. · Evidence: Partner proteins regulate DAT surface expression (syntaxin-1A, Rit2, alpha-synuclein) in wild-type. Not tested for variants. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading treats the carrier as autonomous. Here partner levels set the surface level. · Rejection: Overexpressing the partner has no effect on surface carrier in knock-in neurons. · Discriminator: Partner-binding-site mutation on the variant (genetic), against export motif changes (H4). Prediction: if the partner route is real, the partner gain raises surface and lowers locomotion on the plain variant and not on the binding-site-dead one. Otherwise nothing changes.

H14. Statement: The hyperactivity is largely a developmental circuit adaptation, so rescue works only if applied early, and adult rescue of the carrier level will not normalise behaviour. · Evidence: Hyperactivity in constitutive knockouts and knock-ins may reflect developmental compensation. For this carrier: none known. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading assumes the phenotype follows the current carrier level. Here it follows the history of carrier loss. · Rejection: Adult-onset rescue (a switchable wild-type allele) fully normalises locomotion. · Discriminator: A recombinase-switchable wild-type allele in the knock-in, induced either in early postnatal life or in adulthood. Prediction: early induction lowers locomotion and raises surface, and adult induction raises surface but leaves locomotion partly high. If the carrier level alone mattered, both would lower locomotion equally.

H15. Statement: A positive modulator of the transport cycle of the few surface carriers that exist (a gating or turnover enhancer) restores clearance without changing surface carrier. · Evidence: Transport-enhancing allosteric ligands are proposed for some SLC6 members. For this variant: none known. · Usual reading vs this one: The usual reading says the loss is of protein number. Here it is a shortfall in per-molecule activity, so number is not the lever. · Rejection: Modulation of residual surface carriers fails to normalise uptake in knock-in synaptosomes. · Discriminator: Introduce a mutation in the putative allosteric site (genetic) and compare with the chaperone (H1). Prediction: locomotion falls and surface is unchanged with the modulator. A chaperone raises surface, and a site-dead mutant abolishes the modulator effect but not the chaperone effect.
