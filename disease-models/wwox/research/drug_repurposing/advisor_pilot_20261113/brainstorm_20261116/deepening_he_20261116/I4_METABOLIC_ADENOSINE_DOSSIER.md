# I4 · Metabolic, ketone and adenosine axis (HE with ADV; v1, 2026-11-16; non-canonical; hypothesis, not finding; not medical advice)

Written for: ADV, SCI, IT (second reader to be named by ADV). Mechanism level only: no dose, no recommendation to use any agent or diet, reference genotype class only, BLOCK-1 flags on every class named. Labels: **KNOWN**, **HELD-DATA** (table), **ASSUMED**, **INFERRED**. Computation: `i4_metabolic/` (panel table, composition check, tone arithmetic, HDAC rank), all reproducible from the held tables.

## Answer first
1. **The premise "Adora1 lower, Adk lower while Gfap doubles" holds in one place only.** It is a single hippocampal nuclei pool per arm (GSE158684, S deletion, no variance): neuron-like Adk 156.8 to 126.7 CPM (−19%), Adora1 39.6 to 19.3 (−51%), astrocyte-like Gfap 145 to 328 (SCI and IT recounts agree). The human KO organoid also has Adora1 −0.51 log2 (SCI's pretest). **The bulk tables do not show it:** Adk and Adora1 are inside the 1.5-fold band in the P17 neuron-deletion rows, and in the P47T line Adk is −0.06 to −0.18 log2 (not significant) and Adora1 is +0.29 (cortex) and +0.32 (PFC), not significant. So the Adora1 fall is two for (pool, organoid) and two against (bulk deletion, P47T) and is not established. HELD-DATA, descriptive.
2. **The sign of adenosine tone depends on exactly that unresolved point.** Lower Adk raises extracellular adenosine; lower Adora1 lowers the receptor reserve. As arithmetic (`i4_metabolic/tone_arithmetic.tsv`, assumptions in the script header): with the pool values, tone (adenosine × receptor) is **0.40 to 0.96 of wild type in 12 of 12 parameter cells (lower)**; if Adora1 is unchanged, as the bulk reads, tone is **1.11 to 1.38 (higher)**. So "a lower adenosine brake" is not a consequence of Adk alone; it needs the receptor fall to be real. This is the one observation that separates the two readings: an A1 antagonist step AND an agonist step in null and wild-type neurons (SCI had already noted that the antagonist alone cannot separate tone from receptor reserve).
3. **Valproate's signature on the WWOX-loss glycolysis/lactate module is mostly an HDAC-inhibitor-class signature.** Among the 256 families of the held table, 5 of 7 HDAC-inhibitor families (vorinostat, panobinostat, belinostat, quisinostat, mocetinostat) and valproic acid all sit in the bottom 50 by opposition score (they move the module in the loss direction); the two that do not are givinostat (+1.22) and entinostat (+0.22). Hypergeometric tail 0.0037, **post hoc and descriptive**: the list was assembled after the ranks of every family named -stat had been printed and the bottom-50 cut was chosen after that, so the figure is optimistic and is not a test; it is also one table and one module (`i4_metabolic/i4_hdac_rank.py`, `hdac_rank.tsv`). So the one "coherent" valproate signature is probably not a seizure-target signal.
4. **P47T cortex and PFC (mRNA of a missense line) lean in the glycolytic direction.** Hk1 CTX +0.44 (FDR 0.031), Pkm CTX +0.34 (0.0093), Hk2 PFC +0.67 (0.004), Ldha CTX +0.64 (0.44), Slc2a3 +0.51, Slc16a3 (lactate exporter) +0.93 and +1.12 and +0.71 in CTX, HPC, PFC (FDR 0.14 to 0.34, 0.098 in PFC). Ldhb −0.16, Bnip3 and Pdk1 flat. The astrocyte-enriched genes of that region have a median shift of −0.08 (n 1,122), so a change in astrocyte share does not explain it (composition check, `composition_check.json`). Descriptive: n 5 v 5, FDR as the table gives it; mRNA is not flux.
5. **One surprising prediction that can fail.** Ketone bodies are themselves HDAC-inhibitor labels (ASSUMED, general background), as valproate is; if the HDAC route sets the module, **a ketone body should move the HK2/LDHA module in the loss direction in neurons while lowering glycolytic flux** (expression and flux separate). The diet and valproate would then be cousins by HDAC and *not* by anything the seizure axis uses. Test in the slice and culture arm below; both outcomes change what the diet class is allowed to claim in the matrix.

## D2 · Why it is non-obvious
The standard reading treats the diet as a seizure-axis class and valproate as its mechanistic cousin. The held data say the cousinship, if any, passes through HDAC inhibition, which acts on the module in the loss direction, while the diet is expected to lower glycolytic demand. Second, "lower adenosine brake" reads Adk and Adora1 as pointing the same way; arithmetic shows they point opposite ways on tone.

## D3 · Consequences across scales
* **Cell:** module transcripts and an energy readout (ATP/ADP, KATP current) in null and wild-type neurons, with a ketone body, an HDAC-inhibitor label, and a glycolysis-lowering arm. **Surprising prediction 1:** the ketone body moves the module in the loss direction (expression) and lowers lactate output (flux). **Prediction 2:** "more adenosine, less response": in null neurons an adenosine-sensor reads equal or higher tone while the A1 agonist-induced hyperpolarisation is smaller (receptor-limited), if Adora1 is really lower.
* **Slice:** burst rate depends on substrate in null slices and not in wild type (the added arm of the brief); the genotype-by-substrate interaction is the rejecting observation.
* **Animal:** burst burden or seizure latency under fasting or ketosis differs by genotype; the hippocampus (which does not burst) is where Adk and Adora1 fall in the pool: the receptor fall could be the compensation of I3, not a cause.
* **Patient:** response to the ketogenic diet by child, with variant domain and age; response to valproate by child; EEG change with ketosis. A hypothesis for IT's per-child re-extraction (I2), not a result.

## D4 · Minimal formal model
`i4_metabolic/i4_tone_arithmetic.py` (deterministic, 24 cells): tone = adenosine × receptor^r × effector^g with adenosine ∝ Adk^(−s); exponents are ASSUMED and stated; RNA is a proxy for capacity. It is a sign calculator, not a simulation. The dynamical version belongs in SCI's I1 model (J_a and tau_a are its adenosine parameters); the harness can scan them (see `sim_harness/`), and the sign result above says the scan must cross J_a downward AND upward.

## D6 · Intervention logic (labels only; no dose; BLOCK-1)
* Ketogenic diet (a clinical class, not a drug; BLOCK-1 flags: growth, lipid and metabolic effects in children; not assessed here); ketone esters (supplement label, not an approved antiseizure agent).
* KATP openers (diazoxide-type; approved for another indication; flags: hypotension, fluid retention, hyperglycaemia) acting on link 4; KATP blockers (sulfonylurea label) as the experimental counterpart: **aggravation counterpart** if KATP-open is the brake.
* Adenosine route: A1 agonists (research tools; cardiac and respiratory depression flags), adenosine kinase inhibitors (research tools; historically toxic: ASSUMED, to be re-checked); both would face a receptor-limited response if Adora1 is truly lower.
* HDAC-inhibitor class (valproate approved; others oncology; flags: haematological and hepatic) on link 1 in the loss direction: an aggravation hypothesis on the module, sign for seizures unknown.
* Module-opposing labels from the held table (metformin +0.87, carbamazepine +0.74, sirolimus +0.44 in O_E; metformin: lactic-acidosis flag, approved for another indication): they oppose the module; whether that matters for bursts is untested.

## D8 · Stronger / killed by
Stronger: a genotype-by-substrate interaction on burst rate; a replicated Adora1 fall with variance; ketone-body module direction as predicted. **Killed by (tests that could fail):** no genotype-by-substrate interaction with adequate power (kills the substrate route); antagonist and agonist responses equal in null and wild type (kills reduced-A1-brake); ketone body moving the module against the loss direction (kills the HDAC-cousin link only, not the substrate route).
