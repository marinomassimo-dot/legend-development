## Answer first
**NARROW (the pre-registered Reading B holds; the cap is supported as a WORDING-level limit on this source's own headline, not as a new discriminator).**
1. **The simple rule is not cleanly refuted by this source, and neither is its negation.** On spiking and burst frequency the median CBZ response FALLS in all three backgrounds (WT, GoF, LoF); what differs is size (visual estimates: spiking GoF about -60%, LoF about -9%; burst GoF about -75%, LoF about -37%) and the within-genotype star. Direction differs only for synchrony (GoF falls, LoF flat to slightly up) and for the network-burst count of the LoF line (4 of 16 organoids before, 10 of 16 after). The figure title "opposite responses" is therefore stronger than the rate panels.
2. **No between-group drug-response test is printed.** The printed comparisons are mutant against WT at baseline and each genotype before against after drug. "Significant in GoF, not in LoF" (spiking ** against ns) is the pattern the assignment names as not interpretable. Answer to "require a between-group comparison or say none exists": **none exists.**
3. **The unit is the organoid, one iPSC line per genotype.** Clone and independent-differentiation numbers are not stated anywhere in the letter or the supplement.
4. **Nothing separates sodium-channel action from the authors' synaptic explanation.** The letter proposes that CBZ facilitates excitatory transmission and cites one rodent hippocampal-slice paper; no perturbation in the organoids tests it. It is a RIVAL by citation only.
5. **Verdict on the question:** a baseline MEA abnormality that two genotypes share (above the matched control) carries no information, by itself, about the direction or size of a drug response; the weight for choosing a probe has to come from a printed between-genotype test of the paired change. That statement is largely carried already (RP-016 asks for a genotype-by-treatment term). What this source adds is small: a time-matched vehicle arm, readouts reported one by one, and a count of organoids with a paired-design test. Weak and optional.

## Checks asked for, answered as they change the conclusion
1. **Replication unit.** The n printed is organoids (8, 16, 16; four per group for RNA). Three iPSC lines: the E512K patient line (GoF), its CRISPR correction (isogenic control) and an indel line made in the patient line. How many clones of each line, and how many independent differentiations, are not said (the induction protocol is "referenced from our previous work"). A genotype effect is therefore confounded with line, and organoids from one differentiation are pseudoreplicates of it.
2. **Pairing and the tests.** The statistics section names the Mann-Whitney U test (unpaired), an unpaired t test and the chi-square test for "two groups"; no paired test is named although the heatmaps and the methods describe the same organoids before and after drug. An unpaired test on paired data loses power: "ns" for LoF with n 16 and wide spread (spiking range about 0.7 to 5.8 Hz) does not mean no change.
3. **The network-burst count.** 4 of 16 against 10 of 16. My computation from the printed counts as two independent groups: chi-square uncorrected p 0.033 (the printed star), with Yates correction p 0.075, Fisher exact two-sided p 0.073. If the same 16 organoids were counted twice, McNemar exact gives p 0.031 only if all four positive organoids stayed positive and none was lost (six gained, none lost); with one lost, p 0.070. The paper does not say which; the claim "paradoxical network bursts" rests on a count that is borderline under any reading, in one line, with no vehicle recording. GoF network-burst incidence is not reported at all, so "paradoxical" has no GoF comparator.
4. **Starting level.** LoF starts higher on spiking and bursting than GoF (medians about 2.3 against 1.5 Hz and 0.27 against 0.12 Hz) and with a wider spread; synchrony medians are similar (about 0.115 against 0.14). Baseline level could therefore bear on the size of the rate response but cannot explain the synchrony difference. WT sits near the floor on all three (a fall from 0.2 to 0.05 Hz, 0.03 to 0.005 Hz) and its synchrony star cannot be given a direction from the plot.
5. **Normalisation or suppression.** No background is normalised toward the matched control: GoF after CBZ (about 0.6 Hz) remains above WT before CBZ (about 0.2 Hz). The pattern is suppression in WT and GoF, a smaller and unsupported fall in LoF on the rates, and no fall on synchrony. Spiking, burst frequency and synchrony disagree for LoF, as the assignment anticipated.
6. **The competing explanation.** The letter does not print the word adenosine; its sentence is "CBZ has been reported to promote the excitatory synaptic current" with one citation, whose title names an A1 receptor antagonist action in rodent hippocampal CA1 slices (a different preparation, species and region). No A1 agonist or antagonist, no synaptic blocker and no second sodium-channel blocker is applied in the organoids. An A1 action would be present in the WT and GoF organoids too, where CBZ suppressed; the letter offers no reason why it should dominate only in LoF other than the transcript comparison below. Status: RIVAL by citation; sodium-channel action and the synaptic explanation are not separated.
7. **The transcript comparison (Fig 3).** It compares GoF against LoF only (n 4 and 4 organoids; unpaired t test on TPM for the boxplots; differential expression with a raw p below 0.05 and |log2 fold change| above 1 for the enrichment). The isogenic WT is not in it, so "synaptic genes are elevated in LoF" cannot be told from "reduced in GoF". The sodium-channel panel shows SCN2A lower in LoF than GoF by about a quarter (visual), SCN8A lower too, and SCN1A, SCN3A, SCN9A not different.
8. **What the LoF allele is.** The line carries a 17-bp deletion (c.1530_1546del, p.Lys511Argfs*5). Fig S2 shows plasmid Sanger traces (WT and mutant plasmid, not the iPSC line) and a single flat current trace from transfected HEK293T cells (100 pA, 10 ms scale; no current-voltage plot, no n, no WT trace beside it). The iPSC genotype itself is not shown and the letter does not say whether the indel sits on the E512K allele or on the other one. By codon arithmetic (my derivation, not printed) the deleted span includes the first base of codon 512, so on the E512K allele the line would be wild-type over null, and on the other allele it would be E512K over null; the two readings predict different things and the letter does not decide between them. The authors themselves note that this frameshift "specifically represents SCN2A haploinsufficiency".
9. **The clinical observations are not independent validation.** One E512K patient moved to CBZ with no further epileptiform activity (uncontrolled, n 1, after "recurrent seizures"); one N916S twin pair with seizures worsened on oxcarbazepine and good efficacy on levetiracetam (n 1 family). N916S is a graded loss of function in HEK293T cells (adult isoform: inactivation V1/2 shifted about -5 mV, recovery about 2.7 against 1.5 ms, activation voltage and slope and persistent current not different; neonatal isoform: recovery only), and it was not modelled in organoids. The organoid LoF allele and the clinical allele differ, as the assignment says.
10. **One background.** Three lines from one patient; no population statement is possible.

## One discriminating prediction (not executable here: per-organoid values are by request; no contact)
With the authors' per-organoid paired values, a between-genotype test of the within-organoid ratio (drug over its own baseline) will separate GoF from LoF on synchrony (ratio of the printed medians about 0.35 against about 1.1; the per-organoid ratios are what the test needs) but will NOT establish a different sign on spiking or burst frequency, where both genotypes fall; and a time-matched vehicle-only second recording in the LoF line will show a smaller change in the network-burst count than 4 to 10 of 16. Refuting observations: a non-significant between-group test on synchrony, or a vehicle arm that reproduces the rise in network bursts. If either holds, the "different response" reduces to a drift or a count and this source becomes a teaching example only.

## Hand-off condition for a WWOX comparison (not a recommendation)
If the human-organoid benchmark of RP-043 is ever exposed to an antiseizure probe: matched corrected model (the corrected control of the same line background) and a time-matched vehicle arm; replication unit stated as independent differentiations of at least two clones per genotype; prespecified readouts reported one by one (rate, burst, synchrony, network-burst incidence), each with its biological denominator; and a printed between-genotype test of the paired change. The baseline hyperexcitability of a WWOX-deficient organoid earns no weight on the choice of a probe by itself. The existing WWOX state is untouched: RP-043 still carries the week-16 WOREE-over-SCAR12 contrast as pooled-only, so there is no per-line response contrast to compare.
