A1.2 What does not hold or changes how a reader takes it

(a) PMID 20384718 "25 of 95, derived, about 26 percent" is wrong as a per-exposed rate.
Abstract (Europe PMC core record, Results): 107 children, 95 treated and 12 not; 81 "had MRI scans during, but not before" treatment, 25 abnormal (30.9); a separate group of 14 "scans before, during, and after" treatment, of whom 4 had abnormal signal during treatment. The 81 (no scan before) and the 14 (scan before) cannot overlap, both must lie inside the 95 treated (they received vigabatrin), and 81 + 14 = 95 exactly. So by the abstract's own counts every one of the 95 exposed children was imaged during treatment, and the finding appeared in 25 + 4 = 29 of 95 = 30.5 percent. 25/95 = 26.3 percent undercounts by dropping the 4 of 14. Consequence: the stated per-exposed range "from about 7 percent ... to about 26 percent" is not the extreme: its top is about 30.5 percent (PMID 20384718), not 26. (The 4 of 14 had baseline scans, so they are new findings.) The row B1-41 note already contains the 14-child subset but the card did not use it.

(b) "not shown to be independent (two come from one department)" understates the overlap.
- PMID 36601084 Introduction (last paragraphs): "In our previous study ... the incidence was 32.5% [19] ... we conducted this study in a larger cohort by incorporating IST patients into the previous study cohort." Reference 19 in its reference list is Xu Y, Wan L, He W et al., Epilepsia 2022; 63:120-129, PMID 34786694 (the 25 of 77, 32.5 percent report; also PLA General Hospital, Beijing, per PubMed affiliations; DOI 10.1111/epi.17121). So the 77 of PMID 34786694 are, by the later paper's own statement, the cohort that the 172 of PMID 36601084 extends. The two rates are nested, not just from one department.
- A second same-department pair exists: PMID 28230253 (UCLA, Hussain) and PMID 39570186 (UCLA, Sathe). Sathe Results states "There was no overlap with our previously reported vigabatrin cohort", so no patient overlap there, but one group and one centre, with the same clinicians (Discussion: "both cohorts were managed by the same clinicians").
- So four of the eleven reports come from two departments; the eight rates are at most seven independent cohorts. Whether 19054414 (ten sites, US and Canada) or 39641935 (multicentre registry) share patients with others is not stated in the abstracts or held text (NOT CHECKED).

(c) "eight rates ... lie between 22.1 and 44.7" omits a ninth rate that sits at the bottom edge.
Table row B1-39 (PMID 19054414, abstract): prevalence of prespecified MRI abnormalities 22 percent in vigabatrin-treated against 4 percent in naive infants (205 infants, 332 examinations, ten sites), no counts in the abstract. It is a rate among exposed infants imaged, and 22 percent is below the stated minimum 22.1 (rounding makes the comparison undecidable). The card's list is correct for rates with counts but a reader will take "eight" as the whole set. The same table also holds rows with no usable rate (B1-14, B1-15, B1-18..B1-20, B1-42) so "eight" is right only as "eight with counts in the source".

(d) "PMID 28230253: 6 of 40 asymptomatic imaged children" at "about 15 percent" is not like for like with the others.
Abstract (Hussain 2017, Results): 143 treated, 104 with detailed exposure data, 45 with at least one MRI during treatment; "among the limited subset of asymptomatic children who underwent MRI (n = 40), 6 exhibited VABAM"; separately "4 children with symptomatic VABAM among 104". The 4 symptomatic cases had the finding, so they were imaged: the 45 imaged are at least the 40 asymptomatic plus those 4 (40 + 4 = 44 <= 45). So the rate among all imaged children of that cohort is probably about 10 of 45 = 22.2 percent. That is derived from two sentences of an abstract (the full text is not held; it is not stated that the 4 lie inside the 45), but it means this set is not a low-rate set; the "about 15 percent" belongs to an asymptomatic subset only. Per exposed child with detailed data: 10/104 = 9.6; of 143 treated: 10/143 = 7.0 (same caveat). Sathe's own Discussion gives the prior study as "6/40 patients" aVABAM and "4/104 (3.8%)" sVABAM, consistent with this reading.

(e) PMID 35180571 "44 of 288 children seen with West syndrome, not all exposed or imaged" is a reading, not a finding of the abstract.
PubMed abstract (Reyes Valenzuela, Epilepsy Behav 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.yebeh.2022.108606): Methods "database of 288 patients with WS seen between 2014 and 2020. All patients who received VGB ... were evaluated"; Results "In 44 of 288 patients with WS receiving VGB, MRI findings compatible with VABAM were identified". The Results sentence can be read as 288 all receiving vigabatrin. The number exposed and the number imaged are not given. If 288 is the whole database, the rate per exposed child is higher than 15.3. The card's clause "not all exposed or imaged" should read "the abstract does not state how many of the 288 were exposed or imaged".

(f) "unexposed comparators exist" hides that they differ.
From the table and sources: 20384718 0 of 12 unexposed normal; 18783433 0 of 56 unexposed infantile-spasms children; 19054414 4 percent in naive infants; 39641935 VABAM-like changes in 7 of 52 (13.5 percent; 2 of 23 TSC without VGB and 5 of 29 non-TSC epilepsy without VGB; Results, "VABAM-like MRI abnormalities not VGB-related"). A reader of "unexposed comparators exist" will expect rates near zero; one of them is 13.5 percent. (Row B1-15, PMID 23789722: the ten "controls" were ICISS infants without a movement disorder, not shown to be unexposed, 3 of 10 had the changes; PubMed abstract only; not an unexposed comparator.)

(g) Side finding, not in the card text: Stevering's Discussion says Reyes Valenzuela reported symptoms "in 25 of 44 VGB-treated VABAM cases (56.8%)". The Reyes Valenzuela abstract (PubMed) says VABAM was asymptomatic in 25 of 44 (symptomatic 19 of 44, 43.2 percent, which is also how PMID 36601084's Discussion cites it). Do not carry Stevering's secondary statement.

(h) Stevering Introduction/Discussion give a literature range of 15 to 47 percent for earlier VABAM prevalence. Question: is the identification of the label's N=205 study with PMID 19054414 supported by numbers (205, 22 percent, 4 percent), or is it an inference to be labelled as one?

Answer: it is an inference from matching numbers, and it is strongly supported; the label cites no source.
- Label section 5.3 (whole text read): "In a retrospective epidemiologic study in infants with infantile spasms (N=205), the prevalence of MRI changes was 22% in vigabatrin-treated patients versus 4% in patients treated with other therapies." No citation, no authors, no age range ("infants" only). A search of the whole label XML for "205" finds only that sentence. Section 1.2: indication "monotherapy ... 1 month to 2 years of age" (the card's statement of the labelled indication age is correct). Section 8.4 gives no age for the 205 study. So "the label does not state the age range of the N=205 study" is correct.
- PMID 19054414 abstract (Wheless 2009, Europe PMC core record): "332 cranial MRIs from 205 infants (aged <or=24 months) with IS treated at 10 sites"; "prevalence of prespecified MRI abnormalities was significantly higher among vigabatrin-treated versus vigabatrin-naive subjects (22% vs. 4%; p < 0.001)"; retrospective review. Three numbers match exactly (205 infants with infantile spasms, 22 percent, 4 percent), the design (retrospective; infants with infantile spasms; treated against comparator) matches, and 24 months or less sits inside the labelled 1 month to 2 years.
- Differences to keep visible: the label's comparator is "patients treated with other therapies" where the paper says "vigabatrin-naive"; and the label's older-patient figure (blinded review of prospective clinical trials in refractory complex partial seizures, 3 years and older, N=656, against placebo) does not match the abstract's older arms (668 children aged 2 to 16 and adults, 2,074 images, retrospective, exposed against naive). The match is therefore for the infant arm only; the label's text is a regulatory summary, not a quotation of the paper.
- The card's phrase "the study these figures match" already labels it a match. Recommended wording (small change, makes the status explicit):
> infancy (infantile spasms; the labelled indication age is 1 month to 2 years; the label does not state the age range of the N=205 study and cites no source; the study its figures match (205 infants, 22 percent against 4 percent), PMID 19054414, gives infants aged 24 months or less, an identification inferred from the three matching numbers and not stated by the label; the label's separate older-patient figure, N=656 from placebo-controlled trials, does not match that paper's older arms)
