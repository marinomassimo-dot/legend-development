## Conflicts between sources
1. Apparent neonatal "leakiness": the older ratio-based literature (summarised in the Saunders review) reports higher brain and CSF ratios in young animals for small hydrophilic markers; the same review and the sucrose and mannitol primary papers attribute it to CSF and volume dilution, not to higher transfer rate (Preston 1995: same Kin, bigger initial volume). The P19 mouse antiepileptic table, in contrast, shows ratios about equal to adult. Different markers, ages (P4 vs P19), species and times.
2. Theophylline: neonatal ratios higher than adult (Habgood 1998), attributed to lower plasma protein binding in neonates and explicitly not to Pgp; levetiracetam (negligible binding) at P19 mouse is equal to adult. Both are consistent with a protein-binding explanation, which is not applicable to ethosuximide; neither addresses an unbound-drug ratio directly.
3. Pgp: protein first detected at P7 and rising to a plateau (Matsuoka), "uphill" through P56 (Omori), yet R123 brain transfer at P14 is 2.4 times the adult value (functional immaturity of the whole barrier, or a substrate-specific effect). The P19 mouse paper places the Pgp plateau around P28 (a statement in its Discussion, citing others).
4. Phenytoin: P19 mouse 0.54 against a cited adult 0.3 (different strains and doses; authors attribute to dose).
5. Brain-water measurement: Gottschalk gives 85.59 to 76.56% over 13 to 149 d in male Sprague-Dawley rat; the Wwox null water measurement (Cheng) is in mouse at P20 with a different dry-weight protocol; they are not comparable and the Cheng percentage axis is read from a figure legend, not a printed value.
