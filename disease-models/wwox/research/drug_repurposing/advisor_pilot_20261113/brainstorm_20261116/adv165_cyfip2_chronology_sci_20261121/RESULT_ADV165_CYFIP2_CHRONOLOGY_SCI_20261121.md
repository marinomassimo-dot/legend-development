## Answer first
**The pre-registered call is P-weak for hippocampal CA1 IBA1 and U for prelimbic mPFC, and it does NOT show that a glial marker precedes the onset of recurrence.** Two things are true at once and neither orders recurrence onset:
1. A SMALL microglial-intensity change exists early: CA1 IBA1 is +8 percent over age-matched WT at PWK 7 (97.5% interval +2 to +14 percent; 6 of 8 mutants above the highest WT), before the first age with documented behavioural recurrence (PWK 14). It is the same size at PWK 1 (+11 percent), which is the neonatal-spasm age, and it persists at PWK 28 (+16 percent). It is absent in mPFC, absent in GFAP, and absent in all 10 hippocampal microglial-activation transcripts at PWK 7 (qRT-PCR, 0 of 10 intervals above 1).
2. The LARGE, recognisably reactive glial state (IBA1 about x2, GFAP about x1.9 then x2.6, cytokine transcripts x1.4 to x8) first appears at PWK 14, when recurrence is already daily: all four mutants had events every day of a five-day recording, 9.4 per mouse-day at PWK 14 against 10.3 at PWK 28 (Fig 1B). PWK 14 is therefore not an onset; it is the first age anyone looked. Recurrence may have begun at any point between the PD30 EEG (stated negative in the paper, from an earlier study I did not read) and PWK 14, and PWK 7 lies inside that unobserved window.
So: no marker can be placed before recurrence, one small marker is placed before its first DOCUMENTATION and also before nothing in particular (it is present from the neonatal age), and the large glial response coincides with, or follows, documented daily events. The dataset is closed for chronology (reopening inputs below).

## Why the paper reports IBA1 "at PWK 14" and I report a PWK 7 difference (arithmetic, not their exact test)
The authors used a two-way ANOVA with Sidak correction. At PWK 14 the mutant IBA1 values are bimodal (per animal: 1.08, 3.25, 2.25, 1.14, 3.01, 1.12, 1.22, 3.16; SD 0.995) while the WT spread is 0.04 to 0.08. A pooled error term averages those variances; with equal-n approximation the pooled SD is about 0.36, the standard error of a PWK 7 difference about 0.18 and the PWK 7 contrast t about 0.45. So a small early shift is invisible to that analysis by construction, and visible to a per-age Welch. I did not run their ANOVA; this is a reason it could be missed, not a finding that it was. A per-age interval without correction for ages is the more liberal choice; at an age-multiplicity level (99.4 percent, post-hoc) the PWK 7 IBA1 interval still excludes zero but only just (+0.005 to +0.160), so I read it as a small, consistent shift and not as a strong one.

## The seizure side (what supports chronology at group level, and what cannot)
* Fig 1B (behavioural video, separate animals): PWK 14, four mutants, per-mouse five-day totals 36, 51, 46, 54, every mouse with events every day (daily minimum 2, 7, 6, 9); PWK 28, totals 49, 42 (died on day 5), 58, 56. Mean per mouse-day 9.35 against 10.25. The pre-registered statement that PWK 14 is the first DOCUMENTED, not the first, age is confirmed by the data: a daily rate of about 9 cannot have just begun. (The paper says events are more clustered per hour at PWK 28; the daily counts are similar.)
* Fig 1C (video-EEG at PWK 20, three survivors of about 80 percent perioperative loss): total seizures 120, 39, 113; tonic-clonic 112, 29, 105; myoclonic jerks 8, 10, 8; no events in three WT. The only electrographic evidence, survivor-selected, and not at an immunohistochemistry age.
* The seizure cohorts and the marker cohorts are different animals at every age; no marker can be tied to a seizure in the same animal. PWK 7 has no behavioural or EEG observation in this paper.
* PWK 14 within-genotype heterogeneity (post-hoc): mutant CA1 IBA1 is about 1.1 in four animals and 2.3 to 3.3 in four; GFAP 0.8 to 3.0; NeuN 0.09, 0.14, 0.11 in three animals. If the sheet row labels (Animal 2, 3, 5 in each block) identify the same mouse, which the sheet does not state, the neuronal loss and the strongest IBA1 rise sit in the same mice. Since all four behavioural-cohort mutants had daily events at PWK 14, a "reactive" subgroup of about half the animals at an age when every observed mutant seizes daily says the burst is not simply a function of ongoing events; the authors make the same point about sampling density (they ask for denser sampling around PWK 14).

## Four clocks, kept apart (DREAM)
Perturbation: germline, from conception (no onset inside the series). First seizure-like event: neonatal spasms at about PD8 (PWK 1), not re-observed here. First electrographic seizure: not located (EEG negative at PD30 per the paper; positive at PWK 20; none at 7, 14, 28). Behavioural recurrence: first documented PWK 14, already daily. The early CA1 readouts at PWK 1 and 7 sit after clock 2 and in the unobserved stretch before clock 4, so they cannot be assigned to either. The only connection kept: for an adult-recurrence precedent in any model, the series must carry seizure-free ascertainment at the same ages as the tissue.

## Limits and what would change this
Adjacent germline monogenic mouse model of a different disease; the allele acts mainly in neurons; two regions; four ages; markers are mean intensities, not cell counts; per-age Welch on n of 7 to 9; 32 comparisons were computed in total, so one marginal interval proves little (the P-weak call rests on one marker in one region). A PWK 7 interval including zero for IBA1 in a larger series would move the call to U; a seizure-status-tagged PWK 4 to 12 series would move the question to a real chronology. Nothing here is medical advice.

## Rejecting observation (stated before, restated)
Per-animal CA1 values with an IBA1 or GFAP difference at PWK 7 whose interval excludes zero would have overturned the text reading; IBA1 did, GFAP did not, and the early IBA1 shift is as large at PWK 1, so it is a genotype-associated mild shift, not a recurrence precursor.
