## Answer first
1. **ADV's reading of the legend is partly wrong, and the figure and the main text both say so.** The lanes marked "Dead" (panels A to D: the last four lanes, one HD and three LD) **do carry a blot and a printed value**; the main text says "mice from either treatment group that failed to survive exhibited reduced WWOX expression" (Figures S5 A to D). So the source holds a descriptive contrast of survivors against non-survivors, not survivors only. A floor under survival is still not computable: the sampling age of the non-survivors is not stated, n = 4 against 4, one blot per region.
2. **The quantification is printed numbers under each lane, not bars** (panels A to G and J are raster; the page has no text layer for them). Only panel K is a bar chart, and its geometry could be measured. The blind delegate and I agree on all 80 printed numbers and on the Dead-bar membership of every lane (one small-print alternative, 0.09 or 0.08, immaterial).
3. **Lowest value in a surviving mouse, by region (upper-bound statement, survivors only: a value seen in a mouse alive at the sampling age, not a threshold needed for survival).**

4. **Verdict on ADV's two observations, region by region (they are not exclusive).** R1 (a survivor at or below about 10 percent in a region at P90 or later) is **met in the cerebellum only**: two LD lanes at P90 (0.09 and 0.1, against a knockout lane at 0.02 on the same blot, so resolved from the null there) and one HD lane at P180 (0.1, **not resolved from the knockout lane of the same blot, which also reads 0.1**, rule R4). R2 (no survivor below about 50 percent in cortex and midbrain) is **met**: lowest cortex 2.0 times WT; lowest midbrain 0.9 printed, 0.56 against the WT mean, a margin that depends on which WT lane is the denominator. So a roughly 6 percent restoration is **below the lowest value in a survivor in every region, by a factor of about 1.5 in cerebellum (0.09 against 0.06), about 9 to 15 in midbrain, about 33 in cortex**; it is **not excluded as incompatible with survival only if the survival-relevant region is one the neuronal promoter reaches weakly (here the cerebellum), and the source does not say which region matters**. That is the premise that stays open.
5. **Survivor against non-survivor, per region, descriptive (post-view rule R5; n = 4 against 4, P90 blot, sampling age of non-survivors not stated; no test).** Cortex: non-survivors 0.5 to 1.5, survivors 2.0 to 2.6, **ranges do not overlap**. Midbrain: non-survivors 0.1 to 0.6, survivors 0.9 to 5.2, **no overlap**. Hippocampus: non-survivors 2.5 to 12.9, survivors 1.4 to 28.2, overlap. Cerebellum: non-survivors 0.06 to 0.2, survivors 0.09 to 0.6, overlap. A reading that the data allow and do not establish: in cortex and midbrain, lanes of mice that died were lower than every lane of a survivor, while the cerebellum and hippocampus do not separate them; the main-text sentence ("reinforcing the link between effective protein restoration and survival") says the same thing in words. Confounds: the non-survivors may have been sampled at death, younger than P90 (stated nowhere), loading (HSP90) differs by lane, and dose and fraction are not separable here (three of four non-survivors are LD, and a dose effect on survival is the paper's own main result).
6. **Later ages (HD, survivors by construction).** P180 (n = 5 lanes): cortex 4.7 to 10.6, midbrain 1.3 to 2.9, cerebellum 0.1 to 0.5 (0.1, 0.3, 0.4, 0.5, 0.2). P300 single mouse (panel J): cortex 8.2, hippocampus 10.7, midbrain 5.6, cerebellum 1.4. Panel K (P240 and P300, n = 3, **bar geometry calibrated to the printed axis ticks: tick 0 at 489.45 and tick 20 at 571.9 page units, 4.123 units per axis unit**): mean cortex 9.5, hippocampus 7.2, midbrain 5.0, cerebellum 2.15; the upper whisker puts one SD at about 5.9, 3.3, 2.0 and 2.4. The wild-type bar reads 0.85 against its nominal 1, so a calibration residual of about 15 percent is carried. The cerebellum SD exceeds its mean, so at least one of the three animals is far below the mean, and the per-animal minimum at P240 and P300 is **not recoverable** from a mean and an SD with n = 3.

## Cross-checks and their limits
* **Blind transcription** (a different delegate, no legend, values or question; model claude-sonnet-5-5; I stated effort medium in the brief but the Agent tool has no effort parameter and the census read back **high**): 8 panels, 80 lanes, all counts matched, no unreadable number, and every value agrees with mine.
* **Densitometry (mine, not blind; panels A to D only):** per-lane band over HSP90, normalised to the first WT lane. Rank agreement with the printed numbers is high (Spearman 0.91 to 0.99), but the method compresses low values (the knockout lane reads 0.28 to 0.83 against a printed 0.02 to 0.1, a background floor), so it confirms **order** and cannot confirm a 10 percent value. It was not run for E to G and J.
* Conflicting statements inside the source, not resolved: the main text says "LD-treated mice did not survive to P90" while Figure S5 shows two LD lanes outside the Dead bar at P90; any LD "survivor" row carries this, and the HD lanes (P90 two lanes, P180 five, P300 three) are the cleaner survivors.

## What this cannot say
Survivors are a selected set; one blot per region and age with no replicate for A to G; a western-blot ratio is not molecules per cell, and WT lanes on one blot differ two to three times (1 and 2.2 in midbrain, 0.7 to 1.7 in P180 cortex); regions mix cell types; one laboratory; no function readout attaches to a lane; the roughly 6 percent of the suppressor tRNA is an enzyme-activity percentage in another disease, so this is a bracket, not a match; no statement here is about any person.

## Which comparison it could change
* **RP-037 suppressor-tRNA row (class E):** "open premise: fraction needed" becomes "bracketed: the benchmark's lowest survivor values are about 10 percent in cerebellum and above 56 percent in midbrain and 200 percent in cortex; about 6 percent is below every one; whether a cerebellar-level restoration is enough, or a cortex- or midbrain-level one is needed, the source does not say" (IT: the class-E row wording, and the gene-addition row: "floor unknown" becomes "observed upper bound per region, survivors only, and non-survivors lower in cortex and midbrain").
* **Gene addition:** the expression-level premise (8.2, 10.7, 5.6, 1.4 times WT in one P300 mouse) is unchanged; the P90 survivor range gives a second reading of the same lever: regions that carry the dose-response (cortex, midbrain, hippocampus) sit at 0.9 to 28 times WT in survivors, the cerebellum at 0.09 to 2.15 (panel K mean).
* **Design brief:** a stated expression range for any partial-restoration arm: the lower end observed in survivors is regional, and a cortex or midbrain readout is the one that separated survivors from non-survivors here.
