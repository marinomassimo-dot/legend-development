## Answer first
1. **The held originals do not show whether hypoglycaemia drives excitability, seizures and death or only accompanies them, and they cannot.** No held original manipulates glucose; the rescues manipulate neuronal WWOX, which normalises glucose, growth, seizures and survival together, so they support a **central origin of the hypoglycaemia**, not glucose as the cause of the seizures.
2. **Timeline gaps (all preparations):** no glucose before P10; the neuron-targeted deletion seizes from **P9** and its first glucose value, if one existed, would come at P10 or later; glucose in the null is about 95 against 130 mg/dl at P10 to P11, about 58 (and about 40 in one panel) at P14, about 57 at P19; ECoG starts at P14, so earlier onset is unobservable; **no glucose at the time of any seizure or ECoG epoch; no same-animal pairs**.
3. **Dissociations the originals contain.** *Death with near-normal glucose:* the low-dose neuronal vector (glucose not different from wild type at P30, all dead by about P75); the cytomegalovirus-promoter vector (glucose normal **at P14 only**, survival about 80 percent to P30 then 40 percent by about P32 to 35, glucose later unknown); the 4E10 no-WPRE group (n = 3). All rest on survivors and small n. *Seizures without documented hypoglycaemia:* the P47T adults (blood chemistry "comparable", glucose not itemised), the neuron-targeted deletion (glucose never measured), the lde rat (serum constituents normal at 28 days, secondary, quoted by the dossier and cited by that paper to an earlier one whose abstract reports raised urea and creatinine). *Low glucose without seizures:* none documented (the MBP-promoter vector: glucose about 50 and all dead by about P15, seizures not assessed).
4. **The one-line summary of my brief's framing:** "glucose normalises while animals die" for the cytomegalovirus vector is only half-supported (glucose was measured at P14 only).
5. **Peripheral arm:** the skeletal-muscle conditional knockout gives the **opposite** glucose sign in adults (fasting hyperglycaemia, impaired glucose tolerance, insulin resistance, higher insulin, higher lactate, lower muscle FDG uptake, lower dark-phase food intake; adult, text-level, values in plots), so peripheral WWOX loss does not explain the null pup's hypoglycaemia and says nothing about seizures or survival; the 2014 report (null cells and tissues) gives lower insulin, higher lactate and low glucose at an unstated age.
6. **Stop rule (as written by ADV): "no age-resolved glucose datum beyond P10 and no link to seizure timing".** Age-resolved glucose beyond P10 **exists** (P10 to P19, one report), the link to seizure timing does **not**: the rule's conjunction is not met, so the axis is **not closed**; but nothing further is readable from the held originals: what remains is an access item (the rat original) and laboratory measurements.

## Measurements that would separate drive from accompaniment (hypothesis level; no dose)
1. **Blood glucose in the seizure-and-death lines** (neuron-targeted and neural-progenitor-targeted pups, P7 to P14), because those lines seize and die and no glucose was ever reported for them: hypoglycaemic would put the crisis downstream of neuronal WWOX loss; normoglycaemic would show seizures and death without it.
2. **Glucose at the time of seizures** (same-animal pairs with video-EEG or ECoG from P9, with the sampling burden stated): does glucose fall before or after the first seizure.
3. **A glucose-restoration arm without WWOX restoration** (feeding or glucose supply in null pups) read for seizure frequency, ECoG and survival: the only design that tests sufficiency; its risks to pups are the laboratory's to state.
4. **Ketone, lactate and insulin by age, and milk intake or stomach filling** (none measured in any held original) to separate fuel availability from intake.

## What it does to the ketogenic-diet comparison (U15, ranked WEAK, group G3; card E04)
The card's own evidence is clinical and tiny (tried in three patients in one cohort and continued in one; suspended in a null-genotype report) with no metabolic readout in any WWOX-deficient neural system. On the originals: **its rationale neither gains nor loses decisively.** It would gain plausibility (an alternative brain fuel) only if hypoglycaemia or glucose use contributes to excitability, which nothing shows; it would reduce to a generic anticonvulsant action if seizures occur with normal glucose (the P47T adults and the neuron-targeted line suggest they may). A dietary intervention that lowers blood glucose acts on pups that are already hypoglycaemic from about P10 and die: whether it helps or harms is unknown (a hypothesis-level caution, not advice). **No change of tier by me; the metabolic lever does not become a comparison on this evidence.**
