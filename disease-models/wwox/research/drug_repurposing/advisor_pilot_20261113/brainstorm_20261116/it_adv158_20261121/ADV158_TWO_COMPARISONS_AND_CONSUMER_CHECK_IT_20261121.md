## Comparison 1: Kv7 opener against ethosuximide for the second network-level slot of the bridge pilot (OPP-05 against OPP-03, conservative portfolio)
The decision at stake (bridge slice panel, one page of 2026-11-20): which class, if any, earns a place beside ethosuximide, ordered "by the ratio of the effective concentration to the top of the held exposure band; classes with no band are ordered by potency only and said to be so".

Context-specific rows, kept separate (source values from the held sheets; INFERENCE marked):

|---|---|---|

**Reachable in-silico input.** The arithmetic of a margin from held numbers. For Kv7 the harm-linked exposure is bounded, not measured: if the repeated-dose exposure lay between the 15 and the 30 mg/kg single-dose levels, the ratio of harm-linked to effective brain concentration is 4.27 / 3.58 = 1.2 up to 6.64 / 2.23 = 3.0 (INFERENCE; adult mouse effective values against rat pup exposures: species and age differ). For ethosuximide **no harm-side exposure exists to put in the ratio**, so no band top and no margin can be formed.
**Search done for the symmetry** (two routes, abstract level): PubMed title and abstract, ethosuximide with apoptosis, neurodegeneration or neurotoxicity and neonatal, immature or developing: 3 hits, none on a developing brain (one adult-rat study in which ethosuximide prevented NMDA-antagonist neurotoxicity, PMID 12192617); Bittigau 2002 (PMID 12417760, DOI 10.1073/pnas.222550499, abstract) names phenytoin, phenobarbital, diazepam, clonazepam, vigabatrin and valproate as causing apoptotic neurodegeneration in the developing rat brain and does not name ethosuximide in either direction; its full text was not reached (PMC sent none for PMC137548). That is a search result, not a statement that ethosuximide is harmless to a developing brain. Two first-pass PubMed outputs I read for completeness were off-target and are not used.
**Simple baseline.** The page's own rule: Kv7 carries the penalty "no margin measured in juveniles"; ethosuximide stays the bridge lead because it is the observed in vivo effect. **What this shows:** the ordering between the two rests on an asymmetry of what was measured (harm measured for Kv7, not searched deeply for ethosuximide), not on a margin; the rule gives a band, and so a ratio, only to the class whose harm was measured.
**Result that would reverse the preference.** Any one of: (a) a juvenile repeated-dose Kv7 exposure measured at or below the effective range with no apoptosis, or an apoptosis-free exposure at least about twice the effective brain value (the penalty lifts; Kv7 then moves ahead on the network readout as the page already says); (b) a developing-brain harm datum for ethosuximide at exposures inside 0.3 to 1 mM (the lead gets a band, and the order is then by the ratio); (c) the bridge slice panel result itself (Kv7 acting only between 10 and 30 microM: not exposure-compatible).

## Comparison 2: lithium: general, GSK-3-specific or a regimen effect, with a second GSK-3 class (OPP-04, ordering row 5, ambitious portfolio)
**Reachable in-silico input.** Arithmetic on the held vector values of Cheng 2020 (mean stage over minutes 1 to 60, vehicle against lithium, N printed): wild type 0.27 against 0.12 (12 and 8), heterozygote 0.42 against 0.16 (12 and 12), knockout 1.20 against 0.22 (6 and 7). Fraction remaining: 0.44, 0.38, 0.18 (the proportional gap 54, 62, 82 percent, SCI). The interaction ratio, knockout fraction over wild-type fraction, is **0.41 as a point value** (INFERENCE), inside the class "at most 0.7" of the frozen in vivo priors file.
**Why it does not move the preference.** SCI's bracket gives sigma_log 0.54 to 0.94 for the knockout and 1.14 to 1.30 for the wild-type vehicle arms, and about 1.45 for the one treated wild-type arm it prints (CV 2.7 to 3.4). The standard error of the log interaction ratio from the two vehicle arms alone is about 0.40 to 0.54; with the one printed treated arm it is about 0.65 to 0.74, so the 95 percent interval is about 0.19 to 0.90 (vehicle arms only) widening to about 0.10 to 1.75: it contains 1 as soon as one treated arm is counted, and the other three treated arms are not bracketed (INFERENCE, my arithmetic on SCI's table). A ratio on four floor-bound arms of one global-null series is **a grouped functional result, not a genotype effect**; the held reading "general effect in all three genotypes" stands. For the second class (five GSK-3 chemotypes, HE batch 9): no measured brain or plasma exposure for any of the five, alpha-beta separation at most 2.5-fold, selectivity NOT_ASSESSED, so no input exists that selects one.
**Simple baseline.** General effect: the same proportional gap in every genotype (ratio 1). **Result that would reverse it:** a pup series with a stimulus that lifts the wild-type arm off the floor and per-animal summaries giving an interaction ratio at most 0.7 with its interval excluding 1 at the planned n (about 180 to 320 per cell, held); or, for GSK-3 specificity, a second GSK-3 class with a measured juvenile brain level that reproduces the lithium effect while a non-GSK-3 comparator does not. Neither is available now; the pup brief stays under its stop rule.

**(a) Why the exposure bridge fails.** Five separate causes, any one enough: one arm has no juvenile value at all; the two values that exist come from different species and ages; the matrix is total tissue (or unsettled) and free concentration is nowhere measured; the harm exposure was never measured at the regimen that caused harm (single dose measured, repeated dose not); and no Wwox pup pharmacokinetics exist (null pups are reported hypoglycaemic from week 2, so even a wild-type juvenile value may not transfer; INFERENCE).
**(b) The adjacent comparison it leaves available.** Concentration as an experimental variable instead of an inference: the bridge slice panel fixes 0.3 to 1 mM for ethosuximide and a stated range for the Kv7 opener in the same null slices, so effective concentration is read against the held band without any cross-species exposure bridge; and, within one agent and one age, the pup stage-2 series reads each agent's effect against its own exposure measured in the same pups (the brief's exposure arm). Both need laboratory work; neither is an in-silico step.
**(c) The input that would make the question worth reopening.** Any one of: a juvenile mouse total and free brain concentration at the knockout test window (P12 to P17) at the regimen used by Cheng 2020 for ethosuximide and for lithium, measured in the same pups; a juvenile mouse repeated-dose brain level for a Kv7 opener with its apoptosis readout in the same animals; or a published developmental-toxicity study of ethosuximide that reports exposure. With any one of these on both sides of a contrast, the margin arithmetic above becomes a comparison and the symmetric audit stops being a table of gaps.

## Lessons to keep (transferable, with boundary)
* **A "taken from sheet" row inherits the sheet's later dated lines only if they are read.** My ADV-156 Kv7 row quoted a value the sheet had since qualified three times. Application: before a state table row leaves a sheet, read the sheet's dated lines; boundary: a verbal summary of a sheet is not a read.
* **A band exists only where harm was measured.** A rule that orders by effective-to-band ratio gives the penalty to the agent with measured harm and a pass to the agent with none. Application: ask which agent's harm was searched before reading an ordering as a margin; boundary: only for orderings that use a harm-side quantity.
* **"Link missing" measures what was searched** (the levetiracetam lesson) holds on the harm side too: "not found by two routes" is the strongest sentence these records allow, and full text unreached is a route result, not a negative.
