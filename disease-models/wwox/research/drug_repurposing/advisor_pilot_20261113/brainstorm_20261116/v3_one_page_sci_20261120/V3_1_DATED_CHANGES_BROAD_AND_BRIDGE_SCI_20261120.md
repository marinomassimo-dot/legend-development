## 2. Broad page, v3.1

**2.1 The rule that selects "the arms that moved on Track 1" (fixed before data).** Primary lineage readout: the **per-animal mature-stage score from sections** (the readout whose SD, 0.32 log2 units, sized the page; nuclei are secondary). Primary contrast: each treated knockout arm against the knockout vehicle arm on that score, direction toward the wild-type vehicle level, **Holm over the three treated arms**. An arm **moved** if either (a) its point estimate recovers at least half of the knockout-vehicle against wild-type-vehicle deficit, or (b) its Holm-adjusted two-sided p is below 0.05. The rule is deliberately generous: Stage 1 is a signal detector, a false "moved" costs only a Stage 2 cohort, a false "did not move" at confirmed exposure drops an arm. The laboratory may change the threshold, but only before the first animal; the rule is written into the protocol with the Track 1 allocation list. All other lineage readouts, MBP and the wild-type arms are secondary and labelled so; this is also the multiplicity statement (three treated arms, one primary readout, one primary contrast each).

**2.2 Stage 2 is a new cohort.** Tracks 2, 3 and 4 need animals that have not been through Track 1 handling (a Track 1 animal is frozen or immersion-fixed). Stage 2 therefore has **its own litters, its own vehicle and wild-type arms, and its own n**. The page does **not** derive that n: no held SD exists for the g-ratio or for the callosal compound action potential in this line (the held EM values are n = 3 per genotype, structural, from the neuronal-deletion paper), so the per-arm n for Tracks 2 and 3 is **set by the laboratory from a stated SD before Stage 1 ends, and the page says it is open**; until it is set, no Stage 2 animal count and no total is quoted. **Litter supply, stated as an assumption** (one knockout in four from a heterozygote-by-heterozygote cross, a litter of about 7; a neuronal-deletion Cre breeding scheme may yield differently, so this is **not a held value**): the 48 knockouts of Stage 1 (4 arms of 12) need about 190 pups **born**, not the "about 100" the page lists, which counts pups used (48 knockouts, 24 wild-type, 16 satellite, extras); Stage 2 adds its own births and a delay of at least one more breeding round, because the arms to open are known only after Track 1 is read.

**2.3 Wording of the "no harm" cell.** The row reads **"Track 1 positive, no harm VISIBLE at Stage 1 (body weight, counts)"**. Conduction and myelin-thickness harm are visible only at Stage 2 (Tracks 2 and 3), as the page already says; the table now says it too.

**2.4 The n and its SD.** The SD behind n comes from 7 animals (4 control and 3 knockout; 5 degrees of freedom). The 95 percent one-sided upper bound of an SD at 5 degrees of freedom is **about 2.1 times** the observed value, so n grows about 4.4-fold (section 4.2): rescue of half the deficit on the mature score needs **about 10 per arm at the observed SD and about 37 per arm at its upper bound**; on the stage score about 16 and 61. **The page's "12 per arm" is therefore stated as "the quick power for a large rescue", and Track 1 as a signal detector**, not as a test that closes an arm at the half-deficit level: a Track 1 negative at confirmed exposure excludes a large rescue only, and a smaller rescue is **not excluded** (the row "Negative at confirmed exposure" in the v3 table is amended in 2.7). The page's "a quarter-deficit rescue is not detectable" stands and is stronger at the upper bound (about 142 per arm).

**2.5 Handling (IT SHOULD items, all adopted).** (a) **When is genotype known:** by toe or tail sampling at P3 with a same-day result is the assumption; randomisation within genotype and litter needs the result **before the first dose**. If it is not available, pups are randomised by litter before genotype, the arm sizes become random, and the knockout supply is oversampled accordingly (the arm sizes are reported). (b) **The interval from the last dose, and from the last separation or gavage, to sacrifice is identical across arms and genotypes** (set before data; it matters for the phospho-tau panel, where handling can move the phosphorylation state). (c) A **small undosed handling-control group** (a few littermates per litter, no separation and no gavage) is added **if animals allow**, taken from the surplus pups; it is not an arm and enters no primary contrast.

**2.6 The line is a neuronal deletion: engagement control (ii) and the unknown row.** The page names Wwox neuronal-deletion pups. Therefore the **ex vivo OPCs from these pups are Wwox-intact**; the lineage deficit in this line is non-cell-autonomous or compositional. Control (ii) (ex vivo OPC differentiation in primary OPCs from knockout and control littermates) is **a drug-responsiveness control: it shows that OPCs of this background respond to the agent in vitro, not whether Wwox-deficient OPCs do**. The unknown row is reworded: **"whether OPC-intrinsic Wwox loss changes the response to M1 antagonism (not testable in this line; this design does not address it)"**. A line deleting Wwox in the lineage itself would be needed; none is proposed here.

## 3. Bridge page, v3.1

**3.1 Two kinds of null (ADV's instruction); the asymmetry of IT's point 3(a) revised.** (a) A **mechanistic null** (the target is not engaged, or is engaged without effect) is **not reachable while the potency is disputed**: recombinant human Cav3 persistent T-current IC50 0.6 mM (one origin) against a CACNA1I FLIPR value above 5 mM (**one ChEMBL transcription of an "inactive up to the highest tested" entry**), and native-tissue studies disagree. If pup exposure cannot pass the 5 mM-class bound within tolerance, the mechanistic null **never fires**, and by that route ethosuximide could be confirmed or left unresolved but not shown not to act on the target. (b) A **pragmatic null is reachable and is what a bridge objective needs: no benefit in the knockout pups at the highest tolerated exposure, measured, at or above the adult brain exposure at which ethosuximide is effective in the standard provoked-seizure assay** (anchor: total brain 134.9 ug/g, about 0.96 mM, at 72.3 mg/kg i.p., adult mouse, 45 min; the homogenate dilution correction is not stated, up to about 2.9 mM). **It demotes ethosuximide as a bridge lead in this model** (a bridge candidate must show an effect at tolerable exposure) **and claims nothing about the target.** IT's statement "never demoted" holds for the mechanistic null only. **"Negative without exposure" stays the uninterpretable class.**
*Definitions fixed before data.* **Highest tolerated exposure** at an age = the highest brain exposure, measured in satellite pups at the time of the provocation, at the highest dose that passes the motor and ataxia pre-test, keeps the righting reflex and does not lose weight against vehicle. **Pragmatic null** = at that exposure, at that age, with the assay panel valid, the 95 percent interval of R (knockout treated over knockout vehicle, within the genotype-by-treatment frame of v3) has its **lower end above 0.7**: a benefit as large as the effect the design is sized for is excluded. **This is a decision of this page, not of ADV or IT: the stricter band "interval inside 0.85 to 1.15" of v3 would need far more animals** (section 4.3: about 115 per arm for the expected interval to just fit and about 250 for 80 percent probability at log SD 0.54; about 350 and 780 at 0.94) and is kept only as the name of the "null band"; ADV and IT are asked to confirm the 0.7 exclusion or choose another margin.

**3.2 Reachability of the exposure (IT 3(b), 3(c)): "not reachable within tolerance" is not established.** The v3 sentence said a pup exposure above 5 mM may not be reachable. What is held: adult total brain 0.96 mM (to about 2.9 mM with the unstated homogenate correction) at 72.3 mg/kg i.p.; the Cheng regimen is 150 mg/kg i.p., 2.07 times that dose; **linear scaling (assumed; no source reached) gives about 2 to 6 mM at the Cheng dose, which straddles 5 mM.** Neonatal clearance is **from memory only (no source reached)**, so a pup ceiling may be higher or lower. The pilot's exposure series decides, and the page does not pre-judge it. **Wherever the rule uses the 5 mM bound, it carries the label "one ChEMBL transcription"** (in 3.1 and in the table below).

**3.4 The pilot decides "set n or stop" on the upper bound of the SD (IT 3(d)).** The pilot has **8 pups per genotype and age** (IT's figure; the laboratory may field more). The 95 percent one-sided upper bound of an SD from 8 animals (7 degrees of freedom) is **about 1.8 times** the observed value. The rule: n is computed from the **upper bound**, and "set n" is chosen only if that n is within what the laboratory can field; otherwise **stop**, and the recorded result is that this readout cannot resolve the effect. A lucky pilot SD of 0.3 has an upper bound of about 0.54, which sizes the interaction at about 72 per cell (288 animals), not 22 (88): the rule prevents a lucky pilot from turning a stop into a study that is not what it was sized for (section 4.3).

**3.5 Floor and ceiling (IT 3(e)).** A stimulus chosen to lift wild type off the floor may push the knockout to its ceiling and compress R toward 1. The pilot must therefore **show the knockout is off its ceiling at the same stimulus that lifts wild type off the floor**. **Choice stated: one shared stimulus per age**, because the genotype-by-treatment contrast and the shared-session design depend on it. If no shared stimulus gives both genotypes a non-floor, non-ceiling response, **a per-genotype stimulus is not accepted for the interaction**: the interaction is not attempted, each genotype is read as a simple effect on its own stimulus, and the result is a provoked-seizure lead in the knockout with specificity deferred to INV-04.

**3.6 Tie-break wording (IT 3(g)).** The v3 clause "ethosuximide stays the lead by its prior position" is replaced by: **if the margins also tie within a factor of 1.5, ethosuximide stays the lead because it has an approved label (absence seizures; a label statement for people of three years and older, not a statement about mice) and paediatric clinical data, while no gaboxadol label was found; gaboxadol stays the rival; the stated decision remains "no efficacy preference; choose on specificity, harm and label".** The reason is the label and the paediatric record, not incumbency.
