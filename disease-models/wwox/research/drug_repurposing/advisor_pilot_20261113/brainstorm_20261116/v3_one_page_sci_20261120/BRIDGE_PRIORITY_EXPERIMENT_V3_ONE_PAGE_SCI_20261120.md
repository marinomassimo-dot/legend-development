# Bridge objective, priority experiment v3: one page (SCI successor, 2026-11-20; consolidates v1 (`UNIT_C_BRIDGE_COMPARATIVE_CASE_...`), v2 section B, v2.1 point 2 and the pup brief `PUP_BRIEF_V2_CONSOLIDATED.md` sections 2 and 3 (stages 1 and 2), which stay as history and are not edited; where this page differs it supersedes them; hypothesis level; doses and concentrations are source research values, not recommendations; no dose, no schedule, no recommendation for or against any use of any agent in any person; reference genotype class only; not medical advice)

**Read this page in one sitting.** It is a design for a laboratory, not a result; nothing here is started. The aim is the bridge objective: seizure burden with a conservative benefit and risk aim. The experiment is a **provoked-seizure** test; it says what a drug does to a pentylenetetrazol seizure at an age, not what it would do to the spontaneous-seizure phenotype.

## 2. The experiment
**Stage 1: pilot (first, decides everything else).** Knockout and wild-type vehicle pups at the candidate ages; the laboratory's own published standard PTZ assay; fixes the stimulus that gives wild-type pups a non-floor response, the window (60 minutes as in Cheng 2020, or another stated before data), and the observed SD of log S. **Decision it yields: "set n or stop".** If the observed SD makes the simple effect of ratio 0.7 need more animals than the laboratory can field, the study stops and the recorded result is that ethosuximide's effect in the knockout cannot be resolved by this readout.

**Stage 2: ethosuximide with the dose-exposure series and the age series.**
- **Ages.** The null is tested at **two ages, P14 and P18** (P14 is the first age with a documented electrographic abnormality; P18 is before the apoptosis and vermis findings dated about P19 to P20); the laboratory may substitute ages from its own colony before data, two stay. The wild-type series adds **P25** (inside the window to about P28). **The age floor is claimed only from the wild-type series**: the page says where the wild-type response appears, not where the knockout effect begins. The chosen ages are stated beside every effect.
- **Arms and design frame (unchanged from the pup brief).** All three genotypes in the same session at the same age; shared vehicle; block randomisation within genotype and litter; syringes coded; scoring blind from coded video by a scorer not present; litter as cluster; a motor and ataxia pre-test per arm; an assay-sensitivity panel in wild-type pups at every age (a flat-profile and a rising-profile agent; a failed panel is an assay failure, not a negative); a status stopping rule written before the first animal.
- **Dose-exposure series.** Ethosuximide at **three exposures chosen by the laboratory around the Cheng 2020 regimen**, with **total brain and serum measured at the PTZ time in satellite pups at each age** and the dose-exposure relation fitted (two exposures suffice at an age only if the lower one is effective in the panel agent). The measured brain level, not the dose, defines "an effect of the drug".
- **Genotype-by-treatment test.** The contrast R(knockout) over R(wild type) with R = mean S treated over mean S vehicle on the log scale, litter bootstrap; an effect is R at most 0.7 with an interval excluding 1; a null is an interval inside 0.85 to 1.15; "no genotype specificity" is an interaction ratio of 0.7 to 1.43. **Reachability:** a simple effect of 0.7 needs about 36 to 109 animals per arm at the SD above; the interaction needs about 180 to 320 per cell, four cells, **about 700 to 1,300 animals and 350 to 630 knockouts for one drug against vehicle**, so **the genotype-by-treatment test is not attemptable with the full-hour area summary unless the pilot finds a log SD of about 0.3 or less** (the binary generalised-seizure outcome needs about 20 to 38 per arm for a fall from 50 percent to 10 or 20 percent, and is the named alternative). Otherwise the main run is read as **a provoked-seizure lead in the knockout** and specificity is deferred to the INV-04 design.
- **The potency that makes the null interpretable.** A null is interpretable only with measured exposure and engagement: a null at brain exposure above the 5 mM-class bound is a null under both potency readings; **between 0.6 and 5 mM it is "uninterpretable between the two potencies"**; below 0.6 mM it is a dose null. **A limit to state plainly:** adult total brain was about 1 to 3 mM at about half the Cheng dose, so a pup exposure above 5 mM may not be reachable within tolerance; if the pilot shows it is not, **the null class is unreachable and only a positive (or an unresolved) result is informative**. A selective Cav3 blocker arm beside ethosuximide needs its own engagement control (a published Cav3-dependent slice readout from the same line, or a selectivity-confirmed exposure), otherwise its null is not read.
- **The gaboxadol rule (fixed before data).** Arm: gaboxadol at two doses below the sedating dose of the laboratory's own pilot per age (the source's 3.5, 5 and 10 mg/kg are a P8 to P9 mouse series, not a protocol), same vehicle and readout. For each agent and age, R as above at the highest dose that passes the motor and ataxia pre-test and does not lose the righting reflex; **sedation margin** = the lowest dose at which any of 8 pups loses the righting reflex over the lowest dose with R at most 0.7. **The lead** is the agent with the smaller R at its highest non-sedating dose when the interval of the difference of the two log R values excludes 0 and exceeds ln(1/0.8); if the interval includes 0 the two are **tied on protection** and the lead goes to the agent with the larger sedation margin; if the margins also tie within a factor of 1.5, **ethosuximide stays the lead by its prior position, gaboxadol the rival, and the stated decision is "no efficacy preference; choose on specificity, harm and label"**. Gaboxadol also depends on the chloride gradient at the age tested (held ADV-065 work).
- **Spontaneous burden.** If the line shows spontaneous events at P14 or P18, a spontaneous-burden readout is mandatory in the same animals before the provocation (video, or ECoG over a fixed pre-test window; ECoG spikes are documented in null pups from about P14, and EEG or ECoG before P14 is the first step of ordering row 11). If it does not, the result is stated as **a provoked-seizure lead** and the spontaneous-burden study is the named next step. Nothing here claims a spontaneous-burden effect.

## 3. Outcome table (each row names a decision; measured exposure, two ages, panel valid)
| Outcome | Reading | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Effect in the knockout (R at most 0.7, interval excluding 1) at one age or both; wild-type series flat or smaller; pre-test passed | provoked-seizure lead in the knockout; specificity unresolved if underpowered | carried to a spontaneous-burden and specificity study; the age where it holds is named |
| Same-size effect in wild type at the same age | general anticonvulsant effect | carried only as a bridge-by-seizure-control agent, stated as general; choice rests on harm and label |
| Interval inside 0.85 to 1.15 at brain exposure above both potency readings, engagement passed | null in the knockout at this assay | ethosuximide falls; the rival moves up; age is not the reason (series and panel valid) |
| Null with exposure between 0.6 and 5 mM | **uninterpretable between potencies** | repeat at the next exposure within tolerance or add a native-tissue engagement readout |
| Null below 0.6 mM | dose null | repeat with dose raised or route changed |
| Effect only at a sedating dose | not benefit | arm dropped or redosed |
| Panel agent fails at an age | assay failure | move the age; the null is not read |
| Interval spanning 0.7 and 1 | underpowered | n set from the observed SD (Stage 1); no decision |

## 4. What the experiment cannot show
Specificity of the effect to the Wwox-deficient genotype at feasible n (unless the pilot SD allows it); a spontaneous-seizure benefit; a mechanism (Cav3 involvement is not tested without engagement or genetics); anything about juvenile harm at the effective exposure beyond sedation and weight; which agent is better on the benefit axis if the two tie on protection; anything about people. No dose, schedule or use in any person is proposed.
