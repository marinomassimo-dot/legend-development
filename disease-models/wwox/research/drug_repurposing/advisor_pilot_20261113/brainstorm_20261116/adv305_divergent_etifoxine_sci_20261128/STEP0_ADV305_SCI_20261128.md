## Answer

Prediction: held. For (B) no JAK-inhibitor dataset with developmental stage and cell resolution exists; the trophic arm alone does (it is not baricitinib and not WWOX), so (B) cannot decide inflammatory engagement against loss of trophic support: the missing observation is a paired phosphoprotein (pSTAT1/pSTAT3) and first-sheath or survival readout in the same developing Wwox preparation under a JAK inhibitor.
For (A) the timing question (fast receptor effect against delayed, precursor-dependent steroid effect) cannot be settled from data in hand: every arm of the new paper is pre-incubated 40 minutes to 3 hours, concentrations differ (3, 5, 7.5, 10, 20 and 100 microM), exposure is nominal, chloride is not controlled, and no blockade of etifoxine in the same preparation is shown (laboratory dependency; registered as the missing observation: acute versus pre-incubated response of etifoxine with and without a TSPO or 5-alpha-reductase block, with ganaxolone in the same cells). **What the held public data can decide is a narrower, real question:** whether the anti-hyperexcitability effect of etifoxine in a developmental epilepsy model tracks (E1) direct GABA-A potentiation, (E2) TSPO-linked neurosteroid synthesis or (E3) a steroid-independent TSPO or metabolic action, by comparing five ligands that differ in exactly those properties in one laboratory's assays. This changes the role of etifoxine in INV-10: a probe of the TSPO class, or a second GABA-A-modulator comparator beside ganaxolone.

**Primary: (A), narrowed. Alternative, distinct: (B), with the missing observation above and a note that the Laitman and Steelman developmental results conflict in the trophic arm.**
