## Objective 1: broad functional recovery (developmental and durable)
- **Currently preferred reading: none defensible by name.** The strongest *pharmacological* starting point is the **GSK-3 class** (lithium plus a second, selective GSK-3 class; INV-09): the only in vivo drug effect in null pups, a link to Wnt and myelination (sign divided across studies). But the lithium effect is general (also in wild type), so on this record it is not a broad-recovery candidate, only a regimen question.
- **Strongest rival:** a mechanism acting on the endpoints the gene-addition rescue moved, in particular **myelination and neuronal maturation**: a pro-myelinating route (INV-06; structural myelin endpoints with neuronal-AAV9 rescue are held; conduction measured once), **ISR attenuation** (INV-02) and **metabolic or NRF2 routes** (INV-07, INV-08). The glucose axis (null mice hypoglycaemic from week 2) is not decidable as cause or accompaniment.
- **What could reverse or settle the preference, with accessible analyses:**
  1. A **premise screen**, pre-registered, per mechanism: in the held WWOX-deficient datasets (the human organoid RNA-seq with two knockout clusters, the P17 per-animal mouse deposits with the stage covariate), does the *pathway the candidate acts on* move in the direction the candidate would reverse, after adjustment for maturity and cell composition, in the unit of biological replication? A mechanism whose premise is absent or opposite drops out; one with a replicated, adjusted, same-direction signal survives. (Reuses the ADV-068, ADV-069 and ADV-070 method.)
  2. A **target and exposure table** per surviving agent: potency at its target and at competing targets, plausible free brain exposure in juvenile mice at the regimens used, and whether the in vivo effect already seen (e.g. lithium's) is explained by the target at those exposures.
  3. **Endpoint mapping:** which of the gene-addition endpoints (survival, growth, motor, structural myelin, glucose) each surviving mechanism could plausibly move, from perturbation data or cross-disease evidence where context transfers.
- **Priority experiment (to be chosen after the screen):** an in vivo or slice experiment on the surviving mechanism whose positive, negative and ambiguous outcomes differ in meaning; not named yet.

## Objective 2: partial or bridge benefit with a more conservative benefit and risk aim
- **Currently preferred reading (weak, to be overturned): ethosuximide**, because it has an in vivo Wwox-null effect, a label for another seizure type and the only conservative candidate with a **named decision** (INV-04: is the effect WWOX-specific or general, and where is the age floor). Its limits are severe: effect not separable from age and a floor, no Cav3 link to WWOX, T-type transcript level flat from P14 to P28 in the one dataset read, label not established below 3 years.
- **Strongest rival:** the **NMDAR-class route** (d-APV is the only agent with a direct Wwox-system readout, 5 slices from 3 animals; the use-dependent pore blockers differ in kinetics, trapping and magnesium dependence), and the **Kv7-class route** (Kcnq2 transcript rising P14 to P28, background-confounded; the cell-against-slice mediation experiment exists in the map). **Challenger and less-developed route to test:** **tonic-inhibition enhancers** (extrasynaptic GABA-A or neurosteroid-type modulators), suggested by the registered Gabrd and tonic-current predictions and by the bisect simulation, where a neurosteroid-type class ranked second after d-APV and T-type last; the pediatric label status of agents in this class is to be verified, not assumed.
- **What could reverse the preference:**
  1. **Robustness of the class ordering across model structures:** does the simulated order (d-APV, neurosteroid-type, NMDAR pore, sodium, AMPA, synaptic GABA-A, T-type) survive alternative circuit structures and plausible parameters, not only many seeds of one structure? A class that wins under one structure only is a prediction conditional on that assumption.
  2. **Exposure mapping:** which classes act at the exposures that are plausible in juvenile mice, with research concentrations kept apart from any patient dosing; a mapping from a class to a model parameter is not yet a compound prediction.
  3. **General against specific:** for each agent, does the effect in wild type equal the effect in the null (INV-04 logic applied to the others)? A purely general antiseizure effect may still be useful for a bridge aim but must be said so.
  4. **Harm against phenotype** per agent and age (held: vigabatrin retina and optic pathway PARTIAL; lithium narrow margin; NMDAR-class developing-brain signals): conservative is examined per agent and context, not assumed from a label or prior pediatric use.
- **Priority experiment (to be chosen):** the one whose outcome separates the leading class from its strongest rival; the shared slice preparation is an option only if it addresses that selected question.

## What would make me change the note
A premise screen that kills the GSK-3 premise or promotes a rival; an exposure table showing that the in vivo effects are not explained by the candidate target; a model-structure study in which the class order flips; a harm cell that excludes a candidate for the conservative aim. ## Dated methodological integration, 22:19Z on 2026-11-19 (host clock), from the operator
Applies to ADV-095, ADV-096 and ADV-097; frozen results and the baseline reproduction are preserved; no new gate and no mandatory passage through the operator; methods stay with the roles.
1. **Normalisation of the simulated class order.** In `pilot_bisect_harness_model.py` each arm lists its own multiplier and the factor is `multiplier ** s` (d-APV J_N x0.3; NMDAR pore blocker J_N x0.8; T-type J_T x0.75; AMPA J_A x0.8; GABA-A J_I x1.2). d-APV and the pore blocker perturb the same parameter (s = 0.164 equals s = 0.885), so an order in crossing strength s is partly **imposed by the coefficients**: it is not a ranking of classes and the 8 of 9 pass of the registered predictions (P1 especially) carries this caveat. Comparisons are to be made on a normalisation-independent scale (the crossing multiplier, i.e. the fractional change of the targeted parameter) between biologically comparable perturbations or pharmacological profiles supported by data; the stability of an order that the coefficients impose is not superiority of a class. The version-1 text above that uses the simulated order as a ranking is read with this caveat.
2. **The broad transcriptomic premise screen is a source of evidence, not a universal condition of access.** Absence of an RNA change or an opposite sign does not automatically exclude a lever; protein activity, compensation and functional results count; contrasts are shown before and after the maturity and composition adjustments, distinguishing possible confounders from components of the phenotype.
3. **The simulation wrapper is "parametric sensitivity"**, distinct from any true structural alternative (Kv7 is not in the model: an assumption). A few informative **joint scenarios** are added even if single variations do not change the winner; the planned compute is reorganised, not expanded.
4. **SCI chooses the exposure-effect relation per agent**; occupancy C/(C+IC50) is one option that needs explicit assumptions and comparable data and is not a universal mapping to the circuit.
