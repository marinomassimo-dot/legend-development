- **What could reverse or settle the preference, with accessible analyses:**
  1. ## Objective 2: partial or bridge benefit with a more conservative benefit and risk aim
- **Currently preferred reading (weak, to be overturned): ethosuximide**, because it has an in vivo Wwox-null effect, a label for another seizure type and the only conservative candidate with a **named decision** (INV-04: is the effect WWOX-specific or general, and where is the age floor). - **What could reverse the preference:**
  1. **Robustness of the class ordering across model structures:** does the simulated order (d-APV, neurosteroid-type, NMDAR pore, sodium, AMPA, synaptic GABA-A, T-type) survive alternative circuit structures and plausible parameters, not only many seeds of one structure?   3. **General against specific:** for each agent, does the effect in wild type equal the effect in the null (INV-04 logic applied to the others)? A purely general antiseizure effect may still be useful for a bridge aim but must be said so.
  4. **Harm against phenotype** per agent and age (held: vigabatrin retina and optic pathway PARTIAL; lithium narrow margin; NMDAR-class developing-brain signals): conservative is examined per agent and context, not assumed from a label or prior pediatric use.
- **Priority experiment (to be chosen):** the one whose outcome separates the leading class from its strongest rival; the shared slice preparation is an option only if it addresses that selected question.
