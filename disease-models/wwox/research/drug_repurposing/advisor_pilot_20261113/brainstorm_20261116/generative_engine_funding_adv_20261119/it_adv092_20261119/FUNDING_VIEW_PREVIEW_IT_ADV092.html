Study-preparation diagnostic

This diagnostic does not select candidates. Scientific selection compares a preferred intervention with its strongest rival after pertinent in-silico analyses, for one candidate and priority experiment per objective when justified. Quotes are not required. See roadmap_current.md: Scientific selection before laboratory work.

2. Preparation sensitivity: three declared orderings

Each ordering is a lexicographic key (no weights, no sums): A = decision named, null control, weakest link resolved, assay sharing; B = weakest link resolved, decision named, null control, assay sharing; C = assay sharing, decision named, weakest link resolved, null control. Rank 1 is first; equal keys share a rank. A wide range means the position depends on the ordering chosen, not on the evidence. The keys measure how ready a study is to be judged (a decision named, a null that can be read, the weakest link reachable, a preparation already shared); they contain no benefit, no safety and no cost, so rank 1 is not a verdict on a candidate. Assay sharing counts candidates tagged to one preparation, which may be several stages of one programme rather than one study.
