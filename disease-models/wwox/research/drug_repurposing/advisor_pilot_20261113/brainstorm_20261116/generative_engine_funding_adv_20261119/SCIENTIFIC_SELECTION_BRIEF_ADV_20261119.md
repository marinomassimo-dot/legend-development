## SCI: execute the analyses that can change the choice

Have autonomy over cases and methods within the mission. Reuse pertinent complete readings and actual data before requesting more work. State which analyses were executed, which remain proposals, and which cannot answer the question with the available inputs. Examples to choose from, not required stages:

- Reanalyse held molecular or functional data where biological replication and context permit; distinguish donors or animals from cells, nuclei and technical repeats. A signature may reflect compensation, injury or cell composition rather than a direction to reverse.
- Compare candidate actions and relevant exposure against the intended target and competing targets. Catalogue labels, incomparable potency assays and generic CNS annotations do not supply the missing pharmacology. Keep research concentrations separate from patient dosing.
- Explore competing mechanistic models constrained by observations. Ask whether a preference survives plausible parameters and model structures, not just many seeds of one assumed mechanism. A mapping from a drug class to an arbitrary model parameter is not yet a compound prediction.
- Use perturbation signatures or cross-disease evidence where the mechanism, cell type, developmental state and endpoint make transfer informative. Reuse existing nulls and negative results; reopen an unsuccessful route for a reason such as new data or a materially different method, not because it is available.
- Consider targeted structural or residual-protein work when it addresses a specific recoverable defect. Predicted binding or stability does not itself establish functional rescue.

Look for the evidence that could overturn the favoured explanation. Additional reasoning can strengthen, weaken or leave a preference unchanged; all three are useful when the reason is recorded. Generate a new alternative when a current one fails. Do not require a fully proven mechanism before a well-controlled functional comparison. Disease-specific attribution can matter without making an ordinary symptomatic benefit irrelevant.
