## Answer first
**NARROW.** One INV-07 reading changes; no INV-07 comparison does. The paper cannot separate astrocyte lactate PRODUCTION or EXPORT from neuronal USE, because it has no neuronal use measurement at all. What it does carry, in astrocytes, is a measured case that makes INV-07's lactate readout more specific.

**The one named endpoint (INV-07, astrocyte class):** read lactate as a label-resolved pair of pools, cells and medium from the same wells, with [U-13C]glucose-derived lactate (M+3) beside the unlabelled pool, next to OCR and ECAR. Do not read a total lactate, an ECAR or a proton-efflux rate as the production readout.
* **Why (the measured case, Figs 2 and S3):** with halved respiration, ECAR, proton efflux rate and glucose-derived glycolytic intermediates were unchanged (no significant difference), while lactate fell in BOTH compartments (cell pool about 62 to 65% of WT, medium about 70%; Fig 2E, read from the image). In the one labelled-glucose experiment (Fig S3A, n = 3 cultures), the fall sits in the UNLABELLED lactate pool (about 270 against 195 ng/mL, P 0.0021 as printed) while the glucose-derived M+3 pool is the same (about 120 against 125, P 0.87) and M+1 is the same (P 0.85). So the missing lactate is not glucose-derived. ECAR cannot show that; a total lactate cannot show it; INV-07's existing sentence ("ECAR is not a lactate readout without an oxygen correction; measure lactate and pyruvate directly with redox state") stops one step short of it.
* **Strongest rival to the astrocyte-production reading:** consumption or re-oxidation of lactate in the same cells (the authors themselves list "enhancing its oxidation as a compensatory mechanism"), or a non-glycolytic carbon source or medium-pyruvate difference, or a state change (GFAP up, thinner arbors and more TOMM20 and VDAC1 in the AAV arm, Figs 7 and 8). A lower pool, even label-resolved, does not by itself say production fell.
* **Refuting observation (as the assignment framed it):** in the WWOX-deficient astrocyte class at the relevant age, total lactate and the glucose-derived and unlabelled pools are unchanged in cells and medium while a downstream neuronal readout differs: production and export are then preserved and the difference lies downstream, so the astrocyte-supply reading is refuted for that preparation. The export sub-branch has its own refuting pattern: a cell pool that is equal or higher beside a lower medium pool is export-limited; both pools lower (Murlanova's pattern) is not.
* **What this does NOT add:** the (a) against (b) comparison. INV-07 and SCI44 PH3 already carry it (astrocyte Ldha loss and neuronal MCT2; add-back arms; extracellular lactate; burst endpoint). The paper offers no neuronal use arm to improve it. No change to PH3.

## Prior art first, and what the paper adds to it
* SCI44 PH1 (per-class readout; the dominant PDK differs by class), PH3 (lactate-side tool in the KO slice, pyruvate add-back, extracellular lactate; astrocyte Ldha and neuronal MCT2; A4D04 GFAP-Cre knockout), PH4 and the A4D12 overturn, ADV-105 (serum lactate raised in the null, no brain-compartment datum; S-KO brain lactate before P9 seizures as the missing step 3), INV-07 next_test (marker-resolved material; direct lactate and pyruvate with redox; ECAR caveat). The held WWOX originals have no cell-resolved lactate or PDH value.
* **Not new (duplicates with new labels):** "read per cell class", "measure lactate directly", astrocyte lactate to neuron.
* **New in kind, small:** (1) a measured dissociation between respiration, glycolytic input and a lower lactate pool, with the label showing where the deficit sits (above); (2) a measured case that brain-compartment lactate (PFC tissue about 65% of control, CSF about 25%) fell while SERUM lactate did not (Fig 3J, n = 7 to 8 males; serum P 0.79). That is a boundary for ADV-105: serum lactate cannot stand in for the brain compartment in either direction, which supports the "missing step 3" wording and nothing more.

## What the neuronal endpoint can and cannot support
* Supports: after astrocyte Npas3 loss (post-P21), L5 current-spike output at higher currents is lower (L2/3 not; RMP, Rin and rheobase unchanged), basal-dendrite spine density is lower (both layers), and in the AAV arm mEPSC frequency is lower. The AAV excitability change is not significant (P = 0.1029, 7 cells from 4 mice per arm).
* Does not support lactate USE: no neuronal lactate uptake, MCT2 dependence, KATP dependence, neuronal lactate-derived TCA labelling or NADH/ATP readout. The lactate-to-neuron chain is the Discussion's proposal ("can be, at least in part, explained by the chronic shortage of extracellular lactate"), with a dashed arrow in Fig 10.
* The rescue does not separate supply from use or from a systemic effect: one i.p. injection of sodium L-lactate, 1 mg/kg, 1 hour before training, with the behaviour as endpoint (cue freezing); D-lactate is the control. The text reports no blood, CSF or brain lactate after the injection. By my arithmetic (25 g mouse, sodium lactate 112 g/mol, 1.5 mL blood) the dose is about 0.2 micromole, at most about 0.15 mM if all stayed in blood, against an ordinary blood lactate of low millimolar (general knowledge, not from the paper). I do not conclude the rescue is wrong; I conclude it is not a measured restoration of an extracellular lactate pool. An excitability rescue exists only in the AAV arm, 24 h after injection, 3 cells from 1 mouse (Fig S10).
* GC2 specificity in vivo is not shown: the GC2 add-back (Fig 2F to I) is in the germline-HET culture only. It restores basal and maximal respiration and both lactate pools (cell pool about 93% and medium about 97% of empty-vector WT, read from the image). The printed ATP-linked respiration P values are 0.069 and 0.0705, so the text's "restored" for ATP-linked respiration is not supported by those tests. In WT cells GC2 overexpression did not raise lactate (no dose-response).
* Transcription-factor loss: GC2 was chosen from a filtered candidate list (E-box, astrocyte-enriched, mitochondrial); Acss1 and Klf15 fall too (Fig 1D). The in vivo OXPHOS fall is attributable to Npas3 loss, not to GC2 specifically.

## EMPATHY and DREAM (once, because they change a decision)
* **Premise class.** Before any further fuel or PDK-side intervention in INV-07 ask whether the premise is production, transport or use. This model shows why: both pools fell, so export was not the limit, yet the label shows the deficit is not glucose-derived, and a use or consumption change inside the astrocyte is not excluded. Production, export, consumption and neuronal use are four statements; a pool or a rescue answers none alone.
* **DREAM.** SCI44's compartment ambiguity is connected to a pair (perturbation, assay): a measured astrocyte-restricted perturbation of the mitochondrial route plus compartment-paired, label-resolved lactate with OCR and ECAR. The paper delivers that pair in astrocyte culture and it separates export-limited from production-or-consumption-limited. The connection is DISCARDED on the neuronal side: no use assay exists, so (a) against (b) is not advanced.

## Limits and what would change this
Adjacent model (a transcription factor, post-P21, mouse, PFC); adult cognition sets no direction, age, seizure effect or compound for WWOX-DEE. Figures 2, 3 and S3 viewed as images and numbers read from them (approximate); Figs 1 and 4 to 10 not viewed (legends and text only); supplementary Figs S1, S2, S4 to S10 captions only; no GEO matrix downloaded (the series record page only). Fig S3 is n = 3 with an unlabelled-pool difference; a 30% total difference could hide behind the labelled pools, but the unlabelled pool itself is the one that moved. A per-class, label-resolved astrocyte lactate pair in a WWOX-deficient human neural preparation, with a neuronal use measurement, would decide this; none is held.
