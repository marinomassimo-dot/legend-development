# I3 COMPENSATION ASYMMETRY: why the hippocampus does not burst (dossier v1, SCI, 2026-11-16; hypothesis, not a finding; mechanism-level; not medical advice; reference genotype class only)

Written for: ADV (cross-reading), HE (second reader), IT and the operator (the first block is the five-minute reading). Labels: KNOWN (source), HELD (table or full text in the repository), ASSUMED, INFERRED. Models are hypothesis generators, not evidence.

## Answer first
* **The idea, reshaped by a held table I had not read at the right resolution.** The hippocampus loses Wwox more than the neocortex (S line, transcript TPM: 0.15 of control against 0.32), loses more PV transcript (0.29 against 0.66), and yet does not burst (Breton 2021: no bursts in hippocampal slices; HELD). So *something other than a smaller deficit* holds it. The ADV seed named NPY. The held table says the NPY story is real in fold and weaker in absolute terms, and it names three other candidates that are at least as strong:
  1. **Peptidergic brake (the seed).** Npy rises 5.7-fold in hippocampus (94 to 537 TPM) against 1.95-fold in neocortex (475 to 926), but the *absolute* rise is the same (+443 against +452 TPM) and the null hippocampus ends with 58% of the neocortical level. The Y2 receptor (Npy2r) is 14-fold more abundant in hippocampal controls than in neocortical ones (11.8 against 0.85 TPM). So the ligand-receptor pair exists in hippocampus and barely in neocortex; the fold-change reading was the wrong one, the receptor-availability reading is the one that makes the asymmetry (HELD, INFERRED).
  2. **Sustainer drop.** Grin2b falls to 0.28 of control in hippocampus (116 to 33 TPM) and 0.89 in neocortex; Grin2a to 0.53 against 0.89 (HELD). In the I1 model the NMDAR sustainer is the quantity that sets burst duration and, below a threshold, burst existence. A hippocampus that has *lost its sustainer* needs no brake at all.
  3. **Tonic GABA-A (the most sensitive knob of the I1 model).** The delta subunit (Gabrd), the cortical tonic subunit, is 10-fold more abundant in neocortical than hippocampal controls (83 against 8.5 TPM) and falls to 0.43 of control in the null neocortex; the alpha-5 subunit (Gabra5), the hippocampal tonic subunit, is 6-fold more abundant in hippocampus (84 against 14 TPM) and is kept at 0.69 (cortex 1.16) (HELD, n 2 to 4, bulk). In the I1 model a 20% rise of the tonic weight cuts the burst rate 77%, a 40% fall quadruples it. So the *tonic inhibition each region depends on* is lost in neocortex and largely kept in hippocampus: a fourth route that needs no brake at all (INFERRED).
  4. **Somatostatin and chloride headroom.** Sst rises 2.5-fold in hippocampus against 1.35 in neocortex (HELD); the KCC2-to-NKCC1 transcript index moves 1.51 in hippocampus and 0.91 in neocortex (INFERRED; see I6).
* **Why this is worth pushing, not parking.** These routes make different predictions about the *same* slice, so one experiment separates all four (D5b). The tonic route predicts that hippocampal tonic inhibition (alpha-5, blocked by an alpha-5-selective negative modulator, a research tool) unmasks bursts and that a Y2 blocker does not; the brake route predicts that blocking Y2 unmasks hippocampal bursts; the sustainer route predicts that a GluN2B potentiation (or a raised co-agonist level) unmasks them and a Y2 blocker does nothing; the chloride route predicts that unmasking follows an NKCC1-type load or a KCC2 inhibitor and changes burst shape first.
* **What the I1 model adds (script outputs in this folder).** In the corrected oscillator a presynaptic brake of 15 to 25% on recurrent excitation is enough to cut the burst rate several-fold and to shorten the bursts to blips (`brake_cliff.tsv`); a 40% drop of the NMDAR weight alone silences it (`oat_scan.tsv`). So the sizes needed are in the range of the held transcript changes for *each* route (brake 15 to 25%, NMDAR weight -40%, tonic weight +20 to 40% or lost 57% in the cortex): the held data cannot choose, the experiment can.
* **Depth reached: model, experiment-ready.** Not repurposing-ready: no agent is proposed for use; labels below carry flags.

## D1 Statement and chain
1. Wwox falls in both regions, more in hippocampus (HELD: S line 0.15 against 0.32 of control; the earlier -85 and -68% were computed on the same tables).
2. Both regions lose PV transcript, hippocampus more (HELD 0.29 against 0.66), and the bursts exist only in neocortex (HELD, Breton 2021): so the inhibitory-marker loss cannot by itself make bursts.
3. A *regional* brake or a *regional* loss of the sustainer exists in hippocampus (ASSUMED; four candidate forms above; HELD transcripts are consistent with each).
4. The brake or the lost sustainer lowers the recurrent excitation below the bursting threshold (INFERRED from the I1 model; a graded cliff, not a switch).
5. Unmasking: removing the brake (Y2 or Y1 blockade) or restoring the sustainer (GluN2B potentiation or co-agonist) should turn the hippocampal slice into a bursting one *only if that route is the operating one* (prediction, not data).
6. In neocortex the same route would be too weak: Npy2r is nearly absent (0.85 TPM) and Grin2b is not lost (HELD), so adding a Y2 agonist or an adenosine route (the shared presynaptic brake) should reduce neocortical bursts (prediction; the I1 model gives the size).

## D2 Why non-obvious
The standard reading has two parts: Wwox loss is neuron-intrinsic, and the hippocampus is simply less affected or less recombined. Both are contradicted in the held tables (Wwox falls *more* in hippocampus; the signal is in both astrocyte-like and neuron-like nuclei). The second standard reading, "NPY goes up more in hippocampus, therefore it compensates", rests on a ratio and fails on absolute numbers. The reshaped idea is: *regional availability of the receptor or loss of the sustainer*, not "more ligand".

## D3 Consequences across scales
* Cell: hippocampal CA1/CA3 pyramids in the null should show a small NMDAR-dependent late component in any evoked burst-like event; neocortical L2/3 a large one (the I1 duration variable).
* Slice: Y2 blockade unmasks bursting in null hippocampus if and only if Y2 tone is present; the same blocker should *shorten the interval* of neocortical bursts less than in hippocampus because Y2 is nearly absent there (HELD receptor level).
* Animal: seizure spread should respect the asymmetry: a hippocampal focus unmasked by a Y2 antagonist in the null.
* Patient (observational): whether hippocampal-onset semiology is rare in the published cases is NOT established here (the A1 cases table was not read for this; first task under I2); an observation of temporal-onset seizures in a child on an agent that lowers the Y2/NPY tone would be informative (weak: no such agent is declared).
* **Surprising prediction.** The hippocampus is not "spared": it is *held below threshold*, so any manipulation that lowers its brake by a modest amount (the model needs a brake change of the order of the 15 to 25% window) should produce a burst phenotype with the *same interval statistics* as neocortex (median about 26 s) once it crosses; the shape (interval distribution) is a property of the network, not of the region.

## D4 Model
`rebound_oscillator.py` (I1 dossier) with a presynaptic brake parameter `brake` that scales recurrent excitation (1 - brake multiplies the AMPA and NMDAR recurrent terms). Outputs `brake_cliff.tsv` (rate, interval and duration against the brake, corrected model) and the one-at-a-time scan `oat_scan.tsv` (J_N and J_A rows). The model is fitted to the neocortical baseline only; it has no hippocampal data; the cliff sizes are properties of the fitted network. A model is a hypothesis generator, not evidence.

## D5 Discriminating experiments
(a) **No new data.** (i) The held bulk tables: add Npy1r, Npy2r, Sst, Grin2a and Grin2b to a single table next to the P47T all-genes table (5 v 5) and see whether the P47T hippocampus, a different mutant line, shows the same asymmetry in Grin2b and Npy2r (cost: one script; outcome: asymmetry reproduced in a second line favours a *Wwox-dependent* mechanism; absent favours a line-specific or batch effect; each can fail). (ii) Per-child semiology rows from I2: are there hippocampal-type semiologies? (b) **One slice experiment.** Null hippocampal and neocortical slices, with a Y2 antagonist, then with a GluN2B-selective positive modulation or a co-agonist, then an NKCC1-type load: burst incidence in hippocampus under each; outcomes (i) bursts appear only with the Y2 route: brake; (ii) only with the NMDAR route: sustainer; (iii) with the chloride route: headroom; (iii-b) only with the alpha-5 or delta tonic route: tonic inhibition; (iv) with none: killed for these three, the asymmetry then needs a structural explanation (connectivity) and the idea is replaced by one (the named test). (c) **Clinical-observational.** Record onset topography (EEG localisation) per child against the timing of NMDAR-active or Y2-active exposure.
