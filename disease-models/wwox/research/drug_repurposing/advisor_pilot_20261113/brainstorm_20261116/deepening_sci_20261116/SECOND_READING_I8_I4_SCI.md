## Answer first
* **I8, the weakest link is the counting.** The five held features of the pyramidal profile are not five independent observations, and two of HE's six fits count a channel for a feature it cannot carry at rest. A single-compartment conductance model (`intrinsic_profile_model.py`, MODEL, tuned only to the wild-type row) says: a loss of about 30% of the **resting K+ leak** reproduces the knockout resting potential, input resistance, sag and rebound at once (RMP -74.8 mV, Rin 150 MOhm, sag 2.05 mV, rebound 1.78 mV against held -73.6, 143, 2.57, 1.86); raising Ih alone reproduces sag and rebound and **opposes** resting potential and input resistance (as HE found); lowering tonic GABA-A conductance or raising T-type conductance does almost nothing to the profile; a smaller cell with the same densities (every conductance and C x0.72) reproduces the input-resistance rise and the unchanged time constant and not the resting potential. So the profile asks for a *resting conductance loss with a hyperpolarising reversal*, and the PY candidates that carry a resting conductance are not the ones in the table.
* **I4, the two-step design is sound and I would keep it; three changes.** (1) The agonist step cannot be read on burst rate (floor effect: both genotypes go silent); read it on holding current. (2) The model says adenosine is a weak knob at the fitted baseline, so the A/G arithmetic will have little power on bursts and should be powered on the holding current. (3) The brief's arms and your steps are compatible, but the multiplicity changes (below).

## I8: attacks, in order of weight
1. **The features are correlated, not independent (HELD Table 1 of Breton 2021, files/fulltext/PMID34634460_Breton2021_EPMC_2026-09-27.xml).** Resting potential (-79.25 to -73.62 mV) and input resistance (102.9 to 143.0 MOhm) are two readouts of one change (fewer open resting conductances). Sag and rebound are two readouts of one deep hyperpolarising deflection. The action-potential amplitude (76.3 to 68.7 mV) is downstream of the resting potential for any voltage-gated Na+ channel, because a depolarised rest inactivates a fraction of the channels (ASSUMED: the steady-state inactivation half-point of neuronal Nav is a few millivolts positive to the control resting potential; a 5.6 mV shift therefore costs a measurable fraction of the available current without any change in channel number). So the five features are about three: resting conductance, the hyperpolarising deflection, and the spike amplitude, and the third is partly a consequence of the first. The count of 8 or 6 "fits" in `sign_fit_summary.tsv` overstates the evidence by about a factor of two.
2. **'Ih elevated' is the paper's inference, and the held numbers cannot separate it from a deeper deflection.** The words in the held paper: "the hyperpolarization activated potassium current was elevated, as evidenced by a larger hyperpolarization in response to a negative current injection" (Results, Table 1 discussion). The word HCN does not occur in the held full text (0 matches), no Ih current or ZD7288 experiment was done on these neurons; the sag is a **voltage** at a fixed -200 pA (Table 1: 1.18, 2.04, 2.57 mV for S-WT, S-HT, S-KO; the paper defines it as the steady-state deflection minus the most negative deflection), and the input resistance rose 39%, so the same current gives a 39% deeper deflection (about 20.6 mV against 28.6 mV by Rin x current, INFERRED upper estimate), which by itself enlarges the sag in mV with no change in Ih density. Normalising by the deflection leaves about a 1.6-fold rise (5.7% to 9.0%, INFERRED), not zero, so some Ih or other time-dependent conductance change is possible but not established. Your row for HCN1 ("M+ fits on 3 of 4") should be marked: the sag and rebound readings are not Ih density.
3. **Kcnh7 and Kcnt2 cannot carry a resting-potential effect (ASSUMED from general channel physiology, to be checked with a source).** ERG-family channels (Kv11.x) open positive to about -60 mV and inactivate quickly, so their contribution at -79 mV is small; Slack/Kcnt channels are Na+-activated and matter during firing. Your table marks both "ASSUMED weak" and still scores them as 2/0 fits for M-; they should be scored "uninformative for resting features". The channels that set rest in cortical pyramids are K2P (TASK, TREK), Kir and Kv7 (M-current), none of them in your table. **Request:** extend the PPxY scan to Kcnk1, 2, 3, 4, 9, 10, 13, 18 (K2P), Kcnj2, 4, 6, 10, 12 (Kir), Kcnq2, Kcnq3, Kcnq5 (Kv7: my recollection, ASSUMED and to be checked by your scan, is that KCNQ2/3 are Nedd4-2 targets with PY motifs); a PPxY motif in a resting K+ channel would make I8 fit the profile, and Kv7 is the target of an antiseizure class (a label with a boxed-warning screen, see I1 D6).
4. **Nav threshold.** AP threshold is not different (HELD), while amplitude falls. Density change normally moves both (ASSUMED); the amplitude fall alone is expected from the depolarised rest. So the pyramidal AP amplitude is not evidence for a Nav density change. **The interneuron AP amplitude is the one feature that keeps its independence**: interneuron resting potential did not change (-67.1 against -71.5 mV, ns, 5 and 6 cells from 4 and 5 animals) while the amplitude fell 26% (60.78 to 45.03 mV, p < 0.05, same table). That points at the interneuron spike generator and is the best-supported statement in your addendum; it is small-n and one paper.
5. **Heterozygotes and the dose test (HELD).** Table 1 and the Results text: S-HT shows the same shifts as S-KO in resting potential (-75.51 against -79.25 and -73.62), input resistance (143.3), sag (2.04) and AP amplitude (68.8), and **bursts occur in heterozygotes** ("the spontaneous bursts resulting from WWOX deletion were also observed in heterozygote mice, and manifested similarly as those observed in the knockouts"; the knockout hippocampal slices are indistinguishable from controls and heterozygotes); rebound is significant in S-KO only (1.86 against 1.24 ns, S-WT 0.96). Consequences for I8: a surface-level mechanism that tracks WWOX protein dose predicts an intermediate heterozygote, the held pattern is step-like for four features (haploinsufficiency or a threshold); and **the one feature that does NOT reach significance in heterozygotes, rebound, is the one my I1 trigger needs** (see I1 dossier v2: rebound is not necessary for the burst).
6. **Cell size is a competing explanation for the passive features.** The input resistance rose 39% while tau was unchanged (18.55, 18.79, 21.25 ms, ns): the capacitance fell about as much as the resistance rose, which is what a smaller cell does (MODEL: all conductances and C x0.72 gives Rin x1.39 and tau unchanged). Smaller, less mature neurons would also fit my held maturation calibration thread (GSE150123). It does not explain the resting-potential shift. It must be ruled out before any channel is invoked: capacitance (a membrane test) and soma size are free readouts in the recordings that exist.
7. **What I would add to the decomposition experiment:** record capacitance and the tau separately; read the sag as a *ratio to the deflection* at matched steady-state deflection (e.g. current adjusted per cell to a fixed -20 mV), then the Ih contribution is measurable without a pharmacological step; add a leak-channel arm (a broad K2P or Kv7 label, mechanism level) to the P47T/null/wild-type comparison.

## I4: amendments
1. **Conflicts with my brief (`experiment_brief_presidio013_sci_20261116/EXPERIMENT_BRIEF_V1_1.md`).** None on the arms: M03 already has an A1-antagonist class arm with adenosine-deaminase reversal on holding current, resting potential and input resistance, and M25 asks for a substrate arm that does not exist yet (brief section 12). Conflicts of design: (a) the brief is paired per slice, baseline then agent, with a time-matched vehicle group and the animal as the unit, Bonferroni 0.0083 for six arms; your six steps plus four substrate conditions would need a new multiplicity rule (a tiered rule: the two-step A/G reading as one pre-specified contrast, the rest descriptive); (b) the saturating A1 agonist is not reversible within a slice, so it must be the last step or run in separate slices; (c) the brief's pilot of four animals per genotype fixes the variance; the A/G design is only worth powering after it.
2. **Read G on holding current, not on bursts.** A saturating A1 agonist is expected to silence bursts in both genotypes (a floor effect: A and G would then be undefined on the burst endpoint). Use the outward holding current or hyperpolarisation of a recorded neuron (the same cells as step 1), and keep the burst endpoint for the antagonist step (A) only.
3. **Power.** MODEL: with the fitted baseline, scaling the adenosine weight changes the burst rate by little (weight x0.6: rate 2.3 against 1.8 per minute; x1.4: 1.5), whereas the tonic GABA-A weight or the noise amplitude change it several-fold. Adenosine is a weak knob for *burst rate*; the A/G logic stands for the *holding current*, which is where it will have power. Size it from the pilot variance of the holding current (the brief's 4-animal pilot), not from burst counts.
4. **Adenosine deaminase belongs in separate slices** and answers a different question (all adenosine tone, all receptors); keep it as your step 2 but as its own contrast, not inside the A/G arithmetic.
5. **Neurons:** layer II/III pyramidal cells first (the cells with the held profile), PV-like interneurons as a second set (the interneuron amplitude fall of point 4); the same cells for steps 1, 3 and 4 give A, G and the KATP effect per cell, which is where the power is.
6. **KATP step:** sulfonylurea-type blockers and diazoxide-type openers have non-KATP actions (ASSUMED, label-level knowledge to be checked); read the step as 'KATP-sensitive component' and pair it with the glucose arm only after the A/G reading.
7. **Add one prediction to the harness for the same J_a scan:** J_a x0.6 gives a rate rise of about 25%, x1.4 a fall of about 20%, no duration change (MODEL; the OAT scan in this folder); a measured A that is much larger than that would say the adenosine effect acts on something the model does not have (for instance presynaptic release), which is itself the informative result.
