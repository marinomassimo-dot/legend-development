# Ketamine: possible positive side effects and indirect positive effects, a model-level analysis for the meeting (SCI, 2026-11-17; operator directive relayed by ADV; non-canonical; hypothesis level; mechanism level; no dose, no schedule, no recommendation for or against any use; held files only, no retrieval, no delegate; not medical advice; reference genotype class only)

Written for: ADV (assigned it), the meeting. **Scope statement.** The question is posed in the abstract: a single exposure to an NMDAR pore blocker, given within a sedation episode, followed by a transient improvement lasting weeks. No individual, case detail or testimony is recorded here and none was used. Labels: HELD (a source in the repository), KNOWN-GENERAL (textbook-level knowledge **not** held in the repository and not re-checked here), ASSUMED, INFERRED, MODEL (a hypothesis generator, not evidence). **Nothing here is evidence about any child.** The held models describe a neuron-specific Wwox knockout slice, not an unspecified neurological condition.

## Answer first
1. **Within my model the effect disappears with the drug.** The NMDAR pore block enters as a fall of one weight (J_N); at x0.85 the held oscillator gives burst duration -54% and burst interval +55% (R17a/R17c, MODEL, fitted to neocortical slice baseline, concentration-to-weight mapping not identified). When the weight returns, so does the behaviour. Every state variable the oscillator has relaxes in seconds (resource 6 s, chloride 17.8 s, adenosine 11.5 s, T-type availability 15.7 s, NMDAR variable 0.1 s); there is **no** homeostatic gain, E/I set point, plasticity or maturation variable. So the model cannot carry an effect that outlasts the drug by weeks, and that is a property of its structure, not a finding about biology.
2. **A weeks-long effect needs a slow variable the model does not have.** Four candidate structures are in section 2; **none is held**, all are ASSUMED; the nearest held observation is a washout overshoot (about 1.85 times baseline bursting after an NMDAR tool block in the slice), which points the opposite way from a lasting benefit.
3. **The rival explanations are many and most are more parsimonious than the drug for a weeks-long course** (section 3): illness resolution, regression to a pre-illness baseline, sleep and rest, seizure-burden change, medication changes (including deprescribing) and reporting effects all act on a weeks timescale without needing any lasting drug action. The drug's own lasting-action candidates (plasticity programmes, KNOWN-GENERAL for adults, and the I9 maturation idea) are hypotheses with no WWOX or paediatric support in the repository.
4. **Indirect positive effects the held files carry** are all tier 3 or tool-level (section 4). **The harms are also held** and must sit beside them: a developing-brain apoptosis datum, a human harm signal for a competitive antagonist, and the maturation cost of block.

## 1. What the model does with an NMDAR pore block, and whether it can remember
* **Registered values (HELD, `DOSSIER_I1_REBOUND_OSCILLATOR_SCI.md`; `PILOT_ARM_EXPECTATIONS_SCI.md`; transfer table R17a/R17c).** J_N x0.85: interval +55%, duration -54%; J_N x0.8 in the S-KO arm: incidence 0.99, bursts -46%, median duration 0.76 to 0.30 s; the S-HT arm falls from incidence 0.17 to 0.04 and S-WT from 0.11 to 0.01. Duration falls before incidence. Size is per fractional weight change; the mapping from a concentration of a use-dependent open-channel blocker to a weight is not identified (the block depends on activity and voltage, so a fixed J_N factor is a simplification).
* **I3 (compensation asymmetry, HELD dossier).** The hippocampus does not burst although it loses more Wwox; one candidate route is a lost NMDAR sustainer (Grin2b to 0.28 of control in the S-line hippocampus). That statement is about regional baselines, not about drug response, and the routes are not separated by held data.
* **I7 (critical fraction, IT dossier).** Pore blockers act on the current r directly with a narrow margin (a critical fraction of the NMDAR current below which bursts cannot be sustained); MODEL.
* **Slow variables in the model:** resource r, chloride c, adenosine a, T-type availability h, all with time constants of 6 to 18 s (BASE in `rebound_oscillator.py`). The burst interval (median about 26 s) is set by them. **Memory test by construction:** every held run starts from the fully rested state (u 0, r 1, c at rest, a 0, h 1), which is exactly the state a network is in a few time constants after any block ends; from that state the S-KO model returns to incidence 1.00 in 600 s. So in the held runs there is no hysteresis: recovery from a rested state leads back to baseline bursting. I did not run an up-and-down sweep of J_N (the held cliff scans are one-directional); bistability of this oscillator is therefore untested, and I rate it 0.1 likely.
* **I9 (PV maturation, ADV).** m(t) is proposed but not built; ADV's own update v2 demoted its support (one hippocampal pool, P47T line not lower) to a pointer.

**What a weeks-long effect would need (structure; none held):**
| # | Structure | What would give the weeks | Held? | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | A slow homeostatic variable (synaptic scaling or set-point, hours to days) that integrates an activity deficit during the block | A reset set point that returns slowly. Note the sign: homeostatic scaling after a block usually raises excitability, the opposite of a benefit (the held 1.85-fold washout overshoot is the nearest observation) | no | ASSUMED |
| S2 | A maturation or plasticity state m(t) (I9; or a structural-synapse variable, days) advanced by a brief burst of plasticity and not reversed by the drug leaving | A ratchet: the step persists | no (proposed, not built) | ASSUMED; the adult-rodent and adult-depression literature on a lasting synaptic effect after a single exposure is KNOWN-GENERAL and not held |
| S3 | Bistability in a slow variable (two attractors, a separatrix) | A transient pushes the system into the non-bursting basin and it stays | no; the held cliffs are graded, not switch-like | ASSUMED; the up-and-down test above would check it for the present oscillator |
| S4 | A non-neuronal slow variable (inflammation, glia, metabolism) that the block moves | Weeks follow the resolution time of that variable | no | ASSUMED |
A model with S2 or S3 would predict an effect that outlasts the drug only if the change in state is itself what produces the benefit; S1 predicts the opposite sign first. Whether any of these exist in a Wwox or other genetically defined brain is unmeasured.

## 2. Pharmacology facts that bound the question (KNOWN-GENERAL; not held; not re-checked)
Ketamine is a use-dependent open-channel NMDAR blocker with a short elimination half-life (hours) and active metabolites; its effects on mood in adults after a single sub-anaesthetic exposure are reported to last about a week; it has been used in refractory status epilepticus; and it has dissociative and sympathomimetic effects and an anticholinergic co-medication is often given with it to reduce secretions. **These are recollections to be verified before any use**; nothing in the repository's held files confirms them. The only held ketamine facts are that mouse Wwox in vivo recordings were taken under ketamine/xylazine (Repudi 2021, held; an NMDAR-antagonist caveat the repository already recorded) and one case of an infant with ATP1A2-related DEE who had ketamine then memantine, with timing not separable (IT baseline arm, not WWOX).

**What a registry or retrospective record could separate:** the order of events in time (onset before or after each exposure and each illness-resolution day); whether the same response occurs after episodes with the same drug and different co-factors; the magnitude relative to the person's documented baseline range; an effect-size comparison between those exposed and not exposed to ketamine within the same condition and the same admission type (confounding by indication is the first problem: sicker people receive ketamine more often). **What it cannot separate:** a combination, because two components that co-occur in every episode are not separable by any amount of records; and the unmeasured baseline variation of a single person. This is a limit, not an obstacle to be solved.

## 5. Meeting-ready judgements (labelled)
* JUDGEMENT 0.85: if a person has a documented transient improvement lasting weeks after an episode of sedation, a non-drug or non-NMDAR component (rows 3, 5, 6, 8, 9) explains at least part of it. It is a judgement from the table's timescales, not from the person's data.
* JUDGEMENT 0.15: a single anaesthetic-dose NMDAR block is the sole cause of an improvement that outlasts the drug by weeks. Without a structure S1 to S4 this is a hypothesis with no model support here.
* For the Wwox reference genotype class, a specific prediction from the held model: **if** a pore-block reduction of the sustainer shortens bursts by about half (E2), the effect on a bursting brain would be gone with the drug within the model's minutes-scale relaxation; a benefit lasting weeks therefore cannot come from the burst-suppression mechanism alone and would require an S2 or S3 structure.
* **What I would add to the open questions (named, not run):** (i) an up-and-down sweep of J_N in the oscillator to test for hysteresis (model-only, about ten minutes, prior 0.9 for no hysteresis); (ii) a slice experiment in the S-KO preparation: pore block for a fixed window, washout, then measure the burst rate for hours (asks whether any lasting change exists in the null slice at all); (iii) the retrospective-record items in section 3.
