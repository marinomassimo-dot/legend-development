## I19 proximity to threshold (ADV)
1. **The plateau is an incidence plateau, not a rate plateau.** 'Far past threshold, a modest shift changes nothing' holds for the yes/no incidence of a slice; clinical resistance is about the frequency of events, and in the oscillator the burst rate falls strongly at the null (A1: -68% bursts in bursting slices at S-KO; `RESULT_I19_CURVE_AND_RIVAL_I1_SCI.md`). The model supports 'incidence response is non-monotonic across genotype' (zero at S-KO, largest at S-HT, floor-limited at S-WT; predictions frozen first) and does not support 'resistance at the null' for rate.
2. **Distance changes magnitude, not sign.** In the model no arm aggravates at any genotype setting, and the aggravation counterpart (T-type weight x1.25) is inert (S-HT -0.02, S-WT +0.02). 'Benefit and aggravation both possible near threshold' needs a second variable that changes the sign of the net effect (a different component dominating in different children, or a regime dependence such as the strong steep rebound gate that made tonic-selective potentiation aggravate in 2 of 47 sets); distance alone yields larger and smaller effects of the same sign plus noise. Surprising prediction (1) (worsening proportion highest in intermediate genotypes) therefore needs that second variable stated.
3. **Held anchors and power.** The sealed I2 table holds zero seizure-worsening statements in 42 children (IT) and 17 children with specific semiology and outcome; D5a will tabulate a table that cannot test the variance claim (at least 8 children per class stated in D8; no class reaches it). Genotype severity class is confounded with onset age, variant domain and report source (Oliver 2023 has 83 rows with no outcome).
4. **Surprising prediction (3), 'same drug more effective on incidence in S-HT than in S-KO'** is implied by any logistic indicator; it cannot discriminate I19 from a trivial saturation. The discriminating prediction is the rate view: equal relative rate change at S-KO and S-HT (frozen D4 in my curve; holds).
5. **What I would keep:** the stratification advice (D6) and the S-HT arm of the pilot; it is the cheapest way to read the slope of the incidence curve.

## I17 onset classes (IT)
1. **The species mapping is the weakest step.** D1(4) maps mouse P0 to P9 onto the human neonatal period. Human term birth corresponds to roughly rodent P7 to P10 for cortical development (ASSUMED, general developmental neuroscience; a source should be cited), so the human neonatal month corresponds to the mouse window around P7 to P14, which overlaps the held slice window (P13 to P17) at its younger end, not the 'before P9' window; the 'neonatal class has no mouse correlate' claim depends on a mapping that could be off by about a week either way.
2. **Onset ages of days (0.02 to 0.07 months) are four children from two reports (Oliver, Chong)**; a report that gives 'first day of life' or 'in the first days' converts to 0.02 to 0.07 months by an interpretive step; with n = 4 the class is a stratifier at 28 days (11 of 37 children before 1 month), not a separate circuit; ascertainment (severe neonatal cases are reported) and the shared consortium of two of the reports are alternatives.
3. **The first-look count mixes rows and children** ('1 resolved, 1 partial, 2 none (3 children)' are four rows for three children), and PAM rows are mostly polytherapy rows in the not-attributable class (attribution follows success: single-agent benefit 79% against 17%, my second reading of I2). It is labelled uninformative; I agree.
4. **D4 should use E_GABA minus resting potential, not starting chloride.** In immature neurons both the chloride level and the resting potential are depolarised; the sign of GABA-A depends on their difference (HE's attack on I6, accepted). In the oscillator the PAM sign flips weakly at a steady chloride of 12 mM or more (E_Cl at the model's -65 mV reference); with the held resting potentials the flip would be at 6.7 to 8.3 mM; so the claim 'PAM works worse in the neonatal class' is a prediction of the headroom model and is only weakly supported at network level (`RESULT_I19_CURVE_AND_RIVAL_I1_SCI.md`, section 3).
5. **Kept:** onset class as a stratifier at 28 days in the D5b extraction (free), and the slice experiment at P6 to P9 (perforated patch, isolated NMDAR current with ifenprodil).

## I18 PY proteome test (HE)
1. **Carrier definition.** PP.Y is a necessary motif for a class I WW ligand and far from sufficient: functional WW binding depends on flanking residues, accessibility in a disordered segment and phosphorylation (ASSUMED, general WW-domain knowledge). If a minority of the 118 carriers are real targets, a pooled median difference moves little: the primary statistic (difference of medians) is the wrong tool for a sparse-target hypothesis; a tail statistic (the share of carriers beyond a fold-change threshold against matched non-carriers) or a rank-sum on the absolute fold change would be more sensitive. HE's share view (23 of 118 carriers up with BH < 0.05, against 19.5% non-carriers: it is 19.5% against 15.3%) is already descriptive in that direction and shows nothing large.
2. **Opposite signs cancel.** The preregistered rule assumes one direction for all carriers (M+ or M-); HE's own I8 v2 says the sign may be motif-specific. A two-sided statistic would detect that. **I ran an unregistered dispersion check with the prediction written first** (`PREDICTION_I18_DISPERSION_BEFORE_RUN.md`, sha256 63e51c4ac078f4c2a17ff828d6356fef1e5880f6db68e3bb2f9c0e2875aef89f; `i18_dispersion_check.py`, on HE's parsed table and carrier list): carriers' median absolute deviation of log2 fold change over matched non-carriers' within intensity deciles: **Rd = 0.93, one-sided permutation p about 0.5** (0.525 and 0.537 in two runs with different shuffle order). So the table shows no excess spread among carriers: neither opposite-signed nor sparse effects are hidden in this table; INCONCLUSIVE stands as 'the table is not informative about this' and no stronger.
3. **Decoy logic.** PPxF and PPxA shift +0.0185 and +0.0157 against +0.0315 for PPxY (ratio 0.59 and 0.50): above or at the preregistered 'half'; with the secondary sets (LPxY +0.0383, p 0.020) the data cannot separate a PPxY-specific effect from a general rise of proline-rich proteins, which HE states. One more decoy would be informative: a motif of the same composition scrambled in order (PxPY, YPPx) as a sequence-composition control, and carriers restricted to proteins with the motif in a predicted disordered region.
4. **The endothelial context is the larger limit**, as HE writes: non-neural cells, partial silencing of unknown efficiency, WWOX not a row, biological n unknown, soluble-dominated table (13 membrane carriers of 118, 3 usable); the NEDD4 rise (+0.33, BH 0.016) was seen before the registration (disclosed); the SCN1A row at the 99.1st percentile of intensity is a plausible identification or parsing flag and HE rightly refuses to use it.
5. **Kept:** the preregistration, the matched-strata design and the disclosure of what was seen first; the next step should be the membrane-enriched proteome of the P47T arm (brief 13.5b), which is the only measurement that touches the channels.
