## Answer first
1. **No held number among the four prefers one model.** The rival (the frozen oscillator with J_T = 0, J_a = 0, kappa_c = 0: no trigger, no adenosine, no chloride dynamics; nine free parameters) fits all four held targets (loss 0.11): interval median 26.7 s (held 26.2), duration median 0.69 s (0.66), interval shape 1.48 (1.43 [1.11, 1.84]), duration shape 2.50 (2.45 [1.93, 3.11]). The two-stage model, refitted to the same four targets (sixteen free parameters; loss 0.15), also fits them: 24.8 s, 0.664 s, 1.60, 2.49. **The frozen two-stage fit missed the duration shape (5.2) only because shapes were not in its loss.** My prediction O1 (the rival would miss the duration shape, above 3.5) was wrong. **Both models miss the same held number:** duration Q1 0.19 s against the held 0.53 s (held Q3/Q1 = 2.0; both models give about 5): the held duration distribution has a narrower core than a gamma of shape 2.45; neither model reproduces it.
2. **A held-number difference exists only through the incidence gradient, and it is conditional.** With the held graded components mapped as before (rebound to J_T, excitatory amplitude, phasic inhibition), the two-stage model reproduces S-HT 0.2 and S-WT 0.13 incidence; the rival has no trigger to which the held rebound gradient can attach, so at the held excitatory and phasic gradients alone its baseline incidence is 0.8 at both S-HT and S-WT (held 0.17 and 0). This is a statement about my mapping (a rival with a graded drive term could do the same); it is not a held-number preference.
3. **The models disagree on DURATION responses, not on burst-rate responses.** At S-KO, 200 slices (noise floor: the replicated baseline differs by 3% (bursts) and 3% (duration) in the two-stage model and 2% and 6% in the rival at 200 slices; I read effects against a conservative +/-15%; at 60 slices the rival's null T-type arm moved duration by +48% and interval by +32%, so those 60-slice numbers are not used):
| arm | bursts: two-stage / rival | duration: two-stage / rival | interval: two-stage / rival | discriminates? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| neurosteroid-type (tonic x1.2, J_I x1.1) | -76% / -75% | -9% / **+29%** | +173% / +157% | duration sign (weak: +29% at 200 slices) |
| sodium, pyramidal (sigma x0.9) | -42% / -44% | +7% / **+52%** | +45% / +62% | **duration, beyond the noise floor** |
| sodium with interneuron share (sigma x0.9, J_I x0.7) | -8% / -27% | +17% / **+64%** | +11% / +36% | **duration (and bursts)** |
| resource x0.6 (the duration-only class) | +20% / +23% | +64% / +97% | -9% / -15% | no: both lengthen the duration, both shorten the interval slightly |
| T-type x0.75 | -15% / +9% | +1% / -7% | +9% / -6% | no: the rival's arm is null by construction; the two-stage effect is within the noise |
Arms where the two models AGREE (no information about the model; first pass, 60 slices): d-APV (bursts -93% / -80%), NMDAR pore (-52% / -26%, same sign, within a factor 2), AMPA (-34% / -42%), synaptic GABA-A (-32% / -30%), GluN2B NAM (null / null).
4. **Serial dependence (300 networks, 900 s): the statistic that the argument rests on does not discriminate; a neighbouring one does.** rho(duration, following interval): rival +0.24, two-stage refit +0.22 (frozen +0.145). My prediction (rival above +0.30) failed. rho(preceding interval, duration): **rival +0.37, two-stage refit +0.18, frozen +0.17**: the rival's burst is longer after a longer rest (the refractory variable sets both); this is the number an event list would decide (n bursts per slice and slice count of the held set: 119 bursts in 18 slices, about 6.6 per slice, so a per-slice estimate has a standard error of about 0.5 and the pooled estimate with slice as cluster about 0.1 (rough): a difference of 0.19 is at the edge of detectability even with the list).
