## 2. The burst-sequence test, no new data (`PREDICTIONS_BURST_CORR_FROZEN_BEFORE_RUN.md`, sha256 20741e1faa598af61a09f45926c5c2de027ede18aefcf1d4be7b42e6dbc2909b; `burst_correlation.py`, `burst_correlation.tsv`)
**The per-burst list is NOT held.** Breton 2021 gives the medians (duration 0.66 [0.53-1.06] s, interval 26.2 s, 2.29 [1.1-4.2] bursts per minute), the gamma shape parameters of Fig. 1D (**duration 2.45 [1.93-3.11] for 119 bursts; interval 1.43 [1.11-1.84] for 101 intervals; 18 slices, 9 animals**) and Supplementary Table S1 (group-level medians and PAC ranges, baseline and two drugs; `files/supplement/PMID34634460_r2/mmc1_text.txt`); no event times, no per-slice values. The marginal histograms of Fig. 1D carry no pairing. The only routes are the authors' event lists (an operator item with the Fig. 4 request: one request can name both) or the geometry of the example traces of Fig. 1A (a handful of bursts, underpowered). So the correlation cannot be computed from held data.
**What the model itself says (300 fitted networks of 900 s):** rho(duration, following interval) within network = **+0.135** (sd 0.20; pooled 0.160), rho(preceding interval, duration) = +0.131; all three frozen predictions hold (C1 +0.05 to +0.35, C2 0 to +0.30, C3 pooled minus within below 0.1). **The oscillator does NOT predict independence**: a longer burst depletes the synaptic resource, adenosine and the T-type-like availability, which recover over seconds to tens of seconds; the verbal two-stage statement is about which parameter controls which quantity (T-type weight: interval +92%, duration +5%), not about independence of the sequences. A one-stage threshold-with-refractoriness rival predicts a larger positive correlation (IT); the held data cannot compare them; a measured within-slice rho of about +0.1 to +0.15 would fit I1, one above about +0.4 would favour the rival (an estimate, not a frozen threshold; a threshold should be fixed with IT's rival simulated first).
**Correction to my I1 dossier (disclosure):** the held gamma shapes exist (above). My model's interval shape 1.60 lies inside the held interval [1.11-1.84]; **its duration shape 5.2 lies far outside the held [1.93-3.11]** (the model's duration distribution is too regular, CV 0.44 against the held 0.64): I wrote that I could not reproduce 2.45 from the quartiles; the better statement is that the model misses it.

P1 (suppression up to 9 mM) holds; P2 (near 1 at 11 and 12) holds; **P3 (aggravation above 1.25 at 13 and 14) fails: the aggravation is only +6 to +8%** because the network is at its burst ceiling (about 240 to 270 bursts per 600 s); P4 (the crossing sits at E_Cl = -65 mV, 11.4 mM) holds (the ratio crosses 1 between 11 and 12 mM). ## 4. Is the graded excitatory gradient e a step at the median? (ADV's paragraph)
In model terms e is a linear difference of the excitatory weights between knockout and control, e = 0.10 from HE's medians (S-KO 15.32 against S-WT 13.86 pA); S-HT at f = 0.92 means the heterozygote carries about 0.92 of that difference, i.e. **near the controls, a step at the median** (the held KS tests, all three pairs significant, say the distributions differ in the tails; a median does not carry that). It does not change the held-size result: the held-size runs used e = 0.06, 0.1 and 0.2 at S-HT positions 0.9 and 0.5 for all components together; the e = 0.10 rows at 0.9 are inside the binomial windows (sigma 0.05: S-HT 0.15, S-WT 0.05; sigma 0.10: 0.20 and 0.10), and the component-specific setting used here (rebound at its held 0.69, e at 0.92, phasic at 0.9) gives S-HT 0.23, S-WT 0.14, also inside. The result is more sensitive to the unmeasured S-HT sIPSC position: phasic at 0.7 puts S-HT at 0.44, just outside the window (39%).
