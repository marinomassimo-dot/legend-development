## Answer first
1. **The same chronic-seizure state gives two different pictures depending on whether the tissue is bulk or sorted for neurons.** In **bulk hippocampus**, two systems reproduce most of the S-line panel with the S-line sign: rat pilocarpine at 84 days (GSE50079; 8 of the 9 genes readable in both share the sign; Gabrd -1.01 [-1.33, -0.74], Gfap +1.55, Slc12a5 -1.05) and mouse intra-amygdala kainate at 14 days (GSE319769; read as secondary, it fails the rule's 21-day criterion; 10 of 13 readable genes share the sign; Gabrd -0.40 [-0.64, -0.17], Gfap +3.02, Pvalb -1.14). In **sorted neuronal nuclei at 3 months (the rule-qualified primary, GSE330624, mouse)**, nearly nothing in the panel moves: Gabrd -0.35 [-0.68, -0.00] (below the 0.4 readability threshold), Npy +0.21, Sst -0.02; only Gja1 in the glial fraction is readable and shares the sign (-0.89 against -0.82).
2. **What this does and does not say.** The S-line values are themselves bulk tissue (HELD, GSE158681), so a bulk comparison cannot tell a per-neuron change from a change in what the tissue is made of; the P47T composition test already found that the Gabrd fall tracks the reactivity axis at animal level (HELD, `RESULT_GABRD_COMPOSITION_SCI.md`). The new fact is the **upper reference for a per-neuron seizure effect**: in neurons sorted from epileptic hippocampus, Gabrd falls by about 0.35 log2 units (CI reaching 0), against -1.67 in the S-line bulk. So a seizure state accounts for a bulk Gabrd fall of the size seen in the seizure models (-0.4 to -1.0), and does not by itself account for the -1.67 in the S-line bulk; but the S-line is a P17 conditional neuronal deletion, a different system from adult seizure models, so this is a transfer test, not a decomposition. The three readings stay open: (a) composition and reactivity in bulk (a seizure consequence); (b) a per-neuron effect of the genotype that seizures do not produce; (c) age, since seizure models and P17 lines differ in age by weeks to months.
3. **The registered criterion was met formally in all three and informs only in the bulk ones.** The pre-registered count criterion (at least half of the genes readable in both share the S-line sign; probability I gave it: 0.55) is met: primary 1 of 1 (Gja1), rat 8 of 9, mouse 14 d 10 of 13. On the primary it rests on one gene and is **uninformative**; I report it as met and not as support. The pre-registered statement "a majority with the S-line sign, including Gabrd, says the axis is what a chronic seizure state does" holds for the two bulk series; for the sorted neuronal series the sign agrees (-0.35) and the size does not, which is the reading in point 2.
4. **Ledger L10a (value: Gabrd log2 fold, chronic against control, NeuN+ fraction, GSE330624): -0.35, bin negative (my probability 0.55; naive baseline 0.8 on the same bin).** Locator: `cause_or_consequence_GSE330624.tsv`, row Gabrd, column C_log2. The sign bin is scored as observed; the value is below the 0.4 readability threshold and its interval touches 0, which I state beside the score.

## Limits stated
n = 4 per group (each sample pools two animals) in the primary, 6 per group in the mouse 14-day series, 3 per group in the rat; bootstrap intervals are wide and single-gene calls weak. GSE319769 fails the 21-day criterion and is secondary by the registered rule; its 3 and 7 day reads (14 of 18 and 13 of 14 same-sign genes) are secondary too. GSE50079: a rat transcript table on an older annotation; **7 of 26 genes could not be mapped (Gabra5, Npy, Npy2r, Cacna1i, Kcnq2, Adora1, Kcnj10)**, so Npy and the neuropeptide arm are absent from the second system; the platform is a 36-cycle reader, normalisation by CPM. The primary is nuclear RNA from sorted nuclei: not comparable one-to-one with the S-line's whole-tissue TPM. Different species, models (intracortical kainate, intra-amygdala kainate, pilocarpine), ages and decades; no time-matched series exists, so time, species and fraction are confounded across the three. A change in Gabrd mRNA in bulk says nothing about receptor protein or tonic current.
