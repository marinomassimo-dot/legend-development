# GABA-A potentiation against starting chloride in the oscillator (request from IT's I17 D4; predictions before the run; SCI; MODEL)
Design: the fitted frozen oscillator with steady chloride c0 in {6, 9, 11, 12, 13, 14} mM, potentiation pam 0 and 0.3 (combined tonic and phasic, load not coupled), 40 networks of 600 s per cell, same seeds for the pair; rate ratio R = rate(pam 0.3)/rate(pam 0).
Model fact used: the oscillator's relative GABA-A driving force G is referenced to a resting potential of -65 mV (frozen; the held resting potentials are -79.25 and -73.62 mV, so the model's crossing sits at 11.4 mM and the held one would sit at 6.7 to 8.3 mM; disclosed).
Predictions: P1: R < 1 (suppression) for c0 up to 9 mM. P2: R is near 1 (within 0.8 to 1.25) or undefined at c0 = 11 and 12 mM, where G is close to zero and the network is already at or beyond its burst ceiling. P3: R > 1.25 (aggravation) at c0 = 13 and 14 mM, where G < 0 (depolarising GABA-A) so potentiation adds excitation. P4: the sign change is a property of the reference potential, not of the oscillator's dynamics: the crossing c0 equals the chloride at which E_Cl = -65 mV (11.4 mM) within 1.5 mM.
What it says for I17: a neonatal-like starting chloride flips the PAM sign in the network model only when E_Cl passes the model's resting reference; with the held resting potentials the flip would occur at lower chloride (6.7 to 8.3 mM), which the frozen model cannot show.
