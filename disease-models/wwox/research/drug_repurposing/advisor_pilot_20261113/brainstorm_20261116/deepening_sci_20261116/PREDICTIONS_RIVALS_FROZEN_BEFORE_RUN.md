# Rival graded components for the S-HT incidence test: predictions written BEFORE the run (SCI, 2026-11-16; ADV's request; hypothesis generator, not evidence)
Same design as `het_incidence_test.py` (frozen I1 model unchanged): genotype axis lam 0 (S-KO) to 1 (S-WT), S-HT at lam 0.5; rebound weight J_T x (1 - 0.48 lam) and excitatory weights J_A, J_N x (1 - 0.2 lam) (e = 0.2) stay graded in every variant; the tonic weight is NOT graded; ONE rival component is graded instead; slice variability sigma 0.05 and 0.10; 100 slices per genotype and setting; a slice bursts if it has at least 1 burst in 600 s. Held targets: S-KO 36/42 (86%), S-HT 4/23 (17%), S-WT 0/11 (0%). Success: S-KO >= 70%, S-HT 5-35%, S-WT 0-10% (knockout window relaxed to >= 70% because the model's knockout is about 100%; disclosed). Reported per component: the smallest scanned fractional change t that succeeds (at both sigma), and S-HT/S-WT incidence there.
Components and scanned t (WT value = KO value x (1 + t) unless stated):
 (i) phasic inhibition J_I x (1 + t lam), t in {0.3, 0.6, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0}. Held for S-HT (Breton 2021 Fig. 4 legend): sIPSC amplitude and frequency distributions differ between S-HT and S-KO (KS p 2.5e-69 and 4.6e-5) and between S-WT and S-KO (1.8e-100 and 6.2e-8); no S-HT against S-WT test and no mean values are in the text, so S-HT sits at or near S-WT (lam about 1, not 0.5): J_I is run with S-HT at lam 0.5 AND at lam 0.9.
 (ii) NMDAR sustainer: J_N additionally x (1 - t lam), t in {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4}. Held: mEPSC amplitude graded in all three pairs (KS p 2.0e-4 S-WT v S-HT, 5.0e-7 S-WT v S-KO, 1.3e-6 S-HT v S-KO); mEPSC decay time unchanged S-WT 5.18 v S-KO 4.84 ms (no overt change of the NMDAR:AMPA proportion).
 (iii) chloride loading as a graded steady [Cl]i: c0 = 6 mM x (1 - t lam), t in {0.1, 0.2, 0.3, 0.4} (the wild type has the lower chloride; the relative driving force G is referenced to 6 mM so lower c0 raises both tonic and phasic inhibition).
 (iv) PV-timing delay: the model has no delay parameter (inhibition follows activity with the activity time constant only); NOT run, none added.
Predictions (signs and ranges):
Q1. J_I (i): phasic inhibition is a weaker knob than the tonic weight in the oscillator (one-at-a-time scan: +20%: rate -37%; tonic +20%: -77%); success needs t >= 1.0 (WT J_I at least twice the KO value) or fails within the scan; at S-HT lam 0.5 the S-HT incidence falls in 5-35% only for t >= 1.5.
Q2. J_N (ii): success at t of 0.2 to 0.3 (as steep as tonic or steeper: J_N -40% alone nearly silences the network).
Q3. Chloride (iii): success at t of 0.1 to 0.2 (E_Cl moves with the logarithm and the driving force is small; G is extremely sensitive to c0).
Q4. Therefore the incidence criterion does NOT single out tonic inhibition: three steep knobs (tonic, NMDAR sustainer, steady chloride) each succeed with a fractional change inside what the held data allow; it separates only the weak knob (phasic J_I) from the steep ones.
Q5. Whether S-HT at lam 0.9 for J_I lands in 5-35%: no (above 35%) for any t scanned (an S-HT near wild type bursts too rarely or too often? it bursts at 0-10% when WT is silent); statement: with S-HT near WT the 17% needs lam about 0.7 to 0.8.
What each outcome changes: Q4 true -> the I13 incidence sentence must read 'a steep graded inhibitory or excitatory component, tonic one candidate'; Q1 false (J_I succeeds at t <= 1.0 with held-allowed size) -> the graded sIPSC deficit alone could explain the incidence and I13's S-HT arm loses its decisive status; only tonic succeeding -> a model-level discrimination.
