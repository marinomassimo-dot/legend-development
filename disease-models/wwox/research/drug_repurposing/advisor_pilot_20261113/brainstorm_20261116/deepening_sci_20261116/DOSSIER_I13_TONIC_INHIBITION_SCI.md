# I13 TONIC (EXTRASYNAPTIC) INHIBITION DEFICIT: is the null neocortex a network that lost its tonic brake? (dossier v1, SCI, 2026-11-16; hypothesis, not a finding; mechanism-level, no dose, no recommendation; not medical advice; reference genotype class only)

Labels: KNOWN (source), HELD (table or full text in the repository, file named), ASSUMED, INFERRED, MODEL (a script output in this folder, a hypothesis generator, not evidence). ## Answer first
* **The idea.** Wwox loss lowers the delta-containing, extrasynaptic GABA-A receptors that give a cell its tonic inhibition; in neocortex, where delta is the dominant tonic subunit, the network then sits nearer its burst threshold; in hippocampus, which depends on the alpha-5 subunit (kept) and has little delta, it does not. The decisive measurement is the **tonic GABA-A current** (a standard slice measurement) in null against wild-type neocortex and null hippocampus.
* **Three held observations in its favour, one independent of the data that suggested it.**
  1. Gabrd (delta) transcript falls to **0.43 of control in null neocortex** (S line, 83 to 36 TPM) and 0.31 in hippocampus (8.5 to 2.6 TPM, small base); Gabra5 (alpha-5) is **kept in hippocampus at 0.69 and not lowered in cortex (1.16)**; the delta subunit is 10-fold more abundant in cortical than hippocampal controls and alpha-5 6-fold more abundant in hippocampal than cortical controls (HELD, `regional_baseline.tsv`, GSE158681 and GSE158682, n 2 to 4, bulk).
  2. **The same direction in the independent P47T line**, which keeps Wwox mRNA (Wwox log2 fold -0.13 to +0.03): Gabrd log2 fold **-0.82 in prefrontal cortex (FDR 0.09), -0.80 in cortex (FDR 0.06), -0.93 in hippocampus (FDR 0.34), +0.08 in cerebellum** (5 v 5; HELD, `wwox_data_it24_p4_20261104/results/p47t_all_regions_tested_genes.tsv.gz`). The cerebellum, with the highest delta level of all (9,868 normalised counts), is the one region without a fall (no cerebellar burst phenotype is recorded in the held material; it was not looked for). The FDR values given are the `FDR_no_sex` column of the table; `FDR_with_sex` is 0.122 (PFC), 0.0998 (CTX) and 0.447 (HPC). No gene-level FDR passes 0.05 for Gabrd in either line; the direction and size agree in two lines and it is not a multiple-testing-proof finding.
  3. **The held intrinsic profile is compatible** (input resistance +39%, resting potential depolarised, in both heterozygotes and knockouts: Breton 2021 Table 1) with a loss of resting conductance. *But see the counter-evidence: the single-compartment model says tonic loss is not the best explanation of that profile.*
* **The model's statement (MODEL, `oat_scan.tsv`).** The tonic GABA-A weight is the most sensitive knob of the fitted burst network: +20% cuts the burst rate by 77%, +40% silences it, -40% raises it 4.3-fold; the equally small change of the synaptic (phasic) weight acts about half as strongly (J_I +20%: rate -37%). So *if* the null neocortex has lost about half its delta-type tonic conductance (Gabrd 0.43), the network model puts it on the bursting side of a threshold. Potentiating the remaining tonic conductance should suppress bursts more than potentiating the synaptic receptors at the same fractional change.
* **Counter-evidence found while writing it (kept in the open).**
  (i) A single-compartment conductance model tuned only to the wild-type row (`intrinsic_profile_model.py`, MODEL) reproduces the knockout profile (resting potential, input resistance, sag, rebound) with a ~30% loss of the **resting K+ leak** (RMP -74.8 mV, Rin 150 MOhm, sag 2.05, rebound 1.78 against held -73.6, 143, 2.57, 1.86); a loss of tonic GABA-A conductance moves the resting potential by 0.05 mV only because its reversal (about -80 mV, ASSUMED from slice chloride) sits near rest, and raises the input resistance by 4 to 8%. So the held profile does not require, and weakly disfavours, tonic loss as its cause (the conductance size is unidentified in the model: a larger tonic share would raise the effect on input resistance but not on the resting potential).
  (ii) **Heterozygotes burst in 17% of slices (4 of 23) against 86% in knockouts (36 of 42) and 0% in wild type (0 of 11)** (HELD, Breton 2021 Results), while carrying the same step profile as knockouts (resting potential, input resistance, sag, AP amplitude): the step features cannot set incidence, so a graded quantity is needed; a tonic current intermediate in heterozygotes would fit, one equal to the knockout's would not; the Gabrd level in heterozygotes is not held. (A first version of this line said heterozygotes burst equally; that was a misreading, corrected.)
  (iii) The GABA transporters GAT-1 (Slc6a1) and GAT-3 (Slc6a11) transcripts are lower too (0.52 to 0.67 and 0.59 to 0.64 in the S line; P47T cortex -0.12 and -0.48 log2, ns): lower uptake raises ambient GABA, a compensation that could keep the tonic current normal despite fewer receptors (INFERRED). The tonic current, not the transcript, is the measurement.
  (iv) The human-spheroid time course (`maturation_slopes_circuit.tsv`, GSE150123, five lines, elapsed culture time) gives Gabrd almost no information (mean 0.84 counts per million; slope +0.39, 95% interval -0.5 to 1.28): a maturation lag cannot be tested for delta with this proxy; Gabra5 (+2.4 per log10 day), Gabra1 (+1.9) and Slc6a1 (+2.1) rise with time, so a lag would lower them, and in the null mouse they are kept or fall little (a point against a pure lag for the tonic subunits; a pointer only: human spheroids, elapsed time).
* **Depth reached: model; experiment-ready on the tonic-current readout. Repurposing logic below is at the mechanism-label level only.**

## D1 Statement and chain
1. Wwox loss lowers Gabrd transcript in neocortex (HELD, two lines); alpha-5 in hippocampus is kept (HELD, S line; P47T hippocampus Gabra5 log2 -0.19, ns).
2. Delta-containing receptors carry most of the tonic current of layer II/III neocortical neurons, alpha-5 of CA1 pyramidal cells (ASSUMED from general receptor physiology; not checked against a source here).
3. Fewer receptors lower the tonic conductance unless ambient GABA rises (INFERRED; GAT transcripts are lower, which would raise it).
4. A lower tonic conductance moves the network towards the burst threshold (MODEL: tonic weight -40% gives a 4.3-fold higher rate).
5. Hippocampus keeps its tonic subunit and has other protective differences (I3: sustainer, brake), so it stays below threshold.
6. Tonic potentiation (delta-preferring or neurosteroid-type) should push null neocortex back below threshold (MODEL: +20% weight, rate -77%).

## D2 Why non-obvious
The standard reading of "loss of inhibition" in this model is about PV interneurons and synaptic IPSCs (reduced sIPSC frequency and amplitude, a held result of Breton 2021). I13 says the *tonic* component, which is not an interneuron-firing quantity, is the sensitive one, and that it is silent in the usual synaptic assays (the sIPSC recordings used a 140 mM chloride pipette by design, HELD). It also says that GABA-A potentiation is not a single class: tonic-preferring and synaptic-preferring modulators act with different strength on the same network (MODEL).

## D3 Consequences across scales
* Cell: a gabazine-sensitive holding-current shift (the tonic current) smaller in null neocortical pyramids than in wild type and no smaller in null CA1; a smaller response to a delta-preferring agonist; the response to a GAT-1 blocker (a label) *smaller than wild type despite higher ambient GABA* if receptors are the limit.
* Slice: tonic potentiation lowers burst rate before it changes burst duration (MODEL: tonic x1.2 gives rate -77% with duration -10%); synaptic-only potentiation changes duration and rate together (J_I +40%: rate -59%, duration -30%).
* Animal: an age signature: delta expression rises over the weeks after P13 in rodents (ASSUMED, not checked) so the burst window might close as the tonic conductance matures, which would predict that the bursts disappear in older slices of null mice unless the deficit grows.
* Patient (observational): neurosteroid-type agents (tonic) and benzodiazepine-type agents (synaptic) should not have the same response profile; the I2 re-extraction of per-child outcomes carries the agent and the worsening columns for that comparison.
* **Surprising predictions.** (1) Raising ambient GABA with a transporter blocker barely changes the tonic current in null neocortex while it does in wild type. (2) A tonic-selective potentiator suppresses bursts at a fractional change at which a synaptic-selective one has little effect. (3) A wild-type slice with its tonic conductance lowered by roughly half (a delta-selective negative modulator, research tool) should begin to burst: wild type is nearer the threshold than the usual "no bursts in wild type" suggests (model: any null parameter set within a 40% change of the threshold; wild-type parameters are not fitted).

## Update v3 (SCI, after ADV's addendum and HE's I8 v3): where the resting-conductance step comes from, and what I would test first
HE's PPxY scan (I8 v3, as relayed by ADV): no PY-motif channel carries the resting-conductance group, and the K2P, Kir and Kv7 channels have no PPxY or LPxY motif, so the leak reading of the intrinsic profile (this dossier, intrinsic model) has no WW-domain mechanism behind it: the step needs another route. The candidates and the order in which I would test them with recordings that already exist or are planned (null, P47T, S-HT, wild type):
1. **Cell size (capacitance), first.** Free in any whole-cell recording (membrane test); input resistance +39% with tau unchanged is what a smaller cell does (MODEL: all conductances and C x0.72 gives Rin x1.39, tau unchanged). It explains the passive features and not the resting potential; if capacitance is lower in null and S-HT the step is at least partly size, which would point at maturation (I9) rather than at channels.
2. **Transcriptional or maturational effect on a leak channel.** Kcnk2 (TREK-1) is lower in P47T cortex (log2 -0.45, ADV's pointer; FDR 0.17/0.19) and in S-line tables (to be added to `regional_baseline.py`); a transcriptional or developmental effect needs no WW-domain binding. Test: the same recordings at two ages inside P13 to P17 (the step should grow with age if it is maturational), and a K2P-channel pharmacology step (a mechanism label) in null against wild type: a smaller K2P-sensitive current identifies the leak.
3. **An indirect effect** (ambient neuromodulator or ambient adenosine/GABA acting on a leak conductance): test by the adenosine and tonic-GABA steps already in the design (I4 addendum, this dossier D5b): a difference that vanishes under an enzyme or an antagonist is indirect.
4. **A non-canonical ligand** (a WW-domain interaction with a non-PPxY motif): last, because it needs new binding work, not existing recordings.
