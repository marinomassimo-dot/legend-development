# I6 CHLORIDE HEADROOM: does GABA-A turn from brake to fuel inside the burst? (dossier v1, SCI, 2026-11-16; hypothesis, not a finding; mechanism-level; not medical advice; reference genotype class only)

Written for: ADV (cross-reading), HE and IT (attackers), the operator (five-minute reading of the first block). Labels: KNOWN (source), HELD (table or full text in the repository), ASSUMED, INFERRED. The model is a hypothesis generator, not evidence.

## Answer first
* **The idea.** Chloride is not a slow variable that sets the 26-second interval; it is a *headroom* variable that decides what GABA-A does *during* the 0.66-second burst. Extrusion (KCC2, a time constant of seconds to tens of seconds, ASSUMED) is too slow to act inside a burst, so inside the burst the chloride level at burst onset, set by the KCC2/NKCC1 balance, is the only thing that matters. Where headroom is small, an extra GABA-A load (a positive modulator) moves E_Cl past the resting potential before the burst ends, and the same drug that brakes in one cell fuels in another.
* **What the small model says (script `chloride_headroom.py`, 240 rows, no fitted parameters, deterministic).** With the steady chloride at 4 or 6 mM and a per-burst rise of 2 mM, GABA-A stays hyperpolarising to the end of the burst even with a threefold potentiation; at 10 mM steady chloride the same burst crosses E_Cl = resting potential 0.61 s into the burst with a half-fold potentiation and 0.26 s with a threefold one (c_eq 10 mM, per-burst rise 2 mM, tau_c 15 s). So the sign of a GABA-A positive modulator is a function of two numbers nobody has measured in this model: steady intracellular chloride and the chloride load of one burst.
* **A held number points at the headroom.** KCC2 transcript (Slc12a5) falls to 0.64 of control in neocortex and 0.68 in hippocampus; NKCC1 (Slc12a2) falls to 0.70 and 0.45 (HELD, `regional_baseline.tsv`; two to four animals per group, transcript level). A KCC2-to-NKCC1 ratio index moves 0.91 in neocortex and 1.51 in hippocampus (INFERRED arithmetic): lower loading capacity relative to extrusion in hippocampus, none in neocortex. That is the I3 asymmetry seen from the chloride side.
* **A held number the idea must live with.** The whole burst reverses at about -31 mV in whole-cell voltage clamp (Breton 2021 Fig. 3, 30 bursts, 5 animals, HELD): excitatory and inhibitory conductances both flow. The held recordings never measured E_GABA (no gramicidin perforated patch; the sIPSC recordings used 140 mM chloride in the pipette by design, HELD in the methods). So the single measurement that decides the idea is absent from the repository.
* **Depth reached: model.** Next: experiment-ready (D5b below). Not repurposing-ready: the intervention logic is mechanism-level and every agent named carries its flag.

## D1 Statement and chain
1. Wwox loss makes neocortical neurons depolarised with more sag and more post-inhibitory rebound (KNOWN/HELD: Breton 2021 abstract and Fig. 2).
2. In a burst both inhibitory and excitatory conductances flow (HELD: reversal -31 mV).
3. GABA-A flow loads chloride; the load per burst is unknown (ASSUMED 0.5 to 8 mM scanned).
4. Extrusion by KCC2 acts on a time scale (seconds to tens of seconds, ASSUMED) far longer than the burst (0.66 s, HELD), so within a burst the level at onset sets how much driving force is left.
5. KCC2 transcript is lower in both regions and NKCC1 lower, more so in hippocampus (HELD); transcripts are not transport function (ASSUMED relation).
6. When E_Cl passes the resting potential before the burst ends, the inhibitory arm of the burst becomes depolarising late in the burst (INFERRED from 1-5): inhibition fails at the moment it is most needed.
7. GABA-A positive modulation raises the load in proportion to the potentiation, so it shifts the crossing earlier: a dose-independent sign problem at the mechanism level (no dose is proposed).

## D2 Why non-obvious
The standard reading treats the KCC2 question as one about a *resting* E_GABA (immature-like depolarising GABA in epileptic encephalopathy) and a KCC2 enhancer as the obvious corrective. Here the resting value is secondary; the burst-time *change* of E_GABA relative to a resting potential that is itself depolarised (HELD) is what matters, and a KCC2 enhancer could do little inside a burst because its action is slow (model: the within-burst result barely moves between tau_c 5 s and 15 s). Its action is on the headroom (steady level), the quantity that sets the crossing (model: crossing time 0.61 s at 10 mM steady, no crossing at 8 mM). The two models are complementary, not the same claim: in the I1 oscillator (corrected) chloride accumulates over the slow recovery and acts on the *interval* (extrusion three times slower: rate four-fold, interval -68%; duration unchanged), because there the chloride rise is a slow variable; this headroom model asks what the same chloride does *inside* a burst, which I1 does not resolve. Both say the same thing about the direction: weaker extrusion aggravates, stronger extrusion helps little (I1: x0.35 lengthens the interval 29%).

## D3 Consequences across scales
* Cell: gramicidin-perforated recordings in a layer II/III pyramidal cell should show E_GABA at burst onset closer to rest in the null than in wild type, and a further shift of several millivolts by the end of a burst. Both halves are testable and each can fail.
* Slice: GABA-A positive modulation changes the *shape* of the burst (late-burst inhibition fails: a longer or higher-amplitude late component) before it changes the rate. KCC2 or NKCC1 modulation changes burst shape the same way in the opposite direction.
* Animal: a modulator that lowers burst rate in wild-type-like regions could raise it in null neocortex; the response should depend on age through the KCC2 maturation curve (the bursts are at P13 to P17).
* Patient (observational): response to GABA-A positive modulators should split by semiology and age (I2 data) and not only by genotype; a record of worsening on such agents is the signal.
* **Surprising prediction (nobody expects it from the standard view):** two slices with the *same* resting E_GABA but different chloride load per burst respond to a positive modulator with opposite signs. Neither a resting-potential measurement nor a KCC2 immunostain would tell them apart; only a within-burst E_GABA trace does.

## D4 Model
`chloride_headroom.py` (one compartment; Nernst E_Cl; chloride influx proportional to the driving force of the burst-time membrane potential, -55 mV ASSUMED; first-order extrusion; burst every 26.2 s HELD; seeded not needed, deterministic). The constants are ASSUMED and scanned; no fit; a model that has no fitted parameters cannot be right or wrong about the cell, it states which two numbers matter. Parameters in: c_eq 4 to 10 mM, tau_c 5 to 60 s, per-burst rise 0.5 to 8 mM, potentiation 0 to 2. Output: `chloride_headroom.tsv`. A one-line physical note: influx stops when E_Cl reaches the burst-time membrane potential, so loading alone cannot make E_Cl pass the burst potential; what it can do is pass the *resting* potential, which is the relevant crossing for a cell that sits at -65 mV between bursts.
Link to I1: in the corrected I1 oscillator chloride enters G(c) = relative GABA-A driving force; the result is above (interval, not duration). A post-hoc test coupling the chloride load to the potentiation did not make a GABA-A potentiator raise the burst rate in 18 retained parameter sets (of 300 drawn); so the within-burst sign flip of this dossier is a prediction of the small headroom model only and is not reproduced by the network model.

(b) **One slice experiment.** Perforated-patch E_GABA at burst onset and end in null versus wild-type neocortex and null hippocampus; a positive modulator (label) and an NKCC1 blocker (label) applied after baseline. Outcomes: (1) E_GABA closer to rest in null cortex, shifting further in burst, modulator raises burst duration or rate: the idea holds; (2) E_GABA no different from wild type and the modulator suppresses bursts: killed *for chloride as the cause of the sign problem* (this is the named test); (3) E_GABA shifted but modulator suppresses: the crossing happens but does not matter at the network level; the rebound term of I1 dominates.

## D7 Combination
With I1: chloride sets the *late-burst* inhibitory efficacy and therefore duration and shape; the T-type trigger sets the interval (the I1 dossier). With I3: the hippocampal KCC2-to-NKCC1 index (1.51 against 0.91) is a candidate part of why the hippocampus does not burst (it keeps headroom). With I4: adenosine and energy: chloride extrusion is an ATP-dependent process, so a metabolic limit shortens headroom. Contradiction to settle by experiment: I1 says GABA-A potentiation suppresses bursts in the fitted regime (and raises them only for tonic-selective potentiation with a strong steep rebound gate, 2 of 47 sets); I6 says it can fuel them where headroom is small, but the network model did not reproduce that with a coupled load; the settling experiment is D5b (outcomes 1 to 3).

## D8 Stronger / dead
Stronger: a measured E_GABA at burst end more positive than at onset by at least the amount in the table (2 to 4 mV per mM); a modulator that raises burst duration before it changes rate; a protein-level transporter dataset. Killed by: perforated-patch E_GABA equal to wild type at onset and end with a modulator that suppresses bursts (D5b outcome 2). Parked-with-trigger: none; it stays ADVANCE.

## Update v3 (SCI, after HE's attack `attack_i6/ATTACK_I6_HE.md`; accepted, two points)
1. **The reference resting potential was wrong for the held cells.** The headroom model compared E_Cl with -65 mV (ASSUMED). The held layer II/III resting potentials are -79.25 mV (S-WT) and -73.62 mV (S-KO) (Breton 2021 Table 1). E_Cl equals these at an intracellular chloride of **6.68 mM (S-WT) and 8.25 mM (S-KO)** at 130 mM outside (recomputed here from the Nernst equation, -26.7 mV x ln ratio; HE's 6.7 and 8.3 agree), not at 11.4 mM as with -65 mV. Consequences, rerun (`chloride_headroom.py run <out> <V_rest>`, outputs `chloride_headroom_vrest_-79.25.tsv` and `chloride_headroom_vrest_-73.62.tsv`; steady chloride 4 to 10 mM, per-burst rise 0.5 to 8 mM, extrusion 5 to 60 s): (a) with the wild-type rest, GABA-A passes the resting potential inside a burst for steady chloride 6 mM when the per-burst rise is 1 mM or more, and for 8 mM at any rise scanned; (b) with the knockout rest it needs a per-burst rise of 2 mM at 6 mM steady chloride (0.5 potentiation) and any rise at 8 mM; (c) **at equal chloride the depolarised null is about 1.6 mM farther from the sign flip than wild type**, so the "drug fuels in one cell, brakes in another" prediction needs the null's intracellular chloride to be at least about 1.6 mM higher than wild type (HE's falsifiable condition, accepted). The earlier statement that crossing needs 10 mM steady chloride was an artefact of the -65 mV reference and is withdrawn; the network model (I1) still uses -65 mV as the reference of its relative driving force (frozen with the harness; a limitation, not corrected).
2. **NKCC1 composition (HE).** Per HE's reading of the held class table, bulk Slc12a2 is an oligodendrocyte-lineage transcript (neurons 5.3, newly formed oligodendrocytes 100.1, myelinating 40.1, OPC 20.8 FPKM; I did not re-check the table) and falls where myelin genes fall; the hippocampal KCC2-to-NKCC1 index of 1.51 therefore reads composition, not a neuronal loader. Together with the P47T non-replication (Update v2) the asymmetry index is withdrawn as evidence.
3. **D5b is changed as HE proposes:** record E_GABA minus resting potential per cell at burst onset and at burst end (a per-cell difference), not E_GABA alone; the sign flip is the crossing of zero of that difference.
