# Hypotheses to set tests for, part 1

## M01. Slicing protocol sets the surviving interneuron fraction and therefore the knockout burst incidence

* **Statement (IPOTESI).** The burst incidence reported in Wwox-deficient neocortical slices depends on the slice preparation: a protective cutting solution that spares fast-spiking interneurons in older animals raises the surviving inhibitory fraction in both genotypes and lowers the knockout burst incidence, while a conventional sucrose cutting solution lowers it less, so part of the reported genotype difference is a preparation difference.
* **Why a leap:** the usual reading treats burst incidence as a property of the genotype; this reading treats the preparation as a modifier whose size is unknown.
* **Existing assay or held table:** the burst recordings in the slice preparation of the INV-05 package (burst incidence per slice and genotype); parvalbumin-positive counts on the same slices as a second readout.
* **Single discriminating observation:** knockout and wild-type slices from littermates cut in the two solutions on the same day, burst incidence per slice and PV-positive cell density in the recorded field. (a) Burst incidence differs by cutting solution within knockout and tracks PV density: the genotype difference is partly preparation-dependent and the L7 design needs the solution as a factor; (b) no difference by solution: the reported incidence is robust to this preparation choice.
* **Repurposing class (labels, source):** none; a methodological hypothesis about the readout.
* **Held-data pre-test:** none run; the held tables carry one preparation per paper.
* **What would change my mind:** knockout incidence identical across solutions with equal PV density in both.
* **Cost:** about 8 to 12 animals per genotype and solution, plus staining of the recorded slices.

## M02. Sodium-channel targets are absent at P6-P8 and present at P13-P15: "no link" at P6-P8 is "target not yet there", not a negative
* **Statement.** The held mouse neuron class (P7) reads the sodium-channel alpha subunits at low values and below non-neuronal classes (Scn1a 2.5 FPKM against 4.8 in OPC; Scn3a 8.6 against 11.3; Scn8a 6.2 against 8.9), while the held human cortical neuron class reads them neuron-enriched (SCN1A 18.5 against 3.8, 4.9-fold; SCN3A 13.1 against 3.0; SCN8A 12.1 against 5.5). So a use-dependent sodium-channel-blocker label (class label only) should reduce bursting in the Wwox-null slice at P13-P15 but little or not at P6-P8, the reverse of the NMDAR pattern (ifenprodil-sensitive share 53 percent at P6-P8, 14 percent at P13-P15). **Rejecting observation:** an equal burst-rate reduction at both ages, or none at either.
* **Why a leap.** The matrix reads absence of a thread as absence of a link. This drops that: absence of a thread at an age where the target is barely read is a measurement of the target, not of the class.
* **Existing assay or held table.** The INV-05 slice burst assay at its two ages (P6-P8, P13-P15); held tables: `he_leap_targets_mouse.tsv`, `he_leap_targets_human.tsv`, and the P7 and human rows of the SCI44/IT47 tables.
* **The discriminating observation and its two outcomes.** Burst rate in null slices with a use-dependent sodium-channel-blocker label against vehicle, at both ages. Age-gated effect: the sodium-channel class gains a P13-P15 cell and its rank differs between the age windows and the pooled ranking (the age-window ranking of the matrix becomes the primary view for this class). Equal or absent effect: nothing changes; the class stays at zero for want of a thread, and the age explanation is rejected.
* **Repurposing class (labels only).** Sodium-channel blockers: carbamazepine-type and lamotrigine-type (mechanism labels; source: standard mechanism of action, not a WWOX source).
* **Held-data pre-test.** The held tables cannot reject it: there is no later-age mouse neuron class, and species differ. Result: not rejected, not tested. No held table contradicts it.
* **What would change my mind.** A later-age mouse neuron RNA table showing Scn1a, Scn3a and Scn8a at P13-P15 neuron values at or below the P7 ones, or a held burst-assay result at P6-P8 that a sodium-channel blocker reduces.
* **Cost.** One table lookup (a later-age neuron class from a public RNA set, if one is obtainable) for the target part; one slice experiment at two ages with two arms for the effect.

## M03.

**Statement.** The tonic Gi-coupled adenosine A1 drive onto a K+ conductance is smaller in the knockout slice (for example through higher astrocytic adenosine kinase in the gliotic tissue, or less extracellular adenosine from an energy-handling bottleneck), which contributes to the depolarised rest, higher input resistance and burst susceptibility; this is rejected if an A1 antagonist shifts holding current, rest and input resistance by the same amount in knockout and control neurons.

**Why a leap.** The thread explains the lost resting conductance by a channel itself (Kir2, K2P, Kv7, KATP closed by lactate) or by size plus shunt, and keeps the metabolic arm at the intracellular ATP-sensitive channel. This reading drops the assumption that the missing current is a property of the neuron's own channel pool and instead places it in a missing tonic ligand from the surrounding tissue (the GPCR-gated K+ leak is the same Ba2+- and tertiapin-sensitive family, so the Ba2+ difference current would carry part of it). Neighbours: the J01 card lists adenosine kinase with astrogliosis as a literature lead for glial reactivity, and the ledger notes non-T-type routes of ethosuximide including GIRK inhibition (PMID 37275776); neither links adenosine tone to the Wwox rest, resistance or bursts.

**Existing assay or held table that can test it.** The Wwox slice recordings with the SCI43 difference-current ladder (WO-17, IT45-Q01); an A1-antagonist step and an adenosine-deaminase arm added in the same cells; a tertiapin-sensitive step to read the K+ component. Held table: `.../nb4_gse158684_glia_20261107/` and `.../gse158684_identity_it14_p1a_20261101/` (one pool per arm; Gfap rises 16 to 41 CPM per the [a session]-007 note) for Adk, Adora1, Kcnj3 and Kcnj6 counts; descriptive only.

**The single discriminating observation and its two outcomes.** The change in holding current, rest and input resistance caused by an A1 antagonist (and its reversal by adenosine deaminase) in knockout versus control cells at the same recording conditions. Outcome 1: the antagonist-sensitive shift is smaller in the knockout and the knockout rest and Rin move toward control values when the A1 drive is restored by an A1 agonist or reduced adenosine removal; the tone is the missing current. Outcome 2: the shifts are equal and restoring A1 drive does not move the knockout toward control; hypothesis rejected.

**Repurposing class and at most 2 labels.** Adenosine-system modulators that raise or mimic A1 drive (A1 agonists, adenosine kinase inhibitors; source: adenosine kinase and A1 tone in gliosis-associated epilepsy, outside knowledge); labels: N6-cyclopentyladenosine (tool agonist), 5-iodotubercidin (tool kinase inhibitor). Methylxanthine antagonists would be the opposite direction.

**Held-data pre-test.** No. No thread table holds adenosine, A1, ADK or GIRK counts for a Wwox system. The only indirect item is the snRNA rise of Gfap, which makes a reactive astrocyte state present in the pool; it neither supports nor rejects an ADK rise. Result: cannot be rejected from the folder; a held-pool lookup of Adk would be a possible first pre-test.

**What would change my mind.** An A1 antagonist that changes nothing in either genotype (slice adenosine tone absent at the recording temperature and flow); a knockout with a larger antagonist-sensitive shift; Syn1-Cre neuron deletion leaving astrocytic Adk at wild-type level in the P17 pools, which would remove the glial route though not a neuronal one.

**Cost to test.** About 16 to 20 animals in the same slice series as [another hypothesis]; 4 table lookups.

## M04. Statement: Wwox loss in oligodendrocyte-lineage cells (the CTGF/OPC threads) thins the myelin of PV+ interneuron axons, which are among the most myelinated, and a conduction-timing deficit in PV axons contributes to the reduced inhibitory output; the oligodendrocyte marker fall in the knockout single-nucleus pool (Plp1 392 to 136 CPM, a held table) is the first sign. Rejecting observation: PV-axon myelin markers (MBP, Caspr) are not reduced in null tissue, or remyelination-promoting agents do not change the burst rate. Test: held single-nucleus pair (Plp1, Mbp, Mobp in KO versus control, pool descriptive only); outcomes: supported -> the myelin line (CTGF, clemastine-type threads) acquires a link to the seizure endpoint it does not have now; rejected -> the myelin line stays a development-endpoint thread. Class label: OPC-differentiation/remyelination mechanism (label). Pre-test: partly present (Plp1), pool-level and composition-confounded (the CTGF package says so). Would change my mind: Mbp unchanged after composition adjustment. Cost: held-table lookups.

## M05.

**Statement.** The 3 to 7 fold extra susceptibility of Wwox-null pups to pentylenetetrazol is a reduced GABA-A-mediated seizure reserve (the sIPSC loss in vivo), so null pups are hypersensitive to GABA-A-site convulsants but not, or much less, to a convulsant acting through a different mechanism; this is rejected if null pups show the same fold excess on a non-GABA-A convulsant in a same-day, same-litter design.

**Why a leap.** The thread treats the provoked-seizure datum as one acute experiment whose ethosuximide and lithium arms ask about drug effects (NX1, WO-20, WO-21, G01 card). It does not ask what the pentylenetetrazol challenge itself reports. This reading drops the assumption that the challenge is a neutral readout of "seizure threshold" and uses its mechanism (a GABA-A antagonist) as the probe of the inhibitory deficit in the whole animal; it also gives the first in-vivo contrast for the slice-level sIPSC finding that the thread says cannot yet be tested for chloride or release.

**Existing assay or held table that can test it.** The provoked-seizure protocol of PMID 32000863 (Racine-type score per minute) and the held extraction tables `.../fig7_quant_ethosuximide_lithium_sci_20261115/output/fig7b_raster_pdf.tsv` and `.../window_statistics.tsv` for the per-minute scores and the late-phase window used in NX1; the G01 card for the lithium-experiment control group (null peak about 3.5, wild type about 1.2).

**The single discriminating observation and its two outcomes.** In the same litters and on the same day, the null-to-wild-type ratio of the late-phase seizure score (minutes 30 to 60 window, ranks) for pentylenetetrazol and for one non-GABA-A convulsant (a potassium-channel blocker or a kainate-type agonist), each at its own pilot-matched level. Outcome 1: the ratio is clearly larger for pentylenetetrazol (heterozygote intermediate for it only); the null deficit is specific to the GABA-A route and fits the sIPSC loss. Outcome 2: the ratios are alike; the null hypersensitivity is general excitability (or a systemic factor such as the low P10 glucose of WO-35), and the sIPSC loss is not what the challenge reads.

**Repurposing class and at most 2 labels.** Under Outcome 1 only: GABA-A positive modulators acting at the benzodiazepine site (class already in the registry via WO-16 and IT53-Q01); labels: diazepam, clobazam. Under Outcome 2: no class is pointed to; the card-level GABA-A arm would lose a premise.

**Held-data pre-test.** Partly informative, not rejecting. The held numbers give a null-to-wild-type ratio of about 7 (late-phase 1.87 against 0.25, ethosuximide experiment) but only about 3 (peaks 3.5 against 1.2, lithium experiment); the thread (NX1 item 4) and the G01 card both stress that the two null PTZ-alone groups differ, so the fold depends on the experiment and a same-day design is required before any ratio is compared. Result: cannot be rejected; the between-experiment spread is itself a reason the contrast must be same-day.

**What would change my mind.** A null-to-wild-type ratio that varies as much between same-day replicates as between the two held experiments; a second convulsant whose threshold shifts in the null by an unrelated mechanism (for example a hypoglycaemia-driven drop in all thresholds), which would need a glucose-matched arm.

**Cost to test.** About 48 to 60 pups (three genotypes, about 8 per genotype per convulsant, same litters), one session per convulsant; 0 table lookups.

## M06. Statement: the matrix cannot discriminate because all arms are scored on burst suppression, a readout shared by every excitation-lowering agent; a readout that separates WWOX-specific from generic effects is the genotype difference in the RESPONSE CURVE shape (slope and plateau of burst reduction versus agent exposure), not the mean effect: a WWOX-specific mechanism predicts a left-shifted or steeper curve in null slices, a generic one a parallel shift scaled by baseline burst rate. Rejecting observation: curves are parallel after normalising to baseline rate for every arm. Test: the same brief's pilot with two exposures per arm, no extra animals beyond one added level; outcomes: shape difference in one class -> that class becomes the first WWOX-specific match with a drug-by-genotype interaction on the ratio scale (the NX1 lesson); parallel everywhere -> the burst readout cannot give specificity, and the next step is a different readout. Class label: none (a design hypothesis). Pre-test: [a session] NX1 sizing already says the absolute scale cannot separate A from B; this applies it. Would change my mind: a held curve pair showing parallel shifts. Cost: one extra exposure level per arm.

## M07.  The high GABA-AT share is a spasm-phenotype base rate, not a WWOX match
Statement: the improved share on vigabatrin in the WWOX children (8 of 9 with partial counted, 4 of 9 resolved) equals the published responder share of vigabatrin among infantile-spasm cohorts of other genetic aetiologies, so the GABA-AT class is a match for the phenotype 'epileptic spasms' and not for WWOX loss; rejecting observation: the WWOX share lies outside the interval of that base rate. Why a leap: F2 counts the high share as support of a WWOX-specific mechanistic match; this reads it as a base rate of the seizure type. Existing assay or table: the sealed extraction (vigabatrin rows) and a lawful public census of vigabatrin response in other genetic spasm cohorts (PubMed abstracts, census level only). Discriminating observation: WWOX share against the base-rate interval; outcome one (inside): F2 is a phenotype prior, the GABA-AT row in the matrix gets a 'phenotype-generic' flag like PTZ; outcome two (outside): a WWOX-specific excess to be examined (still not causal). Repurposing class: GABA-AT/GAT and steroid/ACTH as labels (source: A1 and extraction). Held-data pre-test: not run; needs the external base rate. What would change my mind: a base rate far lower than 8 of 9 from a well-defined cohort with the same coding. Cost: about 5 abstract-level lookups.

## M08.

**Statement:** One latent delayed-maturation axis in the KO neuronal population explains the co-movement of the progenitor markers, the CCN2 rise, an immature NMDAR subunit ratio, an immature chloride-transporter ratio (NKCC1 up relative to KCC2), the higher input resistance and the depolarised rest, so the held expression tables should show these genes moving by the amount a progenitor-minus-neuronal composite projects; rejected if, in the held KO contrasts, the subunit and chloride-transporter genes move opposite to or independently of the composite projected from the time-course calibration set.

**Why a leap:** The files hold these as separate threads: CCN2 as progenitor-state or composition (`ctgf_held_data_adv007_20261114/README.md`, composite projection +1.21 against observed +1.15), GluN2B composition (`T1_T11` 5b), chloride gradient and a maturational offset as one of seven explanations (`chloride_gradient_sci42_20261110/README.md`), and size/maturation as a hypothesis with no lever (KH5 in `potassium_conductances_sci43_20261110/README.md`). This drops the assumption that they are independent: it asks whether the single projection that carried CCN2 also carries the channel, receptor and transporter genes. Partial overlap with the stated "maturational offset" and KH5; the added element is the common axis and the table-level test.

**Existing assay or table in the repository that can test it:** `ctgf_held_data_adv007_20261114/README.md` (GSE156243 KO organoid 4 versus 2 libraries; GSE150123 time-course calibration; the composite projection method; P17 mouse tables; snRNA pair); `ph_k2p_sci45_20261111/README.md` (P7 cell-class RNA table for Kcnk2/3/9); `chloride_gradient_sci42_20261110/README.md` (M02 age series of littermates as the wet arm).

**The single discriminating observation and its two outcomes:** For a fixed list of genes (GRIN2B, GRIN2A, SLC12A2, SLC12A5, the K2P genes, CCN2), compare the observed KO-versus-WT log2 change in the organoid table with the change projected from the calibration set by the same progenitor-minus-neuronal composite. Outcome A: observed and projected agree in direction and rough size for most of the list (as CCN2 already does): single axis, the later wet readout is M02 (an age series), and the burst phenotype is partly a maturational offset. Outcome B: the receptor and transporter genes depart from the projection while CCN2 stays on it: CCN2 is a composition marker only and the channel and receptor changes need independent explanations.

**Repurposing class and at most 2 named examples (labels only):** No class if the axis is real and nothing in the files gives a maturation lever (KH5: no lever). Conditional class under the chloride arm only: NKCC1-directed chloride-loading modulators; label bumetanide (outside knowledge for the name, see D; the class as a "chloride-gradient manipulation" is in the `chloride_gradient_sci42_20261110/README.md` conditional prediction table).

**Held-data pre-test:** Partly. The CCN2 member of the family already agrees (+1.21 projected, +1.15 observed), so the single-axis reading is not rejected; the receptor and transporter genes are not reported in any file here, so the main test is a table lookup that the folder does not contain. Caveats carried from `ctgf_held_data_adv007_20261114/README.md`: 4 versus 2 libraries, the calibration is a time course of unperturbed spheroids, a projection shows what a shift would carry, not that a shift occurred, and the composite projection is unchecked by anyone else.

**What would change my mind:** An organoid library set in which the progenitor markers do not rise while the channel genes move; a mouse P17 table (null or neuron deletion) where the same genes move against the composite; the age-series measurement (M02) showing a constant offset rather than a shifted time course.

**Cost to test:** 0 animals; roughly 10 to 15 table lookups (each gene in three held tables, plus one projection run).
