**Direction.** By the headline structural variable (myelinated-axon number, myelin thickness, or fibre number) the direction agrees in all three sources (less myelin with slower conduction; more myelin with faster). By g-ratio alone it agrees in Palma and Crawford and **disagrees in Nr2f1** (lower g-ratio with slower conduction); the paper itself reads that g-ratio as poorly compacted myelin and calls the nerve hypomyelinated, so the disagreement is partly a question of what the number means. Direction disagreements by the frozen rule (conduction change null while structure moves, or the reverse direction): 0 of 3 by headline variable, 1 of 3 by g-ratio.
**Size.** The size relation is not stable: within one source and one contrast (Crawford, EAE vs normal) the ratio of structural to conduction effect is **10.8 with thickness and 1.04 with g-ratio**, a tenfold swing set by the choice of structural variable; across sources the g-ratio ratio runs from 0.08 and 0.14 (Palma) through 0.66 (Nr2f1) to 1.04 (Crawford). The g-ratio ranks of structural size (Nr2f1 > Crawford > Palma) and conduction size (Nr2f1 > Palma > Crawford) agree at rho 0.50, which with three sources is not a statement. **Reading: closer to E-A than E-B, held at "descriptive": direction is carried by the headline structural variables; size is not carried by any one variable, and a velocity cannot be read from a structural effect size** (a 55 percent fall in thickness accompanied a 7 percent fall in velocity in Crawford; a 14 percent fall in axon number accompanied a 16 percent fall in Palma). This is a reading of three sources, two of them callosal slices, with different structural variables, different contrasts (a drug in development, an autoimmune demyelination, a heterozygous gene deletion) and different conduction readouts.
