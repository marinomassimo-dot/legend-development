# ADV-190 result: do the PyronicSF depletion measurement and its controls constrain GROSS IMPORT versus AGGREGATE UTILIZATION, and what remains necessary to attribute utilization to PDH? (Arce-Molina et al. 2020, eLife, PMID 32142409; SCI successor Z, 2026-11-23; hypothesis level; mouse cortical astrocyte cultures; no WWOX claim; no compound, ranking or promotion; no spend; no simulation; not medical advice)

Pre-registration: `PREREGISTRATION_ADV190_SCI_20261123.md` (commit `c57d0fd22`, before the paper body was opened; it stated that I had seen ADV-189, Codex's legend screen and IT's applied clause text). Receipt: `RECEIPT_ADV190_SCI_20261123.json`. Codex's clause in the WO-07 note stands; this result adds a source pointer only.

## Answer first
**NARROW (pre-registered): a contained source pointer, not a discriminator for PDH.** The paper separates, in astrocytes, an INITIAL ACCUMULATION phase (import) from a DEPLETION phase after carrier blockade (net loss), reads both as rates, and shows that the depletion rate moves in the expected direction, reversibly and in the same cells, under respiratory perturbations (uncoupler up about 3.4 times, cytochrome oxidase block down to about 0.39). That constrains the depletion as an AGGREGATE, respiration-coupled net loss. It does not constrain gross import apart from utilization (the block is partial and residual import is only estimated), it does not address efflux at all, it reports no matrix pH or Dead-sensor control for the respiratory challenges, and it contains no PDH-side perturbation. The signal under blockade alone cannot identify a PDH flux, and the paper says as much by calling the result "pyruvate consumption" by PDH and PC together.

## Observations (stock, net change, gross flux kept apart)
| Quantity | What the paper has |
|---|---|
| **Stock** | one-point in-situ calibration (nominal zero cytosolic pyruvate by MCT-accelerated exchange with 10 mM lactate) plus interpolation in the in vitro dose-response curve (K_D 480 uM, maximal change about 247%); median mitochondrial pyruvate 21 uM at physiological substrates (131 cells, 10 experiments); about 200 uM in the 3 mM pyruvate protocol of Fig 4E; in vitro resolution of about 10 uM in the low micromolar range only "provided that sufficient replicates are available" |
| **Net change, accumulation** | initial rate of rise after a pyruvate step: control 32 +/- 4 %/min, with 10 uM UK-5099 10 +/- 5 (31 cells, 8 experiments; about 69% inhibition); rosiglitazone 78 +/- 6 to 26 +/- 3 %/min (51 cells, 9 experiments; about 67%) |
| **Net change, depletion** | after the block, the stock falls but not to zero: median new steady state 62% of the pre-block level at 10 uM UK-5099 (29 cells, 10 experiments); 53 +/- 12% against 47 +/- 6% at 0.5 and 10 uM (12 cells, 3 paired experiments; "data not shown"); absolute rate median 1.1 uM/s (0.29 to 3.1; 44 cells, 16 experiments; source data give 44 values, 0.29 to 3.15, median 1.09) |
| **Gross flux** | never observed directly: import and utilization are inferred; the authors state the protocol "underestimates the actual rate of mitochondrial pyruvate consumption by an approximate factor of 2" |
| **Respiratory challenge** | uncoupler FCCP 1 uM: depletion rate 19.1 +/- 1.4 to 65.4 +/- 7.8 %/min (29 cells, 3 experiments); azide 5 mM: 21.4 +/- 4.4, 8.3 +/- 2.2 during, 23.0 +/- 1.1 after (16 cells, 3 experiments); three repeated 30 s UK-5099 pulses in one cell: depletion slopes 32, 26, 33 %/min, reversibility shown (9 cells in 3 experiments) |
Preparation: mixed cortical cultures from 2 to 3 day-old mouse neonates, astrocytes at days 8 to 10; imaging at room temperature (22 to 25 C) in KRH buffer with 1 to 2 mM glucose; 3 to 5 mM pyruvate for the depletion protocol, so cytosolic pyruvate stays high while the carrier is blocked.

## Checks asked for, answered as they change the conclusion
1. **Residual import.** The block is partial (69% of initial uptake). The authors' argument that this is not insufficient drug rests on equal steady states at 0.5 and 10 uM and a stated half-inhibition constant of 50 nM; the equal-steady-state data are "not shown". The factor of about 2 is an estimate from that partial inhibition, not a measured bound on import under the block in the same cells. With cytosolic pyruvate at several mM throughout, any import that survives (other subunit composition or "alternative routes", the authors' words) feeds the matrix and the measured depletion is utilization MINUS residual import: a lower bound on net loss.
2. **Efflux.** The word does not occur in the article; the depletion is read as consumption. Any matrix pyruvate efflux or exchange would add to the apparent net loss and is not separated.
3. **pH.** PyronicSF's dose-response curve shifts with pH (a cpGFP property); the authors show the shift is parallel and offer three corrections (the isosbestic point at 435 nm, a parallel pH probe, or Dead-PyronicSF). None is described as applied in the Fig 4 legends I read; the matrix pH assumed for calibration is 7.8 (cytosol 7.2) from the literature. The FCCP and azide challenges change matrix pH, so a pH-driven fluorescence change can add to or subtract from the apparent depletion rate; the 3.4-fold FCCP effect in particular cannot be assigned to consumption alone without a pH control. The paper's reviewer asked how accurate the correction is; the reply is that the parallel shift makes it "pretty robust", argued from Fig 1 supplement 2, not shown for the flux protocol.
4. **Indicator calibration and range.** Affinity 480 uM, so the 140 to 220 uM stocks of the depletion protocol sit on the rising, nonlinear part of the curve (the reviewer's linearity question is answered by one-point calibration and the in vitro curve); the rate in uM/s depends on that conversion; the %/min rates (Fig 4A to D) are in fluorescence units, not uM.
5. **Independence of replicates.** The unit of every test is the cell ("the number of experiments and cells is detailed in each figure"); experiments are not defined (coverslip, culture or plating) and the source-data sheets carry no experiment or culture identifier per cell (Fig 4E: 44 values, 16 experiments), so cell-level statistics cannot be re-grouped by experiment. FCCP and azide data come from three experiments each. A units slip: the Fig 4E source-data column is labelled mM/s while the values and the text are in uM/s.
6. **Cellular context.** Cultured cortical astrocytes at room temperature, neonatal origin; the paper's own simulation (Fig 3) uses PDH and PC parameters from other cells (BRENDA), acknowledged as unconfirmed for astrocytes. The respiratory challenges move the rate through NADH, ATP, membrane potential and pH, not through PDH alone; PC consumes ATP, so an uncoupler also changes PC flux.
7. **What the signal under blockade identifies.** Net loss of matrix pyruvate under a partial carrier block, respiration-coupled, a lower bound on aggregate utilization (PDH plus pyruvate carboxylase plus any other sink and efflux). Not a PDH flux.

## What remains necessary to attribute utilization specifically to PDH (separate quantities, each with an independent condition)
* **Stock:** sensor pH-controlled (Dead-PyronicSF or isosbestic) and calibrated in the conditions of the experiment.
* **Net change:** accumulation and depletion rates in the same cells, with the incomplete-block fraction measured in those cells (two inhibitors or doses, or a genetic block), and efflux bounded.
* **Gross flux:** an orthogonal measure (labelled pyruvate to CO2 or citrate, or oxygen consumption) in matched cultures.
* **PDH specificity:** a PDH-side perturbation (inhibitor, activation or knockdown) in the same cells, with the direction checked against active PDH; a respiratory challenge alone does not do this.

## Consumer, DREAM, rejecting observation
* **Consumer:** the WO-07 design note, as a pointer only; IT may leave it out. Nothing for ranking, compounds, direction or WWOX.
* **DREAM:** the same shape as ADV-188 and ADV-189 (stock, net change, gross flux, function): name the quantity and its independent condition; here the paper itself keeps accumulation and depletion apart and states the factor-of-2 underestimate, which is the careful form of the reading rule. Weak.
* **Rejecting observation:** a depletion measurement with a measured residual-import fraction in the same cells, a pH control under FCCP and azide, and a PDH-side perturbation that changes the rate in the predicted direction would turn the pointer into a discriminator for PDH; the 2020 paper has the first only as an estimate and the others not at all.
