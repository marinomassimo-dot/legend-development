# IT49-Q04 (Kv7/KCNQ in microglia: protein or current, and does an opener act?) read against Vay 2020 (IT, 2026-11-23; route and reading result; no ranking, arm, compound or direction change; nothing here touches the WO-17 clause, which is a neuronal baseline-current question; not medical advice)

## Answer first
**NARROW on the protein half only; the current half, the opener half and the slice half stay OPEN.** The one paper reached is Vay et al. 2020 (J Neuroinflammation, PMID 32248813, PMC7132998, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12974-020-01779-4)): primary rat microglia in culture. It reports KCNQ2, KCNQ3 and KCNQ5 transcripts (KCNQ1 and KCNQ4 not detectable), higher KCNQ3 and KCNQ5 in the LPS (pro-inflammatory) state than in the IL4 state, and immunocytochemical protein staining for Kv7.2 and Kv7.3 (supplementary figure 1; not viewed). It measures **no Kv7 current** (no patch clamp; the authors list patch clamping as a further study), **no membrane-potential effect of a Kv7 manipulation** (the voltage-sensor-probe experiments were done with the HCN blocker only), and **tests no opener**. The only Kv7 manipulation is the blocker XE-991 (10 and 30 microM, 24 h), which reduced migration of resting microglia and changed nothing else measured (survival, proliferation, activation markers, polarisation, phagocytosis); the authors themselves note that XE-991 may be insufficient in non-excitable cells because its efficacy is voltage-dependent, so a null on the other readouts is not evidence that the channels have no function. Rat, primary culture, no slice, no human, no WWOX-deficient material.
