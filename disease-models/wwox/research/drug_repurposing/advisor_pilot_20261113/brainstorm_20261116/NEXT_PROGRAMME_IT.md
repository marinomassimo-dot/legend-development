## What I would NOT do again, with the measured cost
1. **An anonymiser without a check.** My script blanked 10 of 24 hypotheses; run 1 of the judge cost 54,395 output tokens (13.7 percent of the delegates' output of the round) and about 15 minutes, and was unusable. A table of N rows is not proof of N judged items: check the shortest block and require a body-seen echo.
2. **Changing the survival definition after seeing the arms.** The same 24 hypotheses gave 1, 9 or 22 survivors by definition; the arms differ by 1 to 2 items at n = 4 to 5. Fix the definition and the margin before the round.
3. **Five perspective authors at n = 4 to 5 per arm.** Judge runs 56,810 + 26,559 + 13,098 output tokens and no separation. If a generative round is repeated, use about 20 per arm (SCI's estimate) or do not run it.
4. **Fate counts by the author of the item, or tests that cannot fail.** My second read changed 3 of 8 independent categories (J23, H-C3, H-C5 to U); 22 of 31 hypotheses had no held-data test that could reject them. Fix the test first (test-setter, 91,819 tokens), then run it.
5. **A planted pair that does not match the question, or without its key digest pushed first.** Arm B's run had both faults (length tell 1.6-1.8 KB against 3.6-4.1 KB; no digest): its blinding evidence is the weakest of the day.
6. **Landing through the shared root checkout.** Main moved under 25 to 40 gates; my root merge conflict blocked every landing for about 20 minutes; a syntax error I pushed in a `--help` block turned the release surface red for about 10 minutes. Land from an isolated detached worktree, one at a time, run the script's `--help` before pushing.
7. **Reading 'matches the PTZ profile' as 'predicts efficacy'.** F1 equals the PTZ profile on the 7 shared pairs (HE's matched-pair count); the matrix recovers background pharmacology, not a WWOX-specific signal.
