SHORT ANSWER
1. Beyond the Tc-MEP latency there are other heterozygote signals. None is a second animal-level readout in the germline-null heterozygote.
   - The only other quantitative het-vs-control differences come from the neuron-targeted S-HT genotype (Wwox +/flox with Synapsin-Cre). They are in Repudi 2021 Brain and Breton 2021. Breton re-quotes the Brain bursting counts, and the two papers cannot be treated as independent animal sources.
   - The P47T/WT paper (Hussain 2023) has some intermediate means and one het-above-wild-type histology signal. The wild-type groups are small, and I found no het-vs-wild-type test for the behaviour or kidney-weight results.
2. Yes, the sources give reasons to carry harm baselines for heterozygous pups.
   - The only germline-Wwox +/- harm measure is brain weight and brain water at P20. Cheng found no difference, but only in brain.
   - The P47T/WT adult data show a het excess of nephropathy, cholestasis and focal liver degeneration over a wild-type group of 7. They also show reduced survival (not significant) and a glucose mean below wild type (not significant).
   - No pup-age organ, weight, survival or glucose data exist for any het genotype in these sources. Cheng 2020 reports no +/- body weight or glucose.

(E) CONTRADICTIONS FOR HETEROZYGOTES
1. S-HT bursting counts: Brain text "4 of n = 23 slices across 14 animals, 17 %" vs Fig 2 legend "four slices from three S-HT animals showed bursting (20%)". Same inconsistency for S-KO: text "86 %, 36 of 42 slices across 24 animals" vs legend "84 %, 36 slices from 20 S-KO animals". Breton repeats the text version (24 S-KO animals). The legend's 20 % cannot be 4/23 (17 %), and the animal denominator (3 vs 14) differs.
2. Brain Fig 2 power analysis uses S-HT n=11 slices from 8 animals vs the bursting analysis 23 slices from 14 animals: different subsets, not explained.
3. Brain: S-HT "did not show any abnormal phenotypes" (behaviour) vs 17-20 % bursting slices. Breton Discussion: bursts in heterozygotes "manifested similarly as those observed in the knockouts" vs 17 % vs 86 % of slices.
4. Breton: S-HT and S-WT are lumped as S-CTL "where no significant difference exists", yet its own Table 1 shows S-HT differing from S-WT (input resistance, resting potential, AP amplitude, sag) and its mEPSC KS tests give S-WT vs S-HT p=1.98e-04 (amplitude) and 0.0018 (frequency).
5. Cheng: motor behaviour +/- like wt, but Tc-MEP latency +/- differs from wt and not from null (het vs null n.s.); the paper explains it as partial change. Not a contradiction, but an unusual pattern.
6. Hussain: text "intermediate behavioral phenotype" for het (OFT/EPM) while in the sociability panel het is above wt (star in Fig 2d); text says het and wt both had a "clear preference".
7. Hussain: neoplasia 33 % uses a denominator of 18 while the header says n=19 (see B). Text "several P47T/WT mice developed neoplasia ... and nephropathy as typically observed in aging mice" while Table 4 shows nephropathy 72 % het vs 14 % wt (n=7).
