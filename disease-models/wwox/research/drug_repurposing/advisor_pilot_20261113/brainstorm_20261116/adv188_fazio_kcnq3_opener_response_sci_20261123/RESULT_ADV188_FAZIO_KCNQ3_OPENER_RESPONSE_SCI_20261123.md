(Fazio et al. 2026, Front Mol Neurosci, PMID 43047766; SCI successor Z, 2026-11-23; hypothesis level; adjacent mouse Kcnq3 thalamic preparation; no transfer of thalamic age, firing direction, Kcnq3 mechanism or drug preference to cortical WWOX; no compound, ranking or promotion; no contact; no e-mail address to any service; not medical advice)

## Answer first
**NARROW to a methods analogy, with ONE optional wording clause for WO-17 (offered because ADV-173's clause reads a suppression and says nothing about a weak or absent response).** In Fazio a weak opener response sits beside a KNOWN reduction of the target subunit (Kcnq3 deleted or halved) and a higher starting excitability, so a weak response did not exclude a deficit there; but the paper has no drug-free blocker-defined baseline, normalises each genotype to its own control, and compares the current increment by within-genotype tests only, so it cannot separate a smaller baseline from a smaller recruitable pool, and it does not test any of the rivals. The answer to ADV's question is therefore logic plus one illustration, not a measured dissociation.

## What the statistics do and do not compare
* **Current (Fig 4B) and rheobase (Fig 4E): significance within each genotype, not between them.** "RTG failed to mediate a significant IM potentiation" in mutants rests on p = 0.14 and 0.11 against p < 0.0001 in WT, and "failed to raise the current threshold in Kcnq3-/-" on p = 0.31 against 0.0001. That is the "significant in one, not in the other" pattern; no genotype-by-drug interaction is reported for either panel, and the supplement gives SEMs of the RTG values, not of the paired increments, so I cannot compute one. The two Sidak lines for the mutants are both labelled Kcnq3-/- in the supplement (a typo; I read them as +/- and -/-).
* **AP reduction (Fig 4D): a true between-group comparison of within-cell changes** (Kruskal-Wallis on per-cell percentages), but the unit is the cell (8 per group, 3 or 4 animals), the percentage is bounded at 100 and WT sits at the ceiling (most WT cells near 100%, Fig 4D image), and at one concentration.
* **Matched recordings:** the voltage-clamp increment (n 10, 9, 8) and the current-clamp readouts (n 8, 8, 8 and 8, 8, 9) do not have the same cell counts; the paper does not say whether they are the same cells. Within each readout the control and RTG values are paired in the same cell.

## What the paper shows about the rivals (observations from the numbers above; arithmetic from the supplement means)
1. **Weak response beside a known smaller pool and higher starting excitability.** In Kcnq3+/- and -/- the target subunit is halved or absent (Kcnq3 mRNA 0.48 of WT in +/-; Kcnq2 unchanged), starting excitability is higher (lower rheobase, more APs), and the opener's effect on the current increment and on firing is smaller. So a weak response coexisted with a reduced pool; it did not indicate a normal one. The reduction is known from the genotype, not from a measured baseline current.
2. **Readouts disagree within one group.** In Kcnq3+/-, the current increment is not significant (+24%), but the rheobase shift is significant and about proportional to WT (+32.5 pA, +45%, against +52.5 pA, +52% in WT; the treated +/- rheobase, 105 pA, is near the untreated WT value, 100 pA), while AP reduction is intermediate (54% against 94%). A single readout would have given a different answer from another one in the same cells: "weak" is readout-specific.
3. **Normalisation hides the quantity.** A percentage increment is the absolute increment divided by that cell's baseline: with a smaller baseline in mutants the same absolute change would give a larger percentage, so a smaller percentage implies an even smaller absolute change, but the paper gives no absolute values by genotype to say how much smaller, and no baseline defined without the opener.
4. **Age changes the response at constant expression and constant starting excitability.** In WT the RTG-sensitive current is 28.0 pA at p10 against 52.2 pA at p30 (KW p = 0.0074) and AP reduction 67.6% against 93.9% (p = 0.0064), while Kcnq2 and Kcnq3 mRNA do not change (Fig 1A, all p above 0.25) and tonic firing is not different across ages (Fig S3B). A response can therefore be weak in an immature or differently assembled pool with no difference in transcript or in starting excitability (the paper's own reading is a subunit-stoichiometry shift, a speculation).
5. **Ceiling and concentration:** WT firing is nearly abolished by 30 uM, and there is no concentration series, so the data cannot separate lower potency from lower efficacy.

## Interpretations (kept apart; mine)
* A weak opener response CAN coexist with a reduced recruitable current (illustrated here, with the reduction known from the genotype). It can also coexist with higher starting excitability, a ceiling or another assay limit. It excludes a baseline deficit only if baseline and response are measured on the same scale in the same cells and the control response is shown at matched starting excitability and age; none of the three is available in Fazio, and the paper does not claim otherwise (it reads the weak response as a consequence of missing Kv7.3).
* The quantity WO-17 needs is the baseline current (drug-free, blocker-defined) beside the recruitable increment and the firing change, all in absolute units; Fazio supplies the third in percentages and the second in normalised units.
