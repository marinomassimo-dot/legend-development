### Q1 summary
Found: 24 in vivo rodent reports in the table (rat and mouse, roughly P4 to P21), plus four in vitro reports and nine combination or related entries. Direction is mixed. Anticonvulsant or antikindling effects: Mares 2009 (P12 only), Mazarati 2009 (P11, weaker P14, none P21), Dzhala 2005, Cleary 2013 (as phenobarbital add-on), Hu 2017, Edwards 2010 (P4-P9 but not P10-P17), Willis 2015, Zhang 2016, Tahara 2023 (P21), Koyama 2012, Marguet 2015. Null or negative: Johne 2021 (a) and (b) (P11 asphyxia, with phenobarbital), Kang 2015 (P7, P10, P12 ischemia, blunted at P10), Kharod 2018 (P7 PTZ), Minlebaev 2011 (P5-P9 flurothyl), Pizzamiglio 2025 (Scn1a+/-, P21), Yuan 2019 (Scn1b-/-, seizures not prevented). Proconvulsant or paradoxical: Mares 2009 at the higher dose (shorter latency, P12); Kilb 2007 in vitro (more ictal events with kainate). Kasahara 2019 (P11 vs P14) shows bumetanide reversing benzodiazepine/barbiturate worsening in febrile-seizure mice. An explicit age comparison is in the abstracts of Mares, Mazarati, Kang (a), Kasahara, Tao, Edwards, Tahara, Wahab. Combinations: phenobarbital (Dzhala 2008 in vitro, Cleary 2013 positive; Johne 2021 a/b, Kang 2015, Kharod 2018 negative), midazolam (Welzel 2023 a, positive at the higher of two doses and bumetanide alone inactive), diazepam (Kasahara 2019; Minlebaev 2011 had diazepam effective and bumetanide inactive, as separate arms, not combined).
NOT found by these routes: bumetanide in pilocarpine or kainate in vivo in pups between P7 and P28 (Brandt 2010, Töllner 2015, Sivakumaran 2016 and Hampel 2021 are adult; Nardou 2009 is in vitro); audiogenic seizures in pups (Reid 2000 gives no age in the abstract); a pup bumetanide plus diazepam seizure study with a combined arm; and an abstract-level statement of a null result in febrile-seizure pups with bumetanide alone. Not read: Rao 2023 systematic review (37690372) and Sakamoto 2025 (41853301), seen only as titles. Many abstracts were cut at the cap of the display, so a few sentences that hold negative results may be present beyond what was read; all quotations were however checked verbatim.

### Q2 summary
Found: the NEMO phase 1/2 open-label dose-finding trial (term HIE neonates; stopped early; hearing impairment in 3 of 11 survivors; dehydration, hypotension, electrolyte disturbances), the Soul pilot randomized double-blind trial (diuresis the only significant adverse event; hearing impairment 2 of 26 treated vs 0 of 13 controls; authors report additional seizure-burden reduction but the treated group had a higher total seizure burden, and they state efficacy is unproven), a secondary analysis (no long-term difference), one single-case report, PK and tolerability papers in infants for diuretic use (Sullivan 1996, Oliveros 2011, Shankaran 1995), an in vitro bilirubin displacement study (Walker 1988) and three paediatric autism or TSC trials with diuretic-type adverse events (hypokalaemia, orthostatic hypotension, dehydration). Labels: tablet and injection labels carry a boxed statement on profound diuresis with water and electrolyte depletion, an ototoxicity section, 'Safety and effectiveness in pediatric patients below the age of 18 have not been established', and neonatal bilirubin displacement and kernicterus wording; the newer nasal-spray label states it is not approved for pediatric patients including neonates and lists tinnitus and hearing loss. The seizure regular-expression result above shows whether any label mentions seizures.
