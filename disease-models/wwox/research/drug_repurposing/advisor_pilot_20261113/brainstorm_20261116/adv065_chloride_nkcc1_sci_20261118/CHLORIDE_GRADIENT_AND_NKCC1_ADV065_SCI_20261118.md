## Answer first
1. **What the chloride question decides.** Whether GABA-A signalling is inhibitory or excitatory at the experiment age decides the sign of almost every GABAergic candidate, and **E_GABA was never measured in any Wwox system** (two query routes, abstract level: no Wwox paper reports chloride, E_GABA, NKCC1 or KCC2; the two Wwox mouse papers that touch inhibition measured interneuron counts and IPSCs, and Breton 2021's IPSCs were recorded, as relayed by ADV from IT's ADV-048 part 2, with a chloride-loaded pipette, which fixes E_Cl and says nothing about the native gradient). The rodent age data say the switch is region- and preparation-dependent and, for the mouse, **close to the experiment window**: slices of mouse hippocampus and neocortex switch between P2-P8 and P9-P15 (Valeeva 2016); rat CA3 about P8-P10, reversal -55 mV at P0-2 and -74 mV at P13-P15 (Tyzio 2007); rat neocortical GABA depolarising through P2-P10, spikes only in layer 5/6 (Rheims 2008); in vivo, hippocampal interneurons inhibitory by P7 and visual cortex already at P3 (Murata 2020). **The one functional mouse datum inside the window:** high-dose diazepam or pentobarbital **exacerbated** febrile-seizure induction at P11 and not at P14, and the exacerbation was suppressed by bumetanide (Kasahara 2019, AB; the authors keep the sedation explanation open).
2. **Across the candidates already in the loop (INFERENZA; no source states these as measured):** (a) **GABA-A modulators (phenobarbital, benzodiazepines):** direction depends on the gradient; mouse phenobarbital failed at P7 and worked at P10 and P12 (Kang 2015); diazepam not age-flat (ADV-064). (b) **Vigabatrin** (raises ambient GABA): the same dependence (IT's ADV-056 section 3). (c) **PTZ, itself a GABA-A antagonist:** in a depolarising state its action is not simply disinhibition; PTZ is convulsant from P7 in the rat, so the assay does not read the gradient directly, but **the wild-type age series varies gradient maturity as well as age**, and the wild-type floor may partly be gradient maturity (no datum). (d) **Wild-type floor:** unmeasured cause. 
3. **Window, brief and pilot.** The P11-to-P14 mouse window sits at the edge of the experiment ages: **an assay age of P11 or P12 may put GABAergic agents in a paradoxical regime; P14 and later is likelier mature (in the slice and cortex data above, with region caveats)**. For the in vivo brief this means the assay age must be stated against it, and the age series should straddle it. For the slice pilot (P13-P17) the gradient is probably mature in wild type and the **perforated-patch E_GABA readout in S-KO against wild type at matched age** (F03's own endpoint) is the cheap decisive add-on, the one that either opens or closes the GABAergic strand for the window.
4. **An NKCC1 blocker is not more promising than the other candidates on any endpoint at present** (by-endpoint table below); its decisive differences: **its effect window in pups is P11 to P14 only; brain exposure after systemic dosing is disputed and low; the human neonatal trials carry a hearing signal; no chronic-control or WWOX datum**. **Placement:** in the slice pilot, **conditional and second** (after the E_GABA readout, only if S-KO is depolarising); in the in vivo design, **neither as an efficacy arm now** (no engagement control, narrow age effect, ototoxicity and diuresis); an optional **functional probe of gradient maturity** is a GABA-A challenge compared at P11 against P14, not a bumetanide arm (harm and ethics to the laboratory). **Reasoned stop for the literature loop on this strand: the missing item is a measurement.**

## 2. Other NKCC1- or KCC2-directed agents (one line each; AB)
* **Furosemide:** neonatal mineral losses and, in a case-control, hearing loss associated with exposure; pup seizure data not found; also a tool KCC2 antagonist. **Azosemide, torasemide:** rat birth-asphyxia model, alone neither suppressed seizures, torasemide potentiated midazolam, azosemide did not.
* **DIMAEB (= BUM5, bumetanide prodrug), bumepamine** (a derivative the authors say does **not** inhibit NKCC1 yet reproduces the CNS effects attributed to it: an off-target caution), **BUM532, BUM97:** adult models or P11 rat asphyxia, no pup data of the P12 to P28 kind.
* **ARN23746, ARN24092 (selective NKCC1 inhibitors):** mouse Down-syndrome and autism models in adults; no pup seizure data; no human data.
* **KCC2 side: CLP257 and its prodrug CLP290** (the abstract-level dispute over whether CLP257 acts on KCC2 is title-only: no abstract exists for the correspondence): CLP290 in neonatal mouse ischaemic seizures rescued phenobarbital efficacy (Sullivan 2021, age not in the abstract); **OV350 (a KCC2 activator): Cdkl5-knockout pups treated P10 to P21, infantile spasms reduced (Arshad 2026, AB)**, the one KCC2-directed agent with pup data in a genetic DEE model; **prochlorperazine** (a KCC2-enhancer claim for an existing phenothiazine with many other targets); **VU0463271 and ML077 (KCC2 inhibitors):** tools that cause epileptiform discharges in adult hippocampus.
* None has human seizure data beyond the neonatal bumetanide trials; none has Wwox data.

## 4. Next step, and rivals kept
**Reasoned stop for the literature loop on the chloride strand**: every remaining observation is a measurement (E_GABA by perforated patch in S-KO and wild type at P13 to P17 and younger; KCC2 and NKCC1 protein; the functional probe), none obtainable by an agent. The human-visit list gains **Kasahara 2019 full text** (open access, behind a reCAPTCHA page for plain clients). **Rivals kept:** sedation for the P11 exacerbation (authors); an off-target action of bumetanide (bumepamine shows a non-NKCC1 derivative with CNS effects; diuresis and systemic chloride shifts); brain exposure too low for NKCC1 inhibition (five papers) against the PBPK and one rat report that it is reached at a high dose; slice-versus-in-vivo differences in the switch age (Valeeva 2016: slices switch around P8-P9, in vivo mainly inhibitory at P3-P9) and region differences (hippocampus later than visual cortex); a method dispute about energy substrates and depolarising GABA in neonatal slices (Rheims 2008, Ruusuvuori 2010); the pup bumetanide literature is split (several nulls) so even where a gradient is depolarising the drug need not work.
