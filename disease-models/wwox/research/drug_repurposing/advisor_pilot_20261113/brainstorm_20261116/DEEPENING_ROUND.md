## Ideas (seeds by ADV; owners may reshape them; everything below is hypothesis)
I1 TWO-STAGE REBOUND OSCILLATOR (owner SCI): the Wwox-null neocortex behaves like a post-inhibitory rebound network (measured sag and rebound, T13): a T-type-like trigger sets the inter-burst interval (median 26.2 s, gamma shape about 1.4), an NMDAR-dependent sustainer sets the duration (median 0.66 s, gamma shape about 2.5, i.e. about two to three hidden stages). Build the dynamical model (slow variables: chloride, adenosine, resource depletion), fit the held interval and duration statistics, and let it PREDICT the effect of each mechanism class on interval and duration and the sign of a GABA-A positive modulator (rebound makes it aggravate). The surprising prediction: a threshold (bifurcation) between null and wild type that a small change of ONE parameter crosses. Intervention logic: T-type blockade shifts the interval, NMDAR-active agents the duration, GABA-A potentiation may aggravate; HCN and KCC2 enter the model.
I2 PHENOTYPE-CONDITIONAL PHARMACOLOGY (owner IT): WWOX specificity may not be genotype-conditional but SEMIOLOGY- and AGE-conditional: early NMDAR/GluN2B-high window with spasms (steroid, GABA-AT inhibition) versus later rebound-type absence-like and myoclonic events (T-type blockers and valproate-type helpful; sodium-channel blockers and strong GABAergic drive may aggravate: general antiseizure pharmacology to be re-verified). The variable phenotype spectrum and the 'contrasting results' of the older literature are then what a mixture of two circuits looks like. Test (no new data): re-extract the 14 held full texts PER CHILD for semiology (spasms, absence-like, myoclonic, focal, tonic-clonic), age, agent, outcome and WORSENING, then ask whether response segregates by semiology and age. Surprising prediction: agents that fail on average work in a subgroup, and aggravation clusters in the rebound phenotype.
I3 COMPENSATION ASYMMETRY, WHY THE HIPPOCAMPUS DOES NOT BURST (owner SCI, second HE): Wwox falls MORE in hippocampus (-85%) than in neocortex (-68%) and myelin genes fall more there too, yet only neocortex bursts: so the hippocampus compensates (Npy log2 +2.5 against +0.96, receptors Npy1r/2r/5r transcripts down, immediate-early genes down). Chain: loss of Wwox gives a conserved excitation-inhibition imbalance; hippocampal NPY and related brakes contain it; neocortex lacks them. Surprising predictions: blocking NPY signalling unmasks hippocampal bursts (PS-12); an NPY-pathway agonist or an adenosine-augmenting route should reduce neocortical bursts; the same asymmetry should appear for other brakes. Intervention logic: NPY/Y2 agonism, adenosine augmentation, labels only, BLOCK-1 flags.
I4 METABOLIC, KETONE AND ADENOSINE AXIS (owner HE with ADV): the ketogenic diet is a clinical class, valproic acid has the one coherent signature against the glycolysis/lactate module, Adora1 is lower, Adk is lower while Gfap doubles: a hypothesis that Wwox loss lowers the energy and adenosine brake of neurons (a metabolic route to excitability), with the diet and valproate-type agents as mechanistic cousins. Surprising prediction: burst rate depends on substrate in null slices and not in wild type (the added slice arm of the brief); adenosine-tone readouts differ by genotype.
I5 PV-AXON MYELIN AND OPC (owner ADV): myelin genes fall in mature oligodendrocyte nuclei even after composition adjustment and more in the hippocampus; PV axons are among the most myelinated; Wwox in the OPC lineage (CTGF thread) could thin PV-axon myelin and delay inhibitory timing, so inhibition 'exists' but arrives late (reduced sIPSC frequency, preserved markers). Surprising prediction: a conduction-timing deficit measurable as inhibitory latency, not amplitude. Intervention logic: remyelination or OPC-differentiation mechanisms (labels), BLOCK-1 flags.
I6 CHLORIDE DYNAMICS AND ACTIVITY-DEPENDENT GABA (owner SCI): the within-burst E_GABA shift against KCC2 (HSCI-1); NKCC1 and KCC2 transcripts do not support a simple story: develop the dynamic version (activity-dependent loading) and what it predicts for GABA-A PAM and for KCC2/NKCC1 modulators.
I7 CRITICAL NMDAR FRACTION AND THE AGE WINDOW (owner IT): a threshold fraction f_c below which abolition fails; GluN2B-sensitive share 53% at P6-P8 against 14% at P13-P15 (more in whisker-deprived: 49%); a prediction for which class works early and which late; link to I2.
I8 PY-MOTIF COMPETITION: WWOX AS A SCAFFOLD FOR CHANNELS AND TRANSPORTERS (owner HE, second ADV): WWOX WW domains bind PPxY motifs; several channels and transporters carry PY motifs recognised by NEDD4-family ligases (NEDD4L is itself an epilepsy-related gene, ASSUMED to be re-checked). Hypothesis: WWOX competes with the ligase at PY motifs, so Wwox loss raises or lowers the surface level of specific channels (sodium channels, NMDAR/AMPAR auxiliary proteins, ENaC-like channels, K channels). Test with NO literature: a proteome-wide PPxY scan of ion-channel, receptor and transporter sequences from UniProt (public, generic client), joined with the held expression tables and the pharmacology classes of the matrix: a ranked list of channel candidates and the classes that act on them. Surprising prediction: a channel NOT in the usual antiseizure targets carries the motif and is up in the null.
I9 PERINEURONAL NETS AND PV MATURATION (owner ADV, new): PV cells depend on perineuronal nets and activity for maturation; the age window of the bursts (P13-P17) coincides with net formation; PV marker loss is lab-dependent. Hypothesis: delayed or abnormal net maturation makes PV cells 'present but immature' in the neocortex, not lost; held test: net-component transcripts (Acan, Bcan, Hapln1, Tnr, Mmp9) in the held tables; experiment: staining.
I10 SLEEP-WAKE AND THALAMOCORTICAL DEPENDENCE (owner ADV, new): rebound bursts of this kind are state-dependent in vivo (T-type, sleep spindles, absence-like activity); slices cannot show it. Hypothesis: the seizure burden in the null is highest in light sleep and sleep transitions; test: sleep-staged EEG in the null mouse (a design), and in patients as an observational record of seizure timing against sleep (no new data first: held case reports for timing statements).
