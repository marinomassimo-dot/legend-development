## Answers first
1. **K13 (what the input omits): SUPPORTED.** Word count of the complete input (sha256 7c6a7195...): 0 hits for emergence, cystitis, bladder, hepat, biliary, abuse, secretion, laryngo, salivat, vomit, intracranial, neurotox and adverse. Its only harm content is rodent mechanism plus "memantine is generally well tolerated".
2. **Q1, acute ketamine in children: serious events are well quantified and low.** From 8,282 single ED sedations (individual-patient meta-analysis): emesis 8.4%, any recovery agitation 7.6%, clinically important agitation 1.4%; airway and respiratory events 3.9%; age under 2 years OR 2.00 (95% CI 1.47 to 2.72), age 13 or older OR 2.72. Registry of 12,780 ED encounters: critical events 2 (0.016%), high-risk events 67 (0.52%), including 7 laryngospasm. Systematic review of 58,752 children: no deaths or permanent deficits reported, serious events 0.33%. Hypersalivation without atropine: transient airway complications 3.2% of 1,090 sedations, one linked to hypersalivation. Raised intracranial pressure: no rise in 55 paediatric TBI patients (systematic review); a randomized lumbar-puncture study of 25 oncology children found higher CSF opening pressure with ketamine, 26.5 versus 17.3 cm H2O; the label states "An increase in intracranial pressure has been reported".
3. **Q1 seizures: a signal relevant to this class, small numbers.** Electrographic seizures after ketamine 0/39 without epilepsy and 26/94 (28%) with epilepsy; children with epilepsy 3/5 (n = 5; only 24 children in the whole review; PMID 39918155). One case of a generalized seizure in a healthy 7-year-old. The label lists spasms "resembling a partial motor or generalized motor seizure".
4. **Q2, repeated or prolonged exposure: in children these are case reports with no incidence.** A child with FIRES developed cholangiopathy (title and keywords only; PubMed has no abstract). A 9-year-old developed haemorrhagic cystitis on a continuous esketamine infusion (the S-enantiomer, not ketamine). An 18-month-old had withdrawal by WAT-1 and later behavioural impairment after 7 days (n = 1; causality only suggested). Counter-case: a 5-year-old on IV ketamine for over 4 months had no hepatotoxicity or cystitis. Adult data (TRANSFER): 8 chronic-use cholangiopathy cases; 3/51 enzyme elevations in a depression clinic, all attributed to other causes. The label ties hepatobiliary and urinary harms to "recurrent use" or "long-term use or abuse" and states that dependence and tolerance occur with prolonged use. PICU infusion series, median 2.4 days (n = 24): hypotension 13/24, tachycardia 15/24, secretions 7/24, emergence at discontinuation 3/24; two further series (n = 60 and n = 77) reported 3 and 11 potentially related events. SE series: Kuki 3/18 transient events needing intervention, Rosati 0/9 serious, DeVine 0/3; abstracts do not itemise them. No incidence of abuse in paediatric medical use was found.
5. **Q3, human developmental neurotoxicity of ketamine: not established.** Human ketamine-specific data are weak, small signals in both directions: a cohort of 110 infants with congenital heart disease showed -0.34 Bayley motor points per mg/kg; a cohort of 51 preterm infants given one birth-room dose showed no difference at 2 years (versus 16 controls); a very-preterm cohort (n = 435) counted ketamine as one of six agents and found no significant effect. Regulator wording reached is the Ketalar label (DailyMed, 2026-03-27): it warns about exposures over 3 hours in children up to about 3 years, says "The clinical significance of these findings is not clear" and that the human studies "have substantial limitations", and that paediatric safety below 16 is not established. The animal statement in the label includes "neuronal and oligodendrocyte cell loss" (TRANSFER); the same label says 3 h of ketamine in primates "did not increase neuronal cell loss"; a 2026 FDA-funded rat study "do[es] not support juvenile rats as having an increased susceptibility" (TRANSFER). The FDA 2016 communication page was not reached (404 on three URL guesses), only second-hand, quoted in PMID 28953732. No consensus statement naming ketamine was reached; EMA not attempted.
6. **Q4, memantine in children.** The DEE randomized trial (36256600): harms sections NOT read (publisher page blocked); the abstract says "safe and effective", gives no adverse-event counts, reports seizure improvement in 8 on memantine versus 2 on placebo. Autism trials (26978327 and 31027422): serious events rare and unrelated; any treatment-emergent event 85% versus 77% in the 12-week trial; discontinuation for an event 6.6% of 903 in the 50-week open-label study; one communication measure worse on memantine (p = 0.02); a meta-analysis states "memantine aggravated impulsive behaviors"; both trials excluded active epilepsy, so they say nothing on seizure provocation. In a GRIN-variant paediatric cohort (19 gain-of-function individuals), seizure frequency increased on memantine in 3 (Results) or 4 (Discussion) individuals (the paper gives both counts). An adult epilepsy cohort (n = 211) showed no excess (TRANSFER). Label: memantine has "not been systematically evaluated in patients with a seizure disorder"; seizures 0.2% on memantine versus 0.5% on placebo in dementia trials; paediatric safety "not established"; combination with ketamine "has not been systematically evaluated and such use should be approached with caution"; alkaline urine reduces clearance by about 80%; juvenile rats showed apoptosis at 15 mg/kg/day, no-effect level 8 (TRANSFER).
7. **Q6 (WWOX-class links): none met.** WWOX with ketamine or memantine as a studied drug: 0 hits on two routes; the only full-text hit is ketamine used as a perfusion anaesthetic. Class-level links found: the label's animal statement on oligodendrocyte loss in the developing brain; an adult review mentioning PV interneurons, no human finding in its abstract; one adult case of recurrent hypoglycaemia after a ketamine infusion (type 1 diabetes). None is tied to WWOX loss of function.
