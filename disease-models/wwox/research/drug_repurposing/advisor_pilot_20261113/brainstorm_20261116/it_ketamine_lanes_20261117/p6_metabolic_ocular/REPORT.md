## Evidence in brief (rows with quotes: METABOLIC_OCULAR_ROWS.tsv)
* Ketamine, humans, metabolic: 1975 paediatric anaesthesia comparison (30 children): glucose +12.2% from baseline with ketamine (halothane +54.75%, thiopentone +71.7%), free fatty acid -13.4%; an RCT in 60 children (caudal adjunct): insulin and cortisol fell within groups, no between-group difference, glucose not in the abstract; PICU cohort (73 exposed): 42% at least one adverse event, hypoglycaemia not named; four inborn-error or mitochondrial anaesthesia case reports: no glucose harm named ("no severe hypoglycemia" in the AADC series).
* Ketamine, animals (all TRANSFER): no hyperglycaemia in normal fasted rats, a rise in diabetic rats; no effect on fasting glucose, insulin or hypoglycaemia in rhesus; lower first-phase insulin in baboons; most hyperglycaemia reports have an alpha2-agonist partner; 14-day rat: liver glycogen decreased; hepatic gluconeogenesis, lactate and ketone: no ketamine primary study returned.
* Memantine: no effect on blood glucose or islet insulin secretion in mice (TRANSFER); label: did not modify the glucose-lowering effect of a sulfonylurea-metformin product; 0 of 75 openFDA memantine labels contain cataract, conjunctivitis, special senses or hypoglyc.
* Ketamine, ocular: Ketalar label: "Eye disorders : Diplopia, nystagmus, elevation in intraocular pressure."; 13 of 13 ketamine labels carry intraocular, nystagmus and diplopia terms, 0 of 13 carry hypoglyc, glucose or glycem; paediatric IOP: mean difference 1.4 mm Hg (80 children), largest change 1.09 mm Hg (25), median rise 3 mm Hg with 15 of 60 having a brief rise of 5 mm Hg or more, glaucoma children 24.4 vs 17 mm Hg after sevoflurane (8); developing-rat retina apoptosis ex vivo; intravitreal rat no atrophy at 1 month (all TRANSFER); label warning 5.5 (neuronal apoptosis in the developing brain with exposure longer than 3 hours) is not ocular.
* Memantine, ocular: overdose section: coma, diplopia, agitation, visual hallucinations; 2,298 adults with glaucoma: not shown to prevent progression; 38 with optic neuritis: RNFL thinning partly reduced, no acuity difference; used for nystagmus in 48, 16 and 6 patients (improved in two trials; 18.8% stopped for side effects in one); FAERS (5,808 reports): ophthalmic vein thrombosis (n = 4) and scleral discolouration (n = 7) signals, not causal.
* WWOX context (held full texts, DATO): 34747138 "Wwox -null mice also exhibit phenotypes associated with hypoglycemia, impaired bone metabolism, and steroidogenesis"; 36779245 "All cases demonstrated severe optic atrophy, irrespective of age"; 32000863 Wwox-/- mice show "optic nerve atrophy".

## INFERENZA and IPOTESI
INFERENZA: both phenotypes are WWOX-loss features and no source ties either drug to them; a drug effect on glucose or the optic nerve in a WWOX-class patient would add to the disease phenotype and the record cannot size it; ketamine's labelled ocular effects are functional and drug-general, nothing opened links them to optic-nerve degeneration. IPOTESI (untested): a child with impaired counter-regulation could respond differently to ketamine's catecholamine-mediated glycaemic effects; no source addresses it. TRANSFER, never promoted: every rodent, monkey, dog, rabbit and ex vivo retina result.
