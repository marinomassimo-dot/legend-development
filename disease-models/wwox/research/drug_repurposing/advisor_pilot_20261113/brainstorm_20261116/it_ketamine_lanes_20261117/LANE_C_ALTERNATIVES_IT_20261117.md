## Answer first
1. **On the repository's own axis (WWOX-system evidence with independent origins) nothing is better supported than ketamine or memantine, and nothing is clearly worse: every row is thin.** Ketamine and memantine have NO measurement in any WWOX system (cards F04, F01: "No memantine or perampanel has been given to any Wwox system"); the only NMDAR pharmacology in a WWOX system is d-APV in neuron-specific knockout cortical slices (n = 5 slices, 3 animals, one preparation; IT56-Q02: the NMDAR thread's pillars rest on one laboratory). Lithium and ethosuximide have the only in vivo WWOX-model drug data, one provoked-seizure experiment (Cheng 2020; IT56-Q07). Vigabatrin, the ketogenic diet and cannabidiol have case-level WWOX clinical reports with unknown denominators. Gene addition (AAV9) has the strongest WWOX-model rescue and is outside the pharmacological proposals. So the honest answer to "better alternatives, which" is: **none is better supported on WWOX-system evidence; the classes are not comparable across the other axes, and the ordering depends on the endpoint.**
2. **By endpoint (separate; never pooled):**
   * **Rescue of status epilepticus.** Ketamine's evidence is uncontrolled paediatric series (Kuki 15/18 on a seizure-reduction endpoint, Rosati 6/9 on resolution, DeVine 3 infants) plus one halted randomised trial (KETASER01, 10 patients, 2/5 vs 2/5, "No conclusions can be drawn") and one first-line randomised trial in generalized convulsive SE (n = 144, a different setting). The established comparators have randomised paediatric evidence for established SE (ESETT, EcLiPSE, ConSEPT: about half respond, no drug superior) and the anaesthetic infusions (midazolam, barbiturate-class) have only small open randomised comparisons and observational data for refractory SE. **No agent in this niche has WWOX-specific evidence.** Ketamine is therefore one of several unvalidated rescue options for refractory SE in general, not a WWOX-matched one on any held measurement.
   * **Chronic seizure control.** Memantine: one randomised crossover trial in childhood DEE (abstract and registry only; 9/27 vs 2/27 responders on a composite; analysis not stated; full text blocked) plus case-level GRIN/ATP1A2 reports and a seizure-worsening signal in some GRIN gain-of-function individuals. Approved agents have multiple placebo-controlled paediatric trials in other DEEs (Dravet, LGS, CDKL5, TSC, infantile spasms) with quantified harms; **none has been tested in a Wwox model** and cannabidiol, vigabatrin, ACTH-class and the ketogenic diet have only WWOX case-level reports. On paediatric randomised DEE evidence, the approved agents are far better supported than memantine and ketamine; on mechanism match to the one WWOX slice finding, the NMDAR class is the only class with a drug-class pharmacology in a WWOX system (via d-APV, a tool compound).
   * **Development.** No agent in any row has a development endpoint with randomised or WWOX-model evidence; only AAV9 gene addition rescues development in the null mouse. Nothing here compares agents on development.
3. **Harms in children (beside every benefit):** ketamine, short exposure: serious events low and quantified (0.33% in 58,752 sedated children; emergence agitation 1.4 to 7.6% by definition), electrographic seizures after ketamine in children with epilepsy 3/5 in the one small review; repeated or prolonged exposure in children: case reports only (cholangiopathy, cystitis with the S-enantiomer, withdrawal) and PICU infusion series (hypotension 13/24 at a median 2.4 days); human developmental neurotoxicity not established (label and small cohorts, signals both ways). Memantine: well tolerated in autism and clinic cohorts, seizure-disorder safety not established by the label, seizure worsening in 3 or 4 of 19 GRIN gain-of-function individuals; the DEE trial's harm sections were not readable. These are not a recommendation either way.
4. **Testability in the pilot list:** ketamine and memantine are testable only as members of the NMDAR pore-blocker arm (arm table cells C007, C011, C054, simulation tier 3, parked; no WWOX measurement of any pore blocker); d-APV is the positive control; the GluN2B NAM arm is a separate class (radiprodil is the only member with a registered randomised paediatric trial, in GRIN-related disorder). T-type (U17, U25) and lithium (U24) are testable in the Cheng-type provoked assay with a same-day control (one shared design, IT56-Q07). The PV tool arm is a tool, not a drug.

## What the comparison does and does not allow
* **Not comparable across axes:** evidence of a mechanism in a WWOX preparation (thin, one origin per row), randomised paediatric evidence in other DEEs (thick for approved agents, thin for NMDAR antagonists) and harms in children (quantified for ketamine short exposure and memantine in autism, absent for prolonged ketamine in DEE) answer different questions; no ranking by a single score is made.
* **What would change the picture most:** (1) a ketamine or pore-blocker arm with a drug-by-genotype contrast in the WWOX slice or in vivo (the input's own follow-up 1); (2) a per-child account of any WWOX child exposed to any of these agents (an operator item, not an agent task); (3) the memantine trial's full text with harms; (4) a second laboratory's WWOX NMDAR slice result (IT56-Q02, no independent origin found).
* **A statement the evidence does not support:** "mechanism-matched in a way nothing else in current practice is" (K12). Lithium and ethosuximide have drug measurements in a WWOX model (one experiment), d-APV is a tool compound and not ketamine, and the approved agents have randomised paediatric DEE evidence ketamine lacks. The statement is NOT SUPPORTED as worded; "the only drug-class pharmacology in a WWOX slice is NMDAR (d-APV)" is supported as an observation about one preparation.
* Limits: abstract-level evidence for most rows; classes not searched in P5 (Kv7 and T-type paediatric trials) are marked; ChEMBL potencies are single-origin transcriptions; no BLOCK-1 triage was run for any row.

## Update 2026-11-17 (IT; after ADV's blind verification of sampled rows; the text above is unchanged; corrections in `CORRECTIONS_AFTER_BLIND_VERIFICATION_IT_20261117.md`)
* **Ketamine row, label wording:** 'label cautions >3 h in young children' is the label's CLASS statement (section 5.5); the primate result in the same label is that 3 hours of ketamine did NOT increase neuronal cell loss (the increase is described for regimens of 5 hours or longer, with isoflurane), and the label says the clinical significance of these nonclinical findings is not known.

* **Rescue-niche ketamine evidence:** the DeVine infants' resolution is not attributed to ketamine by the timeline in the full text (see the summary); the first-line randomised trial (40186980) reports higher seizure cessation and lower repeat-midazolam and intubation with ketamine plus midazolam and lists no ketamine adverse events; the harms line quoted from it in P5 lost its subject.
