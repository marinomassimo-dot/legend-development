## P2 (paediatric ketamine series)
* **P2-03 DeVine 2023 (37139250, full text):** (a) 'retrospective' is not stated (the paper calls itself 'A Case Series'); (b) 'no genetic aetiology' is not stated (the text lists abusive head trauma, ischemic stroke and ischemic injury after cardiac arrest and never mentions genetics); (c) **material for K7:** "In 2 of 3 cases, seizures resolved with therapy including ketamine" is the authors' Discussion sentence, but the full text shows a temporal disconnect: patient 1 had "five days of ketamine infusion without successful control of subclinical seizures" and EEG resolution documented on hospital day 14, after ketamine was weaned and on four maintenance antiseizure drugs; patient 2 (second admission) had resolution on a spot EEG 15 days AFTER ketamine was discontinued; patient 3 died after withdrawal of life-sustaining care and "it is unclear if seizures were controlled on continuous ketamine". **Carry as: the authors state 2 of 3 resolved on therapy including ketamine; attribution to ketamine is not supported by the timeline; the pooled figure has 0 to 2 DeVine responders depending on the reading, so the pooled count lies between 21/27-type figures and 23/30, with no ketamine-attributable DeVine response established.**
* **P2-06 (26189786, Meyer):** 'retrospective' is not stated in the abstract (a protocol used since November 2009 in all referred patients); the overlap with Rosati 2012 is an inference from the shared start date and the shared Florence unit; every number matches.
* **P2-11 (38926769):** the 38.8 to 59.5% mortality is for 'larger case series', not for adults (those series include children: Gaspard 2013 is '12 P + 46 A'); 0 to 36.4% for smaller series. The verifier also corrected one of ADV's claim lines: in 38926769 'RS' is Rett syndrome.

## P4 (harms)
* **P4-16 (39918155):** '70% with epilepsy' is 94/133 (7 studies that report epilepsy history), not of 141; 3/5 children versus 6/33 adults is limited to the four studies that separate them; 'single administration' is not in the source; 0/39, 26/94 and 3/5 versus 6/33 are exact (the studies are 40 to 50 years old, some in volunteers).
* **P4-28 (Rosati 23197747):** median 6 days (range 2 to 26) refers to the 8 super-refractory patients, not all 9.
* **P4-38 (cholangiopathy case 42997969):** 'treated for super-refractory SE' is inferred from a keyword; the title and keywords do not say ketamine was given for it; n = 1 from 'a child' plus Case Reports.
* **P4-48 (29628409):** 'single dose' is not in the abstract; non-randomised prospective cohort, control n = 16, the authors' 'preliminary' caveat; ASQ 221 versus 230 (P = 0.55) also given.
* **P4-56 (Ketalar label):** the primate result is that 3 hours of ketamine did NOT increase neuronal cell loss; the increase is described for regimens of 5 hours or longer (with isoflurane); the '>3 hours' wording is the class statement in label section 5.5; the label says 'the clinical significance of these nonclinical findings is not known'.
* **P4-72 and P4-74 (memantine in GRIN, 41489401):** the Results name THREE individuals (#10, #25, #32) with increased seizure frequency; the Discussion says 'Four individuals' but names three, and the 'ref 16' after #25 is a superscript citation, not individual #16 (a possible LoF); the authors say cause and extent 'remained unclear in these three cases'. In the LoF/indeterminate/no-effect group (15 of 34) the 3/15 'rare adverse events' are NOT stated to be seizures (the full text itemises one: mild side effects). The 19 gain-of-function individuals are the delegate's subtraction (34 - 15), INFERENZA. **Carry as:** increased seizure frequency in three named individuals with gain-of-function variants (the Discussion says four but names three; cause unclear); 3 of 15 rare adverse events in the other group, type not stated as seizures.
* **P4-30 and P4-64:** not errors of the producer: the sources are internally inconsistent (CI upper 9.88 in Table 2 versus 9.84 in the text; irritability/aggression 8.3 versus 4.9 percent in Table 2 versus 6.7 versus 3.3 and 6.7 versus 4.9 in the text).
## P5 (alternatives)
* **P5-13 (UKISS-type trial 15541450):** 'open' is not in the abstract (masking not stated); omitted: two infants allocated tetracosactide and one allocated vigabatrin received prednisolone.
* **P5-28 (cenobamate in LGS):** 'retrospective' not stated; 'RR' not defined in the abstract; the 12 of 16 responders is for ALL 16 patients (median age 20, 8 paediatric), not for paediatric patients; the increase in three patients was a dose-related exacerbation (improvement up to 50 mg/day, exacerbation at up to 350 mg/day, back to baseline after reduction; descriptors of what the study reports, not guidance) and 3 patients (19%) discontinued.
* **P5-43 (ketamine plus midazolam versus placebo plus midazolam, first-line, 40186980):** the registry number NCT05779657 is not in the stored record; the 'harms' quote is trimmed and loses its subject: the abstract says the ketamine-plus-midazolam group had HIGHER seizure cessation and LOWER repeat-midazolam and intubation; it lists no ketamine adverse events ('no significant differences in other outcome measures'); blinding of the placebo is implied, not stated; grants field empty.
* **P5-56 (dextromethorphan):** both sources used COMBINATIONS (dextromethorphan plus sodium benzoate in the sibling paper; ketamine plus dextromethorphan plus sodium benzoate in the case); the sibling comparison is early versus late treatment, not drug versus no drug: not attributable to dextromethorphan alone.
* **P5-57 (12-child amantadine cohort):** the quoted 4 of 5 is the D/EE-SWAS subgroup; the other 7 (refractory absence including one LGS) had 1 of 7; overall 5 of 12; the 10% adverse-effect figure does not match 1 of 12 (8.3%); no control group.
