# The bridge slice panel: one slice session that orders four agent classes on one network readout in the Wwox-null neocortex (SCI successor window, 2026-11-20; consolidates `../nmdar_holding_potential_sci_20261120/`, v3.1 6.11 and `../slice_burst_non_nmdar_sci_20261120/`, whose outcomes O1 to O6 this page replaces by decisions; hypothesis level; concentrations are bath research values, not recommendations; no dose, no schedule, no recommendation for or against any use of any agent in any person; reference genotype class only; not medical advice)

## What the panel is
One session in the slice assay already characterised for the S-KO line (Breton 2021, held full text: Synapsin-Cre Wwox deletion; acute neocortical slices; layer II/III pyramidal cells and local field potential; P13 to P17; 35 degrees; 1.3 mM Mg2+): spontaneous network bursts, **burst frequency and duration** as the readout, graded concentrations with washout, every agent in the same slices. Hours, not weeks; the controls exist in the paper. **Controls:** d-APV 50 micromolar as the positive control (eliminated the bursts, recovered on washout); carbenoxolone 100 micromolar as the second (frequency -87 percent, p = 0.008, duration -18 percent). Reported with every result: Mg2+, burst frequency and duration against baseline, resting potential at the end of each recording (a depolarising effect is a result, not noise), the GluN2 composition where an NMDAR agent is read, and the holding potential where one is clamped.

## What the panel would change in the pharmacological decision, and the decision each result leads to
The decision at stake is the **order of the four classes at network level in the null cortex** and **which class, if any, earns a second place beside ethosuximide in the bridge pilot**. A result is read only if d-APV eliminates the bursts in the same session (the assay reproduced).
| Result in the S-KO slice | Decision it leads to |
|---|---|
| A class suppresses both burst frequency and duration at a concentration **inside its held exposure band**, with no depolarisation and no increase of bursts | **That class goes ahead of every class that does not, as the next network-level step; among classes that do, the order is by the ratio of the effective concentration to the top of the band; classes with no band are ordered by potency only and said to be so.** The class becomes eligible as an exploratory arm of the bridge pilot on the same condition as the selective T-type arm (v3.1 6.14): tolerable pup exposure measured and no pup tolerability signal |
| Ezogabine acts at 10 uM or less | the Kv7 class moves ahead of ethosuximide **on the network readout**; the narrow window to motor impairment (band top 3.6 uM, motor band 27.3 uM) stays as the class's penalty; **within-class order stays blocked by data** (one opener with a potency spread and a brain concentration-response, one patent row for the next) |
| Ezogabine acts only between 10 and 30 uM | Kv7 is not exposure-compatible on this readout and stays a class-level lead with the penalty restated |
| Ketamine and memantine act at 1 uM or below | the pore blocker class stays as the NMDAR rival; the order between the two is read from the same slices at the GluN2 composition reported |
| Ketamine and memantine act only at 15 uM or more while d-APV works | voltage-dependent relief governs: the pore-blocker class is demoted against a competitive or subunit-selective NMDAR antagonist (a different dossier, not this page) |
| Gaboxadol reduces bursts without depolarising the cells | tonic GABA gets its first network readout in the null; it enters the same rule as every class (band not held, so ordered by potency only) |
| Gaboxadol increases bursts or depolarises the cells | **a harm row for the tonic-GABA class** (the chloride gradient is not held, GABA may be depolarising in these cells at this age); reported as that |
| Ethosuximide shortens bursts at 0.3 to 1 mM | a direct network effect of the lead at brain-compatible exposure; read against the T-type question only with the selective blocker in the same slices |
| Ethosuximide has no effect to 1 mM, and the selective blocker has none | **the T-type class is demoted as an explanation of cortical network bursts only**; nothing changes for its thalamocortical standing, for the PTZ pup experiment, or for ethosuximide's place as the bridge lead (there is no thalamus in the slice) |
| Ethosuximide acts and the selective blocker does not (or the reverse) | an attribution result for the cortical network only: activity of ethosuximide alone points to a non-Cav3 route (the persistent sodium current in native thalamic neurons is the documented alternative); it does not settle the thalamocortical question |
| d-APV does not eliminate the bursts | no reading; nothing above changes |

## What the panel would NOT change
* **The bridge lead and the pup experiment:** ethosuximide stays the lead for the PTZ-provoked pup assay, whose evidence is the Wwox-null pup result of Cheng 2020; a slice burst and a provoked generalised seizure are different network events, and **no slice result ranks agents for the pup assay**.
* **Anything about WWOX specificity or in vivo efficacy:** no published burst or network readout exists in any Wwox-deficient preparation for any non-NMDAR agent; the panel would be the first.
* **The thalamocortical T-type account:** the slice has no thalamus.
* **The ranking inputs, the broad objective, and any statement about a person.**

## Limits
No thalamus (a neocortical slice cannot test thalamocortical T-type effects). The chloride gradient of the S-KO neurons at P13 to P17 is not held (burst recordings used a K-gluconate pipette that sets the recording's own chloride reversal near -63 mV by arithmetic), so a tonic-GABA effect cannot be signed in advance. The burst-state membrane potential was not recorded in the source paper. The nearest published rows come from other substrates (adult kainate-treated rat entorhinal slices for ezogabine and ethosuximide; P5 to P8 mouse slices for gaboxadol; human neocortical slices and P8 to P15 hippocampus for Kv7) and an order of agents in one tissue is not an order in another. Bath concentrations are nominal and brain values are totals, not free fractions. Exposure bands are held only for ezogabine and ethosuximide; the other three classes are ordered by potency, and the page says where.

**Dated line (ADV-110 and ADV's request of 2026-11-20).** The **gramicidin-perforated E_GABA recording** in the same S-KO layer II/III cells at P13 to P17 (wild type beside, sex recorded, bath without bumetanide as the primary condition) is the **shared gate of three classes**: it signs the tonic-inhibition class (gaboxadol as the tool; ganaxolone only if it is later considered, `../tonic_class_adv110_sci_20261120/ADV110_TONIC_CLASS_COMPOUND_COMPARISON_SCI_20261120.md`), the NKCC1 and KCC2 branch (ADV-065, ADV-065b) and the NMDAR slice question (the K-gluconate pipette caveat above); one recording serves all three instead of three. A depolarising or heterogeneous result demotes the tonic class below ethosuximide; a hyperpolarising or shunting result with burst suppression in a plausible band moves it forward. Hypothesis level; no dose; not medical advice.

**Dated line (ADV-117, 2026-11-20; the text above is not edited).** The reading "in band is 10 uM or less" for ezogabine is tightened by the juvenile measurement now read at passage level from the acquired full text (Brown 2016; no receipt): single-dose total brain values in P7 rat pups were about 1.1, 4.3 and 6.6 uM (330, 1296 and 2013 ng/ml tissue; arithmetic, total not free), and the adult mouse electroshock brain EC50 values are 2.23 and 3.58 uM. **10 uM is above every measured juvenile brain value**: a suppression seen only between about 6.6 and 10 uM is read as outside the measured juvenile exposure, like the 10 to 30 uM row. The panel also takes **ICA-069673 as the KCNQ2/3-selective bath tool** (0.7 to 7 uM by arithmetic from its 0.69 uM potency, one document, no exposure found) beside ezogabine, and the ezogabine ladder runs 0.3 to 10 uM with 30 uM kept only as the abolition control. The Kv7 class is not a pilot arm now: a pup tolerability signal exists (repeated dosing at an efficacy-range exposure raised neuronal apoptosis in five of eight regions at P7). Detail, rows and the XE991 case: `../kv7_class_adv117_sci_20261120/ADV117_KV7_CLASS_COMPOUND_COMPARISON_SCI_20261120.md`. Hypothesis level; no dose; not medical advice.
