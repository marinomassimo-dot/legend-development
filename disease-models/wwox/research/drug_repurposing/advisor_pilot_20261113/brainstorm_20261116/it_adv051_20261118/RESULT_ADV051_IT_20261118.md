## Answer first
1. **Are lithium and ethosuximide more promising than the NMDAR-directed agents? On the one endpoint where anything was measured in a Wwox-null mouse, provoked seizures, yes in the plain sense that they have a measurement and the NMDAR agents have none; but neither effect is shown to be WWOX-specific, and the two are promising for different reasons.** On every other named endpoint nothing is measured for any of the agents, so no ordering is supported.
2. **Lithium looks general (INFERENZA).** It lowered the Racine stage in all three genotypes (Cheng 2020, held SCI reading); in wild-type mice acute lithium raises the PTZ clonic threshold (abstracts, one to two origins: Bahremand/Payandemehr family), but a later paper from the same family found acute lithium pro-convulsive and chronic threshold-raising; in rats the effect is "very weak" in non-kindled animals and lithium "did not affect" PTZ, NMDA, kainate or bicuculline seizures (Schmidt 1986; Ormandy 1991); it potentiates pilocarpine and arecoline many-fold (the sensitisation counter-evidence, a cholinergic-convulsant effect, not PTZ). So the Wwox-null arm can add magnitude, not specificity. Other GSK-3 inhibitors are anticonvulsant in several unrelated provoked models and worse or opposite in others (GSK-3 inhibitors worsened status epilepticus in one study; raised GSK-3 activity slowed kainate epileptogenesis in another): a lithium effect in all genotypes is what a general property predicts.
3. **Ethosuximide cannot yet be called knockout-selective (INFERENZA).** It is a classic anti-PTZ and anti-absence agent in adult animals, but in rats it was "effective only in rats of 25 or more days of age" and "did not significantly change" the PTZ CD50 in animals aged 7 to 18 days (Mares and Velisek 1983, 1987; 1998: no effect at 18 days; abstracts, verified in the saved bytes). Cheng's mice are preweaning (the Methods give postnatal day 14 for the western-blot tissue, the Fig 7 legend says day 20, and the paper says 'preweaning mice' for the seizure assays; the exact age of the PTZ test is not stated in the passages I searched), their wild-type PTZ stages are low (floor), and there is no drug-by-genotype test. A non-response in +/+ and +/- pups is what a young-age effect predicts regardless of genotype; the rat-to-mouse transfer of the age profile is untested (no mouse ontogeny datum found). The T-type mechanism is itself contested (Leresche 1998: no action on thalamic low-threshold currents). Ethosuximide is also not a general anticonvulsant: lower pilocarpine threshold (Turski 1987), ineffective in kainate and guinea-pig amygdala kindling.
4. **Cav3 expression or function in Wwox-null neurons: not found in these queries** (two routes, seven queries; one panel cohort lists CACNA1H and WWOX as separate findings). **GSK-3 in Wwox loss: one measurement** (a p-Ser9 blot, "representative results of four independent experiments", Cheng 2020), no kinase assay, no beta-catenin or Wnt readout in Wwox-null tissue (PubMed 4 hits, Europe PMC 6, one relevant); "active GSK-3beta" is an inference from p-Ser9. Cell-line data point both ways (WWOX knock-down raised GSK-3beta phosphorylation and Tau phosphorylation in SK-N-SH; WWOX overexpression lowered GSK-3beta-driven Tau phosphorylation in SH-SY5Y); WWOX-Dvl binding restrains Wnt in cancer lines. **Two readings predict opposite beta-catenin signs** (a p-Ser9 fall lowers beta-catenin; Dvl release raises Wnt output); neither is measured in Wwox-null brain.

## Order of arms for a first in vivo Wwox-null experiment (a design proposal, hypothesis level; the decision is the operator's and the laboratory's)
Common frame (SCI's ADV-046 part A): one session, all three genotypes at the same postnatal age, vehicle and PTZ alone as the same-day control; per-animal summaries and the genotype-by-treatment term fixed before the first animal; a scale chosen beforehand so that a proportional effect and a floor can be told apart (a PTZ stimulus that gives the wild-type pups a measurable, non-floor response is part of the design). Seizure endpoints by a scorer blind to genotype and arm.
1. **Ethosuximide first**: it is the only arm whose outcome changes the decision (selective or age-restricted), and lithium's behaviour in all genotypes is already shown, so the same-session lithium arm adds little except as a positive control.
2. **Lithium second, in the same session** (positive-control-like arm; shares the control), with **one second GSK-3 inhibitor of a different class** and **one non-GSK-3 general anticonvulsant comparator effective at all ages** (valproate in the rat ontogeny data) to separate "GSK-3 route" from "general property". Tool compounds named by package B: TCS2002 and SB216763 (BIO-acetoxime and TCS2002 are one origin).
3. **NMDAR pore-blocker arm last in vivo, and the slice arm first**: the matched-block slice arm of brief 13.11 (Breton S-KO preparation, with SCI's Addendum 14 readout design) needs no animal harm assessment and is independent of the in vivo assay; run it before, and enter the pore blocker into the in vivo assay only with the harms and the stopping rules (provoked-seizure aggravation) written before the first animal. Package A adds nothing on this arm.
Before any T-type claim: a Cav3 measurement in Wwox-null tissue (RNA, protein or current), or an isoform-selective blocker with genetics (the TTA-A2 plus Cav3.1/Cav3.2 knockout design exists in MES); ethosuximide alone cannot establish a T-type route. Before any GSK-3 claim: p-Ser9 after lithium in the same animals, a kinase or beta-catenin readout in Wwox-null tissue.

## Next compound or class (one line each; does it deserve the next loop step?)
* **Valproate**: general-anticonvulsant comparator, now (in the arm list above); boxed warnings (hepatotoxicity, higher risk under 2 years; fetal risk), its GSK-3 action is contested. **Yes, as a comparator.**
* **A second GSK-3 inhibitor (TCS2002, SB216763; CHIR99021 is described by its authors as a Wnt activator)**: tool arm to separate route from property. **Yes, as a tool.**
* **Zonisamide**: T-type-active, infantile-onset series (11, 87 and 127 children; abstracts), Cav3.1 shift corrected in vitro (adult SCA42 cells), and two single WWOX-class case reports where it did not relieve seizures (n = 1 each, no inference). **Low prior; a look, not an arm.**
* **TTA-A2 with Cav3.1 genetics; apinocaltamide; Z944**: need a Cav3 measurement in Wwox-null tissue first; only healthy-adult human data for apinocaltamide. **Not yet.**
* **Inositol add-back with lithium (lithium-pilocarpine)**: titles only. **Mechanism tool, not now.**
* **Tideglusib**: only adolescent safety data (16 subjects, 13 to 34 years), no seizure-model result. **No.**
* **Sulindac (Dvl inhibitor)**: tool for the Wwox-Dvl-Wnt axis, one group. **No.**

## Update 2026-11-18 (IT, after ADV's pivotal check `adv051_check_adv_20261118/ADV051_PIVOTAL_CHECK_ADV_20261118.md` and my own look at Cheng 2020 Fig. 7 panels b and d; the text above is not edited)
(a) Confirmed: no age is stated for the PTZ assays in the held text (preweaning); the supplement PDF is unsearched. (b) Confirmed by eye: +/+ PTZ-alone peaks near 1.3 (panel b, N = 20) and near 1.2 (panel d, N = 12) on a 0 to 6 scale, +/- about 1, -/- about 2.5 (panel b, N = 6) and about 3.5 (panel d, N = 6). (c) Still ASSUMED: the rat ontogeny transfer. Three readings added, all by eye, hypothesis level: (1) in the early window the drop with ethosuximide looks proportionally similar in +/+ and -/- (about 1.3 to 0.8 and about 2.5 to 1.8, roughly a third); the large knockout difference appears late (from about minute 35, about 2 to 2.7 against 0.5 to 0.8), when +/+ and +/- are already near 0.2 to 0.5; so 'significant only in the knockout' may reflect baseline size, variance and floor (the weakest of the three; not a test). (2) Lithium is significant at the wild-type floor (about 1.2 to about 0.2, four asterisks), as a general property predicts. (3) The -/- PTZ-alone groups of panels b and d are different cohorts with different curves, so 'lithium better than ethosuximide' is a cross-cohort impression, not a tested difference. Consequence for the arm order: unchanged in order; ethosuximide remains the arm whose outcome decides selective against age-restricted, now with an age series in wild-type pups as a control that needs no null animal (ADV's design idea) and the scale fixed beforehand.
