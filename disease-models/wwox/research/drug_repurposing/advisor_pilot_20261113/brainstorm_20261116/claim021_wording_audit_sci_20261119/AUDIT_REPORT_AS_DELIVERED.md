(b) Exact source words for the non-SUPPORTED triples

- **T1.** "The mechanism through which Wwox S-KO mice get seizures remains elusive." "These data suggest that the control of pyramidal neuron membrane potential is a factor underlying the neocortical network hyperexcitability of Wwox S-KO mice." The title's "directly destabilizes" is nowhere in the article.

- **T6.** "However, there was no significant difference in the slope of the curve in the S-KOs as compared to the S-WTs and S-HTs". "Because the S-KO and S-HTs were also more depolarized (Table 1), they may have taken less current to depolarize the membrane to action potential threshold." "Since these data suggest that the hyperexcitability of the network was not due to action-potential dependent frequency changes". The Abstract, however, does say "increased action potential frequency", and the Discussion says "increased evoked spike frequency". The "more action potentials" evidence rests on Supp Fig S4, which I do not hold. This is an attestation, not a reading.

- **T8.** "(* p = 1.5e-5; s-WT vrs s-KO p = 3.4659e-23, s-HT vrs s-KO p = 5.4976e-26". "(S-CTLs n = 18 cells from 10 animals, S-KOs n = 17 cells from 10 animals; s-WT: N = 14 cells, 5 animals; s-HET N = 17 cells, 8 animals)". The separate sEPSC arms (14 + 17 = 31 cells) do not match S-CTL = 18 cells. For sIPSC, 7 + 7 = 14 matches. S-CTL occurs 9 times, all in sEPSC/sIPSC text, in the Fig 4 legend, and in Methods > Animals.

- **T13.** No direction statement exists in the source. For Table 1 (not pooled), the HT values lie between WT and KO (RMP -79.25, -75.51*, -73.62*). For the pooled sEPSC and sIPSC there is no S-WT vs S-HT test, so I could not verify the direction. The p-values given are inconsistent. sEPSC amplitude: "s-WT vrs s-KO p = 3.4659e-23, s-HT vrs s-KO p = 5.4976e-26" (HT further from KO). sIPSC amplitude: "sWT vrs sKO p = 1.8341e-100; sHT vrs sKO p = 2.5249e-69" (HT closer to KO). These depend on event counts, so they are only suggestive.

- **T15.** "This indicated" is not the wording; the source's firmer statements are: "These bursts were NMDAR and gap junction dependent." Highlights: "Bursts are NMDAR and gap-junction dependent". Fig 3 legend title: "Neocortical bursting activity is initiated by NMDA receptor-dependent excitatory currents." Discussion: "we identified that NMDAR-associated glutamatergic currents initiate neocortical network bursts in Wwox S-KO slices."

- **T20.** "Given we showed an increased population spike response in the CA3, likely there is hippocampal hyperexcitability, related to axonal conduction, that future studies can address. Also, we cannot discount the possibility of subthreshold excitability changes within hippocampal neurons". Results: "there was an elevated PS amplitude in the S-KOs as compared to S-WTs... with one notable exception." The source says "mechanism of hyperexcitability that predominantly affects neocortical circuitry", not "pathology".

- **T21.** Results give lags only (median 18 ms and 33 ms, with no distance and no velocity). "This directionality was not apparent at the higher frequency oscillations." Legend Fig 2E: "100-400 Hz shows layer V precedes layer II/III by a median of 9.5 ms." "Hence, the Wwox S-KO spontaneous neocortical bursting, in vitro, mimics a key biomarker of pediatric epilepsy". The cited biomarker is "the amplitude of interictal HFOs (> 80 Hz) was strongly coupled to the phase of 5-10 Hz oscillations (Nariai et al., 2011)". Also: "Elevated delta-HFO PAC is observed in the spike-wave complexes of rats...", while the paper's own ranges are delta-gamma and theta-HFO. Delta (0.5-3 Hz) does not lie within the cited 5-10 Hz phase range.

- **T22.** "Whether this phenomenon indicates cell-type specific influence over excitability (such as through impaired differentiation), or rather a generalized effect (such as hypomyelination) that manifests into bursts only in the cortex, remains to be investigated." "Our previous work showed neocortical hypomyelination in the neuron-specific Wwox knockout model". "Though it is not clear whether the Wwox-null mice exhibit a similar electrophysiological phenotype". Introduction: "a characterization of the EEG abnormalities in Wwox knockout mice has yet to be done". Recordings are from P13-17 slices of a Synapsin-Cre model; no EEG is reported here. "Primary" and "not only secondary" are not stated.

- **T25.** "slices were incubated in artificial cerebral spinal fluid (ACSF) containing (in mM): 123 NaCl, 25 NaHCO3, 10 glucose, 3.5 KCl, 1.3 MgSO4-7H2O".
