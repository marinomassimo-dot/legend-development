## Answer first
**BOUNDED STOP (Reading C held): the paper shows concordance under ONE compound, with no downstream or dependency arm. INV-08 X1 and the candidate order stay as written.**
1. **No arm separates the branches.** The only manipulations are the frataxin shRNAs (two hairpins), a scrambled control, omaveloxolone alone (50 nM; 25 nM in the last figure) and honokiol with omaveloxolone. There is no ferroptosis inhibitor, no iron chelator, no GPX4 inhibitor or knockdown, no NFE2L2 knockdown or inhibitor, no erastin-type challenge. The sentence that carries the mechanism is an inference in the Discussion ("the effect of omaveloxolone stabilizing and activating NRF2 explains the increase in GPX4 ... and, therefore, the decrease in lipid peroxidation"), not a result. Honokiol is a second compound, so the combination does not separate branches either (its synergy claim is hedged by the authors: "further studies are needed to confirm synergism").
2. **The one intervention moves the upstream lesion too.** Omaveloxolone started 6 h after lentiviral transduction, before frataxin falls (the authors: levels decline from 24 h), and in the first hairpin it "almost fully prevented the reduction in frataxin levels" (about 0.5 to 0.8 of control by eye, Fig 1B); in the second hairpin the frataxin change is not significant. A rise in frataxin, iron and NRF2 target changes at once is the case where outputs normalising together cannot give a causal path; it is also prevention at the start of knockdown, not rescue of an established deficit.
3. **Within the paper, the markers dissociate from each other.** (i) In the second hairpin (frataxin about 0.2) the peroxidation ratio drops, marked significant in Fig 5G, while survival (Fig 1D), frataxin (Fig 1B) and GPX4 (p = 0.065, Fig 5B) do not reach significance. (ii) Lipid peroxidation is only "partially reduced" (the authors' Results: a 40 % and a 60 % decrease from 2.2-fold and 3.4-fold, which leaves about 1.3 and 1.4 times control by arithmetic on the text, not measured on the figure), while the abstract says "almost completely rescued" and the Discussion says "to control values". (iii) Reduced glutathione and total glutathione were not restored in neurons ("the drug was not able to reverse such decrease"). (iv) In control neurons omaveloxolone raised total NRF2 yet left GPX4 and the peroxidation ratio at control level (Fig 5B, G), so a rise in NRF2 is not by itself a rise in GPX4; the GPX4 gain appears only in the deficient cells. (v) In the donors' fibroblasts total NRF2 was HIGHER than in controls (opposite in sign to neurons), frataxin did not change under the drug, mitochondrial superoxide did not change, GPX4 did not reach significance, and yet the glutathione ratio and MDA were reported as normalised: peroxidation moved without the node the NRF2 route is supposed to pass through.
4. **No WWOX inference.** Rat neonatal DRG neurons with frataxin shRNA and fibroblasts from three FA donors against three controls; nothing here is a WWOX-deficient cell. The paper's own caveat is that the fibroblast results are "supportive for patient stratification or biomarker development".

## Against the held INV-08 X1 material
* **O3 already routes the case that matters.** 'Target activity already high or the risk panel worsens' moves the question to the downstream deficit; O2 and O4 to O7 carry the NFE2L2-loss dependence arms. Portillo adds no arm that these do not already carry, and gives no number that changes a threshold.
* **When a downstream rescue would buy a distinguishable outcome (not added).** Only if the X0 risk panel (GSH, GPX4, lipid peroxidation by genotype, ladder L3) shows a peroxidation lesion in the WWOX-deficient neural preparation. The held WWOX rows show no such lesion: W08 records nothing measured in a WWOX-deficient neural system, W07 is a cancer-cell abstract with a ferroptosis endpoint and the opposite direction to W01 and W02. So the arm would have nothing to separate, and ordering it before another NRF2 chemotype is not supported. If L3 later shows the lesion, the downstream arm becomes a design question for that day, with its competing explanation (peroxidation as a marker of cell loss or of lower frataxin-like iron stress, not a driver) and a rejecting observation (a selective peroxidation block that leaves survival unchanged while the NRF2 probe still rescues it); I record this as a conditional, not an addition.
* Nothing in the paper changes the compound order, adds a compound, or touches INV-08 X0, X2 or X3.

## One transferable distinction (optional, at an ordinary INV-08 edit, not applied now, no hold)
"A risk-panel or marker normalisation under one compound, in a model where the same compound also moves the upstream lesion, is concordance and does not identify the causal path; a rise in NRF2 in non-deficient cells that leaves GPX4 and peroxidation unchanged shows that NRF2 up is not GPX4 up. A selective downstream arm is worth adding only after the genotype baseline shows the downstream lesion." Source: this record.
