# Final text applied by IT35 (v4: round 3 of both delegates; the E7 repair is the delegate's own suggested wording and was not re-read)

> ⚠️ **Competing explanation for the marker phenotype itself (narrowed 2026-11-28, ADV-328).** Blood chemistry of the systemic Wwox-null mouse is held for P18 only (glucose, total CO2 reported as total bicarbonate, BUN, calcium; n = 3/3/4, white cells n = 2 per group; pH and creatinine not measured; [[claim_registry_current#CLAIM 036]], PMID 19936220), so "acidotic" means reduced total CO2 and "leukopenic" rests on two animals per group. Glucose has a longer record: a second paper plots tail-tip glucose of untreated or GFP-injected Wwox-null pups (one pooled line) below wild type at every plotted age from day 10 (about 85 against 122 mg/dL, read from the plot) to day 20 to 24 (about 45; n = 5 per group, SEM, no test printed; nothing is plotted before day 10; PMID 34747138, Appendix Fig S1C and D). Hypoglycaemia and acidosis could alter interneuron marker expression and glial reactivity; neither paper tests that. A systemic null therefore carries a metabolic confounder. The held data do not show that confounder to be independent of neuronal Wwox, and they do not separate it from seizures and growth failure. Pups given a Synapsin-I-promoter AAV9-WWOX at P0 (the plotted percentage of NeuN-and-WWOX double-positive cells is about 52 to 71, denominator undefined; WWOX not detected in oligodendrocytes or in the peripheral tissues examined, with one pancreas panel visibly stained but weaker than wild type and not quantified; astrocyte, microglial and OPC status not examined) have plotted glucose overlapping wild type, and are described in the text as growing normally (the plotted weights in Figs 1B and 2B sit below wild type at most ages; indistinguishable by 6 weeks per the text) and as having no spontaneous seizures (a text statement, unquantified, with no EEG or seizure scoring) (PMID 34747138); their GFAP-positive and Iba1-positive cell counts per field in hippocampal CA3 at P19 are lower than in untreated nulls (n = 3 per genotype, SD, t-test; no wild-type-against-rescued comparison is marked; Fig 3E to G). The conditional allele has been used since 2021 (PMID 33914858; the flox allele itself was documented earlier): Synapsin-Cre deletion reproduces spontaneous seizures from P9 (text) and Nestin-Cre deletion tremors or seizures (onset age not stated), and both reproduce growth retardation and early death (about day 17 to 28 on the survival plots; the text says 3 to 4 weeks), while Olig2- and Gfap-Cre deletion showed no abnormality in gross appearance, weight or survival. The P17 bulk RNA-seq of Synapsin-Cre knockout against control (cortex and hippocampus pooled, 6 against 6 samples, not balanced by tissue) shows higher Npy, Sst and Gfap and lower Pvalb mRNA (Pvalb adjusted P 0.034; on the reader's own split of the normalised counts the Pvalb decrease is small in cortex and larger in hippocampus); no protein or section readout of an interneuron marker or of glial reactivity, and no blood chemistry, is reported for any conditional or null animal in that paper. A cell-restricted deletion therefore does not by itself separate a cell-autonomous marker change from one secondary to seizures and growth failure, because the neuron-restricted animals have both, and the cell-restricted lines without a phenotype (Olig2-, Gfap-Cre) have no transcriptome and no interneuron or glial-reactivity marker data (O-KO has myelin basic protein staining only; G-KO none). Not found in the held texts (dated 2026-11-28): an interneuron-restricted deletion, or a deletion read before seizure onset (P9) with glucose measured in the same animals (body weights at P4 and P7 exist).
