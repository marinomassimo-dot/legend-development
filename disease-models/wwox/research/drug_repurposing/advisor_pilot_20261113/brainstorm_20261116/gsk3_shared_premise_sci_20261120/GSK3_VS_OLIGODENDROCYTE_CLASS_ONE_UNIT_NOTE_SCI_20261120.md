# GSK-3 inhibition candidate and the oligodendrocyte-differentiation class on one comparison unit (SCI successor, 2026-11-20; no ranking; hypothesis level; no dose, no schedule, no recommendation for or against any use of any agent in any person; reference genotype class only; not medical advice)

Written for ADV, HE and IT. ADV asked for one short note that puts the two candidates on the same unit (action, context, benefit sought, endpoint), names what each outcome of the shared experiment does to each, carries the harm cell of GSK-3 inhibition, and **does not rank them**: they are kept apart on purpose (an upstream, protein-level, partial premise against a downstream, transcript-level premise whose lineage meaning is undecided). Companion: `UNIT_C_PRIORITY_EXPERIMENTS_V2_2_GSK3_PREMISE_PANEL_SCI_20261120.md` (the shared experiment, section A.6). Sources read for this note at source: the interactome result and its blind verifier report (SCI, in repository), Cheng 2020 (held XML, passage and Fig 7 legend), Ma 2017 and Kim 2009 (full text through PubMed Central), the openFDA label record of lithium carbonate, ADV-098 batch 9 (HE, main at 7a9f35635) and the unit C broad case. Abstract-level items are marked.

## 1. The unit

| | **GSK-3 inhibition, chemistry unrelated to lithium** | **Oligodendrocyte-lineage differentiation support** (lead instance clemastine, strongest rival a thyromimetic) |
|---|---|---|
| **Action** | inhibit GSK-3 kinase activity (batch 9: ATP-competitive agents at low-nanomolar potency in vitro; tideglusib non-ATP-competitive and time-dependent; none shown beta-selective by an order of magnitude) | M1 muscarinic antagonism on OPCs (clemastine); thyroid-hormone-receptor agonism (Triac, Sob-AM2) |
| **Context** | neuron-internal: WWOX docks GSK3-beta through a motif in the SDR region (L404); loss of the partner would release GSK3-beta toward tau. | **Premise status** | **protein-level, partial**: one partner meets the three criteria with narrowings; no substrate measurement in a Wwox-deficient neuron; the only held tissue datum is lower pS9 in Wwox-null brain at P20 (Cheng 2020: representative blot, ratio numbers, n not stated, each ratio to beta-actin), which is in tension with the registry's "S9-independent" wording | **transcript-level**: the deficit is firm in the mouse; its lineage meaning is UNDECIDED (A2); the thyroid premise for the rival is `PREMISE_ABSENT_TH` |
| **Benefit sought** | restore GSK3-beta restraint on tau and neuronal maturation (SH-SY5Y readouts); in the one in vivo Wwox datum, lithium lowered PTZ seizure activity in the null (the text and legend of Cheng 2020 name only the Wwox-/- mice for the LiCl result, while Fig 7d has three genotype panels: the exact vector-path extraction of 2026-11-15 shows the same sign in 86, 99 and 116 of 119 minutes in +/+, +/- and -/-, with the authors' asterisks printed in each panel, so lithium also suppressed PTZ seizure stage in wild type: a general property or a GSK-3 route is not separated) | functional myelination |

| **Q230P** | effect on binding **untested**; Q230P is in the SDR domain at residue 230, outside the docking stretches 296-320 and 388-412 | not addressed by the class |

**The common observable is narrow.** The two candidates share the context (the P17 cortex of the neuronal-deletion line) and the tissue, not the benefit or the endpoint: one speaks of neuronal tau phosphorylation and seizure, the other of myelin. The shared experiment compares them on **what is measured in the same animals**, not on a common benefit; this note does not construct one.

## 3. The harm cell of GSK-3 inhibition (what is read, at which depth)

**3.1 Embryonic cortical progenitors, read at source (full text).** The study ADV and IT's row T4-04 point to is **Ma 2017** (Front Mol Neurosci, PMID 29234272, PMC5712306): in utero electroporation at E14.5 of siRNA against GSK3-alpha or GSK3-beta, ICR mice, analysis at E18.5, **a genetic knockdown in a subset of electroporated cells, not a drug**; n = 3 brains with 8 slices each. Depletion of either isoform left more electroporated cells in the ventricular zone and raised the BrdU-positive fraction (for GSK3-beta alone a sentence-level "four times higher" fraction stayed in the ventricular zone than in control; no fold is given for alpha); GSK3-beta depletion, alone or with alpha, lowered the Tbr2-positive and the Cux1-positive fractions (conversion to intermediate progenitors and upper-layer neurons prevented); GSK3-alpha depletion raised the Tbr2-positive fraction and left Cux1 unchanged. Its own discussion says the double-knockdown phenotype was recapitulated by GSK3-beta depletion alone and that the single-gene knockout mice show no major malformation, and names off-target effects as not excluded. **Kim 2009** (Nat Neurosci, PMID 19801986, PMC5328673), the genetic reference: the mutant is `Gsk3a-/-; Gsk3b loxP/loxP; nestin-cre` (the blind verifier read the genotype string in the NCBI E-utilities XML), so **alpha is a germline null and only beta is deleted conditionally**, controls `Gsk3a+/-; Gsk3b loxP/+; nestin-cre`; it gave massive hyperproliferation of radial progenitors, Tbr2-positive cells lower by 64 percent (plus or minus 2.7) and Tuj1-positive neurons lower by about 60 percent at E13.5 (figure legends), bigger heads, death at P0 (no cause stated); **3 mutant and 3 control embryos, 10 sections each**. The statement that either single deletion alone has no major malformation is the authors' **unpublished observation**, not a shown result, and the authors say a major reduction of GSK-3 signalling is needed. The only drug given to the animals was BrdU; the other agents (DAPT, LY294002) were applied to dissociated cultures. **Reading for the harm cell:** loss of GSK-3 function in embryonic cortical progenitors expands the progenitor pool and blocks neurogenesis; near-complete genetic loss (germline alpha null plus conditional beta deletion) is lethal at birth; loss of one isoform shows no malformation in Kim's unpublished observation, yet beta knockdown alone in a subset of cells reproduced most of the double-knockdown effects in Ma's electroporation (the two sources disagree on single-isoform loss, and Ma names compensation and off-target effects as the open explanations). A pharmacological inhibitor's degree and timing of inhibition in the embryo is not given by either study, and **no pharmacological in vivo study of embryonic cortical progenitors was found by the PubMed queries I ran** (two queries returned none; one returned an in vitro lithium abstract). The relevance to a postnatal window (v2 doses P3 to P16) and to a postnatal exposure is **not read**: both studies are embryonic, and nothing in them speaks for or against a postnatal effect. Abstract-level in vitro support for the same direction: Hashimoto 2003 (PMID 12605892): lithium raised BrdU incorporation in cultures of embryonic rat cortex and of postnatal rat cerebellar granule cells (no concentration read).

**3.2 Lithium's own limits, from the label text** (openFDA SPL record for lithium carbonate, version effective 2026-04-27; raw JSON digest bc941d63b97c81e6ad02aa7cb97b7400616b83da6aff07e797e63246287f4c3a, not stored in git). The label carries a **boxed warning on lithium toxicity** (closely related to serum concentration and can occur at doses close to therapeutic concentrations). Section 5.1: toxic concentrations (1.5 mEq/L or more) lie close to the therapeutic range (0.8 to 1.2 mEq/L); the neurological signs named include ataxia and, among severe manifestations, **seizure**; in rare cases neurological sequelae "may persist" after discontinuation and "may be associated with cerebellar atrophy" (the label hedges); renal manifestations include nephrogenic diabetes insipidus; cardiac manifestations include QT prolongation. Other sections: lithium-induced polyuria, hyponatraemia, chronic kidney disease, an encephalopathic syndrome with antipsychotics, serotonin syndrome, thyroid (section 5.7, "Hypothyroidism or Hyperthyroidism": lithium is concentrated in the thyroid and can inhibit thyroid synthesis and release, which can lead to hypothyroidism; hyperthyroidism cases are also reported), hypercalcaemia, unmasking of Brugada syndrome. Pregnancy: the label says lithium may cause harm when administered to a pregnant woman, with a floppy-baby syndrome in neonates exposed in late pregnancy; for cardiac malformations it says the data are insufficient to establish a drug-associated risk. Pediatric use: safety and effectiveness established **for monotherapy of bipolar I disorder** at ages 7 to 17 years, not established below 7 for bipolar I. (All of 3.2 was checked line by line against the record by the blind verifier, H5; record set id 01c4facd-ed79-4078-ba33-2044de372d0f, version 1.) **Reading for the harm cell:** a seizure-prone genotype and a drug whose toxicity includes seizure and ataxia is a harm that the one in vivo Wwox datum (lithium suppressing provoked seizures) does not address. The class proposed here is **non-lithium chemistry**, so the label is the harm cell of the only agent with an established indication, not of the class.

**3.3 Non-lithium agents (HE batch 9 and IT's abstract-level rows; neither re-read at source by me).** No brain or plasma concentration was found by HE's routes for any of the five agents; the trial registry lists studies and statuses, not adverse events. Tideglusib has the only documented paediatric programmes (congenital myotonic dystrophy from 6 years; adolescents with autism: statuses only). IT's abstract-level rows (T2-03, T3-07): tideglusib described as generally safe and well tolerated in 16 subjects aged 13 to 34 years over 12 weeks; in adult progressive supranuclear palsy 9 percent transaminase elevation and 13 percent diarrhoea; LY2090314 QT prolongation in 3 of 20 (abstracts; not read by me). **No infant or neonatal data for any GSK-3 inhibitor** were found in IT's rows. The harm profile of the class in a Wwox-deficient neonate is **not found by these routes**.

**3.4 The oligodendrocyte class's own harm cell, for symmetry (from the unit C case, verified).** Clemastine: slower callosal conduction with more differentiated cells at one dose only (50 mg/kg/day in healthy mice), so no margin can be read; thyromimetics: peripheral thyrotoxicosis class, a late start gave no myelination gain, harm of T3 in a hyperthyroid-chow demyelination model. Neither harm cell is smaller or larger than the other on these data: they are different kinds (a developmental-progenitor and organ-toxicity harm for the kinase class; a conduction and peripheral-thyroid harm for the differentiation class), and no common scale exists.

## 4. What this note does not claim
No ranking. No claim that either candidate has a premise strong enough to justify an animal beyond the shared v2 design. The parametric-sensitivity result of the simulator (ADV-096, HE, `RESULT_PARAMETRIC_SENSITIVITY_HE_20261120.md`) is cited only as a **caveat**: it finds no class ranking, the variants are not re-fitted to the held baseline, and the simulator cannot represent what the data sheets show decides real agents; it is not an input to anything above. No dose, schedule or use in any person.

## Dated line, 2026-11-20 (SCI successor; MEETING-20261120T061814Z item, approved by ADV): juvenile and neonatal rodent dosing and tolerability for the five non-lithium GSK-3 agents. **This compares evidence bases, not the merit of any agent, and ranks nothing.**
Method: PubMed through the E-utilities `esearch`, `esummary` and abstract `efetch` (plain client), one query per agent with neonatal, pup, juvenile or postnatal terms and a rodent or rabbit species; abstract level only (the PMC XML of the one open record, Selenica 2007, is a publisher stub), titles of the first 12 hits per agent screened. No dose, schedule or recommendation: the figures are the cited studies' own.
* **Tideglusib: pup in vivo records exist.** Neonatal mouse hypoxia-ischaemia (PMID 27378458, Biochim Biophys Acta 2016): **5 mg/kg intraperitoneally 20 minutes before the insult in P7 pups**, smaller infarct at 24 hours and 7 days, higher phosphorylated GSK-3beta Ser9 and Akt, lower GFAP and phosphorylated STAT3, fewer cleaved caspases; pretreatment, an injury model, no tolerability data in uninjured pups reported in the abstract. Juvenile Cdkl5 knockout mice (PMID 29603837, Eur J Neurosci 2018): **treatment in the juvenile period improved hippocampal development and memory, treatment in adulthood did not** (dose not in the abstract); the one record that bears on **age window** for a GSK-3 inhibitor in a genetic neurodevelopmental line. Myotonic dystrophy DMSXL pups (PMID 31383751, Mol Cell Biol 2019): tideglusib "increases postnatal survival and improves growth and neuromotor activity" (dose and tolerability not in the abstract; a muscle and RNA readout, not a lineage or seizure one).
* **AR-A014418: the preterm-rabbit IVH records (same laboratory).** 20 mg/kg intramuscularly twice a day for seven days from day 1 (PMID 29940337, read at passage level in `GSK3_OPC_DIFFERENTIATION_DIRECTION_READING_SCI_20261120.md`); the cortical neurogenesis companion (PMID 30192926, Cereb Cortex 2019): the acute loss of neurogenic progenitors and the later loss of upper-layer neurons were rescued. Injury model; in the uninjured pups of PMID 29940337 the authors report no myelin change ("data not shown").
* **SB216763 and CHIR-type agents: one pup pharmacology record that bears directly on the panel.** Selenica 2007 (PMID 17906685, Br J Pharmacol; **12-day-old rats, five inhibitors and lithium, brain levels set from pharmacokinetic data**): lithium and CHIR98014 (an analogue, **not CHIR-99021**) lowered tau Ser396 phosphorylation in cortex and hippocampus; **SB216763 and alsterpaullone only in the hippocampus; AR-A014418 and indirubin-3'-monoxime in neither region**; and "inhibition of p-tau in brain required several-fold higher levels of GSK inhibitors than the IC50 values obtained in recombinant or cell-based GSK-3beta enzyme activity assays". Consequences, hypothesis level: (a) the tau-phosphorylation panel of v2.2 A.6 has a pup precedent for the readout; (b) the same agent can move the readout in one region and not another and an agent with low-nanomolar potency in vitro (AR-A014418, batch 9) was ineffective in vivo there, which is the batch-9 point that in vitro potency does not give the exposure-effect relation; (c) it is abstract level, concentrations not read.
* **CHIR-99021 and LY2090314: none found.** No pup or juvenile in vivo record for either by these queries (LY2090314: zero records). Absence by one route at abstract level, not a statement that none exists.
* **Tolerability in uninjured pups: not found for any of the five at abstract level.** The pup records are injury or disease-model studies with an efficacy readout; adverse-event reporting is not in the abstracts. Together with HE's batch 9 (no brain or plasma concentration found by its routes) and IT's rows (no infant or neonatal safety data), the evidence base for the GSK-3 non-lithium chemistry in pups is **injury-model efficacy for two agents (tideglusib, AR-A014418), a pup tau-pharmacology panel for SB216763 and a CHIR analogue, and nothing for two**; the oligodendrocyte class has clemastine's neonatal-mouse plasma Cmax and one-dose conduction harm (section 3.4 and HE's dated line in batch 6). The two bases differ in kind and in which agents they cover; **they are not comparable as evidence of benefit or safety and this line makes no order.**

## Dated line, 2026-11-20 (SCI successor; morning step, ADV's condition on the wording "accessible"): is a GSK-3 agent comparable with clemastine in the broad objective on the held and reachable sources? **Identifiable: no, because** the datum that would reverse the comparison is a **pup free-brain exposure of a named GSK-3 agent at a regimen that moves a lineage-stage readout in a non-injury genetic model, against the clemastine efficacy-to-harm margin in the same preparation; neither exists in a source I could read in full.** The clemastine side is already mined (Palma 2022: healthy-mouse harm at one dose, 50 mg/kg/day intraperitoneally from P5 to P20, more APC-positive cells with slower callosal conduction and fewer myelinated axons; Odell 2024: the minimum-effective-dose paper); the GSK-3 side is the thin one.
