# ADV-095 unit C, bridge objective: comparative case and priority experiment (SCI successor, 2026-11-20; hypothesis level; no dose, no schedule, no recommendation for or against any use of any agent in any person; research concentrations and animal doses are findings of the sources; reference genotype class only; not medical advice)

**Verdict in one paragraph.** For the partial or bridge objective (seizure burden reduction with a conservative benefit and risk aim) I prefer **ethosuximide**, weakly, over its strongest rival, a **tonic-inhibition enhancer (gaboxadol as the tool, ganaxolone as the agent with a label)**. The preference rests on three things the held data give and the rivals lack together: the only drug effect in the Wwox-null mouse that points at a channel-class drug (Cheng 2020: ethosuximide 150 mg/kg i.p., n = 6 against 6 in the null, no genotype-by-treatment test), a measured adult-mouse total brain level of the order of its recombinant potency at half that dose, and a label for a seizure type. It is weak because the age of the Wwox-null drug test is not stated, the target link (Cav3.x) to Wwox is unproven, the potency is single-origin and disputed for native tissue, no juvenile exposure and no juvenile harm cell for ethosuximide were collected, and the rival has a juvenile in vivo seizure result of its own. **Which agent is better is undecidable on the held inputs** for the benefit axis; the exposure data decide only whether an agent reaches its target at the regimen. The simulator is not used to rank: it contributes within-arm signs only (the normalisation note of 2026-11-19 stands). The priority experiment is a head-to-head, exposure-controlled test in Wwox-deficient pups.

## 1. Comparison unit
Intervention and action; context (Wwox-null or neuronal-deletion mouse pups before the end of the second postnatal week to P20, the age at which the Cheng drug tests were run is **not stated**); benefit (seizure threshold and burden, not disease modification); competing adverse effects at the effective exposure (sedation, developmental toxicity); generality (null against wild type).

## 4. Preferred, rival, and why
- **Preferred (weak): ethosuximide.** The measured adult total brain level at 72.3 mg/kg is of the order of the recombinant persistent-current IC50 (ratio 1.6, up to 4.8), the Wwox-null regimen is twice that dose, a drug effect in the null exists, and the label covers absence seizures (safety and effectiveness not established below 3 years). Limits that keep it weak: the age of Cheng's drug test is not stated, a recombinant potency (0.6 mM) and a FLIPR value (above 5 mM) at least 8 times apart, a disputed native potency, the T-type transcript flat from P14 to P28 in the one dataset read, no Cav3-to-Wwox link, GIRK potency unread, juvenile exposure and juvenile harm unexamined, n = 6 against 6 with no genotype-by-treatment test, and no demonstration that the effect is not general.
- **Strongest rival: the tonic-inhibition class.** Reasons: an immature-tissue in vivo result at P8 to P9 (THIP reduced kainate-induced behavioural seizures; the slice result is THDOC at P6 to P8) and registered Gabrd and tonic-current predictions; the rat and mouse exposure data are consistent with reaching the delta-dependent concentration (about 1 uM). Against it: a narrow neonatal window (sedating at 5, fatal at 10 mg/kg in the same paper), no label for gaboxadol, a ganaxolone label only for CDKL5 seizures from 2 years, no juvenile exposure, and GABA-A action depends on the chloride gradient at the age tested (held ADV-065 work).
- **Others and why they are not preferred:** a Kv7 opener reaches its target range but the one juvenile exposure data set shows the effective and the harmful exposures overlapping (rat), and the class was withdrawn; the NMDAR pore blockers have a holding-potential-dependent potency that reverses their order, ketamine falls below the block range by 1 h in the one mouse series, and no juvenile exposure exists; lithium has no regimen-level brain value and its effect is not null-specific; d-AP5 stays a tool.
- **Undecidable on the held inputs:** (a) ethosuximide against the tonic-inhibition class on benefit (no common model readout; the simulator gives signs within an arm only); (b) whether ethosuximide reaches its target in the pups of the Cheng test (age, exposure, dose scaling unknown); (c) free against total brain concentration for every agent but ethosuximide, where it is only plausible.
- **What would reverse the preference:** an ethosuximide brain level in pups at the Cheng dose well below 0.6 mM, or a T-type-independent effect in wild type equal to the null effect (general, then the choice is about harm only); a tonic-inhibition agent shown effective in the null at the age of the Cheng test with an exposure in a non-sedating range; a juvenile harm cell for ethosuximide at its effective exposure.

## 5. Priority experiment (hypothesis level; doses are source research doses, not recommendations)
**Question it separates.** At a stated age, does ethosuximide reach and act on its target at the Cheng regimen in Wwox-deficient pups, is the seizure effect disease-specific or general, and does a tonic-inhibition agent do better at a non-sedating exposure?
**Design.** Wwox-null (or neuronal-deletion) pups and wild-type littermates at one stated age; pre-registered arms: vehicle; ethosuximide at three doses around the Cheng dose with total brain and serum measured at the PTZ time and the dose-exposure relation fitted; gaboxadol at two doses below the sedating one with brain level; the genotype-by-treatment test with an adequate n fixed in advance; readouts: PTZ threshold and latency, spontaneous seizure burden if the line shows it, sedation scoring, weight. A T-type-independent control arm (a structurally related inactive compound or the same exposure in a Cav3-null or Cav3-blocked context) if available.
**Interpretable outcomes.**
- *Positive, specific:* effect in the null at brain exposure of at least the recombinant IC50, absent or smaller in wild type, with the dose-exposure relation: supports ethosuximide as the bridge lead and names the age floor.
- *Positive, general:* equal effect in null and wild type: a general anticonvulsant effect (still useful for a bridge aim but stated as such); the choice then rests on harm and label.
- *Negative with exposure:* no effect at brain exposure well above the IC50: the T-type route does not operate in this seizure model; ethosuximide falls and the rival moves up.
- *Negative without exposure:* brain level below the IC50 at the tested doses: a dose or age problem, not a mechanism result.
- *Ambiguous:* effect only at a sedating dose in either class: not read as benefit.

## 6. What this does not claim
No dose, schedule or use in any person. No efficacy probability. Total concentrations are not free concentrations. Absence of a juvenile value in the sources is a statement about the routes searched, not about the literature.

## Dated line (ADV-119, 2026-11-20; the text above is not edited; SCI; hypothesis level; no dose; not medical advice): Brown 2016 apoptosis limits, from a blind verifier and my own check of the held XML (PMC5214840)
(1) The paper declares **one-tailed tests chosen a priori** ("p < 0.05 (one-tailed)"); the repeated-dose comparisons are Mann-Whitney tests, with no correction across the eight regions stated and **no statement whether the printed P values are one- or two-tailed**. If they are one-tailed, doubling them (arithmetic, mine) gives about 0.017 (cingulate), 0.042 (laterodorsal thalamus), 0.050 (retrosplenial), 0.086 (motor) and 0.094 (somatosensory): two regions clearly below 0.05 and one on the line. (2) **The repeated-dose exposure was not measured** ("we did not measure pharmacokinetics with this repeated administration"); the authors judge it unlikely that its Cmax met or exceeded the single 30 mg/kg dose, which gave no apoptosis, and favour a cumulative-exposure reading. The sentence above on an overlap of exposures is therefore a statement about the per-dose level (15 mg/kg, brain value from the single-dose arm), not a measured repeated-dose exposure. (3) "No apoptosis" after a single dose is "no significant increase against vehicle", not zero; dorsal striatum was not assessable; males only. (4) The schedule: Methods three doses spaced by 4 h; Results "3 exposures over a 12 h period"; Abstract "over 24" (unit absent in the source). **Not changed:** five of eight regions at P below 0.05 as printed; the single-dose negative result; the brain values (C2).
