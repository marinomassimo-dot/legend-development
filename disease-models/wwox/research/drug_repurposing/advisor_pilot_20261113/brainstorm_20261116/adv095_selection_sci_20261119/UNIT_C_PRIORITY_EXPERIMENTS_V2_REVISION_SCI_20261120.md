Each is answered below by what changes, in the order IT and ADV gave them, and each design item names its decision. ## A. Broad head-to-head, v2

**A.0 What the experiment is now.** An exposure- and engagement-controlled test, in the Wwox neuronal-deletion line, of whether differentiation support moves the stage-resolved oligodendrocyte deficit of the P17 cortex (v1 question), with the benchmark's functional endpoints as a second tier, and with the two classes aligned in window and the route difference declared.

**A.1 Window and route (IT point 3).** Both arms are dosed once daily from P3 to P16 and read at P17; the P17 read is the age of our held deficit. Clemastine by oral gavage (as in the source for the plasma value, P3 to P10 there, extended here to P16 and declared as an extension: no source dosed it that long); the thyromimetic by subcutaneous injection (as in the Triac source, P1 to P20 there, here from P3 to P16: declared). The route and the duration differ from the sources and from each other; the head-to-head is therefore stated as **"under these windows and routes"** and does not claim route-independence. A late-start thyromimetic arm (P14 to P17) is not added (n); the window dependence of the class stays a literature fact, not a tested one here.

**A.2 Engagement controls (IT point 2).** (i) **Terminal brain level of each agent** (LC-MS/MS) in satellite pups at the plasma Tmax and at 24 h after the last dose, with plasma in the same animals, so that the null class is no longer read on plasma alone; brain values are reported as total, with the tissue basis stated. (ii) **An ex vivo OPC differentiation readout from the same litters**: primary OPCs from neonatal cortex of knockout and control littermates, drug in the medium at the in vitro effective range of the source (10 to 500 nM for clemastine), differentiation by MBP-positive and CC1-positive fractions: it answers whether Wwox-deficient OPCs respond to M1 antagonism at all, independently of delivery in vivo. (iii) **Thyromimetic engagement**: a thyroid-response transcript in the cortex (the Triac paper reports Hairless as responsive) in the treated animals; it is a TH-pathway engagement readout, not a lineage readout. (iv) **Class engagement in vivo for clemastine** has no equivalent transcript: the OPC proportion shift in treated wild-type littermates (the Odell readout, CC1-negative among SOX10-positive) is the in vivo response control; a clemastine arm without a response in wild-type littermates at the confirmed exposure is a failed engagement, not a negative.

**A.3 Arms and sample size (IT point 4).** Arms in knockout pups: vehicle; clemastine at two doses (2 and 7.5 mg/kg/day of the source, the two doses that separated the sources' endpoints); thyromimetic (one CNS-penetrant agent chosen by the laboratory from Triac or Sob-AM2; dose from the source, exposure confirmed by A.2). Wild-type littermates: vehicle; clemastine at the higher dose; thyromimetic (the harm check; the Palma-type conduction harm is read here, not assumed). **n from the held data, order of magnitude:** the P17 cortex deposit gives per-animal scores of the mature-oligodendrocyte set (control minus knockout 0.895 log2 units, pooled standard deviation 0.32, 4 control and 3 knockout animals; `out_a2/other_explanations_per_animal.tsv`) and of the stage-adjusted score (difference 0.334, pooled standard deviation 0.155). For 80 percent power at two-sided 0.05: about 10 animals per arm to detect rescue of half the deficit and about 34 for a quarter of it on the mature score; about 16 and 56 on the stage score (normal approximation plus 2; the standard deviations come from 7 animals and are themselves uncertain). **v2 fixes n = 12 knockout pups per arm** (half-deficit rescue on the mature score, with attrition), 8 wild-type per arm, plus satellite pups for exposure (4 per time point per agent at two time points for two agents, 16) and a few pups per litter for the ex vivo culture, about 100 pups in all, and the litters needed are the number of litters that yield 12 knockouts per arm. A quarter-deficit rescue is **not reachable**; it is declared as not detectable, never as a negative. The three to four animals per arm of the held deposits cannot carry stage-level contrasts, as IT says; the stage-resolved nuclei readout is run on a pooled-by-animal basis with at least 4 animals per arm per class, which makes it a **cell-level secondary**, and the bulk and section counts per animal are the primary.

**A.4 Readouts and the functional tier (IT point 1).** Tier 1 (stage and structure, P17): stage-resolved lineage readout per animal (sections: PDGFRA-positive precursors, committed-precursor, newly formed, mature markers; or nuclei with at least 4 animals per arm), MBP and electron-microscopy g-ratio by axon calibre (inherits ordering row 8), callosal compound action potential (ordering row 9; the harm readout of the Palma source). Tier 2 (the benchmark's endpoints, inherited and named): body weight daily; survival or time to the humane endpoint in the line (if the neuronal-deletion line survives to P20 or longer; otherwise weight and motor only, and this is stated); a motor score and rotarod at the earliest age the line can be tested (the Repudi benchmark used survival, weight and motor); blood glucose at P14 and P17 (ordering row 7; the null is hypoglycaemic from week 2 in the sources). Seizure threshold is dropped from the broad experiment (it belongs to the bridge).

**A.5 Decisions per outcome (IT points 1 and the "general" class).**
- *Positive on tier 1 at confirmed brain exposure with the engagement controls passed, absent harm in wild-type littermates:* **decision: carry to a functional-endpoint study of that class** (the tier-2 readouts as primary endpoints, a longer window, the harm margin by a third dose) and report that the benchmark's non-myelin endpoints were not moved if tier 2 shows no change: a positive that moves tier 1 only is **partial against the benchmark by construction**.
- *Positive on tier 1 and tier 2 (weight, motor or glucose move):* decision: the class is a broad candidate; the rival is tested in the same design; the benchmark comparison is made on tier 2.
- *Negative with brain exposure at the in vitro range and engagement passed (OPCs respond ex vivo and wild-type littermates respond in vivo):* decision: the route does not reach this deficit; drop the class for this context; the other class stays untested if its own engagement control failed.
- *Negative with failed engagement (no brain exposure, wild-type littermates do not respond, null OPCs do not respond ex vivo):* **uninterpretable**; the repeat is named: raise exposure or change route, with the failed control as the reason; not read as a negative.
- *Counts rise but conduction slows or myelin thins (the Palma pattern):* differentiation without functional myelination; the harm cell moves to the front and the decision is to test a lower dose before any carry-forward.
- *Effect only at the higher dose, or only in wild-type littermates (IT's unnamed case):* a dose-limited or general effect on OPCs with no deficit-specific action; **decision: do not carry forward as a disease-directed candidate; record it as a dose-response of the class on the oligodendrocyte lineage**, to be reconsidered only if tier 2 moves.
- *Thyromimetic positive with TH engagement but clemastine negative:* read first as a premise surprise (the registered check found no RNA evidence of reduced thyroid signalling); the thyroid-response transcript in vehicle knockouts versus wild type is read from the same animals before any inference.

## B. Bridge experiment, v2 (it is ordering row 3 plus named additions)

**B.0 Reconciliation (specified once).** The design is **`PUP_BRIEF_V2_CONSOLIDATED.md` sections 2.1 to 2.10 and the stage 2 row, unchanged**: all three genotypes in the same session at the same age, shared vehicle, blind scoring from coded video, S as the area under the stage curve over 60 minutes, litter as the cluster, the wild-type age series, the assay-sensitivity panel at every age, the motor and ataxia pre-test, the status stopping rule, the dose check per age. v2 adds only the four items B.1 to B.4 and the decision table B.5. The v1 text of this experiment (arms, five outcome classes) is superseded by B.0 to B.5 for the points they touch and kept for provenance.

**B.1 The age (IT point 1 and 2 of the bridge; ADV).** v1 said "one stated age" and "names the age floor", which is incompatible. v2: **the null is tested at two ages and the wild-type age series at three.** Assay ages **P14 and P18** for the knockout and control pups. Reasons from the held sources: behavioural seizures are commonly seen after P12 (Cheng 2020), cortical ECoG spikes and spike-wave discharges in null pups from about P14 (a 2026 report, n = 5 per group, nothing earlier recorded), slice bursting at P13 to P17, death at about three to four weeks; P18 is before the apoptosis and vermis findings dated at about P19 to P20 and P14 is the first age with a documented electrographic abnormality. The laboratory may substitute ages from its own colony before data (the pup brief leaves the age to the laboratory); two ages stay. The wild-type series adds P25 (inside the window to about P28) so that a wild-type non-response can be read as age. **The age floor is claimed only from the wild-type series, not from the knockout ages**: v1's "names the age floor" is replaced by "names where the wild-type response appears".

**B.2 Potency and what makes the null interpretable (point 3).** The null of the ethosuximide arm is interpretable **only with a measured exposure and an engagement readout, not with either potency as printed**: (i) total brain and serum ethosuximide at the PTZ time in satellite pups at each age and dose (the dose check of the pup brief, now with the number measured; it replaces "two exposures per agent" by the measurement); (ii) the T-type blocker arm of stage 2 (a selective Cav3 blocker, the laboratory's criterion) with its **engagement control added** (a published Cav3-dependent readout in slices from the same line, or a selectivity-confirmed exposure); (iii) the decision uses the measured brain level against **both** potencies: a null at brain exposure above 5 mM-class levels is a null under both readings; a null between 0.6 and 5 mM is "uninterpretable between the two potencies" and the repeat named is the higher exposure within tolerance or a native-tissue engagement readout. v1's brain level of about 1 mM total (one adult mouse series) is a hypothesis for the pup, not a fact.

**B.3 The tonic-inhibition arm and a decision rule fixed before data (point 4).** Arm: gaboxadol at two doses below the sedating dose of the source series (the sources: 3.5 mg/kg effective, 5 mg/kg highly sedating, 10 mg/kg fatal at P8 to P9; the laboratory sets doses from a pilot per age), on the same shared vehicle and the same PTZ readout. **Decision rule (fixed now): the comparison variable is protection per sedation margin**: for each agent and age, R = mean S treated over mean S vehicle (log scale, litter bootstrap) at the highest dose that passes the motor and ataxia pre-test and does not lose the righting reflex, and the sedation margin = the lowest dose at which any of 8 pups loses the righting reflex divided by the lowest dose with R at most 0.7. The lead is the agent with the smaller R at the highest non-sedating dose when the difference of the two log R values has an interval excluding 0 and exceeds ln(1/0.8); when the interval includes 0, the two are **tied on protection** and the lead goes to the agent with the larger sedation margin; when the margins also tie within a factor of 1.5, **ethosuximide stays the lead by its prior position, gaboxadol the rival, and the decision is named as "no efficacy preference; choose on specificity (B.5) and on harm and label"**. So "both positive" does not collapse into "we would do the same".

**B.4 Power, and which outcome classes are reachable (point 5).** From the held Cheng figures (ADV-061 addendum, ADV-063): the knockout PTZ-alone groups give a log SD of S of about 0.54 to 0.94 and the optimistic 0.3 is not credible; a simple effect of ratio 0.7 needs about 36 to 109 animals per arm; wild-type pups at the stimulus used are at the floor, so a non-floor stimulus is required; an interaction contrast needs about 180 to 320 per cell, 700 to 1,300 animals and 350 to 630 knockouts for one drug against vehicle. **Reachable outcome classes in v2:** an effect in the knockout (simple contrast), a null in the knockout only if the interval falls inside 0.85 to 1.15 (needs the upper end of the arm sizes, so likely unresolved), an age-by-effect pattern in wild type (the series). **Not reachable with the full-hour area summary:** *positive-specific* against *positive-general*. v2 therefore states: unless the pilot finds a log SD of about 0.3 or less, the specificity question collapses to **"underpowered"**, the experiment is read as a **provoked-seizure lead in the knockout**, and specificity is deferred to the INV-04 design (D03/D08) or to the binary generalised-seizure outcome of the pup brief (about 20 to 38 per arm for 50 percent falling to 10 or 20 percent). The pilot (stage 1) is the first step and decides n.

**B.5 Decision table (replaces the five outcome classes of v1; each row names a decision).**
| Outcome at measured exposure, two ages | Reading | Decision |
|---|---|---|
| Effect in the knockout (R at most 0.7, interval excluding 1) in at least one age; wild-type series flat or smaller; sedation pre-test passed | provoked-seizure lead in the knockout; specificity unresolved if underpowered | ethosuximide (or the winner of B.3) is carried to a spontaneous-burden and specificity study (B.6); the age where it holds is named; the wild-type age series names where the non-response appears |
| Effect of the same size in wild type at the same age | general anticonvulsant effect | carried only as a bridge-by-seizure-control agent, stated as general; choice rests on harm and label |
| Interval inside 0.85 to 1.15 at brain exposure above both potency readings, engagement passed | null in the knockout at this assay | ethosuximide falls; the B.3 rival moves up; the age is not the reason (wild-type series and panel valid) |
| Null with exposure between the two potency readings | **uninterpretable between potencies** | repeat at the next exposure within tolerance or add a native-tissue engagement readout; not read as a negative |
| Null at brain exposure below 0.6 mM | **dose null** (the class label "negative without exposure" is uninterpretable, per IT and ADV point 7) | repeat with the dose raised or route changed; not read as a negative |
| Effect only at a sedating dose | not read as benefit | the arm is dropped or redosed; named as such |
| Panel agent fails at an age | assay failure at that age | move the age; the null is not read |
| Interval spanning 0.7 and 1 (unresolved) | underpowered | the n of the next step is set from the observed SD; no decision |

**B.6 Provoked versus spontaneous (point 6).** The benefit sought is spontaneous seizure burden; the PTZ threshold is a provoked assay. v2: **a spontaneous-burden readout is mandatory if the line shows spontaneous events at the chosen age**: video or ECoG per animal over a fixed pre-test window (ECoG spikes are documented in null pups from about P14: ordering row 11 asks for EEG or ECoG before P14 first), analysed in the same animals before the provocation. If the line does not show spontaneous events at P14 or P18, the result is stated as **a provoked-seizure lead**, and the spontaneous-burden study is the named next step. Nothing in v2 claims a spontaneous-burden effect.

## C. What v2 does not claim
No dose or schedule is proposed for any person. No quantity here is a forecast; the n are order-of-magnitude from held data with the uncertainty of 7 animals (broad) and of the Cheng figures (bridge). The two experiments remain proposals for a laboratory; none is started by this note. v1 stays in the two unit C files; this file supersedes it only where it says so.
