# ADV-095 unit A3 result: the activity-dependent (non-cell-autonomous) branch stops at its registered first test (SCI, 2026-11-19; hypothesis level; held data only; main-session turns; no dose, no recommendation; reference genotype class only; not medical advice)

## Answer first
1. **Registered verdict: STOP THE BRANCH** (ADV's rule: stop if the deficit is not neuron-specific in the held nuclei, or not replicated in a second deposit after adjustment). In the held nuclei (hippocampus, P17, one pool per arm) the activity-regulated panel is **not lower in excitatory neurons** (control 0.502, knockout 0.451, difference -0.05, registered line -0.3) and not lower in glia (mean glial difference 0.00); the neuronal depth control moved +0.15. By the registered rule the deficit is therefore neither neuron-specific nor global in these nuclei, which the stop rule treats as a stop. The second-deposit condition is met in the cortex (adjusted -1.26, every leave-one-animal-out fit negative, -1.32 to -1.09) and only weakly in the organoid (pooled adjusted -0.33, cluster values -0.55 and -0.10, "reference moved"), so it is not what stops the branch.
2. **Why the nuclei cannot test the cortex deficit, and what that does and does not mean.** The nuclei are hippocampal (Synapsin-Cre deletion against a Cre-only control), and the bulk hippocampal S contrast of the same cohort family shows only a small panel shift (raw -0.34, against -1.28 in the cortex); the deficit the branch rests on is a **cortex bulk finding**, and no cortical nuclei deposit is held. So the registered test was run on the tissue where the deficit is smallest. The stop is correct under the pre-registration; it is also true that it does not refute a neuronal deficit in the cortex: it means the held data cannot localise it to cell type, and the activity panel in the nuclei is sparsely detected (mean per-nucleus log2 CPM about 0.5), which limits any class contrast.
3. **The across-contrast coupling does not hold.** Panel shift against mature-oligodendrocyte shift (raw, four mouse contrasts): cortex -1.28 against -1.06; hippocampus S -0.34 against -2.11; N -0.66 against -1.60; null -0.80 against -1.80. The oligodendrocyte deficit is largest where the panel deficit is smallest (hippocampus S), the opposite of a tight coupling; four points and different tissues and models, so this is weak, but it removes the supporting observation (the per-animal rank correlation of 0.89 in the cortex was genotype-confounded). The organoid has no oligodendrocyte lineage and a small panel shift (-0.08 raw).
4. **What this changes:** the neuronal-activity pharmacological route (TrkB, neuregulin-ERBB4, AMPA-receptor potentiators) is **not opened as a second broad route on the held data**; the oligodendrocyte-class unit stays the only broad route under test. The non-cell-autonomy reading is kept as an explanation without a route (the deletion is neuron-targeted in the cortex deposit, and the lineage defect is downstream of neurons in that sense, independent of any activity mechanism).
5. **What would reopen it:** a cortical nuclei or cell-type-resolved deposit of the neuron-targeted deletion (or a held per-animal protein or phospho-readout of an activity-dependent target in the cortex), because the neuron-specificity test needs the tissue where the deficit is. Recorded as a supply need for ADV; not asked from the operator.

* Neuronal reference genes (a depth and tissue control): cortex raw +0.05, adjusted -0.19.

## Alternative explanations kept (each with what would speak for it)
Sampling state, handling or time of death (the whole panel lower in every cell type: not seen in the nuclei, but they are not the cortex); seizure history or sedation (the cortex panel lower while the neuronal reference is flat: compatible); dissection or regional carry-over (not tested again here; the A2 regional panel did not follow the oligodendrocyte score, rank correlation 0.29); a shared stage effect (not supported: the covariate removes almost none of the cortex panel shift); ambient RNA (affects the nuclei contrasts, see above).

## DECISIONS_TAKEN
(1) Applied the registered stop rule as written rather than reading the sparse hippocampal nuclei as support or as refutation; alternative rejected: run an agent round for the activity route to keep the branch alive (ADV's third stop condition would then have been tested before the first two). (2) Reported that the stop does not refute a cortex neuronal deficit. (3) Recorded the cortical cell-type-resolved deposit as the supply that would reopen the branch.
