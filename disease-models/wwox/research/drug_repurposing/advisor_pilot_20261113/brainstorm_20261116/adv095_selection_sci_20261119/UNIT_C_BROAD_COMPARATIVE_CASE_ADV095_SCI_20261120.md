# ADV-095 unit C, broad objective: comparative case and priority experiment (SCI successor, 2026-11-20; hypothesis level; no dose, no schedule, no recommendation for or against any use of any agent in any person; research concentrations and animal doses are findings of the sources; reference genotype class only; not medical advice)

**Verdict in one paragraph.** No candidate is convincingly preferable for the broad objective, and I do not manufacture a winner. The mechanism class that the held data leave standing is **support of oligodendrocyte-lineage differentiation** (the cortical deficit is firm in the mouse: mature-oligodendrocyte transcripts lower in all four P17 contrasts; every lineage stage from the committed precursor onward about -0.5 with the precursor pool intact; unit A and A2). Inside that class the **clemastine class (M1 muscarinic antagonism on OPCs)** is the more testable lead and the **thyromimetic class (Triac, the CNS-penetrant prodrug Sob-AM2)** the strongest rival; the preference is weak, and it holds only because the rival has no premise at all in this tissue, not because the lead is well supported. Neither class has a positive model whose starting state matches the Wwox-deficient cortex of A2. A myelin-directed agent can at best move the myelination-structure endpoints of the gene-addition benchmark, not its survival, weight, motor or glucose endpoints; so even a positive result is **partial** against the benchmark. Two comparisons are undecidable on the held inputs and are named below. The priority experiment is a cell-level, exposure-controlled, head-to-head one.

## 1. What the comparison unit is
Intervention and action; context (genotype class: neuronal deletion of Wwox, the mouse P17 cortex we hold; cell type: oligodendrocyte lineage; developmental window); benefit sought (functional myelination, not seizure suppression); endpoint (stage-resolved lineage readout, myelin structure, conduction). Benchmark: AAV9 gene addition (survival, weight, motor score, myelination structure, GFAP, glucose; one laboratory).

## 4. Preferred candidate, strongest rival, and why
- **Preferred (weak): the clemastine class as the lead instance of OPC differentiation support.** Reasons that hold: its target (M1 on OPCs) acts regardless of the upstream cause of the lag; the one neonatal exposure datum (total plasma 0.13 uM) sits inside its in vitro effective range; its positive in vivo state (a differentiation arrest) is nearer A2 than TH deprivation is; the oligodendrocyte deficit it addresses is the one firm broad finding. Reasons against, stated: the positive states have OPC excess where A2 has an intact pool; the positive groups are small; two myelin-deficient rat models gave no benefit; the harm signal has one dose (50 mg/kg/day) so no margin can be read; no quantitative brain, cerebrospinal or unbound value exists (one qualitative juvenile rat brain detection, no concentration).
- **Strongest rival: the thyromimetic class.** Its developmental result is strong where its premise holds (TH-deprived brain; multi-endpoint; a window of P1 to P20), but the premise is not found here (check 5), the late window argues that a P17 deficit may be past it, and its harm class is peripheral thyrotoxicosis. Not excluded: a state-independent promyelinating action and non-RNA routes.
- **Other rivals, not examined in depth:** gene addition (the benchmark itself, not a small molecule); ganaxolone (a title reporting microglial clearance and remyelination in the corpus callosum, PMID 31332963, unread; it bridges both objectives); benztropine and the azole or sterol inhibitors (OPC culture data; no developmental data). A cortical-cell-type-resolved Wwox deposit would let the lineage-state question be reopened without an experiment; none exists.
- **Undecidable on the held inputs (named as ADV asked):** (a) the efficacy-to-harm margin of clemastine (harm at one dose in healthy mice, effect at 0.5 to 10 mg/kg/day in a different model and age; no dose-response in the same preparation); (b) whether an OPC-excess positive model transfers to an OPC-intact deficit; (c) clemastine against thyromimetic in a common preparation (no head-to-head exists anywhere in the sources).
- **What would reverse the preference:** the experiment below showing no stage movement at confirmed exposure; a thyroid-signalling readout at protein or functional level showing reduced signalling in the Wwox cortex; a harm signal at the effective exposure; a cortex single-nucleus deposit showing the lag is a block with OPC loss rather than a late-stage deficit.

## 5. Priority experiment (hypothesis level; doses below are source research doses, not recommendations)
**Question it separates.** Does differentiation support move the stage-resolved deficit of the Wwox-neuronal-deletion cortex without the conduction harm seen at high dose, and is a thyroid-axis arm better or worse in the same tissue?
**Design (one preparation, both classes, exposure controlled).** Neuronal-deletion pups and controls, pre-registered arms: vehicle; clemastine at two oral doses chosen from the Odell range (one at 2, one at 7.5 mg/kg/day, from P3, the window of the source) with steady-state plasma measured and matched to the source's 44 ng/mL Cmax; a CNS-penetrant thyromimetic arm in the Triac window (P1 to P20, Sob-AM2 or Triac) with a hairless-type TH-response readout as the premise control; readouts at P14 and P17: stage-resolved lineage markers (precursor, committed precursor, newly formed, mature; sections or nuclei), MBP and electron-microscopy g-ratio in small and large axons, callosal compound action potential (the Palma harm readout), body weight, seizure threshold. Wild-type littermates with the same drug in each class (the Palma-type harm check at a low dose).
**Interpretable outcomes.**
- *Positive:* mature-stage markers rise toward control (the -0.5 deficit shrinks) with N1 conduction not slowed and myelin structure up, at confirmed exposure, and the effect absent or smaller in controls: supports pursuing the class for the myelination endpoint of the benchmark.
- *Negative:* no stage movement at confirmed plasma exposure in either class: the muscarinic and thyroid routes do not reach this deficit; the class is dropped for this context (not for others). A clemastine-negative with a thyromimetic-positive would first be read as the premise check being wrong at protein level.
- *Ambiguous:* counts rise but conduction slows or myelin thins (the Palma pattern) at the effective dose: differentiation without functional myelination; margin undecided, the harm cell moves to the front. A response only at the higher dose or only in wild type: general, not disease-specific.
- *Uninterpretable:* plasma below the source value, sedation or weight loss: dose or schedule failed, not the mechanism.

## 6. What this does not claim
No dose, schedule or use in any person. No efficacy probability. No claim that a myelin agent reproduces the gene-addition result. The exposure values are total concentrations from the sources; the lineage state is bulk-tissue and one-pool nuclei data.
