## 2. Corrections to the statements the checkpoint carried (checkpoint I, section 3)
**Clemastine (broad objective).**
1. *Exposure.* Plasma Cmax 44.0 ng/mL, total, LC-MS/MS, steady state after the 8th daily oral dose of 7.5 mg/kg/day (P10 by the verifier's count from "P3 to P10"; Tmax 0.5 h; C24 0.974 ng/mL; n 4 to 5 per time point). In molar terms 44 ng/mL is about 0.13 uM for the free base of molecular mass about 344 (SCI arithmetic, not a source value). No brain, CSF or unbound value in Odell 2024. **A brain detection does exist in one rat paper (PMID 29584714): clemastine detected in juvenile rat brain after 30 mg/kg s.c. by MALDI-MS at 6 h; qualitative only (m/z 344 and 215), no concentration, and the paper is internally inconsistent about the age (21-day-old in Methods; P6, P12 and P15 in the Fig. 5 legend).** So "no brain value" becomes "no quantitative juvenile brain, CSF or unbound value; one qualitative detection".
2. *Minimum effective dose.* 7.5 mg/kg/day was not the lowest dose tested: doses were 0.5, 2, 7.5 and 10 mg/kg/day. At 2 mg/kg/day MBP intensity was already higher (p = 0.003) while CC1+ density and the OPC proportion were not; the paper's own Discussion says the true minimum may lie between 2 and 7.5. The plasma value therefore belongs to the dose that gave the full effect in the paper's definition, not to a measured threshold.
3. *In vitro range.* 10 to 500 nM is stated for clemastine and benztropine jointly; the dose-response itself is in Mei's Supplementary Fig. 1g, which the verifier did not read; the main text does not state its species or medium. The screen used rat OPCs at 1 uM for 3 days. "Without altering the number of oligodendroglial cells" is the result in DRG cocultures (rat E15 DRG neurons), not in pure OPC cultures.
4. *Harm signal (healthy developing mouse).* 50 mg/kg/day i.p. is the only dose described, so no dose-response exists in that paper: it cannot be said at what dose conduction becomes impaired, only that it was at 50. Conduction velocity (N1 slower, p < 0.01, n 10 to 12), fewer nodes (node length unchanged), thinner myelin only in axons of 0 to 0.5 um (no difference 0.5 to 1 um), n 3 mice for electron microscopy; "number of oligodendrocytes" is a higher proportion of APC+ cells normalised to Olig2; strain C57BL/6; sex not stated; P5 to P21 (Methods) or "P5-20" (Results). The dose is 6.7-fold the dose of the Odell plasma measurement (SCI arithmetic: 50 over 7.5).
5. *Negative genetic-hypomyelination result.* Verified with qualifiers: myelin-deficient (males) and PLP transgenic rats, s.c. daily P1 to P21, myelin measured (Luxol fast blue, MBP, ultrastructure, stereology), "no changes in neurological phenotype or survival ... even at the highest dose"; 20 and 30 mg/kg gave mild sedation of 1 to 2 hours; one small significant effect (20 mg/kg, md males, MBP fibre bundles in brainstem). So the negative is not an absolute null on myelin.
6. *Positive hypoxia results.* Wang 2018 (Neuron): oral gavage P3 to P10 with hypoxia P3 to P10, n = 3 per group, Student's t-test: small groups. Tcf4 mouse: clemastine 10 mg/kg i.p. P28 to P42 raised oligodendrocytes and lowered OPCs in vivo (n = 4 per genotype), the OPC excess in the mutants is supported: the opposite starting state from the Wwox-deficient cortex (unit A2: OPC intact, stages from COP onward lower).

**Thyromimetic class.**
7. *Window.* Triac 400 ng/g/day s.c.; treatment P1 to P20 or P22 to P42, analysed at P43, n 3 to 4, comparators saline double-knockout and wild type. Late start (week 3 to 6): "myelination was not improved while a partial recovery of PV+ immunoreactivity could still be detected". So the window dependence is for myelination; interneuron marker recovery was partial in the late start. Sex not stated.
8. *Hartley.* The iCKO-Myrf genetic demyelination model with hyperthyroid T3/T4 chow got worse; sobetirome and Sob-AM2 improved rotarod and myelin there. In the lysolecithin and cuprizone models T3 and sobetirome promoted repair (sobetirome less than T3). The "T3 inhibited repair in one genetic model" statement is correct and incomplete: in two gliotoxin models T3 promoted repair. The class is model- and dose-route-dependent (chow, i.p., oral).
9. *Tcf4 mouse.* In vivo normalisation of OPC and oligodendrocyte density and behaviour is for **Sob-AM2** (1.0 mg/kg/day i.p., 14 days); sobetirome itself was tested only in vitro (Results 1 uM; Methods text says 10 uM: internal inconsistency). The producer's "sobetirome ... in vivo 1.0 mg/kg" is not supported and must not be carried.
10. *Maternal toxicity.* Sobetirome 1.0 mg/kg and Sob-AM2 0.3 mg/kg s.c. daily E12.5 to E18.5 in Mct8+/-/Dio2-/- dams, n = 5; spontaneous abortions with sobetirome, none with Sob-AM2; the Discussion says "possibly due to hyperthyroid-like effects". The context is a transporter-deficient background.
11. *Tiratricol authorisation.* EMA page: marketing authorisation valid throughout the EU on 12 February 2025; SmPC: peripheral thyrotoxicosis in MCT8 deficiency (Allan-Herndon-Dudley syndrome) from birth. The indication concerns peripheral thyrotoxicosis in a defined disorder, not myelination.

**Bridge exposure.**
12. *Ethosuximide potency.* Gomora 2001: the 0.6 mM persistent-current IC50 and state dependence are the paper's finding in recombinant human channels; the sentence that rat and cat neuron studies "failed to confirm inhibition ... at therapeutically relevant concentrations" is the paper's **premise**, which the paper then contradicts ("capable of blocking human T-type channels at therapeutically relevant concentrations"). The recombinant-against-native disagreement exists in the literature the paper cites, but this abstract must not be quoted as evidence that native studies disagree with it. The inactivated-state K(I) values are for methsuximide's active metabolite, not ethosuximide.
13. *Ethosuximide mouse exposure.* Mouse total brain ethosuximide exists with a number: PMID 22315091 (open full text read by the verifier): ethosuximide 72.3 mg/kg i.p. plus saline gave total brain 134.9 +/- 7.73 ug/g (n >= 8); control ED50 155.8 (139.7 to 173.7) mg/kg, with no concentration at that dose in that paper. In molar terms 134.9 ug/g is about 0.96 umol/g (molecular mass about 141; SCI arithmetic, total tissue, not free, density taken as 1; not a source value). Ten more papers have brain or plasma concentrations measured but only abstract-level reading (18566800, 22789660, 17085857, 17116023, 16140589, 21777642, 15525996, 15461671, 11129101, 3144596). The exposure round reads them.
14. *Cheng 2020 regimen.* Verified: ethosuximide 150 mg/kg i.p. 45 min before PTZ 30 mg/kg; LiCl 60 mg/kg i.p. three times within 1 h before PTZ; no drug concentration anywhere; **no age or day is given for the drug experiments** (ages appear only for rotarod, 18 to 20 days, and the Western blot at P14 or P20). Group sizes read from the Fig. 7 image: ethosuximide panel Wwox-/- control 4, PTZ 6, PTZ plus ethosuximide 6; lithium panel Wwox-/- PTZ 6, PTZ plus LiCl 7. The unit of the lithium dose (salt or lithium) remains to be read by the exposure round.
15. *Retigabine in mouse MES (Bettio 2025).* Mouse EC50 plasma 2.26 uM, brain 3.58 uM, ratio 1.58; rat 1.46 and 3.01 uM, ratio 2.06; oral, 0.5 h pretreatment; CF-1 male mice 3 to 7 weeks, Sprague-Dawley rats 5 weeks both sexes. EC50 means the concentration for 50 percent probability of protection (logistic regression, no confidence intervals) at one fixed time; measured plasma and brain concentrations; unbound only for other drugs (Fig. 5C). The mouse dose-response is not significant in the tested range (5 to 35 mg/kg, p = 0.12). The 3.58 against 2.23 uM disagreement with ChEMBL (HN37 paper, Kunming mice) is not settled by this pass; the exposure round item 7 takes it.
16. *GABA-A action of retigabine (Treven 2015).* Seizure-like activity inhibition at 1 uM is in primary dissociated rat hippocampal cultures; the 10 uM enhancement is on recombinant human (tsA201) delta-containing receptors, gamma2-containing receptors unaltered.
17. *Tonic inhibition in immature tissue (Liddiard 2025).* Acute neocortical slices P6 to P8, low-Mg2+, C57BL/6J: the neurosteroid THDOC reduced seizure-like events, the alpha5 blocker L-655,708 did not. In vivo, P8 to P9: gaboxadol (THIP) 3.5 mg/kg s.c. 30 min after kainic acid reduced behavioural seizures; "10 mg/kg was fatal for neonatal mice while 5 mg/kg was highly sedating"; no EEG, observation up to 3 h. So the in vivo agent is THIP, the slice agent THDOC, and the therapeutic window at that age is narrow.
18. *Lithium exposure papers.* Hanak 2015 is a rat poisoning model (acute 185 mg/kg Li2CO3 i.p.; also acute-on-chronic and chronic with renal failure) and Singh 1981 is rat (hyperbaric oxygen context); neither is a mouse regimen-matched value. Both abstract level.
19. *Ganaxolone label.* Verified (effective 2026-09-04, version 14): seizures associated with CDKL5 deficiency disorder, 2 years and older; the indication is the seizures, not the disorder.

## 3. What this does to the unit C reading going in
- The broad comparison keeps its shape (clemastine class against thyromimetic class) but loses two supports and gains one limit: the clemastine plasma level is a single steady-state total value at a dose that is not shown to be minimal; the thyromimetic "in vivo normalisation" is for the CNS-penetrant prodrug Sob-AM2 and Triac, not sobetirome; and the window dependence of Triac is for myelination only (the interneuron marker recovered partially in the late start).

- The ethosuximide comparison now has one mouse brain value (about 0.96 umol/g total tissue at 72.3 mg/kg i.p., the ethosuximide-plus-saline arm of PMID 22315091; the verifier report does not state the strain, age or model) against a recombinant persistent-current IC50 of 0.6 mM; nothing yet pairs a brain value with the 150 mg/kg dose of Cheng, nor an age. Total tissue is not the free concentration at the channel.
- Absence claims in the producer reports should be written as "not found by these routes" with the routes named, as the verifier restated them.
