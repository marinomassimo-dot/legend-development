## Answer first
1. **By the registered rule the verdict is UNDECIDED: no reading's token is true in the only labelable contrast (cortex, 3 against 4 animals).** What the rule does exclude: **LOSS is rejected** (after stage adjustment the pan-lineage set is -0.10 and the OPC set -0.07, far above the -0.5 and -0.3 lines; raw -0.45 and -0.15) and **tissue-wide DELAY is rejected** (see point 3). **BLOCK, as registered, is not met because the COP set is lower** (-0.51, line -0.3), although the OPC set is preserved.
2. **The cortex lineage profile, stage-adjusted (A1, leave-one-animal-out range):** OPC -0.07 (-0.30 to +0.16), COP -0.51 (-0.81 to -0.24), NFOL -0.44 (-0.76 to -0.08), MFOL -0.56 (-1.14 to -0.18), MOL -0.61 (-0.83 to -0.23), structural myelin genes (Mbp, Plp1, Mobp, Cnp) -0.80 (-1.15 to -0.45). Rank correlation of A1 with stage rank -0.90. Read after the rule (post hoc, labelled so): **the progenitor pool is intact and every stage from the committed precursor onward is lower by a similar amount (about -0.4 to -0.6)**, which is the shape of a deficit in progression from progenitor to committed precursor (or fewer cells passing that step), not of a lineage loss, and not of a delayed tissue. It does not separate "a block" from "fewer cells passing" without cell-level data.
3. **Why tissue-wide delay is rejected.** The delay model takes the knockout as a control that is d days younger and predicts each set's shift from the wild-type brain change between P14 and P28 (E-MTAB-6798). Fitted on the neuronal covariate genes alone it gives d = 4.4 days (leave-one-out 3.1 to 5.5), already outside the model's valid range of 0 to 3 days, and it predicts the early-stage sets **higher** in the knockout (OPC +0.14, COP +0.29, NFOL +0.20, because those transcripts fall with age) and the mature sets modestly lower (MFOL -0.28, MOL -0.38). Observed: early-stage sets lower (-0.15, -0.60, -0.58) and mature sets lower by about twice the predicted amount (-0.83, -0.75). Explained fraction 0.03; the oligodendrocyte-specific delay fit (2.4 days) is not larger than the neuronal one (ratio 0.55), so the rejecting observation of the registered DELAY (ratio above 2) is not what rejects it: the failure is the direction of the early-stage sets and f of 0.03.
4. **Nuclei (descriptive; one pool per arm, P17 hippocampus; no p-value; marker assignment not calibrated).** Lineage share of assigned nuclei is the same in the two arms (23.2 and 23.3 percent), so no lineage loss at cell level either. The OPC share of lineage nuclei is 33 percent in the control and 53 percent in the knockout; the mature (MFOL plus MOL) share falls from 42 to 21 percent. This is the block-like composition ADV-065c had already read, now at stage resolution. Per mature-class nucleus, Mbp, Plp1 and Mog log2 CPM are 11.1, 10.2, 8.2 in the control and 5.2, 4.4, 3.0 in the knockout, a very large difference that cannot be read as fewer transcripts per cell: ambient RNA from the abundant mature oligodendrocytes of the control pool inflates every control nucleus, and nuclei assigned "mature" in the knockout are assigned on weaker marker evidence.
5. **What it changes.** The registered decision for UNDECIDED applies: **the oligodendrocyte-class agent unit runs, with the premise labelled "progenitors present, progression beyond the committed precursor lower; block versus fewer-cells-passing undecided", and the priority experiment is the cell-level readout** (lineage-stage counts and a differentiation challenge in the knockout tissue or in an OPC culture from it), not the agent. The agents to test are the ones that act at the progenitor-to-committed-precursor step; those acting only on late myelin wrapping are not matched to the shape found.
6. **Non-cell-autonomy matters for the agent unit.** The cortex deposit is a neuron-targeted deletion (the deposit's own title) and the hippocampal null contrast, a germline deletion, shows the same direction (adjusted MFOL -1.09, MYELIN_STRUCT -1.40, descriptive). If the oligodendrocyte-lineage defect follows a neuronal deletion, it is downstream of neurons (lost axonal or activity-dependent signals) and an agent acting on the progenitor bypasses the cause; whether the progenitor can respond is the open question, and an effect on myelin without restoring neuronal activity is a different claim from the gene-addition rescue.
