## Direction 1: does a glial or energy change precede the first seizure in other early-onset epilepsy models, so that its sign is predicted before the Wwox pre-onset series is run? (adjacent literature, roadmap direction E; answers the radar's glial-density question and the S-KO P5 to P9 test)
* **Initial scientific basis.** Held texts have no timing below P14 for Wwox (ADV-104); the S-KO seizures start at P9; gliosis follows seizures in most epilepsy models (ADV-104's own unfavourable reading); the radar's glial-density sign conflicts between species; ADV-116 found no glial or energy set difference in Scn1a het at P14. Other developmental models with sampling before the first seizure exist in the literature or in public expression sets (candidates to look for, not claims: Scn1a, Kcnq2, Stxbp1, Cdkl5 models; neonatal flurothyl).
* **Best alternative.** Measure in Wwox directly (a laboratory step); until then, no prior. The question here is whether a transferable prior for the SIGN (below, equal or above wild type before the first spike) exists from other models at matched ages, or the literature disagrees.
* **Question approachable now.** In published or public pre-onset series of developmental epilepsy models, what is the direction of glial density or marker, and of an energy or lactate readout, at ages before the first recorded seizure, with age, denominator, n, significance and marker versus function stated per row? A pre-registered prediction for the S-KO P5/P7 series follows (rejecting observation fixed first).
* **Possible consequence for the comparison.** If other models show a pre-onset rise: the glial axis and the energy axis keep a "pre-onset test" status and a stated sign prediction; if flat or lower: the glial arm moves toward "consequence of seizures" and the pilot's cost of scoring GFAP and Iba1 at P5 to P9 stays free but its expected yield falls; if the literature conflicts: R3 with the conflicting rows named.

## Direction 2: a nonsense, frameshift or splice-null allele class and the one lever class not yet compared for it: translational read-through and NMD escape (an undeveloped lever for variant class E; RP-015's nonsense half)
* **Initial scientific basis.** R4 lists class E as RNA-change labels with no lever and no assay; the WWOX gene has a short coding sequence with an N-terminal WW1 and a C-terminal SDR domain, so a truncated product is not equivalent to the full protein; read-through helps only where a premature stop is within reach of a near-cognate suppressor and where NMD is not complete; the literature on read-through in other developmental epilepsies and Rett/CDKL5-type nonsense alleles is adjacent (roadmap E), with agents whose brain exposure and safety facts differ.
* **Best alternative.** For class E the circuit levers (class-agnostic, null-background slice arms) and gene addition, which need no allele-specific premise; read-through would have to beat them on a stated criterion, not on novelty.
* **Question approachable now.** For published WWOX nonsense and frameshift positions (aggregate positions from the literature and variant resources, no individual-level data), how many lie where NMD is expected to be complete versus escaped (last-exon and exon-junction rules), what truncated product would remain, and in adjacent disorders with nonsense alleles what read-through restored and what readout (protein, function, seizure) was measured, with age and denominator. This is a position-level comparison, not a calculation of efficacy.
* **Possible consequence.** A conditional row for class E (which positions could be addressed, which cannot) and a statement of whether read-through can ever outrank the class-agnostic levers for that class; or "no addressable positions", which closes RP-015's nonsense half.
