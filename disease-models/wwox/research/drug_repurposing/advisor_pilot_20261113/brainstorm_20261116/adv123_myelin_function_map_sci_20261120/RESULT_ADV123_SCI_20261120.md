## 1. Answer first
**The pre-registered classification is R2 by the letter and WEAK; R1 is not met; and the one half of R2 that matters for a decision (that myelin contributes to function) is untested by every row.** Three full-text laboratories with EM (B5 Tcf4 clemastine, B8 APP/PS1 miconazole, A07 Tsc2 J4) plus two bulk-marker preprints (B6, A03) show a myelin readout moving together with a functional gain while the neuronal lesion persists; each agent has a named direct route to function (clemastine M1/H1 receptors, J4 on neuronal hyperactivity, ESR-beta on neurons), and no row separates the two. The DREAM association stands in a narrower form: **neuronal rescue closes survival, weight, clasping and (in the Wwox rows) seizure-class functions while the myelin readout is partial, unquantified or read at one age only**, so myelin normalisation is not shown to be a rate-limiting step for those functions; but no row has myelin and function in the same animals at two or more ages, so "not rate-limiting" cannot be told from "lags behind" (TIMING F_FIRST or M_FIRST is assigned to no row).

## 2. Premise correction carried (already sent to ADV)
The unmyelinated pair 51.5 against 26.4 is the corpus callosum at 6 months (Repudi 2021, PMID 34747138, one dose, a different construct). At P17 the EM measure is myelinated axons per field of view (WT 130.3, KO 45.7, treated 104.9; closure 0.70). The function closures are from Obeid 2026 (PMID 42422765): two papers of one laboratory, a cross-paper association, not one experiment. Verified here: Repudi's count unit is the field of view averaged per group with n = 3 mice, the authors read the effect as neuronal restoration promoting OPC differentiation "in a non-cell autonomous manner", and the MBP-promoter hWWOX arm gave weak expression and no improvement (the authors tie it to AAV9 tropism, so it is not a test of oligodendrocyte WWOX). Obeid's high-dose "near-complete" wording for clasping and for MBP are two separate sentences; the MBP measure is bulk intensity, and the supplement panel the text cites (Figure S7I, read by me in the held supplement, `files/supplement/PMID42422765_r2/mmc1.pdf`, legend only) is "representative MBP immunostaining at ~P90" in the low-dose and high-dose groups relative to WT: representative images, no quantification in the legend, but at a later age than P18, so one later qualitative myelin look exists (an earlier producer line that LD mice did not survive to P90 conflicts with that legend and is unresolved; the images themselves were not viewed).

## 4. Readings against the frozen thresholds
* **R1 (downstream) not met.** It needs two independent-origin rescue rows with normalised myelin at SAME_TIME or F_FIRST. Normalised myelin appears only in the Wwox laboratory (6-month count; bulk P18) and as "approaching control" in Carson 2012 (unquantified, n inconsistent); the other rescue rows are partial (A05), not significant (A06), F_ONLY (A01, A02) or one-age (A04).
* **R2 (independently modifiable and a contributor) met by the letter, graded weak.** The threshold is co-occurrence: at least two independent-origin full-text arm-(b) rows with myelin gain plus function gain and no lesion correction; B5, B8, A07 (EM) satisfy it, B6 and A03 add bulk markers. **Caveats that the threshold did not price:** every agent has a named direct route (above); the lesion class is contested in two of the three EM rows (Tcf4 has a cell-autonomous OL component; APP/PS1 is amyloid); function gains are partial or mixed (B8, B6); A07's white-matter MBP shows no genotype difference; B5's density evidence is void. Under the weights rule none of the three is clean, and nothing in the map is a design that separates myelin from the drug's other actions (second mechanistically unrelated pro-differentiation agent with EM and function in one model, or an OL-differentiation-blocked animal).
* **What the map does not show:** no row with serial myelin and function in the same cohort; no genetic reactivation, AAV or ASO study of Rett or Angelman with a myelin readout (routes in the producer reports; "none found by titles and abstracts", not exhaustive); no post-thyromimetic myelin imaging in humans.

## 5. Closing paragraph (the one required by the assignment)
The evidence supports a narrower statement than either pole of the question: myelin is not shown to be a rate-limiting step for the functional closures that neuronal rescue achieves (survival, weight, clasping, seizure-class readouts close while the myelin readout is partial, unquantified or read at one age), but it is a modifiable readout, since drugs shift myelin measures while the neuronal lesion persists and function moves partly in step; whether that myelin shift contributes to the function gain is unresolved, because every such agent also has a direct route to function and the map holds no design that separates them. For the broad comparison this means the **oligodendrocyte-differentiation arm is neither demoted to a readout nor promoted to a contributor**: it keeps its place as a candidate, carries the standing caveat that functional effects of its agents in neuronal-lesion models cannot be attributed to myelin on present evidence, and for WWOX rests on transfer from Tcf4, Tsc2 and APP/PS1 models (no Wwox row tests a pro-differentiation agent; the oligodendrocyte-restricted WWOX arm was uninformative because of weak expression). The **thyromimetic arm is read separately and does not move**: its animal support is one laboratory network, one model and early start only (the late start did not improve myelination), its target is upstream of both neurons and oligodendrocytes so it cannot separate the readings, the Wwox lesion is not a hormone-transport lesion (so its relevance depends on a separate premise not tested in this map), and the human record holds no post-thyromimetic myelin imaging (absence of data, not a negative). **The observation that would reverse the reading** (R2 weak to downstream): a pro-differentiation agent in a neuronal-lesion model keeps its functional gain when oligodendrocyte differentiation is genetically blocked in the same animals (the effect is then direct, R1 gains); if the functional gain disappears while the myelin block is confirmed, the contributor reading is supported.
