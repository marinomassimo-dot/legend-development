## Answer first
1. **The axis is not promoted and not parked: it stays outside the comparison set, and one measurement series decides it.** My pre-registered rules left a hole, which I state rather than fill after the fact: "preferred" needs (a) glycaemia documented before the first seizure AND (b) a metabolic manipulation that moves seizures or survival without neuronal rescue; "parked" needs glucose at or after seizure onset in every line that reports timing. Neither applies: (a) is not met and not refuted (no glucose before P10; the neuron-targeted line seizes from P9 and has no glucose value at all), (b) is absent, and the parking condition fails because the lines that would test it were never measured. The honest class is "undetermined by the held texts".
2. **(b) is absent in every held text and in my searches.** No manipulation of glycaemia or of the PDH axis, in a WWOX line or in a model with a non-metabolic primary lesion, with a seizure or survival readout, was found. The one manipulation in the held texts that moves glucose without WWOX restoration is digoxin (below): glucose only, no seizure or survival.
3. **What is new against ADV-074:** (i) the germline null's growth lag starts at day 4, before any age at which glucose was measured; (ii) the null's hypoglycaemia has a measured mechanism hint (lower insulin, higher glucose uptake and glycolysis in null cells and higher serum lactate), so it is not simple fuel shortage; (iii) absolute wild-type glucose differs about two-fold between laboratories, so "how low" cannot be compared across papers; (iv) the rat "normal serum" statement is second-hand and its own source's abstract reports raised urea nitrogen, creatinine and phosphate, so it cannot carry a dissociation; (v) the one acute glucose manipulation is from one adult-weaned-age experiment with a dose that reads implausibly.

## Q2. Any manipulation of the metabolic axis independent of neuronal rescue
* **Held WWOX texts.** (1) The vector series (Obeid, Figure 1 and Results, first paragraph): EF1 alpha failed survival, growth and glucose together; CMV and CBA gave early rescue of survival and glycaemia together, not sustained; MBP gave none; synapsin gave all. Glucose and survival move together in all five arms, which a common cause predicts and which cannot separate "glucose causes" from "glucose follows". ADV-074 already lists the two arms where death occurs with glucose near wild type (low-dose neuronal vector at P30; CMV at P14 only); both rest on survivors and small n. (2) **Digoxin** (Abu-Remaileh and Aqeilan 2014, Fig 5c legend and Results): an HIF1 alpha inhibitor raised blood glucose in knockout mice 40 min after one intraperitoneal injection at day 21; the legend reads "100 mg/1 kg weight" (implausible as printed), n = 3 per genotype in the legend (an earlier repository note records "no wild-type arm": that disagreement is unresolved and needs the figure, which I did not view). No seizure, survival or growth readout. The same paper: lower serum insulin, higher glucose uptake and lactate in null cells and serum, Pdk1 transcript higher in null MEFs. **This shows the null's glycaemia is acutely modifiable; it says nothing on seizures or survival, and digoxin is a cardiac glycoside, so it is a pharmacological probe of the axis, not a candidate.**
* **Other models.** Seven PubMed searches (titles only, nothing read beyond titles; of the eight allowed): WWOX with hypoglycaemia and mice (2 records: the 2014 paper and a 2015 review of WWOX and cell metabolism); WWOX with glycogen and brain (0); dichloroacetate with seizure (16 records, the first ten are mitochondrial-disease reviews plus the two primary papers already on card I01, PMID 29913407 and 36432491); ketogenic diet with Scn1a, Stxbp1 or Kcnq2 (753 records, ten examined: all reviews or gene-disease studies); neonatal hypoglycaemia and seizure threshold in rodents (0; the query was over-specified); three earlier over-specified queries returned 0. **No primary paper was found in which correcting glycaemia or the PDH axis changed seizures or survival in a model whose primary lesion is not metabolic.** This is "not found by these routes", not "does not exist"; the dichloroacetate seizure datum already on card I01 is negative or slightly proconvulsant in adult mice (abstract level).
* **Rat.** The statement that lde/lde rats have normal serum at 28 days and that this "clearly excluded" a systemic metabolic origin is cited by its own paper to an earlier one that is not held; that earlier paper's abstract reports raised plasma urea nitrogen, creatinine and inorganic phosphate and lower growth hormone (dossier of 19500159, read 2026-08-06). **It cannot serve as a dissociation until the earlier paper is read.** The 2014 text I read does not contain it.

## GSK-3 glycogen paragraph (hypothesis only; competing explanations)
The repository holds, as DATO on CLAIM 035, that WWOX binds GSK3 beta through its short-chain dehydrogenase region, on a 20-residue segment (388 to 407) homologous to the Axin, FRAT and GSKIP docking motif (`dismech_export_spec.md` row 035-a2; I did not re-read the primary paper this window). **None of the four held full texts above contains a brain glycogen, glycogen synthase phosphorylation or glycogen-handling readout**: the word "glycogen" appears in them only inside "glycogen synthase kinase" or in reference titles, and the one PubMed search for WWOX with glycogen and brain returned nothing. Cheng 2020 reports higher GSK3 beta activity (Ser9 dephosphorylation) in null cerebellum, hippocampus and cortex and that a GSK-3 inhibitor lowers seizure severity; that is kinase activity at one site and says nothing on glycogen. Competing explanations for any glucose-handling effect of GSK-3 in the null brain: (1) none, because the docking motif is a binding statement and the tau and beta-catenin substrates, not glycogen synthase, were measured; (2) a glycogen-synthase effect in liver or muscle (the muscle conditional knockout has the opposite glucose sign, adult, ADV-074), with no brain readout; (3) astrocytic glycogen as an activity-linked fuel store, which no held text measures. **Answer: the held data say nothing about glycogen or glucose handling in the null brain.** A GSK-3 agent therefore has no held glycaemia datum either way, and a glucose readout would have to be added to any pup experiment that uses one.

## What this does to the choice (Q4 is in increment 2; the part that already follows)
* The axis cannot become the preferred broad candidate on held data: (b) is absent.
* It also cannot be parked on its own premise: the premise that glucose follows seizures is untested in the lines that seize.
* The deliberately unfavourable reading survives in this form: the 2026 neuronal window normalises glucose, growth, seizures and survival together, so the correction is not separable; the rat dissociation is not usable; and the only acute glucose manipulation (digoxin) has no seizure readout.
* The decisive datum is the one ADV-074 named and nothing newer changes: **blood glucose in S-KO and N-KO pups from P7, with same-animal video-EEG or ECoG from P9**, and then a glucose-restoration arm without WWOX restoration. Cost of the first part: a glucometer reading at the sampling ages, in lines that already exist.
