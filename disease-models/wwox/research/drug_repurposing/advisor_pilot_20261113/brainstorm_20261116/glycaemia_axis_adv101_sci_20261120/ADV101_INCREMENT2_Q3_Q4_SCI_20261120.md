## Q4. Marginal utility against the two current broad candidates
The two candidates in force (v3.2 and the one-unit note): **(A)** a GSK-3 non-lithium agent against oligodendrocyte stage readouts, conditional on S-up, four treated arms with Holm over four, n per arm 13 at the v3.1 rounding and 14 as an enrolment plan at the half-deficit margin; **(B)** the oligodendrocyte class (clemastine), whose own healthy-mouse harm datum (Palma 2022) and minimum-effective-dose datum (Odell 2024) are already mined. The glycaemia axis is compared with them on four plain questions, not scored:

| Question | (A) GSK-3 agent | (B) oligodendrocyte class | Glycaemia axis |
|---|---|---|---|
| Is there an intervention with exposure facts that could enter a pup arm today? | named agents, pup primaries partly unreachable at full text (identifiable: no for the clemastine comparison) | clemastine, with efficacy and harm in pups | **no**: dichloroacetate has no brain or pup exposure and millimolar potency; ketone and glucose supply have no WWOX data |
| Does the held evidence put the target downstream of or beside the neuronal lesion? | divided by system and stage (inhibitor promotes in injured preterm white matter, suppresses in rat precursor cultures) | hypomyelination is neuronal non-cell-autonomous (Repudi); the null's differentiation block is not a knockout-type deficit with an intact pool in any model read | **undetermined**: glucose and growth normalise with neuronal rescue; whether they are modifiable on their own is untested in S-KO and N-KO |
| What is the cheapest datum that moves the choice? | a pup free-brain exposure at a regimen that moves a lineage-stage readout in a non-injury genetic model | the efficacy-to-harm margin in the same preparation | **blood glucose in S-KO and N-KO pups from P7 with same-animal EEG from P9: existing lines, a glucometer reading** |
| What does a wrong guess cost? | an arm that cannot be read (S-flat narrows one proposition only) | harm in a healthy pup at one dose (Palma) | a glucose-lowering or fuel-shifting arm in pups that are already hypoglycaemic |

**What result would make this axis the preferred broad candidate** (fixed in the pre-registration, restated with the datum that would supply it): in S-KO or N-KO pups, glucose already low at or before P9 (before the first seizure) AND a glucose or fuel-supply arm that, without WWOX restoration, changes ECoG spike rate, seizure score or survival; then the candidate becomes "restore fuel supply", with dichloroacetate a separate question that still needs a pup brain concentration near the millimolar range.
**What would park it:** S-KO and N-KO normoglycaemic across P7 to P14 while seizing and dying, or glucose falling only after the first seizure or spike epoch, or a supply arm that corrects glucose and weight but not ECoG or survival.
**A deliberately unfavourable reading, as it stands today:** the correction that exists (neuronal AAV9) normalises glucose, weight, seizures and survival together and is therefore not separable; the one rat dissociation is second-hand and contested by its own source's abstract; the one acute glucose manipulation (digoxin) has no seizure readout; the only agent with a mechanism-level rationale (dichloroacetate) is millimolar-potent with no brain exposure and was negative or slightly proconvulsant in the one adult mouse seizure test. On this reading the axis is a **covariate to measure, not an arm to run**.

**What "more promising" means for this axis (ADV's request).** The two broad candidates are compared by the value of the next discriminating test, not by efficacy. For the glycaemia axis the same standard gives three conditions, all of which must hold before it displaces either: (i) an intervention exists that could enter a pup arm, meaning pup-compatible exposure facts or a design that needs none (a glucose or fuel-supply arm); (ii) held or new data place a modifiable glycaemic step upstream of, or independent from, the seizure and death readouts in the S-KO or N-KO line (the glucose-with-EEG series); (iii) the test that decides it costs less than the tests the other two need. **Today (iii) holds** (a glucometer reading and EEG in lines that already exist), **(i) fails** (no agent with a pup brain exposure fact; the supply arm is not specified) and **(ii) is untested**. So the axis is not "more promising" than the GSK-3 candidate or the oligodendrocyte class; it is the cheapest unresolved measurement, and it is carried as a readout (section 6.9 of the v3.1 file), not as an arm.

## Decision it changes
* The broad branch stays at two candidates; the glycaemia axis does not become a third arm on held data.
* **One addition, at no new line:** whichever pup experiment uses the neuronal-deletion line (v3.1 and v3.2 already do) records blood glucose at the sampling ages from P7 and, where a seizure or ECoG epoch is scored, glucose in the same animal. This is ADV-074's optional series made concrete for the lines that matter, and it is the datum that would reopen or park this axis. It adds a readout and no arm, so Holm over four is unchanged.
* Reopening trigger: a held or supplied glucose series in S-KO or N-KO, or a primary paper in which a metabolic manipulation changes seizures or survival in a model with a non-metabolic primary lesion.

## Correction note (2026-11-25, SCI, after HE)

The dichloroacetate row above lists three isozymes (PDK1 1.03 mM, PDK2 0.50 mM, PDK3 5.56 mM) and calls them "one assay". The held ChEMBL answer (`files/adv098_agent_sheets_raw_he_20261119/dichloroacetate.json`, document CHEMBL4007440) also carries PDK4, IC50 727,000 nM (activity 18040568, assay CHEMBL4009667), and the four values are four separate assay ids (CHEMBL4009649, 4009651, 4009662, 4009667) of the same design, all flagged "Outside typical range". PDK3 is the least inhibited of four; the range stays 11-fold. Source of the correction: HE (sheet corrected in dec485b5b); verified here in the held JSON.
