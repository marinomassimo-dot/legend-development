## 4. The final sentences (corrected after the blind report; these replace the frozen ones)

**S1.** Two Wwox-deficient mouse models have a functional readout of conduction in held full text. In global Wwox-/- mice at 3 weeks the transcranial motor evoked potential amplitude is 11.8 +- 5.4 against 59.2 +- 9.0 microvolt (4 against 10 animals; SEM) and the onset latency 2.44 +- 0.37 against 1.39 +- 0.13 ms (Cheng 2020, Fig 2). In Synapsin-Cre neuronal Wwox deletion at P13 to P17 the N1 latency of the callosal evoked response (callosal stimulation, recording in neocortical layer V, acute slices, one 250 micrometre distance) is longer (read from the graph, about 3.2 against 2.5 ms; Wilcoxon P = 0.0104; 8 slices from 6 knockout animals against 8 slices from 5 control animals), the N2 latency is not different (P = 0.5737) and the N1/N2 amplitude ratio is lower (P = 0.0030) (Repudi 2021, Fig 5D); the paper does not say which fibre class N1 and N2 are, and the control n may be a copy of another experiment's n.

**S2.** Neither has a drug arm for the functional readout, and neither separates a myelin cause from an axonal or excitability cause: the Tc-MEP is a muscle response along the corticospinal tract and peripheral nerve in a model that also has peripheral demyelination, and the slice comparison counts slices, not animals, at an age at which the knockout slices burst spontaneously.

**S3.** No WWOX patient report with an evoked-potential or nerve-conduction result was found by a title and abstract search on two routes (the only hit of the patient string is a mouse paper). The string did not include SSEP, BAEP, EMG or ERG, a result reported only in the full text of a case report would not be found, and the neuron-specific gene-therapy article of 2026 (Obeid) was not audited.

**S4.** Adjacent hypomyelinating, dysmyelinating or leukodystrophy models and patients with a pharmacological arm and a conduction or evoked-potential readout exist: 13 papers by abstract (5 central, 8 peripheral; 9 animal studies, 4 patient studies). One was read in open full text: the taiep rat, whose corticospinal conduction velocity is 13.7 against 42.9 m/s in wild type (an untreated genotype difference), and whose N2 somatosensory evoked response was larger and faster after 4-aminopyridine 1 mg/kg (t(29) = 4.3; the caption gives P < 0.001 and the text P < 0.01). None of the other twelve was read beyond the abstract, so nothing is said about their overlap with the mechanism classes held for Wwox (OPC maturation, ER stress or integrated stress response, energy supply) beyond the abstract.

**S5.** In developing wild-type mice, clemastine 50 mg/kg/day from P5 to P21 increased the proportion of mature oligodendrocytes but slowed the callosal N1 conduction velocity (P < 0.01, n = 10 to 12; N2 similar), and electron microscopy showed fewer myelinated axons and thinner myelin (Palma 2022). In demyelinated patients the same drug shortened the visual evoked potential P100 latency delay by 1.7 ms per eye (ReBUILD, abstract level). A pro-myelinating comparator in a pup design therefore needs a wild-type drug-only arm with a conduction readout, and an oligodendrocyte count cannot stand in for myelin or conduction.

**S6.** Energy supply has a conduction precedent in mouse tissue (callosal compound action potential amplitude is lost without glucose, lactate and pyruvate did not rescue it in the slice, and loading one oligodendrocyte with glucose prevented the loss in about 70% of experiments; abstract level, amplitude, not velocity) but none found by this search in a hypomyelinating model with a drug arm.

**S7 (the one measurement, Step 2).** Readout: callosal N1 conduction velocity from the distance-latency slope over at least three electrode distances, with N2 as a within-slice control, and, in the same animals, the g-ratio and myelinated-axon count, with animals as the unit and seizure status recorded. Model and comparators: neuronal deletion (Synapsin-Cre, already shown slow at the first peak), oligodendrocyte-lineage deletion (Olig2-Cre; present in the same paper without conduction data) and Cre-negative floxed littermates, at an age chosen by the laboratory around P17 to P21, with a neuronal re-expression arm. Predictions: neuron-dependent myelin defect, N1 slow and g-ratio higher in the neuronal deletion, normal in the oligodendrocyte-lineage deletion, restored by re-expression; OPC-intrinsic defect, the reverse; non-myelin cause (axon calibre, excitability, seizures), N1 slow with a normal g-ratio and myelinated-axon count, and unchanged by re-expression only if the cause is not neuronal activity. One N1 measurement alone does not separate the neuron-dependent from the non-myelin explanation; the g-ratio from the same animals does.

**S8.** Pup brief v2 carries a callosal conduction re-measurement with a rescue arm and animal-level statistics as an optional measurement (slice package section 3.5 and ordering row 9: a candidate, not an endpoint; first-peak latency, no velocity, "never in vivo" for the callosal readout) and the germline-null Tc-MEP values as a heterozygote carrier readout candidate (add-on 5(f)). It does not carry the N2 control, a velocity from the distance slope, the oligodendrocyte-lineage arm with a conduction readout, a wild-type drug-only arm, or the same-animal g-ratio.
