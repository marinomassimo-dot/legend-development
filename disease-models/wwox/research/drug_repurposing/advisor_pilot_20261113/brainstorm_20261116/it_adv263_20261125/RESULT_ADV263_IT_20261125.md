## Which surfaces generate most, and why
The mechanism map generates most per sentence: 4 of 8 sampled sentences are unsupported W claims, against 4 of 43 for the cards (small numbers; the order is more reliable than the figures). The cards and investigations do better where they have a seat for it: the card schema asks what was searched and when ("not found in the research", dated queries, coverage), and the supported W claims sit beside such a field. The map, the hypothesis ledger and claim titles are free prose with no such seat, so a negative is written as a property of the world. It also does not move afterwards: nothing re-opens a negative when a later receipt lands, and a queued candidate keeps the negative it was drafted with. Two cheap measures at the writing step, offered as practice and not as a gate: (1) write the negative with its perimeter, "in `<what was searched or read>` on `<date>`, none"; (2) before propagating a candidate that contains a negative, grep the receipts and the paper registry for the negated object since the candidate's date.

A note on search routes: the PubMed abstract query for a pannexin blocker returned 0 although the known WWOX slice paper uses one; only the Europe PMC full-text query found it. A PubMed zero is not an absence.
