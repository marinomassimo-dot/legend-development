# ADV-193 result: does Saha et al. 2024 support a different WO-21 comparison than the generic inositol add-back, and which proximal engagement and functional endpoints make the inositol/IMPA1 route versus the GSK3 route interpretable for a lithium-related change in excitability? ## Answer first
**NARROW (the pre-registered Reading N held, with Reading G partly): the proximal discriminator is real but lives in HEK293T cells; the critical link to neuronal firing crosses preparations.**
1. **In HEK293T cells the two routes are separable on two proximal endpoints** (receptor-evoked calcium release from stores in zero external calcium, and plasma-membrane PIP2 recovery after receptor stimulation with a PH-domain probe): lithium lowers both; an IMPA1-deleted line has the lowered phenotype by itself, restored by IMPA1 cDNA, and lithium has no further effect on either endpoint in the deleted line; extra inositol restores the lithium effect on calcium (Fig 2I and J) and on PIP2 recovery (Fig S2D); an ENGAGED GSK3 inhibitor (beta-catenin raised, Fig 5A, HEK only) leaves both unchanged (Fig 5B, C, H).
2. **In neurons only the lithium side was measured.** In iPSC-derived cortical neurons (one control line, day 45, lithium 10 days) lithium lowers spontaneous calcium transients (about 9 to 4.5 per 270 s, visual medians, Fig 4E), carbachol-evoked calcium (about 250 to 120 nM, Fig 4G) and store release (Fig 4I). There is NO IMPA1-deleted neuron arm (the authors state IMPA1-deleted neural stem cells die at the start of differentiation), NO inositol add-back in neurons, NO PIP2 measurement in neurons, and NO engagement readout for the GSK3 probe in neurons (beta-catenin is shown in HEK only). The rescue was measured in heterologous cells only; the link from the proximal route to neuronal firing crosses preparations at the critical step.
3. **Two details the existing WO-21 text does not carry:**
   (a) **IMPA1 dependence is endpoint-specific.** In the deleted line lithium no longer lowers store release or PIP2 recovery (Fig 3G to J, L) but STILL lowers store-operated entry (Fig 3I and K, ****). The abstract's "abrogated in IMPA1 deleted cells" holds for store release and PIP2, not for entry. A calcium readout that does not separate store release from entry cannot be assigned to IMPA1.
   (b) **The neuronal GSK3 "no effect" is a non-significant result at a different baseline, not an equivalence.** Untreated spontaneous frequency is about 9 per 270 s in the lithium experiment (Fig 4E) and about 5 in the GSK3-probe experiment (Fig 5E), which equals the lithium-treated level of Fig 4E (about 4.5); the probe arm falls about 30 percent (medians about 5 to 3.5; n 55 and 52; ns), against about 50 percent for lithium. No time-matched shared control, no equivalence test.
4. **A transcript or a transient frequency cannot name the route.** The lithium effect on transients needs ten days and is absent after five minutes (Fig S4D and E); the authors' own transcripts show lower voltage-gated calcium channel and glutamate receptor genes (Fig 6C and D) and the probe's transcriptome overlap is at best 3 percent. A transcriptional route to the lowered transients exists independently of receptor-evoked release; a proximal rescue would not by itself identify the route to firing.
5. **Verdict:** a pointer to the primary plus ONE hypothesis-level condition for WO-21 offered as a REQUIREMENT for the same neural preparation (below). It is mostly what ADV's draft condition already says; the source adds the endpoint split (point 3a) and the matched-baseline caution (3b). KEEP is not earned (no neuronal rescue); CLOSE is not right (the HEK chain is a genuine, clean proximal discriminator that the held text did not have).

Laboratory conditions as stated (no advice): lithium chloride 1 mM, 5 days in HEK, days 35 to 45 in neurons; GSK3 probe CHIR99021 10 uM, 24 h; carbachol 20 uM in HEK and 50 uM in neurons; inositol-supplemented medium about 155 uM total against the 40 uM already in standard DMEM, serum not dialysed (the reviewers asked; the inositol content of the cells was never measured, which the authors accept).

## Checks asked for
1. **Independent units.** Statistics are Student's unpaired t tests (Mann-Whitney in neurons) with the CELL as the unit (a minimum of about 100 cells, "from multiple dishes"); PIP2 curves come from two to four experiments with 30 to 60 cells; no a priori sample size, no blinding, no experiment or dish identifier. Neurons are one control iPSC line (D149, neural stem cell derived, a mixed culture with GFAP-positive cells; ROIs drawn on cells that responded). The IMPA1-deleted HEK line is one selected clone.
2. **The IMPA1 occlusion logic.** The deleted line has a lowered baseline on the very endpoints lithium lowers (HEK Cch peak about 580 against 780 nM in Fig 3C, store release about 480 against 700 nM in Fig 3E, slower PIP2 recovery in Fig 3F), so "no further lithium effect" is an attenuated baseline as much as a proof of dependence; what separates the two readings is the cDNA rescue of the baseline (shown) and a deleted-plus-cDNA arm tested for restored lithium sensitivity (NOT shown). The reviewers called Fig 3F "rather unconvincing" (means within one standard deviation).
3. **Baselines move between experiments.** The deleted line without lithium reads about 580 nM in Fig 3C and about 1 000 nM in Fig 3H; neuronal controls read about 9 and about 5 transients per 270 s in Figs 4E and 5E. A cross-experiment comparison is not a matched control.
4. **Store release against general firing against GSK3 output.** Kept apart: spontaneous transients are blocked by tetrodotoxin and nimodipine (voltage-gated, Fig 4D, E), whereas carbachol-evoked release is receptor-PLC-store; the paper links the two only through transcripts and the time course.
5. **A single drug-induced calcium trace cannot identify the route**; here the HEK chain has the knockout, the cDNA rescue and the add-back, the neurons have none of them.

## The ONE condition for WO-21 (hypothesis level; a requirement for the same neural preparation)
A lithium-related change in neuronal excitability can be attributed to the inositol route only if, in the SAME neural preparation that carries the functional endpoint: (1) a proximal readout (receptor-evoked store release in zero external calcium, kept apart from store-operated entry, and PIP2 recovery where measurable) is lowered by lithium and restored by a qualified inositol-route manipulation (inositol supplementation above a replete medium, or IMPA1 re-expression, with the knockout baseline and a restored lithium-sensitivity arm if a deleted line is used); (2) a mechanistically independent GSK3 probe is shown ENGAGED in those neurons (beta-catenin or a phospho-substrate), with baseline and time matched to the lithium arm in the same batch and an equivalence-style comparison, not a non-significant test; and (3) the functional endpoint tracks the rescued proximal readout in the same cultures before firing is attributed. A rescued proximal endpoint with no functional change weakens the functional mechanism (a transcriptional route, which this source documents, is then open); overlap of both engaged controls leaves mixed causality.

Not in this source: a rescue measured in neurons, a PIP2 readout in neurons, an IMPA1-deleted neuron arm, a GSK3 engagement readout in neurons. The WWOX-specific lithium question stays OPEN and untouched (Cheng 2020: one provoked-seizure experiment, engagement not measured).
