## Answer first (what the round found since the operator's 'go much deeper', in order of weight)
1. **The held data now carry one hard constraint that every idea must respect.** Neocortical slices burst in 0 of 11 (S-WT), 4 of 23 (S-HT), 36 of 42 (S-KO) (Breton 2021 Results, re-read), while the intrinsic profile of pyramidal cells (resting potential, input resistance, sag, spike amplitude) is a STEP (S-HT equal to S-KO). Incidence is graded, the profile is not: the profile cannot be what sets whether a slice bursts, and an idea that explains only the profile (I8 in its present form) explains a step, not the gradient.
2. **The model says the graded factor sits on the inhibitory side and is steep, and (SCI's correction of 19:55Z, which I verified in the source) the incidence is reproduced by quantities that are already MEASURED.** First version (`RIVALS_RESULT_SCI.md`, predictions hashed first): a graded inhibitory component of about a third to a half of its effective strength reproduces 0 / 17 / 86% (steady chloride 0.3, tonic 0.6 to 1.0, phasic 1.0 to 2.0); the NMDAR sustainer cannot (a step near wild type) and K+ clearance cannot (`DOSSIER_I16_ASTROGLIAL_K_SCI.md`: wild type still bursts in 65 to 76% at any ratio scanned). Correction after IT's attack: Breton 2021 Results 2.5, re-read by me, gives sIPSC mean amplitude S-CTL 57.3 +/- 31.0 against S-KO 27.5 +/- 19.4 pA (ratio 2.08; S-CTL is wild type and heterozygote pooled), frequency 10.3 +/- 11.5 against 8.4 +/- 10.0 Hz, sEPSC amplitude 23.3 against 24.7 pA (+6%). The ratio 2.08 is the phasic case that succeeded in SCI's scan, so 'phasic is the weak knob' was misleading: it is the one graded component whose size is HELD. SCI's new test with sizes taken from the paper (predictions hashed first, `het_held_sizes_test.py`, MODEL): phasic deficit as held, a small excitatory gradient and a graded rebound give S-KO 99 to 100%, S-HT 21 to 28%, S-WT 13 to 27%, inside the binomial windows of all three held incidences, **if S-HT sits near the controls in sIPSC**; with S-HT midway it fails (55 to 78%). So at model level the incidence needs no tonic or chloride change; I13 and I6 are allowed, not needed, and the 'chloride is the most efficient component' ranking is withdrawn (it was a distance to a cliff in a fitted network, with no held size). Limits: a fitted model whose knockout overshoots (98 to 100% against 86%); the sIPSC means are as printed, with SD at the event or cell level not stated; S-CTL pools two genotypes by the authors' rule, so S-HT alone is not in the text.
3. **The same model gives each graded component a fingerprint on two challenge ladders, and the K+ ladder alone cannot separate them** (SCI, `RESULT_I15_THRESHOLDS_SCI.md`, predictions hashed first, 3 right, 1 half, 0 wrong): wild-type 50% thresholds, K+ axis (drive increment) chloride 0.154 and phasic 0.145, tonic above the scan; Mg2+ axis (NMDAR multiplier) tonic 4.7, chloride 3.1, phasic 2.1, NMDAR contrast 1.5. (The fingerprints are for telling candidates apart if the phasic deficit does not turn out to be the whole story; see item 2.)
