# I10 Sleep-wake and thalamocortical dependence of the null's seizures (ADV; HYPOTHESIS; not medical advice)
Answer first: a rebound-prone network (measured sag and rebound in null neurons) should be STATE-dependent in vivo: seizure burden highest in NREM sleep and at state transitions, spike-wave-like activity in the spindle range. Slices cannot show it; one in vivo EEG with sleep staging can, and the case reports may already hold timing statements.
D1 Chain. (1) Null neurons show larger sag and rebound (HELD, Breton 2021 T13). (2) Rebound bursting through T-type calcium and HCN currents underlies thalamocortical spindles and spike-wave activity (KNOWN in general). (3) Ethosuximide, a T-type blocker, partly reduced the late phase of PTZ seizures in null pups (HELD, specificity not shown). (4) So the null cortex is easily recruited by the thalamocortical rebound rhythms of NREM sleep (INFERRED). (5) Seizures cluster in sleep and sleep transitions (PREDICTED).
D2 Non-obvious: it moves the question from 'which receptor' to 'which brain state', and explains why the same drug helps one phenotype and not another (links to I2).
D3 Consequences. Animal: sleep-staged EEG and EMG in null pups P14-P21: spike-wave-like discharges at 6-12 Hz in NREM; onset clusters at NREM-to-wake transitions; sleep deprivation raises the burden (known in many epilepsies; here a prediction for the null). Patient: timing of seizures against sleep in case reports. SURPRISING: GABAergic potentiation, which should help, may increase these discharges (rebound), while a T-type blocker reduces them, in vivo as in slices (links to I1).
D4 Model: the I1 oscillator with a state variable (thalamic drive) that raises the ignition probability in NREM.

Outcomes: clustering in NREM supports; no state dependence kills it.
D6 Intervention logic: T-type blockade (labels), sleep-state modulators (labels), and the aggravation counterparts: strong GABAergic drive in a rebound network; BLOCK-1 flags.
D7 Combination: I1 (oscillator), I2 (phenotype-conditional), I5/I9 (why the cortex fails to restrain the thalamic rhythm).
