# I5 PV-axon myelin and inhibitory TIMING (ADV; HYPOTHESIS, not a finding; not medical advice)
Answer first: the inhibitory deficit of the Wwox-null neocortex may be a deficit of WHEN inhibition arrives, not of how much. A late or jittered PV output fails to stop recruitment, so the circuit ignites (burst) although PV cells and GABA are present. One test decides it and needs no literature: the latency of disynaptic inhibition against excitation in null versus wild-type neocortex and hippocampus.
D1 Chain. (1) Wwox falls in neurons (KNOWN, Breton 2021) and in astrocyte-like nuclei too (HELD, one pool). (2) Myelin genes fall in mature oligodendrocyte nuclei even after composition adjustment (Mbp -36 percent, Plp1 -32 percent, HELD, one pool) and in all four neuron-deletion bulk contrasts, MORE in hippocampus (Mbp -77 percent) than in neocortex (-56 percent) (HELD). (3) Neocortical PV basket-cell axons are heavily myelinated and their myelination is activity-dependent (KNOWN in general; ASSUMED at P13-P17 here). (4) Thinner myelin or shorter internodes lengthen and jitter conduction and raise failure at branch points (ASSUMED). (5) Late, jittered inhibition cannot terminate recruitment: the E-I loop crosses a delay threshold (INFERRED). (6) The held fall of sIPSC frequency with preserved PV-cell numbers in some labs is then failed or late release, not missing cells (INFERRED).
D2 Why non-obvious: the usual reading counts PV cells or GABA; this reads the delay of the loop. It also explains why PV markers are NOT lost in every laboratory's data.
D3 Consequences. Cell: PV firing intact, conduction latency to targets longer, internode length and g-ratio of PV axons altered. Slice: feedforward inhibition lags excitation by more milliseconds in null neocortex with unchanged IPSC amplitude. SURPRISING: the hippocampus, which loses MORE myelin-gene signal, does not burst: so the deficit is necessary and not sufficient; the burst needs it AND the missing neocortical compensation (I3). A second surprise: developmental pharmacology: a remyelination or OPC-differentiation mechanism would help only if present in a P10-P20 window.
D4 Model. A feedforward E-I loop with an inhibitory conduction delay d and jitter sigma: burst probability as a function of (d, sigma, recurrent gain); the held burst interval and duration statistics fix the gain; the model gives a delay threshold d*. Pseudocode only here; HE's simulation harness can run it.
D5 Experiments. (a) no new data: the held tables for a node/paranode module across all contrasts (Scn8a, Kcna1, Cntnap1, Cntnap2, Nfasc, Mag, Mobp, Cldn11) by region and cell class; (b) one slice experiment: paired recordings or field recordings of disynaptic inhibition latency in neocortex and hippocampus, null against wild type, plus the MBP/Caspr/PV triple stain of PV axons on the SAME sections as the Aldaz PV count in the brief; (c) clinical-observational: myelin-sensitive MRI against seizure phenotype, as a design only. Outcomes: longer latency AND thin PV-axon myelin supports; normal latency kills it.
D6 Intervention logic: remyelination or OPC-differentiation mechanisms (muscarinic antagonism of the clemastine type, thyroid-hormone-pathway agents, other labels) act on link 2 to 4; none is an antiseizure agent; BLOCK-1 flags needed; the aggravation counterpart is any agent that adds delay (none known).
D7 Combination: I3 supplies the missing brake in the hippocampus; I1 turns the delay into the trigger of the two-stage burst; I9 asks whether the PV cells are mature.
D8 Stronger: a latency lengthening in null neocortex. Killed by: normal latency with normal PV-axon myelin (needs the experiment, not literature).
D9 Cheapest next step: the held-table paranode/node lookup (SCI or HE) and adding a latency readout to the pilot's recordings.
