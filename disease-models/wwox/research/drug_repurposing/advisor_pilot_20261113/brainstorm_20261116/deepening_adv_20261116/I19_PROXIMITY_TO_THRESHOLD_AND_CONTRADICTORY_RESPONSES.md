# I19 PROXIMITY TO THRESHOLD AS ONE CAUSE OF CONTRADICTORY, VARIABLE AND RESISTANT DRUG RESPONSES (dossier v1, ADV; free seat; HYPOTHESIS; mechanism level; not medical advice; reference genotype class only)

Written for: IT (owner of the repository table that can test it; second reader), SCI (model), HE (harness), the operator. Labels: HELD (source file named), MODEL, ASSUMED, INFERRED. Seed: the operator's own question, put as three observations that the literature treats separately: **contrasting results, a phenotype spectrum that is too variable, and drug resistance**. Idea: they may be three faces of one variable, the distance of a given brain from its burst threshold (I15), because a threshold system answers a small perturbation in very different ways depending on where it sits.

## Answer first
* **The idea.** In a threshold system the response to a fixed small perturbation is not a property of the drug alone; it is a property of the drug and the distance to threshold. (a) **Near threshold** (the heterozygote-like state: 17% of slices burst; in patients, milder or hypomorphic genotype, a favourable state or age): a small change in either direction flips the system, so benefit and aggravation are both possible and look like contradictory results across reports; the same agent will 'work' in one child and 'worsen' another. (b) **Far past threshold** (the null: 86% of slices burst, the model overshoots at 98 to 100%): the system is saturated, so a drug that shifts the threshold modestly changes nothing, which is what drug resistance looks like. (c) **Far below threshold** (wild type, 0 of 11): nothing bursts, nothing to treat. The *phenotype spectrum* is the spread of distances in the population, and the three response patterns are read off one axis.
* **Why it is not trivial.** It predicts WHO is variable, not just that someone is: variability of response should be largest at intermediate severity and smallest at the two ends; aggravation reports should cluster in milder or intermediate genotypes and ages, resistance in the most severe. It also says that a single class-level ranking (the matrix) cannot be stable across patients even if its mechanism is right, because the same class has opposite signs at two distances. That is a statement about the instrument's ceiling, and it is checkable.
* **Held anchors (all re-read).** Burst incidence 0/11, 4/23, 36/42 slices for S-WT, S-HT, S-KO (Breton 2021 Results, `files/fulltext/PMID34634460_Breton2021_EPMC_2026-09-27.xml`), i.e. a graded distance across the dose axis; SCI's fitted model puts the null above threshold (incidence 98 to 100% against the held 86%) and the incidence curve needs a steep graded inhibitory component of about a third to a half of its effective strength (SCI's rival-component test, MODEL, in the I1 dossier and `het_incidence.tsv`); the I2 extraction (IT, sealed): 14 held reports cannot separate semiology groups, zero seizure-worsening statements, a few 'new type after exposure' leads (vigabatrin followed by myoclonic seizures in 2 children, topiramate by spasms in 1; I14: cannot discriminate).
* **Depth: chain; testable on a column that the other-report search can carry for free.**

## D1 Statement and chain
1. Burst probability per unit time is a steep function of a distance variable x (I15; steep because the model shows a 20% change of one brake weight moves the rate 77%: MODEL). 2. x differs across genotypes by dose (HELD pattern above), across ages (the burst window is P13-P17 in mouse; INFERRED for children) and across states (sleep, fever, drugs). 3. A drug moves x by a small amount d (its effect on the relevant brake or drive). 4. The change in burst or seizure probability is f'(x) times d: large near threshold, near zero on the saturated plateau (INFERRED from 1). 5. Reports of drug effect average over children at different x: wide, contradictory distributions where f' is large, 'no effect' where it is small.

## D2 Why non-obvious
The usual explanations of contradictory AED results are heterogeneity of cohort, publication, and mechanism class. This keeps the mechanism class and adds a second, quantitative variable whose signature (variance largest at intermediate severity) is different from any of those.

## D3 Consequences across scales
Slice: the same drug shifts the K+ or Mg2+ onset by a constant amount (I15 threshold shift) but changes burst incidence by very different amounts in S-HT (steep part) and S-KO (plateau): a within-experiment demonstration of the point. Animal: drug effect size in the heterozygote, or in the P47T-like intermediate state, exceeds that in the null for a threshold-shifting agent; aggravation, if it exists, shows first in the intermediate group. Patient (observational): variance of the response, and the proportion of aggravation reports, should be higher in intermediate genotype classes (hypomorphic or missense with residual function) and at the age with the steepest dependence; resistance (no response, repeated agent failures) should be commonest in the most severe class.
**Surprising predictions.** (1) Among children, the proportion with a *stated worsening or new seizure type* after an agent is higher in the intermediate-severity genotype group than in the null/null group, even though the null/null group is the more severe: this would be a mechanism-free sign of proximity. (2) The matrix class ranking disagrees with itself between genotype strata: the top set computed on the null/null children differs from that on the intermediate children (the instrument's ceiling). (3) Hypothesis-level: the same drug in the same slice preparation is more effective on incidence (not on threshold) in S-HT than in S-KO.

## Update v2 (ADV, 2026-11-16 20:30Z, after SCI's second reading `SECOND_READINGS_I19_I17_I18_SCI.md` and the model curve `RESULT_I19_CURVE_AND_RIVAL_I1_SCI.md`, both read by me on main at 410d8baf9; IT's table of the 42 children by severity class as a record)
What the readers took away, and what I withdraw:
1. **The plateau is an incidence plateau, not a rate plateau.** 'Far past threshold a modest shift changes nothing' holds for whether a slice bursts at all; clinical resistance is about how often events happen, and in the oscillator the burst rate at S-KO still falls strongly under a threshold-shifting arm (-68% in bursting slices, A1). The model supports 'the incidence response is non-monotonic across genotype (zero at S-KO, largest at S-HT, floor-limited at S-WT; predictions frozen first)' and does NOT support 'resistance at the null' for rate. D1 step 4 and the 'far past threshold' sentence of the answer-first are narrowed to incidence.
2. **Distance changes the MAGNITUDE of an effect, not its sign** (MODEL: no arm aggravates at any genotype setting; the aggravation counterpart, T-type weight x1.25, is inert, S-HT -0.02, S-WT +0.02). So the claim 'benefit and aggravation are both possible near threshold' needs a SECOND variable that changes the sign of the net effect. Candidates the models already hold: a regime variable (a steep rebound gate with a tonic-selective potentiator gave aggravation in 2 of 47 parameter sets), steady chloride load (the potentiator sign flips only weakly, +6 to 8% at 13 to 14 mM, ceiling-limited) and, at another level, thalamocortical state (I10). **I19 is therefore restated as a two-variable idea: distance sets how large the effect is and how noisy it looks across children; a regime variable sets its sign.** Contradictory reports then need both, and aggravation clusters in children in the aggravating regime, not simply at intermediate severity. Prediction (1) of D3 (higher worsening proportion at intermediate severity) is withdrawn as stated; what stays is that effect-size variance should peak at intermediate severity.
3. **Prediction (3) of D3 ('the same drug is more effective on incidence in S-HT than in S-KO') is withdrawn as a discriminator:** any logistic indicator implies it. The discriminating form is on the rate: equal relative rate change at S-KO and S-HT (SCI's frozen D4 holds).
4. **Power.** The sealed table holds zero worsening statements and no severity class reaches 8 children with a stated outcome (IT: biallelic or null-like 17 children, 12 with outcome; residual or missense 22, 10 with outcome, class by a keyword rule written after seeing the table); severity class is confounded with onset age, variant domain and report source. The held anchor of I19 is therefore only the S-HT incidence curve; the clinical half stays a design (D5b column, minimum 8 per class) and not a test.
5. **What stays:** the stratification advice (D6: stratify by severity class and age before pooling, report distributions not only means) and the S-HT arm of the pilot as the cheapest way to read the slope of the incidence curve. Status: ADVANCE, depth chain narrowed; kill: effect-size variance equal across severity classes at n of 8 or more per class in the D5b extraction.
