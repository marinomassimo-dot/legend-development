# I9 Perineuronal nets and PV maturation: 'present but immature' (ADV; HYPOTHESIS; not medical advice)
Answer first: PV cells may not be LOST in the Wwox-null neocortex but IMMATURE at the age when the bursts appear (P13-P17), because the activity-dependent program that matures them (Npas4, Arc, Fos, perineuronal-net assembly) is lower in the held tables. That reconciles the laboratory-dependent PV marker findings and predicts a transient window.
D1 Chain. (1) Immediate-early genes are lower in null neuron-like nuclei (Fos 3.05 to 1.40, Arc 9.40 to 4.68, Npas4 6.96 to 4.68, Egr1 10.32 to 6.15 CPM, HELD, one pool; the independent line P47T not lower). (2) NPAS4 and ARC drive inhibitory synapse formation onto excitatory cells and PV maturation (KNOWN in general). (3) Perineuronal nets form around PV cells in neocortex from about P14 and stabilise inhibition (KNOWN in general). (4) A PV cell with low Pvalb, no net, an immature GABA-A alpha subunit mix and immature Kv3 content has weaker, slower output (ASSUMED). (5) Bursts then appear in the window where inhibition should have matured and may fade or persist later (INFERRED).
D2 Non-obvious: it makes the PV problem developmental, not degenerative, and it predicts it is REVERSIBLE in a window.
D3 Consequences. Net components (Acan, Bcan, Hapln1, Tnr, Ptprz1) low or late in null neocortex; the GABA-A alpha2-to-alpha1 switch late; the Kv3.1/3.2 content low. SURPRISING: burst rate falls with age in the null if maturation catches up (an age series P13, P21, P30), whereas a degenerative picture predicts a rise or a plateau.
D4 Model: a maturation variable m(t) for PV output with an activity-dependent rate; burst probability = f(1 - m); two parameters from held numbers; fits the age window.
D5 Experiments. (a) no new data: net-component, subunit-switch and Kv3 transcripts in all held contrasts and the PV-class pools; (b) PV, net (WFA) and Kv3.1 staining at P14, P21, P30 in the same animals as the bursts; an age series of the burst rate; (c) none.
