## What the route is
A rare pediatric disease priority-review voucher is a voucher the US drug regulator awards **to the sponsor of an approved application** for a drug that treats a rare pediatric disease. It entitles the holder to priority review (a review goal of 6 months) of one later application, and the voucher may be sold or transferred without a limit on the number of transfers. It is a regulatory and commercial instrument of a drug sponsor; it is not a grant and it is not available to a research group for an in-silico or laboratory result.

Source read: 21 U.S.C. 360ff as served by the Cornell Legal Information Institute page, fetched 2026-11-20 (`https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/21/360ff`). Points taken from that text:
- the disease must be serious or life-threatening with manifestations primarily in people from birth to 18 years, and rare within the meaning of the orphan-drug section;
- the application must contain **no active moiety previously approved** under the ordinary pathways, and be an application under section 505(b)(1) (a full application); it must rely on clinical data from a pediatric population, must not seek an adult indication in the original application, and be deemed eligible for priority review;
- the awarding authority ends for approvals after **30 September 2029** in the version served on that page.
Not checked: whether that page is the version in force today, the regulator's own guidance, any recent change to the programme, and the court decision about old "deemed approved" applications that a third-party repository (CarterPry/RareDiseaseArb) describes in its README (I read the README, not the ruling or the statute text it cites for that point).

## Which part of our work it would touch
- **The generative-engine packages (decision-grade in-silico packages):** only indirectly. A package could be one input to a drug owner's case, but the voucher is earned by an approval, which needs clinical development far beyond anything in this repository.
- **The bridge candidate (ethosuximide, SCI's priority experiment):** the text read requires that the active moiety has not been previously approved. Ethosuximide is an old approved drug (approval history **not checked**); on that text alone the route looks closed to it. Whether any other agent on the shortlists (for example gaboxadol or ganaxolone) has an active moiety never approved in the US is **not checked**; I would not state it from memory.
- **The disease criterion:** the reference genotype class is a rare, pediatric-onset condition and plausibly fits the definition, but a rare-pediatric designation is requested by a sponsor and was **not assessed**.
