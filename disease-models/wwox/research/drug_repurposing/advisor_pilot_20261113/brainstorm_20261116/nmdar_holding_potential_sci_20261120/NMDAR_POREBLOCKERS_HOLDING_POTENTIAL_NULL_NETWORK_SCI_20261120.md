# NMDAR pore blockers by holding potential against the held membrane state of the Wwox-deleted neocortex (item 2 of the reserve; SCI, 2026-11-20; hypothesis level; no dose; reference genotype class; not medical advice)

Asked by ADV: which held recording from a Wwox-deficient line gives a resting or burst-state membrane potential, or a chloride gradient from which one could be bounded, before any outside literature; and what that does to the ketamine against memantine order that HE's batch 4 found to flip with the holding potential. Time-boxed to one sitting; no delegate; no outside literature read.

## Answer first
1. **A held bound exists and it straddles the point where the order flips; no order between ketamine and memantine can be read.** Breton 2021 (held full text; neuron-specific Wwox deletion, Synapsin-Cre; layer II/III pyramidal cells in acute neocortical slices; P13 to P17; 35 degrees; ACSF 1.3 mM Mg2+) gives resting membrane potential **-79.25 mV in wild type (13 cells, 5 animals), -75.51 in heterozygotes, -73.62 in the knockout (40 cells, 15 animals; both starred as different from wild type; Table 1; the table does not say whether the spread is SEM or SD)**, action-potential threshold -41 to -44 mV, and the **reversal potential of the current underlying the LFP bursts: -31 +/- 9.5 mV for the whole burst, +6 +/- 11.6 mV at the onset window, -36 +/- 9.8 mV for the high-frequency component** (voltage clamp at -70 to +20 mV, 30 bursts from 5 animals, K-gluconate pipette).
2. **What that bounds, and what it does not.** The burst-state membrane potential itself was **not recorded** (voltage-clamp reversal potentials of the synaptic current, not unclamped potentials during bursts). An unclamped cell driven by a current that reverses at about -31 mV (and nearer 0 mV at onset, excitation-dominant) cannot go beyond that reversal, and spikes occur above the threshold of about -41 mV; so the potential at which NMDA channels conduct during a burst lies **between the knockout resting value (about -74 mV) and the reversal (about -31 mV at most, nearer 0 mV only at onset)**. That is my inference from the held numbers, not a measurement.
3. **Against HE batch 4 (one document, human receptors in HEK293 cells, L-glutamate response):** at -60 mV ketamine 500 nM (GluN2A) and 510 nM (GluN2B), memantine 840 and 780 nM (less than 2-fold apart); at -40 mV ketamine 53.3 and 15.2 microM, memantine 28.1 and 25.6 microM: the order reverses on GluN2A (memantine about 1.9 times more potent) and not on GluN2B (ketamine about 1.7 times more potent). **Every pore-blocker value falls 30- to 100-fold between -60 and -40 mV.** The held bound (-74 to -31 mV) covers both sides of the flip, so the held data cannot say which agent is the better pore blocker in this network; they say that **at the depolarised part of a burst, tens of micromolar, not sub-micromolar, are the relevant potencies for both agents** (their potency at -40 mV, and weaker still toward 0 mV), and the subunit composition at P13 to P17 (not held) changes which of the two is stronger.
4. **A held contrast that matters more than the order:** d-APV at 50 micromolar **eliminated** the spontaneous bursts in the S-KO slices and they recovered on washout (Breton 2021, Results on pharmacology; LFP in layer II/III); carbenoxolone at 100 micromolar cut burst frequency by 87 percent (p = 0.008) and duration by 18 percent. d-APV is competitive and not voltage-dependent in the way pore blockers are, so **NMDAR dependence of the bursts is held for a competitive antagonist, and no pore blocker was ever tested on these bursts**. The held potentials say a pore blocker will be weakest exactly in the depolarised burst state.
5. **Chloride gradient: not held, and the recording fixes it.** The burst recordings used a K-gluconate pipette with about 12 mM chloride (1 mM MgCl2 and 10 mM NaCl, so internal chloride about 12 mM) against about 129.5 mM in the ACSF (123 NaCl, 3.5 KCl and 1.5 CaCl2), which sets the recording's own chloride reversal near -63 mV (my arithmetic with the Nernst equation at 35 degrees from the stated solutions; whole-cell dialysis, not the native value). The inhibitory recordings used a 140 mM KCl pipette at -70 mV (the SCI42 reading: the native gradient is not reported by this paper; gramicidin perforated-patch recording, the measurement that would give it, was proposed and not done). So **no native chloride reversal or GABA reversal exists in any held Wwox-deficient recording**, and the burst reversal of -31 mV mixes an excitatory component (near 0 mV) with an inhibitory one set by the pipette.

## What it does to the NMDAR within-class comparison
* **No change in the order; the comparison is not decided by potentials alone.** Ketamine against memantine is a tie that depends on the potential, the GluN2 subunit and the Mg2+ concentration (1.3 mM in this ACSF; the batch 4 sheet found no Mg2+-matched per-subunit value), none of which is pinned for the null network at P13 to P17.
* **The cheapest discriminating experiment bypasses the potential question:** apply each pore blocker, in graded concentrations, to the S-KO slice burst assay of Breton 2021 (LFP in layer II/III, d-APV 50 micromolar as the positive control already in that paper), with the burst frequency and duration as readout; it reads the potential, subunit and Mg2+ effects together at the network state, at the age and preparation where the bursts were characterised. Exposure facts in vivo are separate and not in this note.
* **What would change a choice:** a pore blocker that suppresses the bursts at concentrations compatible with a brain exposure fact (none held for either agent in the pups) would put it ahead of the competitive tool; a pore blocker that fails at concentrations well above its -60 mV value would show that voltage-dependent relief governs and demote the class as a pore-blocker route (a competitive or subunit-selective antagonist would remain a different question).

## Limits
Slice, P13 to P17, layer II/III pyramidal cells only, one laboratory; potentials are resting and voltage-clamp reversal values, not burst-state membrane potentials; the table's spread statistic is not named; the single-document pore-blocker values are HE's reading of one 2024 document (human receptors, HEK293, room-to-body temperature not checked); my chloride arithmetic assumes the stated solutions and 35 degrees; nothing here is an exposure statement.
