## 1. Identifiability: what a structural rival needs beyond a parameter change
Facts from the model and ADV-096 (`parametric_sensitivity_20261119/RESULT_PARAMETRIC_SENSITIVITY_HE_20261120.md`, `rebound_oscillator.py`):
- The oscillator has one excitatory-inhibitory population with NMDAR, AMPA, T-type, GABA-A, tonic GABA, adenosine and an intracellular chloride state `c` whose Nernst potential sets the GABA-A driving force `G(c)`. **A chloride-dependent inhibition term already exists**; "altered chloride-dependent inhibition" is a parameter change here (`c0`, `tau_c`, `kappa_c`: the `cl_tau_mult` and high-chloride variants of ADV-096), not a new structure. "Excitatory gain" is `J_N` and `J_A` (the `nmda_mult` variant). "Altered inhibitory recruitment" is `J_I` (the `ji_mult` variant), but only as a gain, not as a recruitment threshold.
- **Not in the model:** a Kv7 current (ADV-096 used a drive offset as an assumed stand-in) and a second interneuron class. These are the only true structural alternatives of the brief.
- **The held fit is thin.** The baseline is fitted to two held numbers (median interval 26.2 s, median duration 0.66 s, with an inferred interval-rate IQR); every other constant is assumed (docstring of `rebound_oscillator.py`). The genotype contexts are assumed multipliers (the adapter's own docstring: S-HT rebound 0.69, excitatory 0.92, phasic 0.9, "the last ASSUMED"). So several rivals "fit the same held measurements" trivially: two numbers do not identify some twenty parameters.
- **The ADV-096 variants were not rivals in the sense of the brief:** they were not re-fitted, and their baseline burst counts range from 0.06 to 3.05 times the baseline (section 5 of the ADV-096 result). A different circuit state is not a competing explanation of the same data.
- **What a structural rival needs (my estimate, not measured):** a Kv7 current is one gating state plus three parameters (peak conductance, half-activation and slope, time constant), none held; a second interneuron class is one more population plus about six parameters (recruitment threshold, gain, time constant, two cross-couplings, weight onto the pyramids), none held. Each costs about 50 to 150 lines of model code, a new frozen adapter and **one extra re-fit to the same two held numbers with one free parameter per rival** (the baseline fit is named in a comment of `rebound_oscillator.py` as `fit_baseline`, but no such function is in that file; where SCI's fitting code lives, and its run time, I have not found or measured). Without the re-fit the rival is a variant, not a rival.
- **Rule 1 (the change):** a rival enters the pilot only if, after its own re-fit, it reproduces the two held numbers within the held interval, and the free parameter used for the re-fit is declared. Two rivals that pass and then diverge on a perturbation are a prediction; two that were never re-fitted are a sensitivity analysis (what ADV-096 is).
- **Still not identifiable after Rule 1:** an order between classes acting on different parameters on one pharmacological scale (ADV-096 section 4), and the weights of the rivals. The pilot therefore predicts the **sign or range of one perturbation response per rival**, never a class ranking.

## 2. Baseline and held-out test
- **Baseline = the current evidence-only method, written as a forecaster, not as a story.** One person who has not seen the simulator output writes, before the simulation is frozen, one forecast per prediction from the held literature only (the existing engine-evaluation BASELINE condition is the precedent: `deepening_he_20261116/engine_eval/`). A second, one-line baseline is the **generic suppression rule** named in the brief: "any anticonvulsant-class perturbation lowers the burst count", a fixed probability on the decrease bin (a rule, not a fit). Both sit in the same sidecar as the rival forecasters (`who` column), so the existing scorer sees them identically.
- **Held-out unit = the study, not the slice or the animal.** The two held numbers come from one study (Breton 2021), so the model is fitted on that study and **no retrospective split exists inside held data: n is one study.** A retrospective test therefore needs a second study that reports interval or duration in the same system, with the sources' shared provenance declared (same laboratory or mouse line = one cluster, counted once). Whether such a study is held is SCI's question, not mine; I have not searched for it.
- **The prospective test is the real one:** predictions timestamped and frozen by commit **before** the outcome table (the Nappi per-animal data, the Aqeilan-team organoid source table) is opened; `prediction_ledger.py guard` answers `OUTCOME_MAY_OPEN` only if every freeze, rule and baseline is committed and unmodified. A simulator cannot recognise an outcome (the contamination that hit the language-model scorer, 10 of 12 pairs recognised), **but the modeller can**: the rival parameters must be frozen without reading the unreleased outcome, and the freeze states which published results were read when the parameters were chosen.
- A poster or table with missing denominators makes the prediction `unscorable`, a status the ledger already has; it is never scored by the nearest bin.

## 3. What counts as improvement, a tie, an overconfident failure (existing code where possible)
Per prediction the scorer already prints the natural-log probability each forecaster put on the observed bin, and the total per forecaster. I add only the **reading rule**, written before any outcome:
- **Improvement:** the rival's total log score beats BOTH baselines **and** it wins on at least 5 of 5 scored independent outcomes (the exact one-sided sign test then has p = 1/32 = 0.031; with 4 outcomes the smallest possible p is 0.0625, so 4 cannot count). This is SCI's existing rule ("beats both baselines on a second ledger of this kind") plus the sign-test floor.
- **Tie:** anything else without a confident miss. A tie says the simulator added nothing measurable at this n; it is a valid and expected result.
- **Overconfident failure:** an observed value in a bin to which the rival gave 0.05 or less while the evidence-only baseline gave 0.20 or more. One is enough to record the rival as overconfident; it is a one-line filter on the per-prediction output of the scorer.
- **Calibration: not calibrated.** With about 2 data sources of 3 to 6 contrasts each, sharing laboratory and cohort, the effective independent n is 2 to 4. A reliability curve needs tens of independent outcomes; below that the report says "not calibrated" and prints counts, never a rate used as a weight (the ledger's `SMALL_N = 30` rule already says this).

## 4. Grid coverage, uncertainty within a model, uncertainty over structure: three separate numbers
- **Coverage:** the fraction of the declared parameter box (the held interval of each assumed parameter) in which the rivals' predicted signs differ, per prediction. A statement about where the rivals disagree, not about biology.
- **Within a model:** the empirical distribution over seeds at each cell (at least 10 seeds), binned into the sidecar for that forecaster.
- **Over structure:** the disagreement between forecasters, **reported as separate rows and never averaged**, because no defensible weights exist (not identifiable after Rule 1). A mixture is offered only if a stated source gives the weights, with a sensitivity table.
- A fraction of favourable scenarios, or of seeds crossing a threshold, is a model statistic; it is labelled as such and is not a clinical probability.

## 5. Computational limits
- **Measured (ADV-096 job record):** 1,206 single-seed simulations in at most 3.1 h at 2 workers under a 3 GB scope (memory available stayed near 10 GB, load 3 to 4), so about 18 worker-seconds per simulation (derived, not re-timed).
- **Pilot size:** 3 rivals x 6 contexts (3 genotype settings x 2 ages or preparations) x 2 strengths (0 and 1) x 10 to 20 seeds = 360 to 720 simulations: about 1 to 2 hours at 2 workers, within memory. **No bisection:** a paired fixed-strength comparison removes the bracket.
- **Why not 3 seeds:** in ADV-096, 49 of 71 crossing cells had at least one bracket end with fewer than 3 of 3 seeds on one side of the threshold; with three seeds a unanimous vote occurs by chance a quarter of the time at a boundary. A forecast needs a seed distribution: at least 10 seeds, 20 where a rival's bin is narrow.
- **Limits that remain:** the genotype contexts are assumed multipliers; floating-point results can differ in the last digits across machines (the harness `check` prints it); a nearly silent variant (0.06 of baseline in ADV-096) has no interval and counts as not holding.

## 6. How to remove the change, and the strongest counter-explanation
- **Removal:** all new material sits in one new folder (the frozen scan, the rival adapters, the sidecar, the outcome table): `git rm -r` of that folder is the whole revert. No existing file is edited (`sim_harness.py`, the oscillator, the ledger and the scorers stay byte-identical), and the optional `forecast_sidecar.py` is one file to delete.
- **Strongest counter-explanation to this proposal:** the simulator adds nothing beyond the evidence-only baseline. Its rivals diverge only where **we** chose the assumed parameters and the genotype multipliers, so a "win" would come from those choices and from where the bins were cut, not from structure; and at an effective n of 2 to 4 non-independent outcomes a win cannot be told from luck even if all outcomes go its way. The honest expected result is a tie. The pilot is still worth running for two reasons: it is cheap (about two hours of compute and a hand-written sidecar), and a tie or a confident miss is information that closes the question for this model class, which the hindcast (12 pairs, contaminated) could not do.
- **A simpler argument that could replace the pilot:** if the two rivals differ in sign on only one perturbation, say which one and ask whether a held or public report already contains that response; then the decision needs no simulation, only a reading (SCI's alternative).
