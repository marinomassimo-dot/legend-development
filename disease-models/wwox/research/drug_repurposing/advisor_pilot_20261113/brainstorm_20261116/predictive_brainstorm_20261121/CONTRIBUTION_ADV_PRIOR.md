## What the held evidence already says about prediction
- The 12-pair drug-outcome hindcast (ADV-091) showed no separation (AUC 0.44 to 0.63, intervals include 0.5) and the scorer recognised 10 of 12 outcomes: a prospective, timestamped test is the only clean kind; a retrospective one on known outcomes cannot be made blind.
- The oscillator sensitivity (ADV-096, HE) varied parameters of one model: it can compare a class against itself across variants, not classes against each other on one scale, and not different biological structures. A structural-alternatives test needs a model that can represent them; the current one cannot (no Kv7 current, no second interneuron class, chloride dependence only through one parameter).
- The human organoid data (Brain 2026) give group-level event rate, amplitude and active-ROI percentage; the per-line table behind Fig 6A(ii) and the per-animal mouse data (Nappi) are not public. So any test on them is a FUTURE external test, not a current one.

## Options ADV will compare (no preference yet)
- **O1, circuit pilot (the brief's example).** Two or three structural explanations (inhibitory recruitment, chloride-dependent inhibition, excitatory gain, plus a generic-suppression comparator) fitted to the same held measurements, one divergent prediction on data not yet released. Needs: held measurements at one unit and age; a model able to carry the three structures. Risk: the structures may fit the same held data equally (non-identifiability), so the divergent prediction rests on the assumptions that distinguish them.
- **O2, organoid withheld-endpoint prediction across lines.** Predict a withheld maturation or activity endpoint for a held-out line from held data. Risk: the held data are group-aggregate (few lines), so there may be no honest holdout; a comparable-assay requirement may leave too few independent units.
- **O3, a simple pre-registered prediction rule with no new model.** Fix now, in writing and timestamped, the direction or range of what the evidence-only method says about a named comparison (for example, whether a WWOX-model effect of an inhibitory-targeting class is expected to exceed a wild-type or generic effect), plus what would reverse it, and score it when Nappi or Aqeilan data appear. Cost: near zero; weakness: it tests the existing method, not a new capacity.
- **O4, no pilot now.** If no design changes which experiment or comparison goes first, record exactly why (for example, the decision is fixed by exposure or by a missing assay, not by a prediction) and name the one datum whose arrival would reopen it.

## Scoring language fixed in advance (applies to whichever pilot is chosen)
- IMPROVEMENT: the pilot's prediction, scored on units it never saw, beats the evidence-only comparator on a pre-declared measure with the interval excluding a tie; AND the pilot changes which comparison goes first.
- TIE: no measurable difference, or the prediction is unevaluable because denominators are missing (an unevaluable prediction is recorded as unevaluable, not as a success).
- OVERCONFIDENT FAILURE: the stated range excluded the observed value, or a stated probability exceeded what the number of independent outcomes can support. A fraction of favourable scenarios is never a clinical probability; calibration is claimed only with enough independent outcomes.
- Both goals stay separate: broad developmental recovery and the narrower adjunctive goal.

## Counter-explanation ADV will hold against every pilot
A prediction that improves on the comparator because both read the same studies and the same laboratory (shared provenance) is not evidence of predictive capacity; each test must declare which units are independent of the training inputs.
