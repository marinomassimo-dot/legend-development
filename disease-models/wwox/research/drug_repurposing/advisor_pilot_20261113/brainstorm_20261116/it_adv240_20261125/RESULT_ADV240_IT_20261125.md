## 1. Answer in six lines
1. **Measured base: 12 WWOX cases among 788 solved cases (1.5 percent) in seven series with a countable per-case or per-gene table** (range by leave-one-series-out 1.3 to 1.6 percent); with the seven series whose solved total I read only approximately or whose table is not gene-countable added, 12 of about 965 (1.2 percent). It is a **base for the denominators of RP-035, not an incidence**: series were chosen by title and format, and the WWOX-rich ones are infantile-onset cohorts (section 3).
2. **Four of the 14 series opened contain WWOX cases (Henry 2025: 4 of 278; Barcia 2025: 6 of 310; Scheffer 2022: 1 of 36; Palmer 2018: 1 of 16); ten contain none.** The two large ones carry 10 of the 12 cases.
3. **Panel subtotals are given where the series gives one:** infantile epileptic spasms syndrome 4 of 51 solved (Henry); early-infantile DEE 6 of 72 solved (Barcia, text subtotal).
4. **Predictions against outcome:** P1 (pooled share under 0.5 percent) **wrong**; P2 (at least half of the series with zero WWOX) **held** (10 of 14; of the seven pooled series only three are zero, so the reading depends on the base); P3 (one series without a per-gene table) **held** (three); P4 (the one-in-1285 Swedish figure within a factor of five of the pooled share) **wrong** (about 19 times lower). SCI's Swedish series was not re-identified here: the Swedish series opened (Henry 2025) has 4 WWOX individuals among 278 solved.
5. **Series that would most move the denominator are not in the public bucket** (access list, section 5, for the operator PDF file; not contacted).
6. **Surprise (one line):** the WWOX count in the large French panel series is **six of 310 solved, all six in early-infantile DEE**, and one of the six is a variant of unknown significance that the series lists in its own table of 310 causal-or-reported findings; a share that counts only pathogenic and likely pathogenic variants is 5 of 309.

## 3. What the number does and does not say
* **Who is in it:** infantile-onset and early-life cohorts from tertiary centres; the two large series are WWOX-rich because panels and genome analyses of the last years include WWOX (the Paris panel lists WWOX in both versions; the Sydney text notes that not all earlier panels included it).
* **Series selection:** by title, free full text and a supplement in the bucket; no series was chosen for containing WWOX (the WWOX-bearing series already in the repository, `PAPER 201` to `PAPER 206` and `PAPER 217` to `PAPER 220`, were found by searching for WWOX and are **not** used as a base; by a PMID search of the paper registry, the literature log and the full-text queue, three of the four WWOX-bearing series here (Barcia, Scheffer, Palmer) are not held; Henry is held as `PAPER 241`, whose record already states four WWOX diagnoses, which agrees with the count here).
* **Patients counted once:** by genotype and age no re-reported patient was found between the four WWOX-bearing series; Henry 150 and 151 may be siblings; the Palmer patient is a previously published case, so a later series could re-count it.
* **Denominators are solved cases, not tested cases:** WWOX per tested case is lower, and a WWOX share among severe infantile epilepsy depends on which phenotypes the series admits.
* **For RP-035:** a defensible plain-language base is "about 1 to 2 percent of solved cases in infantile-onset epilepsy cohorts, with zero in several smaller series", and it should be quoted with the table above, not as an incidence.
