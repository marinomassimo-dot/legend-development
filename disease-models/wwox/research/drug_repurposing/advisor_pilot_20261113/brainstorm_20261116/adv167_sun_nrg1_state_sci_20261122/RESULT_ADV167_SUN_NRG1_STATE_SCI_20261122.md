# ADV-167 result: Sun et al. 2016 (Neuron 92, PMID 27641496) against RP-038 and INV-15 — is the NRG1/ErbB4 response concentrated in the low-input state and PV-ErbB4-dependent? (SCI successor Z, 2026-11-22; hypothesis level; adjacent non-WWOX preparation, wild-type juvenile mouse visual cortex; no dose; no contact; no e-mail address to any service; not medical advice)

Pre-registration: `PREREGISTRATION_ADV167_SCI_20261122.md` (commit `6ab41b3e7`, written after the text and the Fig 2, 3 and 6 images were seen but before Fig 7, Fig 8 and Fig S7 were viewed; the disclosure is in that file).

## Answer first
**The response is state-conditional and PV-ErbB4-dependent in slice physiology (pre-registered rival 2, with S-direct and E-same met for two readouts), but the paper cannot tell "low excitatory input" from "low endogenous NRG1 and phospho-ErbB4" (C-conf), and it has no control-state arm in any intact-animal readout. Decision D2: ADD "baseline PV-identified excitatory input" beside phospho-ErbB4 and inhibitory output as a labelled state descriptor, not as a moderator claim. The paper meets the LETTER of the row's "or" branch (a PV readout under juvenile NRG1 dosing) and not its intent; the row stays parked.**
1. **State contrast is direct for two readouts.** (a) Direct glutamate-uncaging response, NRG1-to-control ratio, normal against deprived PV cells: about 1 against about 11, P 4.6E-05 (Fig 3K, Mann-Whitney, cell level). (b) Evoked IPSC in layer 2/3 pyramidal cells, NRG1-to-control ratio: normal about 1 (6 cells), deprived about 6 (16 cells), PV-ErbB4 knockout about 1 (5 cells); normal against deprived P 6.1E-04, deprived against knockout P 0.003 (Fig 7C). For the synaptic input to PV cells (Fig 3J) the printed tests are within-state (deprived control against deprived NRG1 ***P 0.001; one n.s. P 0.2) and the text says bath NRG1 "does not significantly modulate" control PV cells (n = 13): that one is S-subgroup, with the direct contrast supplied by 3K and 7C instead. Table S2 arithmetic (printed means, mine): detected EPSC count normal 148, deprived 50 (ratio 2.96); with bath NRG1 normal 140 (0.95 of its control) and deprived 156 (3.12 of its control); amplitude per EPSC 6.14 against 5.3 (0.86); so the deficit is mostly EPSC number, and bath NRG1 adds about 3 percent to the normal-state count-times-amplitude index and about 4.1 times to the deprived one.
2. **PV-ErbB4 dependence is shown in the same state and, for IPSC, the same readout.** Deprived PV cells in PV-Cre;ErbB4 flox/flox: bath NRG1 no effect on excitatory input (n = 6 cells, P 0.9; Fig S7A to C); the ErbB blocker AG1478 abolishes it (n = 10 cells; Fig 6C, D, I); evoked IPSC response absent in the knockout (Fig 7C). Intracellular PKC-inhibitor peptide (n = 8) and botulinum toxin light chain (n = 7) also abolish it, and NMDA-receptor block does not (n = 5): a postsynaptic PKC- and exocytosis-dependent AMPA route in the recorded PV cell. Caveats from the authors' own supplement: Cre deletion does not start until PV expression begins at about two weeks, ErbB4 "is reduced but not eliminated" during the 4-day in vivo window, and knockout PV cells already have much weaker baseline input (non-deprived, 6 against 11 cells, P 0.02; Fig S7D, E), so the knockout is a developmental model and a low-input state at once. Null results rest on 5 to 6 cells.
3. **C-conf holds: low input and low ligand co-vary in every manipulation.** Monocular deprivation lowers PV-cell NRG1 immunostaining to 67.9 percent and phospho-ErbB4 to 49.5 percent of control (Fig 2G, H; phospho-ErbB4 from 3 to 4 mice per condition, P = 0.05 stated) while it lowers PV excitatory input; chemogenetic PV silencing for 24 h lowers NRG1 (P 0.003; 7 against 5 mice) and local input together; exogenous NRG1 restores ligand, phosphorylation and input together. No arm lowers input while keeping ligand and phospho-ErbB4 normal, or the reverse. The authors' own model assigns the cause to lost ligand. So "concentrated in the low-input state" and "concentrated in the low-ligand state" are one observation here. The knockout arm (low input, receptor absent, no response) shows the receptor is required; it does not separate the two.
4. **In vivo evidence has no control-state arm.** Phospho-ErbB4 and NRG1 immunostaining after subcutaneous NRG1 exist for normal, deprived, and deprived plus NRG1, not normal plus NRG1 (Fig 2G to I). The in vivo slice effect (1 h after 1 microgram per mouse: 5 cells; 48 h of 0.5 microgram three times daily: 10 cells) is in deprived animals only. The ocular-dominance experiment compares NRG1 (n = 6 mice) with saline (n = 7) under 4 days of deprivation (shift about 0.08 against 0.18, P 0.004; values from the page image) and knockout (n = 6) with flox littermates (n = 8; P 0.014) without NRG1; NRG1 was not given to knockouts in vivo, so dependence of the in vivo NRG1 effect on PV-ErbB4 is inferred from the slice work and from the knockout's own impaired plasticity.
5. **The effect overshoots the normal level.** In deprived cells bath NRG1 takes input to above the control mean (text: "restored to levels above that of controls"), and 1 h of in vivo NRG1 gives about 4 nA against about 1.1 nA in normal cells (bar heights from the page image of Fig 3J, approximate). A ceiling-limited restoration would not overshoot; the normal state's flat response (3 percent) therefore reads as a state difference, not a ceiling, but the cause (ligand deficit against input deficit) stays open (point 3).

## Deliverable for the decision (RP-038 / INV-15; brief 9.13)
**D2: add one labelled state descriptor, do not call it a moderator.** The brief's pair (phospho-ErbB4 in PV cells plus sIPSC) is sufficient to read an NRG1-pathway response, but Sun shows that the same manipulation reads as a large effect in one state and none in another, and that the state is defined by PV excitatory input and by NRG1/phospho-ErbB4 together. A Wwox-deficient tissue could have these two decoupled; Sun's manipulations never decoupled them. So reading baseline PV-identified excitatory input next to baseline phospho-ErbB4 would do what Sun's design could not: show, in the tissue of interest, whether input and ErbB4 signalling are low together, separately, or neither. Proposed one-clause text for IT (named delta; brief line 9.13 or its successor):
> Read the baseline excitatory input to PV-identified cells (frequency and amplitude of excitatory currents in PV-reporter cells) in the same cohort beside baseline phospho-ErbB4 and sIPSC, and report the three together. A null inhibitory response in a control-like baseline state is expected in the published juvenile model (Sun 2016: no bath-NRG1 effect on control PV cells) and is not by itself evidence of an absent lever; a response confined to a low-input state does not show that input, rather than ligand, is the moderator unless baseline phospho-ErbB4 is read alongside.
Notes that bear on feasibility, not asserted as design: (i) PV-reporter identification starts about two weeks after birth (authors' supplement), so for a neonatal window this readout may not be available and the developmental-timing concern stays; (ii) Sun's input measure is photostimulation-evoked total synaptic current, not spontaneous EPSC frequency, so a spontaneous-current readout is a different quantity and would need its own reading; (iii) no arm, no dose, no compound is proposed.
**Not recommended:** adding baseline input as a separate arm, a gate, or a reason to raise the row (D1 not met: no control-state arm in any intact-animal readout, and C-sep not met).

## Does the paper meet the row's adjacent-context trigger without the WWOX premise?
* **Letter (RP-038 revival condition: "an NRG1-modulator seizure or PV readout under neonatal or juvenile dosing"):** met for the PV-readout half. Recombinant NRG1 EGF-domain peptide, subcutaneous, juvenile (P27 to P31) wild-type mice, PV-specific readouts (excitatory input to PV cells, evoked inhibition, phospho-ErbB4 in PV cells) and a functional readout.
* **Intent:** not met. No seizure readout; no Wwox or epilepsy preparation; the dosing is minutes to four days in a plasticity paradigm (monocular deprivation) whose low-input state is induced, not a disease state; the sign premise (does Wwox loss lower ErbB4 signalling in PV cells?) is untouched; the developmental-timing conflict of `RESULT_RP038_SCI_20261121.md` (neonatal P2 to P10 dosing as a published schizophrenia-like model) is not resolved, because P27 to P31 is a later window than the neonatal one and the paper reports no after-effect, only acute readouts. Consequence: row status stays **parked**; the CONDITIONAL class is unchanged; no rank change. What the paper adds to the class is one conditional statement: in juvenile wild-type visual cortex the enhancing peptide acts on PV drive when PV drive is low and ligand signalling is down, and not when it is control-like. Whether Wwox-deficient PV cells are in such a state is the untested premise.
* **Counter-case (Gu et al. 2016, PMID 27707966):** named by the assignment, not pursued, not read.

## Rejecting observation (restated)
A significant NRG1 effect in the matched control state would have supported the generic rival; the text, Table S2 (140 against 148 EPSCs, amplitude 6.7 against 6.14) and Fig 3K, 7C show none at cell level. A knockout that still responded would have broken E-same; it does not (P 0.9, P 0.003 against the deprived group).

## DREAM (kept only because it changes which readouts are paired)
Upstream excitatory input to PV cells and downstream inhibition move together here with intrinsic PV excitability unchanged (Table S1), and the direct response changes about 11-fold: an input change without an intrinsic change. For a Wwox assay this means "sIPSC changed" cannot be assigned to PV drive, release probability or postsynaptic receptors without the baseline input and phospho-ErbB4 pair: that is the one connection kept (the third paired readout above).
