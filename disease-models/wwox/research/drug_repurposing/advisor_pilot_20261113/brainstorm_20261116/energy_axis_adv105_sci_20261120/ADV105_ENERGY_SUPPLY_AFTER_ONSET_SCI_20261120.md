# ADV-105: does correcting brain energy supply (or glial state) AFTER onset contribute to maintenance or functional recovery, and which agents have juvenile or paediatric exposure facts (SCI, 2026-11-20; hypothesis level; exploratory; no dose, schedule or recommendation for or against any use of any agent in any person; reference genotype class only; not medical advice)

Pre-registration: `PREREGISTRATION_ADV105_SCI_20261120.md` (committed before any external search). Method and stop rule are SCI's. The assignment text is ADV's message of 2026-11-20 (RP-033).

## Answer first
1. **After-onset evidence exists for energy-supply lesions, but it is lesion repair or a standard diet, not a symptomatic fuel arm tested against a comparator.** The clearest row is a Glut1-deficiency mouse study (PMID 28106060, abstract level): repletion of the transporter protein before symptoms prevents disease, **repletion in early-symptomatic mutants that had lived with low brain glucose still restores the microvasculature and ameliorates disease, repletion late in adulthood is devoid of benefit**. So a post-onset window can exist, and it can close. It is gene replacement in a transport lesion, so it says nothing yet about fuel given to a pup with a neuronal-protein lesion.
2. **For fuel itself (ketone-based diets, triheptanoin, dichloroacetate) the evidence found by these routes is uncontrolled or surrogate-level:** ketogenic diet in Glut1 deficiency is the standard first-line (consensus statement, abstract) and in pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) deficiency 129 patients in 40 mostly case reports plus two clinician surveys report improvement in seizure control, motor function and cognition with no comparator; a 2026 randomised crossover trial of dichloroacetate in 34 children with PDH deficiency lowered lactate by 20 percent but **missed its primary motor endpoint (P = 0.512)** and its longer-term benefit and survival rest on an open-label extension and a natural-history comparator. All of these started after onset (that is the only way they exist), so they are "after onset" by construction, but none separates "energy supply corrected" from "seizures reduced by another route".
3. **Glial-state manipulation after onset in a developmental epilepsy or hypomyelination model: not found by these routes** (one search, then the ADV-104 sources; the closest hits are acquired-epilepsy models and a neuronal serotonergic drug in a Dravet mouse). No glial row is promoted.
4. **Exposure (B):** no agent has a measured brain or CSF concentration in a pup. Juvenile facts exist only as (a) dichloroacetate: juvenile rats had less measurable drug in tissues than adults after repeated dosing, children clear it faster after repeated doses (abstract level), (b) blood ketone levels as practice targets in children with PDH deficiency and measured C5 ketone levels after triheptanoin in children aged 4 to 16, (c) natural ketosis of the suckling brain. "No sheet" is not "incompatible": it is a gap the null pup would have to fill by measuring.
5. **Coherence with the ADV-101 null-pup facts: not separable, with one clear contradiction.** The null's lower insulin, higher glucose uptake and glycolysis in null cells and higher serum lactate argue **against a Glut1-type transport failure** as the model (Glut1 deficiency is low CSF glucose with normal blood glucose and limited uptake) and are **compatible with a PDH-type or glycolysis-excess state** (lactate accumulation, the PDH-deficiency signature) but equally with seizures consuming glucose (neonatal flurothyl seizures in 4-day-old rats: glycolytic rate fivefold, lactate up, glucose down). No brain compartment measurement exists in the null.
6. **One paragraph (the question):** see "Should an energy readout or arm be explored".

## A2. Glial-state manipulation after onset in developmental epilepsy or hypomyelination models
Not found by these routes: one targeted search and the ADV-104 evidence (Rett microglia replacement not replicated; preterm-rabbit and cuprizone positives are injury-dependent myelination models; ganaxolone is an adult focal-lesion study). The closest new hit is fenfluramine in a Dravet mouse with a survival and neurodegeneration-marker endpoint (38018342, abstract; a serotonergic drug acting first on neurons, pre-symptomatic gliosis prevention was in a zebrafish model). The glial-compartment energy proposal (38767952, abstract) is a review argument that medium-chain triglycerides feed astrocytes and add to the ketogenic diet; it proposes prospective studies and reports no outcome. **No glial row is promoted and no after-onset claim is made for glia.**

## C. Coherence with the ADV-101 null-pup facts
Facts (held, ADV-101 increment 1): hypoglycaemia from the second week, lower insulin, higher glucose uptake and glycolysis in null cells, higher serum lactate; growth lag from day 4; the neuronal vector rescues glucose and survival together; the S-KO seizure onset is P9.
* **Against a Glut1-type transport failure:** that lesion has normoglycaemia with low CSF glucose (17718830, abstract); the null has low blood glucose with raised cellular uptake. A transporter-limited uptake would not raise uptake.
* **Compatible with a PDH-type or glycolysis-excess state:** raised lactate is the PDH-deficiency pattern (1999 case: infantile spasms with high blood and CSF lactate, both lowered with dichloroacetate and thiamine, 10567050, abstract), but **PDH activity was unchanged in one human knockout fibroblast line** (ADV-101 increment 2), so the direct support is absent.
* **Rival, equally fitting:** seizures cost energy and raise lactate (21136154); hypoglycaemia that begins in week two and growth lag from day 4 start before the P9 onset in the S-KO only if the germline null behaves like the S-KO, which is unproven. **Verdict: not separable; one reading (Glut1-type) contradicted.**

## Should an energy-supply readout or arm be explored in the null pups
**A readout: yes, and it is already half built.** v3.1 6.9 (glucose from P7) and 6.18 (satellite P5, P7, P9, P12) carry glucose, GFAP and Iba1; the evidence above says the variables that would **separate** a cause from a consequence are the ones no held text has for any Wwox animal: **blood lactate, a ketone level (beta-hydroxybutyrate) and, where feasible, a brain-compartment measure (CSF or tissue glucose and lactate)** at the same ages, in the S-KO as well as the null (the S-KO tells whether hypoglycaemia needs more than the neuronal lesion). Adding lactate and ketone to the same satellite blood draws needs no new arm; I propose it as an addition to 6.18 for ADV's decision, not as made.
**An arm: not supported yet, and a post-onset arm is the only form the question allows.** The only after-onset evidence is lesion repair (a window that closes with age) or a standard diet in a transport or PDH lesion, with no comparator; in the null pup the fuel is already abnormal in the opposite direction from Glut1 deficiency. If the readout shows glucose and lactate abnormal **before** the first seizure (S-KO P9) and normal brain-level fuel is excluded, then a **post-onset fuel-sufficiency arm** started in week two (the age of the documented hypoglycaemia) in the S-KO or the null is the first design with a question this note can pose, with survival and seizure count as the endpoints and blood glucose, lactate and ketone as the manipulation check; its risks and feasibility are the laboratory's to state.
**What would weaken the idea:** (i) glucose and lactate change only after seizures begin and normalise when seizures are suppressed by another route (consequence, 21136154); (ii) S-KO pups are normoglycaemic and normolactataemic while null pups are not (the axis is peripheral, not neuronal); (iii) blood glucose is low while CSF or tissue glucose is normal; (iv) a fuel arm that corrects blood values but not seizures or survival.

## Limits
Every row is abstract level (a read of the abstract, no full text, no receipt): none of the numbers above enters a model or a ranking. Twelve searches in one database, titles and abstracts only; "not found by these routes" is not absence, and the neonatal hypoglycaemia-seizure row has one search behind it. The glial question was answered by reuse and one search. The DCA phase III comparison is to a natural-history cohort, so its survival result is not a controlled effect. The mouse Glut1 post-onset result is for a transport lesion with a vascular component. The coherence verdict inherits ADV-101's cell-level (not brain) measurement of glucose uptake. No dose or schedule is given; the blood ketone range is a published practice target, not a recommendation. Reference genotype class only; not medical advice.
