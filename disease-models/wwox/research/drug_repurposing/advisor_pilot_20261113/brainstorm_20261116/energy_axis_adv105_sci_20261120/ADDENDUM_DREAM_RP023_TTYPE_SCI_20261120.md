## 1. Does the null's high lactate make RP-023 step 2 for Ldha and Ldhb more interesting?
**Not by itself; it makes step 3 testable.** Step 2 (are the genes readable in neurons) is passed and said nothing about whether the Wwox-null neuronal phenotype is metabolic. The chain breaks at step 3: no held neuronal metabolic readout in Wwox, and the model sign is two-sided (losing Ldh could lower lactate production and relieve a glycolysis excess, or could cut a fuel the neuron needs). The held lactate is **serum** lactate in the germline null plus a glycolytic shift in null cells; the neuronal transcript hint is inconsistent between the two held sets (S-line excitatory Ldha +0.36, astrocyte +0.73; neocortex set Ldha -0.13, Ldhb -0.35; one animal or pool per arm in one set, three against three in the other). So the high lactate does not raise the standing of an Ldh-type hit, and **the seizure-consequence rival is equally good** (neonatal seizures raise lactate and lower glucose; 21136154, abstract). What changes is that the 6.19 readout supplies the missing step-3 datum: **a brain-compartment lactate (terminal CSF or tissue) in the S-KO**, the neuronal-lesion line. Reading: lactate raised in the S-KO brain before the P9 seizures supports a neuronal metabolic state, and then an Ldh-type hit has a defined sign question to ask; lactate raised only after seizures, or only in the germline null, keeps the break at step 3 and leaves RP-023 where it is (status waiting, resume condition unchanged: a collaborator laboratory with MEF or neuronal screening capacity, or a neocortical single-nucleus table). **Consumer: the readout line (v3.1 6.19); RP-023's row is the second consumer** (suggest to ADV that its `resume_when` gain "or an S-KO brain-compartment lactate series from the satellite timecourse"; I do not edit the portfolio).

## 2. Does the T-type class interact with the energy readout?
**Yes, as a confounder in a direction that flatters the readout.** TTA-A2 in adult rodents lowers core body temperature and body weight gain and Cav3.1-null mice resist diet-induced weight gain (v3.1 6.15, abstract level; one agent, adults). A lower temperature lowers metabolic rate, so a T-type arm could lower lactate, change glucose and perhaps improve survival without correcting any energy defect, and in pups that already fail to gain weight it could add harm. Nothing is held for ethosuximide on temperature, weight or glucose in any pup. Rules that follow, as hypothesis-level readout rules for the pilot: (i) the satellite draws (glucose, lactate, beta-hydroxybutyrate) are taken in **every** arm including vehicle and the bridge-lead arm at the same ages; (ii) core temperature (added by 6.15) is reported beside the energy variables, and a change in the energy variables in a T-type arm is not read as correction of the axis unless temperature is within the vehicle range or adjusted for; (iii) a survival gain in a T-type arm with lower temperature and lower weight gain is flagged as possibly metabolic-rate-mediated. **Consumer: the bridge page and its pilot (v3.1 6.14 and 6.15), which would read these variables at once.** This is a design rule, not a finding about ethosuximide.
