## 4. The connection that the sweep leaves open (relation known in parts, new as a combination)
**Sources and observations (each in its own system and direction):** (a) glioblastoma line T98G, **gain** of WWOX, alamarBlue conversion higher, explained by DLD mRNA more than 3-fold up (Kosla 2014, ADV-218; the registry holds only a stub); (b) bladder line, **loss** of WWOX raises PrestoBlue conversion and the **gain arm is null** at every point (Kaluzinska 2021, ADV-218; stub); (c) four glioma lines, **gain** of WWOX lowers a resazurin readout in all four (`PAPER 405`); (d) human neural progenitors, **knockdown** lowers PrestoBlue about 32 percent at 270 min, raw RFU, no n (`PAPER 022`). **Proposed relation:** none; the relation is that one assay chemistry carries three signs, so a sentence "WWOX modulates mitochondrial activity" built on dye readouts would pool them. **Competing explanations:** dye conversion tracks cell number and proliferation, not respiration (a gain that slows growth and a loss that speeds it will move the dye in opposite directions with no change in per-cell respiration); a diaphorase or reductase transcript change (the authors' own explanation in (a)); medium, density and read time. **Discriminating measurement:** the same dye read-out normalised to cell number and a respiration read-out (oxygen consumption) in one WWOX-deficient neural line, plus a reductase-transcript check; if the dye follows cell number and not oxygen consumption, none of the four bears on `NR-03`. **Consumer to update:** `NR-03` (complex I and respiration) already guards compound scoring; the stubs become records at the next batch and each should carry its system and direction (gain or loss) in its first line. This is a **new combination in the repository**, not a novelty claim against the literature, not a claim, and it changes no need, card or ranking.
