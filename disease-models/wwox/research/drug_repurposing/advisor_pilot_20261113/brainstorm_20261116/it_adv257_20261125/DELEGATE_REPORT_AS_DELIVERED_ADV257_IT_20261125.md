SECTION 3: INCLUDED and NEAR_MISS rows with quotes

A. 35433113 (SLC26A4 A-2G; Feng et al., Mol Ther Nucleic Acids 2022, DOI 10.1016/j.omtn.2022.03.015). Label INCLUDED. FULL TEXT read (PMC8987850).
- What was destroyed: "The A-2G mutation, which is located at the 3′ splice site of intron 7, disrupts the highly conserved dinucleotide AG and causes predominant skipping of exon 8."
- What rescued, and readout: "After ASO treatment, the percentage of exon 8 inclusion was markedly increased from 31% (untreated) to 67% and from 21.5% (untreated) to 61.5% for the two patients’ cells, respectively." On transcript identity: "We have validated the two transcripts by sequencing (Figure 3B)." The ASO is MOE/phosphorothioate, cholesterol-conjugated for PBMC uptake.
- The authors' own claim: "We noted that none of those previously developed therapeutic splice-switching ASOs target mis-splicing caused by disruption of canonical splice sites. Our data prove that these mis-splicing events are still rescuable."
- Caveats, all from the text:
  - The ASO does not target the acceptor. It occludes an hnRNP A1/A2 silencer (ISS-F1) at intron 8 positions 10-32, so the effect is on exon definition.
  - The untreated mutant already gives a residual full-length (FL) transcript (about 20-31% FL in patient cells). The ASO raises that existing product; the authors call the mutated site a non-canonical splice site.
  - Minigene and mouse show a third product, intron 7 retention (IR7), which the authors say is absent in patient cells. The ASO also raised IR7 in the minigene.
  - Humanised mouse: FL went from 13% to 28% in liver, with no significant change in inner ear or kidney.
  - I could not read Figure 3B (Europe PMC image URL returned 403). I have not confirmed that the ASO-induced FL junction sits exactly at the original acceptor. Criterion 4 (no 1-nt shift or new site) rests on the authors' sequencing statement and on FL being the same band as in wild type. Treat as INCLUDED but with this check outstanding.
