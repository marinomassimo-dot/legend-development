## 2. Question
Is there, in the literature, a compound class or biologic that modulates ErbB4/NRG1 signalling, with brain exposure shown or stated, and a seizure, seizure-threshold, PV-interneuron or inhibitory-network readout in a developmental (neonatal or juvenile) or genetic-epilepsy model, and in which direction does it act?

## 3. The sign is fixed first (the direction of any handle depends on a sign nobody has measured in neurons)
* **Primary reading (H-ADV-1):** WWOX loss weakens ErbB4 signalling in PV interneurons; the handle class that would counter it is **enhancement** (NRG1-pathway agonism or positive modulation).
* **Counter-direction (R3, provisional):** if WWOX loss releases more ErbB4 intracellular domain, the handle class is **reduction** (ErbB kinase inhibition, secretase modulation).
* The two are evaluated separately; a handle is credited **only for the direction in which its primary shows an effect**, and the sign mismatch with the working hypothesis is stated in every row. No handle is credited for "modulates ErbB4" without a stated direction and a stated readout.

## 4. The discriminating observation and the outcome rules (written now)
**Discriminating observation:** a primary in which an ErbB4-signalling modulator (compound, peptide or biologic) is given to a living neonatal or juvenile animal or a genetic epilepsy or interneuron-deficit model, with route and a brain-exposure statement (measured or inferred, labelled which), and a **seizure, seizure-threshold, spike-wave, PV-cell or inhibitory-transmission readout**, with n, statistic, age and denominator.
| Reading | Sentence this strand will carry |
|---|---|
| At least one such primary shows benefit in the direction the sign needs, in a neonatal, juvenile or genetic model, route and exposure stated | A PV-axis pathway handle **enters** the comparison as a conditional class with exposure facts, harms (ErbB signalling is pleiotropic; oncogenic and cardiac liabilities named) and the sign premise. |
