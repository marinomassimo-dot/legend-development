## Answer first
1. **The handle question has a first answer: CONDITIONAL, not ENTERS, not ABSENT.** One class exists with a stated in vivo effect in the needed direction: **enhancing NRG1-ErbB4 signalling with the NRG1 EGF-domain peptide reduces seizures in adult rodent models when given into the ventricle**, and every primary that reduces ErbB4 signalling in PV cells or neutralises NRG1 worsens them. Nothing in the primaries gives a seizure or PV readout under neonatal or juvenile dosing, and the one neonatal regimen with brain exposure (subcutaneous peptide, with ErbB4 activation shown in neocortex) has a documented developmental price. No systemic, brain-penetrant, drug-like handle with a seizure or PV readout was found.
2. **The sign of the working hypothesis is consistent with every seizure primary read** (enhancement helps, reduction worsens), **but the sign premise itself is untested for WWOX**: the only WWOX datum is an Erbb4 transcript change of -13 to -16 percent in one pool per arm (H-ADV-1), and R3 left the sign of WWOX's effect on ErbB4 in neurons open. A handle credited by a PV-ErbB4 model is therefore a handle for a **premise**, not for a measured WWOX deficit.
3. **A developmental-timing conflict blocks any use of the class in the pup window as it stands**: the same neonatal peptide regimen (given over postnatal days 2 to 10) that raises excitatory drive onto PV interneurons and inhibitory currents in juvenile cortex (Abe 2011, abstract level) is the published protocol of a schizophrenia-modelling paradigm, and gives adult-stage disinhibition of nigral dopamine neurons (Namba 2016, full text). Direction-in-adult and harm-in-development are both on record.
4. **My own previous sentence needs correction:** the producer reported "no neonatal systemic study exists"; the blind verifier found Abe 2011. The absence claim is withdrawn.

## Absences, with the routes they rest on
* **No seizure readout under neonatal or juvenile NRG1/ErbB4 modulation:** none found in four query families by the producer (Europe PMC and PubMed) and the verifier's three routes (PubMed, Europe PMC abstract level, a 200-record open-access full-text sample); **these are routes over one bibliographic corpus, not independent corpora** (the verifier measured zero Europe PMC records absent from PubMed); a full-text-only mention beyond rank 200 or a paper using NRG1 as a tool without naming it in the abstract would be missed. "None found", not "none exists".
* **No systemic, brain-penetrant agonist with a positive seizure outcome:** none found (same limits); the systemic rows that exist are a null seizure result with measured exposure (rat), a neonatal ex vivo PV and IPSC result (mouse), and a ferroptosis-attributed benefit for an ErbB-kinase inhibitor.
* **No ErbB kinase inhibitor with ErbB4 identified as the seizure-relevant target and a benefit:** none found.
* **No in vivo seizure aggravation by an administered NRG1:** none found; the aggravating signals are genetic overexpression in slices (an epileptiform tendency, 2012 abstract), perinatal exposure with adult disinhibition (above), and receptor-level GABA-A current reduction (abstracts).

## What this changes in the comparison, and what it does not
* **PV-axis and interneuron-circuit rows of the lever tables:** a new **conditional class, "NRG1 EGF-domain peptide (enhancing)"**, written with its exposure facts (ventricle in adults, no measured brain level in any seizure primary; subcutaneous neonatal peptide with ErbB4 activation reported in neocortex, abstract level), its harm (a perinatal regimen on record as a schizophrenia-model and as a cause of adult disinhibition in a non-cortical system; ErbB signalling pleiotropy: tumour and cardiac liabilities as background, not read here) and the **sign premise** (WWOX loss weakens PV-ErbB4 signalling: unmeasured). It is not a drug-repurposing candidate (no approved agonist; the labels in IT arm B were tool peptide and a pan-ErbB inhibitor class, background, and the inhibitor class points the wrong way).
* **Bridge group:** an enhancer class that no antiseizure agent represents; entry is conditional and carries the timing conflict.
* **The cheapest bridging observation is unchanged and is now better motivated:** an NRG1 response of the inhibitory output in Wwox-deficient versus control interneurons (IT arm B `H-C5`: the NRG1-induced change in sIPSC frequency and amplitude in knockout versus control slices, with a signalling readout such as phospho-ErbB4). It is the one observation that tests the sign premise on which every credit above depends. Nothing is prescribed; the experiment is carried as a row.
* **Not changed:** the reference-genotype comparison, the WW1-ERBB4 binding open-question row, R3's provisional drop of the release sign, the class-E rows.

## Limits (not hidden)
Tan 2011, Li 2011 and Liang 2020 are abstract-level: their ages, dose, n and statistics are not known to me, and the two Nature Neuroscience papers carry the reduction-direction arm. Abe 2011 is abstract-level. No supplement of any primary was read. The verifier's p-value reconciliations are its own computations from printed F values, not printed numbers. The Mitchell 2013 and 2004 receptor-level abstracts and the 2012 overexpression abstract were read at abstract level only. A Racine score in five-minute bins, a kindling latency and a c-Fos ratio are different kinds of readout; none is a survival or neurodevelopmental outcome. No dose is stated anywhere in this note. The agent-class searches did not include small-molecule ErbB4 positive modulators beyond the generic terms used.
