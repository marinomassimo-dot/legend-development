THINGS THAT MAKE A NUMBER LESS CERTAIN
1. Source 2 Fig 5 is a 150 ppi raster. The C-panel lines are 1 px wide, so (d) has sub-pixel error. The S-KO line is mostly hidden under the dots, so its 0.5 and 1.0 values rest on a slope fixed from the exposed right-hand end only. The S-Control line is read directly through the dots.
2. In Source 1 Fig 4B the two fitted lines overlap and cross near 0.7, and dots hide parts of both. The values at 0.3 and 0.8 sit near the ends of the drawn lines, and the line end points are only approximately located.
3. Axis labels: Source 1 Fig 2B reads "mm/s" as printed. At 0.5 this is an implausibly slow conduction velocity for a myelinated tract, so treat the printed unit as a possible labelling error, but it is the unit as printed. The Fig 4 scatter x-axis has no unit printed. The Source 2 count panels have no area unit, only "per FOV".
4. Source 1 Fig 4 truncated y-axes (g-ratio bars 0.70-0.84 and 0.60-0.74; counts 150-350) mean bar tops are measured against tick pixels, not bar baselines.
5. Source 1 Fig 4 layout note above: the legend assigns the g-ratio bars to B, but on the page they sit in the A column.
6. The Source 2 caption names no summary statistic for the D(ii) and B middle lines, and the legend SEM statement does not match the visible error-bar span in B.
7. All values are one reader's pixel reads, with no second independent read.
