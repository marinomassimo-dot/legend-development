## Answer first
**Disposition (a): CLOSE the causal transfer as untested.** The paper's myelin measurements are cross-sectional (one age, P21, males), come from a broad Nestin-lineage model, rest on three animals per genotype, and the endpoints that would separate impaired OPC maturation from loss of the oligodendroglial lineage from downstream injury move in different regions. All three conditions of test T-a hold (below). There is no restricted-cell comparison, and the same animals carry striatal atrophy and gliosis at P21.

**ONE source-linked readout constraint that INV-06 and ADV-181's rule do not already state:** express the OPC marker (PDGFRA+) and the lineage marker (SOX10+) **nested within the oligodendroglial lineage**, i.e. report SOX10+ cells per area and PDGFRA+ as a fraction of SOX10+ in the same sections of the same animals, region by region; a fraction of all nuclei (DAPI+) in single-label sections cannot tell loss of the lineage from arrest of maturation. Locator: Fig 5b to 5e (SOX10+ and PDGFRA+ are shown as separate single-label stainings, each as % of DAPI+). INV-06's present text names marker level beside sheath structure with axon length as denominator, and ADV-181's rule names per-cell and per-field denominators for MBP, a retained-material control and an independent new-sheath measure; neither names a lineage-nested denominator or region-matched reading of lineage fractions.

## Test T-a (all three hold)
1. **Cross-sectional, one age and sex.** Fig 5 carries the badge "male, P21" in the figure itself (the legend does not state age); the model's males die by P60 (Results, Fig 1d text: "none surviving beyond P60") so no later stage exists, and the paper has no time series of the lineage fractions. Locator: Fig 5 badge; Results "Male XDP mice display severe motor impairments".
2. **Endpoints in different regions.** Fig 5d: SOX10+ (% of DAPI+) lower only in the corpus callosum (p = 0.0038), not significant in cortex or striatum. Fig 5e: PDGFRA+ (% of DAPI+) higher only in the striatum (p = 0.0211), not significant in cortex or corpus callosum. Fig 5g: Luxol fast blue positive area lower only in the corpus callosum (p = 0.0104), not significant in the striatum. So the corpus callosum shows fewer lineage cells with an unchanged OPC share and less myelin lipid (a loss pattern), while the striatum shows more OPCs with unchanged lineage share and unchanged lipid (an accumulation pattern, no maturation readout). Read as images, values approximate: SOX10+ about 50% against about 15% in the corpus callosum; PDGFRA+ about 3.5% against about 5% in the striatum; LFB+ area about 95% against about 10% in the corpus callosum. The ultrastructure panels (Fig 5h, i) are qualitative: no counts, no g-ratio (no such term in the text), and Fig 5h shows only XDP females and XDP males, not the Cre-only controls; the text compares males with females (the females are heterozygous mosaics).
3. **n and model.** Fig 5d, e, g: "n = 3 mice/genotype"; two-way repeated-measures ANOVA with Šídák's test for 5d, e. The model is NesCre driving the humanised XDP allele in "Nestin+ progenitor and their daughter cells", "nearly all CNS cells" (Results, model paragraph; abstract), so neurons, astrocytes and the oligodendroglial lineage all carry the allele.

## Does the paper distinguish impaired maturation, loss of lineage and injury?
No. The only same-region pair that bears on maturation is the corpus callosum (SOX10+ share down, PDGFRA+ share unchanged, lipid down), which fits loss of mature cells or of the lineage but is not tested against arrest because PDGFRA+ is not measured as a fraction of SOX10+. The striatal increase of PDGFRA+ is read by the authors as "expansion or accumulation of OPCs in response to injury or myelin disruption" (Discussion), and the authors state that whether progression is "driven by the degeneration of existing mature OLs, impaired differentiation of OPCs into OLs, or a combination of both remains to be explored" and that "future studies will define whether OL dysfunction is a cause or consequence of neurodegeneration". The paper does not claim to distinguish them.

## Restricted-cell comparison (question 3)
None. No lineage-restricted deletion, co-culture, graft or conditioned-medium experiment. The only other comparison is XDP females (X-inactivation mosaics) against males, a sex comparison within the genotype. Injury is measured in the same animals at the same age: "pronounced striatal atrophy accompanied by increased markers of glial reactivity and neuroinflammation particularly within white matter regions" at P21 (Results, immunohistochemistry section; Fig 3), that is, injury markers sit in the same white matter where the callosal SOX10+ share falls, and bulk RNA of cortex and striatum at P21 shows mature oligodendrocyte transcripts down with OPC transcripts (Cspg4, Pdgfra) unchanged. Nothing supports an intrinsic oligodendroglial deficit over injury or a neuron-dependent effect.

## Other points, for the record
* **Panel against legend:** the Fig 5f legend says Luxol fast blue staining is decreased "in the corpus callosum and striatum"; panel 5g shows the striatal difference not significant (large spread among three animals).
* **Human tissue (Fig 6 legend):** n = 3 individuals per group; MBP integrated density in the medial frontal cortex near the callosal sulcus lower (p = 0.0008), while the SOX10+ share is not significantly different (p = 0.0722); other regions not significant. Myelin loss without a significant lineage-fraction change in the human region, in three individuals, is the one comparison where the myelin and lineage readouts diverge.
* **DREAM:** the corpus callosum and striatum give opposite lineage readings from the same animals at the same age; that is the same shape as the earlier lesson that a stock at one time is not a trajectory (ADV-179), applied to region: one region's fraction cannot stand in for another's. Association moderate for the shape, none for a shared mechanism. No WWOX row is touched.
