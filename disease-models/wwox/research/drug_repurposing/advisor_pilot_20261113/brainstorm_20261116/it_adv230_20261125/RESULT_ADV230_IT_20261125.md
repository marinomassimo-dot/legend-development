## 1. Answer in six lines
1. **The pattern "the improved individual is younger" does not survive when age at the start of memantine replaces the age printed in Table 1.** The Table 1 age column is the age at the time of the table (individual 27 is listed at 4 years but started at 14 months; of 24 individuals with a stated start age none is within a year of the table age, and for two, 25 and 34, the start age is **higher** than the table age, which cannot be right for an age at the time of the table: the supplement's age cells are unreliable for those two), so ADV-225's reading of that column was an age-at-table pattern, not an age-at-start one (prediction P1 confirmed).
2. **Of the four same-variant sets, the frozen rule is not met.** Direction by set, age at start: Met641Ile (27 against 10) **older improved** (14 against 7 months); Met706Val (14 against 13) **younger improved** (8 years 1 month against 15 years); Val667Ile (33 and 34 against 32) **mixed** (improved at 2 years 6 months and at 15 years 9 months, not improved at 6 years 6 months, and the 15 years 9 months value cannot be reconciled with the supplement's own birth-year cell); Gly611Val (21 against 3) **not testable** (both improved, start age of 21 not given). One set clean in the predicted direction, one against, one mixed, one not testable. The rule asked for three clean ones.
3. **What does separate the pairs is mostly exposure and ascertainment, not age.** Met641Ile: 14 months of treatment (a published case report, three other antiseizure drugs) against 4 days (registry, none). Val667Ile: 33 is a 5-day inpatient seizure count, 32 an 18-month course on roughly 1.7 to 2.6 times the dose of 33; the original authors themselves offer age **or** natural history as the explanation and test neither (section 4).
4. **Disposition: (a) for the age question** (held-source answer, negative); **(b) for the columns that do not exist** (section 5: outcome definition and rater, exposure measurement, baseline seizure rate, duration for two individuals, start age for five of nineteen gain-of-function individuals); **(c) does not apply**, because age is not the only separator on offer: no separator is supported by four pairs.
5. **Post hoc and not in the PREREG (labelled):** across the 14 gain-of-function individuals with a stated start age, the improved ones (n = 10) have a median start at 62 months, the not-improved ones (n = 4) at 129 months; exact permutation p = 0.22; with the two inconsistent individuals (25 and 34) removed the medians are 61 and 78 months (n = 9 and 3, p = 0.38). Ranges overlap in both versions, and the **youngest start of all (7 months) is a not-improved individual treated for 4 days**.
6. **Surprise (one line):** the cohort's "non-responder" group is not a group of non-responders: 3 of its 5 members had seizures **worsen** and two of those stopped treatment after 4 days and 3 months, so "does not respond" and "is harmed or stops early" are pooled into one comparator for every age or variant contrast.

## 4. Confound by set (frozen list) and the primaries' own statements
* **In vitro data cannot separate the members of a set.** Supplement S1 (all 29 variant rows read) gives one functional row per variant and names the individuals together ("#10, #27", "#13, #14", "#3, #21", "#32, #33, #34"), so any in vitro property, memantine sensitivity included, is identical within each set by construction; whatever separates responders within a set is clinical, exposure-related or an ascertainment artefact.
* **Met641Ile (27 against 10).** Confounds 2 and 6 (case report against registry), 4 (14 months against 4 days) and 5 (three co-drugs against none) all present: **the contrast is explained by ascertainment and exposure**; the age direction is opposite to the pattern in any case (the improved individual started later). The paper's own text makes the same point: "the treatment duration varied greatly".
* **Met706Val (14 against 13).** Both registry, same dose, same co-drug; improved younger by about 7 years. Not explained by confounds 2, 3 or 6; confound 4 cannot be assessed (no duration for 13); the rater is not named for either. This is the only clean set in the predicted direction, and it is one pair.
* **Val667Ile (33, 34 against 32).** The only same-source contrast (33 and 32, one article) has the improved individual younger (30 against 78 months), but the dose differs by a factor of 1.7 to 2.6, the observation window differs (5 days against 18 months) and the article names two explanations without testing either: "the timing of treatment (proband 2 was treated at a younger age than proband 1) or just ... the natural history of the disease (proband 1 had good initial developmental and seizure response on levetiracetam but deteriorated over time)". The registry individual 34 improved at the oldest stated start but that start age cannot be trusted. **Age is one of two untested explanations for one pair.**
* **Gly611Val (21 against 3).** Both improved (one "mild", one "initial, then plateau"): not a responder contrast. The paper's "approximately the same age (about 8 years)" for the pair is the age at last assessment for 21 (8 years; start n/a), not an age at start.

## 5. Columns that do not exist in any held or open source (disposition b, named)
* **Outcome definition and rater.** Response was "subjectively assessed through the clinician's personal evaluation and feedback from parents" in four categories; no scale, no blinding, no pre-fixed criterion in any of the papers read. This blocks every set.
* **Baseline seizure rate and a measurement window** for most individuals (the one measured series is the 5-day inpatient count of 33).
* **Exposure measurement**: no serum or CSF level in any held source; the Val667Ile article itself reasons that the low CSF level of memantine and the reduced potency may prevent receptor occupancy.
* **Duration** for 13, 33 (stated "none") and 34; **age at start** for 21 and, among the 19 gain-of-function individuals, for 5 (11, 22, 24, 21, 6).
* **Concurrent-drug changes during memantine** are stated for some and "n/a" for others (21 and several others).
* **Not retrieved:** the Platzer 2017 primary behind individual 21 (28377535) and the Kyriakopoulos 2018 primary behind individual 25 (30151416); named, not bypassed.

## 6. Exploratory, not in the PREREG
Gain-of-function individuals with a stated start age (14 of 19): improved group (cohort groups 1 and 2) at 14, 17, 18, 30, 61, 63, 96, 97, 108 and 189 months; not-improved group (group 3) at 7, 78, 180 and 228 months. Median 62 against 129 months; exact two-sided permutation p = 0.22 (all 1001 allocations of 4 of 14 enumerated). Without individuals 25 (start age 19 years against a table age of 7) and 34 (15 years 9 months against 11): 61 against 78 months, p = 0.38 (all 220 allocations of 3 of 12). The comparison pools "no effect" (13, 6) with "worsened" (10, 25, 32) and with two courses of 4 days and 3 months. It is not a separator test; it is the reason the question is not closed.
