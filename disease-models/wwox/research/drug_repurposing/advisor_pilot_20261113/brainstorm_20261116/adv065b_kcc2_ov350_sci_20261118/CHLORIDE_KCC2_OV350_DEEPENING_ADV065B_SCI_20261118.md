## Answer first
1. **Limit 2 of ADV-065 is closed, and it weakens the statement a great deal.** The Kasahara 2019 open-access full text was obtained by NCBI E-utilities (one plain request; the PMC article page stays behind a challenge page and was not retried). Read in full (FT, text, figure captions and the two tables): at P11 the high-dose modulators did **not** raise the incidence of clonic seizures (5 of 5 in saline and with each); they shortened latency, lowered the threshold temperature (tendency) and raised LFP epileptic events; bumetanide co-treatment suppressed the clonic incidence and the LFP amplitude and event count but **not the latency**, and bumetanide alone already lowered the incidence (2 of 4). **Tables 1 and 2 (found by the blind verifier, confirmed by me) add what the abstract and Discussion do not mention: all 5 mice given high-dose diazepam died, at P11 and at P14, with and without bumetanide; the P11 arms in which bumetanide "suppressed" clonic seizures (0 of 5 with diazepam, 1 of 5 with pentobarbital) had average body temperatures of 37.7 degrees against 39.6 in saline; at P14 the 0 of 5 clonic seizures with diazepam coincides with 5 of 5 deaths.** Groups were 4 to 5 mice and the authors keep sedation with impaired heat dissipation open. The paper therefore does **not** isolate a chloride mechanism, and the "P11 to P14 window" is a pharmacological inference with a lethality and temperature confound, not an E_GABA measurement. ADV-065's "undone by bumetanide" is withdrawn as evidence; dated lines follow.
2. **KCC2 side, one sentence per agent (section 3):** OV350 has one open full text (Arshad 2026, Ovid-funded: Cdkl5 knockout pups, behavioural spasm scoring, n 5 versus 8, effect on days 11 to 13 only, E_GABA measured only in untreated slices from day 14 on, no sedation, weight, temperature or survival measure, no wild-type OV350 group) and one primary pharmacology paper (Jarvis 2023: adult male mice, neurons already hyperpolarising); CLP290 has a neonatal mouse ischaemic-seizure paper (benefit at P7, none added at P10, none or worse without the S940 site); CLP257's mechanism is contested in two full texts; no KCC2 agent has human seizure data.
3. **"More promising" per endpoint (section 5):** the KCC2 branch is more promising than an NKCC1 blocker on **one** endpoint, **development** (early treatment, adult outcomes, in a genetic DEE model), on evidence level only, hypothesis level; it is not more promising on acute rescue, provoked seizures, chronic control or harms; against the other candidates of the by-endpoint table it is **not rankable** (their development data were not searched here, and the "only" in ADV-065 is withdrawn).
4. **The chloride dependence is stated for both branches (section 4):** an NKCC1 blocker can act only if NKCC1 sets a depolarising E_GABA; a KCC2 activator only if KCC2 is expressed, rate-limiting and not saturated. Both are gated by the same unmeasured fact in the Wwox preparation. In the one DEE model with data (Cdkl5), the reduced KCC2 was sampled at p14 and p21, the spasm window is p10 to p14, and the treatment effect sits on p11 to p13, before the sampled deficit.
5. **Conditional experimental hypothesis (section 6):** perforated-patch E_GABA first, in S-KO against wild type, at a younger window (P9 to P12) and the pilot window (P13 to P17), sex recorded, bath **without** bumetanide as the primary condition (the Cdkl5 assay had bumetanide in the bath and so could not see an NKCC1 contribution), plus a KCC2-blocker-sensitive component; a KCC2-activator arm only if the null is depolarising and the KCC2 component is reduced. Access to OV350 is not public (no registry, ChEMBL or PubChem record by that name): an operator-level matter, not an agent action.
6. **Exposure (limit 3):** "five papers against two" is a premise-dependent reading, not a tally: the two camps measure different things against different references (section 7). A pup datum now exists (P10 male rat, brain to serum ratio 0.007 to 0.009, FT); a pup free fraction was not found by two routes.
7. **Reasoned stop for the literature loop on the KCC2 branch.** The next questions are measurements (E_GABA and KCC2 in a Wwox preparation) and one question to the operator-level channel (is OV350 obtainable); named triggers to reopen are in section 8.

## 4. Arshad 2026 read as closely as the open sources allow (FT; both sexes, litters randomised, Ovid-funded)

## 6. The chloride-gradient dependence, stated for both branches, and a conditional experimental hypothesis
**DATO and INFERENZA.** Both branches work through the same unmeasured state. An NKCC1 blocker lowers influx, so it can lower [Cl-]i only where NKCC1 sets it; a KCC2 activator raises extrusion, so it can lower [Cl-]i only where KCC2 is present, rate-limiting and below its ceiling. Evidence that the second condition matters: CLP290 benefit tracks the degree of KCC2 hypofunction (P7 yes, P10 no; none or worse without S940; Sullivan), the neonatal capacity of KCC2 for enhancement is described as present and "saturated" in adults (Doi), and OV350 was shown only in neurons already hyperpolarising (Jarvis). Evidence that the first condition matters: an NKCC1 effect in the pup literature is age-bound (ADV-065). **A KCC2 activator is not a free alternative to an NKCC1 blocker:** where the gradient is already hyperpolarising the effect is expected small or absent, and where a depolarising E_GABA comes from NKCC1, a KCC2 activator and an NKCC1 blocker converge on the same variable.

**Conditional experimental hypothesis (IPOTESI; each step conditional on the one before; the slice package already carries the E_GABA readout).**
| Step | Measure | If the null differs from wild type (depolarised) | If the null equals wild type | What it separates |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 (first, cheap) | perforated-patch E_GABA in S-KO and wild-type neocortical neurons at **P9 to P12 and P13 to P17**, sex recorded (Cleary 2013 reports male and female GABA-polarity reversal at different ages in rat), as a **fraction of cells** as well as a mean (the Cdkl5 knockout showed a subset, n 4, and a silent group); bath **without** bumetanide as the primary condition, plus a bumetanide-sensitive component, plus a VU0463271-sensitive component | proceed to step 1 | the GABAergic strand is closed for NKCC1 and KCC2 agents in this model at these ages; vigabatrin and GABA-A modulators lose the chloride rival | gradient present or absent, and whether NKCC1 or KCC2 carries it |

## 8. Rivals kept; named triggers; next step
**Rivals kept:** sedation (Kasahara authors; OV350 has no sedation measure; CLP290 prolongs midazolam sedation at P7); off-target action (CLP257 at GABA-A; bumepamine reproduces CNS effects without NKCC1 inhibition per its authors; no OV350 selectivity table in the text read); exposure (adult-only OV350 levels; bumetanide premises); sex and age effects (both sexes unsplit in the OV350 pup data; polarity reversal timing differs by sex in rat); company funding and internal inconsistencies in the Arshad paper; small groups and unadjusted daily tests; slice against in vivo switch ages.
**Reasoned stop, with named triggers to reopen:** (1) a measured E_GABA or KCC2 value in any Wwox preparation; (2) a published OV350 pup EEG or sedation control, or an independent replication of the p11 to p13 effect; (3) Ovid naming an epilepsy, CDKL5 or DEE indication for a KCC2 agent (the 2022 press release behind the "epilepsy franchise" link slug is not read); (4) a pup free brain fraction of bumetanide; (5) the full Gagnon reply. Until one fires, nothing in the literature changes the next step, which is step 0 of section 6.
**One question outside the agent loop:** is OV350 obtainable for a collaborating laboratory, and on what terms (operator to the sponsor; this note makes none).
