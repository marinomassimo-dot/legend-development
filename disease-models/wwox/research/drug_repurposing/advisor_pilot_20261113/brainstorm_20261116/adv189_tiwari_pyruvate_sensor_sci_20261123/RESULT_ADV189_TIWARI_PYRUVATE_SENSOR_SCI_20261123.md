## Answer first
**NARROW to a methods note with ONE optional conditional clause for WO-07 (design logic; weak).** The paper validates the matrix pyruvate sensor for the ENTRY side only: complete loss of the carrier subunit abolishes the response, and graded partial changes are resolvable in HEK cells. It contains no consumption-side or PDH-side perturbation, no sensor kinetics (the metric is a peak at a 10 mM external pyruvate step), no matrix pH control for the sensor, and it never puts the sensor and an ATP or release readout under the same perturbation in the same cultures. So the paper does not show that the sensor separates entry from PDH regulation; that separation rests on my stock-versus-rate logic, which the paper is consistent with but does not test. In a future WWOX experiment the sensor would add one thing that respirometry, ATP and a lysate PDH assay leave ambiguous: a measure of the matrix stock in intact cells, which does not depend on a phosphorylation state surviving homogenisation. Pre-registered exit: reading A.

## Checks asked for, answered
1. **Sensor response against transport RATE.** The reported metric is the peak at a plateau reached under a 10 mM step (chosen "on the basis of the affinity and dynamic range of the parental sensor", not a physiological level); no initial rate, no time constant. The plateau is a stock (entry minus consumption, plus any efflux, at the sensor's range). The control trace in Fig 2F rises over about 100 s to a plateau and falls on wash-out. So the paper's metric cannot, by itself, give a rate, and a plateau cannot tell a smaller entry rate from a larger consumption.
2. **pH and dynamic range.** The Methods apply a pH correction only to Syn-ATP (with cyto-pHluorin); no pH, calibration or saturation control is described for the matrix sensor. Membrane potential (TMRM) was checked in HEK for MPC1 KD and Sirt3 KD (unchanged; 318 to 388 cells) and not in neurons. Pyruvate entry is proton-coupled, so matrix pH and potential bear on the sensor and the carrier. Dynamic range: the control neuron response has a wide spread, so a moderate change would need larger n; complete loss is easy to see; increases were never tested, so a ceiling on the high side is unknown.
3. **Independent unit.** Neurons: 3 cultures, 12 to 21 neurons; HEK: many cultures but the tested unit is FOV; release data are synapses and events nested in 3 cultures and 9 to 16 coverslips. The knockdown check is mpc1 mRNA in cortical cultures (3 cultures, 0.08 of control, Fig S2A) while the sensor and release were in hippocampal neurons; HEK mRNA 0.43 of control gives a fully abolished response.
4. **Pairing of uptake and oxidation or ATP.** No pairing: the sensor is read under shRNA knockdown (Fig 2F, G) and the ATP under UK5099 (Fig 2I, J), in different cells and cultures; the sensor is never run under UK5099 and the ATP never under MPC1 KD; the schematic (Fig 2H) shows both reporters in one terminal but no co-imaged data are given. Release was measured under UK5099, Zaprinast and MPC1 KD with rescue, never with the sensor in the same neurons.
5. **What can be combined from Fig 2 E-G and I-J.** Only a qualitative statement: an entry perturbation lowers matrix pyruvate accumulation (genetic, axons) and lowers presynaptic ATP in lactate/pyruvate (pharmacological). Both are what an entry limitation predicts; neither distinguishes it from a PDH limitation, because no PDH-side perturbation is in the paper to show what the sensor and ATP do then.
6. **A substrate control that does not separate as stated.** The text says UK5099 did not affect presynaptic ATP in glucose (Fig S2B), but that panel is n = 10 paired neurons by Wilcoxon, not significant, with the mean ratio falling from 2.3 to 1.24 (about -46%), against 2.39 to 1.0 (about -58%, p < 0.05) in lactate/pyruvate (Fig S2C). Same direction and similar size; no condition-by-drug test. So "oxidative ATP depends on the carrier only without glucose" rests on significance in one condition and not the other.
7. **Versus the simplest SCI44 pair.** Respirometry under UK5099 (L09, rat E18 cortical neurons, maximal pyruvate oxidation cut by over 50%, n = 4, partial read) and ATP both fall when entry is blocked and would fall equally if PDH were restrained: they give a low oxidation without saying which step. The lysate PDH assay (INV-07 row 1) addresses the PDH side but, as the audit noted, a phosphorylation state lost on homogenisation gives a normal lysate result. A matrix stock in intact cells would address that gap in principle.

## Interpretation (mine, separated from the observations)
* **Logic:** matrix pyruvate = entry minus consumption. Entry-limited: low initial accumulation rate and a low pool. PDH-limited with intact entry: normal initial rate, normal or HIGHER pool, with oxidation and ATP low. The paper shows the first half (that the sensor falls when entry falls) and none of the second.
* **Asymmetry to watch:** the sensor was tuned to show loss of entry; its response to a RISE in the pool (PDH restrained) was never shown and may meet a ceiling at a 10 mM step. A future experiment should calibrate the rise with an in-experiment PDH-side perturbation (a PDK inhibitor or a PDH knockdown) in the same cells; if that does not raise the stock, the sensor cannot separate the two and the lead closes.
* **What the extra measurement resolves:** the ambiguity "low oxidation: entry or PDH?" when the lysate PDH assay is normal but its phospho-state may be lost. It does not resolve how much PDH is active, or whether a WWOX change is cause or compensation.
