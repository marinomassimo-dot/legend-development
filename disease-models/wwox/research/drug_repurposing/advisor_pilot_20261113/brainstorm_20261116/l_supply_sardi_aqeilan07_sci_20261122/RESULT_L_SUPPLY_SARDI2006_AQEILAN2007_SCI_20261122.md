## Answer first
1. **P1 sign (rule frozen 2026-11-19): INCOMPATIBLE with the release arm, now at full-text level.** In Sardi 2006, ErbB4 cleavage and nuclear E4ICD/TAB2/N-CoR signalling repress astrocytic gene expression: NRG1 antagonises CNTF-induced astrogenesis of rat E14.5 neural-precursor cultures and presenilin inhibition blocks that; E4ICD binds the GFAP and S100b promoters only after NRG1 and not under presenilin inhibition; ErbB4-null mouse cortex has raised GFAP and S100b at E17.5; only the cleavable ErbB4 isoform rescues it, and kinase-active E4ICD lowers GFAP in wild-type cortex. If WWOX loss released more nuclear ICD, fewer astrocytes would follow; the organoid phenotype is more astrogenesis. The provisional label "abstract only" is removed.
2. **But R3 tested one sign of a two-sign model.** Aqeilan 2007 proposes two effects of Wwox on ErbB4 (its Fig 5 and Discussion): it may bind the ICD and keep it from the nucleus (cited from the 2005 report, not shown in this paper) AND it stabilises full-length membrane ErbB4. The new data are on the second: in 556 breast cancers, Wwox is associated with membranous ErbB4 (P = 0.0003) and overall ErbB4 (P = 0.0002) but NOT with nuclear ErbB4 (P = 0.82); in COS-7 cells Wwox slows degradation of exogenous ErbB4; Wwox siRNA lowers endogenous ErbB4 in MCF-7; Wwox-null MEF clones carry less ErbB4 than a wild-type clone. **Under the stabilisation arm, WWOX loss means less ErbB4, less ICD and less repression of astrocytic genes: more astrogenesis, the same direction as the organoid phenotype and as the ErbB4-null cortex (post hoc: COMPATIBLE with P1).** Nothing in the supplied papers measures ErbB4 in WWOX-deficient neural cells.
3. **Net for the question "does the ERBB4 branch stay?":** the release arm stays dropped as a reason for a localisation test (the 2007 tumour data do not show WWOX loss raising nuclear ErbB4, and the ICD-retention premise is cited from 2005, "soluble ICD forms ... coimmunoprecipitated with Wwox (data not shown)"). The stabilisation arm is open and untested in neural cells. It is a different test from the one IT59-Q01 names, and it is cheaper: ErbB4 (full-length JMa) abundance in WWOX-deficient neural precursors or organoids against control, with ErbB4 not assumed to be detected in the same cells as WWOX. I did not look for such data beyond one text search of the analysis folder (no ERBB4 expression comparison found there; not an absence finding).
4. **What this does not do:** it does not link ErbB4 to PV interneurons or seizures (Sardi 2006 concerns precursors and astrogenesis; no interneuron or electrophysiology), and it does not make any NRG1 or ErbB4 modulator a candidate. The RP-038 class sign premise (an NRG1 peptide in a Wwox-deficient brain) is NOT settled; only the astrogenesis route has a sign, and that route has a premise (less ErbB4 in WWOX-deficient precursors) that nobody has measured.
