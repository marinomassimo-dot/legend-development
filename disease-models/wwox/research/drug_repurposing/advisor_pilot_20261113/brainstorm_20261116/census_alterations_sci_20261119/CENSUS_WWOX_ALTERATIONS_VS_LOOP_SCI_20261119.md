# Census: documented Wwox alterations against what the loop holds as a lever or comparison (SCI, 2026-11-19; held notes only, no delegate; non-canonical; hypothesis level; no dose, no recommendation for or against any use of any agent in any person; reference genotype class only; not medical advice)

Asked by: the operator's night mandate (guiding question: which documented alteration in WWOX can support a pharmacological lever or comparison that LEGEND is not yet using, which alternative explanations remain, which experiment would distinguish them) and approved by ADV with two additions (evidence level and preparation of each alteration beside what the loop holds; end with the TWO alterations of highest marginal value that have no pharmacological comparison). **Method and its limit:** "what the loop holds" was found by file names and keyword hits over the 30 cards in `cards/`, the 32 rows of `research_portfolio.tsv`, `batch_sci_20261105/map/open_questions_map.tsv` and IT's ADV-053 files; **a keyword hit is not a reading of the card**, so a hit shows presence of the term and a miss shows absence of the term, not of the idea. The evidence statements about the alterations come from the saved full texts scanned in code (gene-therapy papers 2021 and 2026, Hussain 2019, Steinberg 2021, Breton 2021) and from abstracts (Repudi 2021, the lde/lde rat, the P47T knock-in).

## Answer first
1. **Of the eleven rows below, six have a card or a comparison in the loop, one partly (neurogenesis), one lies outside this question (cerebellar degeneration), and three have none**: the **interneuron and PV deficit** (open questions only: RS-NP3A1 and IT56-Q08, a chemogenetic tool arm, not a drug), the **DNA-damage-response signature** (the term appears incidentally in four cards; no lever, no comparison), and **impaired axonal conduction** (no hit anywhere).
2. **A lever on a one-origin alteration is not supported just because a card exists**: GSK3-beta activation, the Wnt activation and the DNA-damage signature each rest on one laboratory and one preparation; the hypoglycaemia, hypomyelination, hyperexcitability and neuroinflammation rows have several readings but most come from one laboratory's mouse and organoid work; only hypomyelination and hyperexcitability have an independent species (the lde/lde rat; the rat audiogenic and EEG report).
3. **The two alterations of highest marginal value without a pharmacological comparison:** (A) the **interneuron and PV deficit**, with the **WW1 partner (ERBB4, PPxY) mechanism as its branch**, and (B) the **DNA-damage-response signature**, which ranks second only because the third gap (axonal conduction) stops at once: its one source is closed (no PMC copy, no Europe PMC full text) and neither open gene-therapy paper reports a conduction measurement. For each, the cheapest next step, the alternative explanation, the discriminating observation and the stop criterion are in section 3.

## 2. Why the evidence column matters for the lever column
* **GSK3-beta, Wnt and DNA-damage rows are one-origin rows:** a card or an arm on them inherits one laboratory's preparation (null mouse brain lysate; one organoid line). The ADV-067 result (lithium effect looks similar in all genotypes; no lithium-alone arm) is the live example.
* **The interneuron row is the only one with opposite directions across preparations** (fewer GABAergic neurons and PV transcript in mouse, more GAD67 in organoids), so it is also the row where a hidden alternative (maturation delay, a late-stage effect of lethality, a species or preparation difference) is most likely to be doing the work.

## 3. The two chosen alterations: cheapest step, alternative, discriminating observation, stop criterion
**(A) Interneuron and PV deficit (with the WW1 partner branch).**
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Why highest value | the best-documented gap that ties directly to the loop's gain-control framework (a GABA-A modulator is only as good as the inhibitory machinery it acts on); two mouse origins; open questions exist but no pharmacological comparison; the WW1 binding-loss P47T knock-in epilepsy (abstract) and the held PPxY census (ERBB4, NETO2) give a mechanism branch |
| Alternative explanations | fewer PV cells; less Pvalb per cell; delayed maturation (PV expression rises across the weeks the null survives, and Breton warns the age window may mimic a late-stage disorder); a preparation or species difference (organoids go the other way); a nucleus-capture artefact in a one-pool deposit |
| Cheapest next step | **in silico, no laboratory:** fetch the public per-animal bulk deposits behind IT13 P3a (GEO series GSE158681, GSE158682, GSE158685 are indexed) and compare Pvalb with other late-maturing neuronal genes and with Sst, Vip and Gad1 per animal: a maturation delay predicts the late-maturing set falls together; a PV-specific loss predicts Pvalb and PV-cell genes fall while other late genes do not (IT13 already found the pan-interneuron panel flat; the maturation control is what is missing); units are animals, not nuclei |
| Discriminating observation | PV-specific fall with normal late-maturing genes in animal-separated libraries; then per-animal PV immunostaining (laboratory) |
| Decision it would change | whether a PV-directed pharmacological comparison (a class that restores fast-spiking function, against the generic GABA-A modulator arm) is added to the slice pilot, and the priority of the PV chemogenetic tool arm IT56-Q08R |
| Stop criterion | the late-maturing set falls together (maturation or lethality-stage effect): stop, no PV-specific lever; or the deposits are not animal-separated enough (fewer than three animals per arm): stop at "a measurement" |
**(B) DNA-damage-response signature.**
| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Why second | one origin and no lever, so lower value; chosen because the step is fully in silico and the replication structure is explicit; axonal conduction (the third gap) stops at its closed single source |
| Alternative explanations | proliferation or maturation state differences between knockout and parental organoids; apoptosis or culture stress; clone and batch (one parental line; the knockout libraries split in two clusters in my recomputation) |
| Cheapest next step | in silico: score a pre-specified panel of canonical DNA-damage-response genes per library in GSE156243 (six libraries, one line) and test whether the signal holds in both knockout clusters and survives normalisation to neuronal and progenitor panels; no new data |
| Discriminating observation | a DNA-damage signal that is present in both knockout clusters, absent after neuronal-composition normalisation controls, and (laboratory) gamma-H2AX or 53BP1 foci that rescue lines abolish |
| Decision it would change | whether any DNA-damage-directed class enters the comparison set at all (today none) or the row stays a descriptive one-origin observation |
| Stop criterion | signal confined to one knockout cluster or lost after normalisation: stop; a lever search is not started on a one-line descriptive signal |
**Considered and stopped:** axonal conduction (closed single source; a K-channel-blocking class would also be pro-convulsant, so a comparison would be harm-limited); hypoglycaemia and neuroinflammation (already carried by cards and, for glia, by the parked H4 route).
