Three further observations from the figure that ADV-051 does not carry (each by eye, each hypothesis-level):

1. **The early-window drop with ethosuximide looks proportionally similar in all three genotypes.** In the first 20 minutes the +/+ ethosuximide curve sits about 0.3-0.4 stage below the PTZ-alone curve (peak about 1.2 against 0.8; "n.s." with N=20 and N=16), and the -/- curve sits roughly 0.7-1 stage below (peak about 2.5 against 1.5-1.8; significant with N=6 each). That is about a one-third reduction in both. If this reading holds, the pattern "significant only in the knockout" is a difference in baseline size and variance, not in proportional effect. This supports IT's conclusion from a second side (floor) and does not yet support selectivity.
2. **Lithium is significant at the floor in every genotype.** Panel d: +/+ PTZ-alone (N=12) peaks at about 1.2 in the first minutes and is below 0.5 afterwards; with LiCl (N=8) it stays about 0.2 or lower, marked with four asterisks; the same for +/- (N=12 each); -/- peak about 3.5 falls to about 1.3 (N=6 and N=7). A near-complete suppression in animals that barely respond is what a general property predicts (IT's point 2), and it sets the contrast with ethosuximide's non-significance in animals of equal baseline.
3. **The two drugs were not compared against a common control.** The PTZ-alone groups of panel b (N = 20, 18, 6) and panel d (N = 12, 12, 6) are different cohorts, and the -/- PTZ-alone curves differ in shape and peak (about 2.5 against about 3.5). The paper's sentence that the lithium effect is "better than" the ethosuximide effect is therefore a cross-cohort impression, not a tested difference. This should travel with any statement that lithium is "stronger" than ethosuximide in this model.

## What this changes (decision value)
* IT's conclusion stands on (a) and (b) as checked; its remaining weak point is (c), the mouse ontogeny of ethosuximide against PTZ, which no held source gives.
* A cheap discriminating control that ADV-051's arm list does not carry, proposed here as ADV's own suggestion (hypothesis; a design idea for SCI and a laboratory, not a decision): **an age series in wild-type pups** (the age of the Cheng assay against an older age, same stimulus and scale). If ethosuximide fails in young wild-type pups and works in older ones, the age explanation is supported without needing any null animal, which cannot be tested beyond about 4 weeks; if it works at the young age, the age explanation weakens and the knockout-selectivity question reopens. It uses no knockout animals and answers the question that decides the order of arms.
* ADV-051's arm order (ethosuximide first because it is the only arm whose outcome changes the decision) is unchanged; this note adds one control to it and one source gap to close.
