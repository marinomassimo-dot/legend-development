## Position in five sentences
The candidacy that survives the audit is narrow: ketamine (a use-dependent NMDAR pore blocker) for **acute rescue of refractory status epilepticus**, held at hypothesis level, with one cheap discriminating slice experiment attached; the memantine "then" half is weaker and I defend it only as a second arm of the same experiment. What did not survive, and I do not defend: "the only drug mechanism-matched" (K1, K12), the 6/6 vs 3/3 GSK-3beta duration flip (K4), the pooled 24/30 (K7), the DeVine attribution, the coverage percentages as a ranking (K3), and any claim of chronic-control superiority over approved agents. The case that remains rests on a class-level, one-laboratory slice result, uncontrolled paediatric series and the absence of better-controlled alternatives in the rescue niche. That is thin, and it is what it is: candidacy by non-exclusion plus a falsifiable test, not by demonstrated benefit. No ketamine datum exists in any Wwox system ([B] answer-first K1; [C] answer 1).

## (a) Five strongest supports

**S1. The only NMDAR-class pharmacology in a WWOX system is a spontaneous-activity, genotype-specific, near-complete block.**
DATO: neocortical slices of neuron-specific knockout mice burst in 36 of 42 slices (24 animals) against 0 of 11 wild-type slices; d-APV 50 uM "eliminated the spontaneous bursting events", n = 51 bursts, 5 slices, 3 animals ([B] section 1 table; blind check [BV] C02, C03 SUPPORTED). The antagonist was a competitive glutamate-site blocker, not ketamine ([B] K1 row; [A] F9): the transfer to a pore blocker is INFERENZA. Why it counts: it is the disease genotype's own network phenomenon, not a provoked threshold assay, and [C] "what the comparison does and does not allow" itself keeps "the only drug-class pharmacology in a WWOX slice is NMDAR (d-APV)" as supported.
Refuted by: a pore blocker at block depth matched on the evoked delayed NMDAR response that does not cut burst frequency by at least half (SCI's own prior for success 0.70, [B] 5.3 P1); abolition shown to be Mg2+-relief loss (IT40-Q01, [B] section 6 item 5); or a second laboratory failing to reproduce (origin collapse, [PROG] "What the repository already holds").

**S2. The rescue endpoint needs only drug-present suppression, which the held model and slice both allow, and it never needed the claims that failed.**
DATO: the oscillator keeps no memory of a transient NMDAR-weight reduction over minutes to about an hour ([HYS] "Answer first"; MODEL, not evidence). INFERENZA: acute rescue asks for suppression while the agent is present; it does not need plasticity, the GSK-3beta flip or a lasting benefit, so the collapse of K4 (strict label match 2 of 9, effective n of the "prolonged" side 1, [B] section 3) does not touch it. The held rival R-A ([B] 5.2) is exactly this reading.
Refuted by: no suppression at matched block depth (slice); or in vivo burst or seizure burden after stopping above a time-matched vehicle (R-B, below).

**S3. In the rescue niche nothing is better controlled, and ketamine has the largest paediatric series with quantified short-exposure harms.**
DATO: for refractory SE the anaesthetic infusions have no placebo-controlled paediatric RCT, only small open randomised comparisons and observational data; ESETT, EcLiPSE and ConSEPT concern established SE, not refractory ([P5] Q2 and answer Q2; [C] answer 2). Ketamine: first-line retrospective comparison termination 61% (23/38) against midazolam 28% (22/79), OR 3.97, adverse effects 3% against 24% ([V-B] P2-05 and [V-D] P5-41, both SUPPORTED); pooled paediatric cessation 50% to 51% in two reviews ([V-B] P2-09, P2-10, SUPPORTED); serious events 0.33% in 58,752 sedated children ([V-C] P4-07, SUPPORTED); Kuki 15/18 above 50% seizure reduction ([V-B] P2-01). INFERENZA: "one of several unvalidated rescue options, none with WWOX data" ([C] answer 2) is a fair statement of equipoise, and equipoise is what hypothesis-level candidacy needs.
Refuted by: a confounding-adjusted or controlled paediatric comparison showing no termination advantage; the survival figure in the same cohort (55% against 82%, unadjusted, P = 0.002, [V-D] P5-41) is the observation that could do it if the confounding by indication (ketamine used more often in acute symptomatic seizures) turns out smaller than the authors state. KETASER01 gives nothing either way (halted at 10, 2/5 against 2/5, "No conclusions can be drawn", [V-B] P2-08).

**S4. A class signal in DEE not selected for NMDAR genes, and cross-gene sequence observations.**
DATO: the only randomised crossover trial of an NMDAR pore blocker in childhood DEE, registered before start, reports 9/27 against 2/27 responders (at least 2 of 4 criteria), seizure improvement 8 against 2 and EEG improvement 8 against 2 ([P1] Q1; [V-A] P1-01, P1-08, P1-09, P1-12, P1-19 SUPPORTED). The abstract states no genetic stratum ([V-A] P1-07), so INFERENZA: a signal in an unselected DEE population is mechanism-agnostic support for chronic block. Sequence observations: ATP1A2 infant "partially responsive to ketamine ... seizure-free when memantine ... was added" ([V-B] P2-16); GRIN2D proband "dramatic electroclinical improvement" on ketamine plus magnesium ([V-B] P2-17). Both n = 1; the first is memantine added, not given after ([A] F8).
Refuted by: trial full text showing a paired analysis that is not significant (exact McNemar 0.0156, 0.039 or 0.065 depending on discordant pairs, [A] section 3; the test is unnamed in the abstract, [V-A] P1-13/14) or responders concentrated in NMDAR-gene etiologies; the harms tables of the same paper (blocked, [P1] short paragraph).

**S5. The input's self-inflicted brake dissolves on verification (support by non-exclusion, weak by construction).**
DATO: "expected harm in a WOREE infant is larger" is NOT SUPPORTED ([P3] answer first; [IT-S] K6); no paper tests a Wwox-deficient brain with ketamine; the five rodent papers are hours-long PND7 paradigms against a human regimen of days ([P3] section 3); human developmental neurotoxicity of ketamine is "not established" ([P4] answer 5); the label's primate datum is that 3 hours did not increase neuronal loss and 5 hours or longer did ([V-C] P4-56). INFERENZA: nothing held excludes the candidacy on harm grounds that the input asserted.
Refuted by: a 2 x 2 genotype by exposure pup experiment with an interaction ([P3] section 4), or any report of a WWOX child's exposure with outcomes (operator item, not an agent task, [PROG] K11).

## (b) Where the lanes, verifiers or host were too harsh, and too kind

**Too harsh**
1. *Mechanism rivals read as rivals to the candidacy.* R-D (HCN1 via Ih, [B] 5.1) undermines "mechanism-matched" and the NMDAR explanation, not the rescue endpoint. The blind verifier notes the Chen 2009 mechanism is that of the anaesthetic-type action, slices from ketamine/xylazine-anaesthetised mice ([BV] C28). In a rescue setting an anaesthetic-type route is part of the drug's effect, not an off-target nuisance. The lanes should have said: R-D and R-F change the *reason*, not the *endpoint*, and they collapse ketamine into "one anaesthetic among several" (see (e)).
2. *DeVine.* The host's finding (a) is right for patient 1 and patient 2's second admission ([V-B] P2-03: resolution on day 14 after ketamine was weaned; spot EEG 15 days after discontinuation). But [P2] Q1 table row "Time to response" records patient 2's first course as "Seizures ceased within 1 hour of initiation" ([P2] file `p2_ketamine_series/REPORT.md`, Q1 table). The blind verifier's quoted rows do not cover that sentence, so it is producer-reported and unverified, and midazolam/pentobarbital co-infusions confound it ([V-B] P2-03). The careful statement is "a temporal link in one of four courses, unverified", not "none".
3. *Coverage metric.* [A] F14 is right that the ranking is one node deep, but dropping it entirely loses a structural fact stated by HE: on the entry's own graph all four curated paths pass through the bursting node ([A] section 4). That is a statement about where to measure, not about efficacy.
4. *Lithium and ethosuximide as the counterweight.* They hold the only in vivo drug data in a Wwox model, from one provoked-seizure experiment, lithium N = 6 and "effect also in wild type" ([C] table, Lithium row; [B] section 2). d-APV has no drug-by-genotype contrast either, because wild type does not burst ([REPO] section 3, first row). The gap is symmetric, and the lanes count only one side as a gap.

**Too kind**
1. *Repudi 2021.* The in vivo hyperexcitability of the null mouse was recorded under ketamine/medetomidine anaesthesia and the genotype contrast stood ([REPO] section 3; [STM] Layer 1). IT files this as "says nothing on ketamine's own effect". It is not a test (no ketamine-free arm, absolute rates not those of an awake brain), but it is the only in vivo ketamine-exposed Wwox-null recording the repository holds, and an NMDAR-dependent hyperexcitability under anaesthetic-range ketamine could have collapsed and did not. INFERENZA, weakly against the candidacy; it belongs in the table as non-confirming.
2. *SCI's 0.70 prior* (P1) leans on an oscillator fitted to the same slice ([B] 5.3 "Basis"; [STM] "a simulation cannot confirm itself") and R-E has no model support ([B] 5.3 P2). The prior is a class-transfer guess with a correlated basis.
3. *"Nothing is clearly worse" ([C] answer 1).* True on WWOX-system axes; false on chronic control, where approved agents have placebo-controlled paediatric RCTs ([C] last table rows; [V-D] P5-01, P5-04, P5-07, P5-10 SUPPORTED) and memantine has one abstract-only composite. Both are transfers, but one transfers controlled data.
4. *The washout overshoot* (about 1.8 times, error bar about 0.8 to 2.8, no test, text says "returned to normal", [BV] C03, C07) is rightly called one untested point; but [HYS] (model misses it) and [HOM] (a slow gain gives 2.5 to 3.5 times, never a lasting suppression) both lean toward rebound, and no held datum leans the other way. A rescue candidacy should price withdrawal rebound, not only transient suppression.

## (c) The experiment that would most change the ranking

SCI's proposal ([B] 5.3, final paragraph) is the one I would put first, with two additions: a memantine arm (the sequence half) and a sedation-matched comparator in vivo (the only way to reach R-F). Preparation: Breton's S-KO neocortical slice (P13 to P17, 1.3 mM Mg2+, 35 C, [B] section 1), a pore blocker, memantine, d-APV, time-matched vehicle slices; block depth matched on the evoked delayed NMDAR response (Supplementary S3C, not held, [B] section 1); burst rate during block at graded depth and at 30, 60 and 120 min after washout; Ih sag in the same slices; n counted in slices and animals, not bursts. Stage 2 (IPOTESI, only if stage 1 passes): neuronal Wwox-deletion mice with continuous EEG, pore blocker against a non-NMDAR sedative at EEG-matched sedation depth and vehicle ([OP] follow-up 1; [B] section 4 agrees with items 1 and 2).

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Burst frequency at block of at least half the evoked response | falls by at least half (prior 0.70) | falls, but d-APV at the same evoked-response block falls less | falls | falls | not a slice question |
| Burst frequency at shallow block (under about one fifth) | no change (0.55) | no change or fall | **rises** by at least a fifth (0.20); bell-shaped curve | no change | not a slice question |
| Ih sag | unchanged at suppressing concentrations | lowered (prior 0.75 for a fall; the discriminator is the d-APV contrast) | unchanged | unchanged | not a slice question |
| 30 to 60 min after washout against vehicle | within +-30% (0.55) | within +-30% or a drug-specific trapping tail | within +-30% | **at least 1.3 times**, decaying with its own time constant (0.30); d-APV same | not a slice question |
| Memantine against ketamine after washout (IPOTESI, pharmacology ASSUMED, [B] 5.1) | smaller or equal overshoot if trapping is weaker | differs from d-APV | similar | equal if the gain is homeostatic | not a slice question |

Priors are SCI's, written before data ([B] 5.3). A lasting fall (at most 0.7 times, prior 0.15, R-C) would be a surprise under every column. Effect on rank: pass (suppression at matched depth, no sag confound, no bell, rebound at most +30%) unparks the pore-blocker arm cells C007, C011, C054 from simulation tier 3 ([IT-S] "Testability"); a fail on row 1, or a rise at shallow block, removes the NMDAR rationale and leaves ketamine as one anaesthetic among comparators whose evidence the lanes already rate as larger.

## (d) What the candidacy needs from the harms evidence, and the hardest harm

**It needs:** (1) short-exposure harms quantified low: held ([V-C] P4-01, P4-05, P4-07). (2) Harms at the exposure a rescue actually uses: held descriptively and small, not comparative: PICU infusions, median 2.4 days, hypotension 13/24, tachycardia 15/24, emergence reactions 3/24 ([V-C] P4-24); 11 potentially related events in 77, 6 needing intervention ([V-C] P4-26); Kuki 3 of 18 transient events needing intervention ([V-B] P2-01); Jacobwitz 2022, median infusion 85.7 h, events requiring intervention 3/69, "No patients experienced withdrawal symptoms/signs during ketamine wean" ([V-B] P2-04). (3) No genotype multiplier: unmeasured, not refuted ([P3] section 4). (4) No pro-convulsant signal at the exposure used: **not established**.

**The hardest harm: seizures provoked by the drug.** DATO: electrographic seizures after ketamine in 0/39 without epilepsy and 26/94 (28%) with epilepsy; children with epilepsy 3/5 against 6/33 in adults with epilepsy ([V-C] P4-16 PARTLY, P4-17 SUPPORTED, PMID 39918155); the label lists spasms "resembling a partial motor or generalized motor seizure" ([P4] answer 3). Why it is the hardest for this candidacy: it attacks the benefit direction itself, not a cost beside it; it is mechanistically coherent with R-E (low-occupancy block on PV interneurons, Picard 2019, [B] 5.1) in a slice that already has smaller sIPSCs (57.3 to 27.5 pA, [B] 5.2 R-E); memantine worsened seizures in three named GRIN gain-of-function individuals, cause "unclear" ([V-C] P4-72); ketamine "caused seizures" in a GRIN2D mouse (TRANSFER, [P2] Q4). The defence has only these answers: n = 5 children; 40-to-50-year-old studies, some in volunteers ([V-C] P4-16 limits); and the continuous-EEG infusion cohorts report termination, reduction, no change (46%, 28%, 26%) with no category for worsening ([V-B] P2-04), which is silence about an event, not absence of it. I do not claim it is answered.

**Second hardest (structural):** every rescue regimen in the held series runs far beyond the label's cautioned exposure (more than 3 hours in children up to about 3 years, [V-C] P4-50), and the age window covers WOREE infants; the label's own animal basis is split and "clinical significance ... not clear" ([V-C] P4-56, P4-50), but no status-epilepticus infusion cohort measures neurodevelopment ([P3] section 5; absence claim marked provisional). **Third:** unadjusted survival 55% against 82% ([V-D] P5-41).

## (e) The weakest point of my own argument

1. **Candidacy by default.** S1 is class-level and one-laboratory; S3 and S4 are uncontrolled or abstract-level; S5 is non-exclusion. The same pattern could be written for several arm-table classes ([C] table), so the defence ranks nothing; its only discriminating content is the S1 prediction, which is why (c) matters more than (a).
2. **The mechanism-agnostic version of S3 collapses.** If benefit is any anaesthetic-type effect (R-D, R-F), ketamine is one infusion among midazolam and barbiturates, which have larger observational bases and equally no WWOX data ([C] last row; [P5] Q2). The reason to prefer ketamine specifically (NMDAR-regenerative burst in a slice with weakened inhibition) rests on pharmacology the lanes label ASSUMED because two primary papers returned a reCAPTCHA page ([B] 5.1 and section 7).
3. **Unsupported premise.** No file I read states that refractory status epilepticus is a characteristic problem of the genotype class; what is held is "early-onset drug-resistant seizures" ([A2] evidence items 17 and 18). The endpoint comes from the input's reading ([OP] finding 4), not from a WWOX source. No WWOX child exposed to ketamine appears in any published report (two routes, [P2] Q4), so the clinical prior is entirely transfer.
4. **The sequence half is the thinnest.** Memantine was added, not given after, in the ATP1A2 case ([A] F8); in a second ATP1A2 patient the order was reverse ([P1] Q3); WWOX is not an NMDAR-gene disorder, and in the GRIN cohort loss-of-function or indeterminate variants showed less benefit ([A2] comparator facts; [V-C] P4-74). The randomised trial is abstract-only, analysed unpaired, composite, with harms unread.
5. **The Repudi datum** (b, too kind, item 1) is the one held observation that could point the other way, and I can offer only that it is not a test.
