## (a) Five strongest objections

**O1. "Mechanism-matched" is a class label resting on a different drug, one preparation and one laboratory; nothing about ketamine or memantine has ever been measured in any Wwox system.**
- DATO: the slice drug was d-APV 50 uM, a competitive glutamate-site antagonist, not a pore blocker; carbenoxolone, the only support for the gap-junction half, is non-specific by the authors' own words; the pannexin blocker did not reproduce it (SCI section 1; VSCI C03, C04, C06 SUPPORTED). The key WWOX sentence does not name the drug (A F9). The NMDAR pillar is one laboratory, one preparation (LC row "d-APV": n = 5 slices, 3 animals; ORIGIN file IT56-Q02/Q07).
- DATO: ketamine inhibits Ih in cortical pyramidal neurons of 14- to 22-day-old mice, the same age window, on a property (sag) that Breton found raised in S-KO neurons (SCI section 5.1; VSCI C28 SUPPORTED); low-dose ketamine effects in adult mouse cortex run through GluN2A on PV interneurons (VSCI C29 SUPPORTED), and S-KO slices already have smaller sIPSCs (SCI section 5.2, R-E). INFERENZA: a ketamine effect in an S-KO slice could be HCN1-mediated or net-disinhibiting, and d-APV cannot tell.
- Refuted if: a block-depth-matched pore blocker suppresses S-KO bursts at all depths tested, with no bell-shaped dose-response, unchanged Ih sag, and a post-washout rate within about 30% of time-matched vehicle at 30 to 120 min (SCI section 5.3, P1 to P5). Label: IPOTESI-test, not a result.

**O2. In the one DEE where NMDAR block is mechanistically indicated by definition, the in vivo class record is mixed to adverse, and nobody carried this forward.**
- DATO (I read the abstract, PubMed 40277233): in the Grin2d V667I gain-of-function mouse with spontaneous seizures, ketamine 0.5 mg/kg had a "limited effect" on ECoG, "higher dosages (4 or 10 mg/kg) caused seizures", memantine and phenytoin a "small corrective effect". It sits in P2 Q4 (TRANSFER) and A2 (dismech item graded REFUTE) and appears in neither ITS nor LC.
- DATO: memantine worsened seizure frequency in three named GRIN gain-of-function individuals, cause unclear (VC P4-72 PARTLY: the paper says four, names three); electrographic seizures after ketamine in 3 of 5 children with epilepsy, 26 of 94 with epilepsy overall, 0 of 39 without (VC P4-16, P4-17; the studies are 40 to 50 years old and small); the Ketalar label lists spasms resembling seizures (P4 answer 3).
- INFERENZA: if "NMDAR block should help" fails in a model whose lesion is the receptor, a WWOX slice dependence cannot be read as a prediction for in vivo seizure control; R-E predicts the same bell shape.
- Refuted if: the input's own experiment 1 (spontaneous-seizure Wwox-null mice, video EEG) shows seizure reduction at low and high exposure with no pro-convulsant window.

**O3. The 24/30 "prior" is neither a prior for this endpoint nor for this population.**
- DATO: DeVine's three infants have no seizure outcome in the abstract (A F6); the paper's own timeline puts EEG resolution after ketamine was weaned or stopped (patient 1 day 14 after 5 days of infusion "without successful control of subclinical seizures"; patient 2 resolution "15 days after discontinuation"; patient 3 died, no resolution) (VB P2-03 PARTLY; the host's finding a). So DeVine contributes 0 to 2 responders attributable to ketamine, not 3, and the best clean figure is HE's 21/27 (A F6), with endpoints still unlike (">50% reduction" vs "resolution").
- DATO: Kuki's non-inflammatory stratum is 8 patients by subtraction, no genes, no genetic-DEE group (P2 Q2; VB P2-01); "WOREE falls in the better stratum" has no datum behind the middle step (P2 headline 2). A secondary table prints Kuki as 7 of 18 seizure termination, unverified (VB P2-09; P2 Q1 note).
- DATO: aetiologies in the three series are acquired or structural (meningitis, encephalitis, FIRES, abusive head trauma, stroke, post-arrest, FCD with two surgical cures among Rosati's non-responders) (P2 Q1 table; A F6); no paediatric series has a genetic stratum (P2 Q4). Better-powered numbers are lower: 46% (32/69), 14/19 episodes, pooled 50 to 51% in two non-independent meta-analyses (P2 headline 4; VB P2-04, P2-09, P2-10). The only randomised paediatric trial was halted at 10 patients, 2/5 vs 2/5, "No conclusions can be drawn" (VD P5-45). Comparative cohort: termination 61% vs 28% but survival to discharge 55% vs 82%, confounded by indication (VD P5-41).
- INFERENZA: every efficacy figure is for add-on to a regimen of benzodiazepine or barbiturate infusions; no series separates ketamine from the other anaesthetic changes made at the same time (DeVine: average of 3 concomitant ASMs; Rosati: midazolam add-on). Refuted if: a series with a genetic-DEE stratum, or a controlled comparison with concurrent-anaesthetic changes held fixed, shows response.

**O4. The "ketamine then memantine" sequence is two single weak observations joined by a mechanism that is not WWOX's.**
- DATO: the only DEE trial is one crossover, 27 analysed, composite responder (at least 2 of 4 criteria; the registry has 5, adding serum inflammatory markers), enrolment 31 vs registry 30, twins removed without stated reason; the unpaired Fisher/Wald figures treat 54 observations as independent; "P < 0.02" is reachable under an exact McNemar test only if about 7 memantine-only and 0 placebo-only pairs, and the thresholds fit an unpaired uncorrected chi-square (A section 3; P1 Q1; VA P1-08, P1-09, P1-14, P1-19). Harms and body text were never read (P1 headline; VA P1-16). Caregiver impression 10 vs 7 was not significant (VA P1-12).
- DATO: the ATP1A2 "sequence" is one infant in whom memantine was added to ketamine while ketamine was only "partially responsive"; the genetic result came in week 2 of memantine; in 33493807 patient 5 the order was memantine, then ketamine (A F8; P1 Q3; VB P2-16). The mechanism there is Na/K-ATPase and glutamate handling.
- DATO (P4 answer 6; not in a blind-verified row): the memantine label reports apoptosis in juvenile rats at 15 mg/kg/day, no-effect level 8, and seizure disorders "not systematically evaluated". INFERENZA: the input's premise that memantine "avoids the duration flip" is untested (the flip itself does not survive, SCI section 3), and chronic NMDAR blockade for years in an infant brain is an exposure beyond every toxicity paper, which are hours to 7 days. A2: memantine works in GRIN gain-of-function, "disorders of the NMDA receptor itself", where the drug targets the lesion; WWOX is not.
- Refuted if: paired per-patient data show at least 7 memantine-only responders with 0 reverse, or an independent replication; harms by arm are readable and benign in seizure-prone children.

**O5. There is no endpoint on which ketamine is both WWOX-relevant and evidenced; "repurposing" is being used for an aetiology-agnostic acute use.**
- DATO: rescue of status epilepticus: the evidence is from non-WWOX populations and carries zero genotype-specific information (P2 Q4: no WWOX ketamine report on 3 PubMed queries and 2 Europe PMC full-text hits that are anaesthetic mentions). Whether ketamine is used in refractory status epilepticus does not depend on a WWOX rationale, so a WWOX "candidacy" adds nothing. INFERENZA: nobody established how often WOREE infants reach refractory status epilepticus, so the size of this niche is unknown; the input's "the setting WOREE infants present in" (IN finding 4) is unsourced.
- Chronic seizure control: nothing for ketamine (no oral or chronic paediatric DEE evidence; prolonged paediatric exposure is case reports only: cholangiopathy title-only, cystitis with S-ketamine, withdrawal after 7 days, PICU hypotension 13/24) (P4 answers 4; VC P4-24, P4-32, P4-38 PARTLY).
- Development: no agent has a development endpoint with randomised or WWOX-model evidence (LC). Human neurotoxicity is "not established" in both directions (P4 answer 5).
- The efficacy regimens are multi-day (Kuki median 7.5 days; Jacobwitz median 85.7 h) while the input's phase table says "short infusion" (A2 section 4 notes the input's own tension). INFERENZA: "short rescue" is a proposal that the cited efficacy data do not test.
- Refuted if: a WOREE or WWOX-genotype series, or a Wwox-null in vivo drug-by-genotype contrast, exists for any single endpoint.

## (c) Better-supported alternatives, endpoint by endpoint

**Rescue of status epilepticus: none is better supported; WWOX adds no information; not comparable on WWOX axes.** DATO: the comparators (midazolam, barbiturate-class infusions) have no placebo-controlled paediatric RCT in refractory status epilepticus, only small open randomised comparisons and observational data; established-SE RCTs (ESETT, EcLiPSE, ConSEPT) are a different line (LC; P5 Q2; VD P5-31, P5-34 SUPPORTED). Ketamine has one first-line randomised trial (144 children, ketamine plus midazolam vs placebo plus midazolam; abstract only, registry number unverified; VD P5-43 PARTLY), one halted trial and uncontrolled series. So on this endpoint ketamine is at least not worse supported than its comparators. The prosecution cannot show it is indefensible here, only that the evidence is aetiology-agnostic.

**Chronic seizure control: approved agents are better supported on paediatric randomised DEE evidence and quantified harms; none is better supported on WWOX-system evidence; across axes not comparable.** DATO: cannabidiol, fenfluramine, stiripentol, ganaxolone, everolimus, vigabatrin and hormonal therapy, ketogenic diet have placebo-controlled or randomised paediatric trials with harms in Dravet, LGS, CDKL5, TSC, infantile spasms (LC; P5 Q1; VD P5-01, P5-04, P5-07, P5-10, P5-22 SUPPORTED, abstract depth). INFERENZA: each is a transfer from another aetiology to WWOX and nothing in a Wwox model supports any of them; WWOX-level support is case-level with unknown denominators (cannabidiol, vigabatrin, ACTH, ketogenic diet; LC). Memantine's single crossover is weaker than any of these on design (O4). Lithium and ethosuximide: only drugs with in vivo Wwox-null data, one experiment, provoked threshold; ethosuximide had no effect on behaviour in +/+ and +/- at a low PTZ dose (VSCI C09), lithium also acted in wild type (LC); lithium harms in infants and ethosuximide paediatric trials were not searched by any lane (LC limits). So the honest ranking is by axis, not by drug, and no alternative dominates.

**Development: none is better supported; not comparable.** DATO: only AAV9 gene addition rescues development in the null mouse (preventive window, outside pharmacological proposals; LC). No drug has a development endpoint with randomised or WWOX-model evidence.

## (d) What the lanes missed

2. **Concentration relevance.** The ex vivo ketamine-class data SCI cites as "nearest" (S-ketamine 100 to 1000 uM abolishing seizure-like events, VSCI C30 PARTLY, 100 uM only; Ih EC50 about 8 to 16 uM, VSCI C28) were not set against concentrations reached with clinical use. IPOTESI from general knowledge, unverified here: these are well above typical clinical free levels. 4. **Sedation context as the confounder** is in SCI's rival table as R-F "generic"; no lane tried to quantify how many ketamine responses in the series coincide with weaning of other anaesthetics (DeVine's timeline suggests it).
5. **Wwox-null in vivo recording under ketamine/medetomidine anaesthesia** (STM Layer 1 row 6, Repudi 2021) kept the genotype contrast; STM says it "says nothing on ketamine's own effect". INFERENZA, low weight: it is the only in vivo Wwox-null brain exposed to an anaesthetic-range NMDAR blocker, and hyperexcitability remained; firing rate is not seizure burden, so I do not treat it as evidence against.

## (e) The weakest point of my own argument

- It is largely an argument from absence. Zero WWOX data for ketamine is also zero harm data; the harm side (K6, K4) is as unsupported as the benefit side, which is why O3 and O5 carry more weight than any harm claim I could make.
- O2 uses a gain-of-function GRIN2D mouse at 4 to 10 mg/kg: opposite genotype, ECoG, dose and anaesthetic state not shown in the abstract, and ketamine can be pro-convulsant in wild type at some exposures. It shows class unpredictability, not Wwox-null behaviour. I read an abstract only.
- On rescue of status epilepticus, ketamine is at least as well evidenced as its comparators and short-exposure harms are quantified and low (VC P4-05, P4-07). If "candidate" means "an option clinicians weigh in refractory status epilepticus of any cause", my case fails; it holds only for "candidate for repurposing on a WWOX rationale".
- If "candidate" means "worth one cheap discriminating slice or in vivo experiment", ketamine or a pore blocker passes, because it is a pre-registered gap (arm table, SCI P1 to P5, IN experiments 1 and 2); my objections O1 and O2 are reasons to run it, not to drop it.
- My comparative claim (c) is mostly "not comparable". The one clean comparative statement, that lithium and ethosuximide outrank ketamine on in vivo WWOX evidence, rests on a single N = 6 provoked-seizure experiment from one laboratory.
- I did not independently re-read the primary papers; my evidence is the lanes' quotes, three verifiers' checks, and one abstract. Where those share an abstract-level limit (Kuki, Rosati, Schiller blocked), so does this memo.
