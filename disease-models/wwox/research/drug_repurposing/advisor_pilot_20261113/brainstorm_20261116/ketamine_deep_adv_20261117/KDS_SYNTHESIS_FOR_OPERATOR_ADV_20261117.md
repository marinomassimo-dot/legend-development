## Read the conclusions below with these five limits (added 2026-11-18 after the operator's follow-through note; under targeted review by SCI, ADV-046)
1. **Concentrations.** Every comparison with human plasma uses the highest value found in the sources read. That is not a biological limit: total plasma, free fraction, CSF, tissue and bath concentrations are different quantities; infants and status epilepticus are unmeasured; so 'above the highest value found' is a statement about what was found, not about what any tissue receives.
2. **Genotype.** 'Significant in one genotype and not in another' is a description, not a treatment-by-genotype interaction test; nothing here claims an interaction for lithium or ethosuximide.
3. **Priors.** SCI's priors are subjective, written before the data; they are not validated predictive probabilities and carry no calibration claim (on 13 preregistered weights of the model comparison SCI scored -9.81 against -9.01 for coin flips: worse than a coin).
4. **Negative results.** An unfavourable result weakens the specific hypothesis tested in that preparation; it does not refute every intervention on the drug class. Rule used (SCI): one agent at one exposure in one preparation is an agent-level result; a class-level demotion needs two structurally different agents at verified depth.
5. **Comparisons.** Ketamine, lithium and ethosuximide differ in preparation, endpoint, direct evidence and transfer; a statement that one is better supported refers to the named axis only, never a general superiority.

## The answer in plain words
1. **Is ketamine a repurposing candidate for the reference genotype class?** Only in a narrow, hypothesis-level sense. No experiment has ever tested the effect of ketamine in any WWOX system (cell, tissue, animal or person); the only ketamine exposure of a Wwox-null animal that the repository holds is incidental anaesthesia in Repudi 2021, with no ketamine-free arm. The slice finding on which the report builds ("bursting is NMDA-receptor dependent", Breton 2021) was obtained with **d-APV, a different drug that blocks the receptor at another site**, in one preparation from one laboratory (n = 5 slices, 3 animals). Ketamine is therefore a candidate by **class** (a use-dependent NMDA-receptor pore blocker) and by **non-exclusion** in one niche, the acute rescue of refractory status epilepticus, where it has the best randomised support of its class (a first-line add-on trial, not refractory status epilepticus) and is one of several options for any aetiology, and by nothing specific to WWOX. The report's statement "mechanism-matched in a way nothing else in current practice is" does not stand.
2. **As a chronic treatment?** Not supported. The memantine half of the proposed sequence rests on one crossover trial in mixed childhood DEE, readable only as an abstract and a registry entry (analysis unpaired in the report, composite responder, harms unread), plus single cases in another disease (ATP1A2), in one of which memantine was added to ketamine, not given after it.
3. **Are there better alternatives, and which?** **None is better supported on WWOX-system evidence; every row is thin, and the classes are not comparable across the other axes, so the ranking depends on the endpoint.** In the rescue niche ketamine has the best randomised support of the NMDA-receptor class (one first-line trial in paediatric generalized convulsive status epilepticus: 144 children, ketamine plus midazolam against placebo plus midazolam, cessation at 5 minutes 76% against 21%, abstract only; and one randomised trial in refractory status epilepticus, halted early), while the real comparators (midazolam and barbiturate-class infusions) have a larger observational base, no randomised paediatric trial in refractory status epilepticus, and no WWOX data; none of this involves WWOX. For chronic seizure control the approved agents with randomised paediatric evidence in other aetiologies (cannabidiol, fenfluramine, stiripentol, ganaxolone, ACTH and vigabatrin, everolimus, the ketogenic diet) are far better supported than memantine or ketamine, but each is a transfer from another disease. The only drugs with in vivo data in a Wwox-null mouse are lithium and ethosuximide (one experiment, one laboratory, a provoked-seizure assay, N = 6 knockout mice in the key panel; lithium's effect was also significant in wild type, ethosuximide's was significant in the knockout and not in wild type or heterozygotes: a description by genotype, not an interaction test: Cheng 2020's reported figures allow none (group sizes, mean and SEM of a stage per minute, an unspecified one-way ANOVA; the not-significant ethosuximide groups had the larger N and a baseline stage near the floor of a 0-to-6 scale)). Gene addition (AAV9-WWOX) has by far the strongest preclinical rescue and is outside the pharmacological question.
4. **The hardest harm** is not the one the report stressed. It is that the drug class can **provoke seizures** (electrographic seizures after ketamine in 26 of 94 people with epilepsy and 0 of 39 without, 3 of 5 children with epilepsy in one small old review; ketamine at higher doses caused seizures in a GRIN2D gain-of-function mouse; increased seizure frequency in three individuals (four in the Discussion) of 19 on memantine in a GRIN gain-of-function cohort in which 14 of 19 improved; cause unclear). Short exposures in children are quantified and low (serious events 0.33% in 58,752 sedated children); prolonged exposures in children are known only from case reports and intensive-care series, and human developmental neurotoxicity is not established in either direction.
5. **What to do with it** (decision-level, not clinical): the candidacy is worth **one cheap discriminating experiment**, not a belief. The experiment is the one the team converged on: in the Breton knockout slice, a use-dependent pore blocker against d-APV at matched fractional block, burst rate at 30, 60 and 120 minutes after washout against time-matched vehicle, and the Ih sag in the same slices (it separates "blockable by the drug class" from "d-APV-specific", disinhibition, rebound and a non-NMDAR route). It needs a laboratory (operator item). A ready-to-send package exists for the operator to use from the operator's own account (cover note of about 150 words, one-page summary, neutral candidate-group table from the repository's own records; every sentence the operator must decide or fill in is tagged; no contact detail was looked up; `experiment_brief_presidio013_sci_20261116/outreach_adv042_sci_20261118/OPERATOR_OUTREACH_PACKAGE_SCI_20261118.md`). Its author flags what is not ready: the bath-concentration paragraph reads as a clinical-range statement that points to one drug and is better deleted, letting the laboratory choose the agent and the series; the 120-minute read has no hashed prior (the priors cover 30 to 60 minutes); the 1.8-fold overshoot is one untested figure reading; mouse-line availability, bath conditions and the choice of pore blocker are the laboratory's open items; nothing says any listed group is able or willing. The host agrees with deleting the bath-band paragraph from the page that is sent. A stage-2 in vivo test in neuronal Wwox-deletion mice is the report's own proposal and the team agrees with it.

## The evidence in three layers, with harms beside every benefit
**Held (DATO):** (a) neuron-specific Wwox-knockout neocortical slices burst (36 of 42 knockout slices, 0 of 11 wild type); d-APV 50 uM abolished the bursts; carbenoxolone reduced them. (b) Ketamine inhibits the Ih current in cortical pyramidal neurons of 14- to 22-day-old mice at 20 uM, absent in HCN1-knockout neurons: the same age window and the same property (sag) that Breton found raised in the knockout neurons, so a ketamine effect in this slice could be HCN1-mediated and d-APV cannot tell. (c) Low-dose ketamine effects in adult mouse visual cortex run through GluN2A on parvalbumin interneurons: the disinhibition route, against a slice that already has smaller sIPSCs. (d) Paediatric ketamine in refractory status epilepticus: uncontrolled series (Kuki 15 of 18 with more than 50% seizure reduction, 3 of 18 transient events needing intervention; Rosati 6 of 9 resolution with midazolam add-on and no serious events; DeVine 3 infants), pooled cessation about 50 to 51% in two reviews, a retrospective first-line comparison with termination 61% vs 28% and unadjusted survival to discharge 55% vs 82%, one randomised first-line trial in generalized convulsive status epilepticus (144 children, ketamine plus midazolam vs placebo plus midazolam, cessation at 5 minutes 76% vs 21%, fewer repeat midazolam doses and intubations, no significant difference in other outcomes; abstract only), and one randomised trial in refractory status epilepticus halted early (10 enrolled in the report, 57 in the registry; 2 of 5 vs 2 of 5). (e) Memantine: one crossover trial (abstract and registry), label statements (seizure disorders "not systematically evaluated"; neuronal degeneration in juvenile rats at 15 mg/kg/day on postnatal days 8 to 17, no-effect 8; neuronal lesions after a single dose on day 14), seizure worsening in three named GRIN gain-of-function individuals. **Harms beside these:** drug-provoked seizures (above), infusions longer than the 3-hour caution of the label in young children, hypotension 13 of 24 in intensive-care infusion series, hepatobiliary and bladder reports only as case reports, withdrawal, and unmeasured developmental effects.

**Transfer (INFERENZA):** from a class (d-APV, memantine, other pore blockers) to ketamine; from rodent hours-long neonatal paradigms to any clinical regimen; from other aetiologies of status epilepticus and from GRIN-gene disorders (where the drug targets the lesion) to WWOX.

**Hypothesis (IPOTESI):** that a pore blocker suppresses the knockout bursts at matched block depth (SCI's subjective prior 0.70, written before any data and not a validated predictive probability); that block at shallow depth raises bursting through disinhibition (0.20); that bursting rebounds above baseline after washout (0.30; the held 1.8-fold overshoot is one untested point; the oscillator alone shows no overshoot (early-to-pre ratio 0.94 to 1.02, a miss SCI recorded) and does not reproduce the 1.8-fold bar; only the homeostat toy, whose slow gain gives rebound by construction, overshoots, so the lean to rebound is an assumption of the toy, not a result of the model); that a lasting benefit exists (0.15); that any transient benefit seen clinically arises from the sedation or co-medication context, not the NMDA receptor (a rival that no slice experiment can reach).

## What would change the ranking (discriminating, in order)
1. **Matched-block slice experiment** (above), with the prediction table across five rival readings (transient NMDAR suppression, non-NMDAR route, disinhibition, rebound, sedation context). A pass unparks the pore-blocker arm; a fail on burst suppression at matched depth, or a rise at shallow block, weakens the NMDAR rationale tested in this preparation (it does not by itself refute any intervention on the class) and leaves ketamine's candidacy resting on the rescue-niche evidence alone. SCI's model comparison (ADV-046 part B; a hypothesis generator, not evidence; one run, one parameter set, the held models unchanged) changes the design: a matched depth series (about 0.1, 0.2 and 0.3 of the evoked response) for BOTH the pore blocker and d-APV instead of one deep point (an Ih component puts a pore blocker below d-APV at equal intermediate depth, a disinhibition component above it, and at deep block the Ih cells sit at the floor); reads at about 5 and 15 minutes as well as 30, 60 and 120 after washout (the toy's adaptation rebound is over within 30 minutes); paired sag; a post-washout fall is called lasting only if the evoked delayed response is back within 10%. A deep-block failure to suppress bursting is not by itself a rejection of the NMDAR route (a large disinhibition component gives the same observable). Not represented in the model: block kinetics, any sag variable, an anchored disinhibition coupling; the PV premise behind disinhibition is transferred, not measured (IT, RP-019: a marker-positive PV count 44% lower in the hippocampus of 2-week-old global knockouts, n = 7 against 7, and, separately, a smaller sIPSC, 27.5 against 57.3 pA, in neocortical layer 2/3 of neuron-specific knockouts; no animal or cell has both; no perineuronal-net, mIPSC or evoked-IPSC measurement was found).
2. **Spontaneous-seizure Wwox-null mice with continuous EEG** and slices from the same animals, pore blocker against a non-NMDAR sedative at matched sedation depth and vehicle (the report's experiment 1 with the sedation control it lacks).
3. **Any report of a WWOX or WOREE child exposed to ketamine** (none exists in PubMed or in the opened full texts; a registry query is an operator item, because it is outreach).
4. **The closed sources** (human-visit list, now 37 plus 7 PDFs): the memantine trial full text (design, paired analysis, harms), Kuki 2024 and Rosati 2012 (stratum composition, adverse events), the SAGE and OUP collision papers. Human plasma: sub-anaesthetic adult volunteers 85 to 248 ng/mL (0.36 to 1.04 uM; CSF median 42 ng/mL, 0.18 uM); paediatric anaesthesia peaks up to about 2,100 ng/mL (8.9 uM); one adult on a prolonged ECMO infusion about 1,019 ng/mL (4.3 uM). The Ih action (recombinant HCN1-HCN2 EC50 8.2 to 15.6 uM; 20 uM tested in neurons) lies at or above the highest plasma values found in the reports opened (the 20 uM test in native neurons is higher), is not shown at the sub-anaesthetic volunteer exposures, and is not measured in infants, in tissue or in status epilepticus; the authors of the HCN1 paper themselves call the recombinant range 'clinically relevant', so the pharmacokinetic reading and the primary paper disagree about the same quantity and this synthesis does not settle it. Plasma values do not weaken the HCN1 route as a hypothesis about the slice, because the bath concentration is the experimenter's choice; they bear only on whether a clinical exposure could reach it, which is unmeasured. S-ketamine abolishing seizure-like events at 100 uM is a mechanism observation made at 11 times the highest plasma value found (a comparison with what was found, not a biological limit). Not stated by any source found: free brain concentration in any human, any concentration in infants, any concentration in status epilepticus or a multi-day infusion beyond the one ECMO case, total versus free. For the slice experiment: test bath concentrations around 0.4 to 1 uM, 2 to 4 uM and 8 to 10 uM, with 20 uM only as a labelled higher probe, and record the concentration that gives deep block; the laboratory chooses the agent and the series, and if deep block needs a concentration well above the plasma values found, the clinical reach of that result is unknown rather than excluded. That refractory status epilepticus is a characteristic setting of the genotype class is NOT supported by the held record (IT, ADV-037; the host re-counted the child table: status epilepticus of any kind in 9 of 42 held children, from 2 to 3 reports of the 12 that contribute children, onset at or before 12 months or unknown; 0 of 6 rows with the word 'refractory' also state status epilepticus; intensive-care wording on fewer than 3 children); the tables record semiology and exposures, not a census of status epilepticus, so the rescue niche here is a general paediatric setting, not a documented feature of the class. Metabolic and ocular harms against the Wwox-null hypoglycaemia and the optic atrophy of the class (IT56-Q13, one producer, abstract level, no second reader): undeterminable; no source links ketamine or memantine to a WWOX-class phenotype (0 hits on two routes); ketamine's ocular effects are labelled (diplopia, nystagmus, raised intraocular pressure, paediatric rises small), its metabolic effects are mostly animal and confounded, and there is no paediatric hypoglycaemia series. The Repudi 2021 in vivo recording under ketamine/medetomidine anaesthesia kept the genotype contrast: it is not a test of ketamine, but it is the one held observation that could point the other way. ## Update 2026-11-18 (host clock about 08:55Z): memantine and the other NMDA-receptor-directed agents (ADV-050, SCI; dated; the text above is kept as written)
* **Is memantine, or another NMDA-receptor-directed agent, more promising than ketamine?** Not in general; it depends on the endpoint, and on none of the three endpoints does any agent have WWOX-system evidence. Acute rescue of status epilepticus: ketamine, on evidence level (a randomised first-line trial in another setting, a halted randomised trial in refractory status epilepticus; memantine has nothing here). Chronic seizure control: memantine, on evidence level only (one abstract-level randomised crossover trial in childhood developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, against case reports and ICU series for ketamine), with a seizure-worsening signal in some individuals with gain-of-function GRIN variants and a label that says seizure disorders were not systematically evaluated; better hypothesised tolerability is not efficacy. Development: neither. Memantine is a comparator or a probe, not a priority candidate.
* **The kinetics premise, previously assumed, is now read from primaries at the level of cultures and recombinant cells** (SCI; one delegate, 74 rows; 14 pivotal quotes re-matched; a blind verifier on 13 claims: 11 supported, 2 partly, corrected; the host checked one pivotal quotation independently at PubMed): both drugs need the channel open to block and to unblock; trapping is partial for both by the numbers (86 and 71 per cent in one culture study); memantine alone has a second site; Mg2+ weakens both about 16-fold at GluN2A and GluN2B; the two are close but not identical in kinetics. No source gives these properties in an acute slice or at 35 C, and no primary states them for d-APV: the transfer to the slice stays an inference for every agent.

## Update 2026-11-18 (host clock about 09:55Z): vigabatrin, the leading candidate on chronic control in children by class case data (ADV-056, IT; dated; the text above is kept as written)
* **What the class data can bear: only "reported".** Vigabatrin exposure is documented in 22 children in 10 reports (21 to 26 distinct children on the held bytes, after a blind recount that agreed on every count and corrected the bounds); no child is documented as receiving it alone with a known full regimen; a response is stated for 11 of the 22 (8 responses, 3 non-responses) and every response is qualified (partial, transient, or clinical while EEG still recorded attacks); the four classes of the card overlap in the same children, so counts are not additive. * **The harm side is better characterised than the benefit side, and it transfers.** The label's boxed visual-field warning describes irreversible loss with onset from weeks to years and no exposure known to be free of risk; in infants loss may not be detected until it is severe, and the monitoring tests depend on cooperation. ## Update 2026-11-18 (host clock about 10:05Z): the age window of each candidate's effect in young rodents (ADV-054 unit 1, SCI; abstract level; dated; the text above is kept as written)
