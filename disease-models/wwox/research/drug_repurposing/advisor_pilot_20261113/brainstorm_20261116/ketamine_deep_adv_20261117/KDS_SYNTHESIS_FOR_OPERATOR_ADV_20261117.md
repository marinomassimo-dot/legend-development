## The answer in plain words
1. **Is ketamine a repurposing candidate for the reference genotype class?** Only in a narrow, hypothesis-level sense. No experiment has ever tested the effect of ketamine in any WWOX system (cell, tissue, animal or person); the only ketamine exposure of a Wwox-null animal that the repository holds is incidental anaesthesia in Repudi 2021, with no ketamine-free arm. The slice finding on which the report builds ("bursting is NMDA-receptor dependent", Breton 2021) was obtained with **d-APV, a different drug that blocks the receptor at another site**, in one preparation from one laboratory (n = 5 slices, 3 animals). Ketamine is therefore a candidate by **class** (a use-dependent NMDA-receptor pore blocker) and by **non-exclusion** in one niche, the acute rescue of refractory status epilepticus, where it has the best randomised support of its class (a first-line add-on trial, not refractory status epilepticus) and is one of several options for any aetiology, and by nothing specific to WWOX. The report's statement "mechanism-matched in a way nothing else in current practice is" does not stand.
2. **As a chronic treatment?** Not supported. The memantine half of the proposed sequence rests on one crossover trial in mixed childhood DEE, readable only as an abstract and a registry entry (analysis unpaired in the report, composite responder, harms unread), plus single cases in another disease (ATP1A2), in one of which memantine was added to ketamine, not given after it.
3. **Are there better alternatives, and which?** **None is better supported on WWOX-system evidence; every row is thin, and the classes are not comparable across the other axes, so the ranking depends on the endpoint.** In the rescue niche ketamine has the best randomised support of the NMDA-receptor class (one first-line trial in paediatric generalized convulsive status epilepticus: 144 children, ketamine plus midazolam against placebo plus midazolam, cessation at 5 minutes 76% against 21%, abstract only; and one randomised trial in refractory status epilepticus, halted early), while the real comparators (midazolam and barbiturate-class infusions) have a larger observational base, no randomised paediatric trial in refractory status epilepticus, and no WWOX data; none of this involves WWOX. For chronic seizure control the approved agents with randomised paediatric evidence in other aetiologies (cannabidiol, fenfluramine, stiripentol, ganaxolone, ACTH and vigabatrin, everolimus, the ketogenic diet) are far better supported than memantine or ketamine, but each is a transfer from another disease. The only drugs with in vivo data in a Wwox-null mouse are lithium and ethosuximide (one experiment, one laboratory, a provoked-seizure assay, N = 6 knockout mice in the key panel; lithium's effect was also significant in wild type, ethosuximide's was significant in the knockout and not in wild type or heterozygotes). Gene addition (AAV9-WWOX) has by far the strongest preclinical rescue and is outside the pharmacological question.
4. **The hardest harm** is not the one the report stressed. It is that the drug class can **provoke seizures** (electrographic seizures after ketamine in 26 of 94 people with epilepsy and 0 of 39 without, 3 of 5 children with epilepsy in one small old review; ketamine at higher doses caused seizures in a GRIN2D gain-of-function mouse; increased seizure frequency in three individuals (four in the Discussion) of 19 on memantine in a GRIN gain-of-function cohort in which 14 of 19 improved; cause unclear). Short exposures in children are quantified and low (serious events 0.33% in 58,752 sedated children); prolonged exposures in children are known only from case reports and intensive-care series, and human developmental neurotoxicity is not established in either direction.
5. **What to do with it** (decision-level, not clinical): the candidacy is worth **one cheap discriminating experiment**, not a belief. The experiment is the one the team converged on: in the Breton knockout slice, a use-dependent pore blocker against d-APV at matched fractional block, burst rate at 30, 60 and 120 minutes after washout against time-matched vehicle, and the Ih sag in the same slices (it separates "blockable by the drug class" from "d-APV-specific", disinhibition, rebound and a non-NMDAR route). It needs a laboratory (operator item); the bath concentrations to test are set by the pharmacokinetic reading below. A stage-2 in vivo test in neuronal Wwox-deletion mice is the report's own proposal and the team agrees with it.

## The evidence in three layers, with harms beside every benefit
**Held (DATO):** (a) neuron-specific Wwox-knockout neocortical slices burst (36 of 42 knockout slices, 0 of 11 wild type); d-APV 50 uM abolished the bursts; carbenoxolone reduced them. (b) Ketamine inhibits the Ih current in cortical pyramidal neurons of 14- to 22-day-old mice at 20 uM, absent in HCN1-knockout neurons: the same age window and the same property (sag) that Breton found raised in the knockout neurons, so a ketamine effect in this slice could be HCN1-mediated and d-APV cannot tell. (c) Low-dose ketamine effects in adult mouse visual cortex run through GluN2A on parvalbumin interneurons: the disinhibition route, against a slice that already has smaller sIPSCs. (d) Paediatric ketamine in refractory status epilepticus: uncontrolled series (Kuki 15 of 18 with more than 50% seizure reduction, 3 of 18 transient events needing intervention; Rosati 6 of 9 resolution with midazolam add-on and no serious events; DeVine 3 infants), pooled cessation about 50 to 51% in two reviews, a retrospective first-line comparison with termination 61% vs 28% and unadjusted survival to discharge 55% vs 82%, one randomised first-line trial in generalized convulsive status epilepticus (144 children, ketamine plus midazolam vs placebo plus midazolam, cessation at 5 minutes 76% vs 21%, fewer repeat midazolam doses and intubations, no significant difference in other outcomes; abstract only), and one randomised trial in refractory status epilepticus halted early (10 enrolled in the report, 57 in the registry; 2 of 5 vs 2 of 5). (e) Memantine: one crossover trial (abstract and registry), label statements (seizure disorders "not systematically evaluated"; neuronal degeneration in juvenile rats at 15 mg/kg/day on postnatal days 8 to 17, no-effect 8; neuronal lesions after a single dose on day 14), seizure worsening in three named GRIN gain-of-function individuals. **Harms beside these:** drug-provoked seizures (above), infusions longer than the 3-hour caution of the label in young children, hypotension 13 of 24 in intensive-care infusion series, hepatobiliary and bladder reports only as case reports, withdrawal, and unmeasured developmental effects.

**Transfer (INFERENZA):** from a class (d-APV, memantine, other pore blockers) to ketamine; from rodent hours-long neonatal paradigms to any clinical regimen; from other aetiologies of status epilepticus and from GRIN-gene disorders (where the drug targets the lesion) to WWOX.

**Hypothesis (IPOTESI):** that a pore blocker suppresses the knockout bursts at matched block depth (SCI's prior 0.70, written before any data); that block at shallow depth raises bursting through disinhibition (0.20); that bursting rebounds above baseline after washout (0.30; the held 1.8-fold overshoot is one untested point; the model and the toy both lean to rebound); that a lasting benefit exists (0.15); that any transient benefit seen clinically arises from the sedation or co-medication context, not the NMDA receptor (a rival that no slice experiment can reach).

## What would change the ranking (discriminating, in order)
1. **Matched-block slice experiment** (above), with the prediction table across five rival readings (transient NMDAR suppression, non-NMDAR route, disinhibition, rebound, sedation context). A pass unparks the pore-blocker arm; a fail on burst suppression at matched depth, or a rise at shallow block, removes the NMDAR rationale and leaves ketamine as one anaesthetic among comparators.
2. **Spontaneous-seizure Wwox-null mice with continuous EEG** and slices from the same animals, pore blocker against a non-NMDAR sedative at matched sedation depth and vehicle (the report's experiment 1 with the sedation control it lacks).
3. **Any report of a WWOX or WOREE child exposed to ketamine** (none exists in PubMed or in the opened full texts; a registry query is an operator item, because it is outreach).
4. **The closed sources** (human-visit list, now 37 plus 7 PDFs): the memantine trial full text (design, paired analysis, harms), Kuki 2024 and Rosati 2012 (stratum composition, adverse events), the SAGE and OUP collision papers. A bounded attempt by SCI (ADV-040) to find lawful free routes for these three failed on every route an agent can use (Europe PMC, PMC converter, OpenAlex, Semantic Scholar, Crossref, preprint searches; the publisher pages and the CORE and BASE services returned challenge or key-required pages and were not bypassed). ## Limits and what remains unverified
SCI's blind check of IT's K-level verdicts and of the lane C table is done; IT's dated corrections to lane C were requested and had not landed at the time of writing, so lane C is used here with those corrections applied by the host where this document states them. Most clinical sources are at abstract level because the full texts are closed (Cloudflare, embargo); a claim from an abstract is labelled as such. The general pharmacology of use dependence, trapping and subunit selectivity is ASSUMED in the mechanism lane (two primary papers returned a reCAPTCHA page, not bypassed). **Concentrations (SCI, ADV-036):** the ex vivo ketamine concentrations are mostly above what was measured in people. Human plasma: sub-anaesthetic adult volunteers 85 to 248 ng/mL (0.36 to 1.04 uM; CSF median 42 ng/mL, 0.18 uM); paediatric anaesthesia peaks up to about 2,100 ng/mL (8.9 uM); one adult on a prolonged ECMO infusion about 1,019 ng/mL (4.3 uM). The Ih action (recombinant HCN1-HCN2 EC50 8.2 to 15.6 uM; 20 uM tested in neurons) is therefore reachable only at anaesthetic-range peaks, not shown at sub-anaesthetic exposures and not measured in status epilepticus: the HCN1 rival is WEAKENED, not dropped; S-ketamine abolishing seizure-like events at 100 uM (11 times the highest human value found) is a mechanism observation at a supra-clinical concentration. Not stated by any source found: free brain concentration in any human, any concentration in infants, any concentration in status epilepticus or a multi-day infusion beyond the one ECMO case, total versus free. For the slice experiment: test bath concentrations around 0.4 to 1 uM, 2 to 4 uM and 8 to 10 uM, with 20 uM only as a labelled supra-range probe, and record the concentration that gives deep block (if it exceeds about 10 uM the arm's clinical translation is out of range). That refractory status epilepticus is a characteristic setting of the genotype class is NOT supported by the held record (IT, ADV-037; the host re-counted the child table: status epilepticus of any kind in 9 of 42 held children, from 2 to 3 reports of the 12 that contribute children, onset at or before 12 months or unknown; 0 of 6 rows with the word 'refractory' also state status epilepticus; intensive-care wording on fewer than 3 children); the tables record semiology and exposures, not a census of status epilepticus, so the rescue niche here is a general paediatric setting, not a documented feature of the class. Metabolic and ocular harms against the Wwox-null hypoglycaemia and the optic atrophy of the class (IT56-Q13, one producer, abstract level, no second reader): undeterminable; no source links ketamine or memantine to a WWOX-class phenotype (0 hits on two routes); ketamine's ocular effects are labelled (diplopia, nystagmus, raised intraocular pressure, paediatric rises small), its metabolic effects are mostly animal and confounded, and there is no paediatric hypoglycaemia series. The Repudi 2021 in vivo recording under ketamine/medetomidine anaesthesia kept the genotype contrast: it is not a test of ketamine, but it is the one held observation that could point the other way.
