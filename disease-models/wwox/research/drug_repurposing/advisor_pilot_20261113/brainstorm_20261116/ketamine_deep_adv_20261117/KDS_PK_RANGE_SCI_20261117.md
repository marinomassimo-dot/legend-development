## Answer first
1. **The ex vivo concentrations of lane D are not in the range measured in patients at sub-anaesthetic exposure, and are at or above the top of what was measured at anaesthetic-range exposure.** Highest human plasma value found: about 2,100 ng/mL (8.9 uM, paediatric nasal peak); the next ones: 1,860 +/- 883 ng/mL (7.8 uM, S-ketamine, paediatric i.v. peak at about 3 min) and 1,019 ng/mL (4.3 uM, one adult on ECMO at steady state during a prolonged infusion for another indication). Sub-anaesthetic adult volunteers: 85 to 248 ng/mL (0.36 to 1.04 uM), median 113 (0.475 uM); CSF median 42 ng/mL (0.18 uM).
2. **Lane D's Ih rival: weakened, not dropped.** The recombinant HCN1-HCN2 EC50 for racemic ketamine (Chen 2009: 8.2 uM on the voltage shift, 15.6 uM on current amplitude = 1,950 to 3,700 ng/mL) is 0.9 to 1.8 times the highest human peak found and 17 to 33 times the sub-anaesthetic median; the S-ketamine values (4.1 and 7.4 uM = 975 to 1,760 ng/mL) lie **inside** the paediatric i.v. peak range. The native-neuron test concentration (20 uM = 4,750 ng/mL) is 2.3 times the highest human peak found and 4.7 times the ECMO steady state. So the Ih action is clinically reachable only at **anaesthetic-range peak plasma exposures**, and is **not** shown at sub-anaesthetic exposures; whether it is reached during a prolonged infusion for status epilepticus is **not measured** (no status-epilepticus concentration was found).
3. **Riepe 2026's 100 uM (23,800 ng/mL) is out of range:** 11 times the highest human peak found, 23 times the ECMO steady state, 210 times the sub-anaesthetic median, and of the same order as high-dose rat serum (10,000 to 30,000 ng/mL = 42 to 126 uM, total, Sarkar 2026). The ex vivo support for "ketamine-class agents abolish NMDAR-driven seizure-like events" stays as a mechanism observation at a supra-clinical concentration; it does not support a clinical-range claim.
4. **Two corrections run in opposite directions and are both unmeasured:** free fraction lowers the free concentration (reported binding 10 to 30% in a review sentence, 57 to 70% for (R)-ketamine in one in vitro study: free fraction 30 to 90%, INFERENZA), widening each gap by 1.1 to 3.3 times; brain exposure may exceed plasma in the rat ((R)-ketamine brain-to-plasma AUC ratio 1.57 to 2.25, Wang 2025; a rodent transfer), narrowing it by up to 2.3 times; the one human brain proxy (lumbar CSF, healthy adults, sub-anaesthetic) is **below** plasma (CSF:plasma AUC ratio 0.917).
5. **For the slice experiment (brief 13.11):** test the pore-blocker class over three bands, expressed as bath concentration (a slice bath has no plasma protein, so the bath value corresponds to a free concentration): about 0.4 to 1 uM (sub-anaesthetic plasma range), about 2 to 4 uM (adult/paediatric anaesthetic-range range of the lower measured peaks), about 8 to 10 uM (top of the measured paediatric peak); 20 uM only as a labelled supra-range probe and 100 uM not at all as a clinical-range arm. The brief's matched-block calibration should **record the concentration that achieves deep block**; if deep block needs more than about 10 uM, the arm's clinical translation is out of range and must be written so. These are **bath concentrations for an ex vivo experiment, not an exposure statement for any person.**
