## 1. B1: what Breton 2021 showed about NMDAR and gap-junction dependence (DATO unless marked)
**Source:** Breton et al. 2021 (PMID 34634460), held PMC XML (156,866 bytes, sha256 `934b4e1a42ac19f5cd8912994fb171beabdeb94a6631b23d9a383dab1db906aa`), text and the held Fig. 3 image read.
| Item | What the paper states |
|---|---|
| Preparation | neuron-specific Wwox knockout (Synapsin-Cre; S-KO, S-HT, S-WT); "Both male and female mice aged P13 to P17"; coronal neocortical and hippocampal slices 400 to 500 um; ACSF with **1.3 mM Mg2+**, 3.5 mM K+, 1.5 mM Ca2+, recorded at 35 C; local field potential in superficial layers (layer II/III) and layer V |
| Spontaneous bursting | S-KO 36 of 42 slices (24 animals); S-HT 4 of 23 (14 animals); S-WT 0 of 11 (7 animals); hippocampal slices indistinguishable from controls |
| Agents and concentrations | d-APV 50 uM; carbenoxolone (CBX) 100 uM; brilliant blue FCF (BB-FCF, a selective pannexin blocker) 10 uM; TTX 1 uM for miniature recordings |
| d-APV | "Blocking glutamatergic neurotransmission with d-APV (50 uM) eliminated the spontaneous bursting events"; text: on washout "the frequency, duration and peak-to-trough amplitude returned to normal"; n = 51 bursts, 5 slices, 3 animals |
| CBX | "decreased the frequency of the events by 87% (p = 0.008) and their duration by 18% (p = 0.0274), with no change to their amplitude"; **not reversed on washout**; n = 36 bursts, 4 slices, 3 animals |
| BB-FCF | effects "not recapitulated"; affected 10 of 25 bursts, "a subset ... may be influenced by pannexin 1"; n = 73 bursts, 6 slices, 3 animals |
| Evoked delayed response | a layer V stimulus evokes a delayed negative deflection in layer II/III, larger in S-KO, "NMDA receptor mediated, as addition of d-APV reversibly suppressed the response" (Supplementary Fig. S3C; not held) |
| What the authors say about CBX | "CBX may not be specific to gap junctions. First, it could block NMDA receptors (Chepkova et al., 2008), which could partially account for our observations" (and cite Rouach 2003 that CBX network block "is not mediated by an action of gap junctions") |
**The held figure (gr3, image-read by me; approximate to about 0.1; values are normalised to baseline):** frequency: d-APV 0 (no bar); CBX about 0.1; BB-FCF about 2.5 (error bar about +-0.9); **washout of d-APV about 1.8 (error bar about +-1.0); washout of BB-FCF about 2.6**. Duration under CBX about 0.57 and amplitude about 0.6.
**Findings from reading text and figure together:**
1. **"Abolished" is accurate for d-APV and "reduced" is the accurate word for gap junctions.** The abstract and the input say "NMDAR and gap junction dependent"; the results heading says "Gap junctional blockade reduces, whereas glutamatergic blockade abolishes".
2. **The gap-junction half rests on CBX alone**, a non-specific agent, with the pannexin blocker not reproducing it and no matched-effect control (open as IT40-Q02).
3. **The washout overshoot is in the figure, not in the text.** The text says "returned to normal"; the figure's d-APV-washout frequency bar sits at about 1.8 times baseline, **with an error bar that reaches about 0.8 to 2.8, i.e. includes 1.0**, and the BB-FCF bar, an agent with no claimed effect on bursts, sits at about 2.5 with a similarly wide bar. No test of the overshoot is reported; the unit is bursts pooled from 4 to 6 slices of 3 animals; the normalisation basis is not stated. So the held "about 1.85 times baseline" is **one noisy, untested point estimate**, a hint of rebound and not a result.
4. **Text and figure disagree on CBX**: text duration -18% and "no change to amplitude"; figure duration about -43% and amplitude about -40%.
5. **What the preparation allows for a use-dependent pore blocker (INFERENZA, not measured):** d-APV acts at the glutamate site and needs no channel opening, so its abolition shows that NMDAR activation is **necessary** for the bursts in 1.3 mM Mg2+ (necessity, not sufficiency, and not necessarily NMDAR gain: loss of Mg2+ relief would stop any NMDAR-regenerative burst, IT40-Q01). A pore blocker blocks only open channels: a bursting network supplies openings, so block should build with activity, and the evoked delayed response is the assay that can match block depth. Trapping, off-rate, subunit preference and Mg2+ competition decide whether the block persists after washout (which bears on whether an overshoot appears) and are **not** tested in this paper. The paper gives no ketamine, memantine or other pore-blocker datum.

**Counts after reading:** strict match **2 of 9** (36334642, 41618072); partly 5; **no 2**. The input's "6 of 6 inhibit" is not reproduced: of the six, one has no ketamine arm, one reports the opposite direction, and three report restoration of a model-induced change or an unattributed increase.
**Does the 6/6 versus 3/3 split by duration survive? No, not as a finding about duration** (INFERENZA from the rows):
1. **Exposure duration is confounded with the model.** Every "inhibit" paper with a disease model uses a stress or depression model (SPS twice, corticosterone, chronic unpredictable stress), where the model itself moves p-GSK-3beta and ketamine is reported to move it back; every "activate" paper uses healthy tissue. Model, not duration, is the cleaner separator.
2. **Dose range is confounded**: "activate" side 100 mg/kg x 7 days, a 4-hour anaesthesia session, 20 mg/kg x 6; "inhibit" side 10 mg/kg, 0.1 mg/kg with guanosine, cells. Two of the three "activate" papers are anaesthetic-range or hours-long exposures in PND7 pups.
3. **The duration labels are not the authors' own.** 31263401 and 33733293 are single sessions of hours, not "repeated or prolonged" days; the same PND7 paradigm is called **"acute exposure"** by its own authors (38840775: "we demonstrate that acute exposure to ketamine decreases DISC1 expression ... decreases in phosphorylated GSK-3beta, which denotes increased GSK-3beta activity"). Only **one** study (41618072) is a days-scale repeated exposure with a measured p-Ser9, so the effective n of the "prolonged" side is 1, not 3.
4. **Independence fails**: 39167905 and 37392896 are one group, model and dose; 31263401 and 33733293 share the PND7 hours-long paradigm. The p = 1/84 arithmetic is right for a perfect 6/0 against 0/3 split and is meaningless for dependent, relabelled studies.
5. **The "activation" label is a phosphorylation proxy read beside cell-death markers** (31263401: in vivo readout was caspase-3 only; 41618072: one of eight blotted proteins, total Gsk-3beta unchanged, no kinase assay). A fall of p-Ser9 in apoptosing cells may be consequence, not cause (IPOTESI); these papers cannot separate them.
**Reports the input did not retrieve** (bounded search, 10 query strings logged with hit counts in the producer file; "not found by these queries" is not an absence claim): **opposite or null to the input's pattern**: 39158787 (aged male rats, acute and 7 daily doses, no change in the p-Ser9 ratio in PFC or hippocampus; full text read); 23548331 ("despite previous reports of ketamine-induced inhibition of GSK-3, we were unable to detect regulation of its activity", single sub-anaesthetic dose, abstract); 29763645 (HT22 cells, no change in p-Ser9, combination of subthreshold concentrations); **opposite** to "acute inhibits": 24603008 (a single anaesthetic-range dose in adult rats **lowered** p-Ser9; abstract), 32705526 (a single administration raised GSK-3beta activity at high doses; abstract), 38840775 and 40024338 (neonatal, hours-long "acute" exposure lowered pGSK-3beta), 29218434 (NSC-derived neurons, p-Ser9 down, GSK-3beta not GSK-3alpha). **Pattern, IPOTESI only:** the lowered p-Ser9 reports cluster with neonatal age and with high or anaesthetic-range single exposures, and nulls with 5 to 15 mg/kg in adult and aged rats, so **dose, age and model are the candidate drivers, not days of dosing**; a designed test would be needed. **Where the input is right on this arm:** one adult study (41618072) does show a fall of p-Ser9 with apoptosis markers after 7 daily doses in healthy mice, so "age alone does not explain it" is fair for that paper; and the input itself says the set is vote counting.

## 4. K5 and K11 in brief
**K5 (GSK-3beta matters in WWOX):** Cheng 2020 supports a Wwox-null GSK-3beta signal (p-Ser9 down at P20 in three regions) and a lithium effect on PTZ-induced seizures. It is a global knockout, a provoked-threshold assay, one laboratory, and lithium has other targets (INFERENZA). It is a reason to measure p-Ser9 in a ketamine arm, not a reason to expect benefit from raising it with an NMDAR blocker.
**K11 (six proposed experiments), compared with the held pilot arm list and brief V1.1 (sections 13.8 to 13.10):** (1) in vivo ketamine and memantine in neuronal Wwox-deletion mice with video EEG and slice readouts from the same animals: **agree**; it adds the in vivo step the brief lacks; it needs the slice conditions the brief fixes (Mg2+, temperature, burst definition), a time-matched vehicle, block depth matched by the evoked delayed NMDAR response, and a pore-blocker arm (13.9) beside d-APV. ## 5. D: mechanism and rivals
### 5.1 Ketamine, d-APV and memantine on the axes the slice question needs
| Axis | d-APV (the slice tool) | Ketamine (the input's drug) | Memantine (the input's chronic alternative) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Site and mechanism | competitive, glutamate site; no channel opening needed | open-channel (pore) blocker, use-dependent | open-channel (pore) blocker, use-dependent, lower affinity | ASSUMED (general pharmacology); the two primary papers I tried (Glasgow 2017, Widman 2018) returned a reCAPTCHA page, a STOP, not read |
| Use dependence and trapping | none; block does not build with activity | block builds with activity; partial trapping after washout | faster off-rate, less trapping | ASSUMED; decides whether bursting overshoots after washout |
| Interplay with Mg2+ (this slice: 1.3 mM) | independent | competes at the pore | competes at the pore | ASSUMED; Breton's ACSF holds 1.3 mM Mg2+ |
| Subunit and cell selectivity | not subunit-selective at 50 uM | low-dose effects in vivo route through **GluN2A on parvalbumin interneurons** in the one primary read (Picard 2019, adult mouse visual cortex: deleting GluN2A globally or from PV cells "abolished the rapid enhancement of visual cortical responses and gamma-band oscillations by ketamine") | not read in this lane | DATO for ketamine (mouse, adult, cortex, TRANSFER to a P13-17 Wwox-null slice); the disinhibition reading |
| Non-NMDAR targets that touch the repository's threads | none known | **HCN1**: ketamine inhibits Ih in **cortical pyramidal neurons of 14- to 22-day-old mice** (20 uM, wild type, not HCN1-knockout; recombinant HCN1-HCN2 heteromer EC50 about 8 to 16 uM), hyperpolarising them and raising input resistance (Chen 2009); K2P/TREK-1: not read in this lane | not read in this lane | DATO (Chen 2009, native neurons in the same age window as Breton; the effect size is the paper's, the transfer to S-KO neurons is INFERENZA) |
| Ex vivo anti-epileptiform effect of a pore blocker | d-APV abolishes the S-KO bursts (Breton) | S-ketamine **abolished seizure-like events** in Mg2+-free slices from 6- to 12-week-old mouse hippocampus at 100 to 1000 uM (Riepe 2026) | not read | DATO (adult hippocampus, Mg2+-free, high concentration; TRANSFER) |
**Why the HCN1 row matters.** Breton reports that S-KO pyramidal neurons are depolarised and show **increased sag current** (an Ih footprint). A drug that also inhibits HCN1 would act on that very property, so an effect of ketamine on the S-KO slice could go through HCN1 and not through NMDAR, and d-APV, which has no HCN1 action, cannot tell the two apart. This is the single most important gap in "mechanism-matched": the matching is at the drug-class label, not at the target actually engaged in these neurons (INFERENZA).

**The one observation I would put first (before any in vivo or multi-day work):** burst rate at 30, 60 and 120 min after washout of a fixed, block-depth-matched exposure to a pore blocker **and** to d-APV, each against a time-matched vehicle slice, with the Ih sag measured in the same slices. Outcomes and what each favours: both return to baseline (R-A); both overshoot then decay (R-B, a slow variable); the pore blocker differs from d-APV (R-D: a class-specific action such as trapping or HCN1); both stay low (R-C, a lasting change; I give it 0.15). It needs no new animal model, only the preparation the repository's brief V1.1 already specifies (13.9 uses the same slice arm), and it separates all five rival readings except R-F, which is not a slice question.
**Where the input is right about the model.** Its own limits are right: partial, use-dependent, reversible block means "pharmacological block as complete deletion of a node" overstates reach; vote counting is not meta-analysis; lithium data are provoked-threshold; all rodent WWOX models die at 2 to 4 weeks, so a multi-day exposure is a large fraction of a mouse life (the last fits the held shortened-lifespan caveat).
