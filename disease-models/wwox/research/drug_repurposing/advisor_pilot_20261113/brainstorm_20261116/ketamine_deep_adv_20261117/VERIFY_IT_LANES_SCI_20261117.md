## S01. DeVine 2023 (PMID 37139250), complete full text (PMC page, 137,868 bytes, sha256 `72752a8d418f41fa3e5df30c5f86d59025ebe14ca823b605bd497728fa583ec9`, no challenge text)
**Right: 2 of 3.** Discussion, first paragraph: "In 2 of 3 cases, seizures resolved with therapy including ketamine, with 1 patient having a temporarily associated dramatic improvement in seizures after initiating ketamine." Patient 3: "Continuous ketamine infusion was continued until the time of death. Owing to family's decision to withdraw life-sustaining care, it is unclear if seizures were controlled on continuous ketamine." The abstract, which is all HE had, states tolerability and a reduced benzodiazepine infusion in one case and no seizure outcome: HE's abstract-level reading is accurate for the abstract and incomplete for the paper.
**Effect on 24 of 30:** on the input's own logic (count of responders) DeVine contributes 2, so **23 of 30 = 76.7%**. **Caveats the source itself supplies:** "resolved with therapy including ketamine" is not "responded to ketamine"; patient 1: "Following 5 days of ketamine infusion without successful control of subclinical seizures" ketamine was weaned and an EEG on hospital day 14 "showed resolution of seizures" on four maintenance agents; patient 2: seizures "ceased within 1 hour of initiation of ketamine in conjunction with midazolam", relapsed on hospital day 56, resolved after ketamine was restarted. Concomitant antiseizure medications averaged three. So the 2 of 3 is resolution under polytherapy, not an attributable response. The pooled figure also mixes three endpoints (HE F6), unchanged by this.

## S02. KETASER01
Rosati 2022 (PMID 35833327, EPMC core record, abstract): "only 10 individuals (five for each study arm ...) were enrolled"; "Two of the five patients (40%) in the experimental arm were successfully treated with ketamine and two of the five (40%) children in the control arm, where successfully treated with thiopental"; "No conclusions can be drawn regarding the objectives of the study." **Supported.** Registry NCT02431663: overallStatus TERMINATED, whyStopped "Futility", but **enrollmentInfo count 57 (ACTUAL)**, against 10 in the paper: the registry record is stale or counts differently; IT's text does not mention it.
**Not supported as worded: "the only randomised paediatric ketamine trial found."** IT's own P5 table holds a second randomised ketamine trial in children, PMID 40186980 / NCT05779657 (n = 144, ketamine plus midazolam against placebo plus midazolam, first-line generalized convulsive status epilepticus): "Cessation of clinical seizures at five-minute occurred in 76% of children in the Ket-Mid group compared with 21% in the Pla-Mid group (risk ratio 3.7; 95% confidence interval 2.3-5.9; P < 0.001)", with fewer repeat midazolam doses and intubations. KETASER01 is the only randomised paediatric ketamine trial in **refractory** status epilepticus found and it was halted; the sentence must say so.

## S03. Kuki 2024 and Jacobwitz 2022
Kuki (PMID 38461760, abstract): "The median duration of treatment was 7.5 days (interquartile range: 2.8-15.5 days)"; "Ten patients had inflammatory etiologies"; "Three (17%) patients experienced transient adverse events requiring intervention within 24 h of initiating ketamine administration"; 18 patients, so non-inflammatory n = 8 by subtraction (the abstract states no count for them). **Supported.** Jacobwitz 2022 (PMID 35817569; PMC page, no challenge text): "Sixty-nine patients were treated with a ketamine infusion"; Results: "The median total duration of ketamine infusion was 85.7 hours (IQR 49.7-128.0)"; abstract: "Three patients (4%) had adverse events requiring intervention". **Supported.** Note for use: Kuki is super-refractory (SRSE, 2008 to 2023, 18 patients), Jacobwitz refractory (RSE, 69); the two durations are medians of different populations and are not the same variable.

## S04. Memantine trial, abstract against registry
Abstract (Schiller 2023, PMID 36256600): "Thirty-one patients (13 females) enrolled. Two patients withdrew prior to initiating medication and two (twins) had to be removed from analysis. Of the remaining 27 patients ..."; responder "defined as >=2 of: >50% seizure frequency reduction, electroencephalography improvement, caregiver clinical impression improvement or clear neuropsychological testing improvement" (four criteria). Registry NCT03779672 (ClinicalTrials.gov v2 record): enrollmentInfo count **30** ACTUAL; primary outcome "Responder defined as having >= 2 of (1) EEG improvement, (2) decreased seizure frequency, (3) cognitive improvement, (4) caregiver impression of improvement, (5) Serum Inflammatory Markers Change" (five criteria); masking QUADRUPLE, PHASE4, crossover. **Supported**, with two additions IT does not list: masking is "double-blind" in the abstract and quadruple in the registry; the abstract gives no reason for removing the twins and no named statistical test (so "the trial's paired analysis" in the input is not supported by the abstract).

## S05. NCT07224581
Registry record (verified 2026-11): overallStatus RECRUITING; PHASE3; allocation RANDOMIZED, parallel; masking TRIPLE; enrollment 100 (ESTIMATED); condition "GRIN-related Neurodevelopmental Disorder"; official title: "a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled Phase 3" preceded by an open-label Phase 1b. **Supported.** Qualifiers: the condition is a neurodevelopmental disorder, not DEE as such (IT already notes the definitional choice); radiprodil is a GluN2B-selective negative modulator, a different class from the pore blockers of the input.

## S06. Short-exposure paediatric harms
Green 2026 (PMID 42138678, abstract): "We identified 20 qualifying studies totaling 58,752 children"; "Serious adverse events (defined as sentinel above plus positive pressure ventilation, oral airway, bolus intravenous fluids, sedation insufficient, escalation of care or hospitalization) were noted in 0.33%"; sentinel outcomes 0.0041%. Green 2009 (PMID 19501426): "In 8,282 pediatric ketamine sedations, the overall incidence of emesis, any recovery agitation, and clinically important recovery agitation was 8.4%, 7.6%, and 1.4%". **Supported.** Context to carry: intravenous ED procedural sedation (single dose)and a broad definition of "serious"; not comparable with a multi-day infusion for status epilepticus; emesis 8.4% is not in IT's sentence.

## S07. Electrographic seizures after ketamine
Tunc 2025 (PMID 39918155, abstract): "Eight papers were eligible for inclusion with 141 subjects (24 children)"; "28% (26/94) of subjects with epilepsy had electrographic seizures after ketamine administration"; "In four studies where pediatric and adult subjects could be separated, children with epilepsy had electrographic seizures in 60% (3/5) of cases compared to 28% (6/33) of cases of adults with epilepsy". **Partly:** 3/5 is correct, but the denominator is the five children with epilepsy in the four age-separable studies, not "one review of 24 children"; the setting is procedural sedation; the review's headline is 28% of subjects with epilepsy. The wording must read "3 of 5 children with epilepsy in four age-separable studies (a systematic review of 141 subjects)".

## S08. Memantine in GRIN gain-of-function
Karnstedt 2026 (PMID 41489401; Europe PMC XML in the P4 folder; full text read): Results: "In three individuals (#10, #25, and #32), seizure frequency worsened during memantine treatment; however, the exact cause and the extent of the seizure exacerbation remained unclear"; Discussion: "Four individuals with GoF GRIN variants who received memantine treatment experienced a memantine-related worsening of seizure frequency (Individuals #10, #25, 16 and #32) and/or EEG". **Supported: IT's "3 or 4 of 19" is exactly the paper's own inconsistency.** Beside it (benefit): "Off-label memantine treatment was associated with clinical improvements in 14 of 19 individuals (74%)" and seizure burden reduced in 7 of 18 with epilepsy; individual #10 "was only treated for 4 days".

## S10. "No WWOX or WOREE child is reported with ketamine"
Two routes re-run today: (1) PubMed (the PubMed search tool): `(WWOX OR "WW domain-containing oxidoreductase" OR WOREE OR SCAR12 OR DEE28) AND ketamine`: **0 hits**; (2) Europe PMC REST: the same terms in title or abstract: **0 hits**; in the full-text index `(WWOX OR "WW domain-containing oxidoreductase" OR WOREE) AND ketamine`: **33 hits**, almost all oncology, lung, vascular and psychiatric papers where the terms co-occur. The two WWOX-model hits in the P2 folder carry ketamine only as an anaesthetic: Repudi 2021 (PMID 34747138): "Mice were anesthetized using ketamine/medetomidine (i.p; 100 and 83 mg/kg, respectively)" for in vivo cell-attached recordings; the 2026 gene-therapy paper (PMID 42422765): "deeply anesthetized using an overdose of ketamine and xylazine" before perfusion. **Supported as a statement about the searched literature.**
**Omission:** ketamine **was** given to Wwox mice as an anaesthetic in vivo (Repudi 2021, electrophysiology under ketamine/medetomidine; which genotype groups were recorded under it I did not check), with no seizure endpoint; so "no NMDAR antagonist has been given to a Wwox animal in vivo" is true for a therapeutic or seizure endpoint and not for anaesthesia exposure, and neither paper reports a ketamine effect.
**What "not found in these queries" covers:** the indexed titles and abstracts of PubMed and Europe PMC, and the open-access full texts Europe PMC indexes. It does **not** cover closed full texts, case reports published without these terms, conference supplements (the full-text query returned "AES 2014 Annual Meeting - Online Abstract Supplement", not opened), registries, or ICU and hospital records.

## S11. Lane C
**Verified rows.** d-APV "n = 5 slices, 3 animals": matches Breton (n = 51 bursts, 5 slices, 3 animals). Lithium row "provoked PTZ threshold, N = 6; effect also in wild type": the held Cheng 2020 Fig. 7d (image read) shows KO PTZ N = 6, PTZ plus lithium N = 7 (marked ****), and also significant in +/+ (N = 12 against 8) and +/- (N = 12 against 12): **correct, and it means lithium's effect is not specific to Wwox loss.** Ketogenic diet row (KIWE, PMID 37977712): "Three infants died during the trial, all of whom were randomly assigned a ketogenic diet", "The deaths were judged unrelated to treatment by local principal investigators and confirmed by the data safety monitoring committee": **verified.** Ganaxolone CDKL5 (-30.7% against -6.9%), ESETT (47%, 45%, 46%), the cannabidiol, fenfluramine, stiripentol, everolimus and vigabatrin trials named in the row are present in the P5 table with abstract-level numbers (I did not re-derive each number). Ketalar label ">3 hours in children <=3 years": **verified** ("Long-term cognitive deficits may occur when used for longer than 3 hours in children <=3 years"), derived by the label from "published animal studies ... the clinical significance of these findings is not clear".
**Findings.**
1. **Answer-first, first clause: PARTLY, overstated.** "Nothing is better supported than ketamine or memantine, and nothing is clearly worse" conflicts with the table: ketamine and memantine have **no** WWOX measurement; the NMDAR thread has d-APV in one slice preparation; **lithium and ethosuximide have in vivo drug-response data in Wwox-null mice** (Fig. 7: ethosuximide N = 6 against 6, significant in knockout, n.s. in +/+ and +/-; lithium N = 7 against 6). On the in vivo WWOX axis they are better supported than ketamine or memantine, which is also the table's own K12 verdict. The rest of the sentence (classes not comparable on the other axes; the ordering depends on the endpoint) is the right reading.
2. **Status-epilepticus cell omits a benefit, a comparative signal and a harm that IT's P5 table holds.** The cell lists "uncontrolled RSE series (15/18, 6/9, 3 infants); KETASER01 halted RCT"; P5 also holds (a) the positive first-line randomised trial (76% against 21%, RR 3.7, fewer intubations; PMID 40186980); (b) a retrospective single-centre comparison (PMID 37783824): "Seizures terminated more often in study participants administered ketamine (61%, 23/38) than midazolam (28%, 22/79; OR 3.97 ...)" **and** "Survival to discharge was higher among study participants who received midazolam (82%, 65/79) than ketamine (55%, 21/38; P = 0.002), although treating clinicians did not attribute any deaths"; (c) pooled estimates: "SE cessation was attained in 51% (95% CI 43-59) of cases with the addition of ketamine" (PMID 38881333) and "50% in children" (PMID 41409370). The harm (lower survival) and the benefits belong beside each other; the answer-first paragraph carries neither.
3. **Ethosuximide row omits its genotype selectivity**: significant in knockout, n.s. in +/+ and +/- in the same experiment, which is the one feature that favours it over lithium as a Wwox-linked observation (one experiment, one laboratory, provoked seizures).
4. **"Placebo-controlled paediatric RCTs in ... infantile spasms" (the approved-agents row): PARTLY.** In the P5 table only the 1999 vigabatrin study (n = 40, double-blind placebo for 5 days) is placebo-controlled; UKISS, ICISS and the hormone comparisons are open or single-blinded. "Placebo-controlled" holds for Dravet, LGS, CDKL5 and TSC rows, not for infantile spasms.
5. **Column mixing:** "Paediatric clinical evidence in DEE (design)" is filled for ketamine and the anaesthetic infusions with **status-epilepticus** evidence, and for the approved agents with chronic DEE evidence; the two are different endpoints (the answer-first text says so; the column heading does not).
6. **Radiprodil** is named in the testability paragraph (a GluN2B class with a registered randomised trial) but has no row in the table.
**Summary:** most cells are supported; two overstatements (the first clause; the infantile-spasm placebo wording) and the omissions above should be corrected before the table goes to the synthesis.
