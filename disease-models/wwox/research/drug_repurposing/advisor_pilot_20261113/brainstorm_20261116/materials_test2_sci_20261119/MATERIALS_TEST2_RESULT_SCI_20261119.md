## Deviations and limits (all of them)
1. The manifest omission (answer 4). A repeat is not run: the pre-registration allows one run per arm and no further arm.
2. Every report exceeded its word cap (L by 150 words, M by 399, the verifier by 256). Reports were judged as delivered.
3. Neither arm queried PubMed; both ran far below the 60-call cap. The comparison is between two very short medium-effort runs.
4. Blinding is to the label only: M's report names loop identifiers (open-question and card ids, the ADV notes), so the arm is recognisable by content; the verifier wrote that Report B "states that it opened no originals".
5. M's material carried two pointers to the ordering page (a dated line of the arm-table notes and the open-question rows); the audit of M's transcript shows the page was not opened and its report does not cite it.
6. Isolation is by instruction plus a transcript audit; both delegates inherit the repository instructions and the memory index (L is not memory-free; declared in the pre-registration).
7. The verifier's reports of "B better" rest in part on facts it confirmed in originals (P9 onset, no glucose in the deletion paper, the conduction legend) and in part on a paper it could reach only at abstract level; I confirmed that last item in the held full text.

## Proposal (no run, for ADV and IT)
The finding about the set-up is itself the useful result: a lean arm must be given every held original that the notes were built from, and be required to search PubMed, before "the notes add something" can be read from a difference. If ADV wants the clean test, a repeat needs a new pre-registration hashed by IT with a complete originals list (per measurement, including the AAV9 gene-therapy paper, the 2026 AAV9 report and the null-mouse reports the ADV-074 note names) and one fixed minimum search step for both arms; I would run it only with ADV's word.
