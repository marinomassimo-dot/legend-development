**Checks of the claims that decide each order**

Report A
- Its first choice rests on the rescue arm "separat[ing] a myelin-driven deficit from a secondary effect of seizures or poor condition". A neuron-targeted or global re-expression rescues seizures, growth and survival together. The abstract above shows this, and Report B says the same. A rescue arm therefore cannot make that separation.
- It needs a rescue line, expensive slice work and animal-level re-analysis. Report A itself calls it "high cost".
- Report A calls glucose weakly supported ("no held original linking glucose to seizure timing"). It misses the documented null hypoglycaemia and its neuronal rescue.
- Report A cites Abu-Remaileh without noting that the muscle phenotype is hyperglycaemia. Report A also does not mention the null-versus-missense (P47T) or P17-versus-adult mismatch when it calls Hussain "discordant". This is a transfer across model and age without saying so.
- Report A mislabels the Repudi seizure figure. It writes "latency in n = 6", but the held text gives seizure duration, 68.5 s. It never mentions the P9 seizure onset.
- Report A says Hussain's adult wildtype EEG was "normal". The text gives 0–68 spikes per hour.
- Report A omits the held Cheng heterozygote Tc-MEP prolongation (2.13 ms). It calls the heterozygote evidence "Mixed and mild". It does not state (a), so this is an omission and not an error.

Report B
- Report B states that it opened no originals. Its fact claims therefore come from project notes, and I checked them.
- Verified against Repudi:
  - Seizures from P9.
  - Conduction legend 8 slices from 5 animals (control) versus 8 from 6 (knockout).
  - EM 180 versus 55 per field.
  - Synapsin-Cre, acute slices, P13–P17.
  - The legend gives no velocity, and there is no rescue arm in that paper.
- Verified against Cheng: het 2.13, null 2.44, WT 1.39 ms. The values are correct, and Report B does not credit a conduction measurement with settling the heterozygote question.
- Supported only by the abstract of 34747138 (not held): hypoglycaemia rescued with seizures by neuronal AAV9.
- Unverified by me (producer notes): "no glucose before P10", the CMV-vector glucose measured at P14 only, the same-animal pair absence, and the PV P17 numbers.
- Minor slips:
  - "Held ECoG starts at P14" while Repudi's in vivo recordings are labelled P13–P17.
  - The "would stay first if the lab already had the tissue" caveat attaches to the PV measurement, though glucose is the first choice. This is confusing.
- The sentence "no held original has any datum linking [glucose] to seizures" is an absence claim whose perimeter is the project notes, and it states that perimeter. Its own co-rescue point (neuronal rescue normalises glucose and seizures together) shows the link is a co-occurrence, not a cause. That is the point the report makes.

**Judgement by category**
- (i) More informative comparison of what could change: **B better.** B names the discriminating gaps: no glucose before P10 against seizures from P9, ECoG that starts too late, and the inconsistent conduction n. A reasons mostly from generic considerations.
- (ii) More defensible conditional hypothesis: **B better.** A's headline function for the rescue arm does not hold, and it is a poor "one optional measurement" because it needs a rescue line. B's choice is cheap and anchored in a documented null phenotype. The stated risk is blood sampling in small pups.
- (iii) Better separation of alternatives: **B better.** Same-animal glucose and seizure timing splits "metabolic driver" from "co-rescued accompaniment". A rescue arm does not split myelin from seizure effects.
- (iv) Retrieved question the other report missed: **B better.** The null hypoglycaemia and its rescue, and the P9 onset against the ECoG window. A's one extra item, the Hussain Mbp discordance, is real but would not change the order.
- (v) NO DIFFERENCE: **no.**
- (vi) HARM or needless complexity: **A, borderline.** Quote: "A rescue arm would separate a myelin-driven deficit from a secondary effect of seizures or poor condition." It credits a measurement with changing a decision it cannot change. A's Hussain contrast is also transferred without saying so. B: none that I judge to be harm. B's absence claim carries its perimeter.

**Descriptive**
- First choices differ: A conduction re-measurement with rescue, B glucose tied to seizure timing.
- The reasons differ. A wants a causal and reversibility test and judged glucose to have no direct evidence. B wants the cheap decisive axis that no held original pairs with seizures.
- A fact found in only one report that changes the order: null hypoglycaemia with its neuronal rescue, found by B. I checked it against the PubMed abstract only (34747138), not a held original. It undermines A's reason for ranking glucose fourth.
- Sentences (a) to (f):
  - (a) Neither report states it. B gives the correct values ("1.39 ms ... 2.13 ms ... 2.44 ms").
  - (b), (c), (d), (f) Neither report states them.
  - (e) B writes "the CMV vector measured at P14 only", which is the corrected wording.
