## 2. Findings that change what the other lanes may rely on
(Each: the input's statement, what the abstract says, effect.)

**F1. 34464676 is a tianeptine paper.** Input: counted among the six studies in which a single or short ketamine course inhibits GSK-3β, and quoted ("accompanied by molecular signaling associated with synaptic plasticity (e.g. activation of tropomyosin receptor kinase B (TrkB) and inhibition of glycogen synthase kinase 3β (GSK3β))"). Abstract: the quote is the Background sentence describing "our recent studies" with ketamine and nitrous oxide; the study itself gave tianeptine, and "Phosphorylation of mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), TrkB and GSK3β remained unaltered at 2-hours and at 3-hours post-treatment". The quote is verbatim; the paper does not measure ketamine. Effect: the "inhibited 6/6" cell is 5/5 at most.

**F2. 37392896 reports the opposite sign.** Input: single-dose ketamine inhibits GSK-3β (p-Ser9 up). Abstract (hypothalamus, single prolonged stress rat): "SPS triggered an upregulation in the protein and gene expression of GSK-3β, GR, BDNF, p-GSK-3β, and FKBP5"; "ketamine reduced the protein levels of GSK-3β, GR, p-GSK-3β, and altered the ratio of p-GSK-3β to GSK-3β". The sister study 39167905 (same stress model, hippocampus) reports stress lowering p-GSK-3β and ketamine attenuating that. Both are restorations of a stress change, in opposite directions in two regions, not a baseline ketamine effect.

**F3. The "repeated or prolonged" group is mostly hours, not days, and partly in vitro.** 33733293: six doses 90 min apart in 7-day-old rats; the abstract states that GM1 increased p-AKT and p-GSK3β and does not state the direction for ketamine alone. 31263401: "ketamine increased cleaved caspase-3 in vivo and decreased the expression of p-GSK-3β in vitro" (neural stem cells; no multi-day schedule in the abstract). 41618072 (the only multi-day study, adult mice, 7 days): the Akt/Gsk3β phosphorylation suppression is stated for HT22 cells in vitro; in vivo the abstract states apoptosis and behaviour. Companion studies cited elsewhere in the input as "prolonged": 42808560 (5 injections 90 min apart) and 36371060 (over 6 h). The input's conclusion that "duration rather than developmental age" separates the groups rests on one adult study whose phosphorylation readout is in vitro; the neonatal "prolonged" protocols last hours.

**F4. 40967562 is not separable at abstract level.** The abstract says ketamine "enhanced hippocampal expression of ... phosphorylated glycogen synthase kinase-3β (p-GSK-3β)" and compares short- and long-term schedules for behaviour and cognition, not for p-GSK-3β. Placing it in the "short course" cell is the input's classification; if the long-term arm also raised p-GSK-3β it is a counter-instance to "prolonged activates".

**F5. Context the input does not carry.** 36334642 (effect size MD 1.00, 0.74 to 1.27): rat neural stem cells in culture; "acute low-dose" is the input's wording, not the abstract's. 34089815: ketamine with guanosine in a corticosterone model, schedule not in the abstract. 39167905: stress model (F2).
**Net for the GSK-3β claim**: the nine-study table, as an exposure-by-direction contingency table, cannot be rebuilt from the abstracts; what they support is a set of models in which a p-GSK-3β reading changes with ketamine in either direction depending on tissue, model and preparation. The Fisher p of 0.0119 is arithmetically right for a table whose cells are not supported. The input itself calls it vote counting.

**F6. Pooled pediatric response, 24 of 30.** Kuki 2024: 15 of 18 with more than 50 % seizure reduction; Rosati 2012: 6 of 9 with resolution of refractory status epilepticus; DeVine 2023: 3 infants, abstract reports tolerability ("without adverse events"), a reduced benzodiazepine infusion in one case, and no seizure outcome. 24 requires all 3 DeVine infants as responders. Without them: 21 of 27 = 77.8 %, Wilson 59.2 to 89.4. The input already says the pool mixes three endpoints in uncontrolled series. Omitted from the sources: Kuki "Three (17%) patients experienced transient adverse events requiring intervention within 24 h of initiating ketamine"; 10 of 18 had inflammatory etiologies (so 8 non-inflammatory patients carry the "better stratum" the input assigns to WOREE by analogy); Rosati "Midazolam was administered add-on to prevent emergence reactions" and "2 were cured by surgical removal of epileptogenic focal cortical dysplasia" (structural etiology), "Class IV evidence ... no statistical analysis was done".

**F7. Memantine trial.** Reproduced (section 3). The abstract also states seizure improvement in 8 versus 2 (p < 0.04), caregiver overall improvement in 10 versus 7 (not significant), a composite primary outcome ("responder" = at least 2 of 4 criteria), 31 enrolled, 2 withdrawn and 2 (twins) removed.

**F8. The sequence "rescue then maintenance" is the input's reading of 39970743.** The abstract: one 11-month-old; "partially responsive to ketamine, and she became seizure-free when memantine ... was added to the treatment", during status epilepticus; the genetic result arrived in the second week of memantine. Memantine was added to ketamine, not given after it.

**F9. The key WWOX sentence does not name the drug.** 34634460: "These bursts were NMDAR and gap junction dependent." Quote is verbatim (also in the dismech entry). The abstract does not say which NMDA antagonist was used and does not mention ketamine. "Ketamine is the only drug whose molecular target has been pharmacologically identified inside a WWOX model" therefore rests on the receptor-class dependency, not on ketamine data. The dismech entry contains no ketamine or memantine statement (grep of the full entry).

**F10. 42808560** (PND7 rats, 5 injections 90 min apart): the quoted sentence is exact; the abstract continues "At 16 hours, a return to baseline levels was observed", which the input omits where it lists oligodendrocyte apoptosis among the prolonged-exposure harms.
