### Part 2: the STATEMENT, as a claim to be tested

1. **Does it follow from the SUPPORTED triples?** Only at a coarse level. The label documents visual-system harm (L1, L5, L6). Human WWOX loss has optic-nerve and retinal findings (W2, W3). Mouse data show optic-tract and optic-nerve myelination change (W1, W4). The two sets meet at "the visual system" in general, but the tissues and lesions differ: label field constriction/central retina, versus WWOX optic atrophy/hypomyelination/ERG-VEP change.
2. **Weakest link:** "same organ system" is a label we impose. The label's lesion (peripheral field loss, retinal) is not shown to be the same lesion as WWOX optic-nerve atrophy or hypomyelination. The only direct bridge would be a vigabatrin-exposed WWOX child with before-and-after vision data, and the held sources give none.
3. **What the held sources do NOT establish:**
   - No vigabatrin-exposed WWOX subject has *attributable* vision data. In S-Shaukat both children received vigabatrin. In case 1 optic atrophy is documented at about 6 months, before the vigabatrin sentence in the narrative (inferred from text order). Case 2 has no optic exam stated. Separately, Al Baradie 2022 (a source outside the five named; see Part 3) has patients 2.1 and 2.2 on vigabatrin. 2.1 started at 2 months, has "bilateral optic nerve atrophy", and has no exam date. These are confounded by the null genotype (p.Arg54* appears in both Abdel-Salam and Al Baradie). Nothing supports or excludes an additive effect.
   - Whether child reports are representative: W2 and W3 are n = 1 each. The only denominator is in Part 3.
   - Optic-nerve atrophy versus drug-induced field loss: not shown to be the same lesion, and the label never says "optic atrophy" (L7).
   - L3 is retinal dysplasia in juvenile rats on oral exposure at postnatal days 4-65. It does not say what happens in a mouse, in a Wwox-null animal, or in a human. No mouse or rat vigabatrin data on Wwox deficiency are held in these five sources.
   - W1 has no n and no quantification. W4 shows rescue of myelination, not a retinal or visual phenotype.
4. **Rating: PARTIAL.** Both (a) and (b) are documented, so it is not NO. It is not YES, because the "same system" link is at the broad visual-system level only and has no joint exposure evidence.
5. **Better wording:** "The visual system is documented on both sides: the vigabatrin label describes permanent visual-field constriction and possible central retinal damage, with an explicit statement that the effect of combining it with other causes of irreversible vision damage is 'not well-characterized, but likely adverse'. Held WWOX reports describe optic atrophy and retinal or visual-pathway electrophysiology abnormalities in a minority of children (n = 1 per quoted report), and mouse data show optic-tract and optic-nerve myelination changes. Whether these are the same lesion, and whether any vigabatrin-exposed WWOX child has attributable vision data, is not established by these sources."
