## What transcripts cannot show about T-type current or burst firing
Transcript counts do not give channel number at the membrane, glycosylation or splice state, the voltage dependence of inactivation, the share of Cav3.1, Cav3.2 and Cav3.3 in a given neuron, or the other conductances that decide whether a low-threshold calcium spike becomes a burst (resting potential, the hyperpolarisation-activated current Ih, potassium and leak channels, persistent sodium). In whole tissue a change of cell mix (reported here and elsewhere for interneurons and oligodendrocytes) moves bulk values without any change per neuron. None of the four datasets sampled thalamic relay or reticular neurons, where Cav3.1 and Cav3.2 carry the burst in rodent absence models. A transcript difference is therefore a reason to measure current, not evidence of an excess or a deficit. The only held functional neuron data in a Wwox-deficient preparation (Breton 2021, PMID 34634460, as recorded in SCI's lane note `ketamine_deep_adv_20261117/KDS_LANE_B1_B4_D_SCI_20261117.md`; not re-read here) report depolarised pyramidal neurons with increased sag current, an Ih footprint, and no T-type measurement.

## Is there any source that links WWOX loss to Cav3 at all (a negative needs two routes)
* Route 1, held records: the four datasets above, the three discovery-ledger entries (DL-MECH-170, 171, 172) and the 2026-10-29 and 10-30 notes on T-type potency; nothing connects WWOX to a Cav3 mechanism.
* Route 2, literature: IT's 7 PubMed and Europe PMC queries (abstract level) and four Europe PMC queries by me on 2026-11-18, not limited to abstracts: `(WWOX OR Wwox) AND (CACNA1G/H/I ...)` 71 hits; `... AND ("T-type" OR Cav3.x OR low-threshold ...)` 17 hits; the same with neuron or thalamus terms 41 hits; `TITLE_ABS WWOX AND calcium channel(s)` 10 hits (none concerning Cav3). I opened the six open-access hits that looked closest (PMC13653708, PMC13026608, PMC13516588, PMC7503973, PMC12979860, PMC12237231): each names WWOX and T-type genes or channels in separate sentences, gene lists, cohort tables or review prose; none states a connection. The only text in which WWOX work and a T-type channel meet is Cheng 2020's own description of ethosuximide as "a T-type Ca2+ channel blocker that has anticonvulsant activity", with a citation (held manifest `deepdive_manifests/PMID32000863.json`): the T-type role is attributed to the drug by citation and not measured in the Wwox-null mice.
* Verdict: no link found by two routes; "none indexed in these queries" licenses no more.

## For IT's table (decisive difference only, part a)
The ethosuximide arm currently rests on a descriptive pattern (Cheng 2020: a drug effect seen in the null group, with the other genotypes not significant and no drug-by-genotype test; `fig7_quant_ethosuximide_lithium_sci_20261115`), not on a T-type rationale in WWOX deficiency: the held transcripts neither show a T-type excess nor a consistent change, and no Cav3 current is measured in any Wwox-deficient neuron. The decisive observation remains cell-type-resolved Cav3 transcript or current in Wwox-null thalamic reticular and relay neurons at a fixed age, with composition measured in the same samples (DL-MECH-171's falsifier). It is a laboratory measurement.
