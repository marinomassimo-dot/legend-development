## Answer first
1. **WO-23: no document found pairs the two readouts under two small-molecule classes of GSK-3 inhibitor with both readouts stated for each class, in a neural system. The nearest are three, all abstract level, all with the full text blocked, and none of them shows a Tau-versus-catenin split.**
   * **Cross 2001 (PMID 11279265), primary neurones:** SB-415286 and SB-216763 (**one class, ATP-competitive, two compounds**) protect neurones and "modulation of GSK-3 substrates tau and beta-catenin" is stated for the pair: label SINGLE-CLASS PAIR.
   * **PMID 11696357 (PC12 cells, a neuron-like line; I do not infer neurones from the abstract): the closest to a two-class comparison.** The two ATP-competitive small molecules and an adenovirus overexpressing **FRAT1** (a protein regulator of GSK-3, a different mechanism) were compared; FRAT1 "correlates with inhibition of GSK-3 activity towards Tau and beta-catenin, but not ... glycogen synthase", and the small molecules inhibit **Tau, beta-catenin and glycogen synthase**. The **divergence between mechanisms is on a third substrate (glycogen synthase), not between Tau and beta-catenin**. Label: PAIRED-SAME-SYSTEM only if a protein regulator counts as a class; as a small-molecule class comparison it does not. **This paper is new to the record** (IT35 NB-2 did not list it).
   * **PMID 16014628 (CHO cells and rat cultured neurons), read at abstract level again and with a correction to IT35's phrasing:** lithium produced beta-catenin nuclear translocation and decreased Tau phosphorylation; SB415286 produced beta-catenin translocation and the abstract says nothing about its Tau effect. So beta-catenin is stated for two classes and Tau for one: PAIRED-ONE-CLASS-PER-READOUT. It also says lithium raised amyloid-beta production **independently of GSK-3**: a lithium off-target in the very system.
2. **What the GSK-3 block of the shared panel can cite and what it cannot.** It can cite a precedent for the paired readout (Tau and beta-catenin are co-modulated by GSK-3 inhibition in primary neurones, one class: Cross 2001; and in PC12 with both small molecules and FRAT1). It **cannot cite a precedent that Tau and beta-catenin separate by inhibitor class**; the discriminator for NEED-GSK3B-DEREPRESSION therefore needs its own controls (section 3). Decision answered: the panel **pairs the two readouts in the same tissue**, and the classes are lithium, one ATP-competitive compound and one non-ATP-competitive compound (HE batch 9 holds the molecules), plus a third substrate as the control for a substrate-selective effect (the PC12 paper's glycogen synthase is the model).
3. **WO-12: the 360-fold (ifenprodil) and about 5,800-fold (Ro 25-6981) ratios are Xenopus-oocyte, recombinant values, at abstract level; the conditions the question asks for (glycine, Mg2+, holding potential) are stated in the abstracts only for one of the three papers, and the two exact primaries stay unread.** Hess 1996 (PMID 8768735) is access-blocked by four routes; Fischer 1997 (PMID 9400004) is access-blocked by the PRESIDIO-003 routes (not re-tested). The row therefore drops to **conditions-bound by abstract**, not to confirmed or refuted.
4. **Decision answered for WO-12:** the **GluN2B ratio cannot enter the NMDAR comparison as a slice-relevant 10^2 to 10^3 window**. It is an oocyte value; in native tissue the ifenprodil sensitivity of cortical and hippocampal neurones is **age-dependent and glycine-dependent** exactly in the pilot window (section 5). GluN2B-selective agents may enter the panel only as a **subunit probe with their own conditions stated** (nominal glycine, physiological Mg2+, holding potential) and with a result read against d-APV, not against the compilation ratio.
5. **No dose is given anywhere; concentrations quoted are the sources' assay values.**

## 3. What the GSK-3 block of the shared panel must specify (consequence for the design; hypothesis level)
* **Arms in the same tissue:** lithium; one ATP-competitive inhibitor (SB216763 or CHIR99021, HE batch 9); one non-ATP-competitive inhibitor (tideglusib, HE batch 9). Each with **p-Tau (one site pair that GSK-3 is known to phosphorylate), a beta-catenin readout (non-phosphorylated or S33/S37/T41-phosphorylated, or a TCF reporter) and a third substrate** (glycogen synthase, the control that separated FRAT1 from the small molecules in row 2).
* **Lithium off-target control:** lithium is not a clean GSK-3 arm in this system (row 3: Abeta production independent of GSK-3; PMID 14511119 MEK/ERK in neurones vs astrocytes, from IT35). A second arm must carry the GSK-3 reading.
* **A split is informative only if** the arms diverge on Tau vs beta-catenin while each arm inhibits the third substrate. If Tau and beta-catenin move together under all three, the readouts cannot separate GSK-3 de-repression from a non-specific change; this is the weakening observation.
* I have **not** re-read the panel text; this is the design consequence of the literature facts above, for ADV and HE to place.

## Answers to ADV's two decisions
* **Does the panel pair Tau-phospho and beta-catenin in the same tissue, and with which classes?** Yes in the same tissue; lithium, an ATP-competitive compound and a non-ATP-competitive compound, with a third substrate; there is no literature precedent that the pair splits by class, so the design carries its own controls (section 3).
* **Can the GluN2B ratio enter the NMDAR comparison or is it conditions-bound?** Conditions-bound: the values come from recombinant oocytes (and from one human-transcript series), the glycine dependence is documented at 2B in the 1993 abstract, and the native sensitivity falls with age by 14 days; the selective agents enter only as a subunit probe in the panel with stated conditions, against d-APV.
