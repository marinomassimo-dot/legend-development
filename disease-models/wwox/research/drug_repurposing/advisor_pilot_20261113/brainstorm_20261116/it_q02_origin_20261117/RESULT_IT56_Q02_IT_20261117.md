# IT56-Q02: is there an independent origin for the three observations of the Wwox neuron-knockout slice paper? Result of the bounded search (IT; delegate extraction plus one forward-citation check by IT; non-canonical; hypotheses; not a claim of absence beyond the searched sources; not medical advice)
Observations (one paper, Breton et al. 2021, PMID 34634460, with the related Repudi 2021, PMID 33914858; Aqeilan group with the Toronto Carlen/Bardakjian groups): P1 spontaneous slice bursts removed by d-APV; P2 reduced sIPSC amplitude and frequency in layer II/III pyramidal neurons; P3 a changed intrinsic profile (depolarised rest, higher input resistance, larger sag and rebound).
**Route 1, keyword search by a delegate** (legend-delegate, sonnet, effort high read back x45: output 46,556 tokens, total 176,185 tokens, 15.4 minutes; estimate before launch 100,000-200,000: within): 91 queries (49 Europe PMC full-text-wide, 25 with the gene in title/abstract, 12 electrophysiology-specific, 5 PubMed), about 2,776 unique records, triage of 413 gene-in-title/abstract records (175 with neural keywords) plus a title review of electrophysiology full-text hits; included 3 other-laboratory papers (36828035 P47T knock-in adult video-EEG, Aldaz laboratory; 32000863 knockout motor evoked potentials and seizure susceptibility, Hsu laboratory; 19500159 lde/lde rat EEG, abstract only), 5 original-laboratory papers listed separately. Result: **P1, P2 and P3 not found from another laboratory in these sources.** Related in vivo findings of another laboratory exist (P47T: interictal spikes 0-3098/h against 0-68/h in wild type, n = 3 per genotype; generalised seizures) but they are not slice or cell recordings and do not address P1-P3.
**Route 2, forward citations (IT, Europe PMC 'cited by' of the two original papers at about 03:00Z):** 8 records cite Breton 2021 and 38 cite Repudi 2021; none is an electrophysiological slice or cell study of a Wwox-deficient neuron from a laboratory other than the original one (the citing records are clinical case reports, reviews, a Toronto-group organoid hyperexcitability paper with a co-author of the original group, the P47T knock-in paper, cancer and neurodegeneration papers). So the two routes agree: **a bounded null, not an absence proof.**
**What it means for the decision:** the single-origin collapse of the three pillars is, as far as these two routes reach, a fact about the literature and not only about our search; the phasic deficit (P2) and the intrinsic profile (P3) have no second laboratory; the d-APV dependence (P1) has none. The only other-laboratory neurophysiology are in vivo EEG findings of spikes and seizures in P47T and in a rat line, which support hyperexcitability and not its mechanism. The origin-collapse count of the selection view stays at four independent origins for the top five units.
**Limits stated by the delegate and by me:** supplements, images and figure panels were not examined; papers naming WWOX only in the full text (multi-gene screens) were screened by title only; about 40 clinical WOREE/SCAR12 case reports and cohorts were excluded by scope (patient EEG is not a neuron or slice recording); four papers could not be read in full (19500159, 42397075, PPR960425, 30290271: the last behind a reCAPTCHA page on PMC, which the delegate did not touch; a follow-up command that would have tried further routes was denied by the auto-mode classifier and not retried) and are on human_access.tsv; both routes use Europe PMC and PubMed, so a report indexed elsewhere (a conference abstract, a thesis, a preprint server not indexed) would not be found; absence claims need a second independent route (Google Scholar and bioRxiv/OpenAlex by hand) which I have not run.
