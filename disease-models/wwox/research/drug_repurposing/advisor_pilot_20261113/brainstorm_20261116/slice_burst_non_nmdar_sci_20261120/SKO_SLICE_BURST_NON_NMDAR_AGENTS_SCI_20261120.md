# The S-KO slice burst assay against agents other than NMDAR: which have a published burst or network readout in a comparable preparation, at which concentrations, and what outcome would reorder the bridge candidates (SCI successor window, 2026-11-20; step OBJ_ACCEPTED[54], ADV's steer; hypothesis level; concentrations are bath or tissue research values, not recommendations; no dose, no schedule, no recommendation for or against any use of any agent in any person; reference genotype class only; not medical advice)

Written for: ADV (decision), IT (consumer), HE (sheets). The assay: Breton 2021 (held full text; Synapsin-Cre Wwox deletion, "S-KO"; acute neocortical slices, layer II/III pyramidal cells and LFP; P13 to P17; 35 degrees; ACSF with 1.3 mM Mg2+): spontaneous bursts in S-KO; **d-APV 50 micromolar eliminated them (recovery on washout); carbenoxolone 100 micromolar cut burst frequency by 87 percent (p = 0.008) and duration by 18 percent**; resting potential -73.62 mV in S-KO against -79.25 mV in wild type; the burst-state potential and the native chloride gradient were not recorded (`../nmdar_holding_potential_sci_20261120/`). The NMDAR rival's own slice experiment is v3.1 section 6.11 and is not repeated here.

## Answer first
1. **No published burst or network readout exists in the S-KO line, or in any Wwox-deficient preparation, for any non-NMDAR agent.** The held pharmacology of the assay is two agents, d-APV and carbenoxolone. Everything below is a nearest-substrate readout and is labelled by how far it is from the assay (age, species, region, how the activity is evoked, what is measured).
2. **One document compares 20 antiseizure agents on one spontaneous burst readout, and it orders the classes sharply** (West 2018, full text re-acquired from the open-access bucket): ezogabine (Kv7 opener) is the only agent that abolished the discharges (100 micromolar), with a duration EC50 of 2.8 micromolar and a frequency EC50 of 11.8 micromolar; **ethosuximide up to 1 millimolar did not reduce frequency and prolonged duration (+33 percent at 1 millimolar)**; no direct tonic-GABA agonist (gaboxadol) was in the panel, and the GABA reuptake, GABA transaminase and benzodiazepine agents that were acted only at high concentrations, often prolonging duration. Substrate: adult kainate-treated rat medial entorhinal cortex, layer II, low-Mg2+ and raised-K+ ACSF, discharges at 0.29 Hz lasting about 566 ms. It is a different tissue from the S-KO neocortex at P13 to P17 and **an order of agents in one tissue is not an order in the other**.
3. **Brain-exposure compatibility favours ezogabine and not ethosuximide on this readout**: ezogabine's slice EC50s (2.8 and 11.8 micromolar) sit at and above the mouse brain EC50 for maximal-electroshock protection (2.23 and 3.58 micromolar, two documents); ethosuximide's measured total brain value at 72.3 mg/kg (about 0.96 millimolar) sits at the top of the range in which West 2018 saw no frequency effect.
4. **The slice has no thalamus.** A neocortical layer II/III slice cannot test the thalamocortical T-type mechanism that the absence-type account of ethosuximide rests on (ADV-052 c). **A T-type blocker that fails in this assay would therefore not exclude a thalamocortical T-type effect in vivo**, and a slice result cannot rank ethosuximide against Kv7 or GABA agents for the pup seizure assay. What the slice can order is **what acts on cortical network bursts of this null**, which is a different question and a cheap one.
5. **Where Kv7 and tonic-GABA stand:** Kv7 openers have the densest published slice record (eight papers read at abstract level and West 2018 at full text) and an **immature-tissue mechanistic link to the NMDAR-dependent state** of this assay (Qiu 2007: in P8 to P15 hippocampal slices M-channel blockade favours the interictal-to-ictal transition and M-current augmentation stops ictal events in immature tissue, by countering the sustained NMDAR-mediated depolarisation). Tonic GABA (gaboxadol) has a neonatal mouse neocortical and CA1 slice record (P5 to P9) with a network readout, but at an age before the assay's P13 to P17 window and with the chloride gradient of the S-KO neurons not held. **Kv7 within-class stays blocked by data** (HE batch 2: ezogabine the only opener with both a potency spread and a mouse brain concentration-response); the readout below is a class-level and ezogabine-level lead, not a within-class order.

## 3. What the assay can and cannot order
* **It can order agents that act on cortical network bursts** in the S-KO neocortex: Kv7 openers, NMDAR antagonists (v3.1 6.11), tonic-GABA agonists (if GABA is hyperpolarising at P13 to P17 in these cells: not held for the S-KO; the neonatal papers state GABA is mainly depolarising at P5 to P9; the maturation point in this line is ASSUMED, not shown), gap-junction blockers. Hours, not weeks; the positive control already exists.
* **It cannot test thalamocortical T-type effects** (no thalamus; point 4 of the answer), and **cannot be read as a ranking of agents for the PTZ pup seizure assay**: a slice burst and a provoked generalised seizure are different network events, and the lead (ethosuximide) rests on Cheng 2020's pup data, which no slice result can overturn.
* **Disease state changes the answer**: ethosuximide shortened duration and raised frequency in naive-rat late discharges (the authors' recollection of an earlier paper) and prolonged duration in kainate-epileptic tissue (West); Cav3.1 transcript and the TTA-P2 effect differ between epileptic and control neurons (Nigam). **A null network has its own sensitivity profile, which is exactly what the assay would measure.**

## 4. Pre-registration: which outcome would reorder what (stated after reading the literature, so a stated rule and not a blind prediction)
Design of the readout (not a recommendation and not a protocol): the Breton S-KO slices at P13 to P17 in the lab's own ACSF; one session, bursts first characterised at baseline, then graded concentrations with washout; **d-APV 50 uM as the positive control** (must eliminate bursts) and carbenoxolone 100 uM as the second control; report Mg2+, burst frequency and duration, and the resting potential at the end of recording; ezogabine, ethosuximide, a selective T-type blocker and gaboxadol (if the laboratory judges the GABA reversal acceptable) in the same slices, with the NMDAR pore blockers of 6.11. Outcomes and what each changes:
* **O1. Ezogabine suppresses burst duration at 1 to 10 uM (inside the brain-EC50 band) and ethosuximide does not at 0.1 to 1 mM.** A Kv7 route is ahead of ethosuximide **on the cortical network readout**; it earns a place in the slice experiment's follow-up and as a candidate for the next network-level step; it does **not** change the bridge lead or the pup experiment, and within-class Kv7 stays blocked.
* **O2. Ezogabine acts only at 30 to 100 uM (the abolition band).** The Kv7 route is not exposure-compatible on this readout; it stays a class-level lead only.
* **O3. Ethosuximide shortens bursts at 0.3 to 1 mM.** A direct network effect of the lead at brain-compatible exposure; read against the T-type question only with a selective blocker in the same slices (O4).
* **O4. A selective T-type blocker is inactive while ethosuximide is active (or the reverse).** Attribution result for the cortical network only: activity of ethosuximide without a selective blocker would point to a non-Cav3 route (persistent sodium and other targets: Leresche 1998 in native thalamic neurons); inactivity of both says nothing about thalamocortical T-type (no thalamus).
* **O5. Gaboxadol suppresses bursts without depolarising the cells (resting potential reported).** Gives a tonic-GABA agent its first S-KO network readout; an increase in bursts would be a harm row (GABA depolarising at this age), and would be reported as that.
* **O6. d-APV fails to eliminate the bursts in the laboratory's hands.** The assay did not reproduce; nothing above is read.

## 5. Limits
West 2018 is one laboratory, adult epileptic rat tissue and a non-physiological ACSF; the full text was read from the re-acquired open-access XML (figures not in the bucket); Table 2's EC50 columns are garbled in the extracted XML (cell layout lost), so only the text sentences and Table 1 raw values are used. Every other row is abstract level (PubMed and Europe PMC records read on 2026-11-20); the searches were title and abstract term searches, not exhaustive, and "not found" means not found by these routes. Bath concentrations are nominal and total brain values are not free; the S-KO bursts are NMDAR-dependent in a juvenile mouse neocortex under 1.3 mM Mg2+, unlike the low-Mg2+ models of most rows. The chloride gradient of the S-KO neurons is not held, so a tonic-GABA effect cannot be signed in advance. No receipt is claimed for abstract-level readings; no figure was measured.
