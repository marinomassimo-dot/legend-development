## Question
In callosal slices of the same animals that carry electron-microscopy structure (normal, vehicle EAE, ER-beta-ligand EAE), does structural myelin loss change the stimulus-response (input-output) plateau or slope of the N1 and N2 components (fewer responding fibres: changed recruitment), or only the paired-pulse ratio C2/C1 and refractoriness (conditional failure of fibres that are recruited)?

## Two explanations (fixed now)
* **E-RECRUIT (changed recruitment or responding-fibre proportion):** the lesion lowers the number of fibres that fire at a given stimulus; the input-output plateau and/or slope of N1 (myelinated fibres) falls and C2/C1 barely moves.
* **E-FAIL (conditional failure of recruited fibres):** the same fibres are recruited, but conduction fails or slows under demand; the input-output plateau is similar and the C2/C1 curve shifts (longer refractory or higher failure at short intervals).
(Both can coexist; the table below states what each pattern would be read as.)

## Predicted observable (written before any figure)
My lean is E-RECRUIT: the calibration table (ADV-312/313) shows small velocity changes for large g-ratio changes, which is easier to reconcile with a change in the responding population than with slowed conduction. Predicted pattern for vehicle EAE against normal: N1 input-output plateau lower by a clearly readable fraction, C2/C1 curve (Boltzmann midpoint) shifted by little or not at all.

## Strong counterexample (rejecting observation for the lean)
A C2/C1 rightward shift of the size the paper reports for vehicle EAE against normal, with NO change of the N1 plateau in the same groups. That pattern is E-FAIL.

## Reading rules (frozen)
1. A per-group value counts only if it is a printed table entry or a plotted point or bar read from a figure panel (labelled "eye" with the axis tick used, about 5 percent of axis range); never a value taken from the Results text alone. Text statements are listed apart as text-stated.
2. Components N1 and N2 are read separately; groups: normal, vehicle EAE, ligand-treated EAE as labelled in the paper; the n per group and the replicate unit (slices or animals) are recorded beside every value, or marked UNSTATED.
3. If the figures print no per-group plateau (no input-output curve with readable groups, or only a fitted midpoint), the result is NONE and the exact missing observation is registered: N1 and N2 amplitude per stimulus intensity, with the same animals' EM (g-ratio, axon counts), per group; then stop, no re-scoring of text numbers.
4. The same-animals link (EM and CAP from the same mice) is stated or not stated; if not stated it is UNSTATED, and a group-level comparison is labelled group-level.
5. A cross-check on PMID 31140740 (cuprizone and L-NIO stroke, two bregma levels) stays text-level; figures only if the first delegate has budget left.

## What the result would change
| Pattern | What it changes |
|---|---|
| Plateau (or slope) of N1 down, C2/C1 unchanged | Supports a recruitment reading of a slower or weaker N1 in demyelinating models: for clemastine and dalfampridine the peak-slope caveat (a fitted peak slope shifts when the responding proportion changes) gains a measured precedent; INV-06/RP-049 next experiment should record N1 and N2 amplitude per stimulus intensity and per distance in the same slices before reading any slope as velocity. |
| Plateau unchanged, C2/C1 shifted | Supports conditional failure; the INV-06/RP-049 next experiment adds a paired-pulse or high-frequency protocol; compound dossiers keep a failure-under-demand arm for dalfampridine. |

| Figures print neither curve per group | NONE: the missing observation is registered; no change of role or priority. |
