## Item 2: the KCC2 SLC12A5 report (Europe PMC 42309338) and rows NP3A4 / NP3A5
**What the radar did.** In `capability_scout_log.md` (entries of 2026-11-18) the report appears once, as a hit under the entry `Discovery:reopen:functional_it13_p3a_20261031/NP3A4`. No entry for NP3A5 exists in the log, and 42309338 appears nowhere else in it. So the matcher did not link the report to NP3A5. **My message to ADV that morning said "matched to open rows NP3A4 and NP3A5"; that was my wording error, not the matcher's output**, and IT's reading of the match as wrong came from my sentence. I could not recover from this session what I read when I wrote it; the log is the record.

**The rule the matcher uses** (`framework/scripts/scout_daily.py`, `parse_reopen_table` then `build_questions`): one question per open row (verdict NEEDS-DATUM, PARTIAL or WATCH, with a reopen condition). The search terms are the first three named objects of the row's label plus condition text (acronyms, symbols, drug stems), then one method word; the Europe PMC query is the first two of them joined by AND in title or abstract. A hit is a co-occurrence of two terms, with "no relevance scoring" written into the entry (`Contribution vs existing: unchecked`). Replayed offline on the five NP3A rows: NP3A4 asks `epilepsy AND gramicidin`, NP3A5 asks `gse158684 AND deletion`. The KCC2 paper can come from the first and cannot come from the second. For NP3A4 the match is on topic (a chloride-gradient measurement; the report is a variant with impaired chloride transport, transfer only).

**One real defect found, fixed.** NP3A1's query was `gse158684 AND least`: "at least" in the row's condition text passed as an object word. Fix: `least` added to the stoplist (`STOP` in `scout_daily.py`); the test class `ReopenRowQueries` in `test_scout_daily.py` pins that "least" is not a term and that a chloride row and a glial-deposit row never share a query. Effect: NP3A1 asks a new question once (its cache key follows its terms, by design).

**Limit of the rule.** Two-term co-occurrence will always let weak matches through for rows whose first two terms are common; that is why each entry says the hit is the first look. The intake line IT proposed is added as a short recipe row in `framework/scripts/README.md` section 13: an item matched to a row is triaged by writing the row's question in your own words and what the item says about it, and the matcher output is rechecked against the row text before it is cited.

## Item 3b: BioMedArena glance (about 15 minutes; README, `docs/metrics_guide.md`, `docs/benchmark_datasets.md`, file list)
Repository `AI-in-Health/BioMedArena` (MIT, last push 2026-06-25; a copy `JINGEWU/BioMedArena`, MIT, 2026-04-30). A toolkit that decouples benchmark loading, tool exposure, harness mode, scoring and context management; 166 registered benchmarks, 76 tools, one default seed (42), cost and token counts per benchmark-backbone-mode-seed cell.
* **Scoring of answers.** Per task a `scorer_result` with `method`, `primary_verdict`, `judge_invoked`, `judge_verdict`, `judge_model`. For closed-answer tasks a deterministic scorer runs first and an LLM judge is called only when it says incorrect with a non-empty candidate; **the judge can only promote, never demote** (documented as monotone). For open-ended tasks the judge is the authoritative scorer; the deterministic verdict is kept as metadata. One judge model is pinned per run.
* **Tuned-on versus clean items.** Nothing: the benchmarks are fixed public test sets, loaded by name and split. No notion of a tuned-on item.
* **Contamination check.** None found: no occurrence of contamination, leak or held-out in the README or the two docs read. Public test sets are used as published.
* **Verdict.** Drop as a benchmark source for LEGEND (none of the three things asked for exists). Two patterns are worth a line, both already close to ours: record the scoring method and whether a judge was invoked per item (our STRICT and LOOSE columns do this by hand), and note that a promote-only judge inflates by construction, which is the reason our blind verifier can lower a verdict. The 20-item offline scorer smoke suite (`quick_suite`) is the one idea we do not have: a scorer self-test that runs with no network. Not pursued; the scorers we use are read by hand on small tables.
