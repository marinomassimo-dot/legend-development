## Answer first
1. **Potency, state dependence and exposure do not order the four agents** (HE's finding stands: one origin per agent, no common assay, state-dependence rows disagree in sign, brain exposure not obtainable by a free route for all three). I add nothing there.
2. **What does exist, and BATCH 11 does not carry, is a within-document comparison of ethosuximide with a selective Cav3 blocker in the same mouse seizure assay** (Harman 2026, adult Gabrb3 knock-in mice, atypical-absence video-EEG): ethosuximide 200 mg/kg i.p. and ulixacaltamide 60 mg/kg i.p. each cut seizure incidence by 64 percent against their own vehicle. On that one assay the two are **not distinguishable in effect size**; the selective agent reached it at a 3.3-fold lower dose in mass. No direct contrast between the two was tested.
3. **The dose ratio (3.3) is about three to four orders of magnitude smaller than the in vitro potency ratio** (ethosuximide 0.6 mM or above 5 mM; ulixacaltamide 73 to 86 nM). That mismatch is the most informative number in the comparison and it points two ways that cannot be separated here: ethosuximide acts partly through something other than Cav3 (Leresche 1998 in native thalamic neurons: no effect on the low-threshold calcium current up to 1 mM, 60 percent block of persistent sodium current), or the selective agent's brain exposure at 60 mg/kg is far below its in vitro potency scale (no exposure value held). It argues for a selective-blocker **attribution arm** next to ethosuximide, not for replacing ethosuximide.
4. **The choice that follows (a wording, not a ranking):** ethosuximide stays the bridge lead; if the design carries a selective Cav3 attribution arm, **ulixacaltamide is the only one of the three with an in-hand head-to-head against ethosuximide in a mouse seizure assay**; apinocaltamide may qualify when its 2025 full text is read (comparator not named in the abstract); TTA-A2 and TTA-P2 are tools with mouse records and no ethosuximide arm. This changes no ranking input and rests on no pup datum for any of the four.

## 3. Arithmetic that can change the comparison (every step shown; ASSUMED where marked)
* **Potency ratio.** Ethosuximide 0.6 mM against ulixacaltamide 73 nM (Cav3.2): about 8,200-fold; against 86 nM (Cav3.3) with the above-5-mM row: more than 58,000-fold. Both are cell-assay values from different laboratories (not a within-document pair; the within-document anchor in BATCH 11 is TTA-P2 against ethosuximide, at least about 60,000-fold at alpha1I).
* **Dose ratio in the shared assay.** 200 / 60 = 3.3 in mass; in moles larger, because ethosuximide is 141 g/mol and the selective blocker is a heavier molecule (its own value not looked up here). Casillas-Espinosa gives the same 3.3 in rats (100 against 30 mg/kg, kindling progression).
* **What the gap means.** At equal effect, a 8,000- to 58,000-fold potency advantage corresponds to a dose that is only about 3-fold lower in mass (more in moles, by an amount not computed here). Either exposure per mg/kg differs enormously, or the effect of ethosuximide in this assay is not carried by Cav3 alone. **No held value separates the two.** The selective-arm attribution experiment (ADV-052 d, item 4) is the test; a brain measurement of the selective agent at its effective dose would separate them without the experiment.
* **Ethosuximide exposure margin (ASSUMED linear from one point).** 134.9 microg/g at 72.3 mg/kg is 0.956 mM total (134.9 / 141.17), about 1.87 microg/g per mg/kg. At the Cheng 150 mg/kg: about 2.0 mM; at Harman's 200 mg/kg: about 2.6 mM. Margin over the 0.6 mM row: about 3.3-fold (150) and 4.4-fold (200); over the above-5-mM row: below 0.5. Ten times the 0.6 mM row (6 mM, 847 microg/g) would need about 450 mg/kg on the same linearity. Total, not free; homogenate dilution not corrected; adult mice; pup exposure unknown (the first step in v3.1 section 3.2).
* **No margin can be computed for any selective agent**: no brain concentration is held for any of the three. A ten-times-IC50 target would be 0.73 microM for ulixacaltamide (73 nM), 0.18 microM for apinocaltamide (18 nM) and 0.92 microM for TTA-A2 (92 nM), as **free** concentrations; none is checked against a measured value.

## 4. Anticonvulsant utility apart from WWOX specificity (the bridge half of the operator's step)
Within what is held, T-type blockade works on **absence-type and provoked generalised seizures** (ethosuximide and ulixacaltamide in the adult atypical-absence model; apinocaltamide in two absence rat models; TTA-P2 against PTZ in an ethanol-withdrawal model; TTA-A2 in the MES tonic seizure, where ethosuximide is inactive) and **not on focal kindled seizures** (ethosuximide and Z944 in fully kindled rats; apinocaltamide in a kindling model). The Wwox-null pup assay is a PTZ-provoked generalised seizure score (Cheng 2020), so the class fits the assay type; **what fits the bridge assay says nothing about WWOX specificity** (ADV-052 a: no source links WWOX loss to Cav3, the four held transcript sets disagree, no Cav3 current is measured in any Wwox-deficient neuron). The anticonvulsant utility and the WWOX rationale are separate rows and stay separate.

## 5. What would change a choice (stated after reading, section 0)
* **R1.** The selective attribution arm is chosen on a within-document seizure comparison with ethosuximide in a mouse assay with doses and a tolerability scale reported, not on potency rows. Today that selects ulixacaltamide alone. It would change to apinocaltamide if the 2025 full text names ethosuximide as the comparator with a dose ladder and a tolerability readout; to TTA-P2 only if a naive-PTZ mouse record with an ethosuximide arm appears.
* **R2.** Any pup brain concentration of a selective agent (the first-step measurement of v3.1 section 3.2, extended to that agent) makes exposure the ordering variable: the margin over the human-channel IC50 of the same agent, total and free stated. Without it the selective arm is **uninterpretable as a null**, as ethosuximide is (v3.1 3.3).
* **R3.** A tolerance row: sedation at 30 to 100 mg/kg in adult rats (Casillas-Espinosa) is a reason to read the selective arm's highest tolerated exposure before it is read as a null; a tolerance-limited demotion of the selective arm is a separate outcome from a null.
* **R4.** A Cav3-knockout-by-drug experiment (any of the three agents, or ethosuximide) would outrank every potency or exposure row for the T-type question; none is held (ADV-052 c).
* **Not changing:** no ranking of ethosuximide against any of the three; no statement that a selective blocker is better or safer in a pup; no claim about any person.

## 6. Limits and what is unresolved
Harman 2026 is one laboratory, adult mice, a Gabrb3 knock-in model of atypical absence, i.p. single doses, a crossover with unequal n (9 and 11) and separate vehicle comparisons; sedation not quantified; the two doses were not matched by exposure. Casillas-Espinosa 2015 is adult rats and kindling, a different seizure type. The kindling and atypical-absence assays are not the Wwox-null PTZ assay; no document gives a Wwox-null animal a selective Cav3 blocker. The one-origin flags of BATCH 11 are carried unchanged. The MW of ulixacaltamide was not looked up. Kessler 2025 and the TTA-A2 arousal paper (PMID 20682849) were not read beyond HE's and IT's abstract readings. Not read: the ChEMBL rows (HE's sheet is the origin; raw answers outside git).
