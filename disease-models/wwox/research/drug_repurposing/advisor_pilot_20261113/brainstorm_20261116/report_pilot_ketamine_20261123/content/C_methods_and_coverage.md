# Methods and coverage

AI-curated research brief for discussion with a clinical team. Hypothesis level. Reference genotype class only. No dose, schedule or recommendation anywhere in this brief. Numbers in square brackets are entries of the reference list.

## Corpus and cutoff
* **Source of the content:** a ketamine literature search by an AI-assisted research team (a first synthesis dated 2026-11-17 with dated updates to 2026-11-19, several working notes, two pharmacokinetic notes and a kinetics comparison). Three working sessions and fourteen helper agents read primary sources; each group of claims was checked in a separate context.
* **Verification date of this brief:** 2026-11-23. Every value placed in the evidence and quantitative tables was re-read on that date at the depth shown in its Status column (full text, figure or table; or PubMed abstract), except where a row says it is carried from an internal record (row D1.12) (check record: it groups the decision-critical values into 20 rows, of which 13 were reproduced from full text, a figure or a table and 7 only from PubMed abstracts or the title of one section; none left unverified at decision-critical level. The tables of this brief hold more rows than the check record, because several table rows rest on the same record row, and more table rows are abstract-level than record rows, because the clinical and kinetics tables are mostly abstract-level).
* **Literature cutoff:** records up to the verification date; the newest source used is dated 2026 [8]. A PubMed search on 2026-11-23 for ketamine, memantine or NMDA-receptor antagonists together with WWOX, Wwox or WOREE returned no records.

## How sources were selected
* The search started from one published finding (Breton et al. [1]: Wwox-deletion cortical slices burst; d-APV stops the bursts) and asked what a transfer to ketamine would need: rival mechanisms, exposure, kinetics, clinical settings and harms.
* For this brief a value enters a table only if it reproduces from the original source with a locator. Statements the team holds but that were not re-read in this pass are not placed in the tables (see "Carried but not re-verified").
* Not used for the decision: general selection among eight candidate drugs, vigabatrin, and T-type calcium-channel and chloride-transport branches (they belong to other questions).

## Changes made during internal review
Three statements in this brief were changed after internal review on 2026-11-23, and are logged in the check record: (1) the cerebrospinal-fluid-to-plasma value 0.917 in [14] is a ratio of AUC medians, not of peaks, and now sits only on a labelled AUC row; (2) the absence statement was narrowed to "no dedicated ketamine or memantine intervention comparison in a WWOX system", because the d-APV arm and the incidental ketamine-xylazine anaesthesia are real, distinct observations; (3) a row of the deciding-measurement table is labelled an inference, and the electrographic-seizure counts are labelled events observed after exposure, not a treatment-attributable incidence. Two further rounds of internal review (fresh readers of the rendered brief) changed further wording; a source-internal inconsistency in the pharmacokinetic table of [14] was added to the quantitative section. All are logged in the same check record.

## Limits of this brief
It reports what the sources say about a hypothesis; it is not an assessment of any child, not a treatment plan and not clinical validation. Most clinical sources are at abstract level. Preparations differ (mouse slice, recombinant cells, rat cultures, children, adult volunteers) and are never pooled. The team that made the search is not a clinical review team.
