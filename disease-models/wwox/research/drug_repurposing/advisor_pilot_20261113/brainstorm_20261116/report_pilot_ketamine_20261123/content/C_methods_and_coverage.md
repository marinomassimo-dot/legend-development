# Methods and coverage

AI-curated research brief for discussion with a clinical team. Hypothesis level. Reference genotype class only. No dose, schedule or recommendation anywhere in this brief. Numbers in square brackets are entries of the reference list.

## Corpus and cutoff
* **Source of the content:** a ketamine literature search by an AI-assisted research team (a first synthesis dated 2026-11-17 with dated updates to 2026-11-19, several lane notes, two pharmacokinetic notes and a kinetics comparison). Three working sessions and fourteen delegate agents read primary sources; each lane was checked in a separate context.
* **Verification date of this brief:** 2026-11-23. Every value placed in the evidence and quantitative tables was re-read against the original source on that date (verification ledger: 20 value rows, of which 14 were reproduced from full text, a figure or a table, and 6 from PubMed abstracts; none left unverified at decision-critical level).
* **Literature cutoff:** records up to the verification date; the newest source used is dated 2026 [8]. A PubMed search on 2026-11-23 for ketamine, memantine or NMDA-receptor antagonists together with WWOX, Wwox or WOREE returned no records.

## How sources were selected
* The search started from one published finding (Breton et al. [1]: Wwox-deletion cortical slices burst; d-APV stops the bursts) and asked what a transfer to ketamine would need: rival mechanisms, exposure, kinetics, clinical settings and harms.
* For this brief a value enters a table only if it reproduces from the original source with a locator. Statements the team holds but that were not re-read in this pass are not placed in the tables (see "Carried but not re-verified").
* Not used for the decision: general selection among eight candidate drugs, vigabatrin, and T-type calcium-channel and chloride-transport branches (they belong to other questions).

## Changes made during internal review
Three statements in this brief were changed after internal review on 2026-11-23, and are logged in the verification ledger: (1) the cerebrospinal-fluid-to-plasma value 0.917 in [14] is a ratio of AUC medians, not of peaks, and now sits only on a labelled AUC row; (2) the absence statement was narrowed to "no dedicated ketamine or memantine intervention comparison in a WWOX system", because the d-APV arm and the incidental ketamine-xylazine anaesthesia are real, distinct observations; (3) a row of the deciding-measurement table is labelled an inference, and the electrographic-seizure counts are labelled events observed after exposure, not a treatment-attributable incidence. A second round of internal review (cold read of the layout) changed further wording; it is logged in the same ledger.

## Carried but not re-verified in this pass (kept out of the tables)
Values from the team's search that bear on harms or on other candidates and were not re-read here: paediatric status-epilepticus series counts, hypotension counts in intensive-care infusion series, the serious-event rate in sedated children, a gain-of-function GRIN2D mouse observation, label statements on juvenile rats, protein-binding percentages, a registry count for one trial. They remain in the team's internal records with their own verification states; none supports a number in the evidence tables.

## Limits of this brief
It reports what the sources say about a hypothesis; it is not an assessment of any child, not a treatment plan and not clinical validation. Most clinical sources are at abstract level. Preparations differ (mouse slice, recombinant cells, rat cultures, children, adult volunteers) and are never pooled. The team that made the search is not a clinical review team.
