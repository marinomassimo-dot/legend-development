**Note on Q9b to Q10d: the printed values in [14] do not agree with each other.** The paper prints, for plasma ketamine, a peak of 112.87 ng/mL, a half-life of 592.99 min and an AUC of 2990.36 ng.min/mL (CSF: 42.36 ng/mL, 545.5 min, 2742.64 ng.min/mL), and the transcription is right. But the printed AUC is only about 1.3 times the area of the rising phase alone (a triangle from zero to the peak at 40 min is about 2260 ng.min/mL for plasma), which leaves almost no area for the 28 hours of sampled decline; for CSF the printed AUC is about 1.1 times the rising-phase triangle (about 2540 for 120 min). A single exponential tail from the printed peak at the printed half-life would add very much more than the printed AUC (tens of thousands of ng.min/mL); a two-phase decline would add less, so the size of the gap is uncertain, but its direction is not. The AUC unit, the half-life or the AUC window is therefore off in the paper, and the values for the main metabolite behave the same way (plasma peak 59.27 ng/mL, half-life 1200.23 min, AUC 4820.98 ng.min/mL). The ratio 0.917 is the paper's own pair of printed medians and must not be used as an estimate of CSF penetration. No ratio of peaks is offered either, because the peaks occur at different times.

**Reading these rows.** A nominal bath value compared with a plasma value is a comparison of two different quantities; it says where the bath value sits relative to what was found, not what any tissue receives. Read against the highest group-mean peak found, the applied concentrations of 4.1 to 15.6 uM are in the same range (8.2 uM is 0.93 times it); read against the adult volunteer median peak they are 8.6 to 33 times higher. The three columns are different statistics (a group-mean peak, one value at steady state, a median peak) and are not interchangeable. The highest value found is the top of the few reports opened, a group mean in an anaesthetised-child nasal arm, not a biological limit. Free fraction, brain-to-plasma ratio, infant exposure, exposure in status epilepticus and a multi-day infusion beyond one case were not found in the sources read. The authors of [4] call their effective concentrations "clinically relevant", citing a free anaesthetic plasma level of about 5 to 10 uM ([4], Results, Fig 1C paragraph; their Discussion adds that these concentrations are "somewhat higher than" those for NMDA-receptor inhibition). This brief does not settle that disagreement: the measured plasma values and the primary paper disagree about the same quantity.
