# A. Decision evidence: does the Wwox-deletion slice finding transfer to ketamine?

AI-curated research brief for discussion with a clinical team. Hypothesis level. Reference genotype class only (WWOX-related developmental and epileptic encephalopathy); no individual-level information. This page contains no dose, no schedule and no recommendation for or against any use of any drug in any person. Pack drafted 2026-11-23 by SCI (task ADV-200); every value in the tables was re-read against the original on that date (see C and the verification ledger).

**How to read the tables.** Stage: **D** Data (read in a source), **I** Inference (reasoned from data), **H** Hypothesis (to be tested), **E** Experiment (a proposed measurement). Status: **VERIFIED** (reproduced from the original full text, figure or table, locator given), **ABSTRACT-ONLY** (reproduced from the PubMed abstract opened that day), **UNVERIFIED** (carried from team notes, not reproduced). Numbers in square brackets are entries of list B. Three blocks are kept apart on purpose: WWOX preclinical data, clinical data from other indications, and the proposed comparison.

## The decision this page serves
Whether a research group should spend a slice experiment on a use-dependent NMDA-receptor pore blocker such as ketamine in the published Wwox-deletion preparation. The published finding is that bursts depend on NMDA-receptor activation; it was obtained with d-APV, a competitive antagonist that blocks the receptor at a different site. Ketamine was not tested as an intervention in any WWOX system found (the only exposure is incidental anaesthesia in vivo, W7).

## What the evidence supports, in plain words
* **Data:** neuronal Wwox deletion makes neocortical slices burst; d-APV stops the bursts in the five slices tested; inhibitory currents are smaller and sag is larger.
* **Inference, narrow:** NMDA-receptor activation contributes to the bursts in that preparation.
* **Hypothesis, untested:** a pore blocker would reproduce the effect. Three rivals (Ih, disinhibition, sedation context) make a ketamine result in this slice hard to read unless matched block depth and sag are recorded.
* **Not established:** any benefit of ketamine in a WWOX system (no dedicated ketamine arm exists; the d-APV arm and the incidental anaesthesia are not that), in any person, acutely or chronically; developmental benefit; safety in infants of the class. The clinical data are from other settings and are not pooled.
