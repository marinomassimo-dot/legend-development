# F. The one measurement that would change the choice

AI-curated research brief for discussion with a clinical team. Hypothesis level. This page names a laboratory measurement, not a treatment; it contains no dose and no recommendation.

## The measurement
In the published neuron-specific Wwox-knockout cortical slice preparation [1]: a **matched-depth comparison** of d-APV and a use-dependent pore blocker, with the **burst rate** recorded against the **fractional reduction of the evoked NMDA-receptor response** (several depths, not one), **time-matched vehicle**, reads during block and at several times after washout, and **sag voltage recorded in the same cells**. The laboratory chooses the agent, the concentration series and the pore blocker; the depth reached at each concentration is the quantity to report.

## What each outcome would change
| Observation | Reading it supports | Effect on the choice |
|---|---|---|
| The pore blocker lowers burst rate at matched depth, as d-APV does, and sag does not track the drug | NMDA-receptor route holds for a pore blocker in this preparation | The pore-blocker arm moves from parked to testable in an intact model; it still says nothing about efficacy or safety in children |
| Burst rate falls only when sag falls, and d-APV leaves sag unchanged | **INFERENCE (prioritises an Ih explanation; does not establish it).** An association of sag with the burst effect, with no sag effect for d-APV, leaves mediation open. | It would justify a **new experiment**: an independent perturbation of Ih/HCN in the matched preparation (for example an HCN-selective manipulation) with the NMDA-response endpoint still interpretable. Until then the class transfer weakens only provisionally. |
| Bursting rises at shallow depth and falls at deep depth | Disinhibition component | Dose-dependence is bell-shaped in this preparation; the rationale for a low-exposure effect weakens |
| Burst rate rises above vehicle after washout and decays | Rebound | A transient benefit would not be clean; relevant to any short-exposure rationale |
| The pore blocker fails to suppress at verified deep depth while d-APV reproduces its effect | Weakens the NMDA-receptor rationale for that agent in this preparation | One agent at one exposure in one preparation is an agent-level result; a class-level demotion needs two structurally different agents at verified depth |

## Why this one
* It is the cheapest experiment whose outcomes separate the three rivals that predict the same simple readout (Ih, disinhibition, rebound) in the one preparation where the finding exists.
* It uses d-APV as a positive control in the same slices, so a failure of the pore blocker cannot be blamed on a slice that stopped bursting.
* It needs a laboratory with the knockout line and slice electrophysiology (an operator item).

## What it would not change
Whether ketamine helps any child with this condition; developmental benefit; safety in infants; the clinical comparators in other indications (D). Exposure is a separate condition: the concentration that gives deep block must be reported and compared with the exposures in D, and if it lies well above them the clinical reach of the result is unknown rather than excluded.
