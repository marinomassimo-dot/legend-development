# The deciding measurement: the one experiment that would change the choice

AI-curated research brief for discussion with a clinical team. Hypothesis level. This page names a laboratory measurement, not a treatment; it contains no dose and no recommendation. Numbers in square brackets are entries of the reference list.

## The measurement
In the published neuron-specific Wwox-knockout cortical slice preparation [1]: a **matched-depth comparison** of d-APV and a use-dependent pore blocker, with the **burst rate** recorded against the **fractional reduction of the evoked NMDA-receptor response** (several depths, not one), **time-matched vehicle**, reads during block and at several times after washout, and **sag voltage recorded in the same cells**. The laboratory chooses the agent, the concentration series and the pore blocker; the depth reached at each concentration is the quantity to report.

**What is not yet set.** No numeric decision threshold (for example, how large a fall in burst rate counts as suppression), no sample size and no power calculation exist yet. Setting them is part of the experiment's pre-registration and must be done before any slice is recorded; this brief does not pretend to provide them.

## What each outcome would change
Four alternative readings compete with the simple one (Ih, disinhibition, rebound, and sedation or co-medication context). The experiment separates the first three; the fourth is outside the reach of any slice.

| Observation | Reading it supports | Status of the reading | Effect on the choice |
|---|---|---|---|
| The pore blocker lowers burst rate at matched depth, as d-APV does, and sag does not track the drug | NMDA-receptor route holds for a pore blocker in this preparation | Inference | The pore-blocker arm moves from "not currently scheduled" to testable in an intact model; it still says nothing about efficacy or safety in children |
| Burst rate falls only when sag falls, and d-APV leaves sag unchanged | An Ih (HCN1) explanation is prioritised. An association of sag with the burst effect, with no sag effect for d-APV, does not establish mediation | **Inference (prioritises, does not establish)** | It would justify a **new experiment**: an independent perturbation of Ih/HCN in the matched preparation (for example an HCN-selective manipulation), with the NMDA-response endpoint still interpretable. Until then the class transfer weakens only provisionally |
| Burst rate **rises** when sag falls | The Ih route has the opposite sign from a suppressing effect, as the Breton Discussion warns for an Ih blocker (a cited report found worse seizure-like events) | Inference | Any ketamine effect in this slice would be hard to read as protective; the pore-blocker rationale cannot be tested in this preparation without separating Ih first |
| Bursting rises at shallow depth and falls at deep depth | Disinhibition component | Inference | The relation to concentration is bell-shaped in this preparation; the rationale for an effect at low exposure weakens |
| Burst rate rises above vehicle after washout and then decays | Rebound | Inference | A transient benefit would not be clean; relevant to any short-exposure rationale |
| The pore blocker fails to suppress at verified deep depth while d-APV reproduces its effect | Weakens the NMDA-receptor rationale for that agent in this preparation | Inference | One agent at one exposure in one preparation is an agent-level result; a class-level demotion needs two structurally different agents at verified depth |

## Why this one
* It is the cheapest experiment whose outcomes separate the three alternatives that predict the same simple readout (Ih, disinhibition, rebound) in the one preparation where the finding exists.
* It uses d-APV as a positive control in the same slices, so a failure of the pore blocker cannot be blamed on a slice that stopped bursting.
* It needs a laboratory with the knockout line and slice electrophysiology.

## What it would not change
Whether ketamine helps any child with this condition; developmental benefit; safety in infants; the clinical comparators in other indications (see the negative-results and quantitative section). Exposure is a separate condition: the concentration that gives deep block must be reported and compared with the exposures in the quantitative table, and if it lies well above them the clinical reach of the result is unknown rather than excluded.
