## HIT-1 (LEAP) Discordance is a timing artefact, not a drug property
Perspective used: time structure (discordance of seizure and development endpoints). Statement: development is largely fixed before the treatment window opens (structural findings such as thin corpus callosum and periventricular cysts are already present on imaging before any treatment in the A1 series), so seizure-control classes can change the seizure endpoint without moving development, and the discordance share is the same across agent classes; rejecting observation: among children with both endpoints, the discordance share differs by agent class or by presence of pre-treatment imaging abnormality (children with a normal pre-treatment imaging and a seizure response show development improvement). Why a leap: the usual reading looks for a drug or pathway that decouples the two endpoints; this reading drops the premise that treatment is on the causal path of development at all. Existing assay or table: `batch_sci_20261105/a1_wwox_reports/cases_a1.tsv` columns `concordance`, `imaging_finding`, `onset_age_as_stated` (6 children with both endpoints). Single discriminating observation: discordant children with pre-treatment structural imaging versus concordant children without it; outcome one (discordance tracks imaging): the matrix's development endpoint is not a drug-class question and the F3 null stands as a design fact, development moves to a separate thread; outcome two (discordance does not track imaging): the timing reading is rejected and development-specific classes (GSK3, ISR) keep their rows. Repurposing class: none for development by this hypothesis (a negative hypothesis); seizure classes unchanged. Held-data pre-test: not run by IT; the A1 columns above can reject it. What would change my mind: one discordant child with documented normal pre-treatment imaging and early control, and one concordant child with pre-treatment abnormalities. Cost to test: table lookups (6 children), no animals.

## HIT-2 (LINEAR) The spasm window and the later seizure types are two generators with two class profiles
Perspective used: time structure (sequence of seizure types). Statement: epileptic spasms with hypsarrhythmia in the first months belong to an early-window state (immature GABA/GluN2B balance, the 53% GluN2B share at P6-P8) and respond to the GABA-AT and hormonal classes, while the 'other seizure types' that appear after spasm control (reported in two A1 children after vigabatrin) belong to a later network-threshold state (the 14% share at P13-P15) with a different class profile; rejecting observation: the same class that controls spasms also controls the later focal types in the same child, or the later types appear in children whose spasms were never controlled. Why a leap: the matrix scores one seizure endpoint per age window; this splits the endpoint by seizure type within a child. Existing assay or table: `cases_a1.tsv` (spasm and other-seizure-type statements per child) and the sealed extraction notes; the mouse age windows of WO-11 and INV-05. Discriminating observation: per child, class response for spasms versus for later types; outcome one (profiles differ): a seizure-type endpoint is added to the matrix and F1's age-window logic gets a clinical anchor; outcome two (same class works for both): one generator, and the age-window split is not clinically supported. Repurposing class: GABA-AT/GAT and steroid/ACTH labels for the spasm window (mechanism source: A1 statements), NMDAR-active labels for the later window only as a stated untested class. Held-data pre-test: partial: the 43 attributable rows record seizure type in the notes for some children; not run. What would change my mind: a child whose later focal seizures stop with the same class that stopped spasms. Cost: table lookups.

## HIT-3 (LEAP) The class ranking is confounded by line of therapy
Perspective used: time structure (order in the treatment sequence). Statement: improvement shares by class reflect where in the sequence the class was given (add-on steps after earlier agents, steps that the authors attribute explicitly), so 'resolved' shares rise for classes that are usually added late and fall for first-line classes, and the F1 and baseline concordances partly measure treatment order; rejecting observation: within each class the improved share of children given the class as add-on equals the share of children given it as a tried first step. Why a leap: the usual reading treats a class share as a property of the class; this drops the independence of exposure order and outcome. Existing assay or table: the sealed `it_extractor_20261116/sealed/extraction.tsv` column `status` (tried, added_on, continued, stopped) against `seizure_outcome` by class. Discriminating observation: share by status within each class; outcome one (shares differ by status): the scoring result of 2026-11-16 must be restated on first-step rows only and the 'not claimed' becomes 'confounded'; outcome two (equal): the order confound is not visible at these n. Repurposing class: none (a measurement-validity hypothesis). Held-data pre-test: can be tested now on the extraction (not run by IT before freezing this file). What would change my mind: equal shares within class by status with the interval including a difference of 0. Cost: a table lookup; n is small (43 attributable rows), so the likely result is 'cannot say'.

## HIT-4 (LINEAR) The high GABA-AT share is a spasm-phenotype base rate, not a WWOX match
Perspective used: base rates from other DEEs as priors. Statement: the improved share on vigabatrin in the WWOX children (8 of 9 with partial counted, 4 of 9 resolved) equals the published responder share of vigabatrin among infantile-spasm cohorts of other genetic aetiologies, so the GABA-AT class is a match for the phenotype 'epileptic spasms' and not for WWOX loss; rejecting observation: the WWOX share lies outside the interval of that base rate. Why a leap: F2 counts the high share as support of a WWOX-specific mechanistic match; this reads it as a base rate of the seizure type. Existing assay or table: the sealed extraction (vigabatrin rows) and a lawful public census of vigabatrin response in other genetic spasm cohorts (PubMed abstracts, census level only). Discriminating observation: WWOX share against the base-rate interval; outcome one (inside): F2 is a phenotype prior, the GABA-AT row in the matrix gets a 'phenotype-generic' flag like PTZ; outcome two (outside): a WWOX-specific excess to be examined (still not causal). Repurposing class: GABA-AT/GAT and steroid/ACTH as labels (source: A1 and extraction). Held-data pre-test: not run; needs the external base rate. What would change my mind: a base rate far lower than 8 of 9 from a well-defined cohort with the same coding. Cost: about 5 abstract-level lookups.

## HIT-5 (LEAP) Lead time to control, not class, separates development outcomes among discordant children
Perspective used: time structure (latency between spasm onset and control). Statement: among children whose seizures responded, the developmental outcome is associated with the interval between onset and the first control (how long hypsarrhythmia ran) rather than with the class that achieved control; rejecting observation: two children with similar latency and opposite development, or with different latency and the same development. Why a leap: it drops the class as the explanatory variable and replaces it with the clock. Existing assay or table: `cases_a1.tsv` (`onset_age_as_stated`, treatment status, `developmental_outcome`) for the 6 both-endpoint children; the INV-10 count of 10+ when it grows (PS-09). Discriminating observation: rank correlation of latency with development in children with both endpoints (n = 6, so descriptive); outcome one (association): the development thread is a timing thread and an observational registry design, not a class-ranking; outcome two (none): timing is not the variable and the discordance stays unexplained. Repurposing class: none by this hypothesis (a timing hypothesis; no treatment statement is made). Held-data pre-test: not run; latencies are partly unstated, so this may be untestable at n = 6, which would itself be the result. What would change my mind: latency unstated in most of the six, so untestable. Cost: table lookups.
