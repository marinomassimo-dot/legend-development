## Answer first
1. **P1 (enhanced astrogenesis in knockout organoids): INCOMPATIBLE with the release hypothesis, provisional (abstract only).** The pre-registered expectation is confirmed at abstract level: in neural precursors, neuregulin-driven, presenilin-dependent ERBB4 cleavage releases an intracellular domain that forms a complex with TAB2 and N-CoR, enters the nucleus, binds astrocytic gene promoters and **represses** them, and ErbB4-knockout mice show precocious astrogenesis (Sardi 2006, mouse and rat per the abstract). A 2026 preprint (abstract only, not peer reviewed, same research lineage, so not an independent confirmation) reports an ICD-PARP1 complex repressing the GFAP promoter, with loss of ErbB4 or PARP1 raising astrocyte differentiation. If WWOX loss released more nuclear ICD, fewer astrocytes would be predicted; the organoid phenotype is more astrogenesis.
2. **P2 (fewer PV-positive cells): UNDETERMINED.** No original isolating the ERBB4 ICD in PV interneurons was found; the reports seen concern ErbB4 loss or kinase signalling (ErbB4 deleted in PV cells lowered GABAergic transmission in an Alzheimer-model mouse) or the NRG1 intracellular domain (not ERBB4, not PV). One route only (PubMed term queries, abstracts): this is not an absence finding.
3. **Post hoc item (not pre-registered):** a 2004 rat-culture report (abstract only) found that nuclear ICD accumulation did not change neuron or astrocyte maturation but **promoted oligodendrocyte maturation** (MBP); under the release hypothesis this would predict more oligodendrocyte maturation, the opposite of the hypomyelination of Wwox-loss mice (which is non-cell-autonomous, from neuronal deletion). Weak, abstract level, different species.
4. **Consequence under the frozen rule, applied with the evidence limit:** the simple release hypothesis is **provisionally dropped** and the localisation test is **not motivated by these two phenotypes**; the open-question row is re-worded to ask only whether WWOX binds ERBB4 in neurons. The drop becomes firm only after the Sardi 2006 full text is read (lawful route: the operator's supply path) to confirm that ICD-specific experiments exist and to record sizes and n.
5. **What the check does not settle:** the sign of WWOX's effect on ERBB4 ICD localisation in neurons is still untested (the relation assumes WWOX retains the ICD, from cancer-cell abstracts); a different sign (WWOX required for nuclear ICD function) would reverse the conclusion; astrogenesis and PV changes in WWOX loss may arise through other pathways (the organoid DNA-damage and Wnt findings; stage).
