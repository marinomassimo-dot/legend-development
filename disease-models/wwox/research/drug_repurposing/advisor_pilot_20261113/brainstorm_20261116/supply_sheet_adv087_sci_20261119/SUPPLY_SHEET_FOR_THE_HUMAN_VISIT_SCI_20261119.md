# Supply sheet: papers a person with a browser can fetch (SCI, 2026-11-19; replaces the scattered lists of decision-page item 14; research aid at hypothesis level; not medical advice)

**Nothing in this sheet is sent to anyone: no e-mail, no request, no account, no payment. You only download files for yourself and save them in one folder.**

## How to do it (about 30 to 45 minutes for the first block, the rest at your own pace)
1. Take the texts **in the order of the table** (the order is the decision each could change; stop whenever you like: every row stands alone).
2. Open the link in a normal browser. A "verify you are human" tick box is yours to answer; the computer tools could not. If a page asks for **payment, an account or an e-mail address, skip that text** and write "asked for payment" next to it: do not pay, do not register.
3. Where the table says "library route", ask your public or university library for the article by its DOI (interlibrary loan is free or cheap at most libraries; ask the librarian). If that is not possible, skip it.
4. Save each file **with the name given in the last column** (the PMID at the start is what lets us find it again) in the folder `files/fulltext/` of the shared LEGEND checkout (`/home/desktop/legend-development/files/fulltext/`). If you cannot reach that folder, leave the file in your **Downloads** folder with the same name: we collect it from there.
5. Tell ADV which rows you got, which you could not, and why (one line each is enough).

## What happens afterwards (for ADV or SCI; one command each, nothing leaves the machine)
1. **The three texts read in August whose bytes are missing from this checkout** (rows 14, 15, 16): `python3 framework/scripts/evidence_presence.py --search <Downloads folder>` finds them by fingerprint (SHA-256) even under another name and says RECOVERABLE; adding `--restore` copies an exact match to its declared place and never overwrites.
2. **Every other file:** it is found by the PMID at the start of its name in `files/fulltext/`; the first look checks that it is a real full text and not a payment or "verify you are human" page; a file that is only an abstract page is reported back, not used.
3. **Before anything is recorded:** the text is read completely, then the receipt is tried with `python3 framework/scripts/fulltext_receipts.py record --dry-run` and only then written. Nothing is built on a text before it is read. Row order of reading: 1, 2, 3 first, then 4 to 11 as a group, then 18 to 21 against the PV measurement; rows 12 to 17 when the question they serve (ERBB4, glucose, vigabatrin) is opened.
4. The identifiers above come from the repository's own catalogue; row 12 is given by PMID and title only because the catalogue holds no DOI for it. If a link does not lead to the paper, search the title.
