**Nothing here is medical advice.** No dose, no schedule, no recommendation for or against any use of any drug; the decision belongs to the treating clinical team. The question put to the team: could ketamine, or the sedation context in which it is given, have a transient positive effect, and by which mechanisms? The statement is about the drug and the mechanisms, not about a case.

## In plain words (five lines)
1. **What we know:** ketamine has never been given to any WWOX system in anything the repository holds; what is held is the drug class (NMDAR pore blockers) in a model, in a laboratory tool experiment, and in other diseases.
2. **What we do not know:** whether ketamine does anything for the reference genotype class, whether it helps or harms, and what could explain any transient benefit.
3. **What is hypothesis:** a lasting plasticity change is possible in principle, but nothing held contains the slow process it would need; a possible aggravation counterpart (see line 4).
4. **The harms:** retrieved at abstract level in other settings: hypertension and delirium (3 of 69 in one cohort), dizziness, sedation and dissociative symptoms (mild and transient, certainty low, a review of oral (es)ketamine), emergence agitation (lower with a ketamine-containing combination in one trial); **not retrieved**: quantified paediatric emergence-reaction rates, secretion effects, liver, bladder, abuse liability, repeated exposure; early-life anaesthetic-neurotoxicity is a concern raised for infants and young children; in the held laboratory slice, bursting rebounded above baseline after washout of a competitive blocker. The harm profile is incomplete in the repository.
5. **What we cannot say:** that ketamine was or was not the cause of any benefit, that it is safe or unsafe for anyone, or that any step is advisable; no BLOCK-1 safety screening was ever run for it.

## Layer 2 - GENERAL KNOWLEDGE, UNVERIFIED (recollection; not held; check against a label or review before any use)
**HE's caveat travels with this layer: the repository's harm profile for ketamine is incomplete. Retrieved at abstract level: hypertension and delirium, dizziness, sedation and dissociative symptoms, emergence agitation. Not retrieved: quantified paediatric emergence-reaction rates, secretion effects, liver and bladder effects, abuse liability, repeated exposure, human anaesthetic-neurotoxicity cohorts specific to ketamine.**
* Ketamine blocks the open NMDAR channel with a short half-life (hours) and active metabolites; adult mood effects after a single low exposure are reported to last days to about a week (unverified). This possible **benefit** is paired with its **harms**: dissociation and emergence reactions, raised blood pressure and heart rate, increased secretions, bladder and liver effects with repeated use, labelled abuse potential.
* Early-life anaesthetic-neurotoxicity concern: reported in rodents (several abstracts, each from one laboratory); the human cohort data concern general anaesthetics, not ketamine, and are contradictory and confounded. It is a concern raised for infants and young children; human evidence for ketamine specifically is not established.
* Benefits seen with other sedatives (calm, sleep, seizure control during an infusion) are paired with their **harms**: respiratory depression, hypotension, rebound or withdrawal on abrupt discontinuation, delirium.
* The label status of ketamine in any given person is not held; a clinician decides what applies.

## Layer 3 - HYPOTHESIS (rival explanations of a transient positive effect in a sedation context; none is evidence about any person)
| Explanation | Why it is a rival | Harm or caution beside it |
|---|---|---|
| An illness or inflammation resolved (fever, infection, pain, metabolic derangement) | a return to the pre-illness baseline on the same timescale; anchor: improvement **during** fever in about 1 of 6 children with autism by parent report (retrospective; associated with maternal infection and GI dysfunction; not an improvement after fever and not weeks) | relapse if the driver returns; the same illness may have caused the loss |
| Co-given sedatives, rest and sleep restoration | GABAergic sedatives suppress bursts in the model; sleep debt is a known provoker | sedation, respiratory and withdrawal risks |
| **Co-given anticholinergic agents that reduce secretions** (often given with ketamine: recalled, unverified; the agent class must be recorded) | a centrally acting anticholinergic can change alertness, tone and seizure threshold; a peripherally acting one has no brain effect: the class decides | anticholinergic delirium, sedation, heat and urinary effects; class and timing unrecorded here |
| An antibiotic resolved a driver infection | improvement tracks the infection | some classes lower the seizure threshold |
| A medication change around the episode (withheld, discontinued or reduced) | the benefit would reverse on restart | withdrawing an antiseizure drug is itself a risk |
| Lower seizure or discharge burden from any drug | the one capacity the model speaks to | burden may rebound after withdrawal (the 1.85 times washout overshoot, in a laboratory slice) |
| The NMDAR block itself, through a lasting plasticity change | needs a slow variable (plasticity, maturation, bistability); none is held | **single exposure:** the held acute harm datum is a few hours of block in late-fetal or newborn rat brain (apoptosis, abstract); **chronic block** in a developing brain removes a maturation signal (inferred, no WWOX datum); neither is a human datum |
| Regression to the mean and reporting effects | a recorded low point, then a return | none: this is why a blinded, standardised measure matters |
| Several of the above together | co-occurring components cannot be separated by any amount of records | unknown |
**Judgements (SCI's; from the timescales of the rival table, not measurements):** the non-drug or non-NMDAR explanations are **more parsimonious** for a transient benefit, and a sole NMDAR cause has **no model support here**.
