# Ketamine: a three-layer statement for the operator (IT draft, 2026-11-17; SCI and HE correct it after this draft; no dose, no recommendation; no testimony; reference genotype class only)

**Nothing here is medical advice.** The decision to use or not use any drug belongs to the treating clinical team. This statement separates what the repository holds from what is general knowledge and from what is only a hypothesis; every sentence about a possible benefit has its harm beside it.

## Layer 1 - HELD (the repository and the censuses; origin and tier stated)
| What is held | Origin and tier | The harm or limit beside it |
|---|---|---|
| Ketamine has never been given to any WWOX system (animal, tissue or person) in any held file. | the repository, absence | so there is no WWOX benefit and no WWOX harm to report: both are unmeasured |
| In the neuron-specific Wwox-knockout slice, a competitive NMDAR blocker (d-APV, a laboratory tool, not ketamine) abolishes the bursts. | Breton 2021 via the repository; tool, acute slice | after washout the bursting rose to about 1.85 times baseline: a held hint of rebound |
| In our model of that slice, a pore blocker such as ketamine shortens bursts and lengthens the intervals (-54 percent, +55 percent); the effect disappears minutes after the block ends. SCI's hysteresis sweep found no memory in the model over minutes to about an hour. | simulation (tier 3), fitted to the same slice; the effect lasts only as long as the drug acts | a simulation cannot confirm itself; a weeks-long carry-over needs a slow variable that is not held |
| The null mouse's hyperexcitability was recorded under ketamine anaesthesia and the contrast with controls still stood. | Repudi 2021 (manifest entry 13) | no ketamine-free arm: it says nothing on ketamine's own effect |
| In other diseases, ketamine is reported to stop status epilepticus in about half of cases (retrospective paediatric cohorts and one meta-analysis, abstract level); an effect on development or function beyond seizure control was not found. | HE census, abstract level, tier 3 transfer | adverse events needing intervention in a few percent (hypertension, delirium); evidence graded insufficient by one guideline-style review; confounded by co-medication |
| Nothing was found, in the queries run, on a transient improvement after intensive care, anaesthesia or sedation in genetic neurodevelopmental conditions. | HE census gap closure | "not found in these queries", not "does not exist" |
| Held safety positions: the computational BLOCK-1 triage was never run (tools absent); ketamine is a prescription drug with labelled dissociative and abuse concerns; readiness colour yellow (esketamine red, a boxed or serious-wording label). | IT block-1 README; SCI readiness sheets | none of this is a verified safety profile |
| Held harm signals in other settings: reversible cholestatic liver injury in adults given ketamine with an AMPAR antagonist (77 against 27 percent, abstract); apoptosis after a few hours of NMDAR block in newborn rat brain (abstract); chronic block in a developing brain acts on activity-dependent maturation (inferred, no WWOX datum). | F04 packet; SCI note H1 to H3 | transfer from adults or rodents; thresholds unknown |

## Layer 2 - GENERAL KNOWLEDGE, UNVERIFIED (recollection; not held; to be checked before any use)
* Ketamine blocks the open NMDAR channel and has a short half-life (hours) with active metabolites; adult mood effects after a single low exposure are reported to last about a week. A **benefit** of this kind has its own **harms**: dissociation and emergence reactions (agitation, hallucination), raised blood pressure and heart rate, increased secretions, bladder and liver effects with repeated use, abuse potential.
* Concern about **early-life anaesthetic neurotoxicity** (rodent data convincing, human cohort data contradictory and confounded) applies to infants and young children and is the main reason caution is voiced for developing brains.
* A **benefit** reported with sedation or anaesthesia in other contexts (calm, sleep, seizure control during the infusion) is paired with the **harm** of the same drugs: respiratory depression, hypotension, withdrawal or rebound after sudden stop, and, for several sedatives, delirium.
* The label and BLOCK-1 position of ketamine in a child with a severe neurological condition is not held; a clinician decides what applies.

## Layer 3 - HYPOTHESIS (rival explanations of a weeks-long improvement after an intensive-care episode; SCI's note section 3; none is evidence about any person)
| Explanation | Why it is a rival, not a conclusion | Harm or caution beside it |
|---|---|---|
| An illness or inflammation resolved (fever, infection, pain, metabolic derangement) | returns to a pre-illness baseline on the same weeks timescale | relapse when the driver returns; the same illness may have caused the loss |
| Co-given sedatives, rest and sleep restoration | GABAergic sedatives suppress bursts in the model; sleep debt is a provoker | sedation, respiratory and withdrawal risks |
| An antibiotic treated a driver infection | improvement tracks the infection | some classes lower the seizure threshold |
| A medication change at admission (held, stopped or reduced) | benefit would reverse when restarted | stopping an antiseizure drug is itself a risk |
| Lower seizure or discharge burden from any drug | the one capacity the model speaks to | burden may rebound on withdrawal (the 1.85 times washout overshoot) |
| The NMDAR block itself, through a lasting plasticity change | needs a slow variable (plasticity, maturation, bistability); none is held | the same window carries the aggravation counterpart: chronic block removes a signal for maturation |
| Regression to the mean and reporting effects | a low point recorded, then a return | none: this is why a blinded, standardised measure matters |
| Several of the above together | co-occurring components cannot be separated by any amount of records | unknown |
**Judgements (SCI's, labelled as judgements, not measurements):** about 0.85 that a non-drug or non-NMDAR component explains at least part of a weeks-long improvement; about 0.15 that a single NMDAR block is the sole cause. **What a retrospective record or registry could separate (no experiment on a person):** the order of events in time; the same sedatives without ketamine; the full medication record; the pre-illness baseline; a blinded measure. **What it cannot separate:** a combination whose components co-occur in every episode.

Limits: the statement is built from held files and abstract-level censuses; layer 2 is recollection and unverified; no dose, schedule or recommendation is made; not medical advice.
