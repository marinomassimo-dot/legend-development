## Point 2 · significance within a genotype is not a treatment-by-genotype interaction
**Can Cheng 2020's figures support an interaction test? No (DATO from the held text and the Fig. 7 image).** Reported: group size per panel, a mean ± SEM Racine stage for each of about 60 one-minute points, a one-way ANOVA "to compare the difference among groups" with the factor structure unspecified, stars (the legend defines only *** and n.s.; panel d shows ****, undefined). Not reported: per-animal values or a per-animal summary (area under the curve, peak, mean stage), F or p values, effect sizes, the unit of analysis. With 6 against 7 knockout animals a p below 0.0001 suggests the test used time points, not animals, as observations (INFERENZA; pseudoreplication possible). Group sizes from Fig. 7: ethosuximide (7b) +/+ PTZ 20 against PTZ+drug 16, +/- 18 against 12, -/- 6 against 6; lithium (7d) +/+ 12 against 8, +/- 12 against 12, -/- 6 against 7. A genotype x treatment term needs per-animal summaries (or the authors' data) and a model with both factors; digitising the curves would give a guess about independence across minutes presented as a test, and was not attempted.

## Point 3 · preregistered subjective probabilities are not validated predictive probabilities
What the numbers are: weights written before data so that a later score can be literal (natural log against a naive baseline). They are one forecaster's judgements about one preparation, conditioned on models that forecaster built. **They have no demonstrated calibration.** Scale of the evidence: the SCORE files score single runs of a handful of statements; the ledger holds 172 lines (status: 77 open, 53 held, 23 failed, 13 mixed, 6 unscorable), spread across several predictors and targets, not a calibration set of one forecaster. Arithmetic (mine; crude null: each statement true with probability 0.5, independent): a forecaster with no information reaches a margin over six coin flips of **+0.94 nats or more with probability 0.125** (the hysteresis score), and **+0.32 or more with probability 0.16** (the homeostat score, five statements). Neither margin distinguishes skill from luck; three of the six hysteresis statements (S2 to S4) came from one run, so the statements are not independent either. The checklist test went the other way (worse than the naive uniform) on two runs of a tuned-on target, which also cannot show miscalibration.

## Point 4 · when an unfavourable result weakens ONE hypothesis and not every intervention on the class
General rule used below: **one agent at one exposure in one preparation gives an agent-level result, not a class-level one.** A class-level demotion needs at least two structurally different agents at verified depth; a hypothesis is weakened only if its auxiliary assumptions (matched depth, stable evoked response, complete washout, viable slice, enough animals) were checked; where one failed, the result is **uninformative**, not unfavourable.

| Hypothesis | Unfavourable result (observation) | What it weakens | What it leaves standing | Must hold first, else uninformative | What would be needed to drop it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **R-A** transient NMDAR suppression | at verified deep block (evoked delayed response cut by at least half) the vehicle-normalised burst rate stays within 0.7 to 1.3 (P1 rejection rule), more than five animals per genotype | "burst suppression by *this* agent, at this exposure, in this slice, follows block depth read from the evoked response"; also the evoked response as a proxy of the block that matters for spontaneous bursts | the d-APV result (NMDAR activation is necessary, competitive antagonist, not touched); other pore blockers with other off-rate, trapping or Mg2+ competition (the bath holds 1.3 mM Mg2+); longer or activity-driven exposure (block builds with openings); other ages, Mg2+ levels, in vivo | the evoked response is stable and measurable in both genotypes; block verified at the time bursts are counted; exposure long enough for use-dependent block to build; at least three baseline bursts per slice | two structurally different pore blockers at verified depth failing **and** a d-APV replication succeeding (that pattern isolates the glutamate-site route) |
| **R-D** HCN1 / Ih non-NMDAR route | paired sag unchanged (interval excluding the preset change) under the pore blocker at the suppressing exposure, and pore blocker and d-APV agree on every burst readout | "the agent acts through Ih in S-KO layer II/III pyramidal neurons at the tested bath concentrations" | Ih action at higher concentrations (20 uM in wild type, Chen 2009); K2P or presynaptic non-NMDAR routes; any role of Ih in S-KO bursting (an Ih-blocker arm can still move bursts); sag as a footprint is not a dissection of Ih (input resistance, Kir contribute) | the **paired SD is known** (it is not held: until then a null sag is uninformative, Addendum 13); recording stable (time-matched vehicle cells); agent reaches the neuron at the concentration | an Ih-blocker arm at matched sag **not** changing bursts, with the sag also unchanged by the pore blocker |
| **R-E** disinhibition (bell-shaped dose-response) | the shallow sub-arm (under one fifth block) and a depth series show no rise (within ±20%) in S-KO slices | "NMDAR block at low occupancy raises bursting *in this slice*, for this agent, at the depths sampled" | disinhibition in adult visual cortex in vivo (Picard 2019, a different preparation, age and endpoint); suppression at deep block (R-A); disinhibition in a circuit not recorded here | the evoked response can resolve shallow depths (the measurement floor of this assay is not held); at least three shallow depths, since a narrow window can fall between points; slice-level variance known | a depth series with intervals excluding a 20% rise plus a PV-selective test; **the oscillator has no interneuron NMDAR term: NOT REPRESENTED, not refuted** |
| **Block kinetics** (trapping, off-rate, Mg2+ competition, use dependence) | pore blocker and d-APV give the same burst rate and the same evoked-response recovery at 30, 60 and 120 min after washout | "kinetics differences between these two agents matter in this slice, over this exposure" | other agents with other kinetics; a different exposure length or activity level; a kinetics-dependent failure of R-A (if the pore blocker does not suppress, slow onset on a sparse-opening network is an explanation that one longer or more active exposure separates from R-A failing) | washout verified complete by the evoked response (residual block would mimic lasting suppression); vehicle-matched rundown | not a yes-or-no hypothesis: a unfavourable result narrows the set of agents; it is dropped for an agent pair, never for the class |
| **Adaptation after removal** (rebound, R-B; homeostat-like slow gain) | both agents inside 0.7 to 1.3 at 30 to 60 min with more than five animals per genotype (R-B rejection rule), and the 1.8-fold bar not reproduced | "a slow gain with a time constant of tens of minutes follows an exposure of the length used" | adaptation after hours- to days-long exposure (the hysteresis question, outside the window); a time constant longer than the 120-minute read; rebound in vivo; the 1.8-fold bar as a feature of that dataset (per-slice values are unrecoverable and the interval includes 1.0, so it may be noise or rundown) | time-matched vehicle slices (rundown); a stable 120-minute washout; the 120-minute read has **no hashed prior** (the priors cover 30 to 60 minutes) | not droppable in a two-hour window: a null **bounds** the time constant, it does not remove the mechanism |

Two synthesis sentences that over-link in the other direction: answer 5, "a fail on burst suppression at matched depth, or a rise at shallow block, removes the NMDAR rationale and leaves ketamine as one anaesthetic among comparators", and the brief's "any later effect of this class in these neurons cannot be attributed to the NMDAR dependency alone". **Fit:** "a failure of burst suppression at verified matched depth with one agent weakens R-A for that agent in this slice; the d-APV result and the class stand until a second structurally different agent is tested; a rise at shallow block would support R-E and says nothing against suppression at deep block". The arm also names a class, not ketamine, so a result is not a result about ketamine unless the laboratory chooses it.

## Point 5 · ketamine, lithium, ethosuximide: by preparation, endpoint, direct evidence and transfer
| | Ketamine | Lithium | Ethosuximide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Preparation in a WWOX system | **none.** Incidental anaesthesia in Repudi 2021 (no ketamine-free arm); the slice work used **d-APV**, a competitive antagonist, in neuron-specific knockout neocortical slices P13 to P17 (Breton 2021) | global Wwox-null mice, pre-weaning, one laboratory (Cheng 2020) | same animals, same laboratory |
| Endpoint | none in a WWOX system; slice burst abolition by d-APV (class proxy) | Racine stage over 60 minutes after a PTZ provocation (not spontaneous seizures) | same assay |
| Direct evidence | none for ketamine; for the class proxy: 5 slices, 3 animals, within the knockout, no genotype contrast | stage lowered in -/- (6 against 7), +/+ (12 against 8) and +/- (12 against 12); p-Ser9 lower in untreated knockout brain; lithium engagement not measured | stage lowered in -/- (6 against 6); not significant in +/+ (20 against 16) and +/- (18 against 12) |
| Transfer | status-epilepticus series and trials in other aetiologies; HCN1 in wild-type mouse neurons (Chen 2009); GluN2A on PV cells in adult mouse cortex (Picard 2019); GSK-3beta studies mixed and confounded | lithium's use and mechanism outside this assay were **not reviewed** in this lane | label-level transfer **not reviewed** in this lane |
| What the evidence type cannot say | any effect of ketamine on a WWOX phenotype | genotype-specific action, a GSK-3beta route, or an effect on spontaneous seizures or development | genotype-specific action (no interaction test); an effect on spontaneous seizures |
**There is no common endpoint, no head-to-head test and no shared preparation across the three rows, so no ranking and no general superiority follows.** What can be said: lithium and ethosuximide have in vivo data in one WWOX model against none for ketamine, in a single provoked-seizure assay from one laboratory; ketamine has the largest clinical literature, in other aetiologies. The synthesis sentences "None is better supported on WWOX-system evidence; every row is thin ... the ranking depends on the endpoint" and the by-endpoint table already say this; the two edits needed are 2.1 and 2.2 above.
