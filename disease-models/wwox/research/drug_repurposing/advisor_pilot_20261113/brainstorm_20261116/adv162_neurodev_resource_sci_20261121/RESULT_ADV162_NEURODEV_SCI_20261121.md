## Answer first
1. **What it holds.** A human-only developmental reference of the cortex: three reference networks of cluster profiles (corticogenesis 1,575 clusters, neurogenesis 1,248, gliogenesis 785) built from 948,350 cells and 14,110 genes (WWOX is in the list), every cluster with source study, age, stage and subclass. Tools: eTrace (a gene or gene-set score per cluster across the network, with an optional enrichment test), `mapNetwork` (bulk profiles, or per-cluster single-cell profiles, placed in the network with the 15 nearest reference clusters, a subclass score, a stage annotation and a confidence). Code MIT (R package); data CC BY 4.0. Sizes: figshare 7.1 GB, of which `counts.mtx.gz` is 6.2 GB (not fetched); the four compact stage and subclass tables about 35 MB (fetched, hashed); the three network objects 91 to 198 MB (need R; not fetched; no R on this host, none installed).
2. **Consumer 1 (cell-class and developmental-window premise): PARTIAL by the pre-registered rule.** The compact tables give Wwox by subclass and by stage as **marginals**, not class by stage jointly (the joint sits in the cluster objects that need R). As a marginal it is a pooled cluster-pseudobulk aggregate across source studies: it is NOT a donor or sample unit and is not better than ADV-147 at the unit level. It adds a different measure (depth-normalised expression, 21 to 26 subclasses): WWOX is highest in glia and progenitors, interneuron subclasses are above excitatory neuron subclasses, and mature excitatory subclasses are lowest (numbers below). This agrees in direction with ADV-147 (inhibitory not below excitatory) and ADV-157 (mouse interneuron classes at or above glutamatergic), and changes no premise.
3. **Consumer 2 (RP-043 organoid benchmark): NO in the held version.** The held Brain 2026 text and its five supplementary files contain no mention of NeuRoDev (case-sensitive search, zero hits); cluster identity there rests on comparison with four published human fetal datasets (references 32 to 35: Polioudakis 2019, Ramos 2022, Trevino 2021, van Bruggen 2022), 12 clusters at week 16 (Fig. 2A-D, Supplementary Fig. 3A-E). The published Brain version and the NeuRoDev paper's own text were not read (see depth). So the RP-043 open items (cluster identity, "dorsal, no interneuron cluster", the per-line table) are not answered by NeuRoDev; E-MTAB-14792 (the single-cell data) remains access-limited (standing re-check not before 2026-12-06).
4. **Consumer 3 (a mapping step on public data): PARTIAL, as a coarse correlation only.** A pre-registered, plain-Python, coarse stage-level and subclass-level correlation of the public human WWOX-KO organoid bulk set (GSE156243, 15-week WiBR3 organoids, 4 KO and 2 WT) onto the pooled pseudobulk columns placed all six samples as progenitor-like and early-prenatal in all three networks (best subclass RG in every sample, best stage 2A-early-prenatal in every sample, correlation spread best-to-tenth 0.25 to 0.48): prediction P-A and P-B held, no "no information" trigger. This is NOT a NeuRoDev mapping (no cluster signatures, no embedding, no nearest-cluster scoring, no confidence). KO against WT shows overlapping per-sample values (see below): descriptive, not a finding. The real `mapNetwork` needs R and the package: HE's ADV-163 reports feasibility; I installed nothing.
5. **Premise verified, with its depth.** The operator's premise is true from the Brain 2026 manuscript in the repository: Asia Zonca (first author of NeuRoDev) is co-first author of Brain 2026, Davila-Velderrain is corresponding author with Aqeilan, and the acknowledgement thanks both laboratories. The NeuRoDev paper's own affiliations and acknowledgements were not read (paper text unreachable). Independence (ADV's rule, from HE's ADV-163 note): NeuRoDev counts as the SAME CLUSTER as the Aqeilan programme for any use that tests that programme's results (Zonca and Davila-Velderrain are co-authors of Brain 2026, and Davila-Velderrain is on the HUJI Wwox-null OPC preprint); it is independent only as a normal-development reference. A NeuRoDev-based identity call on the Brain organoids would share authorship and provenance with the benchmark (RP-045 rule).

## The coarse correlation (public human WWOX-KO organoid bulk set; unit = sample; one engineered line)
Query GSE156243 count table (6 samples), CPM, log2(CPM + 1), 10,466 genes with mean log2 CPM at least 2; reference centred on its own row mean; Pearson per sample. Corticogenesis subclass: best RG in all six samples (r 0.249 to 0.278), then cRG (0.194 to 0.234), then eIN in five of six (0.145 to 0.168; miEN in the sixth); stage: best 2A-early_prenatal in all six, then 1-embryonic or 2B-early_prenatal. Neurogenesis best subclass vRG, cRG or eIPC; gliogenesis best eRG or NPC. Per sample (subclass, corticogenesis): the early-neuron columns (IPC, miEN, eEN, imNIT) are lower in KO than WT on average (difference -0.04 to -0.05) and Astro and Opc higher (+0.03), but the KO sample cko2 sits inside the WT range for miEN, imNIT, eEN and Astro, so there is no separation at 2 against 4 samples on one line. **What it cannot show:** composition (a class cannot be shown absent from bulk: the "no interneuron cluster" item stays untestable); the third place of eIN is a similarity of progenitor-type profiles, not evidence of interneurons; no WWOX effect on identity can be read from it.
Guard stated by ADV and kept: because plain Python cannot reproduce `mapNetwork` (cluster signatures, network embedding, 15-cluster subclass score, confidence), I call this a coarse correlation, and no NeuRoDev mapping was run.

## Exploratory connection to ADV-160 and ADV-161 (not pre-registered; descriptive; named consumer: INV-06 and INV-12 wording, state-level reading)
In the gliogenesis network the mean of the 19 core cholesterol-synthesis genes (the ADV-161 set A; all 19 present) varies with glial state in normal human development, without any ISR perturbation: highest in radial glia and cycling or early progenitors (6.0 to 6.9; cOPC 6.25; eAst 6.39), lowest in OPC (4.84) and in late and mature oligodendrocytes (5.22, 5.45), against a myelin-gene mean that rises from about 2 to about 10.8. HMGCR 7.6 (cOPC) against 5.8 (OPC) and 5.1 (lOli and Oli). So (i) the sterol transcript set is state-dependent in a healthy reference, which supports the ADV-160 caveat (read it within a cell state; a state shift alone moves it); (ii) it does not simply rise with maturation (lowest at OPC and in mature oligodendrocytes), so "sterol up with oligodendrocyte maturation" is not a general transcript-level rule here. Pooled across studies and stages; the unit is a subclass aggregate; adjacent reference, not a WWOX datum.

## WWOX-specific evidence against reusable method (EMPATHY)
* **WWOX-specific:** WWOX expression by class in the human cortical reference (above); the premise and provenance facts; the negative that Brain 2026 (held version) does not use the resource.
* **Reusable method or reference:** (a) a developmental reference ships its answer in compact tables before it needs the tool: reading the four 6 to 11 MB tables answered the class question without R or any large file; boundary: marginals only, the joint class-by-stage unit lives in the cluster objects. (b) A dependency-free coarse correlation can show that a sample set is placed plausibly (progenitor-like, prenatal) and that the pipeline works, but it cannot carry identity or composition claims; fix the "no information" outcome first; boundary: it is not the package's mapping. Two lessons, kept with their boundaries.
