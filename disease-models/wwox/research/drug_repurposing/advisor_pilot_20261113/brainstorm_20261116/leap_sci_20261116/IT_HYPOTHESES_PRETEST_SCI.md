## HIT-1 (discordance of seizure and development endpoints is a timing artefact; imaging abnormality already present before treatment) - CONSISTENCY CHECK, not testable as worded
* **What the table holds.** Six children have both endpoints stated (`a1_both_endpoint_children.tsv`): four discordant (seizure response partial or resolved, development unchanged) and two concordant (no seizure response, development unchanged). **All six have `severe_impairment_unchanged` for development: there is no variance in the development endpoint.** The first imaging stated is abnormal in all six: thin corpus callosum with ventricular or cystic changes in the four discordant children (MRI at two months, two months, four months before vigabatrin, five weeks), atrophy with corpus callosum hypoplasia in the two concordant ones (3 and 6 months; 2 years 4 months).
* **For or against the rejecting observation.** The observation needs a child with normal pre-treatment imaging and a seizure response who improves in development, or a child with abnormal imaging whose development improves: neither exists in the table. The held data are compatible with the statement (every child with a seizure response has abnormal pre-treatment imaging and unchanged development) but also with its opposite, since nothing varies. **Result: cannot be rejected; cannot be tested; the hypothesis is not distinguished from "development is unchanged in everyone".** I knew the 4-of-6 count from INV-10 before (consistency, not independent).

## HIT-2 (the spasm window and the later seizure types are two generators with two class profiles) - partial, not rejected
* **What the table holds.** The one child with an explicit order is the first child of PMID 30361190: spasms resolved when vigabatrin was added; other seizure types (focal myoclonic jerks, clonic) followed and improved after levetiracetam was added. PMID 39101447: phenobarbital and levetiracetam for the first focal seizures, topiramate for a tonic episode at 5 months, vigabatrin added at the spasm onset at 8 months (seizures gradually decreased). In neither child does the class that controlled the spasms also control the later focal types. PMID 28721938: spasms returned after vigabatrin and were controlled by ACTH; no later non-spasm type is described.
* **For or against.** The rejecting observation ("the same class that controls spasms also controls the later focal types in the same child") is not found in the held children; the order of the events also makes it uninformative (a later class is always later). **Result: not rejected; two children consistent; n too small and the order confounds it.**

## HIT-3 (the class shares are confounded by line of therapy) - INDEPENDENT TEST; the confound IS visible in the held extraction
* **Values (43 attributable rows, `status_by_outcome.tsv`).** Rows with status `added_on`: 13, all improved (resolved 7, partial 6, none 0), improved share 13/13 (Wilson 95%: 0.77 to 1.00), resolved 7/13 (0.29 to 0.77). Rows with status `tried`: 25, improved 12 (resolved 4, partial 8, none 13), improved share 12/25 (0.30 to 0.67), resolved 4/25 (0.06 to 0.35). The two intervals for 'improved' do not overlap. `continued`: 4 rows (1 improved); `stopped`: 1 row (none).
* **For or against the rejecting observation.** The rejecting observation is stated WITHIN each class; the cells are too small for that (every class has at most 13 rows and mostly 1 to 5; `status` by class is in my working output). Pooled across classes the difference by status is large. Interpretation (INFERENZA): an add-on is reported because it was followed by a response, a `tried` row includes the negative list statements ('ineffective (i.e., ...)'), so the status difference is partly a reporting asymmetry and not only an order effect; the class mix also differs between the statuses. **Result: the order or reporting confound is visible in the held data (pooled), which supports HIT-3's outcome one in its pooled form; the within-class test it names cannot be run at these n.**

## HIT-4 (the high GABA-AT share is a spasm-phenotype base rate, not a WWOX match) - INDEPENDENT TEST against an external base rate (abstract level)
* **Values.** Held, vigabatrin attributable rows: 9 distinct children (`vigabatrin_rows.tsv`): resolved 4 (0.44; Wilson 0.19 to 0.73), partial 4, none 1; improved 8 of 9 (0.56 to 0.98). Published spasm-cessation shares on vigabatrin in infantile-spasm cohorts of mixed aetiology (abstracts): 28 of 52 (54%; Wilson 0.41 to 0.67) at days 13 and 14 in UKISS (2004); 11 of 23 (48%; 0.29 to 0.67) as first-line in Vigevano and Cilio (1997); 24 of 67 (36%; 0.25 to 0.48) on high-dose vigabatrin as responders in Elterman (2001; 8 of 75 on low dose).
* **For or against the rejecting observation.** The rejecting observation ("the WWOX share lies outside the base-rate interval") is NOT met for the comparable quantity: the WWOX 'resolved' share 4 of 9 (0.44) lies inside the range of the three trial shares (0.36 to 0.54) and its interval overlaps theirs. The 8-of-9 'improved' share is not comparable: the trials count cessation of spasms at a fixed day, the WWOX rows count authors' statements of partial or full improvement at unspecified times, and add-on reporting selects responders (HIT-3). **Result: not rejected; the held WWOX vigabatrin share is within the published cessation range; consistent with a phenotype prior (spasms), not distinguishable from it.**
* **Limits:** abstracts only; trial populations differ in aetiology and age; the WWOX rows have mixed timepoints; n = 9.

## HIT-5 (lead time to control, not class, separates development outcomes among discordant children) - untestable from held data
* **Values.** Development is `severe_impairment_unchanged` in all six both-endpoint children: no variance, so no association with latency (or anything) can be computed. Onset ages are stated; time to control is stated for only some rows. **Result: untestable; the held table cannot reject it, as IT's own file anticipates (the untestability is the result at n = 6).** INV-10's count reaching 10 or more (PS-09) is the first chance of variance.
