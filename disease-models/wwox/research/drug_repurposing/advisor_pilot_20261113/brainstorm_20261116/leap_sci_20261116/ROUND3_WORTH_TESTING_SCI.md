# Round 3: what it would test and what it would not be worth testing (SCI, 2026-11-16; OPINION)

Labelled opinion, written for the closing synthesis at ADV's request. Facts of today it rests on: the judge's count depends on its strictness (1 survivor in round 1, nearly all passing in round 1b); the fate metric rewards stating a weak test (H-C5 v H-ADV-1: the same Erbb4 numbers gave N for one and R for the other); arm A (baseline, cheaper) was as good as arm B (N+P 2 of 5 each) and as the authors (9 of 19); U is the largest category (7 of 24, then 3 of 5 in arm B). Mechanism matching only; no recommendation to use any agent.

## Worth testing
1. **The reach of the held data, not the generators.** U means no held table can discriminate, so the next gain is a measured list: for each U item (J01, J05, J10, J12, J13, J15, J18; H-C1, H-C2, H-C4), the single cheapest dataset or experiment that would turn it into R, C or N, and whether a public table exists (a purified later-age neuron table; any Wwox-line electrophysiology outside the Aqeilan-Carlen network; a second convulsant). This costs lookups, not agents, and it feeds the experiment brief directly.
2. **A strong-test version of the fate metric.** Someone who wrote none of the items fixes, before opening any table, the rejecting observation and its threshold for each hypothesis, then the lookups are run. This removes both judge strictness and the "state a weak test" reward. Needs the same 24 + 5 items, so it is a re-scoring, not a new generation round.
3. **The five N survivors and the P items against a second held context** (J07, J14, J16, J22, J24, H-C5): the only items that passed a test that could have failed; a second pool, line or table is the cheapest way to see whether N survives.

## Not worth testing
* **Another arm comparison at n = 4 to 6.** A one-item change is 20-25 points; no difference between arms can be detected, and today's range is the same for every arm. Detecting 0.2 against 0.6 with usual power takes about 20 items per arm.
* **More perspective instructions to move the counts.** Arm A as good as arm B and as the authors says the instruction is not the lever; default to the cheaper arm for generation.
* **Fate counts from pre-tests run by the item's own author** (my five items): not independent.
* **A third judge run for novelty or plausibility.** Two runs already show strictness governs the count; a third measures the judge.
* **Generating hypotheses for the U items without new data.** It adds items the held tables cannot test.

## What would change my mind
If the reach audit shows no public or cheap route for most U items, generation by arm A is then the only cheap activity and a larger arm A batch (about 20 items) with the strong-test metric becomes the fair comparison. If a strong-test re-scoring puts the authors clearly above arm A, the perspective instruction matters after all.
