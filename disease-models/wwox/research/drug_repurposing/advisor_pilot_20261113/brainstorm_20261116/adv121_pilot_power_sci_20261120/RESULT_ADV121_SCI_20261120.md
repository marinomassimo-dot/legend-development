## 1. The answer first
1. **At 13 to 14 animals per arm (the n of v3.1 6.8) and the benchmark's own SD, a pilot can place a candidate on one side of the LD/bar line on weight at P10 (12.6 per arm) and on the two glia readouts and spike-wave rate (2.2 to 5.3 per arm, with caveats below); it cannot on clasping at P18 (16.3 per arm; 8.4 if the candidate is as strong as HD), and it cannot at all on glucose** (P20: 97 per arm against the bar, 9.7 if the candidate is as strong as HD; P10 and P30 never separate LD from HD: 256 and 158 per arm).
2. **At the SD upper bound (2.09 times, 5 df) only a candidate as strong as HD stays within about 15 per arm** (weight P10 14.5, spike-wave 10.0, glia 2 to 9); against the bar itself weight P10 needs 48, weight P15 80, clasping 64.
3. **Reading ages:** weight at **P10** (separation d at least 2.79, and about 90 percent of untreated knockouts still alive) before P15 (d at least 1.83, about half alive); P5 is unresolved (the LD marker is hidden behind HD and its bar cannot be read); glucose only at **P20** (d 1.71, against 0.30 at P10 and 0.38 at P30), an age the pilot's P14 and P17 glucose readouts do not match; clasping only at P18 (single benchmark age); spike-wave is pooled over about P14 to P21, with no age resolution.
4. **The three panels:** S7B and S7D were **measured** (rule: the glia row is not beyond 50 per arm); they changed **no n and no age**, and added one reference (the untreated-knockout level, section 4). The 2021 survival graph and optic-nerve electron microscopy were **not measured** (survival and myelin structure lie outside the short-window families of v3.1 6.25 and carry no SD, delta or age for any row).
5. **What dominates the n:** the SD of the bar-level arm, which the benchmark never printed for weight (the HD error bars are hidden behind the markers at every age read), and the unknown n and unit of analysis behind every bar (section 5).

## 3. What the pilot can and cannot classify (consistent with v3.1 6.25)
A P17 pilot at the n of v3.1 6.8 (13 to 14 animals alive per arm) can say **"at least HD-like"** on weight at P10 or P15, clasping at P18 and glucose at P20 with room to spare at the benchmark's SD, and on the glia and spike-wave readouts with the limits of their unknown n. It can separate a candidate **sitting at the bar** from one at the LD level only on **weight at P10 (12.6 per arm, on an SD that is a ceiling)**; clasping needs 17 per arm, weight at P15 20, and glucose cannot do it at any age (the bar at P20 sits only 5.5 mg/dL above LD while the SD is 11). At the SD upper bound none of the bar-level separations on weight, clasping or glucose fits. The result section 4 rule (broad = the bar on survival or weight **and** on two further families from different systems) therefore leaves, at 13 to 14 per arm, **weight plus glia plus spike-wave** as the only combination that can be satisfied, and glia (cross-experiment baseline, unknown unit) and spike-wave (vehicle contrast only, five pups) are the weakest links. The 6.25 wording stands: **"provisionally broad on short-window families; survival to P60 pending"**, and a pilot that wants the call to carry weight needs **about 20 per arm** (to include clasping and weight at P15) at the observed SD or **about 50 to 65** if its own SD is at the 5-df bound.

**Too noisy to read at the chosen n:** glucose (at every age against the bar: 97 per arm at P20, and P10 and P30 separate LD from HD at no feasible n), clasping against the bar (17 per arm at the observed SD), and any bar-level weight call at P15 (20). **Not comparable with the bridge pilot's 36 to 109 per arm** (v3.1 and the unit C revision): that figure is for pentylenetetrazol seizure severity (log SD 0.54 to 0.94) and a modest 0.7-ratio effect, whereas the n here separate two reference levels of the benchmark. The n here are small because the separations are large; a smaller separation costs the square: halving the weight P10 delta (0.83 to 0.42 g) takes 12.6 to 44.5 per arm and quartering it 172 (`OUTPUT_sensitivity.txt`; at the SD bound 188 and 745).

## 5. The unknowns that dominate the n (largest first)
1. **The SD of the bar-level arm.** Weight bars are hidden behind the markers at every HD age read and at LD P10 (lower half) and P5; the ceiling is the marker half-height (0.33 g). n minus 2 scales with SD squared: at the 5-df bound (2.089) it multiplies by 4.4 (weight P10 bar 12.6 to 48.4).
2. **n and the unit of analysis behind every bar.** n is not printed for weight, clasping and glia; for survival the implied at-risk counts are 10, 10, 10 and 13 against legend n 20, 10, 20 and 30 (ADV-120). Glucose dots counted: P10 WT 10, KO 8, LD 7, HD 11; P20 WT 9, LD 7, HD 12; P30 WT 9, LD 8, HD 13. The GFAP caps (0.07 and 0.12 percent area) are too short to be an animal-level SD of a real cohort: if the unit is a section or a field, the animal-level SD is larger and the glia rows are floors.
3. **Delta itself is estimated, and the bar is a proposal.** The LD mean has its own sampling error (unquantifiable where n is not printed); at P20 glucose the bar leaves only 0.48 SD between the arms.
4. **Pharmacological arms need not share a gene-addition SD** (exposure varies by animal); the 5-df column is the guard, not a measurement.
5. **Survivor and cohort conditioning.** Untreated knockouts are about 90 percent alive at P10, about half at P15, about 10 percent at P17 and none from 17.1 d, so a vehicle arm read at P15 needs about twice the enrolment and at P17 about ten times (steps read from the ADV-120 survival squares); the benchmark's clasping at P18 shows a knockout mean (3.60) although the Fig 3B knockout curve ends at 17.1 d (calibration agreement 0.3 d), so the two panels are not the same cohort or the S4A knockout n is small and unprinted.
6. **Two-arm stand-in.** A pilot has no LD comparator: the n here assumes a concurrent LD-level arm; testing the pilot estimate against the benchmark's fixed LD mean would need roughly half, but treats that mean as exact, which section 5.3 says it is not.

## 6. Method notes and one deviation
Pixel spread (calibration B in place of A) changes the n by at most 0.3 percent (clasping), 0.2 percent (glia) and 0.0 percent on weight and glucose (differences within a panel are offset-free). Sampling spread is the 2.089 factor; glucose's own df (16 to 19) gives 1.37 to 1.42. **Deviation from the pre-registration:** the weight ceiling uses the fixed half-height 8.5 px (0.331 g) for every marker, not each marker's own height (14 to 17 px, 0.27 to 0.33 g): slightly conservative (n up to a few percent larger). The Fig 3C bars were measured by cap detection (`measure_bars_3C.py`): a first stem-based version missed the WT P5 bar and was replaced before any n was computed from it.
