## Answer first
1. **Reconciliation, and what this note adds.** The first pass on KCC2 and OV350 (ADV-065b, landed, consumed by IT57 and by ADV) already read the Arshad 2026 main text and supplement text, Jarvis 2023, the CLP257 dispute and Kasahara 2019. This note does not repeat it. It adds: all seven Arshad figures examined as images (section 2); a recomputation of the repository's own transcript data with the cell-composition question the earlier ratios did not ask (section 3); a full-text check of what the Wwox literature actually contains on chloride (section 4), which found a human organoid system the ADV-065 note missed; the premises that narrowed the search, rechecked one by one (section 5); lever-level and engagement evidence read by one new producer (section 6); eight-field hypotheses with a transfer ledger (sections 7 and 8); and an independent review of the inference, not only of the quotations (section 10).
2. **The central question has a conditional answer: yes, with its rivals.** Interventions on chloride transport are not a weaker form of raising GABAergic transmission: in four preparations (neonatal ischaemic seizures at P7, adult kainate status epilepticus, a Cdkl5 knockout cohort, neonatal rat sedation) a KCC2 enhancer changed the effect of a GABA-A drug, which is compatible with chloride transport acting as a gain control on GABA-A actions and equally with each rival listed in H3 (a ceiling effect, direct GABA-A potentiation by the compound, onset-time and randomisation confounds). The comparison that separates the classes is therefore a **factorial one (GABA-A modulator by transport lever) read together with E_GABA in the same cells**, not a one-agent arm (hypothesis H3).
3. **The missing E_GABA does not prevent the next design, but it forbids choosing a direction.** It leaves open comparators, probe specificity and the order of experiments (H1, H2, H3, H5). It forbids saying which lever to pull.
4. **A human system with the same gene exists, and a glial rival.** Steinberg 2021 (full text) built WWOX-knockout human cerebral organoids with rescue lines; hyperexcitability and epileptiform activity are documented at week 7, the slow-wave power of the knockouts declines with time, and the authors read the spectral recovery as strengthening a role for depolarising GABA (citing general developmental-epilepsy literature) without any chloride or E_GABA measurement (section 4): a bath-accessible preparation without a blood-brain barrier, in which penetration and engagement still have to be measured. My recomputation of the held P17 hippocampal nuclei (one pool per arm, descriptive only) shows an oligodendrocyte-like fraction falling from 19.6 to 4.9 percent and the highest NKCC1 transcript in that class: **NKCC1-directed effects in a Wwox model may be glial (oligodendroglial, microglial or astrocytic), and NKCC1 blockade may be double-edged for myelin** (H4).
5. **What the transcripts say, bounded: nothing about the gradient.** The earlier ratio "not raised, therefore not depolarising" was composition-confounded. Neuron-restricted values in the nuclei pool: Slc12a5 unchanged (excitatory neurons -0.01 log2), Slc12a2 lower (-0.40 to -0.76 log2); organoids: neuron-normalised SLC12A5 higher in all four knockout libraries, but the knockouts split into two clusters. Neither is protein, localisation, transport or E_GABA; n is one pool per arm and one parental line; the nuclei are hippocampal while the bursting is neocortical; and in two other models transcript or protein level did not predict E_GABA (Yuan 2019, Egawa 2023). These data are **uninformative for or against a depolarised gradient**.
6. **OV350 is one conditional probe among KCC2 and NKCC1 tools, not a drug to reposition** (section 9). The Cdkl5 pup dataset shows what looks like a one-day acceleration of a self-resolving behavioural phenotype (my reading of the group means), a faster walking milestone, and adult effects measured 5 to 8 weeks after a 12-day course; there is no wild-type group on the agent, no sedation measure, no E_GABA at the age of the effect, litters (not animals) were randomised, the compound is not publicly obtainable, and the mechanism link is **CDKL5-specific** (CDKL5 co-immunoprecipitates with KCC2). **Correction to what ADV-065b delivered and what IT57 and ADV consumed:** the statement that the KCC2 branch stands above the NKCC1 blocker on the development endpoint is **withdrawn**. The saved abstracts hold neonatal NKCC1-antagonist studies in genetic models with durable adult outcomes (Marguet 2015, a Kv7 genetic epilepsy, controls not adversely affected; He 2019, Fmr1; Pizzamiglio 2025, Dravet, behaviour rescued and seizures not), together with adverse signals (Vargas 2013: neonatal bumetanide gave a thermal-seizure susceptibility similar to that of the R43Q mutant, with a stated potential risk of early treatment, the abstract not naming the genotype of the treated pups; Sakamoto 2025: reduced social interaction in both genotypes after prepartum dosing). On development the two branches are **not rankable**; neither has Wwox data.
7. **Next executable questions** (section 11): the age at which Wwox-deficient animals first seize, burst or show network abnormality (one behavioural line puts spontaneous seizures after postnatal day 12 in the null, Cheng 2020; no EEG onset exists in the sources read; it sets every window in H1 and H5); full texts for the glial NKCC1 literature, microglial and astrocytic as well as oligodendroglial (H4); and the measurements that gate everything and need a laboratory: E_GABA with KCC2 and NKCC1 protein in S-KO slices, E_GABA or Cl- imaging with MEA pharmacology in knockout against rescue organoids at week 7, and the factorial recordings. A narrow independent recheck of the material corrections is in section 12.

**INFERENZA from the figures:** (a) the mechanism chain "activator raises KCC2, lowers chloride, stops spasms" is not shown at the age of the effect: KCC2 was not different at p7, lower at p21, undecided at p14 and unsampled at p10 to p13; E_GABA was measured only in untreated animals at p14 to p21 (a subset of four knockout cells, selected by firing at rest, was depolarised; the other knockout cells, n = 7, were not different from wild type, p 0.58; bumetanide in the bath and a 32 mM chloride load); (b) the acute effect is a shift in timing of a self-limiting phenotype with an inseparable motor-maturation endpoint (time on side and walking are mutually exclusive uses of the same session), so "network activity reduced" and "development improved" are not separated; (c) the adult findings rest on larger groups than the pup curves but share three unaddressed alternatives: **litters, not animals, were randomised to vehicle or OV350 while every statistic is per animal; OV350 knockouts reached status epilepticus about 14 minutes later (about 59 against 45 min) before a fixed-time diazepam dose, (the paper itself says kainate seizures become refractory to benzodiazepines within minutes, so a later onset means less time in status at the fixed diazepam time); and there is no wild-type-on-agent group**; (d) the main text and figures agree on the direction of every headline point except the Fig. 5 legend title, but the supplementary statistics table disagrees with the plots in at least six places (Fig. 2b p14 and Fig. 2e p21 cells, Fig. 3c and 3e rows, Fig. 5g which copies Fig. 5e, Fig. 5i with a printed KO-350 mean near -94 against a bar near -71) and the sociability age is stated three ways: no supplement number should be used without checking it against the plot.
**Reasons a transfer to WWOX would be plausible:** a genetic epilepsy with early motor-seizure behaviour, a KCC2 reduction measured at p21 and possibly p14 in one model, and a Wwox-null survival window of 3 to 4 weeks that overlaps the p10 to p21 treatment window; the age at which Wwox-deficient animals first seize is known only from **one line in one laboratory** (Cheng 2020, full text, saved bytes: "spontaneous epileptic seizures were commonly observed after postnatal day 12" in Wwox knockout mice, frequently induced by noise, strobe light or a new cage; behavioural, no EEG, no onset series; the null mice die by about 4 weeks), and Breton recorded P13 to P17 only and wrote that the age group may mimic a late-stage disorder. **Reasons it is unjustified as it stands:** the KCC2 link is CDKL5-specific; no Wwox E_GABA or KCC2 datum; the observed benefit is one day of a self-limiting phenotype plus adult outcomes with the three alternatives above; Ovid-funded, small, unreplicated; no pup exposure; the agent is not publicly obtainable.

## 9. OV350 against the NKCC1 branch, and where the other tools are ranked
OV350 is **ranked as one conditional in-vitro probe candidate among KCC2 and NKCC1 tools**, not as a drug to reposition. Its pup data in a genetic DEE (Cdkl5) show a one-day shift of a self-resolving phenotype and adult effects without a wild-type-on-agent group, with litter-level randomisation and an onset-time confound for the diazepam finding; neonatal NKCC1-antagonist studies in other genetic models also report adult or behavioural outcomes (Marguet 2015, Kv7, controls not adversely affected; He 2019, Fmr1; Pizzamiglio 2025, Dravet: behaviour rescued, seizures not), with adverse signals (Vargas 2013, genotype of the treated pups not named; Sakamoto 2025, after prepartum dosing); the KCC2 genetic lever is predominantly, not uniformly, supportive (Awad 2016 is a counter-example), and the NKCC1 lever is mixed and cell-type dependent. What OV350 offers a slice or organoid arm is bath application without the brain-exposure premise; what it lacks is a wild-type-on-agent group, a sedation measure, pup exposure, E_GABA at the age of the effect, a selectivity and binding record for OV350 itself (parent compound only), a mechanism link beyond CDKL5, and a public compound record. **CLP257 and CLP290** are ranked for one question only: as the contrast that tests whether an effect follows KCC2 or the GABA-A receptor, always with a GABA-A control; CLP257 is reported in one paper (Cardarelli 2017, full text read in ADV-065b; cited in Sullivan and Doi) to potentiate GABA-A receptors rather than modify KCC2, and Donneger 2026 (full text) reports both actions, so the dispute is unresolved. **Bumepamine** does not inhibit NKCC1 yet suppressed seizures together with phenobarbital in the Johne 2021b abstract (no bumepamine-alone arm there), so it is not shown to be inert: it separates NKCC1 from other actions only with a vehicle arm and an additional inactive structural analogue. The decision-relevant comparison is H3, not "OV350 against bumetanide".
