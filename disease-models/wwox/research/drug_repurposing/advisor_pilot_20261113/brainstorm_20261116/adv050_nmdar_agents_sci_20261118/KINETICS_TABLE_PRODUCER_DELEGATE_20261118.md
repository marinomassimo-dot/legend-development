## 2. Where the primaries agree and disagree (from the table rows only)

Agreement.
- Use dependence (a): Emnett 2013, Mealing 1999 (abstract wording "use-dependent"), Orser 1997 (open-channel block for ketamine), Chen and Lipton 1997 (memantine kon falls with falling NMDA) and Glasgow 2018 ("recovery from inhibition occurs only when the channel is open") all describe block and unblock as needing an open channel. Unblock speed rises with open probability: subtype order of recovery 2A fastest, 2C/2D slowest (Glasgow Tables 1-2); lowering glycine slowed memantine re-equilibration 0.97 s to 2.8 s (Emnett).
- Mg2+ competition (e): Kotermanski and Johnson 2009 give ~16-fold right shift for memantine and ketamine at 2A/2B and ~2-3-fold at 2C/2D with 1 mM Mg2+ at -66 mV; Glasgow 2018 abstract says Mg2+ raises both IC50s by competition at the deep site; Gideons 2014 abstract says the difference between the drugs appears only in physiological Mg2+.
- Memantine IC50 at about -60 to -70 mV in 0 Mg2+ is of order 1 microM in several preparations: 1 microM at -60 mV (Chen and Lipton, rat RGC), 1.4 microM at -67 mV (Blanpied, rat cortical neurons), 0.5-0.8 microM at -66 mV across recombinant subtypes (Kotermanski and Johnson), 2.1 microM (Emnett, rat hippocampal culture).
- Memantine trapping is partial: Blanpied ~1/6 released; Parsons review 15-20% (citing Blanpied); Mealing 71% remained trapped; Kotermanski/Wood tau 0.79 s for the release.

Disagreement or tension.
1. Fraction of ketamine trapped: Kotermanski/Wood (abstract) "nearly all", Emnett "full trapping" (citing others), versus Mealing: 86 +/- 1% remained trapped after 120 s washout, i.e. not complete; Zhigulin 2024 groups ketamine with memantine as "partial trapping". Memantine trapped fraction is ~5/6 (Blanpied, cortical neurons and recombinant 2A/2B) versus 71 +/- 4% (Mealing, cortical neurons, 120 s washout): the protocols differ and the numbers differ.
2. Ketamine subunit order: Kotermanski and Johnson Table 1 in 0 Mg2+ gives 2A 0.33, 2B 0.31, 2C 0.51, 2D 0.83 microM (2A and 2B most sensitive, 2D least); at 1 mM Mg2+ 2A 5.35, 2B 5.08, 2C 1.18, 2D 2.95 microM (2C/2D favoured only 4.5-fold and 1.8-fold over 2A). A 2026 GluN2D review table states "GluN1/2B/2D >> 2A" for ketamine (review, no preparation): this does not match the primary numbers.
3. Memantine at GluN1/2A in 0 Mg2+: 0.80 microM (Kotermanski and Johnson, 10 mM BAPTA internal) versus 2.76 microM (Phillips 2026, internal Ca2+ below 1 nM, 0.1 mM external Ca2+) and 0.69 microM when internal Ca2+ was 10 microM. Phillips attributes the difference to internal Ca2+; the two primaries do not agree on the 2A value unless that explanation is accepted. Other subtypes agree: 2B 0.57 vs 0.90, 2C 0.52 vs 0.73, 2D 0.54 vs 0.59 microM.
4. Speed of memantine versus ketamine offset: Emnett found re-equilibration after a +50 mV step slow and similar (memantine 1.2 s, ketamine 2.1 s; fast component 62% vs 48%) and calls it "surprisingly slow"; the Parsons review frames memantine kinetics as fast. Glasgow found that memantine recovery depends strongly on concentration (tau_w 240 to 2799 ms for 1 to 100 microM at 2A) while ketamine recovery barely does (443 to 707 ms), so which drug is faster depends on the concentration (memantine faster at 1 microM, ketamine faster at 100 microM at 2A in 0 Mg2+). Chen and Lipton report a single macroscopic koff of 0.44 s-1 independent of concentration, which Glasgow's concentration dependence does not reproduce (different preparation, receptor, and voltage protocol).
5. Mg2+ effect on ketamine: Kotermanski and Johnson state that earlier primaries found Mg2+ both reduced (MacDonald 1991) and augmented (Liu 2001) ketamine inhibition, and then show a ~16-fold right shift at 2A/2B.
6. One ketamine site or two: Emnett's introduction notes "some evidence for two ketamine sites" (Orser 1997, allosteric reduction of opening frequency) whereas Kotermanski/Wood report lack of ketamine binding to the superficial memantine site; these are not strictly contradictory (a different second site).
7. Unit slip in a review: a 2009 memantine review gives IC50 "approximately 1 mM"; every other row gives about 1 microM. Treated as a typographical error, not used.

d-APV: no primary text found that states block kinetics, trapping, voltage or Mg2+ behaviour for d-APV; see section 3. The only numbers are one concentration of DL-AP5 (100 microM) blocking ~39.5% of GluN1/2A and ~18% of GluN1/2D current against 100 microM glutamate and glycine (Boehringer 2023), and the class statement that glutamate-site antagonists generally have affinity 2A > 2B > 2C > 2D (Costa 2009, abstract).
