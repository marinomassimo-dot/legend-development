# ADV-050: memantine and other NMDA-receptor-directed agents against ketamine, by endpoint; the kinetics premise from primaries; the agent pair for the matched-depth series (SCI, 2026-11-18; non-canonical; hypothesis level; mechanism and evidence level only; no dose, no schedule, no recommendation for or against any use of any agent in any person; reference genotype class only; not medical advice)

Written for: ADV (host) and IT (consumers: arm table notes). Labels: DATO (stated by a source read), INFERENZA (reasoned), IPOTESI, MODEL (the held oscillator and toy, hypothesis generators), TRANSFER (other species, preparation or disease), ASSUMED. Evidence levels: **FT** = full text (windows read), **AB** = abstract only, **LAB** = current label text, **CS** = case report or small uncontrolled series. "More promising" below is a statement about **evidence level and testability**, never a statement that any agent should be used.

## Answer first
1. **Is memantine, or another NMDAR-directed agent, MORE promising than ketamine?** Not in general; it depends on the endpoint, and on none of the three endpoints does any agent have WWOX-system evidence. **Acute rescue of status epilepticus:** ketamine is more promising on evidence level (a randomised first-line trial in another setting, a halted randomised trial in refractory status epilepticus, paediatric series); memantine has nothing in this endpoint. **Chronic seizure control:** memantine is more promising than ketamine **on evidence level only** (one randomised crossover trial in childhood DEE, abstract-level; ketamine has case reports and ICU series for prolonged exposure), and "better hypothesised tolerability" is not efficacy; the same trial's analysis, harms and responder definition are unread, a GRIN gain-of-function cohort shows seizure worsening in some, and lithium and ethosuximide still have the only in vivo Wwox-null data. **Development:** neither; no evidence for any agent. **Testability in the slice:** the same for both (both are open-channel blockers competing with Mg2+ at the pore): memantine is not easier to test; its use is as a **comparator that separates explanations**, not as a priority therapeutic candidate.
2. **The kinetics premise is now DATO for the main points, with disagreements shown.** Both ketamine and memantine need the channel open to block and to unblock; both are trapped after agonist removal (ketamine 86% in one culture study and "nearly all" in another, memantine 71% in the same culture study and about five sixths in another: **partial for both by the numbers, near-complete for ketamine only by wording**); memantine alone has a second, agonist-independent route; Mg2+ shifts both IC50s about 16-fold at GluN2A/2B and 2 to 4-fold at GluN2C/2D at 1 mM and about -66 mV; the unblock speed correlates with open probability. **For d-APV no primary states kinetics, trapping, voltage or Mg2+ behaviour** (competitive by class). **No source is a slice**: every kinetic number is from recombinant cells or cultured neurons, mostly at room temperature; the planned slice runs at 35 C with 1.3 mM Mg2+. Section 2.
3. **Which agents for the matched-depth series?** The informative design is the **triple d-APV, memantine, ketamine at the same evoked depths**, not a pair, because the two contrasts are orthogonal: memantine against d-APV isolates use-dependent pore block; ketamine against memantine isolates what is ketamine-specific (an Ih action is documented for ketamine in wild-type cortical neurons; **none was found for memantine**, a not-found, not an absence). If only one pore blocker can be run, ketamine is the one the question is about but its result cannot separate R-D from use dependence; memantine is the cleaner mechanism probe but is not the operator's drug. The primaries put the two **close but not identical** in kinetics (IC50 and Mg2+ shifts within about 2.5-fold of each other, re-equilibration times 1.2 and 2.1 s with overlapping error bars in one study, recovery times up to about 4-fold apart at 100 uM in another), so a difference at matched depth could be kinetic or off-target and the triple is what separates them. Section 3.
4. **Next step of the loop.** One bounded dossier is worth doing: a **GluN2B-selective negative allosteric modulator** (radiprodil is the member with a registered randomised paediatric trial, in GRIN-related disorder), as a probe that removes a subunit-defined fraction of NMDAR current without pore block. **No** to dextromethorphan, amantadine and magnesium as new dossiers (reasons in section 5). A reasoned stop for further pore-blocker family members.

**Preparation type against the slice design (ADV's question).** Every kinetic figure above is from **recombinant cells** (HEK293T, tsA201, CHO, oocytes) or **dissociated or cultured neurons** (rat retinal ganglion cells, rat cortical and hippocampal cultures, mouse hippocampal cultures), almost all at room temperature or unstated; **no primary read gives trapping or off-rate in an acute slice**, and none at 35 C or with 1.3 mM Mg2+ and a bursting network (Emnett also used acute hippocampal slices at 30 C, but for LTP and LTD, not for the kinetic numbers; temperatures of the Mealing, Blanpied and Chen and Lipton recordings are not in the abstracts read). The Mg2+ numbers (1 mM) are the nearest to the slice's bath; the temperature dependence of trapping and unblock was not found. So transfer to the slice is INFERENZA for every agent, and for d-APV there is no kinetic datum at all.
**Where the primaries disagree (kept):** ketamine is "fully" trapped in wording and 86% in one measurement; memantine 71% against about 83%; memantine IC50 at GluN1/2A in 0 Mg2+ 0.80 against 2.76 uM; which drug unblocks faster depends on concentration (Glasgow) while Emnett finds them indistinguishable and "surprisingly slow"; the Parsons review frames memantine kinetics as fast. One 2009 memantine review prints "1 mM" for the IC50 (an evident unit slip, not used).
**Consequence for the model's missing premise.** Part B could not represent kinetics. The primaries give the structure that was missing: block and unblock both need openings, and a trapped blocker leaves only when the channel opens. In a network whose openings depend on NMDAR activity this gives a **positive feedback** (block lowers activity, fewer openings, slower unblock), so the recovery of a trapping blocker after washout is set by network activity and by the **evoked-response probes themselves** (each probe opens channels and releases trapped drug). INFERENZA, not a model result: it predicts that a trapping agent can leave a residual block that looks like a lasting suppression and that the probe schedule changes what is seen; the reading rule of Addendum 14 (evoked response back within 10%) must therefore state the probe frequency.

## 3. The agent pair (really a triple) for the matched-depth series, with predictions (INFERENZA from section 2 and MODEL from ADV-046 part B; none is a result)
| Contrast at the same evoked depth | What it isolates | If it differs (rate below the comparator) | If it does not differ |
|---|---|---|---|
| memantine against d-APV | use-dependent, partly trapped pore block against a competitive, activity-independent antagonist | use dependence or trapping in the burst network (blocks more during bursts than the evoked response shows) | the evoked response is a good match for block during bursts, and use dependence is not what separates pore blockers from d-APV here |
| ketamine against memantine | what is ketamine-specific, since the primaries put the two close in kinetics (Emnett: IC50 not significantly different; similar Mg2+ shifts; but see section 6) | an off-target action of ketamine (Ih is documented in wild-type cortical neurons; paired sag decides) or its higher trapping fraction | class-level pore block explains the result; an Ih route is not needed |
| ketamine against d-APV | the question the operator's drug raises | confounded: Ih and use dependence both give "below" (ADV-046 part B) | class-level, NMDAR-dependent |
| GluN2B NAM against d-APV (optional fourth arm) | subunit-defined removal of current without pore block | burst rate depends on the GluN2B share (WO-10 measures that share at P13 to P17; the brief's cited wild-type value was 14% at P13 to P15 against 53% at P6 to P8) | GluN2B current carries little of the bursts |
**Rebound and early reads, per agent (INFERENZA):** d-APV: washout is diffusion-limited and activity-independent, so the model's K0 and K2 cases apply (return to baseline; an adaptation rebound only in the first 30 minutes, hence the reads at about 5 and 15 minutes). A trapping pore blocker: block persists until channels open; in a silenced slice the early reads (5, 15, 30 minutes) can show **residual block with a low evoked response and a low burst rate** (the K1 cases: rate below with E below), the effect larger for the agent trapped more (ketamine, if the 86% against 71% holds), and shortened by activity or by the evoked probes. Memantine's second site is agonist-independent but low-affinity (a secondary source quotes 71 uM at -65 mV for the agonist-independent route; not read in the primary): at bath concentrations reachable it adds little. Combined residual block plus adaptation can give a late rebound with a normal evoked response (K3). All of this is conditional on the slice behaving like the culture; the slice's temperature and Mg2+ are not those of the primaries.
**Decision this changes.** The depth series should name three agents (or at least ketamine and memantine beside d-APV) and the contrast table above, and the outreach package's "the laboratory chooses the agent within the class" should become "we suggest a pair of pore blockers that differ in trapping and in a documented off-target action, with d-APV" (a suggestion; the laboratory may change it).

## 5. Other NMDAR-directed agents: one decisive-difference line each, and the next-step verdict
* **Dextromethorphan:** general pharmacology (a low-affinity NMDAR blocker with other targets) is ASSUMED, not read in this unit, and no kinetic datum was read. Paediatric epilepsy evidence is confounded sibling work and a 13-infant perioperative RCT with an EEG endpoint; label paediatric safety not established; overdose seizures. **Next step: no.** It adds no property the ketamine, memantine and d-APV triple lacks, and its evidence is not about epilepsy with bursting.
* **Amantadine:** the lowest-affinity adamantane in the primaries (IC50 39 uM against 1.4 uM for memantine in one study; trapping voltage-dependent); the only agent here with a paediatric label section (age 1 year and above, per its label) and small paediatric epilepsy series, but in absence and SWAS, and its label and an overdose case say it can worsen seizures. **Next step: no** (low potency would need concentrations at which off-target actions are unknown; as a third pore blocker it adds little).
* **Magnesium:** the endogenous voltage-dependent blocker that competes with ketamine and memantine at the deep site; **already a variable of the brief** (the Mg2+ ladder of section 13.6), not a separate arm. Two-child case series in refractory status epilepticus; label indication is eclampsia. **Next step: no new arm; keep the ladder.**
* **GluN2B-selective negative allosteric modulators (ifenprodil-site; radiprodil):** not pore blockers; remove a subunit-defined fraction of NMDAR current; radiprodil is the one class member with a registered randomised paediatric trial (GRIN-related disorder, recruiting, no results; an infantile spasms phase 1b of 3 infants, one spasm-free, two improved, abstract) and a 2026 structural paper on its binding. The brief already carries a GluN2B-NAM arm and WO-10 (the NAM-sensitive fraction at P13 to P17). **Next step: yes, one bounded dossier** (state dependence and kinetics of the NAM class from primaries, the age dependence of the GluN2B share in cortex, paediatric exposure), as the probe that separates "NMDAR current" from "pore-blocker action". It is a probe, not a priority therapeutic candidate.
* **Memantine:** a comparator and probe in the triple, no priority dossier (its evidence for DEE is one abstract-level crossover trial and its seizure signal in GRIN gain-of-function is a reason for caution, not for confidence). If the memantine trial's full text is reached by a human visit (the existing human-visit item), the paired analysis and responder definition would be the first things to read.

## 6. Rival explanations and counter-evidence kept
* "Kinetics separate the two agents" is **contradicted in part** by Emnett (IC50 and re-equilibration time not significantly different) and by the similar Mg2+ shifts; it is supported in part by the trapping percentages (86 against 71, one study), by Glasgow's concentration dependence of unblock (memantine/ketamine recovery ratio 0.54 at 1 uM and 3.96 at 100 uM at GluN1/2A), by Emnett's larger fast component for memantine (62% against 48%) and by the GluN2A IC50 ratio of about 2.4 to 2.5 in Kotermanski and Johnson. A ketamine against memantine difference at matched depth could therefore be kinetic or off-target; the triple is the design that separates them (INFERENZA).
* "Ketamine is fully trapped" rests on wording in two abstracts and one introduction; the one measurement gives 86%.
* The Ih action of ketamine is documented in wild-type cortical neurons at 20 uM only (Chen 2009, held page; ADV-046 part A); its relevance at the bath concentration needed for deep block in 1.3 mM Mg2+ is **not** established: with Mg2+ the IC50 at GluN2A/2B is about 5 uM for ketamine and 10 to 13 uM for memantine, which sets the concentrations a slice would need (INFERENZA from recombinant cells at -66 mV).
* Memantine in children is better characterised for safety than ketamine in chronic exposure, but its seizure safety in DEE is unread; the autism trials excluded active epilepsy.
