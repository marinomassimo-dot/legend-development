## Informativeness rule (fixed now, applied per analyte)
An `ns` is **informative** only if all hold: the analyte is tabulated (unit stated); n of at least 3 per genotype is stated for that analyte; the stage of the draw is stated and is the
same for both genotypes (a WT sampled at the hom moribund age, or an age-matched WT, are both stated, not assumed); a spread (SD, SEM or per-animal values) is printed so
a null-sized effect can be tested against it; and the printed means exclude a null-sized effect (the hom mean lies on the WT side of half of the null's relative change, with the printed spread).
Otherwise the analyte is **uninformative**: ns is not an absence. Classes per analyte: DIFFERENT (printed test significant or null-sized change in the means); NS-INFORMATIVE;
NS-UNINFORMATIVE (name which condition fails: analyte missing, n, stage, fasting state, pooling, spread); NOT TABULATED.

## One strong counterexample to E1, and what makes an ns uninformative here
1. **Euthanasia by CO2 asphyxiation before the draw** can raise measured total CO2 and stress glucose in every genotype alike, flattening a genotype difference in exactly the two analytes that carry the null's "acidotic" and "hypoglycaemic" labels: an ns for total CO2 and glucose is then uninformative even with a large n.
2. **Nephropathy in 52 percent of homozygotes** (Suppl. Table 4) predicts a BUN rise in at least half the homozygotes; a uniformly ns BUN with a printed spread that includes high animals would contradict E1's "no difference" or show the pooled mean hiding two groups.
3. A **pooled terminal sample** (moribund hom against non-moribund WT, or both sexes pooled) and a **non-fasted draw** make an ns uninformative; an **omitted analyte** (e.g. no pH, no creatinine, as in the null paper) is NOT TABULATED, not ns.
