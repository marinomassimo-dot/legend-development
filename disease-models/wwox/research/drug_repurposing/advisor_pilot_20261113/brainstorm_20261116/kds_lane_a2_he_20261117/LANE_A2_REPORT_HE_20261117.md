* DEE 46 (the entry text names GRIN2D): ketamine with magnesium for refractory status epilepticus, one human case report of improvement (27616483, HUMAN_CLINICAL) and one model-organism item graded REFUTE (40277233: "acute administration of ketamine at a low dose ... had a limited effect"), plus memantine items graded SUPPORT (human) and one REFUTE (individuals with the same GRIN2D variant and completely different outcomes, 41489401).
* Nonketotic hyperglycinemia: dextromethorphan/ketamine as NMDA-antagonist therapy; the KB's items are graded OTHER and HUMAN_CLINICAL ("no significant relation between plasma glycine levels and ..."), and one OTHER item says the drugs' role "has not been" established.

**Orphan-node scan** (`dismech_graph_scan.tsv`: per entry nodes, edges, parentless nodes beyond the first, nodes reachable from the first): 4,066 entries with a graph, 2 without; 1,331 (32.7 %) with at least one parentless node beyond the first, and in exactly those the reachable set is smaller than the node set; median 6 nodes; median share of nodes reachable from the first node 1.0 (two thirds of entries are fully connected). In the WWOX entry the two parentless nodes (atrophy, mortality) are what made the input's denominator 14. The scan treats the first listed node as the root; an entry with several roots by design would be counted as having orphans.

**How far to trust this, honestly.** One run, one case, one observation, not a rate. **My brief localised the check**: it pointed at finding 2 (the GSK-3β table), finding 4 (the pooled response) and the graph counts. The preregistration said the brief "says nothing about expected defects", which is true, but pointing at three regions is a large help that the expectations did not price; so what is measured is a *focused* claim-faithful reading of named regions against supplied abstracts, not a blind search of the whole report. Under those conditions a sonnet delegate at high effort found every defect that lane A found in those regions, and was missed only on a harm omitted from the sources (a defect of omission, which a reader checking claims against abstracts does not see unless it also reads what the abstract says that the report does not). The input's own graph-versus-text inconsistency was found with no outside source, which is why my 0.05 was wrong. What is not measured: an unfocused verification of the whole report; a verification that has to fetch its own sources; a verifier given the KB entry (F11 in its original sense).
For the review stage this suggests: brief verifiers with the claim regions AND the sources, ask explicitly for "what the source says that the claim leaves out" (the one miss), and keep a mechanical quote check as the cheap first layer, since it passes F1, F2 and F6 by construction (the quotes are verbatim).
