The censoring rules accepted in Update v2 (left-censored first-step bursts, right-censored no-burst, per-slice threshold, alternating step order, two titrants whose genotype ordering must agree) are the right ones.

## Weakest link: the K+ titrant reads the intrinsic profile that the heterozygote already shares with the knockout
Breton 2021 states that S-KO and S-HT neurons are both more depolarised (Table 1) and, per the dossiers of the day, carry the same input-resistance rise. A stepwise rise of extracellular K+ shifts the potassium reversal and depolarises cells in proportion to their resting leak: a cell with higher input resistance and a more depolarised rest reaches firing and recurrent drive at a lower K+ step. So the K+ onset concentration will place S-HT with S-KO because of the held intrinsic profile alone, whatever sets the 4/23 versus 36/42 difference in spontaneous bursting. P1 and the 'switch' reading (S-HT within 20% of S-KO) would then be met for a reason unrelated to the burst-controlling variable. The same experiment is informative the other way: if K+ places S-HT with S-KO and Mg2+ places it nearer wild type, the profile (K+-sensitive) and the graded burst variable (NMDAR-sensitive?) are separable, which is the first thing the incidence pattern asked for. So the two-titrant agreement rule should be read as follows: DISAGREEMENT between K+ and Mg2+ on S-HT is not a failure of the endpoint, it is the main result; agreement is the weaker case.

## Second: the Mg2+ titrant is not neutral to the NMDAR term, so it cannot be used to read NMDAR-class drugs
Extracellular Mg2+ is the NMDAR pore blocker. Lowering it unblocks the NMDAR current in every genotype; an NMDAR pore-blocking agent measured against a low-Mg2+ ladder moves the same quantity the ladder moves (competitive, one axis), and P3 ('abolishes bursts at any concentration reached') is partly built in. Likewise a K+ leak opener and a K+ ladder act on one axis. For each pilot arm, state which titrant it shares an axis with and read that arm against the OTHER titrant, with the same-axis pairing reported as a check, not as evidence.

## Third: a shift in concentration is not a shift in drive, and 'the same units in every genotype' is an assumption
Raised K+ moves the reversal logarithmically; Mg2+ block of the NMDAR is sigmoidal and voltage-dependent. A genotype difference or a drug shift in mM does not map linearly to a drive difference, so shifts can differ in mM and be equal in drive, or the reverse, depending on where on the curve the genotypes sit. Express each ladder in the model's own drive variable (a Nernst-equivalent for K+, the Mg2+ unblock fraction for Mg2+) before comparing genotypes or drugs, or compare on a log scale and declare that it is ordinal. 'Distance' is also one coordinate along one axis; two ideas can produce the same shift along an axis through different mechanisms (the arm table shows it), so a threshold shift never identifies the mechanism by itself.

## Fourth: a wild-type slice driven to burst by K+ or low Mg2+ may not be the same event
The null's spontaneous bursts are about 0.66 s long, recur at a median interval of 26.2 s (interquartile range 14 to 55 s). Slices in elevated K+ or low Mg2+ generate interictal- and ictal-like activity with other durations and shapes. The bursting-wild-type control that I asked for in I4 and I13 works only if the driven event matches the null's in duration, interval structure, layer and pharmacology (d-APV sensitivity). State the match criteria first (duration within the null's interquartile range, same sensitivity to d-APV) and treat a mismatch as 'different event', not as a control.

## Fifth: the stated margins overlap and may sit inside the noise
D5b says 'distance' is supported if S-HT onset is in the lower third of the interval between S-WT and S-KO, and 'switch' if it is within 20% of S-WT or of S-KO. If the lower third is the third nearer the knockout, the region 0 to 20% from the knockout belongs to both outcomes; if it is the third nearer wild type, 'distance' predicts a heterozygote close to wild type, which contradicts P1 ('nearer the knockout than 0.17 would suggest'). Define the interval direction and make three disjoint regions with a no-call between them (for example, intermediate = 25 to 75% of the interval; within 20% of either end = switch; the rest uncalled). Then fix the number of slices from the pilot's own slice-to-slice SD: with onset SD comparable to the interval between genotypes, a 20% margin is unresolvable and the call must be 'uninformative' (the same pre-declared stop used in the matrix).

## Sixth: design details that decide whether a number exists at all
Dwell per step must come from the interval distribution (accepted); the ladder is irreversible in practice (ionic shifts, rundown, possible kindling-like plasticity), so randomise or alternate direction in a subset, and put a return-to-baseline check at the end; the cutting and incubation conditions of the slices (thickness, layer II/III versus V, hours since cutting) enter the threshold, so slice order and genotype should be interleaved within the day, animal as the unit.
