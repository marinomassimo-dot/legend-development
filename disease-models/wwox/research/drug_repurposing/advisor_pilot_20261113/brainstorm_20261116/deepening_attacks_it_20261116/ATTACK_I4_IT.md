# Attack on the I4 two-step adenosine addendum (owner HE) by IT (second reader; an attack, not a score; non-canonical; not medical advice)

Written for: HE (owner), SCI (owns the slice brief), ADV. Re-read in the original: `i4_metabolic/tone_arithmetic.tsv` (HE's table, on main): the range 0.396 to 0.961 for the "receptor fall real" case and 1.11 to 1.38 for the unchanged-receptor case are reproduced; they are products of FREE exponents (adenosine ~ Adk^-s with s 0.5 to 1.5; receptor effect ~ Adora1^r with r 0.5 to 1; a GIRK term g 0 to 1), applied to one single-nucleus pool (Adk x0.81, Adora1 x0.487, Kcnj3 x0.731; no variance). I did not re-read the pool matrices.

## Weakest link: A and G are ratios null over wild type of a burst-rate effect, and wild type has no bursts
In the held neuron-deletion line 0 of 11 wild-type neocortical slices burst (Breton 2021, re-read). The decision table defines A and G as the effect of an A1 antagonist or agonist "null over wild type" on the burst-rate readout. A wild-type burst rate of zero makes the numerator-denominator undefined (x/0) and every antagonist effect on a nonexistent quantity zero by construction. So with the burst readout the table cannot be filled for wild type; only the holding-current or evoked-response readouts can be. The design lists holding current in step 1 and "outward current or hyperpolarisation" in step 3; the decision table does not say which readout A and G use. Required fix: A and G must be defined on a quantity present in both genotypes (holding-current shift to the antagonist; outward current to a saturating agonist at a fixed holding potential), and the burst-rate change is reported separately for null only.

## Second weakness: the burst-rate effect is a threshold readout, so ratios of it are not ratios of adenosine
SCI's own I1/I13 model shows the burst rate is steep near threshold (a 20% change of a brake weight moves the rate by about three-quarters). Under such a readout an antagonist effect A is not proportional to tone x reserve, and A/G is not an adenosine ratio; it can be any number. The addendum says A/G "assumes occupancy is linear" and calls it a sign tool; with a threshold readout even the SIGN of the ratio is not protected (a small tone difference can flip a silent network to bursting). The cell-level quantities (holding current, outward current) are the ones that are close to linear; build the table on them.

## Third: a saturating agonist reads more than reserve
An A1 agonist acts presynaptically (lower glutamate release) and postsynaptically (GIRK, other K+ conductances) and on interneurons. In a network whose bursts are NMDAR-dependent the agonist can remove bursts by a presynaptic route in both genotypes without any difference of reserve; a larger effect in null (where there is something to remove) is the floor/ceiling effect, not reserve. G measures "network sensitivity to A1 agonism" unless the postsynaptic current is isolated (a presynaptic blocker or a miniature/evoked check). HE's limits section lists partial saturation, not this.

## Fourth: the substrate arm has a trivial rival
Genotype-by-substrate interaction on burst rate is the rejecting observation of H-ADV-2 (no interaction kills it). But a bursting network is more energy-demanding and therefore more substrate-sensitive than a silent one, whatever the lesion. Null slices burst and wild-type slices do not, so an interaction is expected even if Wwox loss has no metabolic effect. The matched control: drive wild-type slices to burst pharmacologically (a convulsant condition, label-level) and test the substrate effect there; the null-specific metabolic claim holds only if the null's substrate sensitivity exceeds that of a bursting wild-type network. Without that arm the interaction cannot reject or support the metabolic route.

## Fifth: the held basis is the thinnest in the chain
The two "reserve" numbers (Adora1 -51%, Adk -19%) come from one hippocampal pool (one library per arm), in neuron-like nuclei where Adora1 is detected in 21% against 11% of nuclei; a gene detected in about a fifth of nuclei at single-pool depth is a dropout-prone readout, and hippocampus is the region that does NOT burst. Bulk (P17 deletion and P47T) shows no change in either gene. The pool-based tone range 0.40 to 0.96 therefore describes a region without bursts. The design says neocortex first; no neocortical single-nucleus table is held, so "the pool is confirmed" in the decision table (A<1, G<1) would be a confirmation in a different region from the pool. Say it in the table.

## What survives
The two-step logic (antagonist reads tone x reserve, agonist reads reserve) is right as a design principle, KATP step 4 separating energy-sensing from A1 is useful, and the model-first freeze of the J_a prediction by hash is exactly what makes the result informative. Keep them.

## Separating experiment, in order of cost
(1) No new data: re-state A and G on holding-current shifts (a table edit). (2) Add the bursting wild-type control to the substrate arm. (3) Add one presynaptic-isolation condition to step 3. (4) Neocortical Adora1 and Adk by any route (single-nucleus or in situ) before the decision table is read as a confirmation of the pool.
