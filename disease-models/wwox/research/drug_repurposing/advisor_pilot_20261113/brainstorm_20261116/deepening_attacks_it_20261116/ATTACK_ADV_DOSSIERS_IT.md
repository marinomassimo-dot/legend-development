## I5 (PV-axon timing)
**Weakest link: the chain ignores the held measurements it needs, and its step 3 is the likeliest to fail on timing.** I5 argues from myelin-gene transcripts (Mbp -77% hippocampus, -56% neocortex; the dossier's numbers, not re-read by me) while the repository already holds direct P17 measurements (E08-E10: fewer myelinated axons, higher g-ratio, longer callosal latency). Those are the I5 prior and are callosal (excitatory commissural axons), not PV basket-cell axons. Step 3 (PV axons are heavily myelinated at P13-P17) is ASSUMED; in mouse neocortex PV-axon and cortical myelination is mostly a feature of weeks 3 to 8 and later (my recollection, to be re-verified in a source), and seizure onset is stated from about P9. If PV-axon myelin is not yet substantial at the age bursts begin, a conduction-delay mechanism cannot be what ignites them, and the myelin deficit at P17 is as likely a consequence of early hyperactivity (activity-dependent myelination; INV-06 H2) as a cause.
**Best rival:** (i) the bursts and activity change precede and reduce myelination (INV-06 H2; the co-culture without seizures argues for a neuron-intrinsic contribution as well, so the two are not exclusive); (ii) the reduced sIPSC frequency is a presynaptic release or bouton-number effect, not a delay, and the held sIPSC recordings used a 140 mM chloride pipette (noted in I13): an amplitude/frequency result in that condition says little about timing.
**Separating experiment, cheapest first:** (1) no new data: read E08-E11 and the P9 onset statement and state in I5 what the held myelin measurements show and that they are callosal; (2) MBP/Caspr co-stain of PV axons (the dossier's D5b) at P9, P13 and P17 in wild type first: if PV-axon myelin is absent at P9-P13 in wild type, I5 is killed as the ignition mechanism by a measurement; (3) the latency experiment with a PV-specific readout (optogenetic PV stimulation, disynaptic IPSC latency) at P13-P17 and a matched pharmacologically hyperactive wild-type control for the consequence rival. The 'hippocampus loses more myelin signal and does not burst' point is used to make I5 'necessary, not sufficient'; that makes the hippocampus unable to falsify I5. State the prediction instead: latency lengthens in both regions, bursts only where I3's brake is absent, and write down what hippocampal latency equal to wild type would mean.

## I9 (PNN and PV maturation)
**Weakest link: the evidence for immaturity is a handling-sensitive signal from the wrong region.** The immediate-early gene fall (Fos 3.05 to 1.40, Arc 9.40 to 4.68, Npas4 6.96 to 4.68, Egr1 10.32 to 6.15 CPM; the dossier's numbers, not re-read) comes from one single-nucleus pool per arm in HIPPOCAMPUS, which does not burst; P47T shows no fall. Immediate-early genes in nuclei preparations are sensitive to dissection and handling, and with one pool per arm batch and genotype are confounded. They then explain NEOCORTICAL PV immaturity by analogy. A lower immediate-early signal in a non-bursting region is also what 'less activity in the hippocampus' predicts, which I3 already needs.
**Best rival:** PV cells are mature and the network is simply stronger driven; PV marker variation between laboratories reflects antibody, fixation or age; net assembly timing is later than the burst window (nets around neocortical PV cells assemble across roughly the third to fifth postnatal weeks; my recollection, to be verified), so 'no net at P13-P17' is expected in wild type and shows nothing; the discriminating comparison is at P21-P30 and the burst must still exist there, but the held burst data end at P17.
**The 'surprising' prediction is not specific.** Burst rate falling with age is predicted equally by I13 (delta rises with age), I3, a compensation of any sort, and by developmental waning of any burst: it separates 'developmental' from 'degenerative' but does not separate I9 from them. A specific I9 prediction is needed: net (WFA) and Kv3.1 density per PV cell lower than age-matched wild type at P21 with normal PV counts, AND a rescue of bursts by a maturation manipulation that does not act on any other idea's link.
**Separating experiment:** the held-table module lookup is fine as step 1 but its outcome cannot reject I9 (RNA is not maturation). The test is staining in the same animals as the burst recording at P14, P21, P30 with an age-matched wild-type bursting control; kill if net and Kv3.1 per PV cell are normal at P21.

## I10 (sleep and thalamocortical state)
**Weakest link: slices have no thalamus, so I10 cannot be an explanation of the slice bursts that I1 and U1 treat as its evidence.** The chain borrows the neocortical slice sag/rebound (T13) and the ethosuximide result in PTZ pups (specificity not shown, as ADV's own seed records) to predict a thalamocortical state dependence. They may both be true, but the table U1 uses I10 as one column of one picture: the slice bursts and in vivo sleep seizures are different circuit levels.
**Numbers.** D5(a) was done: sleep timing and seizure type were added to the per-child extraction (addendum 1): sleep-wake timing is stated for 3 of 42 children (coded mixed twice with a conflict between main text and a figure legend, asleep once for myoclonic seizures only), so the zero-cost test returned no held data. Say so in I10.
**Best rival:** the clustering at wake transitions and in sleep is the standard feature of infantile spasms and of many childhood epilepsies (to be re-verified) and need not involve rebound; 'NREM clustering' therefore needs the T-type response to carry the specificity (a T-type blocker reducing sleep-clustered events more than wake-time ones), which is the stronger prediction and should be the headline.
**Experiment caveats:** sleep staging in rodent pups before the third week is immature and EEG in a null line with early lethality needs survival to P21 (check from the held line descriptions); define the staging criteria and the survival window first.
