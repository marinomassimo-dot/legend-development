## Weakest link: the 'phasic inhibition is the weak knob' reading (Q1) is contradicted by the one knob whose size the held data measure
In the rivals file, phasic inhibition succeeds only for a wild-type weight of 3 times the knockout's (t = 2.0, S-HT at lam 0.5) or 2 times (t = 1.0, S-HT near wild type at lam 0.9), and this is called 'weak' and 'large'. The held pooled-control to knockout sIPSC amplitude ratio is 2.08, which is the t = 1.0 case: S-HT in the window (0.05 and 0.07), S-WT 0.05, within the success criteria. Of the graded candidates, phasic inhibition is the only one whose required size is measured in the repository, and the measured size equals the size that the model needs. The tonic weight (t about 0.6 to 1.0) and steady chloride (t = 0.3) are not measured at all. So the sentence 'a 100 to 200% higher phasic inhibition' is not an implausible demand; it is what the recordings show (+108% for the pooled controls). This inverts the ranking in 'In plain words': the best-supported graded component by held data is the phasic one, and the 'most efficient' (chloride, 0.3) is the one with no held measurement. Caveats that cut both ways: (1) amplitude is not the model's weight J_I (linear mapping assumed, as the file says), frequency falls only 18%; (2) the pipette condition (high chloride) inflates IPSC amplitude and its genotype ratio may not hold at physiological chloride; (3) S-CTL pools WT and HT, so the S-HT value is not known: if S-HT were at the knockout level the phasic component would be a step, not graded (the KS tests say S-HT differs from S-KO, with events as the unit); (4) 9 knockout cells from 5 animals.

## Second: 'efficiency' compares fractional changes of knobs that sit at different distances from their own cliffs
The smallest t that succeeds depends on how close each knob's baseline value sits to the model's threshold, a property of the fit (made on the knockout's two medians), not of the biology. A knob near its cliff succeeds at a small t whatever its biological range. The table therefore ranks distance-to-cliff in the fitted network; the statement that chloride is 'the most efficient graded component' and 'first-rank' is a statement about the model's tuning. The robust conclusion is the file's first one (inhibition-side graded components can produce the incidence; the NMDAR sustainer as scanned cannot), and it should stop there until sizes are measured.

## Third: the success windows are tighter than the data allow, and the wild-type end hides a degeneracy
Held incidences are 0/11, 4/23, 36/42 slices (re-read in the original, 14 mice for S-HT). The exact binomial 95% intervals of those are about 0 to 28%, 5 to 39% and 71 to 95% (my arithmetic, not read from the paper). The success criteria (S-WT 0 to 10%, S-HT 5 to 35%, S-KO at least 70%) are inside what the data allow and exclude parameter sets the data would accept (for example S-WT 15%); and slices from the same animal are not independent. More important: with a single graded component and everything else at the knockout value, the wild-type end sits at the knob's value times (1 + t) with all other knobs left at knockout level, which is not a wild type; the true wild type differs from the knockout in all knobs at once, and the incidence curve cannot identify which. The joint grading of two knobs (for example 0.15 phasic with 0.2 tonic) would also succeed; so the incidence data cannot rank knobs, only the experiment that measures each component in S-WT, S-HT and S-KO can, as the file concludes.

## What the held data already measure on the graded side (no new data)

The excitatory graded term is therefore real and measured (its size is in the figure, not the text) while the model carries it at e = 0.2 by assumption.
