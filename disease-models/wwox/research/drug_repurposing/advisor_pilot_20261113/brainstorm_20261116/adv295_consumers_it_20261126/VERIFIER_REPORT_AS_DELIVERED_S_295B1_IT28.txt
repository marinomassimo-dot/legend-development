BLOCK A1 (Helton 2026)
Fragment: "so, in this study, the direction of the Wnt-activation effect is the same in both models ... only the number of remodelling markers that respond differs; a model-dependent Wnt direction is not shown by this paper (immature dentate granule cells, mouse, day 14, one dose)".
- "Axin2 fell in the epileptogenic zone ... in both" is supported.
  - Results para 6, Fig 5 and Fig 6 legends: in IHK, Axin2 in POMC-GFP+ immature cells was below baseline ipsilaterally. In IPK it was below baseline in both hemispheres, which were averaged.
  - Fig 5C-D: contralateral IHK Axin2 was unchanged after kainate.
- "Restored to baseline by the GSK3 inhibitor in both" is supported. Legends 5B and 6B say SB415286 raised Axin2 to saline-control (baseline) levels. No p-values are given in the text for Fig 5/6, so I cannot confirm the significance of the Axin2 comparisons.
- "Remodelling was reduced in both" is supported.
  - IHK: ipsilateral dispersion (Fig 3C) and migration (3D) fell. Contralateral neurogenesis (3E) fell. Proximal dendrite angle fell in both hemispheres (3F,G).
  - IPK: only dendrite angle fell (Fig 4E). Dispersion, migration and neurogenesis were unaffected (Fig 4B-D).
- "Only the number of markers that respond differs" is supported as a statement about drug response. IPK is a subset of the IHK responders.
- The baseline pathology also differs between the models: no dispersion in IPK. The authors stress model differences, so this clause is not an overstatement.
- "Mouse, day 14, one dose" is supported.
  - 8-10-week-old C57BL/6J and POMC-eGFP mice, both sexes 1:1.
  - 10 mg/kg/day i.p., dosed daily for 14 days from 24 h after kainate (Fig 2 legend).
  - Histology "2 weeks after seizure induction". The exact euthanasia day (14 or 15) is not stated, so "day 14" means about 2 weeks.
- ADD (optional) so a reader of this text alone is not misled: "in the contralateral IHK dentate gyrus Axin2 did not fall although remodelling occurred and was reduced by the drug, so Axin2 restoration does not account for every effect."
