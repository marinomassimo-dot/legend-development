question: The decisive unknown of the bridge is the brain exposure of ethosuximide in the P12 to P17 null pup at the regimen of the one Wwox experiment (Cheng 2020), and nothing is held below adult: the only exposure number is an adult mouse total brain value (134.9 ug/g, about 0.96 mM total, at 72.3 mg/kg i.p., Swiss mice 24 to 30 g) at about half the Wwox dose. Before the exposure step is run, build ONE prior bound from published ontogeny of drug disposition: (1) what is known of ethosuximide pharmacokinetics in rodents and in children by age (half-life, clearance, volume, brain-to-plasma ratio, linearity; the label's paediatric clearance facts; rodent ontogeny of the metabolising enzymes involved; neonatal mouse blood-brain barrier and transporter ontogeny for a small polar drug) as reported, with source anchors; (2) an explicit chain of assumptions from the adult value to a P12 to P17 range at the Cheng regimen, each step labelled data, inference or assumption, with the range both ways (clearance lower in pups raises exposure; a larger distribution volume or a different brain-to-plasma ratio lowers it); (3) what that range means against the held thresholds (the 0.6 mM recombinant and above 5 mM readings of potency, the adult effective band about 2 to 6 mM, and the 5 mM-class bound), and whether the tolerable-exposure rows of the bridge outcome table (pragmatic null, tolerance-limited) are reachable in principle; (4) which measurements the exposure step must make so that the prior is replaced by data (matrices, time points, ages, total versus free), kept to the minimum that discriminates. A prior bound is a hypothesis with its assumptions, not a result: say which assumption dominates the width. Competing explanation and the observation that would weaken the bound per step; pre-register the stop rule; two routes for any 'not found'.
why_now: Today's reading of the bridge ranks ethosuximide only weakly and partly through an absence (no neonatal harm row, no pup exposure); the exposure step is laboratory work, but a prior bound tells the laboratory which doses and time points to expect and whether the outcome table's rows can be reached; it is the one bridge-lead question that no comparison has touched.
