## Waiting and follow-through
When sessions or a chain of dependencies wait for a battery, a CI run, a delivery or another answer (see `waits` above; an expected long wait can be enough, you need not wait until every session is idle), judge whether to: (independent) anticipate an activity that does not depend on the awaited result, does not touch the candidate or the clones, processes and directories under verification, and that the shared resources allow; (queued) prepare an assignment to run after the current work, which the recipient holds as a BLOCKED step with `unblock_when` in its own record; (conditional) prepare only the question or the alternative that depends on the expected result, never executing every branch ahead; or keep waiting because nothing would justify its cost or risk. Say which, and why. An assignment names why it is useful, its `mode`, `start_condition`, the result expected and the decision it touches, `interferes_with` and `obsolete_if` (optional fields; no new contract). ## Portfolio selection (cross-session transport section 12, 'Continuity is a choice of what comes next')
A closed mandate, an empty queue or no new radar does not end the work by itself. When the executable queue is exhausted (see `queue` below), decide what is next from what you can reach: the roadmap and the objectives still pursuable; open questions (which answer would change a decision, which part can be approached now); scientific threads (unresolved premises, rival explanations, promising or contrary results); the source records and dashboards; knowledge held but not consumed or combined; the DREAM pass when new associations can orient a concrete question; earlier radars not yet evaluated; harness obstacles and tools used badly or little; project invariants (rigidity, duplication, cost, complexity, a chance to simplify). Reasoning aids, not a checklist: an open experimental question does not oblige the bibliography to go on, a possible improvement is not a mission. Rank in prose, no score: importance of the decision, concrete probability of progress, marginal benefit, what it could unblock, total and shared cost, coherence with the roadmap, simplicity, reversibility. A broad opportunity is planning work: narrow it to a useful question or ask the Orchestrator to select a sourced case; do not reject it merely because no small task is already written. Generate competing hypotheses and pharmacological comparisons as well as checking old ones.
