# IT44 P3: a resting K+ conductance to add (S11), framed before any compound is looked at

Non-canonical working note. Not medical advice; no dose, schedule or route. Written 2026-11-10 before the search; a later change is a dated addendum. Source of the relation: SCI43 hypothesis KH1 (`potassium_conductances_sci43_20261110/output/hypotheses.tsv`, `S7_coda.md`), which names the action and its premise but no compound and notes that the catalogue holds no activator. | Item | Statement |
|---|---|
| Observation to explain | Wild-type to knockout layer II/III neurons (P13-P17): input resistance 102.9 to 143.0 MOhm (+39 percent), rest -79.3 to -73.6 mV, time constant 18.6 to 21.3 ms; the heterozygote equals the knockout on resistance; bursts 4/23 versus 36/42 (source figures as carried by SCI43; DATO as stated there). |
| Competing explanations | (a) a lost resting K+ conductance, Ba2+-sensitive (Kir2-type or K2P-type), about 2.7 nS implied (KH1); (b) a smaller cell with a fixed shunt, which gives the same implied reversal (capacitance not measured); (c) recruitment of Ih or another conductance; (d) a network consequence under intact synapses (KH4); (e) a developmental or recording difference (KH5). |
| Action that could correct, compensate or discriminate | Raise a resting K+ conductance: openers of Kir2-type or K2P-type channels; also the reverse as a discriminator: a Ba2+-class blocker should shrink the genotype difference if (a) holds. The discriminator for (b) is capacitance, a measurement, not a drug. |
| Contexts where the action could be inert or worse | If the Ba2+-sensitive share per pF is equal across genotypes (a); in a cell where K+ efflux at rest already clamps the potential, an opener could silence the cell (a circuit-level loss of excitability, including interneurons); K2P channels are widely expressed (glia, interneurons, non-neural tissue), so an opener acts on cells the lesion does not touch; developmental stage (the conductance falls steeply from P7 to P28). |
| What must be known to separate these | Capacitance by genotype and the cell-selection rule; the Ba2+-sensitive current per pF; synaptic blockade; which channel family carries it; whether an opener's target is expressed in the cell. |
| Outcome to modify | Input resistance and rest toward the wild-type values at matched capacitance, then burst incidence with a matched-effect control (a non-K+ route to the same suppression). |
