### Interpretation verified (INFERENZA)
* **0.1 is a floor.** Zhang 2014 (PMC4152602), legend of the fold-enrichment-versus-expression panel: "we set any FPKM value <0.1 to 0.1 to avoid ratio inflation". Zhang 2016 (PMC4707064) reports MKI67 and TOP2A as "0.1 ± 0" in mature astrocytes, the same censoring. The 2016 text and GEO records do not describe the floor for the human table; the same laboratory and Cufflinks pipeline make the inference strong, not proven for this file. So the neuron value is "at or below 0.1 FPKM", not a measured 0.1.
* **The neuron sample is one patient, selected by Thy1.** Zhang 2016 Results: "N=12, 1, 5, 3, and 2 patients for astrocytes, neurons, oligodendrocytes, microglia, and endothelial cells"; Methods: "anti-Thy1 (CD90) to harvest neurons". It passes SNAP25 (3.6–4.7 times the whole-cortex values) and GAD1 (8–15 times whole cortex) but RBFOX3 is 0.6 and SLC17A7 16.0 (below whole cortex): the sample is not a pan-neuronal profile.
* **The whole-cortex samples are low too.** KCNQ2/SNAP25 is 0.001–0.009 in the four human whole-cortex samples, against 0.187 in mouse whole cortex (GSE52564 WC1–3), 0.208 in mouse P7 neurons and 0.122 in HPA forebrain projection neurons. RBFOX3 is 0.6–1.8 in human whole cortex against 23.7–31.2 in mouse whole cortex. Two canonical neuronal genes are near the floor in the human whole-cortex samples, so the low KCNQ2 is not specific to the neuron sample. The Zhang 2016 text says whole-cortex samples were not immunopanned; the GEO extraction-protocol text of the whole-cortex record repeats the panning sequence (identical boilerplate); I cannot tell which describes these samples.
* **Permitted:** in this one Thy1-panned adult sample KCNQ2 was at or below the floor, as in the whole-cortex samples (0.2–1.5). **Not permitted:** absence of KCNQ2 expression in human neurons; a species difference against mouse P7 neurons (30.7 FPKM, n=2); any statement about human neurons in general, about development or disease; a causal reason for the low value.
* **Candidate causes for the low value, none asserted:** (1) censoring at the 0.1 floor combined with low assigned reads; (2) 3′-biased or amplification-dependent coverage of a long multi-isoform locus with the Ovation V2 protocol and Cufflinks gene models on hg19 (the unusually low RBFOX3 in the same samples fits a gene-model or amplification effect, not proven); (3) one patient and one Thy1-selected subset (the GAD1 enrichment suggests an interneuron-biased subset; the HPA interneuron clusters nevertheless carry KCNQ2 at 82–89 nCPM); (4) tissue state, resection and about 5 h to dissociation (the paper reports 294 of 19,713 genes changing more than fourfold between resection and the end of the typical protocol); (5) a real low human mRNA level in this tissue; (6) normalisation: whole-cortex housekeeping values are about 5 to 100 times lower than in the immunopanned columns, so scale differs between sample types. Settled by: the raw reads or the supplementary tables of the paper; a second human neuron source with a documented floor; or protein or current in human neurons.

### Interpretation verified (INFERENZA)
* The producer's "0.73 of the neuronal maximum" is the ratio to forebrain projection neurons, not to the maximum (0.68). Corrected wording: "0.73 of forebrain projection neurons, 0.68 of the highest neuronal cluster".
* Does this support "an OPC contribution to a bath-applied Kv7 opener is plausible", more, or less? **At RNA level, more than "plausible"**: OPC is the only non-neuronal class with KCNQ2 RNA in the neuronal range, in two independent datasets in two species (human nuclei, mouse purified cells; the ratios differ by a factor of two and each has no replicate variance: HPA is one pooled value from three donors, mouse n=2 at different ages). **At every other level it supports nothing:** RNA is not protein, surface channel, current or drug response; nuclear RNA is not cytoplasmic; the age of the HPA donors is adult and the mouse neurons are P7, not the P13–P17 of a slice readout.

## 4 · A3 — functional data, age and preparation (depth stated per source)

* **Kv7/KCNQ in OPC/NG2 cells.** Wang et al. 2011 (PMID 21750731; PMC3130044; full text through the PubMed full-text tool, text only, figures not viewed, so partial): rat cortex-derived primary cultures (cortex of postnatal day 1–2 rats, about 10 days in culture before OPC shake-off); KCNQ2–5 mRNA in OPC; Kv7.2, 7.3, 7.5 immunostaining on 26 ± 9%, 27.1 ± 11% and 30.5 ± 7% of NG2-positive cells in rat cortex slices (Kv7.4 not detected; slice age not stated in the text read); a deactivation-relaxation (M-type) current in cultured OPC with peak 61.16 ± 6.32 pA at −40 mV (n=17), XE991 inhibition 46.1 ± 6.8% at 10 µM and IC50 13.3 µM (54 cells), room temperature, 140 mM chloride pipette; the current is nearly absent in differentiated oligodendrocytes; XE991 and TEA increased OPC migration in a Boyden chamber. **No acute-slice recording of this current, no opener tested, no age P10–P20 recording.** Absence perimeter: PubMed only, strings in `queries_verifier.tsv` (QV15–QV16), 2026-11-12; Europe PMC full-text search and preprints not searched. One relevant study.
* **Functional GABA-A on OPC around P10–P20 (abstract level only here).** Vélez-Fort 2010 (PMID 20484634): barrel cortex NG2-DsRed slices, frequency of spontaneous and miniature GABAergic activity "dramatically decreases after the second postnatal week", adult input through spillover onto extrasynaptic GABA-A. Orduz 2015 (PMID 25902404): somatosensory cortex, connectivity peak at PN10, γ2-containing GABA-A at proximal sites of fast-spiking interneuron contacts. Balia 2015 (PMID 24217990): α1 and α5 receptors at interneuron-NG2 synapses in the second postnatal week, zolpidem and α5IA acting on evoked currents, γ2 down-regulated later. Passlick 2013 (PMID 23864689): juvenile mouse hippocampus, benzodiazepines and zolpidem increase the GABA response, GABA depolarises NG2 cells to about −30 mV. **The premise "functional GABA-A in mouse OPC" and the benzodiazepine-site modulation of evoked NG2-cell currents rest on these abstracts**, as the IT46 verdict file already said; their full texts were unreachable to me (PMC page bot check; Oxford Academic 403) and I did not use another route. Reproduced from stored data: Gabra1 OPC 3.60 against neuron 5.49 and Gabrb3 21.79 against 24.53 FPKM; also Gabrg2 OPC 3.70 against 24.13 and Gabra5 0.58 against 5.88 (lower in OPC), Gabra3 23.78 against 10.06 (higher). At P13–P17 the synaptic input is on the decline (Vélez-Fort), the extrasynaptic response persists.
* **OPC activity and neuronal network readouts.** Sakry et al. 2014 (PMID 25387269; PMC4227637; keyword-directed passages of the full text, receipt depth queried_not_full_read): NG2-ablated mice show impaired NMDA-receptor-dependent LTP and inward rectifying AMPA-receptor currents in L2/3 pyramidal neurons of somatosensory cortex, rescued by the NG2 LNS domains; basal sEPSC unchanged (recorded in picrotoxin and AP5); no sIPSC measurement; slice age not found in the text read. Fang 2022 (PMID 35296664, abstract only): OPC-restricted GABA-B1 deletion, reduced interneuron density and function in developing mPFC and network changes. These are chronic genetic manipulations. **No source read reports an sIPSC or burst change after an acute OPC-directed manipulation in a P13–P17 slice.**
* **What the sources cannot say:** whether OPC in P13–P17 layer II/III carry an M-type current; whether a bath opener moves it; whether an OPC membrane change alters neuron sIPSC or bursts within minutes; any human OPC function.
* **Data that would make an OPC contribution relevant to a P13–P17 layer II/III readout:** (1) identified NG2-cell recordings (reporter mouse) in acute slices at P13–P17, near physiological temperature, showing a Kv7-blocker-sensitive current and its modulation by the opener at the concentration used; (2) KCNQ2/KCNQ3 protein in NG2 cells at that age and layer with a knockout-validated antibody; (3) neuron-restricted against OPC-restricted Kcnq2 loss (conditional alleles) with the bath effect on sIPSC and burst measured in each; (4) an OPC-to-neuron route fast enough for the recording window (the known routes are chronic).

## 5 · A4 — provenance of the HPA table and independence of the comparisons

* **Confirmed through the HPA documentation:** `proteinatlas.org/humanproteome/brain`, section "Brain Single Nuclei": "Brain cell type expression profiles are based on single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq) data from the study by Siletti K et al. (2023), comprising over 3 million nuclei sampled across the human brain. For integration into the HPA, approximately 2.6 million nuclei from 11 major brain regions were included and reclustered, with neuronal and non-neuronal populations analyzed separately. The resulting clusters were annotated … and organized into 48 main cell types" (quotation shortened); the page links PMID 37824663. The stored table has exactly 48 cluster types (34 neuronal by my classification, as HPA states) and all 48 names appear on the HPA pages. The release history says the data are "based on the same dataset as before but with updated clustering and cell type naming" in v26.0 (2026-11-11).
* **Not confirmed through the paper:** the Science page answered with a bot check (403) and no PMCID exists, so I read only the PubMed abstract: three postmortem donors, about 100 dissections, more than three million nuclei, 461 clusters and 3313 subclusters. Donors' sex and age, region list, 10x chemistry and the paper's supercluster names were not reached. HPA names (for example "forebrain projection neurons", "pvalb+ excitatory neurons") are HPA's own re-annotation of 48 types and cannot be matched to the paper's 461 clusters from what I read. The table has no donor, region or version field; the hash pins the analysed file; a HEAD request shows the served file has the same size (5,254,086 bytes), so the stored file is probably the current download (size equality, not a hash comparison).
* **Independence:** independent: HPA vs Zhang 2016 human (different laboratory, donors, assay: nuclei 10x against immunopanned bulk total RNA with amplification and a floor), and HPA vs Zhang 2014 mouse (different species and laboratory). Their KCNQ2 disagreement (about 100+ nCPM against 0.1–1.5 FPKM) is therefore a between-assay discordance and neither side overrules the other. **Not independent:** clusters within HPA (one experiment, three donors, one pipeline and re-clustering; ratios between clusters share depth and ambient-RNA effects); Zhang 2016 and Zhang 2014 (same laboratory, immunopanning, Cufflinks pipeline, the 0.1 floor); the mouse neurons (P7) and OPC (P17) of one study come from different ages and different pooled litters.

## 6 · A5 — is the OPC-aware control necessary

| Statement | OPC-aware control | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| (a) a bath-applied Kv7 opener changes the burst or sIPSC readout in the slice | **not needed** | Descriptive of the bath effect; vehicle, dose and genotype controls apply, not a cell-type arm. |
| (b) the effect is neuronal | **necessary** | A bath reaches all cells; OPC is the only non-neuronal class with neuron-range KCNQ2 RNA (A2) and the only non-neuronal class with a reported Kv7-blocker-sensitive current (cultured rat, A3). The statement excludes a class that RNA and one culture study do not exclude. |
| (c) the effect acts through KCNQ2/3 channels | **useful but not necessary** | The channel attribution needs a Kv7 blocker or Kcnq2/Kcnq3 genetic arm; the cell type is a separate question. It becomes necessary if the sentence reads "neuronal KCNQ2/3". Opener selectivity across KCNQ2–5 is not tested here. |
| (d) the effect is the Wwox-specific component | **useful but not necessary** as worded | The necessary control is the genotype-by-drug interaction in matched cells; the OPC arm becomes necessary if the component is also called neuronal. I did not read any OPC data on Wwox. |

Astrocytes and microglia: **astrocytes** need no such arm on KCNQ2 RNA grounds (HPA 0.13–0.14 of neurons; mouse P7 astrocyte 0.22 against 30.68 FPKM neurons). **Microglia** need none for a KCNQ2-dependent attribution (HPA 0.02; Zhang 2016 myeloid at the floor), but human microglia KCNQ3 RNA is the highest value of the HPA table and is echoed by the independent human myeloid samples, while mouse microglia Kcnq3 is at the floor; if the opener acts on KCNQ3-containing channels the microglia are not excluded by RNA. Limits: RNA only, no protein, no current, no mouse data at P13–P17.

## 12 · Where the SCI47 README wording differs from these verdicts
(Added after the verdicts above were written and unchanged by it.)

* **OPC at "0.73 of the neuronal maximum"; "maximum 151.9, forebrain projection neurons".** My numbers: 0.73 is correct against forebrain projection neurons (151.9), but the highest neuronal cluster is ca1-3 excitatory (164.3, a hippocampal class that is also forebrain), giving 0.68; the README's wording (and the S0 and consumer row 1 of the README) should say "of forebrain projection neurons". The "1.36 times the glial maximum" and the 6.9 and over 40 are against the same 151.9.
* **"Not confined to its neuron sample; a floor, not an expression level; cause not established."** Agrees with my reading. I add the source of the floor (Zhang 2014 legend: values below 0.1 set to 0.1; Zhang 2016 "0.1 ± 0"), that 0.1 is the minimum of all 45 columns (32% of genes in the neuron column), the quantitative comparison (KCNQ2/SNAP25 0.001-0.009 in human whole cortex against 0.122 in HPA forebrain projection neurons and 0.187 in mouse whole cortex) and that the neuron sample is one patient selected with anti-Thy1. No cause asserted by either of us.
* **"Does not show a biological difference between species."** Agrees: the table cannot permit the species statement either way.
* **"An OPC contribution ... plausible."** My reading is stronger at RNA level only (OPC 8th of 48 clusters, above 27 of 34 neuronal types, 0.35 in mouse P7/P17 and 0.73 in human nuclei) and unchanged at function level: one culture study (rat, XE991-sensitive current, 61 pA peak) is the only functional Kv7 evidence in OPC; nothing in slice or at P13-P17.
* **KCNQ3 caution (microglia 2,632 against 846 in forebrain excitatory).** Agrees and sharpens: 2,632 exceeds every neuronal cluster (maximum 1,670.8), and the independent human myeloid samples of Zhang 2016 read 9.7-15.7 FPKM, so the human microglia KCNQ3 signal has two supports while the mouse value is at the floor; I keep microglia open for KCNQ3-containing channels and closed for a KCNQ2-dependent attribution.
* **Origin of the HPA table.** The README says the pages retrieved do not state the dataset and the source "was not verified". The HPA brain resource page (`/humanproteome/brain`, moved there in v26.0) does state it: Siletti et al. 2023, about 2.6 million nuclei from 11 regions reclustered into 48 main cell types, with a link to PMID 37824663; so the identification is confirmed by HPA documentation. The README's remark that the paper's supercluster names "resemble" the HPA labels I could not check (paper unreachable), and the HPA names are its own re-annotation. The README's statement that independence from Zhang 2016 is "likely but not documented" is documented here (different laboratory, donors, assay).
* **"Control proposed: an OPC-aware readout ... or a cell-restricted application" and the DREAM row 6 (one shared specificity control for both bath levers).** My A5 narrows it: necessary only for the cell-type attribution (b); not for the bare bath effect, and not for the channel attribution or the Wwox-specific component as worded. An identified NG2-cell recording shows whether an OPC route exists; it does not by itself separate a neuronal from an OPC share when the OPC do respond, which needs the restricted arm. "Both slice-bath levers may reach OPC" rests for the GABA-A lever on abstracts only (full texts unreachable to me).
* **"Verification of the biological inferences (not done)".** Now done by this audit for A1-A5; the README's decision paragraph holds with the corrections above.
