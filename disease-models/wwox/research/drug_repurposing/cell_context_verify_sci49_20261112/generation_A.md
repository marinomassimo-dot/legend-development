## Base (verified; `README.md`, `verify/audit.md`)
KCNQ2 RNA in OPC is in the neuronal range (8th of 48 HPA clusters; mouse P17 OPC 0.35 of P7 neurons); an M-type, XE991-sensitive current is reported in cultured rat OPC (one study, 61 pA peak) and its block increased OPC migration in a Boyden chamber; Kv7 immunostaining on 26–31% of NG2 cells in rat cortical slices (age not stated); OPC receive GABAergic synaptic input peaking near P10 with extrasynaptic phasic input persisting later (abstract level here); no source links an acute OPC manipulation to sIPSC or burst readouts. In the Wwox neuron-specific model the sIPSC amplitude fell about 52% and its frequency about 18% (SCI47 A), and slices burst (36 of 42 knockout).

## GA1 — a bath-applied Kv7 opener may act on a non-neuronal class in the slice (conditional)
**Chain.** Observation: OPC carry KCNQ2 RNA in the neuronal range and a Kv7-sensitive current in culture; a slice bath reaches every class. → Explanation: part of a Kv7 opener's effect on the burst or sIPSC readout could come from the OPC membrane (hyperpolarisation of a cell that depolarises under GABA, with consequences for K+ and GABA handling or for slower OPC-to-neuron signalling) rather than from pyramidal neurons. → Lever: a Kv7 opener at a concentration that has been shown to act on the cell class in question. → Compound or probe: retigabine/ezogabine as a **mechanism comparator** only (SCI43 roles; its neuronal current was not measured in the Wwox model); no compound is verified for OPC action in slice. → Prediction: in an OPC-reporter slice at P13–P17, NG2 cells carry an XE991- or linopirdine-sensitive current that the opener increases at the concentration used on the burst/sIPSC readout; and an OPC-restricted Kcnq2 loss reduces the bath effect by a measurable fraction while a neuron-restricted loss removes the remainder. → Discriminating experiment: identified NG2-cell recordings in the same slices as the Phase 0 cohort, then (only if a current responds) a cell-restricted Kcnq2 manipulation with the same readout.
**Expected under GA1:** a current in NG2 cells and a partial loss of the bath effect under OPC-restricted loss. **Expected under the strongest alternative (a purely neuronal action):** no opener-sensitive current in NG2 cells, or an effect unchanged by OPC-restricted loss. **Rejected by:** no Kv7-blocker-sensitive current in identified NG2 cells at that age and temperature; or an unchanged bath effect under OPC-restricted loss. **Decision changed:** whether a slice result of a Kv7 opener may be called neuronal (the OPC control is required for that statement only; A5), and whether Phase 1 of the two-lever design carries an OPC arm.
**Unresolved premise:** that an NG2-cell M-current exists in acute slice at P13–P17 (nothing in slice is known; one culture study). **Transfer limits:** rat culture (P1–2 cortex, about 10 days in vitro, room temperature, 140 mM chloride pipette) to mouse slice at P13–P17; adult human RNA to mouse; RNA to function; a global bath to a restricted application. **Counter-evidence kept:** the only links from OPC activity to neuronal readouts are chronic and genetic (NG2 ablation: LTP and AMPA-receptor phenotypes in L2/3 pyramidal neurons; OPC GABA-B1 deletion: interneuron density and function), none acute; astrocyte RNA is low; microglia low for KCNQ2. **Novelty:** (a) new combination for LEGEND (the Kv7 lever of INV-18 and the OPC/GABA-A premise of SCI47 A meet in one preparation); (b) not checked; (c) unvalidated.

## GA2 — an adjacent connection: Kv7 in OPC and the myelin axis (conditional, lower priority)
**Chain.** Observation: the Kv7 block increased OPC migration in culture (Wang 2011); the repository carries a myelination thread for WWOX loss and the glia-specific Wwox deletions gave no phenotype in the (underpowered) comparison (W09, SCI46). → Explanation: if a Kv7 opener has an OPC action, it could bear on OPC migration or differentiation, a different readout from burst or sIPSC, and could confound a long-exposure arm. → Lever: the same opener. → Prediction: at exposures longer than a slice experiment, an opener reduces OPC migration or density in culture and does not change neuronal burst readouts in a slice with OPC removed. → Experiment: a migration assay with the opener and the blocker in cultured OPC (the existing literature's assay), only if GA1's first step finds a current. **Unresolved premise:** whether OPC or myelin are altered in the neuron-specific Wwox model at P13–P17 (not measured here). **Transfer limits:** disease (hypomyelination models) to this model, species, culture to slice, migration to function. **Counter-evidence:** one culture study; the direction of an opener on migration is the inference from a blocker's opposite effect, not measured. **Novelty:** (a) new combination for LEGEND; (b) not checked; (c) unvalidated. Not proposed as an experiment now.

## Revised after the adversarial review (V11 audit section 13; Orchestrator, 2026-11-12; the sections above are kept)

**GA1 — revised; novelty (b) is withdrawn.** Links with no source: the explanation (an OPC change acutely alters K⁺/GABA handling or signalling to neurons) and the lever (a concentration shown to act on the cell class); every OPC-to-neuron route read is chronic (NG2 ectodomain cleavage, OPC GABA-B1 deletion). Inference: the additivity prediction, the experiment, "OPC depolarise under GABA" (juvenile hippocampus, abstract), retigabine as a comparator (recollection plus abstracts; none of the sources read reports an OPC action). **Alternatives of at least equal prior plausibility that GA1 did not name:** an action on interneurons rather than pyramidal cells (HPA interneuron KCNQ2 82–89 nCPM); a direct GABA-A action of the opener on a GABA-A readout (retigabine potentiation at 10 µM in a published abstract, PMID 25779225; the antiseizure effect was lost only when Kv7 and GABA-A were both blocked); recording artefacts (sIPSC amplitude SDs about 50% of the mean at n of 5–7 animals). **The "rejected by" outcomes were not discriminating as written:** a soma-level negative at 34 °C needs a positive control, a stated detection limit and a fraction-of-cells criterion (culture was at room temperature and gave 61 pA; only 26–31% of NG2 cells stained; XE991 inhibited 46% at 10 µM); "bath effect unchanged under OPC-restricted loss" needs a power statement and a recombination check. **A design limit:** an OPC-restricted Kcnq2 loss cannot be built with Cre alone in the Wwox model, whose neuron-specific deletion uses Synapsin-Cre, which would also recombine a floxed Kcnq2 in neurons; it needs an orthogonal recombinase, or the attribution is done in a wild-type background, which cannot speak to the Wwox-specific component (the conditional alleles and the Cspg4/Pdgfra drivers are the reviewer's recollection, not read). **Added transfer limits:** temperature; the 140 mM chloride pipette of the culture study; subunit composition (OPC KCNQ3:KCNQ2 about 1.5 against 5.5 in neurons; XE991 IC50 13.3 µM); cell heterogeneity; antibody validation; acute against chronic; neuron class; Synapsin-Cre model against wild type. **Statement of GA1 as accepted (IPOTESI):** *On present evidence a bath-applied Kv7 opener cannot be called neuronal in its effect on the burst or sIPSC readout of the Wwox slice: OPC carry KCNQ2 RNA in the neuronal range and cultured rat OPC show an XE991-sensitive current, but no Kv7 current, opener response or acute route from OPC to the recorded readout has been shown in slice at P13–P17, and an interneuron-class action or a direct GABA-A action of the opener are alternatives of at least equal prior plausibility. The statement "the effect is neuronal" therefore needs identified NG2-cell recordings with a stated detection limit and, if a current responds, a cell-restricted test built so that it does not share its Cre driver with the Wwox deletion.* Novelty: (a) new combination for LEGEND, modest; (c) unvalidated; (b) **not claimed** (Kv7 in OPC has been in the literature since 2011; retigabine's non-neuronal GABA-A actions are published).

**GA2 — dropped as a hypothesis; kept as a two-line note.** The glia-specific Wwox deletions (W09: Olig2-Cre and GFAP-Cre, three mice per genotype, flat and underpowered) say only that an OPC- or oligodendrocyte-intrinsic loss of Wwox is not shown to drive the phenotype; they cannot support a Kv7–OPC link. What the repository already holds and the note missed: the same paper (card H02, Figure 5B) shows fewer myelinated axons per field in the neuron-specific deletion (the field of view is the unit): so "are OPC or myelin altered in the model" is partly answered, and makes an OPC question relevant at the level of the model's phenotype, not of an acute drug effect. "The opener does not change burst readouts in a slice with OPC removed" has no source and acute OPC removal is not an experiment.

**Open questions, as revised.** Added **P (priority 0, before everything)**: does the opener change the burst or sIPSC readout at all, is the change reversed by a Kv7 blocker, does it act on GABA-A currents directly, and is the change interneuron-driven frequency or pyramidal amplitude and holding current. **1** (NG2-cell recordings) stays priority 1 only with a fixed detection design. **Q (priority 2, added)**: a cell-attribution design that does not share its Cre driver with the Wwox deletion. **2** (second human KCNQ2 table) lowered to 3 (it changes no slice decision). **3** (GABA-A full texts) stays 2. **4** (microglia KCNQ3) stays 3, folded into the pharmacology of 1. **5** (neuron- against OPC-restricted loss) revised by the Cre conflict, merged with Q.
