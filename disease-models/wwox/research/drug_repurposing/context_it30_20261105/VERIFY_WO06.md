## 3. Attribution, direction and depth problems found

1. **Item 2 / E03 node-dependence reading is internally inconsistent.** Salubrinal (raises eIF2alpha-P) and GSK2656157 (lowers PERK output) both lowering CHOP and apoptosis (E03; verified in the abstract of 28686850) is the SAME sign from opposite perturbations of the pathway, with compound effects, no target control, no p-eIF2alpha measurement (the paper reports total eIF2alpha and PERK levels), and a cell-death endpoint. That shows the sign is not set by the direction of p-eIF2alpha change (or that both act through a shared downstream or off-target route); it is not evidence FOR node-dependence. Node-dependence needs different signs at different nodes. E03's own breaker column says "the sign is not node-specific here", which contradicts README item 2 ("consistent with ... node-dependence"). E32's transfer text has the same slip ("the sign depends on the node: kinase down and phosphatase down both help").
2. **Item 2 says "the one pharmacological seizure-activity signal is a potentiator (salubrinal, E01)".** There are two mouse KA salubrinal studies with opposite sign (24728926 helps; 31557161 worsens, full text read by me). In both, salubrinal lowered ER-stress/eIF2alpha-pathway markers (E02 in the package; S1 Fig of 31557161), so calling it a "potentiator" is a label from its mechanism class, not an observed effect on p-eIF2alpha. The seizure-endpoint evidence for salubrinal is split, not a signal.
3. **E31 and item 6 claim "new at full text" for the Gadd34-versus-Perk contradiction. It is not new.** The abstract of 27807175 already says "Inhibition of the eIF2alpha holophosphatase GADD34:PP1, increases the phosphorylation of eIF2alpha ... and largely rescues S63del neuropathy. Nonetheless, reducing phosphorylation of eIF2alpha, by Perk haploinsufficiency, also ameliorates ... This contradictory finding ...". The abstract of 32973043 repeats it ("These contradictory findings"). Further, in 27807175 both the Gadd34 result (D'Antonio 2013) and the Perk+/- result (Musner 2016) are CITED earlier work (Introduction and first Results paragraph), not data of that paper; E31 does not mark itself as cited (E32 does). What really is new at full text across the five: E24 (genotype is Perk+/-, a cytokine stressor), E25 (T-cell priming unchanged), E28 (late dimerizer start, authors' own timing caveat), E29 (BiP stays induced after Chop ablation; note the later-time-point "4 to 6-fold at P42 and P120" is "data not shown" in the paper, the quoted P28 sentence only supports "similar"). E26, E27, E30, E33, E34, E35 are restatements of abstract content. "Direction unchanged for all five" (item 5) is correct.
4. **Headline item 1 over-reaches in wording.** "No mammalian primary study ... in which ISRIB, a PERK or GCN2 inhibitor, Sephin1 or an eIF2B activator was tested for seizures or in an epilepsy model" is falsified by the package's own rows: a PERK inhibitor (GSK2656157) was tested in a rat PTZ status-epilepticus model (E03/E04) and ISRIB in a PTZ mouse model (E07). It holds only if restricted to "with a seizure or network-activity endpoint". It also omits genetic mammalian seizure studies from the headline (they are in item 1's last clause and E12), and omits the salubrinal split above.
5. **Item 4 "guanabenz is unusable as an ISR arm on a bursting or network endpoint" contradicts item 8** (guanabenz with an alpha-2A antagonist control is proposed). Supported wording: confounded unless alpha-2 is controlled. E15/E16 also rest on a ligand-potency correlation across a panel in rat CA3 slices at abstract depth; the abstract has no guanabenz-with-antagonist arm, and the "mediated solely by alpha-2A" sentence is about the epinephrine-driven attenuation. Attribution of guanabenz's own slice effect to alpha-2A is therefore inferential.
6. **E12 omits cell-type specificity.** The source says "normalization of p-eIF2alpha in excitatory neurons of Fmr1-/y mice" (excitatory-neuron-specific), not whole-animal CReP knockdown. Quote is verbatim; the intervention description should say excitatory-neuron-specific.
7. E05/E06 (CO donor, febrile seizures) correctly labelled association and abstract-only; endpoint is neuronal damage. E09-E11 correct: Gcn2 deletion was tested on p-eIF2alpha and transmission, not on seizure threshold, in the text read; n-Tr20 is the seizure modifier (the paper does test seizure threshold with n-Tr20 manipulation; I did not exhaustively search for a Gcn2-null seizure arm and the package says it excluded one only "for the text read").
8. Item 3 and E18-E21 are accurate: "none of the PPP1R15A-containing holophosphatases tested" is correct and PPP1R15B is untested (E18 breaker correct). E21 quote alone is the conclusion sentence; the claim "effects persist in PPP1R15A-null / non-phosphorylatable cells" is in the preceding clause of the same sentence and cites an earlier paper (row says "cited study", correct); the quote column should carry the clause. Item 4 "rebuts the mechanism, not the benefit" is supported ("unlikely to make an important contribution to their pharmacological activity").

## 4. "No mammalian seizure study found" and INV-02 test design

- Counter-example search: no mammalian study with ISRIB / PERK inhibitor / GCN2 inhibitor / Sephin1 / eIF2B activator and a seizure or network endpoint found (agrees). Counter-examples to the broader reading (salubrinal, seizure endpoint): 31557161 (worsens) and 24728926 (improves); possible third, 34935153 (abstract only, compound unidentifiable).
- INV-02 recommendation (item 8; ISRIB/CReP test in item 9): partly supported, partly overreach. Supported: keep a network-activity readout; measure p-eIF2alpha/ATF4 at protein level; a guanabenz arm needs an alpha-2 control (E15/E16 at abstract depth); a genetic PPP1R15B arm avoids the Sephin1/guanabenz mechanism problem (E18-E21). Overreach or unsupported by any row: (a) that a PPP1R15B knockdown is a suitable potentiation arm for human WWOX-deficient neurons rests on one excitatory-neuron-specific Fmr1 mouse precedent (E12) that is a low-ISR lesion; (b) item 9's "sign tension" ISRIB-vs-CReP in Fmr1 compares a non-seizure ISRIB result (E14) with a seizure result from a different manipulation; it is a legitimate question but not a tension established by data; (c) no row supports adding a seizure endpoint for a human-cell test (network activity in iPSC networks is a different readout from seizures); (d) the design should now also carry the salubrinal split (31557161 vs 24728926) as a precedent line, which is the "one line naming the precedent" BRIEF_W2 asked for. Labelled IPOTESI, so acceptable as hypothesis, but should not read as derived from the evidence rows.
