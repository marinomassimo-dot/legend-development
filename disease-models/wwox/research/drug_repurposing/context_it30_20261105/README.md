# IT30: compound-level effects with their context, without an invented target

Non-canonical. Not medical advice. Public edition, WWOX-DEE reference genotype class.

**What this adds.** In IT29 the signed perturbation table was written by hand and the raw evidence files were not consumed by anything (compound-only rows carry free-text actions, so the target converter signs few of them). `compound_context_it30.py` reads the raw `evidence_it29_<A-E>.tsv` rows directly. A row counts only when it names ONE compound; combinations (`+`, add-on, versus), lists and classes ("five H1 antagonists") are written to `panel_rows_unattributed_it30.tsv` and never split. Per compound and theme it records which contexts gave which direction and flags a sign that flips (`sign_flips_with_context`), a benefit with a null row (`benefit_not_reproduced`) or a benefit with a stated limit (`benefit_with_a_stated_limit`). A flagged compound becomes a conditional question for the scientific queue (`conditional_questions_it30.tsv`). Nothing is attributed to a target, nothing is carried from a compound to its class.

**Result on the committed evidence (174 rows, A to E).** 34 compound-theme entries from single-compound rows (53 of the 119 compound rows) and 66 panel rows left unsplit; 9 conditional questions, 6 compound-level and 3 class-level (below). Compound-level: rapamycin and vigabatrin (`sign_flips_with_context`); bumetanide (`benefit_not_reproduced`: a null row exists); benztropine, clemastine and clonazepam (`benefit_with_a_stated_limit`: benefit in most rows and at least one row whose authors state a limit, but no null or adverse single-compound row, which is not a failure to reproduce). Each question lists, beside its own rows, `related_rows`: rows that mention the compound but were not used (panel rows, other interventions, observations in the reference genotype), as pointers for whoever answers it. It works on both cases: benztropine and clemastine are the myelination case; rapamycin, vigabatrin, bumetanide and clonazepam the developmental-epilepsy case. Only 1 compound (phenobarbital, adverse in three neonatal-exposure rows) is a proposal in the stage tables (S11): the stage is built from transcript chains, the evidence from pharmacology, so the join is thin. That is a finding about the two layers, not a defect of the adapter.

**Case 2 lesion-class table.** `case2_lesion_conditional_it29.tsv` (agent classes, not compounds) now also reaches the queue: the three classes whose sign differs between the two lesion types (sodium-channel blockers, GABA-transaminase inhibitors, Kv7 openers) become class-level questions (`sign_flips_with_lesion_class`, CQ-IT30-06 to 08), each stating that nothing transfers to a named compound. Total 9 conditional questions: 6 compound-level, 3 class-level.

**Limits.** An independent check (IT30 S12) found and led to fixes for: single compounds sent to the panel because of a comma, a leading digit or a role word in their name; a question that called a stated limit a failure to reproduce; and a missing pointer to related rows (for vigabatrin, the observation in two children with WWOX-null lesions, row E42, sits in `related_rows`, not in the flip). Remaining: the name key is text, so the same compound written with a regimen suffix does not merge (OV350 D20 and D21 stay two entries); endpoints are printed with each context but not matched by a controlled vocabulary. Evidence depth is the packages' own (mostly abstract or partial text). A conditional question is a question: it neither qualifies a compound nor ranks it, and the counts are not independent replications (rows of one study share an origin; `n_sources` is printed). The grouping by package letter keeps endpoints apart (discharge in larvae and adult behaviour after neonatal exposure are not pooled).

Regenerate: `python3 compound_context_it30.py run --out results`. Checks: `python3 test_context_it30.py`.
