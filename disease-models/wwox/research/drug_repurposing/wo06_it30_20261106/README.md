# WO-06: residues of CH-IT28-ISR-ROLE (ISR modulation and seizures; the Sephin1 rebuttal; five abstract-only glial papers)

Non-canonical research material, not medical advice. Nothing here is a finding about WWOX; every WWOX-facing statement is a conditional test design. Precedents outside WWOX are not WWOX evidence.

## Decision at stake and what would discriminate (written before the searches)

Decisions that could change: (a) the INV-02 test design, specifically whether a seizure or network-activity endpoint and which ISR arms it should contain; (b) the sign assigned to an ISR lever in the module (it may depend on the node perturbed and on the stress class).
Results that would be discriminating: a mammalian primary study with an ISR perturbation that has a target control and a seizure endpoint (would move the INV-02 arms); an ISR perturbation that helps seizures in one node and harms in another within one model (would confirm node-dependence); a demonstration that the Sephin1/guanabenz mechanism is not reproduced (would remove those compounds as potentiation arms); a full text that reverses the direction its abstract gave (would change the glial rows). Results that would not discriminate: reviews, cell-death endpoints, associations of p-eIF2alpha with seizure tissue.

## Answer (labelled)

1. DATO: no mammalian primary study was found in which ISRIB, a PERK or GCN2 inhibitor, Sephin1 or an eIF2B activator was tested on a seizure or network-activity endpoint; a PERK inhibitor (GSK2656157) was tested in a rat pentylenetetrazole status-epilepticus model with a cell-death endpoint (E03) and ISRIB in a mouse pentylenetetrazole model with a memory endpoint (E07) (bounded search, queries Q01 to Q13). The closest items are not that: salubrinal reduced seizure activity in kainic-acid mice (abstract only), salubrinal and a PERK inhibitor both lowered CHOP and apoptosis after pentylenetetrazole status epilepticus in rats (cell-death endpoint), ISRIB corrected seizure-induced forgetting (seizure was the inducer; abstract only), and the genetic CReP rescue of Fmr1 audiogenic seizures read in IT28.
2. INFERENZA: the pharmacological seizure-endpoint evidence is salubrinal in two mouse kainic-acid studies with opposite signs (PMID 24728926 reduced seizure activity, abstract only; PMID 31557161 earlier stage-4 onset and shorter stage 4 to 5 interval, full text read), and in both the compound lowered ER-stress or eIF2alpha-pathway markers, so its label as a potentiator is not an observed effect and the one pharmacological inhibitor-class signal is on cell death (E03); in the same rat model the two opposite nodes give the same sign (E03). Opposite-direction perturbations giving the same sign, with compound effects and a cell-death endpoint, do not show node-dependence and show that the sign is not set by the direction of the p-eIF2alpha change; the IT28 rule (measure the sign first) stands; the IT28 rule (measure the sign first) stands.
3. DATO: the rebuttal paper (PMID 29618508) shows, with purified PP1, PPP1R15A and G-actin, that none of the holophosphatases tested was inhibited by Sephin1 or guanabenz, at a concentration 100-fold above the one that gives a cellular proteostatic effect (E18, E19); it cites earlier cell work for no effect on the dephosphorylation rate in cells (E20).
4. INFERENZA: it rebuts the mechanism, not the benefit. Guanabenz also acts on alpha-2A receptors, which by themselves reduce hippocampal epileptiform activity in rat slices (E15, E16), and in eIF2B-mutant mice guanabenz helped and Sephin1 did not (E22). Any guanabenz or Sephin1 arm is therefore not an eIF2alpha-P potentiation arm unless p-eIF2alpha is measured, and guanabenz is confounded as an ISR arm on a bursting or network endpoint unless alpha-2 receptor action is controlled (alpha-2A agonism lowers epileptiform burst frequency in rat CA3 slices; abstract-level, from a ligand-potency panel).
5. DATO: the five glial papers were reachable as open PMC page text (publisher XML is blocked; Methods and figures not read). Direction is unchanged for all five relative to their abstracts (E24 to E35).
6. DATO: new at full text. The CMT1B papers hold, in one model, that loss of Gadd34 (raising p-eIF2alpha) rescues, Perk haploinsufficiency (lowering it) also rescues, Perk loss is cell-autonomous in Schwann cells and not in neurons, loss of eIF2alpha phosphorylation worsens disease, and Chop loss rescues while BiP stays induced (E29 to E35).
7. INFERENZA: this is a worked example that kinase, phosphorylation and effector nodes carry different signs in one chronic misfolded-cargo model, which supports the node-dependence clause; it is peripheral glia, not CNS neurons.
8. IPOTESI: INV-02 should keep a network-activity readout, but the potentiation arm should be genetic (PPP1R15B knockdown) or a chemical arm with measured p-eIF2alpha and an alpha-2 antagonist control; discriminating test: guanabenz with and without an alpha-2A antagonist on burst frequency, plus p-eIF2alpha.
9. IPOTESI: the ISRIB result in a model read as low-ISR (E14) versus the Fmr1 genetic rescue by raising p-eIF2alpha (E12) is a sign tension; discriminating test: ISRIB and CReP knockdown in the same Fmr1-null neurons with p-eIF2alpha, ATF4 protein and one functional endpoint.
10. DATO: not established: whether any ISR drug changes seizures in a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy mammal; the WWOX ISR sign; full texts of the three seizure-adjacent abstract-only papers.

## Evidence by theme

Residue 1, seizures. Rows E01 to E17 in `evidence_wo06.tsv`. Mammalian pharmacology: salubrinal in kainic-acid mice (E01, E02, abstract only), salubrinal and GSK2656157 in PTZ status epilepticus rats (E03, E04, partial full text), a CO donor in febrile-seizure rats (E05, E06, abstract only), ISRIB in PTZ-accelerated forgetting (E07, E08, abstract only). Genetics: n-Tr20 loss raises GCN2-dependent p-eIF2alpha and changes seizure susceptibility, but the text read shows Gcn2 deletion tested for transmission and not for seizures (E09 to E11); Fmr1 CReP and PARS2 fly GCN2 from IT28 are carried as prior reads (E12, E13). ISRIB in Fmr1 knockout has no seizure experiment (E14). A 2025 review's seizure passages name salubrinal only (E17).

Residue 2, rebuttal. Rows E18 to E23. The paper is biochemical: PP1 from two sources, PPP1R15A constructs with and without the N-terminal repeat region, low and physiological ionic strength, 16 h reaction. It does not dispute a cellular proteostatic effect of the compounds, only the proposed target.

Residue 3, glial five. Rows E24 to E35. EAE: IFN-gamma benefit lost on Perk+/- (E24, E25) and oligodendrocyte-specific PERK activation protective on axons without an anti-inflammatory effect (E26, E27). CMT1B: E29 to E35.

## Counter-evidence (recorded on purpose)

Against a salubrinal anti-seizure reading: the same abstract reports lowered eIF2alpha-ATF4-CHOP proteins (E02); IT28 recorded salubrinal worsening prion neuronal loss; the rat SE result is cell death (E03, E04). Against any ISR attribution for guanabenz: E15, E16, E21, E22, E23. Against ISRIB as universal: single injection inert in the forgetting paper (E08); IT28 recorded ISRIB reducing viability under chronic ER stress. Against equating rescue with relief of stress: BiP stays induced after Chop ablation (E29). Against one sign for the pathway: E31, E32, E34.

## Transfer conditions and breakers

Conditions: a neuronal system whose ISR sign is measured by protein; the same perturbation arms with matched controls; functional endpoint including network activity. Breakers: acute chemoconvulsant stress class versus a chronic developmental lesion; knockout or haploinsufficiency standing in for inhibitors; compound effects without a target control; peripheral glia standing in for CNS neurons; cell-death endpoints standing in for seizures; any inference to WWOX without a WWOX measurement.
