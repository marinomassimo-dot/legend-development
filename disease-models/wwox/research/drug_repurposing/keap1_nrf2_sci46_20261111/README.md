## Answer first

1. **The non-covalent binder is identified, and the numbering trap is closed.** ChEMBL CHEMBL3819587, the PDB ligand L6I (5FNU) and PubChem CID 118170767 are one stereo-defined structure (InChIKey ZDNGJXBUEQNFBQ-GCJKJVERSA-N). It is the Davies 2016 lead (27031670); Tran 2019 calls it "9" (Figure 1 legend, Experimental name and mass), Wu 2024 "C7, also known as KI-696", Davies's own number in ChEMBL is 7, Callahan 2026 "4". Numbers are per article. The name KI-696 is a ChEMBL curator synonym plus later authors' statements, not a sentence of the Davies abstract (D01, D02, `output/identity.tsv`; verified blind by V8). Limits: Tran's name leaves the propanoic-acid carbon undefined (a diastereomer mixture is compatible); Costa's "Ki 696-1/-2" are enantiomers from a supplier that the paper does not tie to the ChEMBL structure.
2. **The knockdown arm of the source is not retrievable as numbers.** The Davies text is closed access with no PMC copy. In ChEMBL (97 assays, 115 rows) twelve NHBE assays name siRNA, one row each; the NRF2-siRNA rows carry only a categorical label ("Not active"), with no value, no residual and no knockdown efficiency (one numeric row: activity FC 6.0 in the non-targeting arm). The same siRNA design also carries **bardoxolone methyl (covalent, 100 nM)** beside the binder (1 µM): both classes lose three gene or activity readouts under NRF2 siRNA in one cell type, on labels only (D04, D05, `knockdown_arms.tsv` K01–K02, all [CH]). The first undone step is the paper's Supporting Information, which needs a route the operator authorises.
3. **Where the binder's NRF2 dependence is actually shown with a verified loss: primary rat microglia, not neurons.** In Wu 2024, NRF2 shRNA (mRNA about 0.10 of control by figure read, declared >95%) and CRISPR knock-out (about 0.05) removed the binder's induction of HO1, NQO1 and GCLC (about 4–8-fold to about 1) and its protection from irradiation, and KEAP1 knock-out occluded it (D10). Caveats: no direct Kelch binding measurement in that paper (docking and Co-IP), 5 Gy in Results against 10 Gy in Methods, stable-selected cultures, a viability floor under loss (CCK-8 0.20; TUNEL not at a floor), no loss arm in the in vivo experiment (D11). Winkel 2015 (RA839, a second non-covalent binder, **abstract only**): 105 probe sets regulated in macrophages, two in Nrf2 knock-out macrophages, with CDDO-Me and Keap1 silencing as comparators (D20): the model for the specificity readout.
4. **In neurons the same comparison has failed to read, not failed.** LUHMES (Costa 2024): the NRF2 siRNA left 0.68 of nuclear protein (mRNA 0.36, NQO1 mRNA 0.63), the protection of one enantiomer at 40 µM was the same with and without siRNA (69 against 67% of annonacin toxicity, N 7), and the authors could "not definitely exclude" an NRF2-independent mechanism: a maintained effect under a partial loss is uninterpretable, and NQO1 and HMOX1 mRNA were not induced under annonacin while protection persisted (D08, D09). The one human neuronal use with a binder at 1 µM (hiPSC cortical interneurons, KEAP1-overexpressing and already NRF2/HO-1-high) moved ferroptosis markers in the harmful direction, with no loss arm (Zhu 2025, D21). **No source has the binder in primary neurons or astrocytes with an NRF2-loss arm** (perimeter and two routes in `output/queries.tsv`, `verify/queries_verifier.tsv`; D18).
5. **The covalent side is not a clean NRF2 probe.** Dimethyl fumarate: benefit lost in Nrf2-deficient EAE mice (abstract), but monomethyl fumarate moves Hmox1 and cytokine genes through HCAR2 in a microglial line (antibody arm), 24 other proteins are modified in neurons and astrocytes (abstract), and bardoxolone methyl was extremely neurotoxic in LUHMES above nanomolar and was withdrawn (D13–D15, D08). NQO1 alone is not a mechanism readout (progesterone raises it without NRF2; Costa Fig 6d) (P8).

**What is now interpretable.** A pair of one covalent and one non-covalent reagent in **one neural cell class at a time**, each with and without an **NFE2L2 loss shown adequate at the endpoint time**, a **KEAP1-loss occlusion arm**, reagents **matched on on-target response (nuclear NRF2 and two target genes), not on concentration or binding potency**, a readout beyond NQO1 (a panel or array whose **survivors after loss** are the independent action) and viability beside a function endpoint (`outcome_matrix.tsv`, O1–O10, `experiment_ladder.tsv`). Loss of effect under adequate loss means NRF2 dependence of that endpoint, not absolute specificity; a maintained effect under adequate loss means an alternative route; a maintained effect under partial loss means nothing (O5).

**What can be put to the test for WWOX (conditional, IPOTESI; none is a claim):** NH1 (an insufficient response is corrected by Kelch-pocket occupancy, NRF2-dependently), NH2 (a graded ladder with a risk panel, because an already-induced response may gain nothing or lose), NH3 (the survivor set after verified loss separates NRF2-dependent from independent action of the two classes), NH4 (a compartment: astrocytic against neuronal NRF2). `hypotheses.tsv`. The binder is a **probe**, not a candidate: neuronal permeability of an acid and brain exposure are unknown (nanomolar in epithelium, 40 µM with one enantiomer in LUHMES).

**Still to be shown (not answerable by reading):** NRF2 protein, nuclear NRF2 and target-gene activity in a WWOX-deficient neural cell by compartment (INV-08 X0; no source has any); whether the binder reaches and works in a neuron or astrocyte at a tolerated exposure; what adequate loss means in a neuron; whether any of it improves function rather than viability.
