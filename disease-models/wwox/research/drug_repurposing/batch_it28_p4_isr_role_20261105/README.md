# IT28 P4: what does the ISR sign mean for the choice of lever? (challenge CH-IT28-ISR-ROLE)

The WWOX-specific sign is still unmeasured, so this batch decides what to measure and which pattern discriminates, not which compound to use.

## What the literature says (decision rule)
1. "ISR protective or harmful" is not well posed: the sign of a lever depends on the node perturbed and on the stress class (transient external stress; chronic misfolded cargo; a locked eIF2B lesion). In EAE, keeping eIF2-alpha-P up protects oligodendrocytes and PERK loss worsens disease; in some chronic misfolding models PERK loss helps and a deeper brake helps; in vanishing white matter the ISR is locked on and attenuation corrects it.
2. In neurons the rescuing direction is opposite to the measured deviation: low p-eIF2-alpha in excitatory neurons (Fmr1) rescued by restoring it; high eIF2-P (Down syndrome model, PARS2 fly) rescued by attenuation. Seizure phenotypes therefore run in opposite directions depending on the baseline sign.
3. Readouts come apart in both directions (ATF4-target sets up without eIF2-alpha-P; bulk puromycin blots missing per-cell effects; ratios moving because total eIF2-alpha moved; ATF4 targets confounded by mTORC1). An RNA target set is supporting evidence only.
4. Potentiators (guanabenz, Sephin1, salubrinal) have contested mechanisms and non-reproducible effects across laboratories; PERK inhibitors are the least interpretable lever; eIF2B activators are agnostic to the kinase and sensible only if the protein-level ISR is shown high.

## Discriminating experiment (pre-registered patterns in the sub-folder READMEs)
Isogenic WWOX-deficient and corrected hiPSC neurons or oligodendrocyte-lineage cultures by stage. Attenuation rescues and potentiation worsens: damage. The reverse: compensation. Neither changes function: marker. Epistasis arm: does an eIF2B activator cancel the benefit of PPP1R15A loss?
