## 1. The thread and why (two lines, from the evidence)

**Thread.** NEED-WWOX-MISSENSE-PROTEIN-RESCUE, narrowed to one question: *when a class of small molecules raises the abundance of a destabilised missense protein, can the data separate post-translational action (chaperoning, route blockade) from transcriptional action (HDAC inhibition of the cDNA), and what fold of gain would the Q230P-class allele need for that gain to matter?* Carried to a revised card (A01) and a design that decides which lever arms belong in the Q230P fate experiment.

**Why this one.** (i) The only WWOX datum for the lesion is "transcript comparable, protein not detected" (one membrane), so every proteostasis card (A01 to A05) is gated by the same unmeasured branch, and the P5 table says A01's chaperone potency is absent while its HDAC potency is quantitatively available: that asymmetry can be exploited now, with no new WWOX measurement. (ii) The card's central transfer objection ("no precedent is a non-membrane client; WWOX is cytosolic") turned out, on opening the paper, to be wrong for one precedent of the lot (Munc18-1 is a soluble cytosolic protein), so the question had the largest information value among the open threads: splice (C02) and endogenous boost (C03) are abstentions waiting on tools, not on a reading, and A02 to A05 inherit the same branch question.

## 5. Pharmacology (keyless sources, queried 2026-10-28/29, NOT WWOX EVIDENCE)

Sources: compound catalogue (`LEGEND_CATALOG_CACHE` root cache; ChEMBL_37 2026-05-01; DGIdb as served 2024-06-10; GtoPdb 2026.3.1), ChEMBL REST keyless (molecule x human HDAC target pull, one call per second, 30 compound-target pairs, 693 deposited rows), no keyed API. Tables: `hdac_enzyme_potency_chembl_20261029.tsv`, `applied_over_enzyme_ic50_20261029.tsv`.

* **Mechanism of record.** ChEMBL curates sodium phenylbutyrate as a glutamine-sequestering (ammonia-scavenging) agent only; TUDCA as "Unknown"; vorinostat as an HDAC1/2/3/6 inhibitor. GtoPdb holds the phenylbutyrate and TUDCA ligands with **zero** curated interactions. DGIdb gives PBA only HDAC-family rows (Drug Target Commons, TTD), PRKCA, BCL2, CDKN1A and the urea-cycle genes. **No source carries a folding, binding, thermal-shift or aggregation-prevention datum for either compound**: the chaperone mechanism has no potency anywhere; the HDAC mechanism has one.
* **HDAC potency (enzyme level, ChEMBL).** PBA against HDAC1/2/3/6/8: 64, 65, 260, 240 and 93 micromolar (one document; a second document gives 162 micromolar for the sodium salt on HDAC1). Butyrate in the same document: 16, 12, 9 and 15 micromolar on HDAC1/2/3/8 and **above 2 millimolar on HDAC6**. Vorinostat: median 76 nM (HDAC1) and 38 nM (HDAC6) over 77 and 71 deposited rows. So in that one document (CHEMBL1671826) butyrate is about 4 to 29 times more potent than PBA on class I and PBA engages HDAC6 where butyrate does not. **This is document-dependent**: the second document that supplies the 162 micromolar sodium-PBA row (CHEMBL5136998) lists butyrate at 300 and 400 micromolar on HDAC1 and HDAC2, weaker than sodium PBA, while a third (CHEMBL5096206) repeats butyrate's low values without any PBA row. The statement that a class-I inhibitor did not reproduce the mutant gain therefore holds only if butyrate is at least as strong a class-I inhibitor as PBA at the applied level; it is weakened, not withdrawn (butyrate at the applied level is above its IC50 in every document, but the margin over PBA is not established).
* **What the applied levels imply (arithmetic on enzyme IC50, not cellular occupancy).** In the two precedents, PBA was applied at about 8 to 78 times its HDAC IC50 (HDAC1 31 to 78; HDAC6 8 to 21): HDAC engagement is expected at the levels that raised abundance, so abundance gain at those levels cannot be called chaperoning without an HDAC-free comparator. Butyrate at equal level is at or above 7 times (class I; 7 uses the weakest document value) but at most 1 to 2.5 times (HDAC6). Vorinostat at its applied level is about 33 times (HDAC1) and 66 times (HDAC6) its median IC50.
* **What this separates.** For the soluble client: class-I HDAC inhibition (butyrate) did **not** reproduce the mutant gain, on the document-dependent premise above; HDAC-free osmolytes **did**; so the gain is not simply class-I HDAC action. It does **not** exclude an HDAC6 contribution (butyrate does not engage HDAC6, and the study has no pan-HDAC or HDAC6-selective arm), and the PAH enzyme precedent shows an HDAC6 lever can raise a mutant cytosolic enzyme. For the ER client a pan-HDAC arm (which engages HDAC6) was also flat. Net: **three non-exclusive classes** (physical chaperoning; HDAC6/HSP90 acetylation; class-I transcriptional) of which the first and third have one measured contrast each in a non-WWOX client and the second has none for PBA.
* **Catalogue finding.** The search layer for the need returns target-linked rows (HSPA8, HSPA1A/B, ERN1 ...) dominated by unnamed DGIdb rows and by compounds with no folding rationale; chemical chaperones are target-less (trehalose, sorbitol: catalogue targets 0; DGIdb PBA family with 35 target rows is a separate record from the ChEMBL PBA family and parent acid, not merged), so a target-based search cannot surface this class at all and the identity of PBA, its acid and its prodrug is split across at least four records. Noted, not fixed (it changes family counts).

## 6. Quantity, localisation, interaction and function: the arithmetic

`munc18_quantity_function_arithmetic_20261029.tsv`, `required_fold_arithmetic_20261029.tsv` (reader arithmetic on panel estimates, INFERENZA; Figures 3, 6 and Supplementary Figure 12 were opened as images in this act).

1. **Quantity versus function in the soluble precedent.** Mutant total protein sits at 4 to 8 percent of wild type in four of five variants; with the PBA arm the total rises 1.05 to 1.65 fold but the Triton-soluble fraction roughly doubles, so the soluble pool rises 2.1 to 3.7 fold; even so the treated soluble mutant pool is about 2 to 7 percent of the wild-type soluble pool, while the vesicle-recycling read-out is at or above the untreated wild-type range for three of the four low-protein variants (R406H, about 44 against about 55, is not; P335L, with about 65 percent protein and little baseline deficit, is excluded from the count). These pool figures combine panels with different loading normalisers and carry the reader error of about plus or minus 10 percent of axis; values near 1.05 fold are not separable from 1.0. Either the read-out (a pixel-threshold count) saturates far below wild-type protein, or wild-type expression exceeds need; the authors state expression in neurons was close to wild-type level, not overexpression, so saturation is the likelier reading but neither is shown. Consequence: **quantity does not map to function in either direction**, and a total-protein blot alone would have under-called this gain in the soluble pool.
2. **Turnover does not explain the deficit.** First-order rates from the chase (WT about 45 percent left at 24 h; mutants about 10 to 30) differ by about 1.5 to 3 fold and the photoconversion data by about 1.2 to 2 fold, while the steady-state deficit is about 12 to 25 fold for four of five. The remainder is synthesis-side or early, unresolved loss (the repository's M1' branch), not an insoluble pellet (totals are boiled lysates). For a Q230P-class allele with normal transcript this keeps the "never made" branch alive even where a turnover difference is measured.
3. **Required fold.** Parity needs 1/R fold, where R is (amount relative to wild type) x (function per molecule relative to wild type). If R is 0.5, a 2-fold gain restores parity; at 0.1 about 10-fold; at 0.01 about 100-fold. Observed gains in the precedents are 1.05 to 9 fold and the **same gain applies to wild type**. R is unmeasured for every WWOX allele, and for Q230P the only bound is "below the unquantified detection floor". So no abundance gain from this class can be called rescue until R is bracketed. This is the relation between residual function and expression increase asked in the mandate: a boost multiplies R, it does not change it, so a boost can only help an allele that already has R large enough, which is exactly the unmeasured quantity (and the reason NEED-WWOX-ENDOGENOUS-BOOST sits behind this one).
4. **Localisation and interaction.** The P252A paper's HSC70 co-IP (P252A only) and lysosomal co-localisation are the only WWOX-variant chaperone-contact data; the interactome supplement that would show whether HSC70 binds wild type is presence-only and does not list HSPA8. A destabilised protein mislocalises by being degraded, so localisation data cannot separate branches; interaction data (P282A) show function loss without destabilisation, which is why a function-per-molecule readout cannot be replaced by stability.

## 7. Counter-evidence sought (and kept)

* GAT-1 G443D: not rescued; the partly trafficked branch is outside the reach of the class (the claim is from PMID 40485335, not 42157447), but glycerol, an osmolyte-type chemical chaperone, did rescue G443D in vitro. Null zebrafish: no rescue (protein not measured). Wild type is raised in every target. Butyrate raised total immature protein and worsened an EEG count in one knock-in line (an HDAC-class liability that a combined PBA/HDAC action would inherit). The PBA arm leaves treated variants at about 0.7 to 1.1 of untreated wild type in GAT-1 by the repository's own arithmetic.
* Munc18-1: PBA on P480L raised total protein only about 5 percent; worm P479L rescue was mixed; the stabilised pool is dominant-negative by design, which is not a WWOX property (WWOX loss is recessive).
* PAH: the HDAC6 lever carried a toxicity signal at a higher level, two sham animals, and the authors' "inhibits degradation" has no turnover measurement.
* WWOX: P252A (faster turnover) and P282A (function loss, no stability defect) show stability without function for P282A (stable but non-functional); no variant in the paper is unstable but functional, so the opposite direction is not shown; the in-silico stability ranking is inverted against the measurements; MG-132 did not accumulate even wild-type WWOX; the transgene-promoter confound (CMV, with a documented HDAC-inhibitor derepression precedent at abstract depth) applies to every overexpression test of the class, including the Munc18 HEK experiments (the butyrate null on the mutant argues against a dominant promoter effect there but does not remove it for PBA).
* Not found: any experiment that gave PBA, TUDCA, an osmolyte or an HDAC6 inhibitor to a WWOX system. One PubMed query each for an enzyme-class PBA precedent and for a PBA-specific CMV effect returned nothing (statements about those queries).

## 8. Discriminating next experiment (design only; nothing was run)

**Name.** The Q230P fate experiment with a three-class lever panel and a wild-type ruler. It extends, and does not replace, the repository's direct-discriminator design (dox-titrated single-copy landing pad, S/P/T fractions, inhibitor arm, chase from a detectable baseline).

**System.** One isogenic WWOX-null human line (neural-lineage if available, otherwise a non-neural line with the limit declared) carrying, at one genomic landing site and under one inducible promoter chosen to be insensitive to HDAC-inhibitor derepression (verified by a reporter control): wild type, Q230P, an empty site, and a catalytically dead SDR control if one can be defined. Transgene mRNA measured in the same wells as protein, in every arm, under treatment.

**Arms (each against vehicle).** (a) PBA; (b) a class-I HDAC inhibitor with no HDAC6 activity (butyrate); (c) a pan-HDAC inhibitor engaging HDAC6; (d) a selective HDAC6 inhibitor; (e) a bile acid with no HDAC activity; (f) two osmolytes (osmotic-matched control for the high concentrations); (g) a proteasome inhibitor and a lysosomal-pH agent as route arms (A02 logic). Concentrations are set by a prior toxicity titration; none is fixed here.

**Readouts, in separate columns.** Spin-free total, soluble and pellet fractions (S/P/T) with fraction-specific loading controls and an orthogonal detection (a tag or targeted mass spectrometry) so epitope invisibility is separated from absence; transgene mRNA per well; chase from a detectable baseline with a route arm; **a wild-type titration curve** (several induction levels) read with the same function readout, which is the ruler that converts a fold gain into function; the function readout itself (see below). Replication unit: independent induction-and-treatment wells from separate passages, stated per panel.

**Function readout.** No validated per-molecule SDR assay exists. Step 0 is to define one that satisfies the repository's four criteria (measurable, specific to the SDR function, causally relevant, surviving a destabilising missense): activity normalised to soluble amount in the same sample, with null, wild-type and dead-control arms, and a recombinant arm (thermal-shift and cofactor binding, wild type against Q230P, with and without each compound) that tests physical stabilisation with no transcription and no cell. Until Step 0 exists the experiment can answer L0 and L1 only, and says so.

**Predictions registered before the run, both directions.** P-A: mutant protein is detected in some fraction in the vehicle arm. If **no** fraction and no orthogonal signal in any arm, the lesion is upstream of degradation (synthesis or co-translational): the PBA, proteasome and HSP70 classes are not addressed for this allele and A01, A02 and A05 close for it. P-B: if abundance rises only in the soluble fraction, tracks mRNA, and equals the class-I HDAC arm, the class is a transcriptional boost; its relevance moves to NEED-WWOX-ENDOGENOUS-BOOST and the chaperone attribution is retired. P-C: if the rise is not reproduced by (b), is reproduced by (e) or (f), and the mRNA is flat, a physical-chaperone-like action is supported in this system (L0 only). P-D: if the rise reproduces under (c) or (d) but not (b), an HDAC6/HSP90 route is implicated. P-E: if the rise is in the pellet, or the wild-type ruler shows function saturating below the gain, the gain is "stable but inert" and the card moves against the class. No threshold is invented here; fixed fold cut-offs are written at registration from the wild-type ruler.

**What it cannot show.** Anything about a neuron in vivo, exposure at the brain, an organism endpoint, or a clinical effect; and not P252A or any other allele.
