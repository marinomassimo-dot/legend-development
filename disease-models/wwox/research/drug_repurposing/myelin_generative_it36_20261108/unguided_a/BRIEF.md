## Observations (all measured; nothing here says what the cause is)

System A, mouse, neuron-targeted deletion of the gene WWOX (Synapsin-promoter Cre; recombination is partial/mosaic; activity in some glia is not excluded):
- A1. At postnatal day 17 in the corpus callosum: fewer CC1-positive (mature oligodendrocyte) cells and more PDGFRA-positive (precursor) cells (3 vs 3 mice, three sections each).
- A2. Electron microscopy, 3 vs 3 mice: fewer myelinated axons in corpus callosum; more unmyelinated axons in optic nerve; higher g-ratio.
- A3. Callosal slice recording: a compound response (N1) with longer latency in the mutant (8 vs 8 slices, from 5 vs 6 mice; slices are not independent units); the cause of the longer latency (myelin, node, axon calibre, excitability) is not separated.
- A4. In vivo, neocortical bursting; seizures from about postnatal day 9; the animals die at about 3 to 4 weeks. Recording ages for the myelin readouts overlap with the seizures; activity before day 9 is unknown.
System B, mouse, deletion of the same gene driven by an oligodendrocyte-lineage Cre, or by a GFAP-driven Cre: no overt phenotype; MBP staining at day 17 not obviously changed (qualitative, one panel set, no counts, validation of deletion shown in one field each). No electron microscopy, nodal or conduction measure.
System C, co-culture: embryonic mouse dorsal-root-ganglion neurons from null embryos (before any seizure, peripheral sensory) plus wild-type oligodendrocyte precursors added at day 15: after 9 to 11 days fewer myelinating-class (MBP-positive, sheath-like) cells and more premyelinating-class cells on null neurons (two experiments, 4 vs 4). No conditioned-medium, transwell, activity or factor-addition arm was found in the text.
System D, human stem-cell-derived oligocortical spheroids with the gene knocked out in all lineages: hypomyelination at late stages (weeks 30 to 37); the maturation medium after day 71 contains no thyroid hormone.

Functional objective: understand what could restore normal myelin formation and conduction in the neuron-targeted model, without presupposing a drug, a target or a pathway.
