# Loop 2 (broad recovery): restoration after onset, residual function, dependence on lesion

Scientist-b, task `ORCH-ROADMAP-IT8-20261029`, package P5, 2026-10-29. Non-canonical research material. **Not medical advice.** Reference genotype class only. Association is not modulation and modulation is not rescue. Experiments are **PROPOSED, never run**. None of the leads below is WWOX evidence.

## 0. Decision in one paragraph

Of the three registered leads, **two contain a real post-onset restoration and one does not**. Shank3 (PMID 34253921) restores the *same gene* in adults after lifelong deficiency, with within-animal pre/post tracking; Meis2 (PMID 42587157) restores circuit and cognitive endpoints in adult Cntnap2-null mice by over-expressing a *different* gene (a bypass, not replacement; it also changes wild-type cells), and its seizure result is a suppression after treatment before the stated typical onset age (reversal and prevention not separable); Csnk2b (PMID 42190665) treats at P3 only and its authors say efficacy after symptoms "remains to be investigated". Against what WWOX shows (every mouse gene-addition endpoint prevention or early window; one organoid post-onset functional add-back at week 10 to 16 that did not correct radial-glia abnormalities), the leads support a **hypothesis of endpoint-class dependence** (circuit-level function recoverable after onset, prenatal progenitor composition not) that WWOX can test cheaply in organoids and in a slower-onset allele; they do not show that WWOX restoration works after onset. **Transfer is UNVERIFIED** for all three, and the leads differ from WWOX in lesion (haploinsufficiency or viable null versus a null lethal by about P15, or a missense), in mechanism (bypass versus replacement) and in endpoint.

## 1. Observation (depth and locators)

Receipts staged: `FTR-20261029-34253921-01`, `FTR-20261029-42587157-01`, `FTR-20261029-42190665-01` (all `partial_fulltext_read`, closed vocabulary, understated). Table `loop2_recovery.tsv` has one row per study-endpoint with the window state in the module's own vocabulary (`prevented_early_window`, `recovered_post_onset_model`, `symptom_suppression_reversible`, `unmeasured`).

* **Shank3, Nat Neurosci 2021.** A floxed/CreER switch makes mice functionally Shank3 heterozygous knock-outs until tamoxifen (or locally endoxifen) restores expression; adult males aged 4 months or more; 77 animals. Sociability is absent before restoration (paired t, P 0.85) and present at week 5 or later (P 0.0007); controls without CreER or with vehicle show no change. The proportion of mPFC neurons encoding the other animal rises from 9.2 to 35.5 percent and self-encoding falls from 38.2 to 19.5 percent (chi-square on pooled neurons, **the neuron is the unit**; the behavioural paired t tests have 52 to 58 degrees of freedom, i.e. pooled sessions or trials, not 77 animals). Encoding change precedes behaviour by 5 to 6 days (global) and by 0 days (local mPFC restoration; the difference in lag is not significant, P > 0.4). **Within-animal** tracking is by design (alternate-day sessions for about two months); heterozygous, males only, one circuit. Disrupted state is lifelong, so this is genuine *restoration after a lifelong deficit* for a circuit/behaviour endpoint, and says nothing about seizures, survival or structure.
* **Meis2, Nature 2026.** Cntnap2-null mice (viable); AAV given at 2 months. Circuit and behaviour (Figs 3-4, Extended Data 5-9): deficits are shown in untreated nulls of the same age (fewer PV-interneuron perisomatic synapses, higher EPSC, lower IPSC, reduced PV excitability, no object-location or social memory, lower ripple rate), and Meis2 in PV interneurons moves them toward wild type two weeks later; comparator is control-AAV null and wild type, **not a pre-treatment baseline in the same animal**. **Seizures** (Fig 5): ECoG recorded 4 to 5 months after injection (so at about 6 to 7 months); the Results text states that this model can develop seizures after 6 months of age (citing earlier papers); 10 of 14 control-AAV nulls had seizures against 3 of 12 treated (71 versus 25 percent; P 0.018 chi-square; seizures per day P 0.026 Mann-Whitney; one control animal accounts for about 21 per day, outlier excluded in Extended Data 10: effect persists). The paper's own wording is that seizures were "suppressed"; the model is said to develop seizures after about 6 months and no ECoG was recorded before treatment, so in each animal the order of onset and treatment is unknown: the result is **treated before the stated typical onset age; reversal and prevention are not separable**, and the module label `prevented_early_window` is deliberately not used for this adult injection. One of 6 wild-type mice given Meis2 had two seizures (control-virus wild type 0 of 3). **Bypass and non-specificity**: Meis2 also lowers sEPSC and shifts excitability in wild-type mice; a depolarised PV threshold persists ("Meis2-independent compensatory mechanism"), i.e. residual deficit remains. Cells (several per mouse) are the unit in the electrophysiology (KS tests), mice in behaviour and seizures. One lesion, one laboratory group. The paper's title says "neurodevelopmental disorders"; **one** risk model was tested.
* **Csnk2b, Cell Rep Med 2026.** Haploinsufficient mice (50 percent protein); AAV-PHP.eB at P3; adults tested. Treated: survival 50 to 80 percent at day 150, cortical thickness, social novelty, Barnes day 12, seizures (control 7 of 10 with seizures, treated 1 of 10 and 2 of 10). **Limitations section**: treatment at P3 simulates early treatment; "therapeutic efficacy for individuals with already apparent symptoms remains to be investigated"; the authors state they cannot separate recovery from survival or identity. No post-onset arm in the full text read (Results partly read, not exhaustively: UNVERIFIED for supplementary data). Status: `prevented_early_window` (treatment at P3). Seizure incidence in the CAG arm is 2 of 10 against 7 of 10 (P = 0.07, not significant; the hSyn arm P = 0.02); seizure frequency and duration differ in both arms (P 0.002 to 0.006).

## 2. Inference

1. A restored phenotype after onset has been reported for **circuit and behavioural endpoints in two non-WWOX models**; it has not been reported for survival, structure or seizures after onset.
2. The two post-onset results have a feature in common that WWOX does not share: the lesion is compatible with adult life (haploinsufficiency, viable null). A complete WWOX null cannot be treated in adulthood, so the question has to move to a slower-onset allele, to organoids or to an inducible switch.
3. Replacement and bypass are different claims. Meis2 shows that a downstream program can be re-instated while the lost protein stays absent; it also acts on wild-type cells, so recovery is not lesion-specific repair.
4. Residual function and lesion dependence are untested in all three (each used one allele). For WWOX the only within-source contrast is a patient-organoid difference between a severe and a milder genotype class (layer markers and week-16 firing; confounded by variant, individual and batch: CH7 of the context matrix).

## 3. Hypotheses (non-ledger)

**R1** (endpoint class): circuit-level function (hyperexcitability, network firing) remains modifiable by WWOX restoration after onset; prenatal progenitor composition does not. Only the first half is supported by the leads (Shank3 and Meis2 measured no progenitor endpoint); the second half rests on the WWOX organoid arm alone. Support in WWOX: one organoid arm (function recovered, radial glia not). Falsifier: post-onset add-back leaves firing unchanged versus vector control, or normalises radial-glia measures. **R2** (lesion dependence): effect of add-back scales inversely with residual WWOX function (severe class gains more in a function readout than a milder class); falsifier: isogenic lines varying only variant class show no scaling. **R3** (bypass): experience-dependent PV-interneuron plasticity is impaired in a viable WWOX-deficient neural system and re-instating a PV plasticity gene restores it; falsifier: SYT2/PV synapse induction by social experience is intact in the WWOX-deficient adult (then the lead drops for WWOX). WWOX PV/inhibitory markers are reduced in the neuron-targeted deletion (need `NEED-INHIBITORY-HYPOFUNCTION`); plasticity itself is unmeasured.

|---|---|---|
| Patient or isogenic organoids | Record the same organoids at week 7 or 8 (calcium imaging, firing), randomise to AAV9-WWOX or an AAV9 vector control (EGFP) at week 10, re-record at weeks 12, 16 and 24; severe and milder variant classes side by side | the missing **same-organoid baseline**, **vector control** (the present single-cell contrast is against non-treated organoids) and **persistence**; tests R1 and R2 |

| Switchable allele (conditional restoration, Shank3 design) | CreER-controlled restoration with CreER-minus and vehicle controls, tracking in the same animals | reversibility and the lag between a circuit readout and behaviour; allele existence not known: UNVERIFIED |
| Bypass probe (R3) | SYT2/PV synapse induction after social experience in the slower-onset allele, then a PV-targeted plasticity gene versus control | whether the Meis2 route is even applicable |

Discriminating criteria (fixed before data): R1 supported if firing after post-onset add-back falls into the wild-type range versus vector control while a radial-glia measure does not move; refuted if either fails the other way. Alternative readouts: field potentials or multi-electrode arrays besides calcium imaging; layer-marker immunostaining besides transcripts. Analysis plan: the unit is the organoid batch (independent differentiation) or the animal, never cells; "wild-type range" means inside a pre-set equivalence margin and the sample size are fixed before the run from pilot variance (no number is set here); a partial normalisation (between the untreated and wild-type ranges) is scored as such, not as support or refutation. Controls: vector control, wild-type and corrected-isogenic arms, a no-treatment time-matched arm (to separate maturation from treatment), blinded analysis; unit of replication stated per figure (organoid batch, animal), never cells pooled.
