## Answer first

**The figure can be quantified, but only as group curves, never per animal.** Fig 7b (ethosuximide) and Fig 7d (lithium) plot, for each genotype, the mean Racine stage at each minute of 60 with an upward SEM bar (legend: "means ± SEM"); no per-animal point exists in the figure. The lithium panels are vector paths in the article PDF (exact coordinates). The ethosuximide panels exist only as a raster (the PDF's embedded 1785 x 1424 image and the publisher's 1946 x 1627 PNG) and were measured on pixels, calibrated on the axis ticks, from both files.

1. **Ethosuximide late-phase effect against the figure's own variability (DATO from the extraction; INFERENZA for the bounds).**
   * In the null mice (PTZ N=6, PTZ+ETS N=6) the late-phase gap, minutes 30 to 60, is **1.08 stage** (PTZ 1.87, PTZ+ETS 0.80, 58% of the PTZ level); PTZ is above PTZ+ETS in 30 of 30 minutes. The gap is **2.3 times** the standard deviation of the PTZ curve across those minutes (0.47) and 1.7 times the mean per-minute SEM of the PTZ arm (0.63; the PTZ+ETS arm's is 0.29). In that sense it exceeds the scatter that the figure itself shows.
   * It is **not** constant across the hour: minutes 1 to 10 gap +0.76 (41%); **minutes 10 to 30 gap -0.34, the treated arm HIGHER than PTZ (PTZ above in 3 of 16 minutes)**. "Late phase only" is therefore a measured feature: the gap appears after minute 30 and reverses in the middle of the hour. Whole hour: +0.62 (37%).
   * **Whether the late-phase gap exceeds the uncertainty of a group mean cannot be decided from means and SEMs.** The standard error of a window-average gap lies between 0.14 (if the 30 minutes were independent) and 0.72 (if they were perfectly correlated within animal, an upper limit): t between 7.9 and 1.5, against a critical t of 2.23 (df 10). The curve's own scatter (0.47) is smaller than the per-minute SEM (0.63), which points to between-animal differences that persist across minutes and so to the upper limit being the closer one, but that is an inference, not a measurement. The paper marks PTZ against PTZ+ETS in the nulls with *** (one-way ANOVA, input not stated); the figure alone neither confirms nor refutes it.
   * **Wild-type and heterozygous panels (marked n.s. by the authors):** late-phase gap +0.005 (+/-, PTZ 0.53, N 18 v 12) and -0.17 (+/+, PTZ 0.25, N 20 v 16, the treated arm higher). Caveat: in 24 to 25 minutes of +/+ and 25 to 34 of +/- (the two files differ) the red PTZ marker is hidden under the green one, so the visible minutes are those where the two arms differ (this overstates |gap|; the true gap is closer to 0). **A null-specific effect is not shown**: the nulls' late-phase PTZ level is 3.5 to 7 times higher, so a proportional 58% reduction would give about 0.15 (+/+) and 0.31 (+/-) in late phase against 0.25 and 0.53 PTZ levels, which the standard errors (0.05 to 0.28) do not exclude under the upper limit. The paper has no drug-by-genotype test and the figure cannot supply one.
2. **Lithium: does the effect differ between genotypes, or is it the same in wild type? (DATO, exact values.)** It is present in all three genotypes, with the same sign in at least 72% of the minutes in each (+/+ 86 of 119, +/- 99 of 119, -/- 116 of 119); the authors print **** beside PTZ against PTZ+LiCl in each panel (read in +/+ and +/- on a 500 dpi render of the vector page, and in -/- on the publisher PNG).

| genotype (PTZ N v LiCl N) | PTZ mean, minutes 1-60 | PTZ+LiCl | gap (share of PTZ) | late phase 30-60 gap (share) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| +/+ (12 v 8) | 0.27 | 0.12 | 0.14 (54%) | 0.14 (64%) |
| +/- (12 v 12) | 0.42 | 0.16 | 0.26 (62%) | 0.28 (73%) |
| -/- (6 v 7) | 1.20 | 0.22 | 0.99 (82%) | 0.84 (95%) |

   The absolute gap grows with the PTZ level (0.14, 0.26, 0.99) and the proportional gap is larger in the nulls (82% v 54%). Whether that proportional difference exceeds noise is not decidable from the figure (t of the window-average gap lies between 1.7 and 17 for the nulls and between 0.8 and 7.9 for +/+, bounds as above). What the figure does show without a test is that lithium suppressed the PTZ stage **in wild type too**, which is the card flag `nonspecific_also_in_wild_type`, now with magnitude: **a flag confirmed, not a new finding.**
3. **What this changes in the cards (a proposal; the integrator decides, no ranking, card or frozen package was edited).** (i) `graphical_reading` on the ethosuximide card can be replaced by an extracted reading with stated error: the two raster files agree to a mean absolute difference of 0.004 to 0.025 stage, control series read 0.000 to -0.016, and the null series sit on their k/N lattices (median 0.06 to 0.10 of a lattice step). It remains a reading of means and SEMs, not primary per-animal data. (ii) The design flags `one_experiment` and `no_drug_by_genotype_test` are unchanged by the extraction; `late_phase_only` is now measured (gap after minute 30, reversed in minutes 10 to 30); `control_variability_comparable_to_effect` is answered above in two ways, scatter (exceeded) and group-mean uncertainty (undecidable). (iii) The lithium flags are confirmed. **Neither unit's evidence group could rise or must fall under the cards' own criteria on this extraction; the NX1 step (a drug-by-genotype test with a same-day control) stays the one decisive step.**

## Checks that were run (validation.tsv; all are numbers)

* **Fig 7d vector data on their lattices.** Stage scores are integers, so a mean over N animals lies on k/N. All 726 plotted means lie within 0.024 of a lattice step for the N printed in the legend (121 points per series). This independently confirms the axis calibration and the printed Ns.
* **Fig 7b controls (stage 0 in the design).** Raster mean 0.000 to -0.016 stage in all three panels, two files.
* **Two raster files of the same figure.** Mean absolute difference 0.004 to 0.025 stage per series, maximum 0.049. This bounds the extraction, not the figure.
* **The harder case, measured honestly.** The same raster procedure on the publisher PNG's 7d panels (0.5-minute spacing, markers overlapping) against the exact vector values: mean error -0.147 to -0.004 stage per series (low), mean absolute error 0.009 to 0.171, single points up to 1.07 where markers overlap. Fig 7b has one-minute spacing and no such overlap, but this is a bound on what the method can do, not a calibration of 7b; the null series of 7b are protected by the lattice check, the +/+ and +/- series (lattice step 0.05 to 0.08 stage, about 2 to 4 px) are not.
* **Hidden bars.** An upward bar shorter than the marker's half height (about 0.11 stage) is hidden under the marker in the raster; for the uncertainty bounds such bars take 0.11 (upper) and 0.055 (other).

## ADV-061 addendum (2026-11-18): a bracket for the SD of the brief's per-animal summary S, from the same curves
Non-canonical; in silico; zero spend; hypothesis level; no dose; reference genotype class only; not medical advice. Script `bracket_sd_fig7.py` (offline, `-I`; inputs: the outputs of this folder, read only), outputs `output/sd_bracket_fig7.tsv` (120 rows) and `output/sd_bracket_fig7_run.txt`; script sha256 `47e9b8a6abcd6d5c...`, table sha256 `b1c2a8bf33dcf303...`. **Vector check first:** the publisher PDF route (BMC counter PDF) returned a redirect to a cookies page and was not retried; the held PDF has Fig. 7d as vector and Fig. 7b as a raster, as established on 2026-11-15, so Fig. 7d (exact) is used for the wild-type and heterozygote groups and both panels for the knockouts.
**Method.** S = mean stage over minutes 1 to 60 per animal. Per-minute SD = upward SEM times the square root of N (N from the legends: 7b +/+ 20 and 16, +/- 18 and 12, -/- 6 and 6; 7d +/+ 12 and 8, +/- 12 and 12, -/- 6 and 7; **N is assumed constant over the hour: the legends give one N per arm and the figure does not say whether animals that die keep stage 6 or leave the mean; the direction of the resulting bias is not determined (see the dated correction below)**). Bounds on SD of S: minutes independent (lower) and minutes fully correlated within animal (upper, rigorous for the sum of per-minute SDs); intermediate within-animal correlation rho shown (0.3, 0.6, 0.9). Hidden bars (an upward bar shorter than the marker half height) take 0.055 (lower) or 0.11 (upper); raster minutes with no usable body are omitted (so the 7b wild-type and heterozygote groups, with 27 to 37 usable minutes of 60 and a bias toward minutes where the arms differ, are **not** used; 7b knockouts: 53 minutes). The SD on the log scale is **not boundable strictly** (the per-animal distribution is unknown and S = 0 occurs): it is given under a lognormal approximation from the coefficient of variation, sigma_log = sqrt(ln(1 + CV^2)), and is unreliable where CV exceeds 1. The animals-per-arm figure is for a ratio of 0.7 (ln = -0.357), two-sided 0.05, power 0.80 (the brief's z of 2.802).
**PTZ-alone groups (vehicle-equivalent), rho = 0.6 and 1.0 (rho = 0 gives SD of S 0.12 to 0.28 times the mean, a CV of 0.12 to 0.28 and 2 to 10 animals per arm: not credible, because animals differ persistently across minutes):**
| Panel, genotype (N) | mean S | CV at rho 0.6 / 1.0 | sigma_log | animals per arm for ratio 0.7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7b -/- (6) | 1.67 | 0.58 / 0.75 | 0.54 / 0.67 | 36 / 55 |
| 7d -/- (6) | 1.09 | 0.92 / 1.19 | 0.79 / 0.94 | 77 / 109 |
| 7d +/- (12) | 0.41 | 1.45 / 1.87 | 1.07 / 1.22 | 140 / 185 |
| 7d +/+ (12) | 0.27 | 1.63 / 2.09 | 1.14 / 1.30 | 160 / 207 |
The lithium-treated and ethosuximide-treated arms have larger CVs at lower means (for example 7d +/+ with lithium: mean 0.11, CV 2.7 to 3.4). Full table: `output/sd_bracket_fig7.tsv`.
**What it says.** (1) The brief's "log SD 0.6 to 0.8" for the knockout sits inside the bracket: it corresponds to rho of about 0.3 to 0.9 on these curves; the rigorous upper bound is 0.67 (7b) to 0.94 (7d). (2) **The brief's "SD 0.3" corresponds to a within-animal correlation of about 0.05 to 0.15 on the knockout curves, which animals that differ persistently across minutes do not have: it is not a credible case.** (3) **Wild-type and heterozygote pups at the figure's stimulus are at the floor (mean S 0.27 to 0.41, CV above 1.4): a ratio of 0.7 would need about 140 to 210 per arm**, which is why the brief requires a stimulus that gives them a non-floor response. (4) **An error in the brief's sizing is corrected here:** an interaction contrast of size equal to the simple effect has variance 4 sigma^2 / n and needs **twice**, not four times, the simple-effect n per cell (n = 4 (z sigma / delta)^2); with unequal SDs n = (z / delta)^2 x 2 (sigma_KO^2 + sigma_WT^2). With the bracket (knockout sigma_log 0.54 to 0.94, wild type 1.07 to 1.3) that is about **180 to 320 animals per cell**, 4 cells, **about 700 to 1,300 animals and 350 to 630 knockouts (200 to 360 litters at 1.75 knockouts per litter) for one drug against vehicle**. At the equal-SD optimistic value 0.3 it is 22 per cell, 88 animals, 44 knockouts, 25 litters.
**Limits.** Means and upward SEM bars only; no per-animal values; N constant assumed; a lognormal approximation; raster minutes omitted for 7b wild type; two experiments (7b, 7d) with different cohorts; a stage-time area is one possible summary (peak stage, time to first generalised seizure and incidence are other summaries whose variability this does not bound).
 **Independent agreement (2026-11-18, IT, ADV-063, relayed by ADV; IT read the PTZ-alone knockout curves of 7b and 7d before opening this table; its report was not re-read here):** per-minute means within 0.05 (7b) and 0.15 (7d) of mine; per-minute SD (SEM times the square root of N) 1.15 against my 1.32 (7b) and 1.53 against my 1.26 (7d), opposite signs, no systematic bias, the vector-exact 7d the better value; SD of the summed stage bracket (independent to perfectly correlated) IT 9.5 to 62 (7b) and 10.9 to 69 (7d) against mine 11.4 to 71.5 and 9.2 to 56.9: within about 20 percent; IT concludes the sizing flag stands (a CV of S of 0.5 to 1 is realistic, 0.3 only if the minutes were near independent, which the figure's own scatter argues against).

**Dated correction (2026-11-18, SCI, ADV-064 meeting follow-up):** the sentence above first said that where N changes the SD is "under-stated". That direction was not established: two effects act in opposite ways. (a) If fewer than the legend N animals contribute to a minute, SD = SEM times the square root of the true n is smaller than the value used, so the bracket **overstates** the per-minute SD (by the square root of N over n). (b) If animals that die leave the mean, the curves describe survivors, and a full-cohort per-animal summary that scores a dead animal at stage 6 (as the in vivo brief does) has a **larger** SD than the survivor curves show, so the bracket then understates it. The net direction needs the authors' per-animal data and is left open; the sizing conclusions are not changed by it (the bracket is wide; a bias of a few tens of percent in the SD moves the animals-per-arm figures by tens of percent, not by an order of magnitude).
