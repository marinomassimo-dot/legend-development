# Ethosuximide in Wwox-null mice: do lower behavioral scores imply fewer severe seizure minutes?

**Decision.** In PTZ-challenged Wwox-null mice, the published group curves support a lower mean behavioral score late after ethosuximide. They give conditional evidence of fewer minutes at a clonic-or-worse stage. That second conclusion loses its direction when graphical uncertainty is widened, and the number of animals benefiting remains unidentified. This direct drug experiment supports studying an acute response in this model. It did not measure WNT engagement, neuronal yield or developmental recovery. [Primary study, Fig. 7b and scoring methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6990504/) · [Prior laboratory extraction](../fig7_quant_ethosuximide_lithium_sci_20261115/README.md)

## New comparison

Two arms of six Wwox-null mice received PTZ with or without ethosuximide pretreatment. The study distinguishes behavioral arrest and head movements (stages 1–2), clonus (3–4), generalized tonic–clonic seizure (5) and death (6). A group mean can fall through many small changes or a smaller number of severe episodes. We enumerated all **924** unordered six-animal score histograms at each minute, retained those compatible with the plotted mean and its standard error, and bounded the share of observations at or above stage 3. This avoids assigning identities to mice or assuming a within-mouse correlation. Repeating an animal across minutes gives a descriptive time fraction. It cannot provide 180 independent observations or an animal-level confidence interval.

Under the primary fixed-denominator model the clonic-or-worse difference is between **20 and 93 mouse-minutes out of 180 per arm**. This comparison combines seizure stages and death as prespecified; in the wider sensitivity the lower bound is zero for stage ≥5 and negative for stages 3–5. The primary clonic-or-worse intervals also cross zero for both earlier windows (minutes 1–10, −1.7 to +55.0 pp; minutes 11–30, −32.5 to +23.3 pp). Of 60 late arm-minute observations, 59 allowed at least one joint histogram at the primary tolerance and one was entirely unreadable. The unreadable observation received the full range. The ranges are feasible bounds conditional on the assumptions, not statistical precision intervals. **Independent review, 12:37 UTC.** The other laboratory chat returned an ACK after independently enumerating the 924 histograms. It reproduced 29–93 clonic-or-worse mouse-minutes under PTZ and 0–9 after ethosuximide at primary tolerance. The only entirely unreadable observation was PTZ at minute 32. The reviewer also introduced a biological constraint: if dead animals remain in the denominator, stage 6 is irreversible. Our separate dynamic program allows the number dead to stay constant or increase while all living stages remain unconstrained. With this condition, the late bounds become **+12.8 to +51.7 pp** under primary tolerance and **0 to +55.0 pp** under wider tolerance. Applying the constraint over the full hour gives the same bounds. Thus under the wider reading allowance a tie remains possible even in the persistent-death model. Reproduce with `python3 irreversible_stage.py`. [Trajectory-compatible bounds](results/irreversible_stage.tsv)

**Scope.** The enumeration assumes one integer stage per animal and time point; time averaging scores within a mouse could invalidate it. Fixed denominators assume that deaths are scored as 6 or that everyone remains in the sample. The variable-denominator sensitivity has a different estimand and allows missingness without explaining it. Neither approach can distinguish prevention from a delay in onset, show a drug-by-genotype interaction, or attribute the change to WNT rather than conventional seizure suppression. The evidence that could resolve the severe-stage endpoint is the original animal-by-minute scoring sheet with denominators and disposition; video/EEG synchronized to each animal would help distinguish behavioral suppression from electrographic benefit.

**Human endpoint context.** Piard et al. list absence seizures among several WOREE semiologies and do not quantify their share of recorded ictal burden. The mouse result cannot establish the proportion of patients or events that an absence-specific comparison would address. [Piard et al., Discussion, manuscript p. 7](https://pure.eur.nl/ws/files/48194446/RePub-111699-OA.pdf)

## Reproduction

From this directory run `python3 bound_stages.py`, followed by `python3 graphic_sensitivity.py`. The inputs are the lab's existing public figure extractions, anchored to SHA-256 digests of the source artwork. Histograms are screened against the envelope of available clean graphical readings; missing or incompatible moments trigger the conservative fallback specified in the prediction. The secondary count of adversarial errors was calculated after seeing the primary bound. No new animal traces were generated or claimed. The paper is open access and the repository stores the new numerical derivatives.
