## 2 · The question and the form of the answer
Do the three primaries license a **subtype-discriminating pair in one system with diazepam as comparator**, or is the disagreement (α2-preferring in efficacy / non-selective / modestly α2-preferring in affinity) explained by **assay conditions** (agonist pre-activation level, subunit species, expression system, temperature, normalisation, readout type), leaving the class not separable on public data? **The conditions-explain-it premise is the Advisor's hypothesis, not a conclusion**; "the sources are inconsistent and no condition explains it", "no same-system head-to-head exists" and "the trigger cannot be met from public sources" are complete results.
Required: **per-subtype potency and efficacy with every condition and n beside each value**, for clobazam, NDMC and every comparator the papers include (diazepam, clonazepam, zolpidem where present), values **as printed** with units and the printed dispersion; whether the IT14 table transcribes them correctly; whether NP3P3's trigger is met; and a **conditional design position** for the L2 arm.

## 3 · Definitions fixed before reading
* **Same system:** the same expression system, species of subunits, subunit composition (α/β/γ), agonist level, temperature and normalisation in one experiment series within one paper, including the comparator.
* **NP3P3 trigger met** only if one paper reports clobazam and/or NDMC **and diazepam** at the four α subtypes in **one system** with potency and efficacy and n; a cross-paper ratio does not meet it.
* **Efficacy** = maximal potentiation as printed (Emax, % of GABA control or fold) with its normalisation stated; **potency** = EC50 (functional) or Ki (binding); **affinity is not efficacy**; a ratio is the reader's arithmetic and is labelled.
* **Selectivity statements** are the authors' sentences, kept verbatim (≤ 25 words) and separate from numbers.

## 4 · Priors (fixed now; scored afterwards; may fail)
* **G1.** Ralvenius 2016 includes diazepam in the same HEK293 system as clobazam and NDMC (the IT14 table carries a diazepam efficacy ratio), so a **within-paper head-to-head exists for efficacy in rat subunits**; the other two papers do not include all four subtypes for all three compounds in one system.
* **G2.** The three papers' potency values are of the same order (a few-fold), so the disagreement lies in **which quantity is called "selectivity"** (efficacy ratio in Ralvenius; EC50 equality in Hammer; Ki order in Jensen) rather than in discordant potencies.
* **G3.** Differences in agonist level (EC10 versus EC20), species (rat versus human), system (HEK293 patch versus oocyte two-electrode clamp), temperature and normalisation each **could** move efficacy and potency, but the papers do not vary them within a design, so **no single condition can be shown to explain the disagreement from the three papers alone** (INFERENZA).
* **G4.** Hammer's NDMC values rest on n = 2 and Ralvenius's on a small number of cells per subtype; the IT14 table's numbers match the sources but some dispersions and n are missing in it.
* **G5.** Jensen's binding Ki cannot speak to efficacy or to α2-preference of function; "modestly α2-preferring in affinity" is a binding statement about a different quantity.
* **G6.** The NP3P3 trigger is **not met as worded** by any single paper for NDMC; whether it is met for clobazam (and diazepam) depends on Ralvenius's table.
