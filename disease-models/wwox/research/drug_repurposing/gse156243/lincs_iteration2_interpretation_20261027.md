# LINCS reversal output of the GSE156243 WWOX-knockout signature, iteration 2: controls, wrapper versus service, compound identity, context, return to WWOX

Scientist-d, task SD-IT2-LINCS-20261027 (package W3). Exploratory. Not medical advice; class level; no dose or suitability. Everything LINCS-derived is NOT WWOX EVIDENCE.
A knockout signature is NOT a rescue signature and NOT a Q230P or variant-specific signature. A q-value or z-score is not efficacy. Same-lineage services are one vote.

## 0. Declaration, fixed BEFORE any request of this iteration (offline findings that preceded it are listed so the declaration is not blind to them)

Seen offline before declaring (stored files and the public metadata tables only, no new request):
(a) the stored S1P/S1P2 request files are 29,450 bytes, the byte count of the hyphen-stripped S1 request (the verbatim S1 request is 32,714): the shuffled-direction requests were built from the tracked, hyphen-stripped entity map (`uuid_transform_manifest.tsv`, publication-gate transform) and `sigcom_permuted_direction_query.py` never restores the hyphens, whereas S1 was sent from the verbatim map. This yields the hypothesis H-ENC below.
(b) S0 has 8 genes in both arms and a median log2 mean CPM of 3.65 against 5.09 for S1; 70 percent of S1 symbols (587 of 834) lie in the 12,328-gene L1000 space against 61 percent for S0. S0 is therefore not matched to S1 on expression or on L1000-space membership.

Requests (SigCom LINCS `enrich/ranktwosided`, `l1000_cp`, limit 100, sequential, 15 s apart, one request each, no retry, no key):
DIAG (S1 re-sent with hyphen-stripped ids), S1P and S1P2 (the existing shuffled sets, ids with hyphens), N1-N3 (random sets matched to S1 per gene on expression decile and L1000-space membership, arms disjoint, seeds 20261101-03), K1-K2 (pseudo-contrasts: the four KO libraries split 2 v 2, top 517 / 317 genes by Welch t on log2 CPM; no condition difference by construction). Eight requests. If a request errors it is recorded, not repeated.
Direct L1000FWD public API: S1 and one null (N1), one request each, to learn which limits belong to the wrapper.

Predictions (each falsifiable by `lincs_it2_summarise.py`):
- **H-ENC.** DIAG returns an empty ranking (`reversers 0`) while S1P and S1P2 with hyphens return non-empty rankings. If DIAG is non-empty the encoding hypothesis is false and the emptiness stays unexplained.
- **P-N (content null).** In each of N1-N3 the share of `MCLF*` 4 uM / 6 h signatures among top-100 reversers plus top-100 mimickers is below 20 percent (S1: 60 percent; S0: 0.5 percent). At or above 40 percent in any N set: the family is selected by L1000-space/expression content alone (coverage effect) and S1 loses its specificity.
- **P-K (differential-like noise null).** Same quantity in K1-K2 below 20 percent. At or above 40 percent: any high-variance DE-like list pulls the family.
- **P-S1P (direction control, as P7-P9 of the earlier declaration).** In S1P and S1P2 the 4 uM / 6 h MCLF share of top-100 reversers is at least 40 percent AND the strongest |z-sum| is at least 50 percent of S1's (16.65): the family and the score are properties of gene content, not of the up/down pattern. Either one below: direction contributes.
- **P-WRAP.** The direct L1000FWD result for S1 also returns 50 (not 100) similar/opposite hits: the cap belongs to the service, not to ToolUniverse. If it returns more, the cap was the wrapper's.
Outcome vocabulary: USEFUL / INCONCLUSIVE / DISCARD as in the earlier declaration. Absence from a result is "not returned", never "ineffective".

## 1. Query and control quality

**1.1 Input (offline, `derived/lincs_it2_input_quality.json`).** S1, S2 and S3: no duplicates, no gene in both arms, no entity collision (one symbol, one entity). S0: **8 genes in both arms** (up and down were drawn independently), a flaw of the iteration-1 control that the service accepted silently. Composition differs: median log2 mean CPM 5.09 (S1) against 3.65 (S0); genes inside the 12,328-gene L1000 space 587 of 834 (70 percent) for S1 against 509 of 834 (61 percent) for S0. **S0 was not matched to S1 on expression or on the service's gene space**; the P1 comparison with S0 therefore cannot show specificity (it showed only that S1 is not S0).

**1.2 The empty shuffled-direction answer is explained: a client-side encoding defect, not a service behaviour and not a biological negative.** The tracked entity map and requests are hyphen-stripped (publication-gate transform, `uuid_transform_manifest.tsv`); S1 to S3 and S0 had been sent from the verbatim hyphenated ids, but `sigcom_permuted_direction_query.py` loaded the stripped map and sent hyphenless ids (the stored S1P and S1P2 request files are 29,450 bytes, the byte count of the stripped S1 request; the verbatim one is 32,714). Test (H-ENC, predicted before): S1 itself re-sent with hyphenless ids returns `reversers 0, mimickers 0, results []` in 3.1 s, exactly the iteration-1 S1P answer; S1P and S1P2 sent with hyphens return full rankings (345,394 and 353,190 reversers). H-ENC **held**. The iteration-1 statements "the shuffled control returned empty for an unexplained reason" and "the service documents no reason" described a defect in our own request; the script is corrected (`sigcom_permuted_direction_query.py` restores the hyphens), and the old empty response files stay as the evidence of the defect. Lesson, for the rule that already exists in this folder's README (restore or use the verbatim copy before any rerun): **an empty ranking with a 3-second query time and the right total (`maxRank 693215`) is a rejected-input signature**, not an empty result; send a known-good set through the same code path first.

**1.3 Controls matched in size and content (SigCom `l1000_cp`, limit 100, one request each).** N1 to N3: per-gene matched on expression decile and L1000-space membership, arms disjoint, no S1 gene, seeds 20261101 to 03. K1, K2: pseudo-contrasts, the four KO libraries split 2 v 2 (no condition difference by construction, **but the KO libraries are heterogeneous, so K sets are noise-or-within-KO-heterogeneity controls, not clean nulls**). Quantity: share of `MCLF*` signatures at 4 uM / 6 h in the top-100 of each tail.

| Set | reversers | mimickers | both tails | strongest reverser z-sum | strongest mimicker z-sum | top-25 reverser compounds shared with S1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 (iteration 1) | 61 | 59 | **60 percent** | -16.65 | 16.62 | 25 |
| S0 (random, unmatched) | 1 | 0 | 0.5 | -6.23 | 6.66 | 0 |
| S1P direction-shuffled, hyphens fixed | 0 | 1 | 0.5 | -7.39 | 8.35 | 0 |
| S1P2 | 0 | 0 | 0 | -7.58 | 7.45 | 0 |
| N1 / N2 / N3 (matched random) | 2 / 0 / 0 | 5 / 3 / 2 | 3.5 / 1.5 / 1.0 | -6.9 / -6.5 / -6.8 | 7.1 / 6.7 / 6.6 | 2 / 0 / 0 |
| K1 (KO split) | 0 | 16 | 8 | **-16.32** | 14.18 | 0 |
| K2 (KO split) | 16 | 10 | 13 | -11.60 | 12.66 | 2 |

Declared predictions: **P-N held** (all three N sets at or below 3.5 percent, far under the 20 percent line). **P-K held** (8 and 13 percent, under 20; but K1 and K2 are not zero). **P-S1P did not hold** (family share 0 and 0 against a required 40 percent; strongest |z-sum| 44 and 46 percent of S1's, under the 50 percent line): the earlier post hoc reading, that the family concentration is "what a gene-content/coverage match produces" and that S1's output "is not a reversal", **is not supported once the control works**: with the same 834 genes and the direction pattern destroyed, the family disappears, and matched random sets do not bring it back. The concentration of one signature family in the S1 ranking depends on the up/down pattern of S1.
Three things this does **not** license. (a) The family still fills **both** tails of S1 almost equally (61 and 59); a direction-specific reversal would not. The pattern says the family's signatures correlate strongly with S1 in absolute value, with a sign that varies by compound; it does not say the family contains reversers. (b) **Magnitude is not specificity**: K1, a split of KO libraries with no genotype contrast, reaches a strongest reverser z-sum of -16.3, the same as S1's -16.65, with entirely different compounds and cell lines (MCF7, PC3, A375, SH-SY5Y). A z-sum or its rank says nothing about WWOX. (c) One replicate per control: the N and S1P sets are two or three draws each, no variance estimate beyond that.

**1.4 Frequent hitters.** Compounds of the S1 top-100 that also appear in the top-100 of either tail of one or more of the eight null rankings (S0, S1P, S1P2, N1-N3, K1-K2, `derived/lincs_it2_frequent_hitters.json`): reversers 33 of 76 distinct compounds in at least one null, 9 in at least two, including **clozapine and estradiol, S1's rank-1 and rank-2 reversers**, isradipine, rolipram, vandetanib, MK-1775; mimickers 39 of 68 in at least one null, 19 in at least two (entinostat, geldanamycin, tanespimycin, oligomycin-A, antimycin-A, emetine, everolimus, palbociclib, wortmannin, sonidegib and others). Appearance in a null ranking is not proof of non-specificity (the null may meet the compound in another cell line); it removes the assumption that a top-ranked compound is specific to S1. Of the six iteration-1 "stratum-replicated" compounds, sildenafil recurs in S1P, nimodipine and quiflapon in N1, BRD-K55591206 in N2.

## 2. Wrapper versus service

| Question | Direct L1000FWD public API (`maayanlab.cloud/l1000fwd/sig_search` then `result/topn/<id>`) | ToolUniverse `L1000FWD_sig_search` (iteration 1) |
|---|---|---|
| Entries per direction | 50 similar and 50 opposite, for every set (S1, N1, K1, K2); no total | 100 asked, 50 returned |
| Same ranking? | S1 opposite: **identical 50 signature ids in identical order** to the wrapper's | |
| Other direction | the `similar` tail is returned together with `opposite` in one call | the wrapper's `opposite` mode returned only that tail; the `similar` tail was never read in iteration 1 |
| Record fields | q, p, combined score, z, signature id (BRD code, cell, time, dose) in both | same |
| Format traps | the old path `/L1000FWD/` answers 307 to `/l1000fwd/`; body keys are `up_genes` and `down_genes`, wrong keys give HTTP 500 | handled by the wrapper |

P-WRAP **held**: the 50-entry cap belongs to the **service's `topn` endpoint**, not to ToolUniverse; the direct call does not lift it and the iteration-1 text that the cap or the missing total came from the wrapper is withdrawn. What belongs to the wrapper: only that it exposes one direction per call and drops the other, so the `similar` tail was invisible. **No statement of complete coverage is licensed by either route** (50 of an unknown total). SigCom, by contrast, does give a total in its response header (`Content-Range 0-200/693215`), which is the only place in this work where "how many were ranked" is known. L1000FWD, SigCom and iLINCS share the LINCS data lineage; agreement among them is one vote.

**The direct service also changes the reading of the 7 q < 0.05.** For S1: **40 of the 50 `similar` and 7 of the 50 `opposite`** signatures have q < 0.05 (the iteration-1 summary reported only the 7). N1 (matched random): 0 and 0. K2: 0 and 0. **K1: 47 and 50, minimum q 0.** So the same service returns more q < 0.05 signatures for a no-genotype split of the KO libraries than for the genotype contrast, and none for another split: the q-value of a LINCS hit is a property of the query gene set, not evidence about the biology that produced it. No compound code is shared between S1's 47 signatures at q < 0.05 and K1's 100 returned signatures. Consequence: **the number of significant signatures is not a specificity measure, and "7 with q < 0.05" is withdrawn as an indicator of anything but gene-set behaviour.**

## 3. Compound identity (public, versioned metadata; failures kept)

134 distinct BRD codes were collected from: the SigCom S1 top-100 reversers and mimickers (wherever the signature id carries a code), and the q < 0.05 hits of the direct L1000FWD S1 run. Resolution table: GSE92742 and GSE70138 `pert_info` (checksums verified), then ChEMBL_37 by InChIKey, then the mechanism and activity endpoints; DGIdb for target-to-compound rows where ChEMBL carried none. Full table: `derived/lincs_it2_informative_signatures.tsv` and `derived/lincs_it2_compound_identity.json`.
- **129 of 134 codes resolve to a compound name in the two GEO tables, none ambiguous** (one code, one name). **Five failed**: BRD-A35054627, BRD-A42966393, BRD-A85739295, BRD-K55460824 and **BRD-K09398529, the compound that was the most replicated in the MCLF family (5 lines)**; they are in neither table (the family's library is the 2021 release, not Phase I or II), so their identity is **unknown, not inferred**. 20 of the 100 S1 reversers and 22 of the 100 mimickers carry no BRD code in the signature id (older batches named by `pert_name` only): their identity rests on the service's own name field, not on a versioned compound table.
- ChEMBL by InChIKey: 103 matched, **26 not matched**, 5 without a structure. A curated mechanism of action exists for 28 of the 103. **A harness defect found here**: for 82 of the 103 the mechanism query by `molecule_chembl_id` returned zero rows, and for 7 the query by `parent_molecule_chembl_id` recovered a mechanism (sildenafil: PDE5A inhibitor, as shown by the live check). `framework/adapters/chembl.py` had that behaviour (it reported sildenafil as `molecule_without_mechanism`); a second request by parent id is now made only when the first is empty (adapter selftest and `test_repurposing_adapter.py` 19 tests, `test_repurposing_join.py` 42 tests, green). Absence of a curated mechanism is still not absence of pharmacology.
- Identified compounds of the most informative signatures and what a source says about their action (target presence is not therapeutic utility; none is proposed): **TW-37** (6 of the 40 `similar` signatures; DGIdb lists BCL2 and BCL2L1, with noisy extra rows; BH3-mimetic class), **PAC-1** (3; DGIdb CASP3, no interaction type), **mocetinostat** (HDAC1/2/3/8/11 inhibitor, ChEMBL), **simvastatin** (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor, ChEMBL), **selumetinib** (ChEMBL mechanism empty, MEK inhibitor by name, not verified here); the `opposite` tail: **digitoxigenin** (ChEMBL: no mechanism row; DGIdb: ATP1A1, no type), **XL-147 / pilaralisib** (DGIdb: PIK3 isoforms, inhibitor), **DG-041** (PTGER3, inhibitor, IUPHAR row), two BRD codes unnamed or without ChEMBL structure. SigCom reversers (ranks in the table): quiflapon (ranks 9, 15; ALOX5AP by DGIdb), sildenafil (10, 18, 41; PDE5A inhibitor), **istaroxime (25; ATP1A1 to ATP1A4 inhibitor, DGIdb)**, cabazitaxel (27), GDC-0068 (28), nimodipine (59; L-type calcium channel blocker and mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist), vandetanib (45), nutlin-3 (76) (the last four named only). **TWS-119**: ChEMBL activities, GSK3 beta IC50 pChEMBL 7.52 in four documents. **IWP-2**: ChEMBL activities, porcupine (IC50 pChEMBL 6.8; EC50 7.52) **and** casein kinase 1 delta (pChEMBL 6.0 to 7.4); the second action is not Wnt-secretion and is kept.
- Not treated as therapeutic candidates: the list is a set of query outputs, most of it frequent hitters, antiproliferative or stress agents, or compounds with no human WWOX datum.

## 4. Context and specificity

- **Strata kept apart** (library, cell line, tissue, dose, time, perturbation). SigCom S1 reversers: 74 `MCLF*` (no tissue or disease field in the service, so neural or not is unknown, not assumed; the `CN`/`SZ` suffix pattern is not used as an annotation, because no source retrieved here defines it), the rest in tumour and non-tumour lines of the 2017 libraries. L1000FWD hits (2017 library, `cell_info` of GSE92742 as annotated): `similar` 40 = tumour 30 (PC3, MCF7, VCAP, A549, A375), normal-annotated 9 (HA1E kidney 4, HCC515 lung 4, NKDBA 1), one NPC (fibroblast-derived iPSC neural progenitors, differentiated); `opposite` 7 = tumour 2, normal-annotated 5 (HA1E 2, HCC515 2, SKB myoblast 1). 39 of 40 at 10 uM; 6 h and 24 h. **A non-tumour line is not neural; the one neural-lineage line, NPC, is not enriched in S1** (3 reversers and 3 mimickers, against 0 to 8 and 0 to 9 in the null rankings).
- **Library effects dominate.** The same gene set returns the 2021 MCLF family in SigCom and none of it in L1000FWD (older library without those lines): the two services answer from different parts of the same lineage; their disagreement on compounds is not a replication test. A compound seen in one library only is "not returned" elsewhere, not "ineffective".
- **Other explanations kept open for each class in the lists.** Antiproliferative or death-pathway agents (TW-37, PAC-1, cabazitaxel, Hsp90 inhibitors, palbociclib, everolimus, copanlisib, amsacrine), transcription/translation suppressors (cardiac glycosides, emetine) and mitochondrial inhibitors (oligomycin, antimycin) recur among the identified compounds of the mimicker tails of the 2021 library and of the `similar` tail of the 2017 library; HDAC inhibitors (entinostat in 14 of the 70 MCLF mimicker signatures, mocetinostat in the 2017 `similar` tail) are a second recurrent class. These are exactly the perturbations that move many genes in a cell-viability-dependent way. A signature that is highly similar to such perturbations is compatible with a stress/proliferation/composition axis of the organoids (the iteration-1 maturation and composition work found one), and the K1 result shows that within-KO heterogeneity alone yields comparable scores. **This cannot be separated with the data in hand.**

**Overall: the iteration-2 LINCS work adds no hypothesis, no counter-evidence and no candidate to the WWOX portfolio.** What it adds is knowledge about the instrument: how a LINCS reversal query on a differential-expression list must be controlled, what an empty answer can mean, and which of the iteration-1 statements fall. A KO signature is not a rescue signature and not variant-specific; no q-value, z-sum or rank is an efficacy statement; same-lineage services are one vote.

## 6. Errors in earlier work found here (surfaced, not softened)
1. The S1P/S1P2 shuffled control "returned empty for an unexplained reason" (iteration 1 and the README row): our own request defect, section 1.2. The conclusion drawn from its absence (P7 to P9 not evaluable, "content-match" reading) is superseded by section 1.3.
2. The post hoc reading "a gene-content/coverage match to one signature family" is not supported (section 1.3). The balanced two-tail fill stands as an observation.
3. S0 was not matched (8 genes in both arms, lower expression, fewer L1000-space genes) (1.1).
4. The ToolUniverse test attributed the 50-entry cap and the missing total to the wrapper and reported only the `opposite` tail's 7; the cap is the service's, and the `similar` tail has 40 (section 2). Its "S1P and S1P2 yield none" was a valid direction control of the L1000FWD side, but the same service gives K1 many hits: the count is not a specificity measure.
5. The ChEMBL adapter false negative for mechanisms (section 3), found here and repaired.
6. My own: the first L1000FWD call used the wrong body keys (HTTP 500), and my first ChEMBL pass queried mechanisms by molecule id only; both are in the stored scripts' docstrings.

## 7. What was not verified, and reopening conditions
- The identity of five BRD codes, including the most replicated (BRD-K09398529); the 2021 library's compound table (CLUE beta) was not reachable without a key. Reopen: a public versioned table of that release.
- What the `MCLF*` cell lines are (tissue, disease, donor); no source retrieved states it.
- One request per control, no variance; K sets are not clean nulls.
- The classes of selumetinib, TW-37 and PAC-1 rest partly on DGIdb rows with noisy neighbours; ChEMBL had no curated mechanism for several.
- Not run: L1000FWD `similar` through ToolUniverse; CLUE (key required); iLINCS concordance (needs a full signature table); offline use of GSE92742 level 5 matrices (several GB, not downloaded). The most useful next step if LINCS is to stay in the portfolio: compute the S1 contrast against the level 5 signatures of the 2017 set locally, with a permutation null built from the same matrix, so that no service limit applies; this is a bounded job that has not been done.
Reopening condition for any LINCS-derived lead: the same compound class appears for a **second, independent genotype contrast** (a WWOX-deficient neural dataset other than this organoid deposit), is absent from content-matched and KO-split controls, and is not a frequent hitter.
