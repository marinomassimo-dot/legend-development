## Omitted decisive points

1. Emnett 2013 full text (PMID 24101301) contains a direct in-paper test of extrasynaptic preference by memantine with a negative result (70% vs 72%; "little evidence for drug selectivity for extrasynaptic receptors"). Row E20 treats the paper only as a memantine-vs-ketamine comparison.
2. Independent partial tests of the 2:1 premise exist (PMID 25656790 positive with a GluN2C/D attribution; 23727219 agonist-presentation dependence), contradicting H1 clause 2 and the "little NR2 preference" reading (E17).
3. The Breton paper itself reports data bearing on receptor alteration, which E23's "dependence, not alteration" omits: a delayed, d-APV-suppressible field-evoked response larger in knockouts (Supp. Fig. S3, 6 slices/3 animals vs 7/4), with the authors' conclusion "dysregulated NMDAR-mediated signaling"; increased mEPSC and sEPSC amplitude with unchanged mEPSC decay time constant (5.18 vs 4.84 ms) and unchanged frequency; reduced sIPSC amplitude and frequency; more action potentials in knockout pyramidal neurons. This sets the prior for X1 and X4.
4. Burst duration is in seconds (Supp. Table S1); closes the unit question; recording Mg2+ and the 2.29 vs 0.84 discrepancy stay open.
5. E15: source timescale is "tens of minutes", contrast with milliseconds; 0.66 s is intermediate, not "closer to synaptic".
6. E21: both drugs suppressed network activity and bursting in culture at 10 uM; the preparation was culture, not slices.
7. E12: ratio falls from 1.94 to about 1.6 under depolarisation with Mg2+.
8. E08: the abstract's closing sentence advocates "targeted therapy" more strongly than the title.
9. PMID 42201999 (2026, abstract only): memantine, not ketamine, shows Ca2+-dependent and subtype-dependent inhibition and preferential inhibition under neurotoxic conditions; limits the "indistinguishable" framing of E20 and PR-ketamine.
10. Ro 25-6981 and ifenprodil are activity-dependent blockers (Fischer abstract), so any "fractional block at a given concentration" depends on agonist level in the burst.
