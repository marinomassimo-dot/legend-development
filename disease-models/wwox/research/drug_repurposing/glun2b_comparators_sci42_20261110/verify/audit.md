### S3-2 PARTLY — the table is logically coherent, but four arms presuppose properties the sources do not give
1. **A1/A2 axis (percent of isolated NMDAR current removed)** is measured "in separate cells or slices", then applied to the burst circuit. It presupposes that the percent removed in the recorded cell type, compartment, temperature, Mg2+ and glutamate level equals the percent removed where the burst is generated. For d-APV (competitive, hence surmountable: S3 says so) the occupancy during a burst's glutamate transient differs from that during the isolated-current test; for a NAM the ceiling depends on assembly and Zn2+ (earlier package: ifenprodil 88% diheteromer, 32% triheteromer, 32 to 50% with 300 nM Zn2+). E2 ("weight beyond their share of the current") is only defined if "share" is stated for a named cell population; as written it is not falsifiable by a somatic isolated-current measurement. State the cell type and require the isolated current and the burst readout from the same animals.
2. **A3** is internally right (Zn2+ raising the ceiling is the earlier package's datum), but it discriminates none of E1-E4; it calibrates r. Say so, or move it into step 4 as a calibration.
3. **A5/§4 contradiction**: A5's weakening result says "the NAM class cannot be assumed to share a ceiling (D03)" and §4 "Redundant" says a second ifenprodil-site NAM "controls chemotype only, same ceiling". Both cannot hold; the earlier package itself carries radiprodil (82 vs 45 percent by assembly) as a different ceiling. Delete "same ceiling".

### S3-3 NOT SUPPORTED as stated: "NP10679 adds a within-preparation specificity signature (the burst effect following the recombinant pH-potency shift)"
It is logically a conditional, not a signature, and rests on four presuppositions, three of which the files contradict or leave open:
1. **It presupposes that conventional NAMs and d-APV do not follow pH.** D11's own sources say ifenprodil inhibition is pH-sensitive (9143010: smaller at alkaline pH; 10196581: ifenprodil and CP-101,606 enhance proton inhibition), and the NP10679 paper's authors cite Mott 1998 for the class. The same Yuan Results paragraph states that for phosphono-containing competitive antagonists (the d-APV class) acidity **lowers** potency (ionisation), so d-APV may follow pH in the opposite direction (INFERENZA, class-level sentence). "While d-APV and a conventional NAM do not" is therefore not given by the sources.
2. **Protons act on the receptor and the preparation.** For GluN1/GluN2B in oocytes the current at pH 6.9 is 0.21 of that at 7.6 (Yuan, WT, n=8). A pH change that drops the NMDAR current to about a fifth changes the burst readout in vehicle (E3), may leave no window to detect a drug effect at the acidic value (floor), and makes the burst, d-APV and NAM readouts interact with pH. The design's control ("vehicle and d-APV at each pH") is necessary but the contrast needed is a drug-by-pH interaction against a pH-matched reference, not a pH main effect.
3. **It presupposes that the recombinant shift transfers**: the NP10679 shift is declared (about 6-fold; 6.2-fold in the abstract) from human GluN2B in oocytes with conditions unknown; the series data were at 23 C, Mg2+-free, EDTA; the slice is at 35 C, 1.3 mM Mg2+ probably, bicarbonate-buffered. Bath pH set by CO2/bicarbonate does not guarantee cleft pH (the NP10679 abstract itself invokes acidic vesicle content at synapses during high-frequency firing, so a local acidification that follows activity rather than bath pH is a competing route to a "pH-following" effect).
4. **"An off-target effect would not follow the pH shift"** presupposes that no off-target potency depends on pH; the ionisation of the arylpiperazine amine at the two pH values is not given for NP10679 (Yuan excluded ionisation for the 93-series only, by calculated pKa).
What the sources do support: a matched pair that differs in pH sensitivity at similar potency at physiological pH exists in the 93-series (93-31 9.4-fold vs 93-97 2.9-fold; potency similar at pH 7.35: 2.2 vs 4.8 uM); if available, such a pair, or at least ifenprodil run at both pH values, is the missing comparator for any "pH-following" claim. Recommend rewording A4 to: "a drug-by-pH interaction in the burst effect, larger for NP10679 than for ifenprodil and d-APV run at the same two pH values, is compatible with a pH-sensitive GluN2B NAM; it cannot separate E2 from proton-receptor coupling or from state (E3), and it is not a specificity signature (IPOTESI)".
