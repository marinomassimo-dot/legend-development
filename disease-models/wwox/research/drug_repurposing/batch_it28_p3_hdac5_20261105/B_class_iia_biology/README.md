# IT28 P3-B: class IIa biology, catalytic versus scaffold function of HDAC5

Scope: sub-question B of the HDAC5 batch. Rationale under test (IT27 H1): a pan/class-I-leaning HDAC inhibitor (pracinostat) lowers HDAC5-dependent transcription (SLC2A1) in human neural cells. Origin of the edge (A) and pharmacological discordance (C) are covered elsewhere and not repeated here.

Reading status. Full text via free routes (NCBI E-utilities efetch db=pmc XML, 2026-11-05) and read in part: Bradner 2010, Sando 2012, Taniguchi 2012, Cho 2013, Taniguchi 2017 (draft receipts in `receipts/`; sections read are listed in each receipt, figures and supplements not read). The publisher withholds XML full text for Lahm 2007, Schuetz 2008 and the 2019 cardiomyocyte HDAC5 paper, and Fischle 2002, Lobera 2013 and Bantscheff 2011 have no free PMC text: those are **abstract only** and every claim from them is limited to what the abstract says. No fetch was attempted by spoofing a browser. No e-mail address was sent anywhere. Quotes were machine-checked as substrings of the retrieved text.

## Verdict on the premise

**Narrowed, not refuted.** "Catalytic inhibition lowers HDAC5 output" is not supported by any neural primary source found, and the neural evidence that exists points to localisation, phosphorylation and MEF2/chromatin recruitment as the operative variables. There is, however, one non-neural counter-example in which catalytic activity of HDAC5 matters for a transcriptional output (NRF2 targets, cardiomyocytes; abstract only), and one inhibitor paper claiming "gene regulation dependent on the catalytic active site of class IIa HDACs" (Lobera 2013; abstract only). So catalytic dependence of an HDAC5 output is context-dependent and unmeasured for SLC2A1 or for any human neural cell. Also, the direction matters: in the sources below nuclear HDAC5 represses; a catalytic-site inhibitor that removes a repressor activity would, if anything, raise the output, not lower it (inference, not tested).

Catalytic-dead mutants. The literature read gives gain-of-function (H976Y, Lahm) rather than loss-of-function mutants of the wild-type enzyme, which is itself nearly inactive; "catalytic-dead" is therefore a weak control for class IIa (little activity to lose), whereas a H976Y activated enzyme is the informative reagent. The one catalytic-site loss-of-function variant found in neurons is HDAC4 L-H/A (Section 2); its loss of activity was not demonstrated in the text I read.

HDAC3 dependence. Stated by Fischle 2002 (abstract) and repeated as received knowledge in a neural paper (Cho 2013: "Since HDAC5 deacetylase activity requires an association with HDAC3 (Fischle et al., 2002)"; DISCUSSION > Histone modifications...). No primary HDAC5-specific measurement of HDAC3 dependence was obtained in this batch.

## 4. The discriminating experiment

Design in human neural progenitors/neurons (hNPC and iPSC-derived neurons), with the readout fixed before the experiment: SLC2A1 protein and 2-deoxyglucose uptake (functional) plus SLC2A1 mRNA, together with one HDAC5 positive-control target and a nuclear-HDAC5 occupancy ChIP at the SLC2A1 locus. Keep four things distinct and measure them separately: HDAC5 abundance (immunoblot), localisation (nuclear fraction), catalytic engagement (see arm 6) and scaffold function (occupancy).

1. Knockdown (CRISPRi or two independent siRNAs) of HDAC5 alone, with an HDAC4 and HDAC9 compensation check (Cho 2013 and Taniguchi 2012 both show paralog cross-reactivity or compensation).
2. Knockdown plus shRNA-resistant rescue with (a) wild-type HDAC5, (b) nuclear-locked 3SA, (c) catalytic-site mutant, (d) H976Y activated mutant (gain of catalysis), (e) a mutant lacking the HDAC3/N-CoR-binding region of the catalytic domain, (f) a MEF2-binding-domain deletion. Expression level matched to endogenous.
3. Class IIa-selective inhibitor (TMP269 or a TFMO analog; LMK235 has a different selectivity profile) at doses fixed by a target-engagement readout, plus an inhibitor-resistant mutant if available.
4. Class-I-only inhibitor (RGFP966-type for HDAC3; a class I benzamide) and the intended compound (pracinostat), dose-matched on bulk H3 acetylation.
5. HDAC6-selective inhibitor as a negative control.
6. Target-engagement and activity control: acetyl-H3 on the occupied locus, HDAC3 co-occupancy by ChIP, and a trifluoroacetyl-lysine substrate assay on immunoprecipitated HDAC5 (Bradner 2010 substrate class) to show the inhibitor reaches the catalytic site in these cells.

Interpretation grid for "catalytic inhibition lowers HDAC5 output":
- Supported: knockdown lowers SLC2A1, wild-type and H976Y rescue restore it, the catalytic mutant does not, TMP269 phenocopies knockdown in the dose range that engages the target, class-I-only inhibitor does not. (Note this requires HDAC5 to be an activator of SLC2A1, which is the IT27 edge's sign, from one muscle reference.)
- Refuted: (i) the catalytic-dead rescue restores SLC2A1 as well as wild type; or (ii) TMP269 at engagement-confirmed doses leaves SLC2A1 unchanged while knockdown moves it; or (iii) the effect of pracinostat is reproduced by the class-I-only inhibitor and is absent after HDAC5 knockdown (HDAC5-independent); or (iv) the effect of an inhibitor is lost in HDAC5-null cells (off-target) or retained.
- Opposite sign: inhibition or knockdown raises SLC2A1. This is the pattern in the repressor-type sources and in the cardiomyocyte NRF2 case, and would invert the lever.
- Pracinostat specifically: with class IIa not targeted at class I doses (Bradner 2010), any pracinostat effect at those doses is most parsimoniously a class I effect (HDAC3 or HDAC1/2) acting on the same locus; arm 4 plus HDAC3 knockdown is the discriminating contrast. Potency details in sub-question C.

## 5. What transfers to human neural cells and what does not

Transfers (reasonably): the biochemical facts (Lahm 2007, Bradner 2010, Fischle 2002), which concern recombinant human proteins and the conserved vertebrate H976 substitution; the mechanisms of signal-regulated shuttling and 14-3-3/MEF2 handling, with the caveat that the evidence is rodent.

Does not transfer or is not shown: all neural functional evidence is rodent (rat cortical and striatal neurons, adult mouse DRG and NAc), one paper (Sando) is HDAC4, one (Cho) is peripheral sensory neurons, Taniguchi studies manipulate localisation and MEF2 binding only; the cardiomyocyte catalytic result is non-neural; the inhibitor-neural data are in SH-SY5Y and rat midbrain cultures with different phenotypes; none is on SLC2A1; no catalytic-dead HDAC5 in any neural cell. HDAC5 expression and nuclear fraction in the WWOX human systems is not established here (a prerequisite for the lever; IT9 qualification step). HDAC5 mRNA level is not a functional readout of catalytic activity, abundance, nuclear residence or scaffold function.

## 6. Not determinable from the retrieved sources

- What genes Lobera 2013 and JBC 2019 report and with which exact controls (full texts unavailable by free route).
- HDAC3 dependence of HDAC5 specifically, in neural cells.
- Whether TMP269/LMK235 at the cited doses engages HDAC5 in cells (no on-target marker per Bradner; target engagement is an open item).
- Whether the SLC2A1 edge is catalytic in any cell type.
