# Unguided expansion, condition A: Wwox-deleted layer II/III neurons, P13-17

## Answer first

The state (depolarised rest, larger sag and rebound, higher input resistance, NMDA-receptor-dependent bursting) is not explained by one change. Ih (HCN) gain explains sag, rebound and depolarisation, but raising Ih should lower, not raise, input resistance. The higher input resistance therefore needs a second change. Seven explanations are in `hypotheses.tsv`; the four worth a first experiment:

1. H1 (inferred): loss of a K+ leak or subthreshold K+ conductance (Kir, K2P, Kv7), which explains rest and input resistance together. Test: Ba2+-sensitive and XE991-sensitive current in voltage clamp.
2. H2 (inferred): more Ih, via HCN1 surface expression or TRIP8b. Test: ZD7288-sensitive sag, rebound and rest, and HCN1 surface level.
3. H4 / H5 (hypothesised): the state is partly a consequence, of chronic bursting (H4) or of lost inhibitory shunt (H5). Test: record intrinsic properties with synaptic transmission blocked, in bursting and non-bursting slices (the 6 knockout slices that did not burst), and the gabazine effect on holding current and input resistance.
4. H3 (hypothesised): smaller or less mature cells. Test: capacitance, specific conductance and an age series.

The decision they change: a restoring or blocking agent is worth trying only if H1 or H2 holds; if H3 or H4 explains it, channel pharmacology is premature. A failed rescue never refutes a mechanism by itself. Blocking Ih could worsen bursting (H2 counter-evidence), so a direction cannot be assumed.

## Evidence and depth

Everything below is **abstract_only** (PubMed records through the search tool). No full text was opened, so no receipts are staged and no methods, figures or tables are described.

- Observed (PMID 34634460, abstract): the preparation itself is already published. Knockout layer II/III neurons are depolarised with more action potentials, sag and rebound; bursts depend on NMDA receptors and gap junctions; EPSCs are larger and IPSCs smaller and rarer. This is a single origin (one group). Note that a plain Wwox query found it at once, so this condition did not isolate "the question alone" from this paper.
- Observed (PMID 30290271, abstract): whole-body Wwox knockout lost hippocampal PV and NPY interneurons at two weeks. Different region and genotype.
- Transferred: HCN sets input resistance and rest and is trafficked by TRIP8b (PMIDs 24756635, 21593326); reduced Ih gives the opposite phenotype in cortical dysplasia (PMID 21795624); cholesterol affects HCN2 and HCN4 more than HCN1 in CHO cells (PMID 26404789); HCN1 gain-of-function mouse is sensitive to specific antiseizure drugs (PMID 36300716).
- Not found: any link of Wwox to HCN, K+ leak channels, Na/K-ATPase or extracellular matrix (queries Q01, Q06, Q11, Q14, Q17, Q23). Bounded statement: not found after PubMed search on 2026-11-08 with the strings in `queries.tsv`; this is not a negative.

## Transfer limits

Species (rat, CHO cells), region (hippocampus), age (adult, culture) and preparation differ from mouse neocortical slices at P13-17. Whole-body versus neuron-specific deletion differs for interneuron loss.
