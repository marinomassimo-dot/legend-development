# Does ethosuximide's effect on myelin in absence epilepsy transfer to WWOX deficiency?

**Decision.** The rat data support a localized activity-dependent effect on already myelinated axons. They supply little support for ethosuximide rebuilding missing sheaths in WWOX deficiency. The drug's clearest direct Wwox evidence remains the acute provoked-seizure result. A focal excess of sheath thickness within a generally hypomyelinated WWOX brain remains possible, and would change the interpretation.

## Animal-level reanalysis

Knowles and colleagues began ethosuximide before seizure onset in WAG/Rij rats and Wistar controls. Using the source spreadsheets, we gave each animal equal weight. The g-ratio divides inner axonal diameter by the outer diameter including the sheath. A higher ratio indicates a relatively thinner sheath at a given calibre. Raw values and the calculation are in [results.tsv](results.tsv) and [analyze.py](analyze.py). [Primary study, Fig. 1–2 and Extended Data 3, 5](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41593-022-01052-2)

| Endpoint | WAG vehicle | WAG ethosuximide | Wistar vehicle | Wistar ethosuximide |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Seizures/hour at age 6.5 months | 22.05 (7) | 0.28 (7) | 0 (8) | 0 (9) |
| Mean callosal body g-ratio at 7 months | 0.7164 (3) | 0.7547 (3) | 0.7452 (4) | 0.7565 (3) |

The drug-associated increase in g-ratio was 0.0383 in WAG and 0.0113 in Wistar. Their difference was **+0.0270**, ranging from +0.0103 to +0.0390 on dropping one rat in every arm simultaneously. These are descriptive sensitivity bounds. EEG and microscopy had matching IDs, sex, strain and treatment for 11/13 microscopy animals, including just **two** treated WAG animals. Both had zero recorded seizures. Among the three matched WAG controls, seizure rates were 7.8, 31.7 and 62.3/hour and g-ratios were 0.723, 0.723 and 0.703. These counts cannot separate a direct drug effect from seizure suppression or establish individual-level mediation. [Animal matches](matched_animals.tsv)

In untreated cohorts the WAG–Wistar g-ratio difference was +0.0075 in callosal body before seizure onset and −0.0506 at six months, versus −0.0142 in the six-month splenium. Matching body and splenium in the same three animals per strain gave a region-by-strain contrast of −0.0308. In the separate axon-count sample (three per strain/age), the percentage of axons myelinated differed by only +1.6 points before and +1.2 points after onset. This pattern favors disproportionate local thickening over a large tract-wide rise in coverage. Absolute thickness and the fraction myelinated are distinct outcomes. [Knowles et al., source workbooks](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-022-01052-2#Sec36)

## Comparison with WWOX and discriminating prediction

Repudi and colleagues' P17 mouse figure shows about **131, 46 and 105 myelinated axons per callosal field** in WT, Wwox-null and null animals receiving neuronal Wwox restoration (three mice per group). In optic nerve the estimates are 140, 68 and 125. Thus neuronal restoration closed roughly 70% and 79% of the respective field-count deficits. These rounded heights were read from Fig. 5B,H. The paper also reports relatively thin sheaths in null animals and increased thickness after restoration (Fig. 5C,I). Counts per field lack a total-axon denominator. Different ages, strains and anatomical sampling prevent a numerical comparison to the rat percentage or g-ratio contrast. No ethosuximide was given in this Wwox comparison. [Repudi et al., Fig. 5](https://doi.org/10.15252/emmm.202114599)

**Prediction.** If the WAG mechanism transfers, an untreated WWOX seizure-propagation tract should have abnormally thick sheaths among surviving myelinated axons relative to calibre-matched controls, even if few axons are myelinated overall. At equal seizure burden, the response outside the seizure circuit and the change in sheath coverage should be smaller. Conversely, an increase in newly myelinated axons and better conduction at matched seizure burden without pre-existing focal thickening would support an additional developmental mechanism. Optic nerve is an informative second WWOX tract because its own baseline defect is measurable. It would need independent activity measurements before being called seizure-spared. Later rats with genetic absence seizures and neonatal Wwox-null mice have different circuit development. A separate adult Grin2b-haploinsufficient rat study found preserved callosal MBP and CASPR volume despite absence seizures; those measurements do not resolve individual sheath thickness. [Francsics et al., Fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.1177/23982128251405032)
