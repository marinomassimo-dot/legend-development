Structure: header and question (L1-6), Summary (L8-13), Findings 1-8 (L16-69), Mechanistic Model with ASCII diagram and framing table (L71-117), Evidence Base table of 14 PMIDs plus one paragraph of 4 more (L119-137), Limitations (L139-150), Follow-up (L152-163), Conclusion (L165-166).

Claims of measurement versus reasoning: only two WWOX findings are presented as measured and cited: neuron-only Wwox deletion gives reduced OL maturation, fewer myelinated axons, impaired conduction (PMID 33914858) and WWOX loss hyperactivates GSK-3beta with lithium abolishing induced seizures (PMID 32000863). Everything linking these to myelin pharmacology (GSK-3beta, clemastine, T3, neuregulin/NMDA, early hyperexcitability, SDR/lipid) is transferred from non-WWOX systems and is inference. The report itself says no GSK-3beta inhibitor has been tested on the WWOX myelin phenotype and Arm B (OL-lineage deletion) has not been done.

Internal observations (stated, not adjudicated):
- Summary and Finding 1 say the non-cell-autonomous reading is "strong"/"decisive", while Finding 7 and Limitations say an intrinsic contribution cannot be excluded in patients.
- Two framings of the signal (lost positive cue versus excess early activity) predict opposite-sign interventions; the report favours neither but calls the second "the most important conceptual advance".
- The "KB" label is used for facts without a literature citation (GSK-3beta hyperactivation across three regions, the INDIRECT_UNKNOWN_INTERMEDIATES annotation, GO terms).
- The neuregulin quote does not mention BDNF although the table title does.
- The SDR-domain statement and the lithium safety statement carry no citation.
- The diagram places "loss of SDR/lipid scaffold function" as a neuronal-loss branch, whereas Finding 7 treats it as an oligodendrocyte-intrinsic route.
- PMID 34634460 is described as WWOX-null cortex hyperexcitability, but it is not stated whether it is neuron-specific.
- The clemastine, T3 and GSK-3beta myelination sources are remyelination or other-defect models, not developmental hypomyelination.
