# Repurposing-readiness sheets, first-pilot arms (SCI, 2026-11-16; non-canonical)

Written for: IT (BLOCK-1 input), ADV and the operator (which arms a laboratory could run). **Abstract-level, not a safety review.** Mechanism labels for an experiment design: no agent is recommended, ranked or compared; no dose, schedule or route; no clinical statement; reference genotype class only. A flag marks what a public source SAYS, not a judgement by SCI; `none` never means safe.

## Files
* `sheets_tranche1.tsv` (13 agents: memantine, ketamine, esketamine; zolpidem, eszopiclone, diazepam, clobazam, clonazepam; ethosuximide, zonisamide; carbamazepine, lamotrigine, phenytoin). Columns: class, mechanism sentence and source, class-level approved-indication label sentence, blood-brain-barrier statement and source, pediatric label statement and source, interaction/contraindication class, `flag_for_safety_triage`, `flag_basis`, access notes, `bbb_second_route_SCI`, disclaimer.
* `signals_tranche1.tsv` (33 rows): serious tolerability or toxicity sentences, verbatim, with label section or PMID.
* GluN2B-site agents (ifenprodil, Ro 25-6981: tool compounds, not repurposing candidates; radiprodil: clinical-stage): `sheets_glun2b.tsv`, `signals_glun2b.tsv` (see the section at the end).
* `producer/` the delegate's original outputs (BRIEF_PRODUCER.md, sheets.tsv, signals.tsv, README.md), unchanged; `verify_labels.py` + `label_verify.tsv`, `sheet_verify.tsv`, `label_text_sha256.tsv` (SCI's mechanical check); `bbb_second_route.py` + `bbb_second_route.tsv` (second route for the blood-brain-barrier absence claims).

## Flag rule (written in the brief, applied by the source's wording)
`red`: a retrieved source states a boxed warning, or serious, life-threatening or fatal reactions for the agent. `yellow`: a retrieved source states a clinically significant tolerability issue or a named interaction class without those words. `none`: neither found in what was read. When in doubt the higher flag. Result for tranche 1: **red 11** (esketamine, zolpidem, eszopiclone, diazepam, clobazam, clonazepam, carbamazepine, lamotrigine by a boxed warning; ethosuximide, zonisamide, phenytoin by "fatal" or "fatalities" wording in a Warnings sentence of the label read, no boxed warning in that text), **yellow 2** (memantine: a named exposure-changing condition class, no boxed warning; ketamine: emergence reactions, no boxed warning in the Ketalar text read), **none 0**. Eleven red flags are a property of this rule on antiseizure and CNS-active labels, not a ranking: it says nothing about relative safety.

## How it was produced and checked
One producer delegate (`legend-delegate`, sonnet, effort medium) read DailyMed labels and PubMed abstracts; WebFetch returns a model's excerpts, so its quotes were not byte-exact. **SCI then re-fetched the raw label XML from DailyMed's public API (one GET per label, default client, no contact data) and checked every label-sourced quote as a substring after normalising markup and case: 31 of 31 signal quotes found verbatim in the label text (4 first failed only because the delegate had appended a bracketed gloss or a full stop before "[see ...]"; the label text is the same), and 48 of 54 quote-like sheet fields found in the label, 6 in the abstract; two did not match mechanically: eszopiclone's mechanism sentence differs only by an HTML entity for the Greek letter alpha (present in the abstract, PMID 20945020), and phenytoin's indication sentence had dropped "(temporal lobe)" in a joined excerpt, restored verbatim in `sheets_tranche1.tsv`.** The abstract-sourced items were checked by NCBI efetch (PMIDs 33744690, 41869093, 20945020 and others named in the sheets). Label sources are mostly repackager labels (listed with setids in `producer/README.md`), label revision dates vary, and for several labels only the first 100,000 characters were read.

## What the sheets cannot say
* Absence claims were challenged by a second route: of 10 agents with "blood-brain barrier not stated at abstract level" in the first route, a PubMed title/abstract query for each agent with the barrier returned a sentence naming both for 8 (esketamine, zolpidem, clobazam, clonazepam, ethosuximide, zonisamide, carbamazepine, phenytoin), none for eszopiclone and lamotrigine. Several of these sentences are not crossing statements (an aim, a measurement without its result, a drug-panel impairment statement, a nanoparticle paper's premise "poor penetration" for phenytoin); each is typed in the `bbb_second_route_SCI` column. Preclinical and human contexts are mixed.
* Pediatric statements: where none is entered, the producer's extractor returned text that was not clearly a section 8.4 statement (lamotrigine, phenytoin); a labelled pediatric statement may still exist. The clonazepam pediatric cell is a cut fragment of a longer sentence (the part shown is verbatim; the continuation was not captured).
* Carbamazepine mechanism: not stated in the retrieved text; the arm class comes from the brief.
* Mechanism sources are weak for ethosuximide (a trial-protocol abstract), memantine (a medicinal-chemistry paper for its BBB sentence) and lamotrigine (a meta-analysis abstract); the label sentences give the sources' own wording.
* The catalogue's penetrance column from IT's triage is still `not run` (ADMET-AI and RDKit are absent); this note adds source sentences, not predictions. Nothing here is an exposure, a penetration measurement or a safety review.

## GluN2B-site agents (added after the first hand-back)
`sheets_glun2b.tsv` and `signals_glun2b.tsv` hold ifenprodil and Ro 25-6981 (class_label: `tool compound, not a repurposing candidate`; no approved use found in the abstracts read; ex-US approval status of ifenprodil was NOT searched) and radiprodil (clinical-stage; `no approved label found`; PMID 42596612 states it is under clinical investigation as a possible anti-epileptic drug for paediatric use; PMID 42470359 covers its development in GRIN-related disorder, tuberous sclerosis complex and focal cortical dysplasia type II, in `flag_basis` because the sheet has no development-stage column). PubMed abstracts only (DailyMed returned no label for ifenprodil or radiprodil). Flags: yellow 2 (ifenprodil, radiprodil), none 1 (Ro 25-6981), red 0. **The yellow flags rest on one sentence of PMID 38794209 that names extrapyramidal effects for NMDA receptor antagonists as a class and does not name these agents, set by the "higher flag in doubt" rule; the same abstract says GluN2B antagonists such as ifenprodil have "low side-effect profiles".** `none` for Ro 25-6981 means no tolerability sentence in the two abstracts read. The mechanism sentences name the agents as GluN2B inhibitors or antagonists (PMIDs 39067562, 42117259), not binding sites. All quoted sentences were checked against the NCBI efetch abstract text (`glun2b_verify.tsv`: every mechanism, pediatric and signal quote found; only the producer's own prose in `flag_basis` is not a quote). The held potencies in the brief (ifenprodil 155 nM GluN2B v 28 uM GluN2A; Ro 25-6981 9 nM v 52 uM) are not on the sheets. No pediatric safety or tolerability trial abstract was found for radiprodil by the query used.
