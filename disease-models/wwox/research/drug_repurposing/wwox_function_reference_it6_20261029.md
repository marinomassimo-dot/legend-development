## 1. The question and why this file exists

The IT5 P8 thread (`missense_recovery_it5_p8_20261029/README.md`, sections 2, 6 and 8) ended on one gate: *no abundance gain may be read as rescue until a wild-type function ruler exists.* The A01 card carries that gate in its next-measurement field. This file builds the ruler's specification from the published WWOX assays: what each assay measures, what its controls were, whether it can tell "protein present" from "protein functional", and whether it could even apply to a destabilised SDR-domain missense.

Function ladder used (from P8 section 2): L0 soluble protein present; L1 folded, ligand/cofactor-competent (intrinsic); L2 domain-dependent interaction retained; L3 SDR catalytic activity; L4 cell-level phenotype; L5 organism. The six levels requested for this file map as: molecular activity = L3; interaction = L2; localisation = a sub-case of L0/L2 (it cannot discriminate, see 5); cellular response and phenotype rescue = L4; organism outcome = L5.

**Where the SDR-domain activity is unsupported (stated plainly).** There is one catalytic paper (W10). In it, activity is measured in crude bacterial extract with a large host background and no purified enzyme, no active-site mutant, no band identity, no variant of any kind, and no replication since 2011. LEGEND holds no physiological substrate for WWOX; an in-vitro activity on exogenous steroids is not a substrate assignment. The catalytic-triad point mutants (S281A, Y293F, K297A) are cited in the literature only as unpublished observations without data (census, section 3.2). Therefore (i) a "catalytically dead" negative control cannot be defined from the record, (ii) no cell-based or biochemical readout in this file is a WWOX catalytic measurement, and (iii) nothing below assumes that WWOX has an enzymatic activity that could be assayed. If a purified-enzyme assay is ever built (the P8 Step 0), it is a separate project that must first show the activity with a triad-mutant control.

## 5. What the record gives for the question "protein present versus protein functional"

* Present and inert, reported (assignment unverified, see W09): P282A (stable, lost the POLE4 interaction and cancer-panel function) in W09.
* Dose-to-function relation, shown for the wild-type protein in vivo: W12 (promoter, WPRE and dose series), but the protein-per-region values are only partly quantified. LD and HD were not statistically resolved in vector DNA or mRNA, protein shows an untested trend (cortex about 2-fold higher in HD, the paper says "a trend"), and the low dose already carries several-fold wild-type bulk protein in cortex and hippocampus, so bulk lysate protein does not track survival (LD: modest extension, still better than vehicle; HD: near wild type).
* Present, low, and different phenotypes: W13 patient lines.
* Absent and not functional: nulls (all organism and organoid models).
* Unstable but functional: no WWOX example (P252A is unstable, but its function is confounded with abundance).
* Opposite direction (functional with low protein): no datum. A boost multiplies R (amount times function per molecule), it does not change it; R has been measured for no WWOX allele.

## 6. Choice

**Single most informative assay (WFR-1): a wild-type add-back titration, read on a WWOX-dependent phenotype with a published null-rescue-dead-mutant set, with the variant read on the same curve at measured protein.** It is not a new assay type; it is the combination that W06, W12 and W13 already sketch, made quantitative:

1. System. An isogenic WWOX-null human neural line (the KO lines and the AAVS-targeted add-back approach already exist in the literature the Brain 2026 paper cites; the line itself is not assumed to be available here), differentiated to neurons or organoids. A single genomic landing site and one inducible promoter so wild type, the Q230P-class allele, a WW1 binding-dead control (W44F/P47A), an SDR-region binding probe (L404A) and the empty/EGFP control differ by sequence only, plus an isogenic uninduced wild-type arm at the endogenous level. Promoter chosen not to respond to HDAC inhibitors (P8 section 8).
2. Phenotype, two tiers. Tier 1 (ruler readout): glucose uptake and lactate or oxygen consumption in the null cells, because W06 is the one set found in this review that has, in one study, a null background, a wild-type add-back that rescues, a binding-dead arm that does not, and a quantitative plate-compatible readout (limits: WW1-dependent, so it reports the WW end only; the published add-back's level relative to endogenous wild type is unknown, so the titration must be anchored on an uninduced isogenic wild-type arm). Tier 2 (binary disease-relevant confirmation, not a titratable readout): spontaneous Ca2+ firing in neurons or organoids, the one human-neuron readout found with a published patient-line shift and a wild-type add-back with an EGFP control (W13). The published add-back was in the two patient-derived lines (not null lines), n of 3 to 8 organoids per group, no protein level relative to wild type, and the line difference changes with organoid age; no graded dose-response exists for any human neural readout (only the mouse LD/HD pair). The neurite assay (W03) is a possible pre-screen but needs a WWOX-null background first (UNVERIFIED if available). Whether 2D neurons reproduce the organoid firing phenotype is UNVERIFIED.
3. The ruler. At least five induction levels of wild type from below to above the endogenous level, each with measured per-cell WWOX protein (antibody blot plus targeted mass spectrometry with a heavy-peptide standard, which no WWOX study has published; census query count zero) and transgene mRNA in the same wells. The phenotype is plotted against protein. Its shape (threshold, hill slope, plateau) is the reference function. R for any later arm is read off this curve.
4. The variant arm. The Q230P-class allele under the same promoter, protein measured by the same two methods, then placed on the curve. A compound arm is the same cells treated with compound, with the compound also run on the null line (to see any WWOX-independent effect) and on the wild-type ruler line (to see a boost).

**Why this one.** It is the only design that (a) converts a fold gain in protein into a function number (the P8 section 6 arithmetic needs exactly R), (b) separates present-and-inert from absent at the same protein level, and (c) can be falsified by the null/EGFP and binding-dead controls. The weakness of the published rescues is that none anchors the add-back to the endogenous level in the same experiment (W06: level relative to wild type unknown; W12: even the low dose is several-fold wild-type bulk protein in cortex and hippocampus); the titration with an isogenic wild-type arm is what is new. A ruler built in one cell type does not transfer to a tissue dose-response: pattern A holds only inside the system that produced the ruler. It stays at L4. It does not claim catalysis.

**One orthogonal measure (WFR-2): a binding-competence ratio, normalised to the WWOX amount in the same lysate.** Co-immunoprecipitation or pull-down of one WW1-dependent partner (a PPxY peptide or p73-type partner; control W44F/P47A, or Y33R with its limits: Y33R reduces rather than abolishes function and may be dominant-negative) and of GSK3b through the 388 to 407 region, an extreme C-terminal stretch outside the nucleotide-binding core (L404A as a binding probe), each divided by the amount of WWOX captured, wild type against the variant, in the same cells as WFR-1 and with partner expression held constant. It is an intrinsic, per-molecule L2 readout that needs no phenotype and no catalytic assumption; it tells whether a protein that appears binds at the WW end and at one C-terminal surface; neither covers the SDR core where a missense sits. Its limit: it does not show catalysis or L4 function, and a preserved binding is not a function; a lost binding with preserved abundance (the P282A pattern) is the informative result.

## 7. Required controls

1. Null background with the empty or EGFP vector under the same promoter and the same viral or integration load.
2. Wild-type titration (the ruler) in the same experiment, not a historical curve.
3. A WW1 binding-dead arm (W44F/P47A, the dead arm supported for the Tier 1 readout) and L404A as an SDR-region binding probe only: L404A is dead for GSK3b, Tau and neurite readouts and has never been shown dead (or alive) for HIF1a, glucose uptake or firing; 388 to 407 is the extreme C-terminal tail. Neither mutant is shown folded by a test that covers the SDR core (the c-Jun test covers the WW end by my inference).
3b. An isogenic uninduced wild-type arm at the endogenous level, measured on the same plate, so that over-expression (pattern D) can be called against a physiological reference.
4. An absolute abundance method independent of the detecting antibody (targeted mass spectrometry or an epitope tag), because the lesion in question is already known to hide behind one antibody on one membrane.
5. Soluble, pellet and total fractions (S/P/T), so insolubility is not read as absence.
6. Transgene mRNA per well, measured under treatment, so an HDAC-type transcriptional effect cannot be read as stabilisation.
7. The compound on the null line and on wild type; vehicle and osmotic controls where high osmolyte levels are used.
8. Pre-registered, per-arm outcomes (section 8); fixed fold cut-offs written from the ruler at registration, none invented here.
9. Independent replication units: separate inductions and passages, stated per panel; no counting of fields or cells as n.
10. No dead-catalysis control is listed because none can be defined (section 4); an exploratory triad-mutant arm is allowed only if labelled exploratory and with its own expression check.
