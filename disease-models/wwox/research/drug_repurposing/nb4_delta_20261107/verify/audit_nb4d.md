### H3 (Supp Fig 1C and 1D, my own reading of the images)
Panel C. Cortex, P17, rows S-Control and S-KO, columns Hoechst (blue), NeuN (green), WWOX (red), Merge; scale bar 20 um; one high-power field per genotype. S-Control: about 8 large NeuN-positive cell bodies, most with a ring-like (perinuclear/cytoplasmic) WWOX signal. S-KO: the WWOX channel is dim overall; four NeuN-positive cells carry white arrows in the merge (top right, mid-left, mid-right, lower middle). Not every NeuN-positive cell in the S-KO field is arrowed: at least two NeuN-positive cells in the lower part of the field (lower left and lower centre) show a residual red ring that is not arrowed. No counts, no percentage, no n stated in the legend ("Representative images are shown from the cortex region"). Does it support "WWOX loss in neurons"? Yes, in part: it shows loss of WWOX protein in the arrowed cells, one field, one age. It does not show loss in "most" neurons; the panel itself shows some NeuN-positive cells with residual signal (consistent with mosaic excision, but unquantified). About glial recombination it shows nothing: there is no glial marker and no tdTomato channel in the panel.

Panel D. Corpus callosum, P17, rows S-Control and S-KO, columns Hoechst, CC1 (green), WWOX (red), Merge; scale bar 20 um; one field per genotype. S-Control: about 10 CC1-positive cell bodies, several (about 4-5) with a bright WWOX red signal, others dim. S-KO: CC1-positive cells are fewer and less sharply stained; WWOX red signal overlaps several CC1-positive cells (the bottom-left cell has strong signal, the upper-left cell has moderate signal, and signal is also seen near the right-hand cells); WWOX is patchy. No arrows, no counts. Does it support "WWOX present in CC1-positive cells of the knockout"? Yes qualitatively, in the sampled field. It does not show that every CC1-positive cell retains WWOX, and the control row shows that not every CC1-positive cell is WWOX-bright either. About recombination: a WWOX-positive CC1 cell at P17 means the Wwox protein was not (fully) lost in that cell at P17; it cannot show that Cre never acted, nor Cre activity at an earlier stage followed by protein persistence (a long-lived protein is unlikely to matter at P17 but is not excluded), nor anything about oligodendrocyte precursors (CC1 marks mature cells), astrocytes, microglia or interneurons in S-KO. Context that makes the panel informative: panels E and F (Olig2-Cre and GFAP-Cre) show the same antibody and CC1/GFAP staining can show WWOX loss in those glia, so a null result in D is not simply an insensitive assay.

## Omitted decisive points (not in any item)

1. A reporter recombination figure is not an excision figure for floxed Wwox. Paton's 26.2% (whole-brain Southern), 36% (NeuN-high), 25% (NeuN-low) are properties of the X-linked Mecp2 lox-Stop-mCherry knock-in in hemizygous males at 6-8 weeks, which restores a protein. Repudi's floxed Wwox is an autosomal allele with loxP sites flanking exon 1, two copies in S-KO (flox/flox), at P17. Locus, loxP spacing, copy number, chromatin context and Cre-dose per allele all differ; none of Paton's percentages can be carried to Wwox excision in any cell class, and Paton cannot be used to infer a Wwox excision percentage in S-KO neurons or glia. The only Wwox-excision evidence is Repudi Supp Fig 1C and 1D (single fields, no counts); the Rosa26-tdTomato reporter that Repudi's mice carried was not reported.
2. Developmental window: Paton sampled 6-8 weeks, while S-KO mice have seizures from P9 and die at 3-4 weeks, so Paton's data say nothing about Cre activity during the period in which S-KO pathology is established; the only developmental statement (E12.5 onset) is Zhu's LacZ result, cited by both papers.
3. The Repudi text generalises from one glial cell type: "Specific WWOX ablation in neurons, but not in other cells, was validated by observing intact WWOX levels in oligodendrocytes (stained with CC1)". For S-KO there is no panel for astrocytes (GFAP), microglia, OPCs (NG2/PDGFRalpha), or neuronal subtypes such as parvalbumin interneurons; Supp Fig 1E/F test the O-KO and G-KO lines, not S-KO.
4. Background and colony: same stock number but different background (C57BL/6JCrl vs C57BL6/J;129sv mixed); Cre expression of a transgene can vary with background and copy number, so transfer of mosaicism from Paton to Repudi is an assumption, not a source statement.
5. Germline recombination: Rempe 2006 and Luo 2020 are in Paton's reference list, Paton saw weak testes activity; Repudi does not discuss it. Whether the Wwox flox allele was excised in the germline in Repudi's colony is not addressed by either paper.
6. Paton's own authors say the Syn1 line is mosaic (Fig 1 legend "variable across brain regions"; Reviewer Mandel "highly mosaic ... within and between brain regions") and that Syn1-cre "cannot be considered a pan-neuronal Cre driver" (text of the Results); Repudi's own S-KO validation shows arrowed cells only. The two sources are mutually consistent about partial neuronal excision and neither measures glia directly.
