# E-01 PX-478 (Q2 expression, opposes; HIF1A -> BNIP3)

Scientist-b4, 2026-10-30. Not medical advice. A compatible action of a context-free network is not a predicted benefit. Q2 carries the IT7_READOUT6 guard (SLC2A1, HK2, LDHA, LDHB, BNIP3, PDK1 were the IT7 held-out readout): the proposal was legitimately generated, and nothing below evaluates the compound by reversal of those six genes.

## Decision (first)

**KEEP-AS-PROPOSAL, as a mechanistic experiment about the HIF axis, not as a treatment.** Biological promise (given only the WWOX-seed link and target action): MODERATE for the axis, LOW for this compound. Depth of evidence: MEDIUM. Feasibility: MEDIUM in vitro (supply UNVERIFIED), LOW clinically. Priority of the next experiment: MEDIUM.
Distinction: this is "insufficient support for the compound", not "no activity": ChEMBL holds no activity row for PX-478, which is an absent datum, and the primary papers report HIF-1alpha inhibition in cancer cells and xenografts.

## 2. Target, action, measures
ChEMBL: 0 activity rows, 0 curated mechanism rows (NO_ROWS_UNDER_FILTERS; absent, not negative). Action is from papers: PX-478 lowers HIF-1alpha protein and transactivation in cancer lines in normoxia and hypoxia, by lowering HIF1A mRNA, inhibiting translation and, less, deubiquitination (Koh 2008, abstract); in mice it lowers HIF-1alpha in HT-29 xenografts and the HIF-1 target genes VEGF and GLUT-1 (Welsh 2004, abstract). Selectivity and off-targets: not retrievable (no rows); a mustard moiety implies alkylation as an alternative mechanism (inference from structure, not a measured fact).

## 3. Exposure and nervous-system data
Only adult phase 1 (NCT00522652, completed, minimum age 18 years); results not retrieved (a title search for a published phase I report returned no hit). No label, no half-life, no CSF or brain datum, no paediatric datum (0 paediatric registry hits). `exposure_E.tsv`.

## 4. Safety and counter-evidence
Structural alkylator alert (above); embryo-fetal and developmental toxicity of mustards is a class concern, not a measured datum for PX-478. A 2024 rat study of prenatal hypoxia and ASD-like behaviour (abstract only) reports improved behaviour and fewer hippocampal necrotic neurons with PX-478 given from one week or three weeks after birth, and also lower body weight and raised ALP and ALT after the one-week start. Seizure-provoking reports: none found (Europe PMC query PX-478 AND epilep*/seizure*/convuls* gave 17 hits, the first four are HIF papers with no seizure datum; not all opened). No FAERS (not marketed).

## 8. Experiment, readout, falsifier, transfer limits
Experiment (also carries the question of E-02): in WWOX-deficient human neural cells (KO organoids, week 8-15, or hNPC) knock down HIF1A, EPAS1, and both (primary, genetic) and add PX-478 at graded concentrations beside a non-mustard HIF-1alpha inhibitor and a vehicle arm; wild-type cells treated in parallel; add a prolyl-hydroxylase inhibitor without thyromimetic action (not roxadustat) to ask whether HIF is already saturated in KO (WWOX loss lowers HIF-1alpha hydroxylation in the MEF paper). Readouts: HIF-1alpha and HIF-2alpha protein, lactate/ECAR, glucose uptake, glycolytic module genes BEYOND the six IT7 readout genes (PGK1, ALDOA, ENO1, PFKP, PKM), SOX2/NEUN fractions, viability. Adaptive versus harmful valence: if HIF dependence lowers the module and viability or differentiation worsens, the glycolytic shift is adaptive; if differentiation or viability improves, it is harmful. Falsifier: knockdown does not move the module or lactate in KO cells, or HIF-1alpha protein is not higher in KO than WT neural cells. Transfer limits: WWOX-HIF-1alpha data are from MEFs and cancer lines; organoid HIF1A mRNA is not up; PX-478 is an IV mustard with no CNS data; human cells at the week of the phenotype are prenatal-equivalent.
