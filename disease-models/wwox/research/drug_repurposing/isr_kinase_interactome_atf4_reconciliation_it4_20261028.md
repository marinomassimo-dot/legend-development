## 2. Hussain 2018 (fly ref 56): the GCN1 entry, exactly

**Experiment.** Tandem affinity purification-mass spectrometry. Bait: full-length human WWOX with a C-terminal triple SFB tag (S-tag, FLAG, streptavidin-binding peptide), stably expressed in **HEK293T** cells, clones chosen to match endogenous WWOX level. Two purification steps (streptavidin, then S-protein beads), NETN lysis, in-gel trypsin, LTQ Velos ion trap, SEQUEST spectral counts. Two replicates. Scoring: MUSE probabilistic interaction score plus the CRAPome frequency (a database of 411 unrelated control runs); **no empty-vector or untagged-cell purification is described** in the Methods; the specificity filter is the CRAPome and the score.
**Funnel.** 795 proteins (replicate 1), 594 (replicate 2), 383 in both, 253 with CRAPome frequency below 0.2, 216 after the score filter (Supplementary File 2). The authors call the 14 preys with score above 0.3 "a very high likelihood of being direct WWOX physical binding partners" and list 18 more at 0.2-0.3 as "good candidates"; only SEC23IP, SCAMP3 and VOPP1 were validated by reciprocal co-immunoprecipitation (HEK293T, overexpressed tagged proteins, IgG control) and SEC23IP and SCAMP3 by WW1-dependent GST pull-down.
**The GCN1 row** (Data Sheet 2, serial number 94 of 217 rows, the bait being row 1; checked against Data Sheet 1): GCN1 (Entrez 10985) spectral counts 2 (replicate 1; 2 unique peptides) and 8 (replicate 2; 7 unique peptides), total 10; interaction score **0.133**; prey score 0.221; CRAPome frequency 0.160; one PPxY/F motif (residues 238-241, PPkY) and one LPxY/F motif (1296-1299, LPvF). For scale: the bait has 518 and 875 spectra; the median score of the 216 preys is 0.119; the authors' own mean score is 0.129 for preys without a WW1 motif and 0.188 for preys with one; GCN1 is below their 0.2 "good candidate" line and far below the 0.3 line. It was not validated, discussed or mentioned in the text.
**PERK and the other kinases.** EIF2AK3 (PERK), EIF2AK4 (GCN2), EIF2AK1, EIF2AK2, EIF2S1 and ATF4 do not appear in Data Sheets 1 or 2 (symbol search; the only EIF2 hit is EIF2S3L in one replicate list). Of the other translation-linked preys, valyl-tRNA synthetase 2 (score 0.40), asparagine synthetase (0.21), threonyl-tRNA synthetase (0.20) and phenylalanyl-tRNA ligase alpha (0.16) are present, and "tRNA aminoacylation" is one of the paper's enriched Reactome terms (Data Sheet 4). That is a lead for the uncharged-tRNA trigger of GCN2, not a result.
**What this supports.** GCN1 co-purified with WWOX in two out of two replicates in one engineered cell line at a low score. **It does not support "WWOX directly binds GCN1".** The words "direct" and "physical" in the paper are tied to the high-score, motif-bearing, co-IP-validated preys. A co-purification does not separate direct binding from bridging through a complex (GCN1 is a very large ribosome-associated protein; this is background knowledge, not something read here), and no step in the paper tests GCN1 with the WW1 mutants it used for other preys. Nothing in the paper concerns GCN2 activity.

## 3. Sassano 2021 (fly ref 58): what the WWOX row means

**Experiment.** BioID proximity biotinylation, not a co-IP and not a binding assay. Bait: human **PERK (EIF2AK3) fused C-terminally to the biotin ligase BirA***, on the cytosolic side, transiently expressed in **HEK293-T** cells; 50 uM biotin for 24 h; denaturing lysis (0.4% SDS, 500 mM NaCl) and streptavidin pull-down; trypsin, nano LC-MS on a Q Exactive, Mascot, Scaffold. **Control: mock-transfected parental HEK293-T cells treated alike** (not BirA* alone); the authors themselves say later BioID studies "implemented more stringent controls to detect false positives" and that a BirA*-only control was not used. Replicate number is not stated in the text read. Inclusion: at least 2 exclusive unique peptides; Table 1 lists proteins "only detected using PERK-BirA as bait and not in control". The Table 1 caption says FDR below 1% and the Methods say below 10%; the table header says 276 identified proteins and the Conclusions say 129 potential interacting partners (not reconciled in the paper).
**The WWOX row.** Q9NZC7, gene WWOX, WW domain-containing oxidoreductase, 47 kDa: **3 total spectra, 3 exclusive unique peptides, 0 in the mock control, protein identification probability 99%.** It is the correct protein (accession and gene symbol match), not a different one. It is near the foot of the list (the top hit is PERK itself at 415 spectra, then FLNA 94). It was not mentioned in the text and not tested by co-IP; only VAPB (N = 3) and SERCA2 (N = 2) were validated.
**Same list, same experiment, GCN1.** GCN1 (Q92616, 293 kDa) is also in Table 1: 10 total spectra, 10 exclusive unique peptides, 0 in control, probability 100%. So in this experiment GCN1 is a stronger PERK-proximity hit than WWOX. The list also holds ribosomal and translation-factor proteins (RPS3, RACK1, EIF4G1/2, EIF4B, EIF5, EIF5B, EPRS, EIF2A at 61%). For a PERK fusion that sits on the ER membrane and faces the cytosol, ribosome-proximal labelling is the expected background, so the GCN1 and WWOX rows are as consistent with ribosome or ER-translation proximity as with any specific complex (inference, not tested).
**What this supports.** In one overexpression experiment, a biotinylation tag on PERK labelled WWOX peptides three times with none in mock cells, i.e. WWOX was within the labelling radius (nanometre scale) of the PERK fusion or of a protein labelled in its neighbourhood, at least once. "WWOX can interact in proximity with PERK" is a fair paraphrase of the proximity list, but it is **not** evidence of binding, of PERK activation, of a direction of regulation, or of anything in a neural cell; the authors' Discussion in the fly paper uses it only as a candidate route. Over-expressed fusion protein, non-neural cell line, no WWOX-null control, single-digit counts.

**Link between the two lists, stated with its limit.** GCN1 is in the WWOX TAP-MS (score 0.133) and the PERK BioID (10 spectra); WWOX is in the PERK BioID (3 spectra). Three weak, non-validated, same-cell-line co-occurrences do not form a complex; they are what a ribosome-adjacent cluster would also produce. Neither paper tests a WWOX-PERK, WWOX-GCN1 or WWOX-GCN2 functional effect.

## 4. The Atf4 comparisons, reconciled (task 4)

Source: fly paper Results section "Wwox knockdown in the presence of Abeta42 increases lactate concentrations via Atf4", Figure 5H-I legend, Supplementary Fig 5D-G legend, Supplementary Table 1B, Methods "Statistical analysis". Genotypes: pan-neuronal elav-GAL4; Wwox TRiP RNAi versus the RNAi control (BDSC 8622); females, day 14; Atf4 immunocytochemistry (rat antibody gifted by a laboratory, ref 26), mean grey value over four ROIs per brain; the unit of analysis is the brain.

| Comparison | Background | Test, statistic | n | Reading |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Wwox RNAi vs control RNAi, Atf4 ICC (Fig 5H-I) | Abeta42 | Mann-Whitney, U = 0, P = 0.0022 | 6 brains (6 vs 6) | **Measured increase** (about 1.0 to about 1.28 normalised, read from the figure in the first reading). U = 0 is complete separation of the two groups: the smallest two-sided exact P for 6 vs 6 is 2/924 = 0.0022, which is what is reported (derived check) |
| Wwox RNAi vs control RNAi, Atf4 ICC (Supp Fig 5D-E) | LacZ (no amyloid) | two-tailed unpaired t, t = 0.0894, P = 0.9305 | 6 brains (df 10 reproduces P = 0.9305 exactly) | **Increase not observed (non-significant)** |
| Wwox RNAi vs control RNAi, Xbp1:GFP:HG reporter (Supp Fig 5F-G) | Abeta42 only | two-tailed unpaired t, t = 1.365, P = 0.1954 | "6-9" brains; df 13 reproduces P = 0.1954, so 15 brains in total (6 + 9 or 7 + 8) | **Not significant; low precision** |

What follows, and what does not:
1. **Statistically demonstrated interaction (Wwox knockdown x Abeta42) on Atf4: not shown.** The two Atf4 contrasts are separate two-group tests (a Mann-Whitney test in one and a t test in the other, each normalised to its own control); the paper reports no two-way analysis, no interaction term and no cross-arm comparison for Atf4, and the supplementary tables hold no Atf4 values. By contrast the same paper does run a two-way ANOVA with an interaction term for ATP (interaction F = 2.206, P = 0.1633) and for lactate, so the method existed and was not applied to Atf4. "Significant in one arm, not significant in the other" is not a significant difference between the arms. The supplementary legend title says the increase is "Abeta42-dependent"; the data in hand show a significant difference in one background and a non-significant one in the other. Whether the two arms were stained and imaged in the same session is not stated.
2. **Demonstrated absence of an effect without amyloid: not shown.** With 6 vs 6 and t = 0.0894 the standardised difference is 0.05 pooled SD, and the 95% interval is about +/- 1.29 pooled SD. An effect of the size seen in the amyloid arm (U = 0 means complete separation, a large standardised effect) is not excluded by the interval. "Not observed" (the Results wording) is accurate; "no change", as the ISR need and card E01 phrase it, is a stronger sentence than the test supports. Reworded in the need and card (section 7).
3. **Xbp1 reporter.** The reporter reads IRE1 RNase splicing only. t = 1.365 with df 13 is a standardised difference of about 0.7 pooled SD (95% interval about +/- 1.1 SD around it; direction not stated in the legend text read); that is a wide interval that does not exclude an IRE1 effect. The text read describes no positive control for the reporter (no tunicamycin or thapsigargin arm, no ATF6 readout), and it was run only in the amyloid background. A non-significant IRE1 reporter therefore (a) does not exclude IRE1, (b) says nothing about ATF6, and (c) **cannot be used to infer PERK**: PERK is not read by it, and IRE1 being quiet does not imply that PERK is the active arm.
4. **"PERK-Atf4 branch" (Results) and the Discussion.** The Results sentence is "These data suggest that neuronal Wwox knockdown specifically upregulates the PERK-Atf4 branch of the ISR/UPR in Aβ 42 flies"; the Discussion then says the authors "did not determine whether WWOX is activating this pathway directly" and names both PERK and GCN2. The data contain no PERK, GCN2, phospho-eIF2alpha, translation or Atf4-perturbation measurement. Because Drosophila has, to my knowledge, only two canonical eIF2alpha kinases (PERK/PEK and GCN2; not re-verified here), a by-exclusion argument would need both measured or perturbed, and neither is.
5. Atf4 ICC is a protein-level, steady-state readout at day 14 of a chronic model. It cannot tell increased translation of an existing Atf4 mRNA (the canonical ISR route) from increased transcription or stabilisation, and it says nothing about eIF2alpha phosphorylation. Antibody specificity in the fly (an Atf4/crc RNAi control) is not shown in the text read.

Summary of the four distinctions requested: **measured increase** = Atf4 in the amyloid arm (and lactate, outside this note's scope); **increase not observed** = Atf4 in the LacZ arm, Xbp1 reporter in the amyloid arm; **statistically demonstrated interaction** = none for Atf4; **demonstrated absence** = none for either arm.

## 5. Review of the experiment proposed in the 2026-10-28 note (task 5)

The earlier design (same-lysate western for phospho-GCN2, phospho-PERK, phospho-eIF2alpha, ATF4; histidinol/halofuginone and thapsigargin controls; a GCN2 and a PERK inhibitor; puromycin) is a sound start, but it does not by itself discriminate in the cases that matter. Problems found:
1. **Inhibitors are not clean attributions.** GSK2606414 (and its analogues) inhibits RIPK1 and KIT at concentrations used in cells and tissues (Rojas-Rivera et al. 2017; from memory, not re-checked here); GCN2iB and other ATP-competitive GCN2 inhibitors can **activate** GCN2 at sub-saturating concentrations (Szaruga et al. 2023; from memory), so a dose that "lowers phospho-eIF2alpha" and one that raises it can coexist; C16 for PKR also inhibits cyclin-dependent kinases; ISRIB acts on eIF2B **downstream of all four kinases**, so it cannot say which is engaged (its legitimate use is the opposite one: whether ATF4 induction is eIF2alpha-phosphorylation-dependent at all).
2. **Positive controls are not specific.** Halofuginone inhibits prolyl-tRNA synthetase (hence GCN2) but also acts on TGF-beta/SMAD signalling; histidinol is a histidyl-tRNA synthetase competitor and works only in low-histidine medium; arsenite triggers HRI but also PKR, oxidative and MAPK responses; thapsigargin is a PERK activator but also depletes ER calcium, which acts on mitochondria and other kinases. Each positive control needs its own kinase-knockout confirmation in the same cell line.
3. **A pattern "p-GCN2 up, p-PERK flat" is not read in the old note against basal tone.** Kinase loss lowers phospho-eIF2alpha in wild-type cells too (in the PARS2 precedent, GCN2 RNAi alone lowered phospho-eIF2alpha below control). The attribution test is an **interaction**: the WWOX-dependent excess is removed by loss of kinase X and not by loss of the others.
4. **Phospho-antibody reliability.** Phospho-PERK (T980) and phospho-GCN2 (T899) antibodies are used with variable specificity; they need kinase-knockout lysates, and a mobility-shift (Phos-tag) readout for PERK and GCN2 is more reliable than the antibody alone. Phospho-eIF2alpha is lost within minutes of slow dissection or lysis without phosphatase inhibitors.
5. **"Both flat, ATF4 up points to HRI/PKR" is too quick.** ATF4 protein can rise without eIF2alpha phosphorylation (mTORC1-driven translation, transcriptional induction), and HRI (mitochondrial stress through OMA1-DELE1) is a first-line candidate in a model whose reported change is metabolic, not a leftover. HRI and PKR need their own readouts (DELE1 short form; phospho-PKR with a dsRNA control), not exclusion.
6. **Timing and context were absent.** eIF2alpha phosphorylation is transient (GADD34 and CReP feedback); in a chronic lesion phospho-eIF2alpha can return to baseline while ATF4 targets stay changed, and the converse. The fly signal needed amyloid; the one human knockout transcriptome set sits lower than chance without amyloid and is confounded by maturity. A single time point in unchallenged cells would repeat the ambiguity.
7. **Unit of replication and clone effect** were not stated: wells from one clone are not replicates of a genotype.
8. **WWOX-dependence needs a rescue arm at endogenous level**: overexpression of WWOX has effects of its own.

## 6. Revised design (design only; nothing authorised)

**Questions, in order.** Q1 is the ISR (phospho-eIF2alpha, ATF4 protein and targets, translation) raised by WWOX deficiency in a neural system, in which context? Q2 which eIF2alpha kinase carries it? Q3 is it WWOX-dependent (rescue) and necessary for a phenotype (only if Q1 is positive)? Q4 (separate) does WWOX associate with GCN1 or PERK in a way that is not ribosome-bridged?

**A. Fly arm (cheapest; uses the authors' system and closes the statistical gap first).**
- Full 2 x 2: elav-GAL4 Wwox RNAi vs control RNAi, in Abeta42 vs LacZ, all four cells stained, imaged and analysed together; the pre-specified test is the interaction term (two-way model), not two t tests. Add a Wwox mRNA check in every arm and a second independent Wwox RNAi line.
- In the Wwox RNAi + Abeta42 background add Perk (PEK) RNAi/mutant and Gcn2 RNAi/mutant (two independent lines each), with a neutral UAS-RNAi or UAS-LacZ in the other arms to equalise the number of UAS constructs (GAL4 dilution), and an Atf4 (crc) RNAi arm that also validates the antibody. Readouts: Atf4 ICC, phospho-eIF2alpha (antibody validated in fly lysate), Ldh and lactate (the authors' downstream readouts). Note that GCN2 loss can raise PERK activity (the 5xFAD precedent cited by the paper, its ref 60), so chronic kinase loss may compensate; keep one acute or conditional arm if a conditional driver is available.
- Rapid snap-freeze or fixation of heads/brains for any phospho readout.

**B. Human arm (decides Q1-Q3 for the disease context).**
- Cells: isogenic WWOX-knockout and WWOX-corrected (or inducible, near-endogenous re-expression) lines, at least two independent clones per genotype, neural progenitors and differentiated neurons (and astrocytes) at **matched maturity** (a maturity marker panel in every sample), three or more independent differentiations as the unit of replication, clone nested in genotype in the model. Public-edition scope: genotype class only, no individual linkage.
- Conditions: vehicle; a defined amyloid-beta oligomer preparation (characterised, with a scrambled or reverse peptide control); a non-amyloid proteotoxic challenge (low-dose tunicamycin) and a nutrient challenge (amino-acid limitation); times 1-4 h, 24 h and chronic (days), so that transient and persistent ISR are both seen. WWOX genotype x challenge is the pre-specified interaction, as in the fly.
- Same-lysate readouts: phospho-eIF2alpha/total (rapid lysis, phosphatase inhibitors), ATF4 protein, ATF4 targets by qPCR (ASNS, CHAC1, TRIB3, DDIT3, SESN2, PHGDH), global translation (a puromycin-incorporation assay or a puromycin analogue assay), and kinase-state readouts that do not rely on one antibody: PERK and GCN2 mobility shift plus phospho-specific blots checked in knockout lysates, DELE1 short form for the HRI route, phospho-PKR with a dsRNA control. Mitochondrial covariates (OXPHOS subunit abundance, a mitochondrial-stress marker), because the one precedent ISR here is mitochondrial in origin, and tRNA charging (acid-urea northern) plus amino-acid levels as the trigger for GCN2.
- **Kinase attribution by genetics first, tools second.** In both genotypes, knock out or inducibly knock down EIF2AK3, EIF2AK4, EIF2AK1 and EIF2AK2 (two guides each, non-targeting control), including an inducible acute arm. Read the genotype x kinase interaction on phospho-eIF2alpha and ATF4. Re-expression of the wild-type versus a kinase-dead allele of the implicated kinase confirms specificity.
- Pharmacology as support only, two structurally different compounds per target, a dose-response (not one dose), and the target-engagement readout beside the effect: PERK (two unrelated inhibitor chemotypes; show loss of the PERK shift; test the RIPK1 liability with a RIPK1 inhibitor); GCN2 (full dose-response, because of paradoxical activation, plus a GCN2-knockout line as the reference); ISRIB used to ask whether ATF4 induction depends on eIF2alpha phosphorylation at all (ISRIB-sensitive ATF4 = eIF2alpha-P-dependent route; ISRIB-insensitive = another route).
- Positive controls in every line, each confirmed in the matching kinase knockout: thapsigargin (PERK), histidinol or halofuginone with proline rescue or leucine-free medium (GCN2), arsenite or a mitochondrial stressor (HRI), dsRNA (PKR).

**C. Binding check (Q4; only if the ISR arm points to GCN2 or PERK).** Reciprocal co-IP of endogenous or tagged proteins with and without RNase and with a ribosome-release condition, to separate a ribosome-bridged association from a direct one; the WW1-mutant (W44F/P47A, used in Hussain's GST pull-downs) and a GCN1 PPkY (238-241) mutant as specificity controls; an IgG and a non-related WW-domain-bait control.

**Decision table (pre-registered before the experiment; thresholds are the experimenter's, stated in advance, not set here).**

| Observed | Reading | Not shown by it |
|---|---|---|
| Phospho-eIF2alpha and ATF4 not raised in the knockout at any time or challenge, with intact positive controls | no ISR engagement in this system; the compound strand stays parked | absence in vivo, in other cell types, or in a different challenge |
| Raised; the WWOX-dependent excess is removed by loss of kinase X only (interaction), rescued by WWOX re-expression | kinase X engaged in this system | that X is direct, or that the response is harmful |
| Raised; no single kinase loss removes it, a double loss does | redundancy | which is primary |
| ATF4 raised, phospho-eIF2alpha flat, ISRIB-insensitive | eIF2alpha-P-independent ATF4 induction | any eIF2 kinase |
| Raised in knockout, not removed by WWOX re-expression | clone or culture artefact, not WWOX-dependent | |
| Raised only with the amyloid challenge (interaction positive) | context-conditional ISR as in the fly | a WWOX-DEE disease mechanism |

Whichever way the result goes, an ISR-attenuating compound is a hypothesis only after Q1 is positive; GCN2 and PERK stay unprioritised until Q2 is answered.

## 9. What is certain, what is not

Certain (from the sources read): the GCN1 row in Hussain 2018 is at interaction score 0.133 from 10 spectra over two replicates in HEK293T with no stated empty-vector control and no validation; the WWOX row in Sassano 2021 is a three-spectrum BioID hit in HEK293-T against a mock control, and GCN1 is a stronger hit in the same list; the fly Atf4 rise is significant in the amyloid arm only as a separate two-group test and no interaction test exists; no kinase, phospho-eIF2alpha or translation measurement exists in the fly paper.
Not certain: whether a WWOX-dependent ISR exists in any neural system; which kinase; whether WWOX and GCN1 or PERK are in a common complex; whether the Atf4 difference between backgrounds is real as an interaction; the direction of the Xbp1 reporter difference (not stated in the text read).
Two literature statements above are from memory and marked as such (RIPK1 off-target of GSK2606414; paradoxical activation by GCN2 ATP-competitive inhibitors) and should be verified before they are used in any record beyond this note.
