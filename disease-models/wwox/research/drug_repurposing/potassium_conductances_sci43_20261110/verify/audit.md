**Q02 (single-conductance arithmetic).** Derivation: at steady state V = sum(g_i E_i)/G. With one conductance changed by dG (negative for a loss) with reversal E_x and everything else fixed, V1 G1 = V0 G0 + dG E_x, hence E_x = V0 + dV G1/dG, G = 1/Rin. This is the script's formula. Every row of `implied_reversal_points.tsv` reproduces (see `verify/recompute_implied_reversal.tsv`): KO -93.7, HT -88.8, Fmr1 -91.6, Ba2+ -83.4, Kcnq2 -73.7, L3 -79.2/-76.3, L5 -72.5/-74.8, ZD7288 -1.9. Assumptions that can invalidate it, in the order they matter:
1. **The reading is scale-dependent.** E_x is on the recorded scale (recorded = true + delta). E_K true is -97.0 mV for 135 mM/3.5 mM at 35 C (producer -97.5); on the recorded scale it is -97.0 + delta. For delta 0/8/13/16 mV the KO implied reversal corrects to -93.7/-101.7/-106.7/-109.7: it is below E_K for delta of 4 mV or more. A pure K+ loss predicts dV of +6.9/+3.8/+1.9/+0.7 mV against the observed +5.6 +/- 2.4. So "lies in the K+ range" (KH1) is true only for a small offset; with the producer's bracket it is not.
2. **Algebraic equivalence with E3+E4.** Shrinking the membrane (all channels scale) with a fixed shunt of reversal 0 gives E_x equal to the membrane's intrinsic rest. The Wwox pair is reproduced exactly by gs = 1.50 nS (15 percent of WT G; seal resistance about 0.67 Gohm), membrane G x0.668, intrinsic rest -93.7. So a K-like number is a property of any combination that leaves a membrane rest near E_K; it cannot choose between E2 and E3+E4.
3. **Propagated uncertainty (rough, independent normal draws on printed mean and SEM, 400 000 draws).** KO -93.6, 95 percent interval -142.8 to -77.5; HT -88.5, -149.4 to -66.2. P(E_x between -80 and -72) is 0.04 (KO) and 0.12 (HT); the developmental intervals are L3 P7-P14 -94.6 to -73.2, L3 P14-P28 -83.2 to -71.4, L5 -76.9 to -68.8 and -85.6 to -69.2. The KO interval therefore covers L3 P7-P14 (-79.2) and the lower end of the others; the HT interval covers all of them. What the data do allow: E_x more negative than about -77 (KO, 95 percent) rules out a lost depolarising conductance of the Ih or cation type (their reversal near -30 to 0) and a pure maturation-like gain of the -72 to -76 kind at 95 percent for KO only; nothing finer.
4. **Calibration rows do not support the sharpness.** Fmr1 error +2 mV against a measured -89.6 (preprint, unverifiable here); Ba2+ block +6 mV against the same measured reversal (-83.4 vs -89.6); Kcnq2 cKO (a known K+ channel loss) returns -73.7 with 95 percent interval -105 to -60 and the K+ prediction is dV +9 mV (E_K about -103, corrected scale) against +1.6 (p 0.2). The ZD7288 row (baseline read off a panel, dG = -0.45 nS from a +8 Mohm difference printed with p 0.035) is ill-conditioned: halving the difference moves E_x from -1.9 to about +74 mV. Remove that row or show an interval; it is not a calibration.
5. **Other limits:** slope conductance (Rin from the I-V slope of an unspecified step range, includes Ih and Kir rectification) versus the chord conductance that sets rest; steady state; Rin and rest measured at different times (rest in the 50 ms before the first step); differences of group means; the Kcnq2 order of rest values (control -67.3, cKO -65.7) is as quoted but the source full text was unreachable to me; developmental rows come from a different preparation (prelimbic, 31 C, 148 K-gluconate/1 KCl, male, LJP not stated), so only the difference to the Wwox row, not the value against E_K, is meaningful, and that difference (15-20 mV on point estimates) sits inside the Wwox interval.
Statement "the Wwox implied reversal lies in the K+ range and apart from the maturation values": **not a fair reading**; write "point estimates -93.7 (KO) and -88.8 (HT) on the recorded scale; 95 percent intervals -143 to -77 and -149 to -66; below E_K on the recorded scale for any offset above about 4 mV; also produced by a smaller cell with a fixed shunt; no variance-aware discrimination from the developmental values".

**Q03 (KH4 ratio argument).** Partly sound. * **Does the ladder separate E2, E3, E4, E5?** Step 0 (capacitance, tau, access resistance, junction potential, selection) separates E3/E4 from E2 only if capacitance is measured from the capacitive transient or a membrane test and the selection rule is stated: the Wwox Methods confirm pyramidal identity "by comparison of ... input resistance, and membrane time constant", a selection on the compared variables. Add a higher-Ba2+ or K2P-tool step, or say that O2/O4 close only the tested families.
* **Dynamic clamp (step 5).** What it separates: a K+ reversal (hyperpolarising, rest moves toward E_K) from a depolarising shunt (reversal 0: Rin falls, rest depolarises). A better control is a leak with reversal at the WT rest (about -79 recorded): it moves Rin without moving rest, isolating the two effects. What it does not separate: the cause. Any hyperpolarising conductance restores Rin and rest in a smaller cell or a leaky patch; restoration is sufficiency, not a lesion. O1's "dynamic clamp restores" therefore does not weaken E3/E4; only the per-pF current and capacitance comparisons do. A linear injected conductance also lacks Kir rectification, Kv7 gating and dendritic distribution, and its reversal must be set on the recorded scale with the measured junction potential. **Fix the tau prediction**: after restoration to WT Rin the tau is C x Rin: 18.5 ms if C is WT (180 pF), 15.3 ms if C is KO's (148.6 pF). "tau stays flat" (O7, step 5) is not what either predicts; compare restored tau with WT tau and measure C directly.
* **Benefit, rescue, recommendation, clinical preference?** No dose, schedule or clinical preference. Benefit/usefulness statements: KH1 and KH2 "usefulness_without_wwox_repair: Yes"; `function_that_could_improve` wording ("improve") and "bursts may fall" are expectation statements with no source; the S0 caution that normalising a measure is not benefit is not carried into those cells.
