## Decision

1. DATO — Among children imaged during vigabatrin exposure for infantile spasms the brain-MRI
   abnormality is common and reproducible: 22.1 percent of 172 and 32.5 percent of 77 at one
   centre, 28.1 percent of 64 and 44.7 percent of 76 at two others — three independent centres,
   not four. The rates are per imaged child: where the exposed denominator is given it falls to
   about 26 percent, one cohort excluded 143 exposed children with no eligible examination, and
   the pattern also occurs unexposed (7 of 52 in one cohort; 4 percent of naive infants in a
   controlled study). Rows B1-01, B1-08, B1-11, B1-13, B1-35, B1-39.
2. DATO — It is mostly clinically silent and the imaging mostly resolves: 65.8 percent
   asymptomatic of 38, 25 of 44 in a second series, resolution in all 13 with a follow-up
   examination in a third, and an optic-nerve diffusion metric that fell during exposure and rose
   after it stopped. Resolution is reported both after discontinuation and under continued
   exposure, so reversal is not shown to be conditional on stopping. Rows B1-06, B1-14, B1-16,
   B1-18, B1-35, B1-39, B1-41.
3. DATO plus INFERENZA — In humans the age band is supported, not contested. A controlled,
   blinded, ten-site study found the abnormality in exposed against naive infants and no
   difference between exposed and naive children aged 2 to 16 and adults; three further cohorts
   make younger age at exposure or at examination a risk factor, one of them with the shorter
   therapy duration in those affected; one argues age dependence from its absence in adults. The
   one cohort read here at full text found no factor other than a protective co-medication, but
   its exposure began almost entirely under 12 months, so its null cannot test a within-infancy
   window, and the diffusion study tested no risk factor at all. In animals the window dependence
   rests on abstract-level rat and dog studies whose full texts were unreachable. Rows B1-02,
   B1-03, B1-18, B1-39, B1-40, B1-41, B1-42.
4. DATO (abstract level) — Reversibility is endpoint-specific, not global: in the immature rat
   the qualitative images and the vacuolation resolved after a drug-free period while regional T2
   stayed elevated in the hypothalamus, myelin content recovered only partially and behaviour
   stayed abnormal; in the adult rat microvacuolation had not fully resolved after twelve recovery
   weeks. Rows B1-23, B1-25.
5. DATO — The only cohort-level human test of the transfer question did not demonstrate an
   excess: in 76 children a delayed-myelination baseline did not appear to predispose, alongside
   aetiology, microcephaly and oral steroids — at abstract level, graded from routine reports,
   with no stated power, and in a cohort where 14 of 34 cases had originally been reported as
   normal. In the opposite direction, one case report describes the pattern arising on a baseline
   of delayed myelination and callosal hypoplasia. Rows B1-09, B1-10, B1-19.
6. DATO — The hormonal confounder is unresolved and the record is split, not clean: hormonal
   co-therapy travels with the symptomatic form (all 11 symptomatic cases in one cohort, and a
   second cohort of the same group and centre), while its association with the imaging finding
   itself is absent in two cohorts and present for oral prednisolone in univariable analysis in a
   third, with a wide confidence interval. Rows B1-09, B1-11, B1-12, B1-35, B1-36.
7. DATO — No white-matter measurement for a sibling is reachable by an open route: the CPP-115
   package stated in 2012 that no histological data existed, its only juvenile study scored
   cortical and hippocampal injury, and neither OV329 article's main text carries myelin
   vocabulary (the histological detail of one sits in an unretrieved supplement). A standard
   toxicology package for OV329 exists and is stated to be described in two subscription-only
   conference reports, so this endpoint is open by blockage, not demonstrated absent; the one new
   sibling datum reached is an ocular and brain accumulation comparison in mice, which measures no
   myelin endpoint. Rows B1-32, B1-33, B1-34, B1-43.
8. IPOTESI — A raised-GABA axis in a myelinating brain is compatible with the record but is not
   a signed axis and does not displace the molecule. For it: GABA alone reproduced a mild
   myelinated-fibre toxicity in culture (without producing intramyelinic oedema), cerebrospinal
   GABA was higher in two cases (both preterm with post-haemorrhagic hydrocephalus, so injury and
   GABA are not separable), and two genetic conditions that raise GABA show reduced myelination
   (in one, gamma-hydroxybutyrate correlates almost as strongly). Against it: GABA-A signalling
   reduces oligodendrocyte lineage number and myelination while GABA-B signalling accelerates
   precursor differentiation and promotes remyelination, so raising extracellular GABA engages two
   receptor classes with opposite documented signs; and vigabatrin has a molecule-specific
   mechanism of its own — it depletes taurine and inhibits taurine transport, and taurine
   deficiency is reported as the cause of its retinal lesion. One in-vivo dissociation runs
   against a simple class reading: in an autoimmune demyelination model exogenous GABA aggravated
   disease while vigabatrin did not. The discriminator is a matched-engagement comparison of two
   inactivators with receptor-subtype arms; no source read measures it. Rows B1-17, B1-28, B1-29,
   B1-30, B1-31, B1-44, B1-45, B1-46, B1-47, B1-48.
9. INFERENZA — The counter-evidence rows that outside-WWOX harm justifies should be added to
   card H02 and the position left to be computed by the module, not authored: the imaging
   liability for vigabatrin is established in three independent centres and the immature-animal
   myelin endpoint recovers only partially, while for CPP-115 and OV329 the white-matter endpoint
   is unmeasured by any reachable route, so the siblings stay UNKNOWN on it and never share
   vigabatrin's rows. A card-level C3 for vigabatrin remains conditional on the added injury in a
   Wwox-deficient model that the work order registered in advance. No retinal advantage is
   asserted here: it rests on an electroretinogram in rats and one accumulation study, and the
   sibling safety claim carries reviewer doubt in a published peer-review record.
10. IPOTESI — A hypomyelinating baseline could cut either way, and the discriminating experiment
    is a start-window series in a surviving juvenile model with three separate readouts — imaging
    including a quantitative measure, vacuolation, and myelin content — because the two immature-
    and adult-rat studies show those readouts do not recover together while the human record
    covers imaging alone; a blinded-reading arm matters as much, since an abnormal baseline can
    hide the finding, and the receptor-subtype conflict of line 8 gives the series a second
    discriminating arm. Rows B1-07, B1-10, B1-23, B1-25, B1-44, B1-45.

## Evidence by theme

**Incidence and who gets it.** Four exposed cohorts from three centres, one case series, and one
controlled cross-age study. Rates among imaged children: 22.1 percent of 172 and 32.5 percent of
77 (one department, eight shared authors, overlapping years — one centre, two reports), 28.1
percent of 64, 44.7 percent of 76. Where the exposed denominator is stated, 76 of a treated
cohort of about 132 were imaged, so the rate per exposed child is about 26 percent. Unexposed
comparators exist and matter: 7 of 52 untreated children carried the same pattern in one cohort,
4 percent of naive infants in the controlled study, none of 56 unexposed children with the same
indication in another, and all 12 unexposed children normal in a fourth. Aetiology modifies the
rate: 10 percent with tuberous sclerosis against 32.6 percent with other causes; cryptogenic
infantile spasms more likely in another cohort; genetic aetiology in half the cases of a 2026
cohort whose authors call the signal hypothesis-generating. The earliest finding reported is 30
days after start, the peak three to six months after start, and the median age at diagnosis in a
44-case series is under seven months. Rows B1-01, B1-04, B1-08, B1-11, B1-13, B1-16, B1-35,
B1-39, B1-40, B1-41, B1-42.

**Exposure dependence.** Split, and not resolved by depth. Peak exposure, not cumulative, was
associated with the asymptomatic form in one cohort and was the only risk factor in another; a
higher peak amount appears again in two further cohorts. Against that: peak exposure was not
associated in the largest cohort, nor in the same group's later cohort, nor between the two
exposed arms of the diffusion study, and in the largest cohort 37 of 38 cases occurred inside the
conventional range the authors name. Duration and cumulative exposure were unassociated wherever
tested, and in one cohort the children with the finding had the shorter therapy duration. Rows
B1-02, B1-03, B1-12, B1-13, B1-18, B1-36, B1-40, B1-42.

**Age and window.** In humans the age band is the best-supported risk factor in this literature:
a controlled, blinded, ten-site comparison positive in infants and null in children aged 2 to 16
and in adults; younger age at examination with shorter exposure in a second study; age 12 months
or less in a third; younger initiation and age under 24 months in a fourth; and an explicit
age-dependence argument from the absence of the finding in adults in a fifth. The one full-text
cohort that found no age effect enrolled almost only infants, so it cannot test a within-infancy
window. In animals: rat lesion location follows the myelination timetable and the dog lesion
appears when dosing starts earlier and not later — both abstract level, already on card H02 and
unreachable at full text here. In the immature rat, exposure during the myelination window
reduced myelin staining, myelin and oligodendrocyte enzyme activity and myelin basic protein,
and at low exposure produced myelin loss, axonal degeneration, glial death and astrogliosis
without any vacuolation — two distinct lesions, not one. Rows B1-02, B1-08, B1-23, B1-24, B1-39,
B1-40, B1-41, B1-42.

**Reversibility.** Imaging resolves — completely in all 13 children with a follow-up examination,
region by region in the 12 examined after discontinuation of a 43-child series, in a case eight
months after gradual withdrawal, and as a rise in optic-nerve fractional anisotropy within about
three months. But resolution is also reported under continued exposure (the majority of
abnormalities in the controlled study, and one child in another cohort), so stopping is not
established as the operative variable. Tissue does not resolve as cleanly: microvacuolation
persisted after a twelve-week recovery in the adult rat; in the immature rat myelin content
recovered only partially, behaviour stayed abnormal and regional T2 stayed elevated. Rows B1-14,
B1-18, B1-19, B1-23, B1-25, B1-35, B1-39, B1-41.

**Clinical correlates and their absence.** Asymptomatic in the majority (25 of 38; 25 of 44).
Where symptoms occur they are movement disorder (ten of thirteen symptomatic cases in one
cohort), lethargy, encephalopathy and, once, respiratory distress or arrest. A multicentre trial
cohort found the imaging changes in two infants with a movement disorder and in three of ten
comparison infants and concluded the changes are not specifically related to the movement
disorder. Rows B1-06, B1-15, B1-16, B1-20.

**Mechanism: a two-sided axis and a molecule-specific rival.** On the harm side of the GABA axis:
a myelinating explant culture showed mild myelinated-fibre toxicity from vigabatrin and from GABA
alike, with no intramyelinic oedema in either; cerebrospinal free GABA was higher in the two
children with the abnormality than in those without; a genetic block of GABA catabolism gives
dysmyelination greater than in healthy controls, in the dentate nucleus, basal ganglia and
periatrial white matter — the regions of the drug finding; biallelic loss of the drug's own target
was associated with decreased myelination in one infant; and endogenous GABA acting on GABA-A
receptors reduces oligodendrocyte lineage number and myelination in developing cortex slices. On
the other side: GABA and a GABA-B agonist accelerate precursor differentiation and raise myelin
protein, a GABA-B agonist promotes remyelination in vivo after an induced lesion, and in an
autoimmune demyelination model vigabatrin itself did not worsen the outcome while exogenous GABA
did. And the molecule has a mechanism of its own: it depletes plasma taurine, taurine
supplementation reduces every component of its retinal lesion, darkness prevents that lesion, and
it inhibits taurine uptake in cell models. Rows B1-17, B1-28, B1-29, B1-30, B1-31, B1-44, B1-45,
B1-46, B1-47, B1-48.

**The siblings.** The CPP-115 medicinal-chemistry package states in its own words that no
histological data existed for the intramyelinic oedema endpoint. Its only juvenile exposure study
dosed rat pups from postnatal day 4 and perfused them at postnatal day 20, but scored cortical and
hippocampal injury only, and the highest of its three arms was uniformly lethal within days.
Neither OV329 full text contains the words myelin, vacuolation, white matter or intramyelinic,
both used adult animals, and the published peer-review record of one carries reviewer doubt about
the claimed safety advantage. The 2026 progress report states that the standard toxicology studies
of OV329 are described in an earlier conference report — subscription-only, therefore unread — and
adds one new datum: in a two-day mouse infusion OV329 was below the assay's lower limit of
quantitation in brain, eyeball and retina while vigabatrin was measurable in all three, with a
retina-to-plasma ratio of about 4.3. That is an accumulation comparison, not a myelin endpoint,
and the sibling was below quantitation in brain as well, so it supports no brain inference either
way. Rows B1-32, B1-33, B1-34, B1-43.

## Counter-evidence (rows with `role = counter`)

* Human neuropathology against the animal lesion: 62 people examined after long-term exposure with
  no microvacuolation or sheath splitting attributable to the drug and no demyelination (ages not
  stated in that abstract); five earlier human specimens likewise negative, with the animal lesion
  reported absent in primates; and a negative autopsy in a term infant exposed from the neonatal
  period with a malformed brain (B1-21, B1-22, B1-27).
* The only cohort-level human test of the transfer question did not demonstrate an excess (B1-09).
* Exposure intensity and duration are unassociated in several cohorts, and in one the children with
  the finding had the shorter duration (B1-02, B1-03, B1-40).
* The clinical sign and the imaging finding dissociate (B1-15).
* In the one model with pre-existing myelin pathology in which the drug was given, exogenous GABA
  worsened disease while the drug did not (B1-29).
* The GABA axis is unsigned: GABA-B engagement moves myelination the other way, in culture and in
  vivo (B1-45, B1-46), and a molecule-specific taurine mechanism explains the drug's
  better-characterised tissue lesion (B1-47, B1-48).
* Detection works against every count in both directions: an abnormal baseline may hide the
  finding (the authors' own speculation, B1-07) and 14 of 34 case examinations had been reported as
  normal before review (B1-10).
* The sibling endpoint is blocked rather than absent (B1-43), which weakens rather than supports a
  clean-sibling reading.
* Both bounded nulls are counter rows (B1-37, B1-38) and are declared as attestations below.

## Transfer conditions, and what breaks the transfer

Transfers to a WWOX-DEE infant:

1. Age band. The liability is concentrated in the first two years and is absent from the
   exposed-against-naive comparison in older children and adults; WWOX-DEE treatment sits inside
   the positive band.
2. Exposure intensity rather than accumulated amount is the better-supported lever, and the
   finding can appear within the first month and peaks three to six months after the start.
3. Any count in this disease must be read against an aetiology stratum and an unexposed
   comparator, never against a pooled rate, and per exposed child rather than per imaged child.
4. Reversal must be claimed per endpoint — imaging yes, tissue incompletely, myelin content
   partially in the immature animal — and must not be stated as conditional on stopping.

Breaks the transfer:

1. A baseline abnormality is a detection problem before it is a biology problem; no cohort has a
   reader-blinded re-review in children whose baseline MRI is already abnormal.
2. The only cohort-level test of a delayed-myelination baseline did not demonstrate an excess, at
   abstract level, in 76 children, with myelination graded from routine reports.
3. Species. The rodent and dog lesions are the sheath-splitting kind; the human infant evidence is
   imaging, with intramyelinic oedema at post-mortem in very few cases; the animal lesion was
   reported absent in primates.
4. Aetiology and co-therapy are entangled in every cohort: the group with the lowest rate also had
   a protective co-medication, and the symptomatic form travels with hormonal therapy.
5. No denominator exists for WWOX-DEE children exposed, imaged and followed.
6. The mechanism is unsigned. A GABA-raising exposure cannot be assumed harmful to myelin on
   present evidence, and a retina-sparing sibling cannot be assumed myelin-sparing; the sibling's
   endpoint is blocked, not clean.

## What is not established

* Whether a WWOX-deficient juvenile brain is more, equally or less susceptible. Nothing has
  measured it.
* Whether the liability is a class effect of GABA-aminotransferase inactivation or specific to
  vigabatrin. The axis is two-sided by receptor class and a molecule-specific taurine mechanism is
  documented for the drug's other tissue lesion; no matched-engagement comparison exists.
* Whether hormonal co-therapy causes the imaging finding, modifies its symptoms, or marks disease
  severity. No primary report was found that attributes the deep-grey and brainstem pattern to
  hormonal therapy given alone (query Q11), and the imaging association is split two to one.
* Whether the myelin-content deficit that recovers only partially in the immature rat has any
  human counterpart. **No human study read measures myelin content or vacuolation under
  exposure**; the nearest is a quantitative optic-nerve diffusion metric, in the tract that is also
  the retinotoxicity target.
* Whether the aetiology signal of the 2026 cohort reflects genes, referral or imaging practice.
  Its authors say it is hypothesis-generating.
* **Whether the two children of the WWOX imaging report are inside that 64-child aetiology
  cohort.** They cannot be excluded on the published record: the two reports share a first author
  and an institution and the cohort's intake window precedes the report. Until package A1 or the
  Orchestrator settles it, the genetic-aetiology signal of row B1-11 must **not** be used as
  independent corroboration of the WWOX report — it may be partly the same observation. Row B1-11
  and the module proposal that rests on it carry this caveat.
