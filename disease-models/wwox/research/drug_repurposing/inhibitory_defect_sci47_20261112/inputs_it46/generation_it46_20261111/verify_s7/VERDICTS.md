## R1. Reversal potential of GABA-A in neocortical layer II/III pyramidal neurons around P13 to P17

Verdict (a) versus (b): **PARTLY (a, with a stated nuance)**. Chloride homeostasis in wild-type mouse neocortical layer II/III is reported as mature (inhibitory) in the third postnatal week; the P13 to P17 window is the late end of the switch, not clearly past it.

Primary evidence, FULL: Yuan et al., Sci Rep 2019, "Delayed maturation of GABAergic signaling in the Scn1a and Scn1b mouse models of Dravet Syndrome", PMC6470170, PMID 30996233, DOI 10.1038/s41598-019-42191-0.

* Design: acute slices, layer II/III neocortical and hippocampal pyramidal cells, gramicidin perforated patch, mice. Cortical and hippocampal cells were pooled because "We found no significant differences in EGABA between cortical and hippocampal pyramidal cells within each genotype" (Results, "Scn1b-/- neurons have a depolarized reversal potential for GABA").
* Wild-type EGABA, quote (same section, Fig. 4C-D): "The mean EGABA (-53.3 +/- 3.0 mV, n = 20) of neurons in slices from P13-19 Scn1b-/- mice was significantly more depolarized than in slices from age-matched WT mice (-72.4 +/- 4.3 mV, n = 10, p < 0.01 ...)". Age split, same paragraph: "the mean EGABA values are -66.7 +/- 5.6 mV (n = 5) for WT and -51.1 +/- 3.6 mV (n = 12) for -/- neurons at P13-17 and -77.8 +/- 5.9 mV (n = 5) for WT and -57.9 +/- 6.7 mV (n = 6) for -/- neurons at P18-19."
* Second model, Fig. 7C-D: Scn1a WT, P13-21, "-69.7 +/- 3.7 mV, n = 6" versus "-58.2 +/- 3.5 mV, n = 8" in +/-.
* Excitatory early stage (Fig. 1, P4-7, layer II/III and CA1/CA3, K-gluconate low-Cl pipette, holding -70 mV): spontaneous inward events were "bicuculline-sensitive inward currents" "reflecting excitatory GABAergic responses". This is a synaptic-current readout, not a measured EGABA.
* Resting and threshold values in the same paper (Results, last paragraph before Discussion): resting potential -76.6 +/- 2.0 mV (Scn1b -/-, perforated patch) and -70.4 +/- 3.4 mV (Scn1a +/-); AP threshold in Scn1b -/- -54.4 +/- 3.1 mV. A wild-type resting potential is **not reported**.
* Inference: wild-type EGABA at P13 to P17 (about -67 mV, n = 5, pooled with hippocampus) sits about at the resting potential of the mutant cells (-70 to -77 mV) and well below spike threshold, so GABA is inhibitory (hyperpolarising or shunting) but the driving force at rest is small; by P18 to P19 (-77.8 mV) it is clearly hyperpolarising. The numbers are small (n = 5 per age bin) and pooled over two regions. Statement of the switch in the paper's Introduction (literature statement, not a measurement): "KCC2 ... is expressed at low levels at birth and then upregulated to adult levels by the end of the second postnatal week in rodent brain".
* Rat neocortex, ABSTRACT only (PMID 15090604, J Physiol 2004, Yamada et al., DOI 10.1113/jphysiol.2004.062471): gramicidin-perforated patch plus single-cell RT-PCR; "NKCC1 mRNA expression was high in early postnatal days, but decreased during postnatal development, whereas KCC2 mRNA expression displayed the opposite pattern"; the abstract gives no ages or EGABA values. Full text: NOT_REACHED (not open in Europe PMC).
* Caveat on compartment, ABSTRACT only (PMID 28683314, Cell Rep 2017): at the prefrontal axon initial segment GABA "switches polarity from depolarizing to hyperpolarizing but does so over a protracted periadolescent period"; the somatodendritic switch is "over the first postnatal week" in mouse (an abstract sentence, which is a statement not a data cell). A somatic EGABA does not settle axo-axonic or AIS GABA.

Second part, chloride homeostasis shifted toward depolarising GABA in epileptic or injured cortex, or immature human cortex: **SUPPORTED, with method caveats**.

* FULL: Bakouh et al., Brain 2025, PMC11788215, PMID 39106285, DOI 10.1093/brain/awae262. 35 children (25 MTOR-related malformations, 10 "pseudo-controls": peri-tumoural, peri-stroke, Rasmussen). Western blot, Results (Fig. 2D): "Mean expression of NKCC1 was 1.253 +/- 0.072 in MCDs versus 0.674 +/- 0.054 for controls (P < 0001), and values for KCC2 were 0.199 +/- 0.032 in MCD versus 1.281 +/- 0.070 for controls (P < 0.001)". The next sentence prints the KCC2/NKCC1 ratio as "1.253 +/- 0.072 in MCD versus 0.674 +/- 0.054", which are the NKCC1 numbers repeated: a printing error in the source; the ratio itself is not recoverable from the text. Functional readout (Fig. 6A-B): membranes of resected cortex injected into Xenopus oocytes; "EGABA 16.28 +/- 0.59 mV" shifting to "-29.5 +/- 10.24 mV" after rapamycin (n = 14 oocytes, 3 patients); the authors note this EGABA is close to the oocyte's own ECl ("-24.44 +/- 1.68 mV") and argue the shift reflects NKCC1/KCC2 imbalance in the injected membranes. So this is an indirect, heterologous readout in a lesional, mTOR-driven pathology, with three control cases that are themselves epileptic-lesion tissue. It does not measure neuronal EGABA with a perforated patch.
* ABSTRACT: Blauwblomme et al., Ann Neurol 2019, PMID 30597612: 47 slices from 11 children with focal cortical dysplasia; "Blockade of NKCC1 Cl- cotransporters further controlled interictal discharges in 9 of 12 cases"; reduced KCC2 expression. Conti et al., Epilepsia 2011, PMID 21635237: peritumoural epileptic cortex, oocyte EGABA "more depolarized" than nonepileptic cortex, abolished by bumetanide. Ruffolo et al., Epilepsia 2018, PMID 30306542: Dravet autopsy cortex in oocytes, "a shift of the GABA reversal potential toward more depolarizing values in Dravet syndrome, and a parallel increase of the chloride transporters NKCC1/KCC2 expression ratio".
* Immature human cortex, ABSTRACT: Dzhala et al., Nat Med 2005, PMID 16227993: "Cl- transport in perinatal human cortex is as immature as in the rat" (NKCC1 versus KCC2 expression; abstract sentence, no cell read).
* No source I found measures EGABA with perforated patch in human epileptic neocortex of a genetic DEE, or in WWOX tissue.

## R2. "Ki" for vigabatrin on GABA transaminase (0.85 mM and 3.2 mM)

Verdict: **PARTLY**. All three numbers are inactivation constants of an irreversible mechanism-based inactivator (KI from a Kitz-Wilson type analysis, paired with a kinact), not reversible Ki values, but they were measured or quoted under different, partly unreachable conditions and may not be placed side by side as one potency.

* CHEMBL1135615 = Choi S, Silverman RB, J Med Chem 2002, 45, 4531, PMID 12238932, DOI 10.1021/jm020134i, "Inactivation and inhibition of gamma-aminobutyric acid aminotransferase by conformationally restricted vigabatrin analogues". Depth: ABSTRACT only; full text NOT_REACHED (ACS page HTTP 403, Europe PMC not open, no PMC copy). ChEMBL rows, all vigabatrin (CHEMBL89598): activity 1001336 "K inact = 0.24 min-1", assay CHEMBL678641 "Time dependent inactivation of Gamma-amino-N-butyrate transaminase"; activity 1001337 "Ki = 850000 nM", assay CHEMBL678638 "Concentration dependent inactivation of Gamma-amino-N-butyrate transaminase"; activity 1001338 "Ratio = 0.28", assay CHEMBL846525 "Ratio of kinact to that of KI". ChEMBL records the assay organism as Cavia porcellus while mapping the target to human ABAT (CHEMBL2044), so the species field is a curation field I could not check against the paper. Inference: the 0.85 mM is KI (the assay names say "concentration dependent inactivation" and a kinact and a kinact/KI ratio sit beside it: 0.24/0.85 = 0.28); enzyme source, pH and temperature of that paper were not read.
* CHEMBL2203122 = Pan Y et al., J Med Chem 2012, 55, 357, PMID 22128851, DOI 10.1021/jm201231w, "(1S,3S)-3-amino-4-difluoromethylenyl-1-cyclopentanoic acid (CPP-115) ...". Depth: FULL (PMC3257419). ChEMBL rows: activity 12173263 "Ki = 3200000 nM", assay CHEMBL2214167 "Inactivation of GABA-AT" (organism blank, target human); activity 12173262 "Ki = 10000000 nM", assay CHEMBL2214168 "Inactivation of GABA-AT in rat brain homogenate". Locator (Introduction): "In contrast to the high [KI] value reported for vigabatrin as an inactivator of GABA-AT (3.2 mM or 10 mM), [CPP-115] has a [KI] value (at suboptimal pH) of 31 uM." The symbol after "high" and after "a" was dropped in the retrieved text; the reading as KI is an inference from "inactivator" and from the cited kinetics. These two rows are **literature values cited in the Introduction, not measured in this paper**: the paper's Experimental section contains no GABA-AT inactivation assay for vigabatrin (its assays are GABA uptake, GABA receptor binding, off-target panels, microPET, microdialysis, conditioned place preference, ERG).
* CHEMBL5726210 = a granted US patent, US-10189807-B2 (2019), "Tetrahydrothiophene-based GABA aminotransferase inactivators", ingested from BindingDB (ChEMBL source 37; document type PATENT; no PMID or DOI). ChEMBL row: activity 27197495 "Ki = 3200000 nM", assay CHEMBL5732295, whose description is a generic methods blurb for the whole patent (plate reader, LC-MS, crystallography); plus two empty rows (kon, k_off = None). Patent text, Google Patents, Table 1 (the page-fetch summary quoted these cells; I did not see the table image): column headers "Ki (mM)", "Kinact (min-1)", "Kinact/Ki (min-1 mM-1)", and "(S)-vigabatrin 3.2 | 0.37 | 0.11". Conditions in the same patent: "GABA-AT was isolated and purified from pig brain by a published procedure"; inactivation "at 25 C in 50 mM potassium pyrophosphate buffer solution, pH 6.5, containing 5 mM alpha-ketoglutarate"; "A Kitz and Wilson replot was used to determine the kinetic constants KI and kinact". The patent does not say in the text I saw that vigabatrin's row was measured at pH 6.5, but the companion paper (PMC4390550, J Am Chem Soc 2015, FULL) says the kinetic constants "were then measured under non-optimal conditions (pH 6.5, 25 C) using a Kitz and Wilson replot" and that the best analogue "is eight times more efficient an inactivator of GABA-AT than vigabatrin". The enzyme is pig brain; ChEMBL's "Homo sapiens" is a target mapping, not the assay species.
* Side by side? **No.** Different enzyme preparations (pig brain purified; rat brain homogenate; the 2002 species unverified), different pH (6.5 in the patent and 2015 paper; the others NOT_REACHED or not stated), and different provenance: 0.85 mM is a paper's own measurement; 10 mM and one 3.2 mM are cited literature; the other 3.2 mM is the patent's own table. The two 3.2 mM rows (CHEMBL2203122 and CHEMBL5726210) are probably one lineage (same laboratory) and not two independent measurements (inference). A KI of an irreversible inactivator depends on pH, temperature, pre-incubation and enzyme source, so a 4-fold (0.85 vs 3.2) and 12-fold (0.85 vs 10) spread is expected without any real disagreement, and ChEMBL's label "Ki" is misleading for all three.

## R6. Evidence that raising GABA availability (vigabatrin, tiagabine) aggravates or fails in some epilepsies, or controls seizures without improving development

Verdict (a) aggravation in some seizure types: **SUPPORTED** (case and review level; mostly abstract). (a) in "some genetic epilepsies or DEEs": **PARTLY** (one glycine-encephalopathy case pair; nothing I found for vigabatrin or tiagabine in DEEs such as Dravet, STXBP1 or WWOX). (b) controls seizures without improving development in genetic DEEs: **PARTLY** (consistent population-level evidence in infantile spasms and TSC; no genetic-DEE-specific, vigabatrin-attributed test found).

All items ABSTRACT unless stated.

1. Perucca E, Gram L, Avanzini G, Dulac O, Epilepsia 1998, PMID 9578007 (review). Quote: "Phenytoin and vigabatrin also have been implicated in worsening of seizures, particularly generalized seizures, whereas gabapentin has been associated repeatedly with precipitation of myoclonic jerks." Review of literature; not a cohort.
2. Lortie A et al., Neurology 1993, PMID 8232984 (supplement). Quote: "Vigabatrin is rarely associated with seizure worsening, and then mainly in patients with resistant generalized epilepsies." The word "rarely" matters for the strength of premise.
3. Dev Med Child Neurol 1998, PMID 9746003: 18 consecutive children with resistant typical absence seizures; "Vigabatrin was also used in the treatment of two children ... Absences were aggravated in both cases where vigabatrin was added on to concurrent treatment." Cohort: 2 children on vigabatrin.
4. Tiagabine, Knake et al., Seizure 1999, PMID 10486298: one 32-year-old with absence epilepsy and generalised tonic-clonic seizures developed absence status on tiagabine 45 mg daily; remission after lorazepam and dose reduction (case). The abstract also states that "In animal models of generalized epilepsy, TGB induces absence status with 3-5 Hz spike-wave complexes" (a statement, not data read). A related lethargic-mouse paper (Epilepsia 1997, PMID 9118845) frames vigabatrin as "human: proabsence effect" and tiagabine as "possible proabsence" in its Purpose; the retrieved abstract stops before the results, so I did not read what it found.
5. Genetic DEE example, J Child Neurol 2006, PMID 16551461: two infants with early myoclonic encephalopathy due to nonketotic hyperglycinaemia; "Rapidly progressive deterioration was noticed after a few days. Acute encephalopathy ... developed"; recovered after withdrawal in one, not in the other. Worsening of encephalopathy, not a seizure-frequency measure; 2 patients.
6. (b), randomised, abstracts: UKISS (Lancet Neurol 2005, PMID 16239177): spasm freedom at 12 to 14 months "hormone 41/55 [75%] vs vigabatrin 39/51 [76%]", "Mean VABS score did not differ significantly (hormone 78.6 [SD 16.8] vs vigabatrin 77.5 [SD 12.7]...)", and in no identified aetiology "88.2 [17.3] vs 78.9 [14.3]; ... p=0.025". ICISS (Lancet Child Adolesc Health 2018, PMID 30236380): 377 infants, combination of vigabatrin with hormonal therapy versus hormonal therapy alone, "Mean VABS scores did not differ significantly ... (73.9 [SE 1.3] vs 72.7 [1.4], difference -1.2 [95% CI -4.9 to 2.6], p=0.55)" although the combination was more effective on spasm cessation. PREVeNT (TSC, Ann Neurol 2024, PMID 37638552): 56 randomised; "Bayley-III cognitive composite scores at 24 months were similar"; "Incidence of infantile spasms was lower and time to spasms after randomization was later in the vigabatrin group". These are infantile spasms and a genetic disorder (TSC), not WWOX-type DEE, and the ICISS and UKISS contrasts are drug-versus-drug.
7. (b), cohort, FULL (see R7): Jang 2026 (PMC13570280): "none of the 61 unresponsive patients achieved favorable developmental outcome" and "none of 49 (0.0%) with genetic etiology".
8. Balagura 2022 (STXBP1, FULL, PMC9157582): "No correlation was observed between STXBP1_DevScore and age at seizure offset (p = 0.64, Pearson correlation coefficient)"; and, Discussion, remission versus active epilepsy "little difference". Not vigabatrin-specific.

* Jang 2026 (PMC13570280, Table 1 and Fig. 1 caption): 236 children, 175 responders; "Response was defined as absence of spasms for > 24 h and a BASED score <= 2"; favourable (both MQ >= 70 and PMQ >= 70) n = 49, of which Unresponsive row "61 (25.8) | 0 (0.0) | 61 (32.6)"; genetic "49 (20.8) | 0 (0.0) | 49 (26.2)"; structural "81 (34.3) | 3 | 78"; unknown "106 | 46 | 60". So 126 of 175 responders were unfavourable (72.0%), as computed in the README: **confirmed**. Caveats not in the README text: (i) "unfavourable" means MQ < 70 or PMQ < 70, so a child below the cut in one domain counts; (ii) 145 of the 175 responders were prednisolone add-on responders (Table 1: 82 at 40 mg, 63 at 60 mg); the vigabatrin-alone responders number 30 (15 at 100 mg/kg/day, 15 at 150 mg/kg/day), of whom 11 were favourable (4 and 7), so for vigabatrin-alone responders the poor-development share computed from Table 1 is 19 of 30 (63%), still a majority; (iii) the cross-tabulation of etiology by response is not in the text or Table 1 (Fig. 3 is an image I did not view), so "all genetic responders" is a bound from 0 of 49, as the README already says.
* Aramanadka (PMC8498980, Tables 2 and 4): Table 4 "Complete cessation 22 (81) | 40 (46)" and "No response 5 (19) | 47 (54)", OR 5.2 (1.8-14.9): **confirmed** (65% of 62). The cut is SQ below versus above 55 (children at exactly 55 fall in neither column).
* Jonsson 2024 (PMC11296082, Table 2 and Table 1): "DRE n = 22: 18 (82%)" with global delay and "No DRE n = 38: 13 (34%)"; printed RR "4.2 (1.6-11.0)" on both rows: **confirmed**, and the ratio from these cells in this direction is 82/34 = 2.4. Genetic IESS in Table 1: "G 9" with "DRE G 1 (13%)" and "GDD G 8 (100%)", with 1 death, giving 7 of 7 non-DRE assessed children with delay (computed, as in the README); the text adds "All nine individuals with confirmed genetic etiology had GDD despite good seizure control in the majority." The specific etiologies include "trisomy 21 (5)" of the 9 (Table 1), which inflates this stratum for reasons unrelated to a single-gene DEE.
* KCNQ2, Jadhav 2026 (PMC13238869, Table 2): prompt-diagnosis group n = 6, "Seizure-free at age 12 m 6 (100%)", development at most recent review "Normal 2 (33%) | Mild-mod DD 4 (66%) | Sev-prof DD 0": so 4 of 6 below DQ 70 (matches the README 4/6), none severe. The quoted Discussion sentence is present on the PMC page ("have a developmental encephalopathy component that is independent of the epileptic encephalopathy"); the Europe PMC XML text omitted it. Pisano 2015 (ABSTRACT): "Twelve patients had moderate-to-severe developmental delay at follow-up. However, the two patients whose seizures ceased within a few days of onset showed only mild cognitive impairment." This 12 of 15 (80%) and the 4 of 6 (67%) are majorities, not "near-universal", and the Pisano sentence about the two fast responders is a counter-signal that timing may matter.
* Balagura 2022 (PMC9157582): the quoted p = 0.64 correlation is confirmed; the "intellectual disability in all aged over 6" wording in the README row was not located by my searches (I did not read the tables in full).
* Overall: "majority outcome in IESS cohorts (65-72%)" is supported (72% Jang, 65% Aramanadka). "Near-universal in genetic strata" rests on Jang's 0 of 49 bound (not a responder rate) and Jonsson's 7 of 7 (five of nine genetic children have trisomy 21); the KCNQ2 rows (67% and 80%) are lower, and none of the cohorts is WWOX or vigabatrin-specific. The text itself states it is not WWOX evidence and holds as a binary only.
