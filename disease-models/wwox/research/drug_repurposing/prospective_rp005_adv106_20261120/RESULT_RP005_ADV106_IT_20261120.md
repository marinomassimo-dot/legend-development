**What was tested, honestly labelled.** The organised repository knowledge (cards, sheets, notes) plus a delegate as the predictor, against a rule-only baseline and against the same delegate given only the abstract. ## 3. Reading
* **Hit rate:** B 2 of 3 (1 of 2 without S5); A 2 of 3 (1 of 2); C 1 of 3 (0 of 2). Three of five slots are not discriminating (S1 and S4 not determinable, S5 identical in all sets), so **two slots carry the whole comparison.**
* **Did the generator beat the trivial baseline?** **No: a tie** (2 of 3 against 2 of 3), and the tie is not robust (one reading of S3 reverses it). A one-slot margin would not have been evidence in any case.
* **Did the repository materials add anything over the abstract?** In this one text, **yes on one slot**: B (with the materials) was right on S2 where C (abstract only) and A were wrong, because the repository holds a related fact (the 2010 paper's sleep result in Cav3.1 and Cav3.3 knockouts) that led B to infer that the 2009 paper had used the null mice only for the diet phenotype. **No on another**: B and C made the same wrong S3 call. One slot is not a rate.
* **Direction of the misses:** both delegate arms **carried a sentence of the abstract past what it supports**: the abstract says food intake was unchanged in the null mice, and both delegates read it as food intake under the drug in obese animals ('unchanged'), whereas the body reports it reduced; the baseline's weight-loss prior got S3 right. The baseline's miss (S2) is the opposite kind: the target-validation prior ('the effect is absent in the null mice') assumed an experiment that the paper does not contain. The generator therefore overweights cues in the text it is given where the baseline overweights a generic pattern. Also found by the scorer: **the abstract is looser than the body** (the null mice's food intake was 'lower overall' with one significant time point, against 'without changes in food intake' in the abstract) and **the slot definitions were mine and leaked ambiguity** (A1 to A5 in SCI's note: S1 has no option for a phase-dependent effect, S3 fits three experiments, S4 and S5 are 'or' or 'stated or implied' slots). Those are defects of the test design, not of the predictors.
* **Contamination I declare:** I read the abstract before freezing (so did HE and SCI earlier); the slots were fixed before any prediction; the generator ran once; the optional third arm was added before the freeze; no slot was changed after a prediction. Delegates' self-reported effort (sonnet, high) is a report, not a measurement.

## 5. Decision for RP-005: **REVISE, not keep and not remove**
* **Not remove:** one slot (S2) shows the repository's organised facts can carry information that the abstract does not and that a generic prior gets wrong; removing would discard that on the strength of one text.
* **Not keep as is:** the design could not tell the generator from a trivial baseline because only two slots discriminated and one was ambiguous. Revisions before the next case: (1) at least eight slots that a text must settle, each with an 'other or not reported' option and a unit, chosen so that a baseline rule and a generator can disagree; (2) a baseline that includes the obvious domain priors (here: weight loss implies lower intake, target-validation implies an absent effect in the null) written as a table before the generator runs; (3) the abstract-only arm kept (it isolates what the materials add); (4) at least three texts with a blind scorer each, and the decision stated as 'the generator beats both baselines on the pooled determinable slots by a margin written in advance', not as a one-slot difference; (5) a pre-check of every slot against the abstract and a pilot read of the slot wording by a person who has not seen the text.
* **Cost of the revised test:** about three times this one (three texts), roughly 0.3 million delegate tokens and two to three hours of IT time plus one blind scoring pass per text.
