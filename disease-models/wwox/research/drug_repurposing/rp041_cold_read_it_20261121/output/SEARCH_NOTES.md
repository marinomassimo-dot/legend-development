## Order and passes
1. Read A in full (the consolidated text), then B in full (the original plus dated lines).
2. Second pass, side by side: header, section 1 and sizing flag, section 2 items 1 to 11, section 3 table and arithmetic, section 4 table, sections 5 to 7, appendix/dated lines. I built a list of each number, age, arm name and the line that changed it.
3. Targeted searches inside the two files (with a short script, only reading these two files) to confirm absences: "bursting" (no heterozygote incidence given), "E5" (title only), "sex" (A only), "genotyping" (absent), "strain" and "background" (absent), "route" and "intraperitoneal" (absent as an injection route), "S-KO" and "S-HT" (B only and A only), "stage-matched", "P5" and "P8" (priors-set labels and ages), the stage numbers.
4. Arithmetic checks using only numbers printed in the documents: 36 to 109 doubled; 21 cells times 11 divided by 1.75; 33 and 150 knockouts divided by 1.75; 132 divided by 1.75; litter counts. These checks are labelled "my arithmetic" where used.
5. Wrote QUESTIONS, CONFLICTS and TERMS, then ran a script that checks that every double-quoted citation in the "documents say" and location columns occurs verbatim in A or B (after removing bold markers and backticks). Result: all multi-word quotations found in the two files; a few cells with several quotations were checked by hand (C07, C14, C15, C25, C35, C37 appear in the documents with different quote marks or case, which I judged equivalent). Very short quoted tokens (under 6 characters) were not machine-checked.
