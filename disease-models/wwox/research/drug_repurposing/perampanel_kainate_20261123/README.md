# Perampanel beyond AMPA: competing kainate-receptor effects in WWOX-deficient hippocampus

**Decision.** Prioritize the juvenile hippocampal release prediction for a circuit-specific test. The Wwox neuronal-deletion hippocampus has a higher estimated fraction of Grik1 transcripts with the developmental GluK1c-like insert. A presynaptic receptor carrying this variant can restrain glutamate release. The evidence that perampanel inhibits postsynaptic kainate currents is direct; whether it also relieves this presynaptic restraint is unresolved. Consequently a fall in net firing under perampanel would leave the presynaptic question open. The earlier laboratory audit identified GluK5 from the abstract. This analysis uses measured potencies, developmental physiology and new genotype-specific transcript estimates. [Earlier audit, D11](../ampa_comparison_it41_20261109/output/decisive.tsv) · [Decision criteria](prediction.md)

## Quantitative substrate tests in untreated tissue

We classified the 29-amino-acid intracellular insert by comparing the mouse sequence to the curated rat insertion and by requiring both flanks in the translated Ensembl release 92 cDNA. Two mouse Grik1 transcripts included the insert, two skipped it and seven were unclassified. For each animal library we divided inclusion TPM by inclusion plus skipping TPM, then averaged animals equally. Transcript-abundance estimators can allocate reads shared by multiple isoforms; these are processed estimates and do not provide actual junction counts. The first public ENA and NCBI checks for the original P17 experiments yielded no public raw run files. All mice in these bulk series were P17. [Rat reference P22756](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/P22756/entry) · [Mouse reference Q60934](https://www.uniprot.org/uniprotkb/Q60934/entry) · [Mouse tissue data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158681) · [Earlier archive availability check](../perampanel_gria2_editing_20261123/README.md)

For neuronal-deletion hippocampus, leaving one library out gave +12.6 to +22.1 points. Assigning all unclassified TPM adversarially with all libraries retained gave +1.5 to +27.9. These results do not identify a within-cell splicing change or its cause. An exact descriptive relabeling check illustrates the small sample. Of the ten ways to assign two of the five neuronal-hippocampus libraries to the control group, one gave an equal or larger positive difference and two gave an equal or larger absolute difference. The mutant group mean of 55.1% was also close to the 55.0% and 59.6% means of the *control* groups in the neural-lineage and global hippocampal comparisons. The separate control backgrounds are not interchangeable. Stage-specific persistence remains an open explanation requiring independent tissue.

We also tested a specific alternative using the pooled hippocampal single-nucleus sample in each arm. The share of Grik1 nuclear molecules in broad excitatory classes was 17.2% in controls and 17.4% in KO, compared with 60.8% and 67.7% in broad inhibitory classes. Holding the unknown insert fraction constant within each of nine broad classes and an unassigned group, the largest possible change due solely to these observed class weights is **9.42 percentage points** (total variation distance). That is smaller than the central bulk shift but larger than its conservative lower bound. Subtype mixtures within a class, anatomical sampling, nuclear versus bulk RNA, abundance estimation and the absence of independent nuclear pools all limit this comparison. [Observed class weights](results/class_weights.tsv) · [Nuclear dataset](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158684)

For the postsynaptic hypothesis, Grik5 relative to Grik2 changed by **+0.83** log2 units in neuronal-deletion hippocampus (Grik5 +0.32, Grik2 −0.51) but only **+0.29** in cortex. The prespecified +0.5 regional replication criterion failed. Bulk human WWOX organoids had the opposite sign (−0.40, one-library omissions −0.57 to −0.22). Hippocampal exc/inh nuclear pools each showed relative changes of only about +0.13. Relative transcript levels do not establish the number, composition or localization of receptor complexes. **Independent adult P47T hippocampus.** In counts from five animals per genotype, the equally sex-weighted Grik5-versus-Grik2 change was +0.075 log2 units (females −0.008, males +0.159; one-animal omissions +0.003 to +0.147). ### Adult P47T hippocampus, direct junction counts

The independent pilot met its extension rule. The complete series covers **538,274,725 fragments from ten animals** (two lanes per animal, five animals per genotype). All 24 diagnostic probes had zero genomic matches in mm10 and zero off-gene transcript hits in the Ensembl GRCm38 release 92 cDNA reference. The prespecified centered, Q20 first-read count found only **38 informative fragments in controls and 74 in P47T**. The rule required 75 per genotype and 12 in every animal. Four of five controls fell below 12 (3, 3, 6, 8). All five mutants reached 12. Controls actually contributed more total fragments (281.8 versus 256.5 million). General library depth alone therefore cannot explain their lower junction coverage. [Locked prediction](p47t_prediction.md) · [Animal counts](results/p47t_animals.tsv)

## Pharmacology separates two circuits

- At the **mossy fibre → CA3** synapse, perampanel directly inhibited the postsynaptic kainate EPSC (IC50 2.9 µM; reported confidence interval 1.9–4.9). In the same study the recombinant GluA4 AMPA reference had IC50 0.56 µM. Recombinant GluK2/GluK5/Neto1 was near this value at 0.69 µM, whereas GluK2 alone was 26 µM. Receptor composition changes the pharmacology substantially. The native preparation had about 20% rundown during 10 minutes even in control solution. [Taniguchi et al., Table 1 and Fig. 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9295835/)
- At the immature **CA3 → CA1** connection, a selective GluK1 antagonist (ACET) raised miniature EPSC frequency to 168% of baseline in wild type versus 83% in Neto1-null slices (seven recordings each, P4–6). Mean WT amplitude was 103% of baseline. In a separate study, introducing GluK1c in CA3 reinstated an immature presynaptic phenotype at P14–15. These establish a release mechanism in developing hippocampus. Perampanel's recombinant GluK1 measurements used the **1b** variant (19 µM alone, 11 µM with Neto1, 2.8 µM with GluK5). No potency or sign for the native GluK1c-dependent presynaptic signaling function follows from these channel-current fits. [NETO1-dependent release, Fig. 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5494894/) · [Development and juvenile re-expression, Figs. 3, 6–7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298885/)

**Discriminating prediction.** If the Wwox neuronal-deletion hippocampus retains a functional juvenile release brake, selective GluK1 antagonism should increase release probability more in KO than WT at the CA3 output and reduce short-train facilitation. If perampanel also removes this brake it should increase independently measured presynaptic release even while postsynaptic currents fall, with little additional presynaptic effect after selective blockade. If selective antagonism releases the brake but perampanel leaves presynaptic release unchanged at an exposure that decreases postsynaptic kainate currents, the specificity lies in the circuit target rather than a global opposite drug effect. Matching age, axonal pathway, baseline firing, calcium and measured free exposure is essential to interpret the direction. No study here administered perampanel to WWOX tissue.
