## Answer first

**Form of the answer: an improved design that cannot be built yet.** The pair "M1-selective antagonist versus EBP inhibitor" is **not interpretable now**, for two decisive gaps, and one part of what SCI50 delivered must be corrected.
1. **Gap 1: there is no M1-selective reagent of disclosed identity whose EBP activity has been measured in an OPC preparation.** The reagent SCI50 named (PIPE-307, PMID 39083422, Poon et al., PNAS 2024) has **no public structure**: the Expression of Concern states that a global licence and development agreement prevented its inclusion, and whether the compound can be obtained from its developer or licensee is not stated in anything read; the paper has a **Correction (PMID 39432800, 2024-10-21)** and an **Editorial Expression of Concern (PMID 40512803, 2025-06-13)** (both confirmed from PubMed metadata; the verifier's reading of the notice: a licence constraint prevented the structure from being included; the Correction adds a competing-interest statement); the appendix's Safety47 panel (about 80 readouts) lists no EBP, sigma or Kir. The SCI50 producer and verifier **missed both notices**. Its EBP activity is unmeasured in every source reached, by two independent searches. For no **M1-selective** antagonist is there an EBP datum in the sources reached (clemastine and benztropine have positive EBP data in Hubler, and four unnamed antimuscarinics were EBP-negative in EpiSC OPCs): ChEMBL's 334 EBP-orthologue binding rows (all read by the verifier) contain none for clemastine, benztropine, pirenzepine, telenzepine, scopolamine, homatropine, tropicamide, atropine, ipratropium, trihexyphenidyl, oxybutynin, darifenacin, xanomeline, either TASIN-1 record or ketoconazole. **Unmeasured is not inactive**: EBP binds many amine drugs (tamoxifen 5 nM, ifenprodil 5 nM, raloxifene 1 nM in ChEMBL; Sax 2022: EBP dominates 60+ OPC-screen hits, and quetiapine, donepezil, vanoxerine, fenspiride, bazedoxifene and clomifene inhibit EBP in mouse OPCs), so an M1 antagonist cannot be assumed EBP-inert.
2. **Gap 2: M1 tone is not measured where the antagonist is tested.** Acetylcholine in OPC-conditioned medium is about 12 nM at 60,000 cells per well and falls to 5 and 2 nM at 40,000 and 30,000 (PIPE-307 appendix, Supp Fig 3); exogenous ACh inhibits differentiation (IC50 about 58 nM). The four antimuscarinics that blocked M1/M3/M5 in CHO cells and did nothing in EpiSC-derived OPCs (Hubler 2018; M2/M4 not tested; their names are **not reached**: the heatmap is not in the text layer and Hubler's PMC page returned a reCAPTCHA) may reflect missing tone: INFERENZA, untested. Next in line: an add-back arm for CW3388's M1 residue.
3. **The EBP side is better documented but not clean.** **TASIN-1 identity is resolved to the bipiperidine** (Allimuthu Fig 4I viewed; PubChem CID 1329829; ChEMBL CHEMBL4514756 agree; CHEMBL5287158 is a 2021 review-deposit redrawing; Zhang 2016's own drawing was not seen). Its EBP target rests on sterol rescue, knockdown and overexpression, not direct binding ("will be necessary"); residual EBP activity at 10 µM is about 11–12% (single concentration). **The CW compounds are M1-low in one single-point assay, not shown M1-clean:** in the single-point M1-NFAT-CHO antagonist assay (Allimuthu Fig 4E, n = 2, relative to clemastine = 100% at 1 µM) CW3388 is about 18 and 19%, CW9009 about 0 and 3%, CW9956 about 1 and 3%, TASIN-1 about 2 and 10% **at 100 nM, a tenth of the others' concentration**; these are single-point readings, not affinities, and no M2–M5, sigma, hERG or Kir value exists for any of them. Their structures are not drawn in the main figure reached (Figs 2B/3 and Data S1/S2 not reached).
4. **What the primaries do measure that bears on the pair.** Hubler 2018 (EpiSC OPCs): clemastine and benztropine inhibit EBP and enhance MBP+ formation, the other antimuscarinics do not; additivity with ketoconazole is absent only at a maximal ketoconazole dose while T3 stays additive; a **pooled CRISPR EBP contrast exists** (two sgEBP guides: zymostenol rose and MBP+ formation was enhanced, p = 0.0009 on 8 wells; EBP mRNA n = 1 well; not a germline null); a **fourth preparation** (mouse primary cortical OPCs, Extended Data 5: TASIN-1 and amorolfine at 300 nM, MBP+ at 72 h and zymostenol at 24 h) carries the same experiment (legend read, outcomes not). Mei 2016: the clemastine/benztropine effect is abolished in Chrm1-null OPCs, which differentiate about 4-fold more (about 16 against 4 MBP+ cells per field; drug-treated wild type reaches 5.3–7.3× vehicle, about 21–29 per field, my arithmetic from the fold range and not a printed value): **the null sits below the drug-treated level, so occlusion is a weaker confound than SCI50 stated, but it remains one** (a null-versus-null-plus-drug contrast is read only when positive). No source combines an M1 antagonist with an EBP inhibitor in one OPC preparation.

## The conditions and the discriminating outcomes (`output/conditions.tsv`, `output/outcomes.md`)
Gates first: M1 tone measured at a controlled plating density; the M1 arm acts on its own; the sterol profile of each reagent at the dose used (zymostenol/zymosterol); the assay window open (T3 or ketoconazole as the positive control). Then, **as discriminating outcomes**: B (M1-selective) acts, C (EBP inhibitor) does not, sterols flat on B and rising on C, B+C equal to B at submaximal doses → favours the M1 route and weakens EBP as the cause of the clemastine effect; C acts and B does not despite measured tone and a sterol-clean profile → favours the EBP route; both act and the combination exceeds both at submaximal doses with no sterol change on B and no M1 antagonism on C at the doses used → both, independent; B acts but sterols rise on B → B has an EBP activity and the arm is not M1-selective (a sgEBP + B arm separates them); B inert only where tone is absent or an ACh add-back restores the block → absence of tone, not of an M1 route; neither reagent shifts a deficit that is present with reference-genotype OPCs on wild-type neurons while T3 does → an OPC-intrinsic block; reagents act on reference-genotype OPCs on wild-type neurons but the gap persists over reference-genotype neurons and a medium arm reproduces it → a neuronal ceiling. **Outcome "B+C equals B" is also what a ceiling gives: it needs submaximal doses and a T3 headroom arm; a CW3388-plus-agonist add-back arm belongs with the CW compound.** Inconclusive when: the window is not open, tone is below measurable, the dose ladder lacks the in-assay EC50, or saturation (Hubler's non-additivity was at a maximal dose). Three claims stay apart: acts through its target in OPC; useful with a neuronal defect; transfer to a proposal. Outcomes 6 and 7, CD08 (genotype crossing) and CD13 (exposure) are, for **this** question, decoration.

## Update 2026-11-13 (ADV-001; the text above is kept)

The IT29 case-1 compounds were re-annotated by documented EBP status (`../ebp_reannotation_adv001_20261113/README.md`): of 15 favourable compounds 4 (5 with ifenprodil, binding-only) have documented EBP activity (benztropine, clemastine, tamoxifen, quetiapine), 3 have indirect sterol-profile negatives (clobetasol, liothyronine, bexarotene), 3 act on another sterol enzyme with EBP unmeasured (amorolfine, miconazole, ketoconazole) and 4 are EBP-unmeasured (atropine, ipratropium, oxybutynin, sobetirome). **Design position (INFERENZA, not a result):** a sterol-profile (zymostenol/zymosterol) arm per compound before any target attribution is the discriminating control for the compounds with a documented EBP route (clemastine, benztropine, tamoxifen, quetiapine; ifenprodil by binding) and for those with EBP unmeasured (atropine, ipratropium, oxybutynin, sobetirome); for clobetasol, liothyronine and bexarotene an indirect sterol-profile negative lowers but does not remove the question; atropine, ipratropium and oxybutynin are EBP-unmeasured, not inactive.
