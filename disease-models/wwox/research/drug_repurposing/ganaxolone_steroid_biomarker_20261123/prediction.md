# Does low circulating Allo-S enrich for a ganaxolone response?

The WWOX steroid dossier found a mouse C19 conversion with substantial competing metabolism. A tempting extrapolation is that any low circulating steroid level identifies ganaxolone responders. Use the already observed aggregate trial and subgroup summaries to ask a new question: could the unreported high Allo-S placebo-controlled response in the randomized PCDH19 trial reverse the low-versus-high comparison? This is a retrospective partial-identification analysis. Marginal medians, quartiles, and overall responder counts are known before analysis. High-stratum bounds have not yet been calculated.

Start with all tested subjects in the ClinicalTrials.gov outcome tables. In each arm enumerate every ordered assignment to the low and high subgroups, with the published subgroup sizes. Impose ordered individual percentage changes, a physical floor of −100%, two-decimal rounding tolerance of 0.006 points, and the published count with at least 50% reduction. Constrain the quartiles and median of total and low groups. Compare commonly used SAS empirical midpoint-at-ties (Hyndman–Fan type 2), linear interpolation (type 7) and exclusive interpolation (type 6), retaining explicit infeasibility. Use the same convention in both arms. Leave the upper range uncapped. If no convention fits both arms, repeat using only the two medians and responder counts. Estimate the possible high-stratum difference of medians and the possible low-minus-high difference in drug–placebo responder proportions, preserving within-arm coupling. This does not reconstruct participants. Assess direction separately in the prespecified CDKL5 trial subgroups. With no WWOX treatment data, the decision concerns a biomarker eligibility rule only.
