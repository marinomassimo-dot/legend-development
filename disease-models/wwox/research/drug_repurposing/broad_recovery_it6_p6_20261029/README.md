## 1. Thread chosen and why

**Thread.** Quality control and degradation of the Q230P-class protein, narrowed to: *when the degradation route of a destabilised missense protein is blocked or the folding capacity is raised, does the protein that returns carry function; which fraction of it rises; does the answer depend on the lesion and on the expression context; and does the readout separate stabilisation from a boost?* Answered with the best-resolved cytosolic-enzyme precedent that carries an activity readout (cystathionine beta-synthase, CBS) plus the adjacent contrary precedent (ASPA C152W), and carried to a revised card (A02) and a decision-weighted design.

**Why this one, from the record.**
1. P5 (`wwox_function_reference_it6_20261029.md`, read read-only from the P5 worktree; its verification is in progress, so its items are used as designs, not as results) ends on a ruler that converts a protein gain into a function number, and P8 (`missense_recovery_it5_p8_20261029`) did the HDAC-versus-chaperone comparison (not repeated here). What neither did is ask whether *route blockade* (A02, A05) returns protein that is functional, and for which kind of lesion. Cards A01 to A05 are gated by the same unmeasured branch (is the Q230P protein made and cleared); the A02 and A05 cards rest on three precedents (HIKESHI, ASPA libraries, ASPA C152W) none of which has a function-per-molecule readout and one of which is a negative.
2. A cytosolic enzyme with a validated activity assay, a missense allele series and in-vivo data existed and had not been opened in the repository (no ledger event for any of the three CBS papers or the NQO1 chaperone paper before this act). Reading it at the source could change what the card says; searching splice (C02), read-through (B01, B02) or endogenous boost (C03) again would not, because those cards wait on tools or on an allele that has no protein, not on a reading. They were not re-searched in this act and are unchanged.
3. Residual-function-dependent expression increase is the same question seen from the other side: a boost multiplies residual function R. The CBS allele spectrum measures the dependence of the outcome on the lesion class directly.

## 5. Answers to the new questions (reader inference, labelled)

1. **Which function would be recovered.** In every positive precedent it is enzyme activity per milligram of lysate or a relative live-cell probe signal, plus a systemic metabolite (homocysteine) in mice; never activity per molecule. For WWOX no per-molecule SDR assay is validated (P5 section 4); the nearest ruler is the P5 phenotype ladder (L2/L4). So any WWOX route experiment can at most report L0/L1 plus an L2 binding ratio and an L4 phenotype on the ruler curve.
2. **Which fraction rises.** Not total protein alone. In CBS the native tetramer content tracked activity better than total protein, and ERAD inhibition raised T191M activity without changing its turnover, although for I278T turnover rose too (P-5). In ASPA C152W the whole rise was pellet (P-7). A total-protein blot would have misclassified both. Wild type rises too (P-5, x7.7 half-life): the class is a boost of whatever is made.
3. **Does it depend on the lesion.** Yes, in one enzyme: stabilisation sufficed for a regulatory-domain allele (P-1), improved three catalytic-domain alleles (P-2), failed for one (P-3) and for a stable-but-impaired allele (P-11); and the route itself differs by allele (P-5). **INFERENZA, not tested for WWOX, expectations unranked:** two outcomes are equally open (stabilisation sufficient, as for S466L; insufficient, as for the catalytic-domain alleles); the mapping rests on a domain label and modelled burial only (the repository records place Q230 as fully buried and non-catalytic, in-silico), CBS and WWOX differ in cofactor (PLP and heme versus NAD(P)) and assembly, and the repository red-team record (`analysis/data/redteam/WWOX_Q230P_redteam_report.md`) expects a chaperone-mediated-autophagy (lysosomal) route for Q230P as an extrapolation from P252A, which predicts R6 and not R3. The previous sentence about the P8 structural note stands, and: the P8 structural note places Q230 in a buried SDR position close to the catalytic region; if that class behaves like the CBS catalytic-domain alleles, stabilisation alone is expected to be insufficient and stabilisation plus refolding would be needed. The prediction fails if a stabilised Q230P-class protein is active on the ruler.
4. **Stabilisation versus transcriptional increase.** Route blockers act after translation, but P-2 shows the better drug did not track proteasome inhibition and chaperone induction is a co-effect (a transcriptional response of the chaperones, not of the transgene); the transgene promoter was an inducible metallothionein promoter in mice and a lentiviral promoter in HEK293 (not named in the text read). Whether proteasome inhibitors derepress these promoters is not established here (P8 already holds one CMV/HDAC-inhibitor precedent at abstract depth). The discriminating column is transgene mRNA per well under treatment, absent from all four CBS papers: **no precedent here separates stabilisation from a transcript effect**.
5. **Expression context.** One allele, three outcomes: I278T improved in mouse liver (P-2), rose in a human kidney line (P-5, probe signal 574 percent of its own vehicle, not compared with WT in the text read) and was not rescued by HSP70/HSF1 co-expression in a hamster line (P-6). T191M: null in mouse liver (P-3), gain in the kidney line (P-5). Interventions and readouts also differ, so this shows context sensitivity, not its cause.

## 6. Counter-evidence kept

Wild type rises as much or more than the mutant (P-5); tolerated exposure lowered homocysteine by about half without a significant activity gain and with immune-cell toxicity, gave no significant change for S466L (P-4 and P-4b), effective exposure toxic (P-4, P-5); protein up with activity flat (P-6, I278T); insoluble inert protein (P-7); an active-site ligand that stabilises and inhibits (P-8); the best drug does not follow proteasome inhibition (P-2); for the only WWOX missense with a measured turnover (P252A, stable overexpression in thyroid-cancer lines) the route was lysosomal and a proteasome arm did not even accumulate wild type (P8 record of PMID 41124647; independently confirmed in the source by the P6 verifier), and in 49 cancer lines MG-132 accumulated truncated WWOX while the normal-size protein did not change (Watanabe 2003, PMID 14695174, abstract-level, wild-type/truncation data, not a missense experiment), and a related SDR (RDH5) is also an autophagy substrate at abstract depth (P-9). The class stays a probe, not a candidate.

## 8. Next experiment, controls and decision meaning

**Name.** Q230P-class route-and-function experiment on the P5 ruler. It is the P8 fate experiment with route arms and the P5 ruler made explicit, not a new system; it should not run before the P5 ruler exists.

**System.** Isogenic WWOX-null human neural line, one landing site, one inducible promoter with a reporter control for derepression by route blockers; wild type (five induction levels), the Q230P-class allele, a binding-dead SDR-region control (L404A per P5), empty/EGFP control; transgene mRNA in the same wells.
**Arms.** Vehicle; proteasome blocker; lysosome-pH agent; ERAD/VCP blocker (separately, since P-5 shows allele-selective routes); each also on the null line and on the wild-type ruler line; viability readout in every arm (P-4, P-5 show cell damage at effective levels); no arm at a level above the one the viability readout permits. Concentrations are set by a prior toxicity titration; none is fixed here.
**Readouts, separate columns.** Soluble/pellet/total with an antibody-independent detection (tag or targeted mass spectrometry); native-state fraction (native gel or size resolution) beside total; half-life by pulse-chase from a detectable baseline; mRNA per well; the P5 binding-competence ratio and the L4 phenotype on the ruler curve at the measured protein.
**Predictions fixed before running (both directions).**
- R1: no protein in any fraction and mass spectrometry below a floor fixed in advance and stated per sample (a floor above the likely level reads as inconclusive, not as never made), mRNA present, and nascent-labelling shows synthesis: the lesion is upstream of degradation; A02 and A05 close for this allele (as in P8 P-A).
- R2: protein appears only after a route arm, soluble and native, ratio and phenotype fall on the wild-type curve at that protein, mRNA flat, synthesis rate unchanged, null line plus empty vector unaffected: stability lesion; function per molecule near wild type; the word recovery is allowed at L4 inside the ruler's range only and only once that ruler has been validated (until then only L0/L1 and the binding ratio are read). Meaning: route handling matters; the toxic-exposure problem becomes the gate (P-4).
- R3: protein rises, native fraction and ratio do not, variant stays below the curve: catalytic or folding lesion (the CBS catalytic-domain pattern); route blockade is stabilisation without repair; A02 moves against the allele; a refolding lever (not a degradation blocker) is the next question.
- R4: protein rises only in the pellet: ASPA C152W pattern; class closed for this allele.
- R5: protein rises equally for wild type and mRNA tracks it: a transcript effect; moves to NEED-WWOX-ENDOGENOUS-BOOST.
- R5b (boost without repair, the CBS pattern): variant and wild type rise by comparable folds, mRNA and synthesis rate flat, function per molecule unchanged (variant stays on its own curve): post-translational boost, no repair; the question moves to the residual-function bound R, not to A02.
- R6: the route differs from the proteasome (lysosome arm is the one that raises protein, as for P252A): the A02 lever is wrong for this lesion class and the lysosome-pH arm decides.
- R7: effect in the null line carrying the empty vector (a null line without a transgene has nothing to detect): not WWOX-dependent; excluded.
- Exclusion rule: any protein gain seen only at a level where the viability readout fails is excluded and is not read as R2 to R6.
- Added arms and columns: a nascent-labelling (synthesis-rate) column so that a rise is called stabilisation only when synthesis is unchanged; one HSP70/HSF1 arm in the same run, so that R3 can be told apart from a refolding-sufficient outcome (the CBS data show allele-dependent effects: I278T no, R125Q yes).
No fold cut-off is invented; cut-offs are written at registration from the ruler. What it cannot show: neuronal in-vivo behaviour, brain exposure, an organism or clinical endpoint, or any other allele.
