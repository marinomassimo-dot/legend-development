## What the reading changed (the corrections are the result)
1. **A pointer premise failed.** I had selected 37187090 and 38116420 as "three compounds in one assay". In 38116420 (read in full) sulforaphane has no value; only the fumarate ester (EC50 7.93 micromolar as printed) and its metabolite (127.2 micromolar) do, with no relation sign. Sulforaphane, the ester and the metabolite sit together only in 37187090, which the reader saw as abstract plus ChEMBL [CH]: 0.879, 5.114 and "greater than 10" micromolar. The two papers disagree on the same two compounds (5.114 against 7.93; "greater than 10" against 127.2): values from different documents are **not interchangeable** even for one assay name.
2. **NRF2 dependence of the readouts is not shown** where the reader could see it. 38116420 states no NRF2 dependence and its only control is an IKK inhibitor; the paper itself mentions "an independent pathway" for TLR-signal inhibition. 31411465 reads NQO1 enzyme activity in a murine hepatoma line without an NRF2 knockdown, and notes that three compounds induce NQO1 **without any Kelch-domain binding** and that progesterone raises NQO1 without activating NRF2. **NQO1 induction alone cannot separate H5.**
3. **The non-covalent binder exists and has an NRF2-knockdown arm in the source.** It is compound 9 of 31411465 (the compound of 27031670): Kelch-domain binding in the low nanomolar range by three formats, reversible; in human bronchial epithelial cells it raises NQO1, HO-1 and two other target mRNAs at tens of nanomolar, and the source lists assays with non-targeting and with NRF2 siRNA [CH]. The knockdown result values were not retrieved. This is the "KEAP1 compound of a different binding mode" IT43 said it lacked; it is a pointer to a primary source, not a verdict.
4. **A Kelch-domain assay is blind to cysteine modifiers.** Sulforaphane was inactive in the fluorescence-polarisation assay of 31411465. A comparison of "binding potency" across covalent and non-covalent compounds is therefore not a comparison of one action.
5. **A false proposal avoided.** The molibresib row (KEAP1 "IC50 greater than 10,000 nM") comes from a chemoproteomic pulldown in a BET-inhibitor paper; the paper's text has no KEAP1, Kelch or NRF2 content, and the value is a floor set by the top concentration. It is not a KEAP1 measurement. Without the document it would have entered S10 as a catalogue-status KEAP1 row. Reader inference: the supplementary table behind it was not seen.
6. **The "opposite-direction control" is weaker than IT43 wrote.** The only catalogue-status NRF2-lowering row is halofuginone, in cancer lines, from a review (the primaries were seen as abstracts): its reported mechanism is a general repression of protein synthesis through an amino-acid starvation response. It lowers NRF2 as it lowers many proteins. It is **not** an NRF2-selective control for H5. The control for H5 should be NFE2L2 knockdown or a genetic arm, as the package said first.
7. **Two catalogue entries are two structures.** The bardoxolone acid (Kd 48.72 micromolar on KEAP1, SPR, used as a ligand for a degrader) and the methyl ester (EC50 60 nanomolar in an ARE-luciferase line) are separate molecules in ChEMBL; the numbers do not carry across. Whether the authors treat them as one was not reached.
8. **No neural data.** The only brain-derived material is BV-2 microglia and mouse tissue stains in 37187090 (abstract only); no document tests an NRF2 activator or KEAP1 binder in cultured neurons or astrocytes.

## Comparison and experiment (conditional)
* **Comparison.** Within one document the ester and its metabolite differ by about 16-fold in the printed Table 1; across documents nothing is comparable. A covalent electrophile and the non-covalent binder act through different parts of the KEAP1 mechanism and are compared on the **same cell readout that depends on NRF2**, not on binding potency. The engine's own rule (a "limit of" row removes a compound from the evidence; potency across systems NOT_COMPARABLE) gives the same answer for the pooled EC50s.
* **Experiment (replaces the IT43 version).** Genetic arm first (KEAP1 lowering or NFE2L2 raising, with NFE2L2 knockdown as the dependence control), then a covalent electrophile and the non-covalent binder, each with and without NFE2L2 knockdown, read on a target-gene set and not on NQO1 alone, in neurons and astrocytes separately, under one oxidative challenge, with the network outcome last. What raises the lead: a benefit lost under NFE2L2 knockdown for both; what lowers it: a benefit unchanged by knockdown (H5) or worsening (H6); a compartment-specific effect reopens H3.
* **Premise still missing, and not answerable by reading:** NRF2 protein, nuclear NRF2 and target-gene activity in a WWOX-deficient neural system, per compartment. A new measurement.
