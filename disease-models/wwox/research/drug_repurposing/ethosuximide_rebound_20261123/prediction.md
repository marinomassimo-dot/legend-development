# Ethosuximide, post-inhibitory rebound and tonic firing

Does the excess mean Wwox-null cortical rebound after a −200 pA pulse plausibly explain why the mean knockout cell is closer to threshold, and can another anti-burst mechanism of ethosuximide be discriminated from tonic firing suppression? Compute bounds by removing the entire group-mean rebound from the KO only, or from WT and KO separately. Use the published rest, threshold, rebound amplitude and input resistance of WT, heterozygote and neuronal KO layer II/III pyramidal neurons. Express threshold margin in mV and equivalent passive current; do not treat these derived quantities as measured rheobase or as individual-cell probabilities. For an extra T-type availability test use the independent measured native TC steady-state curve if available and both resting voltage and a passive end-of-prepulse estimate; show uncertainty from holding at a common voltage. Record how native ethosuximide affected tonic versus rebound firing in the same paper including preparation, sample unit and pharmacological exposure. A selective burst trigger hypothesis remains viable if synaptic input brings the cells close to threshold or native currents have a distinct state dependence. Reuse earlier HCN work without assigning rebound to one conductance.
