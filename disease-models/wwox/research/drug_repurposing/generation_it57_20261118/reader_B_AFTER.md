# Reader B (AFTER card): final message, copied unchanged (legend-delegate, sonnet requested)

**(1) First three actions**
1. Run a pilot, not the full study. Use wild-type and knockout pups, vehicle and agent, to fix the variability of the 60-minute summary, the assay age, and a stimulus that lifts wild type off the floor. The published effect size cannot power the study.
2. Check the dose at each age before any efficacy reading (exposure, not just administration), and set up the reference-agent panel.
3. Only then size the interaction contrast from the pilot's variability, with the genotype-by-treatment term fixed in advance as the primary test. The card's 180 to 320 per cell is a bracket, not a value.

**(2) Arms, controls, measurements**
- 2 x 2: genotype (wild type, knockout) by treatment (vehicle, agent), randomised and blinded. Sex recorded.
- Wild type also at two or more older ages.
- Reference agents with different age profiles, as an assay-sensitivity panel. Whether the age floor in the mouse matches the rat inference is unresolved.
- A selective T-type blocker beside this agent.
- Seizure type recorded per animal, plus a motor control.
- Dose or exposure check at each age.
- Readouts: 60-minute seizure summary, seizure type, motor performance, exposure.

**(3) What it cannot show, and what I would not claim**
- It cannot show that T-type channels mediate any effect. No T-type current has been measured in any WWOX system, and this agent's cell pharmacology is contested.
- A non-significant interaction at pilot power is not evidence of equal action. Wild type near the floor can fake specificity, and a ceiling does the reverse.
- One provoked-seizure model, one stimulus and one laboratory's lineage do not give clinical efficacy, human relevance or a mechanism.
- I would not extrapolate a rat age floor to the mouse, and I would not state any dose.

**(4) The decision I could not take from the card**
How large a wild-type effect, relative to the knockout effect, would count as "equal" and move the thread out of its group. The card names the consequence but gives no threshold, and without one the interaction result cannot be called decisive. I would ask whoever owns the thread's standing position.
