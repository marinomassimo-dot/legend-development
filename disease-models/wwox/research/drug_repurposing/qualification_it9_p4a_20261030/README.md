## 4. Bumetanide: what free brain concentration would be needed, and what is measured

* **Needed (target).** NKCC1 potency: ChEMBL HT-29 rubidium-uptake IC50 1,540 nM (human; two HEK293 single-concentration readings, 58.8 and 71.7 percent, are not potencies and are not converted); GtoPdb pIC50 5.60 (about 2.5 micromolar; PMID 11882915). A free concentration of about **1.5 to 2.5 micromolar** at the target is therefore the order to reach.
* **Measured (mouse, abstract level).** Brain to plasma ratio about 0.015 to 0.02 (PMID 25449033); free fraction in brain 6 to 17 percent for the three diuretics tested, and at the single 10 mg/kg intravenous dose modelled free brain concentrations in the NKCC1-inhibitory range (PMID 33279441); diuretic-range doses (0.1 to 0.5 mg/kg) do not reach neuronal inhibitory concentrations (PMID 30248303). US label (effective 2025-01-08 on the label read; prescription; boxed warning): plasma protein binding 94 to 96 percent, half-life 1 to 1.5 hours in adults and about 6 hours in neonates, **no peak concentration stated** (human plasma concentration UNKNOWN here).
* **Arithmetic, assumptions stated, not a prediction** (`bumetanide_exposure_arithmetic.tsv`): total brain concentration = free target / fraction unbound = about 9 to 42 micromolar; at a total brain to plasma ratio of 0.015 to 0.02 that corresponds to a total plasma concentration of about 450 to 2,800 micromolar in the mouse model. The figures combine different experiments and neither the neonatal barrier nor transporter maturation in the Wwox-null model is addressed. Wording: a systemic bumetanide response **requires verification of relevant exposure**; the ex vivo bath design is unaffected.
