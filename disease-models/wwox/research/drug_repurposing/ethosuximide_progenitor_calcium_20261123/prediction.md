# Does human neural progenitor expression support a CaV3.1-dependent fate mechanism?

Rat hippocampal cultures exposed to ethosuximide and embryonic cortical progenitors with reduced CaV3.1 predict opposing effects on neuron production. The question is whether the specific subunit is available in the human progenitor compartment and enriched by WWOX loss, before attempting to interpret direction.

Before looking at human CACNA1G results, select the sorted GSE65000 apical and basal radial-glia fractions and the neuronal fractions, with 1 FPKM as a primary detection floor and 0.1 and 5 FPKM sensitivities. In an independent cortex atlas, compare normalized CACNA1G detection in ventricular/dividing radial glia with the IPC-to-newborn excitatory neuron and early newborn excitatory clusters in sample-matched 10–20 week cortex. Include only samples with ≥5 cells in each class. Use ≥1 TPM and positive expression as sensitivity, and apply the same analysis to CACNA1H/I and lineage markers. The available data test broad availability and stage, never native current. Upper eligibility for a broad RG mechanism requires at least three of four apical radial-glia preparations ≥1 FPKM and a median sample RG fraction ≥10% at 1 TPM. These are prioritization thresholds rather than natural biological cutoffs. Finally test concordant genotype enrichment in human organoids using mean log2(1+CPM) and all single-library omissions. These gates cannot justify a safety or efficacy conclusion.
