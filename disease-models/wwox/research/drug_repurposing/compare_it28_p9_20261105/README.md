### 1b. Which single comparison suffices

Order of attribution questions (do not stack them):
1. Specificity first (R1 with a drug-by-genotype term, both genotypes treated, concurrent same-day PTZ-alone control). If no interaction survives, "is it WWOX-dependent" is answered and the Cav3 question reduces to a general question about a provoked response, which is known to be suppressible in animals without a mutation (stiripentol in PTZ-challenged non-epileptic rats, repository note section 11).
2. Only if the effect survives: ONE attribution comparison, chosen by what the first result looks like. The cheaper discriminating one is R4 (one drug, one added arm, exposure measured); R5 is cleaner but heavier. They are alternatives, not a pair.
3. R3 (levetiracetam) is a reference arm for the nonspecificity question, not an attribution tool for Cav3.

## 4. Null results and divergences preserved (not smoothed)

1. Cheng 2020: ethosuximide significant in the null only, but no interaction test, lithium acts in all genotypes and the paper rates lithium better. The wild-type arm is floor-limited, so the wild-type null is unknown, not clean.
2. The T-type attribution has no measurement behind it in Wwox tissue, and a measured non-T-type target (GIRK) sits at the labelled range in oocytes; a GIRK route would predict the opposite sign (repository, prior knowledge).
3. Interaction-tested drug-by-genotype precedent: in the Grin2b +/- rat ethosuximide cut SWD number in both genotypes (interaction p 0.71) and shortened SWD duration in heterozygotes only (interaction p 0.044, wild-type n 5) (PMID 40827489; statistics re-checked in the fetched text). Systemic memantine did not reduce SWD number against saline in the same design (p 0.96 in the text read) but reduced duration. Restriction appears only on a derived measure.
4. SB2193 (Cav3.1-preferring, brain 1.7 uM) did not help Scn1a +/- mice and increased SWD in GAERS, whereas Cacna1g deletion reduces Scn1a, Scn2a and kainate phenotypes (review, PMID 37822150, secondary) and ethosuximide reduces SWD in GAERS (cited by the authors). No reconciliation: sub-IC50 exposure, free fraction, Na+ off-target, a CB1 metabolite and isoform mix are each offered and none tested.
5. Isoform signs diverge: Cacna1g KO resists GBL/baclofen SWD but not bicuculline or 4-aminopyridine seizures (PMID 37822150, secondary); Cacna1i (Cav3.3) KO loses over 70 percent of nRT T-current yet has increased GHB sensitivity (PMID 40564020, secondary); Cav3.2 KO increases excitability in intermediate and mature dentate granule cells (PMID 40068874). A pan-Cav3 block has isoform-level reasons to move in either direction.
6. Wang 2015: SWD from a Cav3.2 gain-of-function variant is suppressed by glutamate-receptor antagonists and not by T-type antagonists (PMID 26220996, abstract): an absence-type phenotype that Cav3 pharmacology did not rescue.
7. Huguenard 2019 review (PMID 30955423, repository note): T-type-dependent relay-neuron bursting may not be essential for SWD, questioning the usual ethosuximide mechanism.
8. Absence seizures v other types: the Cav3 literature above is absence-type (GAERS, WAG/Rij, Cacng2/Cacna1a, Gabrb3 atypical absence). Apinocaltamide reduced convulsive audiogenic and MEST responses only at higher concentration and not focal kindling (abstract). Sodium-channel blockers aggravated spiking in the Hcn1 DEE mouse and SWD in GAERS (cited). Whether the Wwox PTZ pup phenotype is closer to absence-type or to clonic seizures is UNKNOWN here (the carded endpoint is a Racine behavioural stage; no EEG).
9. The only developmental safety datum is class-level and in vitro: progenitor viability falls with T-type blockers that reach Cav3.1, ML218 excepted. Not tested with ethosuximide, flunarizine or efonidipine.

## 5. Next test (one design, not a protocol)

1. Both genotypes (null and wild type, heterozygote if available), treated and untreated, each with a concurrent same-day PTZ-alone control, an explicit drug-by-genotype term and a pre-specified late-phase window. Arms: ethosuximide (the reference), levetiracetam (nonspecificity reference), and one nM-potency pan-Cav3 compound with brain exposure measured at or above its isoform IC50. Primary readout: late-phase stage. Lithium stays as the in-paper comparator (INV-09).
2. If, and only if, ethosuximide and the Cav3 compound agree in the null and the interaction survives: add ONE occlusion arm, Cacna1g deletion in the null with ethosuximide. If the interaction does not survive, stop at the specificity answer.
3. Falsifiers fixed in advance: an effect of equal size in wild type; no effect of the Cav3 compound at on-target exposure; an effect that tracks GIRK or Na+ activity rather than Cav3 potency; levetiracetam equal to ethosuximide.
Efonidipine (racemate, then R(-)) and flunarizine are secondary: they earn a place only as the L-versus-T and channel-breadth contrasts after a result with a measured-exposure Cav3 compound, not as its replacement.
