Juvenile neuronal-Wwox-deletion mouse cortical EPSC amplitudes and burst pharmacology, P17 mouse cortical and hippocampal RNA, adult hypomorphic P47T mouse hippocampal junctions, week-15 human cerebral organoid RNA, and functional antagonist concentrations in defined recombinant and native synaptic preparations.

**Estimates retired.** The proposed 8/25 pools a threshold response with sustained complete remission, and the proposed inferential contrast against 98/137 combines incompatible populations and endpoints. The adjusted OR is a cohort association. A WOREE response estimate, NNT or NNH is indeterminate without comparable baseline and treatment risks in a defined target population. The reported N-of-1 model value remains unverified without its code, assumptions and data. Carryover, background ASM exposure and baseline vigilance remain design questions. Prespecify seizure outcomes, wakefulness and behavior separately in any later clinical design. No WWOX-specific efficacy probability is assigned here.

## WWOX evidence and the two physiological questions

**Cortical data.** Breton, Aquilino et al. found larger miniature excitatory postsynaptic currents at −70 mV in P13–17 neuronal-Wwox-deletion neocortex. Decays around 5 ms make an AMPA-like quantal contribution plausible. The recordings did not separately isolate AMPA and NMDA components, and pooled-event statistics do not establish animal-level precision. The reversible disappearance of spontaneous bursts with d-APV is direct evidence for NMDAR dependence in that preparation (51 recorded bursts, five slices, three animals). The electrically evoked delayed response is a different endpoint and was suppressed, with a residual measurement floor. [Breton et al., Results §2.2, Figs. 3–4 and Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609180/) · [LEGEND source audit](../ampa_nmda_discrim_20261108/output/README.md)

**Cortical inference and experiment.** An AMPA-like quantum can be larger even when total AMPA RNA is stable or falls. An antagonist that stops bursts can act by removing the depolarization that recruits NMDA current. Carry forward the IT41 design: quantify fast evoked excitatory-current block, NMDA current, resting potential, input resistance, and time-to-steady state in the same preparation and medium used to record spontaneous bursts. Use GYKI 53655 and competitive NBQX as different pocket mechanisms, and d-APV as the NMDAR comparison. Perampanel and GYKI share an allosteric collar in AMPARs. NBQX also antagonizes kainate receptors, and its efficacy can depend on agonist levels. A binding preference is insufficient to declare a functional AMPA-selective window. Treat perampanel as a compound comparison, given its activity at some kainate assemblies. If spontaneous bursts persist during an independently verified near-complete fast AMPA block and d-APV then suppresses them, the simple AMPA-required explanation weakens. If bursts stop with either intervention, that alone leaves upstream drive and downstream NMDAR regeneration compatible. Use an independent release-probability measure or matched-drive control to interpret the change, and retain animal as the replication unit. [IT41 evidence and thresholds](../ampa_comparison_it41_20261109/README.md)

**Transcript tests.**

- The P17 neuronal-deletion hippocampal estimate for the Grik1c-like insert was 38.0%→55.1% of classified transcripts (two control and three KO libraries). The corresponding cortex estimate went down 5.4 percentage points. P17 neural-lineage and global deletion hippocampal differences were −4.4 and +3.0 points. In neuronal-deletion hippocampus, single-library omissions preserve the positive contrast. Combining omission with adversarial assignment of unclassified isoforms allows −1.3 points. A bound based on observed broad cell-class mixture was 9.42 points, below the central estimate but above its conservative lower bound. The pooled nuclei resolve neither within-class splicing nor the CA3 terminal protein. The adult P47T hypomorph direct-junction result had the same primary sign (+16.9 points), with 38 and 74 informative fragments, failed coverage criteria and had a range crossing zero under single-fragment reassignment. It may motivate a test. It does not independently validate juvenile null splicing. No comparable human Grik1c inference was established. [GRIK1 quantification and mixture audit](../perampanel_kainate_20261123/README.md)
- The Grik5-to-Grik2 relative RNA contrast was +0.83 log2 units in P17 neuronal-deletion hippocampus, +0.29 in cortex and −0.40 in human cerebral organoids. RNA ratios leave localization and native subunit stoichiometry unmeasured. Mouse Gria2 flip share fell 1.60 points in neuronal-deletion cortex, with no robust hippocampal enrichment. Only five informative splice-junction reads supported the corresponding human classification. For human GRIA2 Q/R editing, 30 reads qualified at the site and only 14 also crossed the downstream splice junction. The 14 all held the edited base, leaving a modest genotype difference unresolved. A subtype-selective calcium-permeability premise remains a hypothesis requiring physiology. [GRIK tests](../perampanel_kainate_20261123/README.md) · [AMPA splicing](../perampanel_ampa_splicing_20261123/README.md) · [Q/R editing](../perampanel_gria2_editing_20261123/README.md)

**Hippocampal hypothesis and experiment.** Taniguchi et al. measured postsynaptic kainate EPSC inhibition at mossy fibre→CA3 (IC50 2.9 µM, CI 1.9–4.9, with appreciable native rundown). An independently established immature CA3→CA1 presynaptic pathway can restrain release through GluK1c-related signaling. The GluK1 construct used for perampanel pharmacology was GluK1b. Hypothesis: one may observe a fall in postsynaptic kainate current while release at another connection increases, stays stable or changes in a different direction. In the juvenile WWOX comparison, identify the presynaptic pathway and use a release readout such as a validated presynaptic glutamate sensor or release-probability assay, with paired-pulse and stable postsynaptic-response controls. Compare selective GluK1 perturbation with perampanel separately from a postsynaptic mossy-fibre kainate EPSC assay. A fall in detected AMPA events under an AMPA antagonist confounds transmission, detection and release. [Taniguchi et al., Fig. 6 and recombinant constructs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9295835/) · [NETO1-dependent presynaptic release, Fig. 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5494894/) · [Juvenile GluK1c re-expression, Figs. 3 and 6–7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3298885/)

## One modelling question for the founder

**At comparable receptor states, do reciprocal substitutions at GluK2 Asn557 and GluK5 Asp540 reproducibly change a physically plausible perampanel contact pattern in the direction of the functional controls, or is the result primarily sensitive to the geometry of the receptor state?**

| Part of the comparison | Inputs and interpretation |
|---|---|
| Functional anchors | Taniguchi Table 1 and Fig. 4. GluK2 ≈26 µM and GluK2/GluK5 0.85 µM in the tested recombinant constructs without Neto. GluK2(N557D) shifts toward greater inhibition (2.8 µM). GluK2/GluK5(D540N) shifts the opposite way (3.0 µM). The homomer fit is weak near the top of the dose series. Score the directional shifts and uncertainty before interpreting fold differences. Functional IC50 measures a current response under one recording protocol. |
| Public structures and state controls | GluA2–perampanel 5L1F as the AMPA reference, withheld-pose GluK2–perampanel 8FWW as the within-preparation ligand recovery control, and GluK2 8FWS/8FWU as alternatives in the same series. Retain and document BPAM344 and relevant modeled environment. Use 9OSF as the first GluK2/GluK5 template given local side-chain completeness, and 9OSG/9OSI as state-sensitivity controls. 7KS0/7KS3 provide older topology and state context, with incomplete GluK5 Asp540 terminal atoms. Group plausible matched states by local collar/pore geometry, not chain letters or ligand labels alone. The perampanel pose in the heteromer remains a prediction. |
| Observable and practicality | Ask the founder which reproducible method they can run. Freeze inputs, state criteria, side-chain ensemble and ranking rules. Check heavy-atom RMSD corrected for symmetry (≤2 Å operational control), pocket placement and gross geometry on the withheld 8FWW reference. Record pose families, Asp/Asn contact persistence and local receptor geometry including the GluK2 Phe658/GluK5 Phe642 orientation, with the same treatment of ions, membrane components and co-modulators across each matched comparison. Treat scoring differences as method-dependent observables. Return exact structures, settings and replicate/state results. |
| Outcome changes the next experiment | A contact contrast that survives state, geometry and preparation checks and recovers the reciprocal functional directions prioritizes **direct perampanel binding or occupancy plus current** in matched WT and mutant receptor preparations. A model that recovers the holo control and shows the strongest reproducible variation with state prioritizes **occupancy paired with current across defined agonist/preactivation conditions** in the same constructs. If the control fails, placement is physically implausible, chemistry and state covary, or the direction changes with input state, the model leaves binding versus gating unresolved. The next comparison then starts from the functional constructs and a ligand-specific binding readout rather than selecting a mutant experiment from the pose. |

[RCSB GluA2 reference](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/5L1F) · [RCSB GluK2 holo](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/8FWW) · [GluK2 state controls](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/8FWS) · [Additional GluK2 control](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/8FWU) · [Heteromer 9OSF](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/9OSF) · [9OSG](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/9OSG) · [9OSI](https://www.rcsb.org/structure/9OSI) · [Earlier heteromer templates](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.66097) · [Functional benchmark](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9295835/)

**Existing LEGEND baselines bound the proposal.** In the 8FWW holo-derived coordinate state, one short CPU Vina run recovered the withheld pose at 0.603 Å. A GluA2 rigid transfer gave 5.791 Å and 12 heavy-atom pairs under 2 Å. A reciprocal 8FWW within-preparation fit gave 0.377 Å. Rigid transfer into 9OSF collided with the GluK5 Phe642 region despite a close collar fit. A ligand-bound Phe orientation knocked down much of that clash while leaving a residual local collision. Opening the corresponding Phe ring in 8FWS did not recover a top-scoring reference-like pose, despite one locally optimized low-RMSD geometry. These controls point to state, fit and scoring as confounders. Local map quality around Asp540 is uneven. The current audit justifies the proposed comparison and leaves a direct Asp contact assignment unresolved. Any demonstration of ligand occupancy requires a ligand-specific assay with known sensitivity, including an AMPA positive control and comparable receptor abundance. Distinguishing binding from gating requires matched functional recordings. Neither structural outcome predicts WWOX clinical benefit or GluK1c release effects.

## Primary-source locators and evidential coverage

- **Zeng et al.** Methods, “≥ 50% reduction from baseline in monthly seizure frequency”. Results at 12 months, the epileptic-spasm and IESS rows, and the corresponding Fig. 3C–D caption. Table 1 gives baseline classifications, not the 12-month denominators.
- **Matsuura et al.** Primary abstract, Methods, “complete remission of epileptic spasms for more than 3 months”. The 0/2 is derived from all observed remissions being structural and two participants having a genetic etiology.
- **Kim et al.** Primary abstract, Results, “73.5 % of patients with PBAEs continued PER treatment”. The percentage is conditional on the affected subgroup.
- **Miao et al.** Table 1 and adjusted-results paragraph, “developmental delay (odds ratio = 0.406, 95% CI = 0.170–0.970”. Cohort predictor, not a risk in WOREE.
- **Meador et al.** Methods, “Continuity of Attention (a measure of sustained attention)” and Table 2. Cognition difference in T-score points, vigilance and somnolence kept separate.
- **Taniguchi and Breton.** Existing coordinate and electrophysiological source audits carry the panel-level claims. No new whole-article read or modeled heteromeric binding result is claimed.
