# Ganaxolone after a burst in WWOX-deficient cortex

**Decision.** Add recovery of native neuronal chloride between bursts to the ganaxolone experiment. Measure firing during the burst separately. An additional GABA-A conductance can carry chloride into a depolarized cell during a burst and out of a chloride-loaded cell once it repolarizes. The sign over the entire cycle depends on how long and how strongly the drug recruits the conductance in each phase. We found quantitative support for testing this switch in other models. The studies do not establish which direction ganaxolone takes in WWOX-deficient tissue.

## How long is the relevant event?

We recovered the first eight bin heights of the published Wwox-deletion cortical field-burst histogram from the high-resolution original. Seven bins wholly below 2 s contain 110 events. Two further events lie in the bin spanning approximately 1.97–2.22 s. Consequently **only 7–9 of 119 events (5.9–7.6%) could have lasted longer than 2 s**. The events came from 18 slices of nine P13–17 mice. Event counts cannot give the proportion of animals with prolonged bursts. Digital bar heights stayed at the same integers under half-pixel perturbations of the calibrated axes and bar tops. In the independent focal pilocarpine study below, the late intervention was actually delivered after a mean of 3.91 s in GFP controls and 3.19 s with KCC2 overexpression. Detection latencies and event definitions differ across preparations. The late effect in this external model is a poor proxy for a typical WWOX slice burst. [Wwox study, Fig. 1D](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609180/) · [Digitized counts](wwox_burst_histogram.tsv)

## A sign-changing prediction for ganaxolone

At a fixed voltage history and chloride equilibrium potential, the added chloride flux is proportional to the time integral of the added chloride conductance times (membrane voltage minus chloride equilibrium potential). A positive integral means chloride enters the cell. The Wwox model gives a mean resting voltage of −73.62 mV, median event length of 0.66 s and reported interval of 26.2 s. We treat that interval as onset-to-onset and use 71.4 s as a sensitivity from the conflicting supplementary frequency. Event voltage, native chloride and the relative drug-induced conductance during the event and the interval were unmeasured.

- **If chloride is already loaded:** taking ECl = −65 mV and the average burst voltage as −40 mV, the chloride-only model predicts net efflux for steady tonic recruitment. The sign reverses only when the extra conductance during the burst exceeds **13.34 times** the added interburst conductance. With a constant-field permeability model the equivalent crossover is **10.43 times** burst-to-rest permeability. Both agree on efflux for constant recruitment. With ECl = −75 mV, both the modeled resting and burst voltages drive chloride inward. The equivalent charge outputs use the prior 10 nM cross-model ganaxolone whole-cell and density scaling and assume the entire assigned extra conductance carries chloride. Their magnitudes are illustrative; the crossover is independent of that shared scale. [Original conductance transfer](../ganaxolone_tonic_conductance_20261123/README.md) · [360 scenarios](flux_scenarios.tsv)
- **An observed GABA reversal of −65 mV would not resolve the direction.** At 32 °C, 130 mM extracellular chloride, 25/16 mM extracellular/intracellular bicarbonate and a bicarbonate-to-chloride permeability ratio of 0.2, the same mixed reversal corresponds to ECl = −72.67 mV. With constant recruitment the ohmic model now gives a small net efflux and the constant-field model a small influx. At 20 mM intracellular bicarbonate, both give influx. These scenarios use assumed ionic activities approximated by concentrations, fixed pH and fixed voltage. Neither cell volume nor transporter flux is fitted. This is a reason to measure intracellular chloride and pH alongside voltage. [Anion sensitivity](anion_sensitivity.tsv)
- **A lower burden of brief bursts is itself a confounder.** To separate reduced loading through fewer bursts from increased recovery after the same load, compare ganaxolone with vehicle after the same conditioning activity and initial intracellular chloride, with interburst voltage and elapsed time matched. Compare the early fall in chloride and the residual immediately before the next burst. A polarity or slope difference at matched activity supports a direct conductance contribution. A difference confined to unmatched spontaneous firing would leave an indirect activity explanation open. Include a separate delayed, sustained-event arm if enough long events occur. The high-chloride recording pipette used in the original Wwox study cannot supply these native values. [Previous WWOX measurement audit](../chloride_gradient_sci42_20261110/README.md)

## Two independent experimental controls

**Direct steroid challenge.** Yelhekar, Druzin and Johansson studied dissociated medial preoptic neurons from 3–5-week-old male rats. In bicarbonate-free recording solution, after experimental chloride loading and at −74 mV with KCC2 blocked, 20 nM allopregnanolone improved five-minute chloride recovery in the presence of TTX (Fig. 7C; TTX 11 cells, steroid + TTX 16; p = 0.039). Gabazine removed the steroid effect. It was not significant at ten minutes (p = 0.15), and resting current showed no detectable change in 11 cells. In a separate comparison (Fig. 7E), normalized chloride remaining at five minutes was about 0.62 in controls versus 0.48 with steroid (13 and 16 cells, graph estimates). The authors attribute the low-concentration effect to miniature synaptic currents. The drug was allopregnanolone. Extending this result to its synthetic analog ganaxolone, intact cortex, native bicarbonate or WWOX deficiency requires a new measurement. [Experimental report, Figs. 4 and 7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5362935/)

**Causal cortical timing control.** Magloire et al. activated parvalbumin interneurons in awake adult mice with focal pilocarpine-induced seizures. For each animal we recomputed stimulation-on duration relative to its own stimulation-off duration in the source workbook, and independently recovered each ratio from the absolute durations in Supplementary Fig. 7. Immediate stimulation shortened seizures in 8/8 GFP controls (mean −25.84%) and 10/10 mice with increased principal-cell KCC2 (−27.58%). After a delay >2 s, all six controls had longer seizures (+45.77%, +2.77 s on average), compared with a mean −0.64% in eight KCC2 animals (+0.03 s using absolute seconds). Five of the latter eight ratios still exceeded 100%. The late-minus-immediate change in the GFP–KCC2 contrast was **44.67 percentage points**, or 23.84–58.37 after independently omitting one animal from each of four comparison cells. These are descriptive influence bounds, not confidence intervals. The late sample necessarily selects seizures that persist. In 27% of long-delay trials the authors used an earlier duration-eligible control event. This analysis neither administered ganaxolone nor measured a WWOX phenotype. [Magloire et al., Figs. 4–5 and Supplementary Fig. 7](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-08933-4) · [Animal-level summaries](timing_by_arm.tsv) · [Sensitivity](timing_interaction.tsv)

A numerical difference in the source has a reproducible explanation. For GFP at 0.5, 2 and >2 s the workbook summary matches the ratio of group-average durations. Each animal row is its own within-animal ratio. Averaging the latter gives +45.77% for late GFP; the ratio of group averages gives +50.78%. Immediate GFP and all KCC2 summaries match the mean of individual ratios. Using the displayed summaries throughout gives a 49.69-point interaction. This aggregation choice changes the magnitude and preserves the direction.

In the same paper, the mean time constants of chloride-sensitive current recovery after a separate loading protocol were 46.47 s in seven control cells and 16.60 s in seven cells with increased KCC2. Applying each cell's fitted exponential to the **hypothetical** 25.54 s between Wwox field bursts leaves a mean 48.4% versus 19.1% of the starting current deviation. At 70.74 s these become 19.9% versus 3.0%. The cells were adult layer V visual cortical pyramidal neurons, not the juvenile Wwox layer II/III preparation. These are transferred **current-recovery fractions** and do not measure intracellular chloride in WWOX. [Cell-level calculation](recovery_transfer.tsv)

## Reproduction and scope

The initial [prediction](prediction.md) preceded the flux calculation. The external timing workbook, source-mean audit, bounded histogram readback and bicarbonate sensitivity were exploratory checks. Run `python3 analyze.py <source-workbook.xlsx>` and `python3 histogram.py <high-resolution-figure.jpg>`. The second script uses ImageMagick to inspect the published image. Both scripts check the original file hash. Only new derived tables, code and attribution are distributed. The cited primary papers and the Magloire source workbook are open under CC BY 4.0. ## Independent check

At 16:23 UTC on 23 November, the independent laboratory chat returned an ACK and reproduced each animal-level ratio, the 44.67-point interaction, the 23.84–58.37-point omission range and the 49.69-point workbook-summary result. We accept its qualification that long-delay trials select persistent seizures. Only 7–9 of 119 Wwox events could cross two seconds. Interburst chloride recovery in native Wwox neurons therefore remains the primary ganaxolone comparison.
