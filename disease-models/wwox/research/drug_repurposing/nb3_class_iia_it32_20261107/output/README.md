## Answer (A): every INV-16 step has a neural precedent; none is on SLC2A1 and none is in human cells

1. DATO: an HDAC5 catalytic-function-loss arm exists in neurons. Two cysteine substitutions in the catalytic domain removed deacetylase activity in vitro but kept HDAC3 binding, MEF2C binding and shuttling; in rat nucleus accumbens neurons they abolished the effect of nuclear HDAC5 on a behaviour and on Scn4b mRNA (PMID 39923817, E01 to E03; full text, supplement not read). This corrects the IT28 P3-B statement that no neural HDAC5 catalytic arm was found.
2. DATO: the same paper's catalytic-site mutant (D870N) is used in the main text only in the in-vitro activity assay, not in neurons; the authors say another binding partner cannot be excluded (E02).
3. DATO: scaffold-type or location-type findings in neurons exist for other paralogs: HDAC4 catalytic-site mutant rescued survival (E15, module-held); HDAC4 mushroom-body phenotype independent of the catalytic site but the eye phenotype dependent on it, in the same fly paper (E04, E05); HDAC7 knockdown acted where TMP269 did not (E08, abstract only).
4. DATO: inhibitor beside knockdown in neural cells exists for HDAC5 (and HDAC9): MC1568 and siRNA agree on neurite growth in SH-SY5Y and rat dopaminergic cultures (E09); LMK235 with class I and HDAC6 controls (E18, abstract only); MC1568 beside HDAC4/5 knockdown on NCX3, inhibition raising the output (E07, abstract only). MC1568 selectivity is disputed in the module (IT28 P3-B).
5. INFERENZA: catalytic dependence is paralog-, cell-type- and output-dependent (items 1 to 3), so none of the alternatives is excluded: catalytic and scaffold functions both matter, and the answer depends on cell type and substrate. No source reads SLC2A1, a glycolytic gene, or human neural cells.
6. INFERENZA: the first INV-16 step with a precedent that needs no new reagent is knockdown with HDAC4 and HDAC9 compensation checks (E09, E11); without a measured SLC2A1 change after knockdown there is nothing to rescue.
7. IPOTESI: the second step is rescue with wild type against the cysteine mutant on a nuclear-locked background (E01), which has a published activity assay; discriminating result: if only the active protein restores SLC2A1, a catalytic role is supported; if both restore it, scaffold function. The inhibitor arm should come last, with target engagement shown, because no paper found pairs a class IIa-selective inhibitor with a catalytic-dead arm in one neural system (E07 to E09 and E01 are different papers).
8. DATO, counter: where "activity" was blocked in vivo the inhibitor was pan-HDAC (TSA), and the authors favour location over activity (E11, E12).

## Re-audit (verify/audit_nb3.md, blind, abstract and open full text; all 24 quotes verbatim)
Corrections and qualifications taken from it: (1) PMID 23141539 is mouse, and its authors conclude that neither neuronal HDAC4 function needs substrate deacetylation; the catalytic-site mutant there is a five-residue Ala substitution scored on survival, not a transcript. (2) PMID 39923817: D870N also lost activity in HEK cells (so the CSCS loss is read as intrinsic to the enzyme), the CSCS pair was also tested on wild-type HDAC5, the in vivo outputs are a behaviour and a tissue transcript (Scn4b), and the authors say loss of binding to another deacetylase-bearing factor cannot be excluded; no SLC2A1 or glucose readout exists. (3) PMID 31572723 merges two systems: siRNA in SH-SY5Y, MC1568 neurite growth in rat midbrain dopaminergic cultures. (4) PMID 24657831 concerns ischemic rat white matter at day 7, not naive tissue. (5) Inhibitor and knockdown agree in four rows and disagree in one (TMP269 inert while HDAC7 knockdown was active). (6) Catalytic dependence is output-specific (PMID 28957664/30962285: HDAC5/HDAC9 deacetylase domains not required for the anti-hypertrophic action). (7) The bounded null for the myelin route rests partly on a keyword scan of one review and on title-level triage: 'not found in the routes searched', not 'does not exist'.
