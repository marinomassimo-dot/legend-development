# Memantine, magnesium and inhibitory versus excitatory neurons in WWOX loss

**Decision.** Prioritize a paired measurement of native excitatory and inhibitory neuronal responses over further pulse-timing simulations. Magnesium unmasks a substantial difference in memantine sensitivity between recombinant GluN2B/D and GluN2B/B receptors. The predicted WWOX-specific increase in the relative supply of the D subunit failed in juvenile cortex and human organoids. Enrichment of all three transcripts in inhibitory neurons also creates a competing hypothesis. Preferential suppression of excitatory input to those cells could weaken inhibition of the network. The sign of this circuit effect remains open. ## Two denominators for the same drug effect

Yi et al. measured recombinant GluN1-1a receptors carrying two B, one B plus one D, or two D subunits in oocytes. Their dose–response curves used continuous 300 µM glutamate and 100 µM glycine. At −60 mV without magnesium, the fitted curves predict 37.5% block for both B/B and B/D at 0.36 µM memantine. At 1 mM extracellular magnesium, the mixed receptor exceeds B/B by 11.59 percentage points of the residual current, above the prespecified five-point threshold. In a mixture containing only B/B and B/D, more than 43% of the residual current must come from B/D to differ from pure B/B by five points. The concentration is an adult CSF reference. Free juvenile WWOX brain exposure is unknown. [Yi et al., Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6858497/) · [All curves and exposure sensitivities](block.tsv)

| B/B | 2.49 | 2.85 | 0.071 |

At −40 mV the additional loss is 0.47, 5.36 and 12.80 points respectively. At −20 mV the relative block is 2.66%, 10.11% and 20.45%, but the study lacks the matching Mg-only curve. Memantine was not measured at −80 mV in Table 3. Multiplication combines two fitted experimental curves. These are deterministic estimates from recombinant receptors, with four to six oocytes per drug curve. The variation in the table from shifting IC50 by one reported SEM measures sensitivity to that parameter only. [Source fits](https://doi.org/10.1113/JP278168)

## Does WWOX loss supply more D relative to B?

Using the knockout–control difference in per-library log2(expression + 1) for D minus B, neuronal-deletion juvenile cortex gave +0.231 (one-library omission +0.109 to +0.359). Human knockout organoids gave −0.387 (−0.619 to −0.078). Both failed the prespecified requirement of a +0.5 or greater shift in each model. Juvenile neuronal-deletion hippocampus gave +1.832, driven by loss of B (−1.885) with little change in D (−0.053). The neural-lineage hippocampus gave +0.589 and the whole-body knockout +0.175, with the latter changing sign on omission. In adult P47T hippocampus the sex-balanced contrast was −0.028, with opposite signs in females (−0.385) and males (+0.330). Cell composition, region, age and allele limit transfer. Juvenile hippocampal nuclei co-detecting Grin1, Grin2b and Grin2d were more frequent among inhibitory than excitatory cells in each of the two pools. Counts were 64/246 versus 142/1,596 in control and 64/293 versus 137/1,262 in knockout. After random thinning to exactly 3,000 RNA molecules per eligible nucleus, expected co-detection was 8.52% versus 2.98% in control and 9.48% versus 4.14% in knockout. There was one pool per genotype. These counts establish a candidate population. They cannot establish protein assembly, a genotype effect or the magnitude of membrane current in cortical interneurons. ## What can native pharmacology distinguish?

In healthy P21–28 mouse hippocampal CA1 interneurons, two subtype-sensitive probes at 10 µM reduced synaptic NMDA responses at +40 mV by 70% (CP-101,606) and 43% (NAB-14). In principal cells the corresponding group means were 50% and 10%. Separate groups contributed seven interneurons per probe. These source measurements allow an exploratory check on what can be inferred about receptor mixtures. We used the fitted drug/subtype curves in Table 2 and bounded weak, unfitted effects at 0–10% for CP on D/D and 0–20% for NAB on B/B. [Yi et al., Fig. 6 and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6858497/) · [Feasible mixtures](mixture.tsv)

Using the NAB calibration without added cyclodextrin, fitting both interneuron probe means exactly requires a 28.1–69.3% B/D current fraction in the restricted three-component model. Allowing three percentage points of error per probe admits **zero B/D**, while still requiring 28.8–77.6% D-bearing current (B/D or D/D). Applying the recombinant memantine fits to these mixtures gives 9.65–12.12% predicted block at −60 mV if component weights remain fixed, or 12.74–25.39% when weights change in proportion to the Mg-only residuals. Under the alternative NAB calibration with cyclodextrin, no mixture fits within three points. Feasible mixtures return at six points. Tolerances are illustrative. Separate cell samples, omitted receptor types, different voltages, phasic versus continuous stimulation and formulation can all change these bounds. Memantine itself was not tested on these native responses. [Mixture assumptions and calculation](mixture.py)

### A third drug challenges the two-probe estimate

A held-out comparison directly tests whether the inferred current fractions transfer to another ligand. Independent of fitting the two antagonists, Table 4 gives changes of +111%, +50% and −29% with 100 µM spermine at pH 7.4 for B/B, B/D and D/D. The compatible two-probe mixtures then predict +68.7 to +71.2% potentiation under exact matching, or +63.7 to +76.1% with three points of tolerance. Native CA1 interneurons instead showed **+37 ± 13%** (mean ± SEM, seven cells). Moving every recombinant calibration by one reported SEM expands the three-point prediction to +60.8 to +80.2%. It still misses the native mean and its one-SEM interval. The sensitivity ranges are deterministic and the native sample is small. This is a failure of quantitative transfer under the tested assumptions. Channel block by spermine depends on voltage, and the recombinant calibration at −20 mV differs from the native +40 mV recording. The discrepancy does not invalidate B/D expression. [Independent quantitative prediction](heldout.tsv) · [Yi et al., Table 4 and Fig. 6G–I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6858497/)

Two additional observations delimit competing explanations. MPX-004, a GluN2A-selective probe, inhibited native principal-cell responses by 55% versus 2% in the sampled interneurons (both groups seven cells). Pure B/B is therefore an inadequate representation of the principal-cell comparator. The subtype-sensitive probes changed interneuron EPSC amplitude without a statistically detectable change in decay. This gives independent qualitative support to the authors’ mixed-receptor interpretation, since pure B/B and D/D receptors deactivate at different rates. Both qualifications strengthen the case for directly measuring native current under memantine before estimating a network effect. [Yi et al., Results and Fig. 6E–K](https://doi.org/10.1113/JP278168)

**Discriminating prediction.** In juvenile Wwox-deficient cortex, compare memantine-sensitive NMDA charge on inhibitory and excitatory neurons under identical extracellular Mg, voltage, input pattern and exposure. Then compare recruitment of each population and network bursts with native voltage free to change. Selective loss of interneuron recruitment with preserved excitatory drive predicts disinhibition. Preferential loss of excitatory drive with preserved interneuron recruitment predicts a different network response. A percentage loss requires the matching absolute charge when comparing cell classes. No tested dataset establishes developmental rescue or seizure benefit from memantine in WWOX deficiency.
