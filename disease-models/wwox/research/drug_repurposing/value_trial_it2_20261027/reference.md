## Sources (abstract depth)
- PL23  = PMID 37428623, Plecko 2023, J Inherit Metab Dis (review). Vitamin B6-dependent epilepsies: ALDH7A1, PNPO, ALPL, ALDH4A1, PLPBP, GPI-anchor defects, all reduce pyridoxal 5'-phosphate (PLP) availability; pyridoxine response also seen in MOCS2 deficiency and KCNQ2; biomarkers exist for PNPO, ALDH7A1, ALDH4A1, ALPL, GPI defects, none for PLPHP deficiency; secondary elevation of glycine or lactate is a diagnostic pitfall; a standardized vitamin B6 trial is advocated for newborns.
- NA24  = PMID 39543701, Naghavi 2024, J Med Case Rep. PNPO deficiency: responsive to PLP and "in some cases" pyridoxine; the reported 10-year-old was seizure-free at 6 months on pyridoxine alone (full text, verifier).
- NI24  = PMID 38407561, Nishino 2024, Am J Med Genet A. One girl, WWOX c.516+1G>A homozygous with paternal uniparental isodisomy, exon 5 homozygously deleted in mRNA; "vitamin B6 and zonisamide were administered ... however, the seizures were not relieved"; later AED changes and ACTH also failed. B6 was given TOGETHER with zonisamide and before the genetic diagnosis (made at 5 months), so relief or its absence cannot be attributed to B6 alone. Abstract-level.
- YO24  = PMID 39101447, You 2024, Mol Genet Genomic Med. One girl, WWOX c.172+1G>C homozygous, minigene splicing defect; seizures subsided after vigabatrin (not B6, not a sodium channel blocker).
- WO17  = PMID 28379373, Wolff 2017, Brain. SCN2A: 71 new + 130 reviewed patients; treatment course reviewed in 66; sodium channel blockers (SCB) often gave clinically relevant seizure reduction or freedom in early infantile epilepsy (<3 months), were rarely effective in onset >=3 months and sometimes induced worsening; truncating mutations only in late-onset non-responders; early-onset = gain-of-function, late-onset non-responders = loss-of-function; good SCB response associated with a "relatively small gain-of-function". Retrospective; the authors "suggest a correlation" (association, not a rule). Abstract-level.
- MO19  = PMID 31353122, Mori 2019, Brain Dev. One boy, heterozygous 16q22.2-q23.1 interstitial deletion (6.8 Mb) that includes or ends inside WWOX (abstract: WWOX "was included within the 6.8-Mb deleted region"; the repository's partial full-text dossier PMID31353122 says the distal breakpoint lies inside WWOX; exons not stated; the dossier is a secondary witness, not a verification), West syndrome; seizures stopped with lamotrigine + valproate after phenobarbital, VPA, zonisamide failed; "no pathogenic variants were detected in the other allele of WWOX" (per the dossier, by an exon-targeted panel only; introns, RNA and parental testing not done - unverified at the paywalled text); the authors say other genes may cause the syndrome. Valproate is not a classical SCB and lamotrigine was never given alone.
- RI26  = PMID 42190144, Riccardi 2026, Neurology. 110-patient burst-suppression EIDEE cohort; abstract lists WWOX among genes with pathogenic variants (gene symbols are stripped from the abstract text; which genes the "early SCB use in selected cases" refers to cannot be read from the abstract).
- WA25  = PMID 41443103, Wang 2026 (epub 2025-12-23), Int Immunopharmacol. LPS-induced acute lung injury model (species not stated in the abstract), lung epithelial cells: WWOX overexpression mitigates erastin/LPS-aggravated ferroptosis; WWOX mediates p53 deacetylation and raises SLC7A11; ferrostatin-1 used to show ferroptosis involvement. Authors call WWOX a potential target for ALI.
- YA25  = PMID 39894307, Yang 2025, Biochem Pharmacol. Hepatocellular carcinoma cells and xenograft: toosendanin (TSN) induces ferroptosis dependent on WWOX, via p53/SAT1 and NRF2/FPN1 (SLC40A1); co-treatment with ML385 (NRF2 inhibitor) and MIRA-1 (p53 activator).
- LU25  = PMID 39955305, Luo 2025, Sci Rep. Paediatric B-ALL; WWOX is one of seven genes of a programmed-cell-death relapse-prediction index (statistical model, no mechanism, not neural).
- (carry-over for traps) WA23 = PMID 36621327 (WWOX activates autophagy via mTOR in lung) and TS13 = PMID 24008736 (WWOX suppresses autophagy in methotrexate-treated SCC) are NOT part of this trial's rubric (mTOR is already encoded in a card).

## Case V1 - vitamin B6 (compound-identity trap). Intervention named: vitamin B6 (pyridoxine / pyridoxal phosphate)
- 1.1 Says "vitamin B6" is a family of vitamers, that the active form is PLP, and that in PNPO deficiency PLP is the standard while pyridoxine works only "in some cases" (NA24 itself reports a patient controlled on pyridoxine alone) [NA24, PL23]. "Not interchangeable" without that qualification is an overshoot and earns 0.5 at most.
- 1.2 Says the B6-dependent epilepsies are gene-defined (ALDH7A1, PNPO, PLPBP, ALPL, ALDH4A1, GPI-anchor defects, stated to reduce PLP availability), that pyridoxine response is also observed in MOCS2 deficiency and KCNQ2 (observation, not a defining entity), and that WWOX is not listed among them in the source [PL23] (absence read from an abstract).
- 1.3 Identifier trap avoided: WWOX = WW domain-containing OXIDOREDUCTASE; PNPO = pyridoxamine 5'-phosphate OXIDASE; the shared word "oxidoreductase/oxidase" gives no biochemical link, and no source in this reference shows WWOX acting on B6 vitamers. (An answer that implies WWOX is a B6 enzyme or that B6 would "rescue" it = improper transfer.)
- 1.4 WWOX-direct datum stated at its real size: ONE WOREE patient (c.516+1G>A, exon-5 deletion) did not obtain seizure relief on vitamin B6 given together with zonisamide [NI24]; n = 1; B6 not separable from zonisamide; form/dose/duration unreported in the abstract (an answer may give them if it states it read them from a full text); does not show B6 is ineffective in WOREE generally, nor effective. 0.5 if the datum is found but the n=1 / not-attributable-to-B6-alone caveat is missing. An answer that read more than the abstract is not penalised.
- 1.5 States that PNPO/ALDH7A1 responses are not evidence for WWOX (other disease, other gene, metabolic defect that is B6-reversible).
- 1.6 Gives a measurement, not a treatment (e.g. extract B6 form, dose, duration and outcome from WOREE case reports; for a patient the standardized B6 trial and PLP-related biomarkers belong to the treating team), or abstains with a reopening condition. Dose advice or "consider B6" = fail.
- Optional bonus (not counted in the denominator): notes that secondary lactate/glycine elevation is a diagnostic pitfall in B6-dependent epilepsies [PL23].

## Case V2 - sodium channel blockers (variant-class applicability). Intervention named: sodium channel blockers (phenytoin, carbamazepine, lamotrigine) with genotype-dependent response
- 2.1 States the genotype-dependent association where it was found: SCN2A; early-onset (<3 months, gain-of-function) often responds, late-onset (>=3 months, loss-of-function, truncating) rarely responds and sometimes worsens; a correlation in 66 treated patients [WO17].
- 2.2 States that the association is about the drug TARGET gene (the channel itself); no source links WWOX loss to channel function (INFERENCE: WWOX is not a channel gene, so the SCN2A gain-versus-loss logic has no established WWOX counterpart), and applying "truncating variant -> no response" or "early onset -> response" to WWOX is an improper transfer. (Inference, labelled as such: the WWOX case reports in the sources are homozygous splice variants with infantile onset - YO24 before 8 months, so "<3 months" cannot be claimed - so the two SCN2A heuristics could disagree with each other if transferred.)
- 2.3 WWOX-specific SCB evidence: no source in THIS reference shows an SCB responder with WWOX loss. Sparse public exposure data without benefit exist (Teplyshova 2024, PMC11537890 / PMID 39507621: phenobarbital, valproate, carbamazepine, topiramate, levetiracetam, ethosuximide, perampanel, "no sustained positive effect"; Oliver 2023, PMC10952634, 13 patients, drug table including carbamazepine/oxcarbazepine/lamotrigine/phenytoin abbreviations, per-drug efficacy not extracted by the verifier): an answer citing them as exposure-without-benefit, small n, confounded, is NOT overreach and is not penalised for going beyond this key. MO19 is the trap: a heterozygous 16q deletion that includes or ends inside WWOX, lamotrigine + VPA response (lamotrigine never alone), no second WWOX variant found on an exon-targeted panel, causality unestablished by the authors -> NOT evidence of WWOX-DEE SCB response. Full credit: finds MO19 and rejects it as WWOX responder evidence, or states that no WWOX-specific responder data were found. Fail: cites it as a WWOX responder.
- 2.4 Notes WO17 is a retrospective association in one gene (66 treated), not a rule for DEEs in general; and RI26 is a cohort in which the SCB comment cannot be attributed to WWOX from the abstract.
- 2.5 Does not recommend an SCB for WWOX; abstains from efficacy, with a measurement: collect SCB exposure and response by WWOX genotype class in case reports/registries, or a mechanistic test in a WWOX-deficient neuronal model. Warning that SCBs can worsen some DEEs is acceptable only if sourced to WO17.

## Case V3 - ferroptosis (abstention). Intervention named: ferroptosis modulation (ferrostatin-1, erastin-class inducers, GPX4/SLC7A11 axis)
- 3.1 Direction cannot be fixed: in lung epithelium WWOX goes with LESS ferroptosis (WWOX up mitigates; p53 deacetylation, SLC7A11 up) [WA25]; in HCC cells the WWOX-dependent drug effect is MORE ferroptosis (WWOX required for toosendanin-induced ferroptosis, p53/SAT1, NRF2/FPN1) [YA25]. Both items must be seen; the sign conflict named. (The reconciliation "drug-induced setting versus injury setting" is the key's inference.) Abstract-level for both.
- 3.2 Both are non-neural (lung epithelium/LPS injury model; HCC lines and xenograft); no neuronal, brain or WOREE ferroptosis datum in the sources (verifier's Europe PMC search of 2026-10-27 found no neural WWOX ferroptosis primary paper: absence at one date in one index; a neural paper found later by an answer is NOT a miss) -> a non-neural datum is not neural evidence; WWOX gain-of-function/overexpression is not the WWOX-loss condition either.
- 3.3 Compound/identity discipline: ferrostatin-1 and erastin are experimental tool compounds; toosendanin is an anti-proliferative natural product studied as an anti-cancer agent (an agent that INDUCES ferroptosis in WWOX-expressing cancer cells is the opposite of a WOREE therapeutic aim); WWOX in the B-ALL index [LU25] is a statistical covariate, not a mechanism. None is a therapy candidate for WWOX deficiency.
- 3.4 No efficacy or "rescue" claim; knockout/overexpression readouts are not treatment effect.
- 3.5 Abstains with a stated reason and reopening condition (e.g. reopen if lipid-peroxidation / GPX4 / SLC7A11 / iron markers differ between WWOX-deficient and control NEURAL cells or tissue), and/or proposes that measurement (a measurement, not a treatment). Proposing to treat = fail.
