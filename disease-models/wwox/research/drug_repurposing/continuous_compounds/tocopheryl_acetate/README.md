# RRR-alpha-tocopheryl acetate and membrane lipid oxidation
context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Molecule and rationale.** Follow the selenite result with oral RRR-alpha-tocopheryl acetate (D-alpha-tocopheryl acetate). The free alpha-tocopherol produced after ester hydrolysis can interrupt propagation of lipid oxidation. This poses a testable alternative when glutathione peroxidase activity is limited. The existing lab identity table lists D-alpha-tocopherol acetate. The small pediatric epilepsy trial names that ester explicitly. Determine the stereochemistry of other comparators whenever possible and retain an unknown designation when unspecified. [Prior WWOX selenoenzyme comparison](../sodium_selenite/repletion.md) · [Pediatric trial lead](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1528-1157.1989.tb05287.x)

**Discriminating prediction before extraction.** At matched lipid substrate, firing burden and membrane alpha-tocopherol, WWOX-deficient neural cells with excess propagating lipid oxidation should show a larger decrease in nonenzymatic oxidation products per unit parent lipid than controls, accompanied by recovery of neural function even if GPX4 activity remains low. Compare intact ester and released free compound at matched *intracellular free* alpha-tocopherol to identify an ester-hydrolysis or delivery limitation. A lower absolute oxidized-lipid level with an equal fall in precursor, or lower serum oxidation without a neural functional response, weakens the mechanistic claim. Timing should distinguish prevention of chemical injury from reversal of established dysfunction.

**Opening screen.** Compare handling or transport candidates TTPA, SCARB1, ABCA1 and APOE with the susceptible lipid-remodelling genes ACSL4 and LPCAT3 in three untreated WWOX bulk preparations. Reuse earlier GPX4 data. A consistent loss of both lipid-remodelling transcripts raises the possibility of less available target substrate; divergent signals make direct oxidized-to-parent lipid measurements essential. Tissue RNA cannot establish free tocopherol levels, transport direction, ester hydrolysis, lipid species or oxidative injury. The organoid maturation protocol used B-27 17504044. Its ingredient list includes DL-alpha-tocopherol and its acetate, reduced glutathione and other antioxidants. Exact concentrations, lot composition and tissue uptake remain unknown. The DL mixture in the medium differs from the named RRR candidate. ## Opening results

**Untreated tissue.** TTPA RNA is low and lower in knockout across cortex, hippocampus and organoid; the sign survives omission of each library. SCARB1 and ABCA1 directions disagree between cortex and the other preparations. Organoid ACSL4 declines while LPCAT3 has a small positive contrast that crosses zero in the omission test. There is no concordant fall of both lipid-remodelling genes across the three systems. Reduced TTPA in a neural preparation cannot be substituted for measured liver alpha-tocopherol transfer, neuronal membrane uptake or a systemic deficiency. Reproduce with `python3 screen_tocopherol.py /path/to/files bulk.tsv`. [Full contrast and omission ranges](bulk.tsv)

**Human withdrawal comparator.** Saito et al. studied one ten-year-old with SECISBP2 mutations. Table 2 reports repeat measurements in that individual and six pediatric comparators. Serum free radical-derived EE-HODE normalized to its parent linoleate fell from 22 to 4.4 µmol/mol after 24 months and rose to 15 seven months after withdrawal. The cholesterol oxidation product 7β-hydroxycholesterol per cholesterol fell from 44 to 5.4 µmol/mol but reached only 7.0 after withdrawal. Relative to the fall on treatment, the two rebounds were 60% and 4%, respectively. The denominator linoleate rose during treatment. The decline in normalized EE-HODE therefore cannot be attributed to simple depletion of linoleate; continued changes in that denominator and differences in turnover or redox biology remain relevant. Multiplying the rounded ratios by their time-matched mean parent concentrations gives approximate EE-HODE concentrations of 28.6, 7.04 and 27.0 nM, a 93% rebound on this absolute scale, versus 60% on the parent-normalized scale. For oxidized cholesterol the corresponding rebounds are 6% and 4%. Products of means are descriptive approximations; the within-sample covariance and individual measurements are unavailable. Serum alpha-tocopherol rose from 8.4 to 15 µM then returned to 9.5, while gamma-tocopherol fell from 4.1 to 0.73 then returned to 3.9. Thyroid hormone replacement began alongside the ester and continued during withdrawal. The single child, repeated samples, different oxidation pathways, and absence of neural measurements prevent inference of WWOX or developmental benefit.
