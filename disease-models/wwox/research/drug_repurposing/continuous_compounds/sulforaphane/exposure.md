# Sulforaphane exposure and the source of neuronal glutathione

**Alternative explanation selected before extraction.** A fall in astrocytic Slc7a11 could be buffered by transsulfuration. Test whether both serial enzymes CBS and CTH increase together in the astrocyte-enriched WWOX pool and whether bulk results agree. Inspect SLC1A1 as a neuronal route for cysteine entry and ABCC1 as a possible route for glutathione conjugate export. RNA gives neither flux nor transporter direction. A low count makes the alternative unresolved.

**Untreated WWOX comparison.** Both enzymes rise numerically in the juvenile hippocampal astrocyte-enriched class (CBS 100.6 to 105.8 CPM and CTH 27.4 to 33.7). CTH was detected in 37/417 control and 48/479 KO nuclei. Each genotype has one pool. This keeps local compensation plausible. Across the bulk preparations the joint increase fails to replicate. In hippocampus CTH decreases by 0.67 in mean log2(1 + normalized RNA), and in human organoids CBS decreases by 0.27. Both decreases survive omission of each library. SLC1A1 decreases in all three tissues and in pooled excitatory nuclei (126.9 to 97.6 CPM). ABCC1 increases in human organoids and decreases numerically in mouse astrocyte-enriched nuclei. These contrasts leave several routes for sulfur delivery and removal open. Independent reruns of the [bulk](bulk.tsv), [nuclear](cell_types.tsv) and [cell mixture](mixture.tsv) results use the [same source script](compare.py).

**Human central exposure.** Zolnourian et al. measured parent sulforaphane and two conjugates separately in serial CSF samples from eight adults receiving oral stabilized sulforaphane after subarachnoid haemorrhage. At 1–6 h on days 3 and 7, 5/95 samples exceeded the assay quantification limit for parent, 5/96 for the glutathione conjugate and 0/96 for the N-acetylcysteine conjugate. These are repeated specimens. The detection proportions cannot be given subject-level confidence intervals from those counts. The parent threshold of 5 ng/mL corresponds to 0.0282 µM. Thus in 90/95 samples the parent did not exceed a threshold about 53-fold below the nominal continuous 1.5 µM culture exposure in Mao et al. This comparison constrains a sustained-parent interpretation. It cannot bound a short earlier peak, free intracellular exposure or conjugate delivery. CSF differs from brain interstitial fluid, and haemorrhage, blood–CSF barrier status, age and delivery formulation limit transfer. [SAS primary study, pharmacokinetic results and Fig. 3 caption](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12975-024-01278-1)

**Mouse tissue chemistry.** In approximately eight-month-old mice sampled 2, 6 and 24 h after oral gavage, the authors' reported tissue comparison was 0.003 nmol/mg in whole brain versus 0.355 in small-intestinal mucosa for summed sulforaphane species. SFN–GSH was the most abundant species in brain. Their collection method describes flash freezing with no vascular perfusion reported. Whole-brain mass per tissue weight gives neither a neuronal parent concentration nor a rate of glutathione depletion. [Mouse tissue distribution, animal methods, results accompanying Figs. 1 and 4, and discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3253624/)

**Human regional response.** Sedlak et al. enrolled nine healthy adults in an uncontrolled paired study. After seven days, hippocampal MRS glutathione increased from 1.11 to 1.38 mM, a 24.3% increase in the reported means (paired P = 0.041). The thalamus and anterior cingulate did not show a significant increase. At most seven subjects could contribute hippocampal pairs because two had cingulate-only scans, other scans were incomplete and there were fit-quality exclusions. The exact hippocampal denominator was not verified. A sensitivity check across the three regional GSH tests gives Bonferroni P = 0.123. This is our sensitivity analysis. The post-treatment scan was within four hours of the final dose. The study cannot isolate washout or cellular origin. The blood–brain association was thalamic (rho = 0.943, q = 0.051), and concerns a different region from the observed mean increase. Its cellular blood total and hippocampal MRS concentrations are different measurements from the redox ratios in the earlier pediatric study. [Sedlak et al., study design, 7-T methods and brain results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5981770/)

**Time course and co-medication.** Bergström et al. report higher astrocyte glutathione and peroxide clearance more than 20 h after a four-hour exposure. This positively supports testing a transient exposure. The publicly accessible abstract leaves the early free-GSH minimum, responsive recipient neuron and attainable concentration unresolved. Socała et al. report lower thresholds in selected seizure tests at an acutely toxic mouse exposure and increased serum and brain carbamazepine with potentiation in electroshock. This abstract-level interaction gives a specific control for attributing an apparent antiseizure effect to sulforaphane. The route and toxicity preclude transfer of the dose or risk estimate to an oral pediatric formulation. [Astrocyte primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20888844/) · [Acute mouse study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28412310/)

**Positive recipient-neuron control.** In rat astrocyte–neuron cultures challenged by impaired cystine uptake, the authors reported that astrocytic NRF2 knockdown blocked the sulforaphane-associated protection (text accompanying Fig. S4H). Medium conditioned by sulforaphane-treated astrocytes protected recipient neurons, and donor astrocyte GSH increased (text accompanying Figs. S6A–B). This goes beyond a glial reporter. The recipient endpoint was survival under oxidative stress. The targeted main-text reading did not establish residual parent in the transferred medium, sulfur flux in recipient neurons, or recovery of evoked firing. In the separate Fig. 2C control, cystine could itself protect against this challenge. That makes medium composition material to the proposed WWOX comparison. [Haskew-Layton et al., primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2951414/)

**Developmental function under valproate.** A separate study compared vehicle, valproate, sulforaphane and their combination in developing human cortical spheroid-derived cultures and mouse cortical neurons. It used 0.1 µM nominal sulforaphane over 24 h. Human weighted mean spontaneous firing showed no significant group difference at 3, 6, 24 h or after 24 h washout (8–10 wells per arm, Fig. 6H). The less variable mouse networks had an early valproate effect. At 3 h, reported changes from baseline were +18.96% for control, −34.61% for valproate, −9.47% for sulforaphane and −24.67% for both. The descriptive add-on difference is +9.94 percentage points and the four-arm interaction is +38.37 points, computed as (combination − valproate) − (sulforaphane − control). Neither calculation supplies an interaction test. The paper describes restoration at 6 h. For the 24-h valproate-versus-control contrast the results text prints P = 0.055. After washout the groups did not differ significantly. This supports a drug-by-challenge or maturation effect worth separating from disease rescue. A return toward higher spontaneous firing in a valproate-suppressed mouse network could have a different consequence in a hyperexcitable WWOX network. [Primary study, MEA methods, Fig. 5 and Fig. 6H–I](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12339368/)

**Discriminating prediction and disposition.** Retain sulforaphane as a mechanistic probe, with no current rationale for advancing a pediatric WWOX trial. If astrocytes can compensate for limited cystine import, a brief exposure under physiological sulfur availability should increase methionine-derived sulfur incorporation into new glutathione after washout, with recovery of neuronal GSH and evoked function in WWOX relative to vehicle and toward the matched control range. Reciprocal cystine and methionine sulfur tracing can distinguish the two substrate routes. Resolve parent and conjugates over time, including the early free-GSH trough, then measure evoked recovery at matched firing burden and viable cell counts. If only glial GSH rises, neuronal function fails to recover, or improvement depends on sustained exposure beyond the measured range, deprioritize the CNS mechanism. In any comparison involving carbamazepine, quantify that drug independently. The next open evidence question is whether recipient functional recovery can be separated from survival, drug carryover and substrate supplementation. Audit the S6 transfer conditions and test whether the VPA study’s compound-alone or interaction gene sets overlap the untreated WWOX sulfur-transport changes. If supplementary data do not support the interaction contrast, record that limit; a combined-versus-control contrast alone cannot answer it. An independent review would change this decision if it identified that missing recipient-level comparison at measured exposure.
