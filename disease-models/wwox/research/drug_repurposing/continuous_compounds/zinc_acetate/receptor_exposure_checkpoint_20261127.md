**Inference and next discriminant.** The spatial gradient after endogenous vesicular release gives a reason to avoid assuming that uniform added zinc reproduces a beneficial effect. In a matched juvenile WWOX input, compare the calibrated baseline and evoked zinc waveform with the waveform during zinc acetate and acetate-matched vehicle. Read late NMDA charge, first release, tonic GABA-A conductance and native GABA reversal or functional inhibitory recruitment in the same preparation. A reduction in isolated late charge accompanied by poorer inhibition or unchanged recurrence lowers priority. Added zinc that reduces late recurrence with inhibition preserved is informative only at the measured local exposure. Chelation may remove both inhibition and excitation effects, so its net response alone cannot establish receptor attribution. Record whether its baseline action is limited to evoked trains.

**Next checkpoint.** This comparison does not establish a WWOX-specific direction or brain uptake after oral acetate. Resolve one close input-level comparator for tonic occupancy versus stimulus-locked zinc and the opposing GABA effect. Prioritize separate EPSC and tonic inhibitory readouts plus a faster zinc measurement before using any three-hour upper bound to set the bench comparison. Check the small blinded pediatric zinc-sulfate study by its own endpoints and salt. Keep zinc acetate active until those checks determine whether it is a useful circuit probe.
