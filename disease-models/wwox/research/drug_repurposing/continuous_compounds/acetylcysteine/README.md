# N-acetylcysteine: substrate rescue or a misleading culture background?

**Question.** Does providing cysteine restore glutathione synthesis and a neural function in WWOX loss, or does substrate handling change extracellular glutamate or network firing while the developmental defect persists? Tissue redox markers, reduced glutathione, glutathione synthesis and cystine–glutamate exchange are different outcomes. Keep NAC uptake and deacetylation, extracellular thiol chemistry, glial uptake and neuronal response distinct. Revisit the laboratory's [earlier redox hypothesis](../../rs_r6_it30_20261106/hypotheses.md) and its [prior medium audit](../../myelin_premises_20261108/verify/items.md).

**A 1,000-fold uncertainty changes the comparison.** The WWOX co-culture PDF, inspected at the printed glyphs on journal p. 3063, lists 5 mg/mL NAC and 5 mM forskolin. The cited reference 23, Elazar et al., gives 5 μg/mL and 5 μM for the corresponding medium. The source protocol and the WWOX study are different experiments; neither proves which amount was dispensed in the other. The earlier medium inventory preserved both quotations. The consequential comparison is with the separate 1 mM NAC condition reported in Wwox-null embryonic fibroblasts. With a molecular mass of 163.20 g/mol, the two neural-media descriptions imply approximately **30.64 mM or 0.03064 mM**, respectively: one is about 30.6 times above the fibroblast condition, the other about 32.6 times below. The range is two incompatible source readings, not a series administered to any cell. These nominal media amounts cannot be used as oral exposure targets. [WWOX co-culture](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awab174) · [Elazar et al., primary cultures](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6371057/) · [Cheng et al., Fig. 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10613160/) · [Molecular mass](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/Acetylcysteine)

In the fibroblast serial-passage system the authors report prevention of microsatellite instability and restoration of senescence induction. The neural experiment compared cells under a common medium containing NAC. A persistent myelination phenotype there is compatible with a substrate-insensitive defect, an ineffective basal exposure, or incomplete protection. There is no within-experiment NAC-free arm in that genotype comparison; it cannot establish a NAC effect or its absence. Forskolin changes by the same nominal factor across the two descriptions, further limiting cross-protocol inference.

**Discriminating prediction.** At matched background cystine, NAC and neuronal activity, an increase in newly synthesized glutathione in WWOX-deficient neural cells should precede a rescue of myelination or physiological recruitment if substrate limitation is causal. A rise in total glutathione paired with stable or rising extracellular glutamate and unchanged functional output would lower priority. Measure the change in each compartment and both genotypes; a redox change seen equally in controls is weak evidence of WWOX specificity. As a pragmatic next step, compare human route-specific parent NAC and glutathione measurements with the lower nominal background and screen the cystine-exchange, glutathione synthesis and glutamate-clearance genes in the archived WWOX models. A preparative record could resolve the media ambiguity but is not a prerequisite to those independent tests.

**Disposition: retain as a mechanistic probe, park for trial prioritization.** Concordant uptake and exchange transcripts, discordant nuclear clearance and a direct biphasic response support a specific neuron–astrocyte test. Reopen for genotype-selective isotope incorporation plus functional recovery at measured exposure, with simultaneous glutamate and firing measurements.
