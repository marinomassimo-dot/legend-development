# Oral parent NAC and intravenous GSH response are separate observations

Katz et al. compared oral NAC formulations and measured CSF thiols before and 90 minutes after the last administration in adults with Parkinson disease. Twelve completed the study. Both reduced and total NAC rose, but no consistent change in CSF cysteine or GSH was found. The source reports a highest-group CSF NAC concentration of 9.26 ± 1.62 μM (publisher Results). Each dose/formulation panel represents three participants; the numerical summary in text does not explicitly identify which redox fraction it denotes. Use the publisher's μM unit; the repository PDF text extraction renders the glyph as mM. The nominal lower neural medium estimate is 3.31 times this adult group value and the upper estimate about 3,309 times; 1 mM in the fibroblast experiment is about 108 times the value. None is a direct tissue-to-plasma partition or a clinical exposure ceiling. [University-deposited primary paper, Methods, Results and Figure 1](https://escholarship.org/content/qt57d8b6sp/qt57d8b6sp.pdf?v=lg) · [Publisher Results with units](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1353802015000759)

Moss et al. measured glutathione by proton MRS after rewarming in term infants with hypoxic–ischemic encephalopathy. These infants had already received repeated intravenous treatment before the paired scan on day 5–6. After a full infusion the mean paired GSH increase was 0.30 ± 0.21 mM (21 infants) at 12–30 minutes after completion; the 24-infant analysis including infusion problems gave a change in group means of 0.26 mM. The three incomplete-infusion observations are a procedural comparison. Some infants received concomitant calcitriol; the reported subgroup comparison lacked random allocation. This is a within-session change in regional tissue GSH, with neither neuronal identification nor a measurement of glutamate exchange. Acute injury and rewarming limit transportability to chronic WWOX loss. [Neonatal primary study, Methods and Figure 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5999009/)

**Inference.** No contradiction arises between a GSH signal in neonatal tissue after intravenous NAC and unchanged lumbar-CSF GSH after oral NAC in adults. Tissue and CSF report different compartments and the patients, sampling times and routes differ. It remains plausible that neural GSH changes at parent concentrations below the nominal media amounts through substrate handling, while extracellular glutamate changes independently. The next comparison needs concomitant reduced/oxidized glutathione, labelled cysteine incorporation and extracellular glutamate or uptake under defined background medium.

## Pediatric oral and adult brain comparisons

Wink et al. randomized 31 children with autism in a 12-week placebo-controlled study. Eligibility required age 4–12, weight ≥15 kg, and ability to swallow capsules. Table 4's blood GSH means increased from 484.8 to 780.3 on NAC and from 474.5 to 640.4 on placebo. The descriptive difference of changes is +129.6 in table units. For the reported mean GSH/GSSG ratios, the changes are +10.1 and +14.4, respectively (difference −4.3). A larger GSH pool does not identify a shift in the redox ratio. The GSH row gives z=1.74 and a one-tailed significance mark; a normal approximation gives about 0.082 two-sided before accounting for multiple endpoints. The primary clinical outcome did not distinguish groups. Only two participants randomized to NAC were on antiepileptic medication (zero on placebo); this cannot establish tolerability with pediatric WWOX polytherapy, nor suitability of that capsule formulation for children unable to swallow it. [Wink et al., Methods and tables 1, 3 and 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4839099/)

An independent four-week oral study in adults found a roughly 6% increase in occipital GSH at 7 T (p=0.3) and 10% at 3 T (p=0.06), with four PD and three control participants completing the study. The authors estimated sensitivity of about 20% for the small 7 T sample. These paired scanner readings are not independent replications; smaller brain changes remain unresolved. Several PD patients reported reversible worsening of symptoms. These observations cannot be used to predict safety or response in WWOX children. Neither this adult oral study nor the infant intravenous HIE series validates an oral WWOX dose or proves developmental benefit. [Coles et al., Results and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5762253/)
