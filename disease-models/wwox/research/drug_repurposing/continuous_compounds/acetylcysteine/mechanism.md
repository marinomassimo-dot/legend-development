# A cysteine-supply hypothesis that makes a different prediction from general antioxidant rescue

**Decision.** Retain N-acetylcysteine for a WWOX-specific neural comparison, with no present basis to prioritize a pediatric trial. The discriminating observation is preserved neuronal function after bypassing limited cysteine uptake, without harmful glutamatergic recruitment at the same exposure. A rise in blood or total tissue glutathione alone cannot make that distinction.

## What changed in untreated WWOX samples

In three existing WWOX bulk datasets, SLC1A1 (EAAT3, a neuronal route for cysteine uptake), SLC7A11 (xCT, the light chain of the cystine–glutamate exchanger) and SLC1A2 (EAAT2, glutamate clearance) all fell. Their directions survived omitting each individual library in turn. The prespecified wider signature failed: the catalytic and modifier glutamate–cysteine ligase subunits did not decline together in both cortex and organoids, and SLC1A3 was inconsistent. The evidence suggests testing substrate handling and circuit regulation separately. It does not show a deficiency in GSH synthesis.

**Cell-composition check.** In pooled hippocampal nuclei assigned to astrocytes, Slc7a11 fell from 352 to 83 CPM, while Slc1a2 rose from 4,910 to 5,392. The usual classifier uses Slc1a2 as one astrocyte marker. Repeating the comparison using only Aqp4 and Aldh1l1 co-detection to select nuclei gave the same directions: Slc7a11 361 to 144 CPM (75 vs 39 molecules); Slc1a2 5,330 to 7,823 CPM. Only 35 control and 43 KO nuclei met that stricter rule. In the excitatory-neuron class Slc1a1 fell from 127 to 98 CPM. This is one pool per genotype, with depth and marker selection effects. The reversal for Slc1a2 shows why bulk expression cannot establish deficient clearance *within* astrocytes. **Numerical counterexample.** Suppose resting extracellular glutamate is governed by unchanged non-exchange release, exchange release with KO/control factor x, and unsaturated first-order clearance with factor k. If q is the control fraction of release from exchange, the steady KO/control concentration would be (1−q+qx)/k. For an illustration only, substitute the ratios of group mean expression for x (SLC7A11) and k (SLC1A2). At q = 0.5 the human-organoid value is 1.00 (0.85–1.19 across library omissions), while bulk mouse hippocampus gives 1.27 and its independently defined astrocyte subset gives 0.48. The latter applies a single-cell-class proxy to both factors and is not an estimate of tissue glutamate. Neither the assumed linear mapping nor q is measured. A glutamate sensor with separate uptake and release measurements would resolve what expression cannot. ## Two direct pharmacological counterexamples

**Bypassing neuronal substrate limitation can work.** Reyes et al. found that NAC restored neuronal GSH and resistance to an oxidative challenge in an EAAC1-deficient mouse model. Blocking the first step of GSH synthesis prevented protection. Mouse CSF parent NAC fell to very low levels within an hour while the neuronal effect was measurable five hours after treatment. The concentration measurements were in separate groups; the study deliberately used a higher dose for quantifying the mouse CSF time course. Its human CSF figure reuses the Katz data and cannot count as a second independent clinical cohort. A partial fall in SLC1A1 transcript in WWOX is a possible entry point, with no demonstration of a corresponding uptake defect. [Reyes et al., Results and figures 2–6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4720670/)

**The sign of a circuit effect can reverse.** In rat nucleus accumbens slices Kupchik et al. found that 0.5 μM bath NAC reduced evoked excitatory postsynaptic current amplitude; 50 and 500 μM increased it. Paired-pulse ratio increased only at the low concentration. Antagonist comparisons supported contributions from group II metabotropic glutamate receptors to the low-exposure effect and mGluR5 to the higher one. The experiment is region-, cell- and stimulation-specific. It does not locate a concentration threshold for WWOX cortex or for lumbar CSF. Even a genuine improvement in neuronal antioxidant capacity could coexist with greater excitatory transmission. [Kupchik et al., figures 2–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3340445/)

## What would change the decision

At the same measured parent NAC and free cysteine exposure, compare WWOX-deficient and corrected neuron–astrocyte preparations with matched baseline firing. Measure incorporation of labelled precursor into **neuronal** GSH, extracellular glutamate through a stimulation-and-recovery period, and recovery of network firing or axon ensheathment. If labelled GSH and function recover without sustained glutamate accumulation or extra bursting, increase priority for preclinical follow-up. If GSH rises while glutamate recovery slows or bursting worsens, deprioritize the molecule for a broad neural indication. If the response is explained by altered medium cystine, record medium dependence. The actual NAC concentration in the published myelination co-culture still lacks independent confirmation.
