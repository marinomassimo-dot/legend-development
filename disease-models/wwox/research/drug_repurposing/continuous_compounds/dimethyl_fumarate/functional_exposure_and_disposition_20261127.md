# Dimethyl fumarate | Rapid synaptic effect, delayed protection and disposition

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

Units and preparations include nominal µM monomethyl fumarate (MMF) in acute mouse corticostriatal slices and rat organotypic cerebellum or hippocampus, sEPSC frequency as percent of each recording's baseline, PI/DAPI fluorescence after oxygen–glucose deprivation (OGD), and reported µM MMF in adult human CSF. The prior [exposure and WWOX RNA checkpoint](exposure_and_native_rna_20261127.md) remains the baseline for this comparison.

**Disposition.** Retain dimethyl fumarate and its measured metabolite as conditional preclinical comparators for acute excitatory recruitment and delayed stress recovery. Defer pediatric WWOX trial priority. The new finding that warrants an acute control is a synaptic response in naïve tissue at nominal MMF within about two- to threefold of the adult CSF range. Its WWOX specificity, active site concentration and effects on inhibition or recovery are unresolved.

**Inference.** The rapid, reversible frequency change makes spontaneous event count alone an insufficient indicator of a delayed recovery effect. An acute synaptic effect may occur at a lower nominal concentration than the injury-survival response or suppression of a microglia-induced decay change. The latter comparisons use different preparations and assays. In WWOX, a drop in spontaneous excitatory frequency can coexist with weaker recruitment of inhibitory cells and unchanged late recovery.

**Discriminating prediction and countercheck.** In the same stage-matched WWOX and corrected preparations, compare acute MMF, washout and a later exposure interval at measured local analyte. Clamp the first input independently and track evoked excitatory and inhibitory recruitment, directly measured release or miniature currents, glucose-derived lactate and ATP recovery. A reversible decline in spontaneous frequency during application with stable matched-input release would favor a change in firing or inputs. A decline in matched-input release that precedes transcription would support an acute synaptic mechanism. Recovery that persists after washout, with initial recruitment and inhibition preserved and no early ATP deficit, would reopen the delayed WWOX protection hypothesis. A lower event count coupled to early loss of ATP or inhibition would lower priority. Reserve a resident-microglia HCAR2 perturbation for a delayed response that persists through the first comparison.

**Pediatric feasibility.** The delayed-release capsule has randomized 10–17-year-old relapsing MS experience. The authorization in the EU covers RRMS from age 13, with limited evidence at 10–12. The U.S. label lists pediatric safety and effectiveness as unestablished. CONNECT reports GI tolerability events in 58/78 DMF vs 22/72 interferon β-1a and lymphopenia in 2/78 vs 0/72. The capsule is labeled to be swallowed intact. A feeding-tube exposure bridge, younger developmental CNS exposure, and WWOX data with background antiseizure medicines remain unverified. The in vitro interaction discussion in the label does not establish compatibility with specific pediatric regimens. [CONNECT Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9520348/) · [EMA §§4.1–4.4](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information/tecfidera-epar-product-information_en.pdf) · [DailyMed §§2.2, 8.4 and 12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=665d7e74-036c-5f68-5b67-ab84b9b49151)

**Reopening criterion.** A stage-matched WWOX comparison with verified local parent or MMF, preserved inhibitory recruitment and a positive energy or post-washout functional endpoint would justify further work. The Gola low-magnesium assay is still known from abstract and preview. Full methods could change the preclinical comparison if they show a low-concentration response with native firing and glycolytic endpoints measured together. The original visual panels and independent regional dose series reduce the value of an additional opinion on the current exposure gap. Experimental or author data that alter that gap merit review.
