# Sarcosine and direct receptor versus transporter effects in WWOX loss of function

`context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`. The planned comparisons separate measured free sarcosine and glycine (µmol/L), transporter kinetics, receptor currents, native chloride reversal and late activity after matched first recruitment.

**Selection.** Sarcosine (N-methylglycine) is an endogenous amino-acid derivative investigated orally in humans. This comparison extends the held glycine work to a different pharmacological question. Sarcosine can serve as a transported GlyT1 substrate that competes with glycine, can co-activate NMDA receptors directly, and can produce a glycine receptor chloride current in a separate preparation. The transporter-bound human cryo-EM state is molecular evidence and does not specify in vivo exposure or activity. The held WWOX glycine study shows lower SLC6A9 across three bulk models, with the nuclear contrast sensitive to myelinating-cell representation. Retain transporter reserve as a measured variable. The human biallelic SLC6A9 phenotype supplies a separate caution about centrally elevated glycine during sustained transporter loss. [Direct NMDAR study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19433577/) · [Direct GlyR study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19619564/) · [Human GlyT1 structures](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-025-57613-z) · [Held WWOX glycine comparison](../glycine/README.md) · [Human loss-of-function control](../glycine/human_transport_control.md)

**Discriminating prediction.** If sarcosine acts in WWOX primarily through restrained glycine clearance, a validated GlyT1-only control should reproduce the prolongation of extracellular glycine and the late effect at matched initial recruitment. Matching the local glycine trajectory between conditions should remove the extra transporter-dependent response. If the acute difference instead persists at matched glycine and after independent GlyT1 blockade, a direct co-agonist comparison and NMDAR-dependent response should separate it. A chloride conductance with a differing reversal or differing dependence on assay glycine would support the GlyR branch. Native reversal and adaptation matter for the circuit sign. Compare the first response and a defined repeated challenge, with pathway-specific readouts and matched local parent. An increase in late excitation or loss of adaptation at attainable exposure weakens the repurposing hypothesis.

**First comparison and decision.** Compare free concentration ranges and controls in the original direct NMDA and GlyR studies against native slice sarcosine experiments and human GlyT1 transport data. Check which human oral studies measured parent, glycine or a downstream marker, and which report quantitative PK. An adult MRS change alone cannot identify receptor engagement. Continue toward a candidate-specific comparison only if available exposure or an interpretable native engagement experiment separates effects. Park pediatric priority without exposure, preserved physiology and safety evidence.
