# Sarcosine in WWOX loss of function

`context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN` for the compound comparison after targeted source observations. Concentrations below are in µmol/L unless specified. Human plasma, brain dialysate, applied slice concentration and normalized assay response are distinct measurements. The [prespecified question](pretest_20261127.md) was whether a direct effect remains after matching glycine dynamics and GlyT1 activity.

## Counterevidence and transfer

- **The seizure endpoint matters.** In Socała et al., the maximal electroshock CC50 moved from 9.92 to 11.93 mA at both highest tested exposures (+20.3%). The timed intravenous pentylenetetrazol model did not distinguish sarcosine from vehicle across the reported endpoints. These were acute provoked thresholds. In Shen et al. 2020, rat hippocampal kindling Racine scores separated on days 3–5 during dosing and at days 11–13, after five days without dosing or kindling. By days 14–15 the reported significance had ended. Lower injury or seizure burden during induction could change later susceptibility. Ferris et al. 2026 sampled at 24 h after the last kindling session, observed lower evoked behavioral progression with six rats per group, and obtained RRBS after quality control in 6/6/5 sham/vehicle-kindled/sarcosine-kindled rats. Eight selected transcripts, including *Grik2*, fell relative to vehicle kindling. The design cannot identify an independent methylation effect after matching earlier seizure burden. It supplies no post-treatment electrographic efficacy result. [Electroshock and PTZ](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1734-1140(10)70279-6) · [Kindling figures](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnmol.2020.00097) · [Ferris, Fig. 1 and §§3.1–3.2, 4.2–4.4](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27177938) · [Replayed rodent quantities](rodent_outcomes.tsv)
- **A selective inhibitor gives a useful opposite context.** In Shen et al. 2015, GlyT1 mRNA was 2.17 ± 0.32-fold higher in human resected drug-resistant temporal lobe epilepsy hippocampus (9 surgical samples, 5 autopsy control samples). The selective inhibitor LY2365109 reduced seizures in the adult mouse kainate model over an acute EEG window. The high tested exposure produced lethal respiratory arrest in that mouse model. This is a comparator safety signal with no transferable sarcosine incidence, exposure threshold or WWOX effect. [Shen et al., §§3.3–3.5, Figs. 3–5](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropharm.2015.08.031)
- **WWOX bulk transcript direction differs from the acquired epilepsy example.** The pooled bulk contrast in log₂(1 + TPM or CPM) for SLC6A9 was −0.935 in juvenile mouse cortex, −0.419 in mouse hippocampus and −0.197 in human organoids, with respective control/LoF library counts 4/3, 2/3 and 2/4. All one-library omissions preserved the sign within each dataset. The held nuclei contrast changes with myelinating-cell composition. SARDH is near zero in cortex (−0.040), increases in organoids (+0.188), and is uncertain in hippocampus (+0.188, omission range −0.256 to +0.522). GNMT is low abundance in these preparations, with no conserved shift across all three. The data support measuring local sarcosine clearance and glycine reserve, with no common evidence of increased GlyT1 activity or sarcosine catabolic failure. ## Prediction and decision

**Inference.** The primary evidence rules out treating sarcosine as a clean GlyT1 probe throughout its nominal active range. Native receptor reserve and response duration determine whether direct engagement changes excitation, adaptation or neither. The historical plasma result makes oral absorption concrete and leaves neural exposure unbounded by these data.

**Hypothesis and discriminating observation.** In a juvenile Wwox-null preparation paired with littermate controls, compare sarcosine with a verified selective GlyT1 blocker at the same first evoked recruitment and measured extracellular glycine and D-serine time courses. Analyze late NMDA charge, the next evoked response and patterned inhibitory recruitment. If glycine retention drives the first drug effect, the comparator should reproduce the change and sarcosine should lose its residual when transporter engagement and both co-agonist trajectories are matched. A residual at matched trajectories that disappears with NMDAR blockade or saturating co-agonist, but remains during verified GlyT1 blockade, would favor a direct co-agonist mechanism. The disappearance under saturation alone is shared by both mechanisms. Monitor native chloride reversal and a separately identified strychnine-sensitive conductance before assigning a GlyR effect. A repeated-challenge comparison must preserve inhibition and show a drug-by-genotype effect. A persistence claim additionally needs parent clearance, activity-matched induction and electrographic follow-up to separate persistent modulation from reduced initial kindling.

**Disposition and reopen condition.** Retain as a preclinical mechanism comparator at measured exposure. Park pediatric WWOX trial priority. Transfer would require age-relevant oral exposure for the exact N-methylglycine formulation, a free central exposure bridge, a WWOX-selective effect with preserved physiology, and evidence on background antiseizure interactions and respiratory and neurobehavioral tolerability. The sources examined provide no controlled pediatric WWOX endpoint. The next useful observation is simultaneous free parent and co-agonist kinetics with a native WWOX functional response at the same concentrations. A standalone review of potency rank or the caption cannot supply that bridge. The curve comparison can be independently rerun locally.
