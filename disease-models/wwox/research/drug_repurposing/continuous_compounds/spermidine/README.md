## Human plasma comparison

In 12 healthy adults, Senekowitsch et al. compared two five-day periods with a nine-day washout. The verum was an algae/soy extract with independent verification of its stated 15 mg/day spermidine content. The placebo used inert filler. This tests the extract, with no confirmation in the inspected Methods of other polyamine content or the origin of circulating spermine. Tables S5–S6 supply 12 pairs of total AUC over the observed collection schedule. The replay gives a mean verum-minus-placebo change of **+34.40 h·ng/mL for spermidine** (2.24%, 5/12 positive, nominal paired 95% CI −232.52 to +301.32) and **+49.27 h·ng/mL for spermine** (8.44%, 8/12 positive, CI +6.33 to +92.20). Single-participant omission gives −11.56 to +129.80 for spermidine and +39.72 to +56.31 for spermine. The exact sign-flip sensitivity p values are 0.808 and 0.032, respectively, assuming paired differences are exchangeable with their negations under the null. Those analyses cannot model period, pre-period baseline or carryover from the published AUC pairs. The spermine result is exploratory, with an unadjusted p=0.0282 in the paper and at least three plasma analytes. No CSF or isotope-specific absorption was measured. ## Route-specific primary comparators

- **Mouse brain distribution and age.** In 18-month-old mice given 3 mM in drinking water with 3–5 mol% d8 tracer, Schroeder et al. report detection in transcardially perfused brain at 1, 4 and 8 weeks (Fig. 1A, n=3 animals stated in caption). The axis is the tissue labeled/unlabeled signal ratio normalized to the matched whole-blood ratio. One missing week-one blood sample used the mean of the other two for normalization. These are relative isotope ratios, without absolute free brain concentrations or a distinction between intact passage and tracer recycling. A separate chronic comparison reports more hypusination in aged hippocampus without a detected change in total eIF5A or TFEB protein. The authors report no detected respiratory improvement in the tested 5-month-old cohort after three months, in contrast with the middle-aged comparison. Neither result estimates developmental efficacy or juvenile uptake. [Schroeder et al., Figs. 1A and 2C–F, Methods and age discussion](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2021.108985)
- **Genetic perturbation.** At 15 days in flies, Liang et al. report that spermidine increased the hypusinated fraction and respiratory readouts in controls, with smaller responses after neuronal DHS knockdown and in DHS heterozygotes. Fig. 5E–H respirometry uses wells pooled from four independent experiments, Fig. 5I–L from three. The mechanism test motivates a crossed treatment contrast with matched baseline function. It identifies a route in fly brain aging and does not establish a mammalian WWOX requirement. [Liang et al., Results and Fig. 5B,D,F–H,J–L](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.celrep.2021.108941)

## Active checkpoint

The evidence supports measuring the limiting step before assuming substrate repletion. A preferred WWOX result is selective recovery of labeled hypusinated eIF5A and completed native cargo degradation before mitochondrial and evoked recovery. Label in spermine or in the hypusine product alone cannot resolve prior interconversion, so quantify species and time courses in parallel and compare a spermine exposure matched at the site of action. A lower eIF5A pool with an unchanged hypusinated fraction suggests an abundance constraint. Accumulation of intracellular spermidine with little or no increase in hypusine suggests an enzyme, cofactor, or downstream processing limit. If an acute circuit effect persists without increased hypusination or turnover, test it separately from delayed recovery. [Prespecified alternatives](pretest_20261126.md)

**Next step.** Verify substrate versus enzyme limitation using a developmental primary comparator and check whether either spermidine or the observed spermine increment overlaps a polyamine-sensitive circuit assay. Test for an attainable free exposure at juvenile brain or CSF, with oral formulation, pediatric safety and concomitant antiseizure medications evaluated separately. An age-matched observation or a concentration-dependent change in inhibition could change preclinical priority and would warrant review. No pediatric exposure conclusion follows from the extract trial. Start with the indexed GRIN2B loss-of-function intervention (PMID 40024627) and the developmental DHPS comparison (PMID 37333770). Audit treatment attribution, genotype-specific direction and analyte concentrations before translating either result.
