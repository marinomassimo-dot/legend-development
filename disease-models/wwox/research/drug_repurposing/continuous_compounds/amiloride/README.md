# Amiloride in WWOX loss of function

**Decision.** Keep amiloride as a secondary laboratory control for a directly measured proton-evoked current. Defer systemic pediatric WWOX development. First test whether the fall in extracellular pH during a juvenile WWOX burst recruits inhibition and shortens the discharge, or primarily limits excitatory-cell firing through other channels. The two mechanisms can coexist. A renal or systemic response cannot resolve their contribution.

## Exposure and native WWOX evidence

**Data.** In the [Jones et al. primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9085317/), nine adolescents and ten adults with cystic fibrosis had mean plasma peak concentrations of 21.7 and 20.6 ng/mL after a single oral exposure, respectively. With the [amiloride free-base mass](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/16231), these convert to **0.09450 and 0.08971 µM total plasma**. The adolescent mean is 16–20 times below the nominal amiloride IC50 estimates for peak current in two recombinant human ASIC1a constructs in CHO cells, and **146 times below** the 13.82 µM IC50 in cultured mouse cortical neurons. Both assays stepped from pH 7.4 to 6.0. At 100 µM amiloride the source values in dissociated cortical neurons from an adult surgery series (12 donors overall, nine cells in this comparison) imply a 95% reduction from the reported mean peak magnitudes under a pH 6.0 challenge. The human cortical bath result is one concentration and does not report an IC50. The comparisons are cross-assay scales without a measured juvenile unbound plasma or brain concentration and without an inferred target occupancy or safety margin. [Vaithia et al., Fig. 5A](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnmol.2019.00133) · [Leng et al., Fig. 2 and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1111/cns.12524) · [Li et al., Fig. 2A–B](https://doi.org/10.1038/jcbfm.2010.30)

**Data.** Global Wwox-null mice had serum total CO₂ 14.50 ± 3.50 mEq/L (4 mice) versus 21.67 ± 0.33 in WT (3 mice), with mean urea nitrogen 37.25 versus 17.67 mg/dL. The printed significance compares pooled WT plus heterozygotes with nulls. Serum from this preparation cannot determine perisynaptic pH in juvenile neocortex. In the separate P13–17 neuron-targeted mouse cortex, d-APV eliminated spontaneous slice bursts in its tested comparison. That sustains an NMDAR-related alternative when changing extracellular pH in the same tissue. [Ludes-Meyers et al., Table 3 and blood sampling](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0007775) · [Bretón et al., §2.2 and Fig. 3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nbd.2021.105529)

**Reanalysis.** The [bulk replay](native_bulk.tsv) gives ASIC1 gene-level log₂ changes of −0.10 in P17 mouse cortex (4 control and 3 KO libraries), −0.01 in P17 hippocampus (2 and 3) and +0.07 in week-15 human organoids (2 and 4). Cortex and organoid contrasts retain their opposite signs on single-library omission. ASIC2 is +0.22, −0.28 and +1.59, respectively, with all three directions stable to that omission. GSE158684 [pooled nuclei](cell_types.tsv) detect Asic1 in both neural classes (31/246 versus 32/293 inhibitory nuclei and 102/1,596 versus 104/1,262 excitatory nuclei). The one pool per genotype gives cell-class coverage, without biological replication across pools. Gene-level RNA cannot distinguish ASIC1a from ASIC1b, resolve the ASIC2 isoform, or measure native conductance or desensitization. These data weaken a common WWOX-wide rise in ASIC1 and direct attention to the preparation and cell class. [GSE158682](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158682) · [GSE158681](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158681) · [GSE156243](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE156243) · [GSE158684](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158684)

**Inference.** Amiloride is an exposure- and state-dependent interpretive tool. Its clinical pharmacology includes renal ENaC block, and a local pH effect could also arise from changing Na⁺/H⁺ exchange. Endogenous pH history matters because mild acid conditioning can leave less ASIC available to respond to the next transient. The US label reports hyperkalemia and renal impairment restrictions and says pediatric safety and effectiveness have not been established. The small cystic fibrosis PK study and the absence of a retrieved juvenile CNS exposure bridge leave feasibility poor for a systemic WWOX study. ## Hypothesis and discriminating comparison

**Hypothesis.** In juvenile superficial WWOX cortex, a brief acid transient can contribute to termination by recruiting inhibitory cells, while the slower pH change can suppress excitatory firing independently of ASIC. A smaller event count can therefore hide a longer discharge once an event starts. Conversely, channel activity during initial recruitment could promote the early response or reduce spontaneous inhibitory tone. Whole-animal acid-base changes add a separate systemic explanation.

**Proposed experiment.** In matched P13–17 neuron-targeted Wwox-deficient and littermate superficial cortex, first record local pH before and during the first and last portions of events with late tail, time to next event and total burden kept separate. At a matched starting pH, ion composition and initial evoked recruitment, replay the waveform measured in each preparation with vehicle and amiloride at verified local exposure, in ASIC1a-competent tissue and tissue with an orthogonal ASIC1a loss-of-function reference. A residual effect of amiloride after verified ASIC1a loss argues for another target. Verify the rapid inward current and loss of the ASIC-sensitive component in the recipient inhibitory and excitatory cells. Read out native inhibitory function and preserve enough initial drive to compare termination. Use matched slow versus fast waveform portions with the same baseline/nadir where supported by the native records. Repeat the key phase contrast on spontaneous events and report both overall counts and within-event persistence. Include the NMDAR-sensitive response and, if drug and reference diverge, measure pH recovery to check an exchanger effect. Existing [pH protocol](../../ph_k2p_sci45_20261111/MEASUREMENT_PROTOCOL_SHEET.md) provides the physiological ionic conditions and NMDAR arm.

| Observation | Decision it changes |
| --- | --- |
| ASIC perturbation removes a timed inhibitory response and lengthens the late tail with matched first recruitment. | ASIC contributes to termination in that preparation. The direction argues against broad local suppression of that component. |
| The validated independent perturbation and parent reduce early initiation or pathological late recurrence with first physiological and inhibitory responses preserved, and the parent effect is occluded by verified ASIC1a loss. | Consider a local target-specific branch. Compare differential effects in controls to establish whether it is WWOX-selective. |
| The slower pH effect persists under verified ASIC loss, or parent alone reduces the first physiological response or changes pH recovery before any ASIC effect. | The corresponding component favors other pH-sensitive current, nonselective suppression or exchange. Amiloride alone cannot establish the mechanism. |
| No reproducible native pH transient or separable acid current in the relevant phase. | Defer the ASIC arm and retain the existing NaV/NMDA/K2P questions. |

**Checkpoint and review.** The result changes the readout under INV-05. Separate event inception, duration and residual firing under matched pH and verify engagement before interpreting an amiloride response. No shared pediatric priority moved. Independent work could change that decision with paired juvenile interstitial exposure and target engagement or with a native pH/current record. An additional reading of the same assay means cannot supply either.
