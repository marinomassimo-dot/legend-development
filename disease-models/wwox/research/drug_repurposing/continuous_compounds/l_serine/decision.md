# L-serine disposition and biochemical reopening condition

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. The opening WWOX comparison found lower astroglial PHGDH RNA and discordant downstream enzyme signals. Retain L-serine as a mechanistic probe. Its current evidence gives low priority for a pediatric WWOX trial.

**Quality control in the actual WWOX model.** Matching the distribution of total nuclear RNA counts across the two hippocampal astroglial pools did little to the direction of PHGDH detection. Across three choices of depth-bin width, control detection was 19.9–20.1% and WWOX knockout detection was 7.1–7.5%. PSAT1 detection was 9.3–9.5% and 9.9–10.2%, respectively. This addresses one technical explanation. There is one pooled library per genotype. Subtype composition, age-dependent maturation, genotype–batch confounding and the distinction between RNA and enzyme flux remain. **Change to the lipid hypothesis.** In an independent brain-specific Phgdh knockout, hippocampi from 8 female mutant and 7 floxed reference mice aged 10–12 weeks had approximately 3.5-fold higher 1-deoxysphinganine; 1-deoxydihydroceramide was also elevated. Total ceramide and dihydroceramide showed no significant difference. In cultured Phgdh-null fibroblasts, Table 3 gives matched serine-deprived versus supplemented deoxysphinganine values of 10.3 versus 1.0 at 6 h and 18.0 versus 0.6 at 24 h, each normalized to the same 6-hour reference. Intracellular alanine/serine was 3.3 versus 1.1 at 6 h and 3.7 versus 2.1 at 24 h. Six-hour WT deoxysphinganine entries are unmeasured, not zero. Fig. 8A also shows that D-serine reduced both atypical and normal sphingoid-base production in this fibroblast system, which makes it unsuitable as a presumed inert stereoisomer control. Neither a fibroblast culture deprived of serine nor an adult brain-specific Phgdh knockout measures the degree of deficiency in WWOX. [Esaki et al., Table 3 and Figs. 6–8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4505526/)

**Competing circuit mechanism.** In adult mouse hippocampal slices, enzymatic depletion of extracellular L-serine reduced long-term potentiation by about half while isolated NMDA field potentials showed no detectable change in that experiment. Separately, inhibiting PHGDH reduced potentiation even in serine-racemase-null slices, implicating a route through glycine. Fig. 4A shows glycine and D-serine moving in opposite directions during early postnatal life as L-serine remained relatively constant. Adult slice plasticity and seizure control are different outcomes. A fixed-amplitude NMDA response or a total-serine measurement can miss a functional change. [Neame et al., Figs. 2–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6789919/)

**Clinical comparisons, with evidence depth.** The primary abstracts and public protocol establish the following limited precedents. In five children with proven PHGDH deficiency and heterogeneous amino-acid treatment, all had fewer seizures, two became seizure-free, and only one showed psychomotor progress during 3–7.5 years of follow-up. In an earlier report, two siblings attained seizure control after glycine was added to L-serine. The reports may overlap and are not pooled. A separate open-label study in GRIN loss of function enrolled 24 children, of whom 23 completed. One stopped for irritability and insomnia. The publication treats EEG and seizures as secondary outcomes; its abstract describes EEG normalization in five and seizure-frequency normalization in one, without establishing an evaluable seizure denominator here. The public registry lists adverse events and developmental/behavioral assessments as primary outcomes and microbiota as its listed secondary outcome. Neither genotype nor study establishes safe exposure, antiseizure-drug interactions or efficacy in WWOX. [PHGDH follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12118526/) · [Sibling report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9708551/) · [GRIN study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38380699/) · [Registered protocol](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04646447)

**Discriminating WWOX prediction.** At matched cellular maturity and in the same compartment, measure the intracellular alanine/L-serine ratio, 1-deoxysphinganine and 1-deoxydihydroceramide before interpreting a change in total myelin lipid. If an abnormal ratio and excess atypical products normalize with L-serine, verify canonical sphingolipid production and functional ensheathment or conduction under matched activity. Reduced atypical lipid alone could also follow generalized inhibition of synthesis. If basal atypical lipids, L-serine supply and flux are normal, or supplementation changes only NMDA-dependent firing without a myelin benefit, lower the priority of a WWOX-specific lipid rescue. Reopen for replicated WWOX-selective biochemical correction that predicts functional improvement without worsening pathological bursts. No such matched WWOX lipid/functional result was established.
