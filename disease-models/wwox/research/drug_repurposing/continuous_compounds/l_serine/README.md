# L-serine and the decision between membrane synthesis and excitatory co-agonism

**Opening prediction, before the RNA comparison.** Juvenile WWOX models show defects in ensheathment and cell development. If a local shortage of L-serine for membrane lipid synthesis contributes, uptake of supplied L-serine into canonical sphingoid bases should precede improved functional ensheathment. Compare an isogenic corrected preparation at the same age, activity and viable lineage-cell count. An increase restricted to total serine or the number of precursors would not establish functional rescue. The same precursor may supply D-serine, a co-agonist at NMDA receptors. A rise in D-serine with NMDA-dependent firing and little new lipid or conduction improvement would weaken the proposed benefit. The two fates can coexist. Measure baseline L- and D-serine separately, and lipid incorporation alongside absolute circuit activity.

**Rationale for opening.** A named nutrient with pediatric replacement experience in a defined defect of serine synthesis provides a biochemical positive control. WWOX has no established equivalent substrate deficiency in the records examined here. The first comparison tests whether three untreated WWOX RNA datasets consistently support lower capacity for de novo synthesis (PHGDH, PSAT1, PSPH) or a narrower downstream difference. Inspect transcript levels for racemase and sphingolipid synthesis in parallel. Differences in tissue composition, maturation and cell state can change the panel without changing substrate availability. [Pediatric genetic comparator](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12118526/) · [WWOX myelin denominator](../clemastine/axonal_denominator.md)

**First result.** KO minus control mean log2(1 + normalized RNA) for PHGDH, PSAT1 and PSPH, respectively: mouse cortex −0.22, −0.40, +0.15; human organoids −0.32, +0.41, −0.06; mouse hippocampus −0.13, −0.12, −0.59. The direction of PSAT1 persists with each single-library omission in cortex and organoids, where its signs oppose. Thus the panel gives little support for a shared three-enzyme defect. An individual enzyme, compartment or substrate supply can still limit flux. The human libraries represent two control and four KO samples. Descriptive contrasts cannot determine enzyme activity, substrate concentrations or cellular origin.

**Cell-class check.** In pooled hippocampal nuclei assigned to astroglia, PHGDH declined from 67.1 to 25.6 counts per million (98 versus 44 molecules; 82/417 versus 39/479 nuclei detected). PSAT1 went from 27.4 to 30.8 and PSPH from 28.7 to 22.1. Only one pooled library represents each genotype. Sparse nuclear counts leave subtype proportions, maturation and RNA capture as alternatives. This result narrows the question to the first step of serine synthesis in the astroglial compartment. It does not show less serine in a cell or establish enzyme activity. **Next comparison.** Track glucose-derived serine in astroglia and supplied L-serine incorporation into sphingoid bases versus D-serine and glycine in matched WWOX and corrected preparations. Prespecify a low endogenous L-serine pool or low de novo flux and separate these alternatives. With matched uptake, a functional rescue following preferential lipid incorporation would support substrate limitation. If the NMDA-dependent circuit response changes without a lipid or conduction gain, examine co-agonism. Both D-serine and glycine can contribute; restoration of a synaptic response could also reflect normalization of abnormally low activity. Measure basal pathological bursts and physiological evoked recruitment separately. Before promotion, test the pediatric genetic comparator for measured central exposure, composition of administered amino acids, seizure versus developmental outcomes, and selection bias.
