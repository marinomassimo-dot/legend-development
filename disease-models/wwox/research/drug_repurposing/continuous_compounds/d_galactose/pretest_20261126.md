# D-galactose · opening comparison

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · prespecified before the expression extraction

The held WWOX data show lower COG-pathway expression and a non-neural lectin/localization phenotype. Galactose is a direct test of whether a terminal Gal delivery step is substrate-limited. It can feed the Leloir pathway and UDP-Gal formation. It could also alter UDP-glucose and other pools. Cytosol, Golgi lumen and glycan incorporation therefore need separate observations. The two assay controls are PGM1-related donor supply and a characterized SLC35A2-related Golgi delivery defect. Partial transport defects can respond to higher donor supply. A galactose response alone cannot assign the bottleneck. [WWOX premise](../d_mannose/README.md) · [PGM1-CDG primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28617415/)

Run the held three-cohort bulk comparison and report all targets. Retain one-library omissions as a descriptive stability check. A repeated direction is a measurement priority and neither a metabolic diagnosis nor a clinical result.

For benchmarking, candidate outputs are cytosolic UDP-Gal and UDP-Glc and their labelled fractions, an independent Golgi delivery or lumen-access readout, terminal galactosylation on occupied N-sites, and Tn-to-core-1 conversion on occupied O-GalNAc cargo. Include a same-cargo abundance measure, glycoconjugate specificity (a Gal signal can include lipids), and stage-matched controls. Keep Gal-1-P, ATP and viability in the interpretation when donor pools change.

- **Supply-limited prediction.** Cytosolic UDP-Gal starts low, Golgi delivery is preserved relative to the donor pool, and the initially abnormal maturation readouts recover with measured donor recovery.
- **Delivery-limited prediction.** Cytosolic UDP-Gal rises with poor lumen access at the same donor level. Compare the response to repair or partial loss of transport, since residual transport may improve as more donor is supplied.
- **Processing-limited prediction.** Lumen access recovers or starts adequate, while specific mature glycoforms and coatomer localization remain altered. A selective N- versus O-glycan response directs the next assay.

Prioritize the molecule further only if a WWOX-specific biochemical defect is paired with a measured, exposure-linked neural response and a relevant functional outcome. The existence of clinical work in another glycosylation disorder supplies comparator material.
