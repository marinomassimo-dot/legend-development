# Ambroxol hydrochloride · first comparative test

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN · transcript abundance in WWOX mouse forebrain bulk and hippocampal nuclei, and human cerebral organoids · GCase activity, lysosomal pH, glucosylceramide and glucosylsphingosine as prospective neural biochemical readouts

**Question.** Does WWOX loss create a deficit in lysosomal glucocerebrosidase activity that ambroxol can relieve, or does the phenotype reflect lysosomal maturation, pH, trafficking or altered membrane composition without reversible GCase limitation? This extends the glycosphingolipid strand. Neu5Ac asked about availability of a building block for mature membrane glycoconjugates. Ambroxol tests a distinct failure mode of membrane lipid disposal. GBA-associated disease data can calibrate the exposure–activity comparison for this case.

**Before examining directional results.** In the same held models, compare GBA1 with lysosomal delivery (SCARB2), catalytic context and substrate generation (PSAP, GBA2, UGCG, ASAH1), lysosomal abundance/acidity (LAMP1, ATP6V1A, CTSD, TFEB, TFE3), and branch enzyme controls (ST3GAL5, B4GALNT1). In mouse matrices map the historical Gba symbol and stable identifier explicitly and retain true unassigned members. Evaluate matched control and knockout library counts, sensitivity to omitting each library, and pooled nuclear directions without treating nuclei as replicates. Tissue-consistent transcript loss would prioritize measuring enzyme capacity. pH/trafficking or an RNA null shifts priority to activity under fixed assay conditions and matched cell composition. Neither RNA direction proves a GCase defect.

**Predictions.** Normalize mature GCase quantity and activity to lysosomal content and assess cellular lysosomal pH before interpreting any catalytic difference. At measured free drug and after controlled washout, a GCase chaperone effect should leave more mature or delivered enzyme, higher lysosomal substrate turnover with concordant glucosylsphingosine and glucosylceramide correction, and selective WWOX improvement in evoked neuronal recruitment or axonal function. A reversible direct occupancy effect can change activity in the presence of drug without persistent mature enzyme or substrate changes. A general pH or lysosomal biogenesis effect should move additional hydrolases or lysosomal volume and weaken after controlling for pH or matching lysosomal content. A change shared by treated isogenic controls at the same uptake, or a drop in native substrate turnover at exposure, rules out the proposed genotype-selective rescue.

**Exposure and priority.** Use adult or pediatric exposure reports only for the same parent, route and formulation. Compare CSF enzyme activity with intact acidic lysosome measurements, and carry sampling timing, free parent and assay pH with the numbers. A clear CSF exposure and tolerability in another indication may aid assay selection. Pediatric chronic CNS exposure, the interaction with background antiseizure medicines and a disease-relevant proximal endpoint must be addressed before trial priority. Stop if exact baseline lysosomal substrate and enzyme chemistry make the WWOX bottleneck implausible or drug action fails at credible exposure.
