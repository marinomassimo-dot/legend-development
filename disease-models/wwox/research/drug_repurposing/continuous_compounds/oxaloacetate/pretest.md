## Research question

Could peripheral glutamate transamination alter the slower extracellular glutamate component in a WWOX network, and can it be distinguished from a direct central effect of oxaloacetate or a downstream labeled metabolite? Name the formulation and verify intact parent at each exposure surface. Compare uninjured or matched-barrier settings, and treat ischemic, traumatic or imposed tracer-loading results as separate models.

## Predictions and controls

- **Peripheral sink.** Measured blood glutamate falls and brain-to-blood tracer flux rises without measured intact parent in the brain. The effect should depend on available vascular glutamate oxaloacetate transaminase and on baseline effective brain-to-blood transfer. A slower extracellular glutamate component may fall while the first per-fusion peak and early inhibitory recruitment are preserved. A large blood change with stable neural exposure and unchanged neural glutamate sets a boundary.
- **Central metabolism or state.** A neural effect persists when plasma glutamate or vascular transaminase activity is held fixed. It scales with measured neural parent or a labeled downstream metabolite and a matched local metabolic or electrophysiological readout. A downstream label alone does not demonstrate intact parent delivery.
- **Lesion or clearance alternative.** A response appears only with injury, high exogenous glutamate or barrier disruption. The next WWOX step then requires direct evidence of endogenous glutamate clearance and transfer under physiological stimulation. A change confined to the first-fusion peak would not establish a vascular sink.

Day-one data plan. Extract paired blood and CSF or interstitial outcomes, time course, tracer direction, geometry, barriers and replication unit. Test whether a two-compartment glutamate model can reproduce the observations with vascular clearance alone, and identify the brain observation that would constrain it. Independently verify kinetic or electrophysiology evidence of central oxaloacetate or a metabolite. Integrate the held WWOX extracellular glutamate and tissue data only at matched developmental and measurement scope. Require central exposure evidence and a relevant spontaneous outcome before considering pediatric priority.
