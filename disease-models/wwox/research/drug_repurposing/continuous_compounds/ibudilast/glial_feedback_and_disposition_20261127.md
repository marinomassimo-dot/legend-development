# Ibudilast in WWOX loss of function

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Disposition.** Park for pediatric WWOX trial prioritization and retain as a preclinical probe of delayed glial feedback. The most informative transfer question is whether ibudilast changes recurrent dysfunction through microglia at measured free exposure and matched first recruitment. The existing WWOX glial observations do not establish temporal order or drug response. A nascent glial feedback loop could be an amplifier after the first electrical disturbance even if it did not initiate the disease.

## Human outcomes and pediatric feasibility

- SPRINT-MS randomized 129 versus 126 adults with progressive MS. The protocol-defined MRI analysis included 121 versus 123 with post-baseline imaging. Brain parenchymal fraction declined by −0.0010 versus −0.0019 per year over 96 weeks, adjusted difference +0.0009 (95% CI +0.00004 to +0.0017). Twenty-week confirmed disability progression had HR 0.74 (95% CI 0.43 to 1.28). Completion at week 96 was 108/129 versus 112/126. Gastrointestinal symptoms, headache and depression were observed more often with ibudilast. The brain-volume signal supplies an adult clinical precedent and leaves the functional, tract-specific and developmental hypotheses unresolved. [Fox et al., author manuscript pp. 5–7 and 14, Figs. 1–2 and Tables 2–3](https://escholarship.org/content/qt2v87p5vz/qt2v87p5vz.pdf?t=rvtll5)
- A secondary analysis of the same trial compared MIF with imaging in 223 participants. It found no clear ibudilast effect on serum or CSF MIF concentration and did not exclude effects on MIF function. This is neither an independent trial nor a measurement of free ibudilast in CSF. It argues against using total MIF as the only pharmacodynamic endpoint. [Ladakis et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37982154/)
- STP1 randomized nine treated and three placebo adults with a selected autism phenotype, in two 14-day cohorts. Treatment deliberately combined ibudilast and bumetanide. The paired scalp gamma analysis shown in Fig. 4C had five + three treated and two placebo participants. The concentration correlations in Fig. 4D pooled day 1 and day 14 measurements using Pearson correlations and do not represent 16 independently treated participants. Changes in exploratory gamma and auditory measures do not identify an ibudilast monotherapy effect or a decrease in seizure burden. The clinical scales did not show a clear benefit. [STP1 primary report, §§2.1, 3.3–3.4 and Fig. 4](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-9059/12/7/1430)
- The Japanese extended-release oral capsule has adult asthma and post-stroke dizziness indications. The Japanese label §9.7 states that pediatric clinical trials have not been conducted for the labeled product, §11 includes gastrointestinal and other risks, and §14.1 instructs against dispensing contents removed from the capsule. The records examined here lack a validated pediatric WWOX formulation and free neural exposure window, a replicable enrichment feature and an efficacy signal. [PMDA Ketas prescribing information, §§4, 6, 9.7, 11 and 14.1](https://www.pmda.go.jp/PmdaSearch/iyakuDetail/231099_4490010N1021_2_04)

## Discriminating test and next decision

- **Hypothesis.** At the same initial activity and cell maturity, WWOX neural tissue could generate a delayed microglia-dependent signal that amplifies later recurrent recruitment or axonal injury. A parallel mechanism is acute PDE-dependent modulation in neurons, astrocytes or the myelinating lineage. Parallel or sequential mechanisms may coexist.
- **Prediction.** In matched WWOX and corrected neural preparations with and without defined microglia, use the within-genotype ibudilast-versus-vehicle difference in prespecified late recurrent recruitment. Compare that drug difference between preparations with and without microglia. The microglial mechanism predicts a larger late benefit in mutant tissue with microglia, after preserved first recruitment and early inhibition. Short-latency changes in cAMP or cGMP and function in the microglia-free arm would favor a direct perturbation. The early response and cell composition must be measured in each preparation.
- **Countercheck.** Independently verify local free parent, count viable cells and quantify physical uptake of damaged material alongside new sheath or axonal conduction. Consider microglia exposure followed by a matched washout and medium transfer. A transfer effect needs measured residual drug below a functionally inactive concentration, a matched drug-spiked medium control, cell-contact controls and evidence of preserved physiological recruitment. For positive results only, compare a validated PDE mechanism control and functional MIF or TLR4 pathway perturbation separately. Do not use impaired movement, lower early firing or less glial staining alone as recovery.
- **Checkpoint.** First look for the native late response with matched early output and verified microglial state, without relying on a strong exogenous challenge to generate the phenotype. Reopen drug attribution only if the response replicates across independent preparations, remains exposure-supported and preserves circuit or glial support function. An independent review could change prioritization if a time-resolved WWOX dataset, pediatric free CNS exposure, or a selective functional rescue becomes available. Current replications of the aggregate calculations would leave the decision unchanged.
