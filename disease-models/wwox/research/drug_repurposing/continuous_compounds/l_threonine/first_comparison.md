## First primary-text checkpoint

Growdon et al. Methods (p. 406, Protocols) report 18 enrolled and **10 patients with lumbar sampling at both period ends**. One other participant underwent a single puncture, and seven declined. Both treatment periods lasted two weeks, with a two-week washout. Table 3 (p. 409) does not give a denominator per mean or identify whether the unpaired sample entered an aggregate. The paired sample count and Table 3 denominator are therefore recorded separately.

| Lumbar CSF, Table 3 | Placebo mean ± SEM | L-threonine mean ± SEM | Difference of reported means | Reported contrast |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Threonine, µM | 51.0 ± 6.4 | 101.0 ± 9.7 | +50.0 µM, +98.0% | p<0.05 |
| Glycine, µM | 8.4 ± 0.7 | 7.6 ± 0.8 | −0.8 µM, −9.5% | No detected difference |

Percent change is 100 × (treatment / placebo − 1). These are differences of reported means with no recovered paired covariance. The two SEMs cannot supply a paired interval or a test of local glycine production. A direct central parent response without a larger average lumbar pool makes bulk glycine elevation an unsuitable assumed mediator. It leaves local spinal isotope flux and receptor recruitment unmeasured. Next verify the motor-score definition and independent cohorts, then compare rat extracellular sampling and the isotope pathway claims.
