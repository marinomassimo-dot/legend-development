# L-threonine, extracellular glycine and spinal motor signs

28 November 2026 UTC. Continuous compound #208, INV-05. context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. [Prespecified comparison](pretest.md) · [Opening comparison](first_comparison.md) · [Replay output](comparison.tsv)

**Disposition.** Retain L-threonine as a conditional precursor and amino-acid transport comparator. Defer pediatric WWOX priority. Adult controlled studies show lumbar parent exposure without a detected increase in bulk CSF glycine. Motor examination signals are endpoint-dependent, and the controlled multiple-sclerosis study did not detect a change in the measured reflex or EMG endpoints. In juvenile rat tissue, a glycine increase coexists with no detected dialysate increase. These data leave a local synaptic precursor effect open but give no basis to assume it.

## Where precursor and product separate

**Rat countercheck.** In Castagné et al., three-week-old male rats (six per group) ate graded diets for two weeks. Compared with middle diet B, high diet C kept the B milk protein base and replaced carbohydrate with free threonine. In Table II, brain tissue threonine rose **10.3 to 43.4 nmol/mg protein** and brain glycine **4.4 to 6.0 nmol/mg protein (+36.4%)**. Plasma glycine rose **342 to 922 nmol/mL**. Yet ex vivo microdialysis detected no diet-related increase in glycine in cortex, brainstem or thoracocervical cord, despite higher dialysate threonine in the high arm. Cord glycine responded to the perfusate sequence, showing a measurable dynamic response. The high arm also grew more slowly. Thick slices undergo equilibration and washout, and membrane recovery differed by probe. The design tests sampled slice dialysate, without local in vivo flux, a precise equivalence interval, or the origin of the whole-brain glycine increment. Earlier acute rat dosing raised cord tissue and isolated synaptosome content, another compartment-level result. [Castagné et al., Methods, Tables I–II and region-specific Results](https://www.academia.edu/47321341/Effects_of_diet_induced_hyperthreoninemia_II_Tissue_and_extracellular_amino_acid_levels_in_the_brain) · [Maher and Wurtman abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6770208/)

**Whole-body conversion and enzyme assignment.** In six healthy adult men over three diets, Darling et al. measured low glycine label attributed to threonine. Their single carbon-1 threonine label and urinary glycine/hippurate proxy reflect whole-body and hepatic weighting. Using published rate means, the glycine share of the two modeled catabolic routes is **9.6% on control, 6.7% on free excess threonine, and 7.0% on protein-bound excess threonine**. These ratios of means are not the paper's average of subject-specific ratios. The absolute glycine rate was numerically higher with both excess diets (diet comparison p=.06), while oxidation to carbon dioxide rose markedly. Molecular sequence and cDNA findings identify human **TDH** as a transcribed pseudogene. Purified human **SHMT1 and SHMT2** have measurable aldolase activity toward natural L-threonine in a recent primary enzyme assay. Preferential activity on L-allo-threonine belongs to another stereoisomer. Neither result assigns a flux to juvenile neural tissue. In a separate nutritional tracer study of ten healthy term newborns in the first 48 h, enrichment attributable to threonine was undetectable in plasma glycine with a multi-labeled precursor. The adult and newborn tracer designs cannot be pooled as an age effect. [Darling et al.](https://journals.physiology.org/doi/10.1152/ajpendo.2000.278.5.E877) · [Edgar, gene and cDNA evidence](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC131051/) · [Hayashi et al. abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41429744/) · [Parimi et al. abstract](https://pure.johnshopkins.edu/en/publications/metabolism-of-threonine-in-newborn-infants/)

**A tracer ambiguity worth testing.** Cleavage retaining threonine carbons 1 and 2 can produce two-carbon labeled glycine from a suitably labeled precursor. With only a carbon-1 label, carboxyl label in glycine could also arise after oxidation to labeled bicarbonate and carboxyl exchange. This exchange has been demonstrated in rat liver mitochondrial preparations. Its contribution to the tiny adult human signal has not been measured. A double-retained label, an early time course, bicarbonate and serine measurements, a systemic product measurement and a local conversion comparison can separate the main alternatives. A signal alone does not assign the route or distinguish direct glycine synthesis from turnover of a pool. [Rat mitochondrial exchange primary abstract](https://academic.oup.com/jb/article-abstract/65/1/77/1044616)

## One discriminating WWOX comparison

**Held evidence.** WWOX-DEE can combine axial hypotonia with limb spasticity. A less brisk limb reflex might coexist with worse useful force or axial support. The held WWOX forebrain work reports lower bulk GlyT1 RNA and uncertain compartment attribution. Those observations neither identify an extracellular spinal glycine deficit nor quantify supply. Rat BBB uptake of threonine was sensitive to a system-L model competitor and partially sodium dependent, leaving shared amino-acid transport as an alternative. Developing spinal preparations show that a glycine conductance can depolarize and still suppress evoked excitation through shunting, or increase propagation in a different age and preparation. [WWOX cohort abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36779245/) · [Held glycine comparison](../glycine/README.md) · [Rat transport primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3138388/) · [Newborn reflex primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11918657/) · [Embryonic propagation primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20053920/)

**Hypothesis.** L-threonine reduces excessive late reflex recruitment through an increase in locally synthesized extracellular glycine. First establish an age-matched native WWOX spinal phenotype that preserves enough first recruitment to interpret a later response. At matched measured free parent and a fixed background amino-acid composition, compare labeled precursor and vehicle. Measure fast appearance of retained-carbon glycine in the same spinal compartment, with circulating labeled product, bicarbonate, serine and competing neutral amino acids as source controls. Verify the sign of native glycine responses without replacing intracellular chloride and separate GlyR-dependent first/late recruitment from NMDA coagonism. Include an independent local conversion comparison that preserves exposure and a direct glycine bypass to check response capacity. Evaluate the motor output at matched first force and confirm that a lower reflex does not track loss of usable recruitment.

- **Supports local synthesis.** The additional labeled product appears locally before or beyond the contribution predicted by systemic product. A late reflex phenotype improves at preserved first recruitment, requires the conversion and GlyR response, and can be reproduced by a matched glycine bypass.
- **Favors alternatives or lowers priority.** Changes follow parent or transport competition without the predicted product and receptor response, or are explained by reduced first recruitment. A larger labeled tissue pool with no resolved extracellular increment supplies conversion evidence only. An adverse propagation or force effect at verified positive glycine engagement lowers priority.
- **Inconclusive.** Unverified free exposure, an insensitive extracellular assay or unbalanced first recruitment makes a null comparison ambiguous.

## Pediatric feasibility and reopening

The short controlled oral studies concern other disorders. A four-week randomized supplementation study in healthy adult men observed limited systemic changes, including nonmonotonic laboratory signals. It did not supply pediatric CNS exposure or tested interactions with WWOX antiseizure polytherapy. The newborn tracer study reflects nutrition and systemic turnover. It does not qualify an oral formulation for chronic use in this setting. Targeted motor function, axial support, feeding and electrographic burden should remain separate if the preclinical result justifies pediatric feasibility work. Reopen for local product at an age-relevant exposure and a beneficial native reflex effect with first force preserved. A second reading would change the decision if it identified a paired local oral exposure/flux dataset or a positive functional endpoint under the same human contrast. [Adult tolerability abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40419835/)
