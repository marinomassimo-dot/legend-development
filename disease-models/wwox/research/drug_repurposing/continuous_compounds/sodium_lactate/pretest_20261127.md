# Sodium L-lactate in WWOX loss of function

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · opening prediction · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Question.** Can measured extracellular L-lactate restore demand-linked neuronal energy and conduction in WWOX, while preserving a physiological balance of presynaptic release and postsynaptic NMDA gain? Use the defined L stereoisomer with a matched sodium, pH, buffer and osmolality control. The held INV-07 work distinguishes production, export and recipient use. This question concerns the recipient response to added compound. **First comparison.** Reconcile adult exposure evidence with separate primary preparations for postsynaptic NMDA and HCAR1-linked presynaptic effects. Record bath, effective compartment, stimulus and recording denominator. Treat a direction from cortex culture and a direction from CA1 slices as competing possibilities. A pharmacologically isolated current, a synaptic event count and a tissue isotope signal answer different questions.

**Discriminating prediction.** At matched extracellular L-lactate and workload in WWOX-deficient and corrected cells, a fuel effect must add labeled oxidative carbon and improve the recovery of ATP, sodium gradients and evoked function. Recipient transport limitation predicts a smaller intracellular increment and less flux despite matched extracellular supply. Measure native astrocyte production and donor-recipient transfer independently where present. A receptor or redox effect may persist without useful oxidative recovery. In the same preparation, compare fixed-input NMDA current with separately measured excitatory and inhibitory recruitment and an HCAR1-dependence control. Measure cytosolic NADH/NAD and extracellular and intracellular pH. A fall in spontaneous excitation with failed inhibitory or patterned recruitment is an adverse-direction result even if ATP rises.

**Decision rule.** Require a verified basal deficit and a selective, functional response at measured exposure. Defer pediatric trial priority if sodium, alkalinization or nonspecific suppression accounts for the signal, or if selective benefit is supported only by adult neurocritical care or mixed resuscitation fluids.
