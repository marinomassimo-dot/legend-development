# Sodium L-lactate, neural exposure and competing circuit effects

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Decision.** Retain sodium L-lactate as a probe of energy recovery and synaptic modulation. Defer a pediatric WWOX trial. Human data support brain access and use in defined settings, and an acute pediatric EEG comparison is available in GLUT1 deficiency. The bridge to local neuronal oxidation, selective WWOX recovery and preserved circuit function remains open. | van Gemert et al. Two 12-year-olds with GLUT1 deficiency and absence epilepsy, separate meal and sodium lactate visits. | Acute video-EEG and clinical absences improved in **one of two** after lactate, and **the same participant** improved after the carbohydrate-rich meal. Neither intervention showed the corresponding change in the other. Sodium, lactate and pH rose transiently in both. | Supports feasibility of observing an acute EEG response in this setting. A separate meal visit is a fuel-response comparator, with no blinded randomized infusion, chemistry-matched comparator or paired measurement of free brain lactate. The duration of observation leaves sustained seizure, developmental and functional outcomes open. [Methods and Results, pp. 366–369, Fig. 1–2 captions](https://www.thieme-connect.de/products/ejournals/pdf/10.1055/a-2134-8766.pdf) |

**Exposure inference.** The adult dialysate measurements show that nominal **3 mmol/L** from the CA1 hyperpolarization assay is in the numerical range of some injured-adult extracellular measurements. The Bernini lactate visit already had a baseline median above 3 mmol/L, and the data do not establish that infusing lactate creates that local exposure in juvenile WWOX. The **10 mmol/L** used for the fixed agonist cortical-current experiment is above the group medians shown here. The wide concentrations in injured tissue and the differences across compartments mean that the study does not establish an upper bound for local exposure. Plasma peaks, infusion formulation, brain MRS concentrations, dialysate and nominal bath concentrations should be tracked separately. [Opening primary comparisons and denominators](first_comparison_20261127.md)

**Clinical countercheck.** Ichai et al. randomized 60 TBI patients to half-molar sodium lactate or isotonic saline and reported 23 vs 53 episodes of raised intracranial pressure requiring treatment over 48 hours. The episode counts include repeated observations, and the comparator differed in chemistry and tonicity. That trial supports a pressure endpoint under its own conditions. Bernini's volume- and osmolarity-matched comparison narrows the contrast and leaves the specific lactate mechanism unresolved. [Ichai primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23749153/)

## WWOX inference and discriminating observation

The archived WWOX comparison has lower bulk SLC16A7 transcript across all three systems. Pooled assigned hippocampal nuclei were **140.64→77.91 CPM**, with **2,593→1,292 UMIs** and the same direction after omitting each cell class in turn. One pool per genotype leaves animal-level replication unresolved. The independent astrocyte analysis already distinguishes production, export and use, and shows why stable labeled pools alone do not establish preservation of flux. [WWOX source and pool analysis](../levacetylleucine/README.md) · [Existing astrocyte analysis](../../advisor_pilot_20261113/brainstorm_20261116/adv177_murlanova_astrocyte_lactate_sci_20261122/RESULT_ADV177_MURLANOVA_ASTROCYTE_LACTATE_SCI_20261122.md)

**Hypothesis.** A subset of WWOX circuit impairment reflects low effective recipient-cell lactate use during demand. The same local exposure can also change excitatory recruitment through HCAR1-associated signaling and change the postsynaptic NMDA component. A lower spontaneous event rate therefore leaves the net evoked response unresolved.

**Predicted pattern.** In stage-matched WWOX and corrected preparations, compare the genotype-by-lactate interaction for the time course of intracellular label, downstream oxidative products and recovery of ATP and patterned firing after the same demand. Match extracellular sodium, osmolarity, pH, chloride, glucose and oxygen as feasible with documented replacement chemistry, and measure intracellular pH given proton-coupled transport. Analyze biological preparations as the replication unit. Different recording outcomes adjudicate the alternatives.

| Observation at measured local exposure | Interpretation to test |
| --- | --- |
| Slower early recipient-cell labeling in WWOX at equal extracellular labeled lactate, with relative preservation of oxidation of a comparator substrate where its local entry is verified | Recipient transfer or intracellular access may limit use. The pooled transcript alone cannot assign the transporter or its capacity. |
| Comparable early intracellular labeling but poor appearance in downstream oxidative products and no selective recovery under demand | A bottleneck after entry would move the next comparison toward conversion, redox or oxidation. |
| Higher time-resolved label in oxidative products with genotype-preferential ATP and evoked recovery, preserved physiology, and persistence under a validated HCAR1 control | Supports a fuel contribution to recovery. Quantify extracellular enrichment, products and net use to account for exchange and dilution. If the same recovery occurs under matched glucose or pyruvate availability, the inference shifts toward a more general substrate limitation. |
| Change in a direct release readout with HCAR1 dependence and no selective ATP recovery, or an increase in fixed-input late postsynaptic NMDA current | Supports a circuit effect that can coexist with fuel use. Track evoked inhibitory recruitment and net output in the same preparation. A presynaptic decline can conceal increased postsynaptic responsiveness. |
| Effects reproduced by the matched ionic/pH comparator or increased late recruitment despite energy recovery | Reassess the selective lactate inference or stop advancement for the circuit indication. |

**Reproducible counterexample.** The recorded cultured-neuron fixed-input current magnitude ratio is 2.30/0.89 = 2.584. Under an illustrative model, let the contribution of NMDA to baseline charge be w, multiply the overall input by r, and multiply only that component by g. Relative charge is r × ((1 − w) + w × g). With r set illustratively to 0.70 and g equal to the observed fixed-input ratio, the sign reverses at w = **0.271**. At w 0.20 the result is **0.922** of baseline charge and at w 0.40 it is **1.144**. The source measured a peak, so its ratio is used here only as an illustrative multiplier for charge. The release ratio and component weights are synthetic. The model holds the inhibition and timing fixed, assumes separability and combines no biological estimates across preparations. ## Feasibility and checkpoint

Acute monitored IV exposure in two older children supports feasibility of measurement in a specific metabolic condition. It provides no chronic or early-life WWOX-specific formulation, interaction or tolerability estimate. The authorized label for a mixed lactated Ringer's formulation addresses fluid/electrolyte use, cautions about infant lactate metabolism and describes potential interference with the elimination of pH-dependent drugs. A study of an exact sodium L-lactate formulation would require independent characterization of local exposure, acid–base and sodium effects and background treatment, including ketogenic diet and medications affecting bicarbonate. The dossier supplies no regimen. [Pediatric proof of principle](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37478891/) · [DailyMed, pediatric use and drug interactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=91ae5b40-9b59-4231-8ab3-69502245d918)

**Reopen** when a juvenile WWOX and corrected comparison at measured neural exposure separates oxidative recovery from presynaptic and postsynaptic responses with preserved evoked inhibition and patterned output. A new independent review could change the decision if it identifies held WWOX target-site exposure or these matched measurements. The adult and pediatric primary findings alone do not resolve that gap. Continue from the compound register.
