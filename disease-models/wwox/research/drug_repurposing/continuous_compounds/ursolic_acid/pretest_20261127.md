# Ursolic acid | Prespecified glial-versus-axon comparison

Frozen at 2026-11-27 21:45 UTC from development/main 4e7083a2c after a title and abstract survey. The question is whether a low-exposure, astrocyte-dependent signal can restore functional first sheaths in the WWOX setting. The compound is the purified parent ursolic acid, with route and formulation reported independently.

- Compare pharmacology in conditioned medium, direct oligodendrocytes and intact injury models. A trophic mediator requires controls for parent carryover, antibody specificity, secreted mediator abundance and viable cell denominators. A marker shift alone leaves sheath structure and function unresolved.
- Quantify parent at the source and qualify the assay. Compare nominal in vitro concentrations and tissue or plasma measurements without equating compartments. A positive effect at a measured, exposure-matched local concentration ranks above a gap bridged by an unmeasured free fraction.
- In a WWOX model, selective benefit requires persistent first sheaths, caliber-adjusted ultrastructure or conduction on WWOX-deficient axons. Compare corrected axons and glia under matched firing and survival. A result confined to healthy axons lowers priority even if lineage markers respond. An intervention that changes isolated lineage cells without restoring axonal outcomes supports a glial effect with a downstream bottleneck.
- Track PPARG, CNTF and the three receptor subunits, CNTFR, LIFR and IL6ST. Compare developmental contexts and cell compartments separately. RNA is a hypothesis check. Glial signaling and native engagement require protein or functional evidence.

The decision concerns preclinical research priority. Any pediatric inference depends on measured target-site exposure, formulation, age and interpretable functional outcomes.
