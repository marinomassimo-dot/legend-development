# Sultiame · low-concentration sodium-current comparison

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

In primary whole-cell recordings from acutely isolated guinea-pig hippocampal neurons, the reported nominal **1–10 µg/mL** sultiame range reduced sodium current by **13–25%**. At 10 µg/mL, reducing sodium current impaired repetitive firing. The abstract describes about **20–40%** lower maximal firing frequency for reductions of that size induced by tetrodotoxin or sultiame. The exposure converts to **3.44–34.44 µM** using **290.4 g/mol**. The nominal range contains the **6.47 µM** highest geometric-mean adult single-dose total plasma peak and the **19.56–32.27 µM** high-group mean and maximum measured adult repeat-dose total troughs. These are comparisons of numerical ranges across distinct settings. They establish neither free CNS exposure nor an effect on human WWOX neurons. No open full-text copy providing the original buffer and pH control was located in the publisher previews or institutional searches. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11325350/) · [Adult single-dose study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6986439/) · [Adult repeat-dose study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chest.2023.09.022)

**Decision delta.** The historical millimolar CA3 acidification comparison is still an ill-matched exposure reference. The sodium-current result makes intrinsic excitability a plausible alternative at lower nominal concentrations. The original recording buffer, reversibility, isoform and pH controls require full-text verification. Lower sodium current in an isolated neuron does not establish direct channel binding or independence from pH.

## Transfer checks

**Pediatric EEG and clinical outcomes use different denominators.** Laliberté et al. reported seizure improvement in **4 of 12** children with uncontrolled seizures at sultiame initiation, and worsening in **2 of 12**. Four of the 16 had been seizure-free at baseline. None of the six named genetic diagnoses was WWOX. In the eight complete EEG pairs, the mean sleep spike–wave index change was **−36.1 percentage points**, persisting at **−28.4 to −41.8** across all single-pair removals. Six of eight had decreases (exact sign-test P = 0.289). The sign check asks a different question from the authors' paired t test (P = 0.007), which uses the size of each change. Neither addresses unknown selection in the eight missing pairs, nonuniform EEG timing, or the effect of background treatment. The NDUFS1 case had rare seizures before treatment and a substantial SWI change. It does not establish mitochondrial rescue. The cohort reported adverse events in **6/16**, with discontinuation attributed to adverse events in three. [Laliberté et al., Methods, Table 1 and Results §§3.3–3.4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/epd2.20250)

**Respiratory reserve belongs in the mechanistic comparison.** Reinert et al. studied fibroblasts from one healthy donor and two adults with LHON after three hours with **150 µM** nominal sultiame. The two pediatric visual-loss cases contributed no cells. At the last FCCP point before antimycin and rotenone, approximate total oxygen consumption from Fig. 2a–c was 35→35, 37→20 and 27→16 pmol/min for the control, MT-ND1 and MT-ND6 lines, respectively (vehicle→sultiame). Drug/vehicle ratios of **1.00, 0.54 and 0.59** are graphical re-estimates rounded to 1 pmol/min. A ±1-unit reading sensitivity preserves the distinction. These quantities describe total uncoupled OCR from three individual lines. The readout does not estimate corrected mitochondrial flux, IC₅₀ or replication across donors. The cells had a different genetic lesion, and 150 µM is **4.65 times** the highest reported total adult trough. The study provides no low-concentration dose response or WWOX risk estimate. Its two temporally associated LHON visual-loss cases do not establish causation. [Reinert et al., PDF p. 4 Fig. 2a–d and pp. 6–7 Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7860214/) · [Calculation](response_counterchecks.py) · [Results](response_counterchecks.tsv)

## Discriminating result

The held WWOX nuclear data show greater Scn1a counts per million in assigned inhibitory than excitatory cells in both control and knockout. That observation motivates measuring both classes and supplies no information on sulthiame binding to individual sodium-channel isoforms. At measured free exposure, compare sodium-current availability and repetitive firing at matched intracellular pH. Then measure late GABA-associated responses under native chloride at matched initial excitability. A residual sodium-current change at matched pH favours a distinct intrinsic effect. A late-selective change at comparable sodium current and pH favours the bicarbonate branch. Test recovery after matched activity separately. Lower ATP recovery or loss of inhibitory recruitment would weaken the case for prioritizing the compound, even with fewer long discharges. [Held channel data and limits](../rufinamide/channels.md)

**Disposition.** Keep sultiame active for the low-exposure comparison and assign low priority to an unselected pediatric WWOX trial. A controlled WWOX trial would require local exposure and preserved inhibitory recruitment and metabolic recovery. Recovering the original recording buffer and pH controls could change the mechanistic interpretation. Transferable priority would need new local-exposure and WWOX data. No WWOX-specific benefit follows from the pediatric series.
