# Sultiame · adult trough exposure and central readout audit

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

## When the adult comparator is a repeated exposure

In Hoff et al., available trough samples in adult obstructive sleep apnoea numbered **10 and 24** in the two active groups. The higher group's mean total plasma trough was **5.68 µg/mL** and its highest observed trough was **9.37 µg/mL**. These correspond to **19.56 and 32.27 µM**, respectively. Relative to the 1 mM lowest reported sultiame bath in the CA3 paper, they are about **51-fold and 31-fold lower**. These are adult respiratory-study troughs. They provide a countercheck to the small single-dose pilot rather than a ceiling on plasma or brain levels, a matched snapshot, or an estimate for children. [Hoff et al., Results and Fig. 3 caption](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0012369223055447) · [Calculation](exposure.py) · [Results](exposure.tsv)

## Which compartment was actually measured

The 2025 study analysed three comparisons. Experiment 5 measured hemolysed blood CA activity after sultiame in four healthy adults and reported a **25–35% reduction**. Experiment 6 measured baseline CA activity in CSF and hemolysed blood of eight separate adults, using pre-intervention samples. The ratio of reported means was **2.78/14.38 = 0.193**. The paired measurement concerned enzyme activity and supplies no drug transfer coefficient. Experiment 7 related plasma sultiame to blood bicarbonate in adult apnoea after treatment. These observations support a systemic pharmacodynamic effect and the feasibility of measuring CSF CA activity. None tied administered sultiame to quantitated CSF drug or a within-person change in CSF CA activity. The OSA comparisons can draw on the same phase II trial and should not be counted as independent demonstrations. [2025 paper, PDF pp. 3 and 6–7, experiments 5–7 and figure captions](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11325-025-03430-z)

## Decision

The order-of-magnitude separation from the reported millimolar slice bath survives use of a higher measured adult trough. Pediatric central exposure remains open. The next discriminator is a neural pH and bicarbonate response at measured free exposure, with matched intracellular acidification as an independent control. Preserve the first evoked response and separate late GABA-associated firing from global excitability. A CSF CA activity measurement alone could not distinguish local drug exposure from baseline differences in enzyme abundance or sample contamination. Pediatric translational priority stays unassigned while the lower-concentration cellular comparison is investigated.
