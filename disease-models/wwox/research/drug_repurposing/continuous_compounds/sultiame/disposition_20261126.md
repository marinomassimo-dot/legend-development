# Sultiame · exposure, developmental countercheck and disposition

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

## Exposure sensitivity

The UK Ospolot suspension product information reports approximately 29% plasma protein binding. Using a free plasma fraction of 0.71 as an assumption, matching the lowest reported nominal sodium-current bath, 3.44 µM, would require a free brain-to-plasma ratio of **0.75** at the adult single-dose geometric-mean peak, **0.25** at the high-group repeat-dose mean trough, or **0.15** at its maximum measured trough. At that maximum trough, the upper reported sodium bath would require **1.50** and the lowest reported CA3 acidification bath **43.65**. These are required ratios calculated by dividing the nominal bath concentration by assumed free plasma concentration. They are neither measured transfer coefficients nor minimum effective concentrations. The bath-to-brain comparison assumes the nominal bath value represents free concentration at the recorded cell. The adult samples came from different protocols. Erythrocyte enrichment, a fixed binding assumption across cohorts and an unknown free pediatric brain exposure all limit translation. ## Developmental countercheck

The 2005 article abstract reports neuronal degeneration in neonatal rats after systemic sultiame at 100 mg/kg and above. First author Daniela Manthey’s archived dissertation describes the underlying dose experiment. It used single intraperitoneal exposure at P7, with assessment after 24 h. At the high 300 mg/kg condition, the reported mean densities of degenerating cells were **1,626 → 16,072/mm³** in frontal cortical layer II (9.88-fold), and **327 → 2,106/mm³** in CA1 (6.44-fold), vehicle to sultiame, with **15 vehicle and 6 sultiame animals** per comparison. The same preparations contributed to the regional measurements. These are ratios of means and the regional ratios do not rank susceptibility, particularly where control densities are low. No pH-matched or exposure-matched control or paired sultiame brain concentration is reported in the sections used here. In the separate age series at 200 mg/kg, differences were reported at P0, P3 and P7, but no significant increase at P14 or P21. The assay used TUNEL at P0/P3 and silver staining from P7, and the within-age tests are not a formal age interaction. The published article and dissertation are one experimental source family. Neither data set supplies a pediatric toxicity margin or a WWOX-specific effect. [Journal abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15869952/) · [Manthey dissertation, printed pp. 29–32, 40–42 and 45, §§2.3, 3.1–3.2 and Table 5](https://d-nb.info/1023696576/34) · [Thesis DOI](https://doi.org/10.17169/refubium-12315)

The held WWOX organoid comparison found less cleaved caspase-3 staining in the ventricular zone, which concerns developing progenitors and a marker at one stage. It cannot predict the direction of a drug-induced loss of postmitotic neurons. Pair early firing measurements with follow-up counts of viable, identified neurons and a maturation readout in the same age-matched preparations. At matched initial activity and measured free exposure, a network effect that precedes any viability loss and preserves evoked output and ATP recovery supports functional modulation. A later fall in activity that tracks loss of viable neurons or maturation supports a developmental cost as an alternative explanation. An independent pH-matched control helps separate general acid–base stress from a sultiame-specific effect. ## Decision

Park sultiame for an unselected pediatric WWOX trial and retain it as a research comparator for bicarbonate–Kv7, sodium-current and late GABA mechanisms. The Ospolot 20 mg/mL suspension labeling reviewed is for Rolandic epilepsy and identifies suitability from 12 kg. The small short West syndrome trial provides an infant research precedent with pyridoxine background. It does not establish chronic developmental tolerability. Systemic acid–base effects and changes in background drug exposure are directly relevant to interpreting any trial signal. [UK product information §§4.1–4.5](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100433/smpc) · [Infant trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14738417/) · [Pediatric EEG and comedication comparisons](sodium_current_checkpoint_20261126.md)

Reopen only if a measured, plausible free CNS exposure produces a selective within-stage WWOX functional effect with preserved evoked inhibition, viable mature neurons and metabolic recovery. Recovering the old sodium recording buffer would refine the mechanistic interpretation. Alone it would leave the decisive exposure and WWOX data gaps open, so an independent review of that source is unlikely to change the current trial disposition.
