# Edaravone opening checkpoint in WWOX loss of function

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · `context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`

**Question.** Does a measured, potentially attainable free concentration of edaravone limit native neural phospholipid oxidation and preserve maturation or patterned recovery in WWOX-deficient tissue? A short-latency chain-breaking effect during oxidative stress would justify a different comparison from selenium or glutathione repletion. The [held selenium work](../sodium_selenite/README.md) frames upstream GPX4 support, and the [ergothioneine comparison](../l_ergothioneine/plasma_and_development_20261126.md) already identifies an edaravone comparator that reduced an oxidation-sensitive readout without reproducing the lineage-marker shift. The latter result makes absolute lineage counts and durable function decisive. The [ubiquinol comparison](../ubiquinol/decision.md) already addresses the quinone pool and respiration. The distinct edaravone test fixes exposure timing and tests a prompt lipid effect with unchanged GSH, GPX4 activity and ATP before the first functional readout. Held mouse and human RNA show competing directions for peroxide-reducing capacity and the susceptible lipid pool, which makes a native lipid measurement necessary.

**Fixed quantitative comparison.** Reconstruct edaravone parent exposure by route and matrix from the human pharmacokinetic studies and direct CSF regulatory tabulations. Establish species and sampling times before comparing CSF with human plasma. Convert total and free plasma estimates separately and retain estimates of protein binding and explicit absence of human neural concentrations where applicable. Compare these with original dose–response data in mouse stem-cell-derived motor neurons and, if source data are sufficient, a second neuronal or oligodendroglial model. Check compound timing, substrate culture conditions, and whether a known rescue condition altered cell number, dye chemistry or lipid peroxide abundance by orthogonal measures. Identify the tested developmental stage, native versus induced stress, and denominators. An ALS phenotype or response to RSL3 does not establish a developmental WWOX mechanism. **Discriminating prediction.** At the same developmental stage and free neural exposure, an early drop in oxidized PUFA phospholipids in WWOX-deficient cells without a preceding rise in GSH or GPX4 activity would support suppression of lipid propagation downstream of enzyme capacity. Fix cell composition and quantify absolute surviving cells and patterned response after washout. An acute effect restricted to a probe signal would require a cell-free probe control and a chemically specific measurement. Rescue restricted to a provoked death model would support a positive-control role. Native lipid rescue with preserved absolute mature-cell output would justify a first-sheath or evoked conduction comparison. Use an orthogonal radical-trapping control and a distinct GSH/GPX4-support condition. Keep genetic sensitivity distinct from an RSL3 result because the attribution of drug effects to a single target depends on the assay.
