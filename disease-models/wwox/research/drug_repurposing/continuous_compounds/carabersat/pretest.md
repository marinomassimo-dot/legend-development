# Carabersat, stereoselective membrane binding and recurrent circuit activity

**Checkpoint #202, 28 November 2026.** Opened after terazosin #201 closed on `development/main`. Carabersat (SB-204269) and its opposite stereoisomer SB-204268 provide an antiseizure comparator distinct from the remacemide NMDA and NaV question. Start from the original membrane-binding and hippocampal afterdischarge reports. The related benzopyran tonabersat is only an adjacent control for interpreting state and target labels, not the active candidate.

**Question.** Does binding to the carabersat-sensitive membrane site predict suppression of long recurrent WWOX activity with first excitatory and inhibitory recruitment preserved? An opposing explanation is that the apparent separation from intrinsic excitability depends on differences in free drug, compound stereodisposition or the high-potassium induction protocol. A second possibility is that the binding site is a marker without a causal relation to the electrical response.

**Predeclared contrast.** First align stereochemical identity, membrane affinity, local free exposure, duration metric and evoked responses. Compare active and opposite enantiomers within the same lab and across independent studies without combining binding and current readouts as target occupancy. Look for a joint relation across more than one stereochemical or structural pair, including whether the reference compounds exclude known-channel mechanisms under comparable conditions. The WWOX transfer should compare isolated first recruitment, spontaneous late burst burden and duration at native potassium and magnesium and verified equal exposure. A residual stereoselective effect after matching recruitment and a consistent association with site-specific displacement would favour the membrane-site hypothesis. Retained binding without functional response, equal suppression after matching exposure, or loss of the contrast without elevated potassium would lower priority. Check whether any effect is merely a reduction in depolarizing drive or inhibitory reserve.

**Stopping rule.** Keep the comparison conditional if molecular identity, human pharmacokinetics or free juvenile brain levels are unavailable. A phase label or adult endpoint does not establish a pediatric window. Do not infer potassium-channel activation or connexin action from class, structure, or vendor labels. Next step is to extract original binding and afterdischarge denominators, concentrations, limits and analogue dispositions side by side.

**Primary starting points.** [Herdon et al., membrane binding](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.bjp.0701331) · [Upton et al., evoked afterdischarge and seizure models](https://doi.org/10.1038/sj.bjp.0701330) · [Separate voltage-current study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10471213/).
