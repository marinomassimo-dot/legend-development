# Triacetin and the supply of acetate for myelin

**Opening question.** Neuronal Wwox deletion can impair oligodendrocyte maturation, sheath formation and axonal conduction. Does adding acetate via glyceryl triacetate (triacetin) bypass a shortage of neuronal carbon for myelin, or does impaired maturation persist with adequate substrate? A myelin phenotype alone cannot identify acetate shortage. Previous work in this programme has already shown how a change in mature-cell markers can diverge from conduction. [Primary WWOX neuronal-deletion study](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awab174) · [Laboratory conduction comparison](../clemastine/README.md)

**Prediction before RNA comparison.** A bypassable shortage predicts a reduction in neuron-to-glia N-acetylaspartate supply or utilization with sufficient oligodendroglial capacity to activate free acetate. Prespecify NAT8L (neuronal synthesis), ASPA (hydrolysis), ACSS2 (cytosolic acetate activation), ACSS1 (mitochondrial comparator) and MBP (lineage/maturation control). If their RNA signal simply tracks the abundance of maturing glia, bulk data cannot distinguish a substrate defect. In a neural co-culture, distinguish acetyl-labelled triacetin from labelled glycerol backbone, equimolar acetate and glycerol controls, with matched vehicle, pH, maturity and axon number. An increase in acetate or tracer incorporation without a sustained increase in ensheathment and conduction would lower priority.

**Scope.** This is a nutritional acetate donor with a small pediatric research precedent in Canavan disease. The conditions have different genetic lesions. The pretreatment WWOX phenotype, regional exposure, functional acetate use, pediatric tolerability and efficacy require separate comparisons. [Canavan safety study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21474353/) · [Earlier pediatric and animal investigation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19685155/)

## First WWOX comparison

In the WWOX mouse study, wild-type oligodendrocyte precursors developed differently when paired with Wwox-null dorsal-root-ganglion neurons (Fig. 6A–D, two independent experiments). This supports investigating a neuronal contribution to glial maturation. Acetate, N-acetylaspartate (NAA) and lipid flux were not assayed in that comparison. [Repudi et al., Fig. 6](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awab174)

| KO minus control mean log2(1 + expression) | NAT8L | ASPA | ACSS2 | ACSS1 | MBP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Mouse cortex, 4 control and 3 KO libraries | −0.81 | −0.91 | −0.62 | −0.77 | −1.17 |
| Mouse hippocampus, 2 control and 3 KO | −1.28 | −1.40 | −0.79 | −0.91 | −2.24 |
| Human cerebral organoids, 2 control and 4 KO | −0.01 | +0.09 | +0.02 | +0.38 | +0.49 |

The direction of all five mouse differences survives omission of each library in turn. Human NAT8L and ACSS2 differences straddle zero with omissions; mean ASPA and MBP are low in both human groups. Species, maturation and cellular composition differ. Expression is TPM in mouse tissue and library-normalized CPM in organoids. Omissions describe sensitivity and do not supply a population-level confidence interval. [Full contrasts and omission ranges](bulk.tsv)

In the mouse hippocampal nuclei pools, both the mix of cell classes and changes within assigned classes contributed to lower ASPA. Of a −28.52 CPM pooled difference, −8.58 CPM came from changing class weights and −19.94 from within-class rates. Those weights are fractions of captured RNA. Depth-standardized transcript detection adds a qualification. At four bins per doubling of capture depth, ASPA was detected in an estimated 62% versus 35% of assigned myelin-forming oligodendrocytes (control versus KO), while ACSS2 was detected in 37% versus 35%. MBP detection fell from 94% to 76%. In the mature class, both ASPA and MBP detection fell further. This leaves an experimental opening for bypassing hydrolysis with a free acetate source in some cells, alongside a competing maturation explanation. It cannot establish spare enzymatic capacity. The datasets supply one nuclei pool per arm, and neither nuclei nor depth bins are biological replicates. [Cell counts](cell_types.tsv) · [Composition decomposition](mixture.tsv) · [Three capture-depth resolutions](depth.tsv)

**Counterexample.** Constitutive loss of NAA synthesis allowed myelination in one murine Canavan model despite removal of that source of acetate. This challenges an obligatory dependence on NAA-derived acetate across developmental conditions. The small exploratory Canavan studies support limited pediatric tolerability experience, with no motor improvement in the reported higher-exposure study. Their observations cannot establish developmental efficacy, WWOX safety or appropriate CNS exposure. The present Canavan comparison uses primary abstracts, pending a check of the underlying functional and exposure measurements. [Genetic counterexample](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25712859/) · [Safety study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21474353/)

**Next decision.** Keep triacetin active for one exposure-and-flux comparison. An initial mouse study reports a 17-fold rise in brain acetate 1–2 hours after triacetin in 20–21-day-old animals. That bulk measurement leaves the relevant cell and metabolic destination unresolved. Compare it with the [tremor-rat lipid and motor study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2877317/), keeping age, lipid denominator, treatment duration and functional endpoint explicit. Look for an actual tracer or exposure link between the two experiments. In WWOX, stable-isotope enrichment alone would also be compatible with substitution of carbon sources at unchanged lipid synthesis. The discriminating outcome is increased absolute synthesis and functional ensheathment at matched cell maturity, with stable axonal conduction or recovery of an established conduction defect. Prioritize a pharmacological comparison only if there is evidence that flux is limited and can be restored. [Starting acetate study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16002461/)
