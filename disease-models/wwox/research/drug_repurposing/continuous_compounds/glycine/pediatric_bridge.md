# Route, analyte and compartment in the glycine exposure bridge

The inclusion criterion for the decision was a measured pediatric post-oral glycine concentration in CSF, with route, sampling time and an interpretable baseline or comparator. A focused search of indexed primary records found a pediatric oral precedent for conjugation in isovaleric acidemia, a pediatric metabolic loading report with a plasma response, and an independent human IV-to-CSF experiment. None of the consulted summaries supplies all the required fields. This is a bounded indexed-record screen; the full primary tables and timing have not been adjudicated and a missing field is unknown.

Interpretation for WWOX: a physiological route from blood to CSF is supported in humans under intravenous administration. A lumbar puncture on the first test day in the four-day study makes the CSF comparison across assigned conditions, not repeated CSF doses within each individual. Oral adult MRS measures a tissue ratio, and a pediatric plasma curve with impaired glycine clearance cannot calibrate synaptic free concentration. Neither an established pediatric exposure margin nor a quantitative conversion to the 200 µM ex vivo condition follows from these records.

Decision: keep oral glycine as a mechanistic probe. Determine whether the genotype-specific late response occurs across measured low extracellular concentrations, as well as under the published conditioning exposure. A selective response only at the high conditioning exposure would increase the need for a route- and compartment-specific bridge. No pediatric administration decision follows.
