# Glycine and activity-dependent NMDA receptor trafficking in WWOX loss

**Selection before the WWOX expression check.** The L-serine dossier already considered co-agonist production and membrane lipid supply. The pH dossier already identified the GluN1 N1 cassette as a modifier of proton sensitivity. Here the intervention is glycine itself. The new question is whether a single exposure could facilitate the first response to glutamate yet weaken a later response through removal of surface NMDA receptors. A change in the balance between excitatory neurons and interneurons could reverse the network consequence. Neither nutritional status nor a fall in the second response establishes benefit. [Existing L-serine comparison](../l_serine/README.md) · [Existing pH comparison](../../nmda_asic_ph_sci45_20261111/README.md) · [Prior splicing study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8271567/) · [Competing inactivation study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5561918/)

**Predictions fixed before the check.** Compare an initial response, surface GluN1 and a later response after matched stimulation and washout. A surface decline with a persisting current decrease supports trafficking. A current decrease with surface receptors preserved leaves inactivation as a competitor. Greater recruitment of inhibitory neurons or an isolated glycine-receptor chloride current can change the net spike response independently. Hold initial activation and free extracellular co-agonist availability comparable, measure both neuronal classes and compare WWOX with its isogenic control. A response common to both genotypes would establish a general circuit effect. For a genotype-dependent prediction establish whether transcripts including the 63-nucleotide N1 cassette can be distinguished from skipping transcripts; gene totals alone cannot answer this.

**First comparison.** Check GRIN1, its receptor partners and glycine uptake and clearance genes in the archived WWOX expression cohorts. Interpret regional signs and leave-one-library sensitivity. This tests whether the proposed system is detectable and whether a shared direction of transcript change exists. Check transcript identity against the archived annotation before estimating an exon-inclusion fraction.

**What changed in WWOX.** SLC6A9/GlyT1 RNA decreased in all three cohorts. KO minus control mean log2(1 + expression) was −0.93 in cortex, −0.42 in hippocampus and −0.20 in organoids, retaining direction on single-library omission. Lower transporter RNA does not give the direction or magnitude of extracellular glycine. Total GRIN1 rose in cortex and fell in organoids; the hippocampal sign was unstable. Run `python3 expression.py FILES output.tsv`. [Full expression panel](expression.tsv)

**Direct check of the relevant cassette.** The archived Ensembl v92 cDNAs contain eight coding Grin1 transcripts identifiable by both flanking sequences and the presence or absence of the N1 peptide. Four include and four skip it. The six other annotated transcripts lack a classifiable protein junction. For each library separately, divide included coding TPM by total classified coding TPM, then compare genotype means. In neuronal-deletion hippocampus this estimate rises from 22.1% to 32.2% (+10.1 percentage points, single-library omissions +8.4 to +11.9). Progenitor-deletion and constitutive hippocampus show +9.1 and +3.8 points respectively. Neuronal-deletion cortex shows +3.6 points but one omission changes its sign. Only 26–49% of total Grin1 TPM belongs to the classified coding forms in the least-covered library of each contrast. These are model-based transcript allocations, with two to four libraries per arm. Cell composition and assignment among similar cDNAs remain plausible explanations. The gene-level human matrix offers no independent junction estimate. **Implication to challenge.** If the hippocampal change persists within the same neuronal class and translates into synaptic receptors, greater inclusion could *weaken* the delayed surface loss after glycine even while initial co-agonism persists. A bulk change arising from the proportion of neural cell types gives a different prediction within matched cells. Next quantify how much independent junction evidence the underlying reads can provide, test the direction against cell composition and check primary cell-class electrophysiology at matched exposure. Pediatric trial priority remains open. Dietary availability does not establish a safe or effective CNS exposure for this hypothesis.

**Human exposure checkpoint.** After oral supplementation 10/11 healthy adult men had a higher tissue glycine/creatine signal. The direction survives omission of one increase. The measurement supplies neither a synaptic concentration nor a pediatric exposure estimate. **Can the hippocampal signal change sign?** In neuronal-deletion hippocampus the mean TPM of coding transcripts including exon 5 rises from 33.3 to 40.5, while skipping transcripts fall from 116.8 to 85.6. For the broader RNA denominator, treating all six unresolved transcripts identically gives about +5.6 points whether all include or all skip. A stronger sensitivity check holds each transcript’s assignment fixed across genotypes but allows assignments to differ between transcripts. The possible contrast now spans −8.17 to +19.38 points. The all-RNA direction therefore depends on which transcripts carry the junction. This is a deterministic identification interval, not a confidence interval. Unclassified processed RNAs need not yield receptor protein, and TPM estimates are held fixed in this analysis. Keep the +10.14-point result explicitly restricted to classified coding RNA. The independent organoid junction count addresses a different species and tissue. [Sensitivity by genotype and region](allocation.tsv) · [Reproduce with allocation.py FILES](allocation.py)

**What the unresolved RNA actually covers.** Three independent diagnostic 31-base windows around the aligned insertion boundaries recover all eight coding classifications. None occurs in the six processed cDNAs. In an exact-block alignment to an inclusion reference (minimum block 31 bases), four of the six have matching blocks only downstream of the cassette, two only upstream. Two downstream transcripts match over their entire lengths (527 and 456 bases). These archived sequences provide no direct evidence for either junction. Assigning them a latent splicing state, as in the sensitivity interval above, is a mathematical stress test; their TPMs are not six additional observations of receptor splice choice. The biological question remains the proportion among junction-bearing molecules and its transfer to functional receptors. For the single verified control, 1/12 informative reads support inclusion (8.3%). Inverting the two binomial tails at 0.025 gives an exact technical 95% interval of 0.21–38.48%, conditional on independent informative reads and equal capture of the two junctions. **Can a different mix of cells explain the apparent splice change?** In GSE158684, inhibitory neurons account for 8.0% of the control pool's Grin1 molecules and 10.3% of the KO pool's. If all inhibitory neurons included exon 5 and all remaining nuclei skipped it, the difference attributable to that redistribution would be about +2.3 percentage points. Allowing any fixed inclusion fraction in each of ten broad classes, the largest magnitude obtainable by changing only their measured weights is 5.59 points. This is a bound within these two nuclear pools, conditional on stable class-specific fractions. The bulk neuronal-deletion hippocampal coding estimate is +10.14 points. The two measurements use different populations and RNA denominators; subtracting the bound from the bulk estimate would not identify a cell-autonomous effect. Subtype mixtures and nuclear versus tissue sampling remain open. **Technical precision at the human coverage threshold.** If the true inclusion fractions were 22.09% and 32.23%, with 20 independent informative reads in each of two control and four KO libraries, exact enumeration gives a 12.9% probability of a zero or negative observed contrast. This conditional calculation excludes biological heterogeneity and capture differences. It explains the limits of the threshold without changing it after observing the counts. **The transporter contrast depends on the sampled cells.** Omitting myelin-forming oligodendrocytes reverses the assigned-nuclei Slc6a9 difference from −0.51 to +0.12 CPM. Omitting astrocytes gives −0.51. One pooled sample per genotype prevents a biological variance estimate. Measure the extracellular glycine recovery time course after matched activation. **Mixed splice forms change the interpretation of bulk RNA.** The expected receptor fractions depend on pairing and on whether one or two included subunits confer resistance to glycine priming. Recombinant mixed receptors are already reported. The discriminating endpoint is delayed surface loss after exposure-matched activation, with verification that the expression system preserves trafficking in uniform controls. [Pairing sensitivity and existing comparator](receptor_pairing.md)

**What could change the decision.** At the same added glycine concentration, a mouse GlyT1 perturbation reversed the current response. A preceding high exposure also changed a subsequent response in WT slices. The WWOX comparison therefore needs separate exposure-naïve preparations, a deliberate repeat-challenge arm, and time-resolved extracellular glycine alongside surface receptors. The animal comparators lack a WWOX allele. The available pediatric route and compartment records do not quantify oral-to-synaptic exposure.
