# Does the hippocampal transporter signal localize to astrocytes?

GSE158684 has one nuclear pool per genotype. Across nuclei assigned to nine broad classes, Slc6a9 declines from 7.810 to 7.297 CPM. Symmetric decomposition by each class's share of total assigned molecular counts gives −0.687 CPM from changing class weights and +0.173 CPM from changing expression within classes. These two components reconstruct the −0.513 CPM net change apart from rounding. Weights here represent RNA contribution rather than headcounts. Sparse detection, class assignment and pooled sampling preclude estimation of a reproducible within-cell effect.

Astrocytes contain only 20 detected Slc6a9 molecules in each pool, spread over 16/417 control nuclei and 20/479 KO nuclei. Astrocyte Gldc falls from 144.4 to 79.0 CPM while Amt rises from 17.1 to 20.3 CPM. The data cannot establish glycine transporter activity or the direction of cleavage flux. They weaken the premise that an enduring decrease of transporter expression inside astrocytes explains the bulk signal. This is a separate nuclear assay and cannot decompose the tissue-level RNA result itself.

Decision: characterize extracellular glycine during and after a matched stimulation in WWOX and control preparations before attributing a co-agonist shortage to GlyT1. Lower uptake could instead prolong extracellular availability. The discriminating observation is the joint time course of extracellular glycine, initial NMDA response and later receptor surface loss.

Run transport_cells.py with the repository and its files directory as arguments. Run transport_components.py transport_cells.tsv for the algebraic decomposition. Input source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158684

Omitting each assigned class and recomputing both RNA denominators reversed the Slc6a9 contrast only when myelin-forming oligodendrocytes (MFOL) were omitted: −0.5135 CPM with all classes, +0.1218 without MFOL, −0.5085 without astrocytes. MFOL contributed 27 Slc6a9 molecules in 186 control nuclei and 11 in 49 KO nuclei; their class-normalized CPM rose from 37.698 to 49.188. This sensitivity identifies the sampled class driving the sign, but cannot distinguish tissue cell loss from differences in recovery or assignment.
