# A transporter perturbation reverses the response at fixed bath glycine

In CA1 pyramidal neurons from acute mouse hippocampal slices, the authors report potentiation of evoked NMDA currents in WT and inhibition in GlyT1 heterozygotes at 250 µM added glycine. At 10 µM and 1 mM they report no significant between-genotype difference. Dynasore abolished the intermediate-concentration depression in heterozygotes. These are electrophysiological endpoints from Fig. 1E–G. The same slice comparison does not directly count surface receptors or measure the extracellular glycine waveform. Only Fig. 1 caption and selected Results paragraphs were read. [Primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8689229/)

**Competing WWOX predictions.** Reduced uptake could shift depression toward a lower *added* glycine concentration, whereas increased inclusion of GluN1 exon 5 could attenuate priming at the *same local exposure*. Either could mask the other in bulk network firing. Compare concentration-response curves within the same neuronal class first against added glycine and then against measured extracellular glycine time course under matched activation. A genotype difference that resolves after local exposure matching favors transport or availability. A residual difference linked to within-class splice composition and delayed surface loss supports receptor-level susceptibility, with subunit partners and downstream trafficking remaining alternatives. Dynamics of local glycine at a synapse may differ from measured bath or bulk extracellular levels. Whole-tissue Slc6a9 RNA cannot be substituted for measured clearance. The 250 µM observation is a positive mechanistic comparator in a different genotype; it sets no WWOX exposure target.
