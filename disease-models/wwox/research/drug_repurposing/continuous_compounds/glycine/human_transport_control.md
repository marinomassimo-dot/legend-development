# A human genetic comparator for the direction of the glycine exposure signal

Kurolap et al. reported two individuals with elevated CSF glycine and normal serum among four affected individuals from two families with truncating SLC6A9 variants. Family 1's proband had CSF glycine of 22 µmol/L against the paper's upper reference of 8 µmol/L and a CSF:serum ratio of 0.08. Neither the two measurements nor the four cases estimate the prevalence of this biochemical pattern in WWOX. In the independent pharmacological arm of the same study, wild-type mouse CSF median was 29 µM; 12 hours after GlyT1 inhibition the median was 68.5 µM (39.5 µM absolute difference, 2.36-fold ratio of group medians). A ratio of medians is not a within-animal fold change. The two species' concentrations and reference intervals cannot be pooled. [Primary study, proband narrative and Fig. 3B–C](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5097939/)

Decision impact: loss of transport can raise central glycine despite normal circulating glycine. Thus normal plasma or a bulk SLC6A9 transcript decrease cannot justify assuming CNS glycine shortage in WWOX. This comparator diagnoses neither impaired transport nor central excess in WWOX. In the experimental model, determine extracellular concentration and decay after matched release before interpreting supplementation. This comparison concerns untreated transporter deficiency and pharmacological transporter inhibition. It does not measure pediatric oral glycine exposure.
