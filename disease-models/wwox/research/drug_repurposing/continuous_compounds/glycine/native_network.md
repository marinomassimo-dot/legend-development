# Acute network response as an alternative to delayed NMDAR trafficking

Two indexed primary studies of immature rat hippocampus identify an important alternative for WWOX. In neonatal rat CA3 (P0–6), 2 µM added glycine reduced the frequency of spontaneous field giant depolarizing potentials. A separate P4–7 rat preparation treated with 4-aminopyridine and low magnesium showed more recurrent epileptiform activity with 10 µM glycine. Age partly overlaps, but concentration, bath composition, activity state, tissue preparation and endpoints differ. These observations cannot locate a threshold between 2 and 10 µM or show exposure feasibility in children. Both are comparisons of acute network physiology in rats without WWOX loss. Source coverage: indexed abstracts, no whole-paper receipt or quantitative magnitude assigned.

Held WWOX panel: the GLRA2 contrast in mean log2(1+expression) is +0.776 in human organoids (single-library omissions +0.281 to +1.270), −0.290 in P17 neuronal-deletion hippocampus (−0.451 to −0.129), and +0.183 in cortex with unstable sign. GLRB is also lower in hippocampus, whereas its human sign changes with omission. The existing expression table supports neither a uniform receptor shift nor a functional glycine conductance. Native intracellular chloride and cell-class recruitment remain unknown.

Discriminating observation: compare spontaneous and high-activity epochs within the same developmental preparation and genotype at matched measured glycine, keeping the ionic composition fixed or separating any magnesium change as its own control. Record cell-attached spikes and chloride-preserving glycine receptor conductance, alongside surface NMDAR and delayed current. An immediate change in bursting tracking GlyR conductance, with unchanged later surface receptors, would favor the acute alternative. Greater delayed surface loss after matched initial recruitment would favor trafficking. Acute effects at low micromolar concentration in comparator tissue make safety direction worth testing before promoting supplementation. They do not establish a WWOX dose, benefit or harm.

**Within-study concentration reversal.** Chen et al. also report opposite effects under one induction paradigm. At 10 µM added glycine, recurrent discharges became more frequent. At 100 µM they occurred at 65 ± 8.3% of the control rate (n=11, sign-test P=0.0117, Fig. 6A,D), while spike frequency and spike number within a discharge were unchanged. The paper's stronger receptor-blocker attribution at high concentration includes taurine experiments and should not be transferred wholesale to glycine. Indexed primary Results passage and abstract checked; no per-preparation values or paired concentration series were recovered. This adds an exposure-response constraint: a lower total event count can coexist with preserved within-event firing, and the same induced network can show an increase at a lower tested concentration. Freeze both event rate and within-event burden as separate outcomes and keep measured-concentration arms exposure-naïve. [Chen et al. Fig. 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4227900/)
