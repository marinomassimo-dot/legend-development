# Myo-inositol, phosphoinositide resynthesis and neuronal M-current

**Prediction recorded before the WWOX comparison.** The relevant substrate is myo-inositol. A disease-specific benefit would require evidence that neuronal uptake or recycling limits phosphatidylinositol-4,5-bisphosphate (PIP2) resynthesis after activity and thereby limits recovery of KCNQ-mediated M-current. If so, at matched extracellular osmolarity and membrane voltage, supplemental substrate should increase labeled lipid production and shorten the recovery of both membrane PIP2 and M-current preferentially in WWOX neurons. A change in the free osmolyte pool, glial abundance or cell volume could instead change total tissue myo-inositol without recovering neuronal M-current. Increased PIP2 could also strengthen PLC-dependent calcium release and increase excitation. Record calcium transients, evoked inhibitory recruitment and pathological firing separately.

**Existing versus new.** The held lithium audit considered myo-inositol add-back to dissect a drug effect in a provoked seizure assay. The D-BHB dossier examined a direct channel ligand. Here the independent question is whether substrate availability limits recovery of signaling lipid and current after depletion. The primary evidence for the molecular link uses cultured cells and superior cervical ganglion neurons; it gives no WWOX-specific response and should not be pooled as a single cortical experiment. [Existing lithium comparison](../../qualification_it21_p3b_20261103/README.md) · [D-BHB comparison](../d_bhb/README.md) · [Primary transporter study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27217553/)

**Pediatric feasibility.** In a randomized trial of 638 infants born before 28 weeks, all-cause mortality before 55 weeks postmenstrual age was 18% on myo-inositol and 11% on placebo (adjusted risk ratio 1.66, 95% CI 1.14–2.43). The trial stopped early. Gestational age, route and regimen limit transfer to children with WWOX; this is a serious safety signal for a common assumption that a nutritional compound is readily transferable. In the follow-up from the same trial, the composite of death or moderate/severe neurodevelopmental impairment had a different denominator and time horizon. Pediatric trial priority is unsupported at entry; this is a mechanistic screen. [Trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30357297/) · [Follow-up of the same cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33758387/)

**First comparison.** Examine uptake (SLC5A3), synthesis (ISYNA1), recycling (IMPA1), phosphatidylinositol synthesis (CDIPT) and channel transcripts (KCNQ2/3). Test whether a uniform delivery or recycling bottleneck transfers between untreated WWOX bulk cortex, organoids and hippocampus, and compare the marker-assigned excitatory and inhibitory nuclear pools. RNA sign neither establishes nor excludes lipid flux. An isolated transporter change is insufficient for a trial rationale.

**WWOX results.** ISYNA1, IMPA1, CDIPT, KCNQ2 and KCNQ3 all had lower RNA in all three bulk datasets, with the negative sign retained on every single-library omission. For ISYNA1 the KO-minus-control difference in mean log2(1 + normalized expression) was −0.83 in cortex (4 control / 3 KO), −0.22 in organoids (2 / 4) and −2.08 in hippocampus (2 / 3). The transporter SLC5A3 fell in both mouse tissues but rose in human organoids. In a separate marker-assigned juvenile hippocampal nuclear dataset (GSE158684, one pool per genotype), excitatory Isyna1 fell from 11.4 to 8.1 CPM and astrocyte-enriched Isyna1 rose from 6.8 to 11.6. Both Kcnq2 and Kcnq3 increased per million nuclear transcripts in both excitatory and inhibitory groups. Slc5a3 had a feature entry but zero molecules in the entire deposited filtered matrix in each arm (3,182 and 2,920 barcodes). This makes the cell-specific transporter comparison uninformative. Whole tissue and nuclear RNA differ in capture, class composition and denominator; models and tissues are not independent biological replications. Neither the nuclear increase nor the bulk decrease measures surface channel protein or myo-inositol limitation. **Alternative that changes the experiment.** Manville et al. found that SMIT1 associates with the KCNQ2 pore and changes KCNQ2/3 gating even when no substrate is applied extracellularly. Consequently, a current difference between SMIT1-expressing and control cells is insufficient to attribute any change to supplemental myo-inositol. The discriminating comparison holds transporter abundance and osmolarity fixed, varies substrate, and measures lipid resynthesis and current recovery after the same transient lipid depletion. A voltage-dependence shift with unchanged lipid resynthesis would support a channel-level explanation; uptake without improved PIP2 recovery would weaken the substrate hypothesis. Next compare the substrate-only and transporter-only arms of the original experiments, with their actual starting medium and exposure time, then examine existing WWOX functional or metabolite readouts. [Manville et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28793216/)

**A human exposure comparator changes interpretation.** Vos et al. followed one child with a homozygous PLCB1 deletion receiving enteral myo-inositol alongside several antiseizure treatments. The four treated CSF measurements in Table S2 (ages 1, 8, 9 and 9 years) span 260.4–300.3 μM. The main text reports the control mean as 188.2 μM. Recalculation from all 18 control observations yields 118.25 μM (sample SD 38.39, range 47.7–183.8), consistent with the supplement's 118.2 and inconsistent with the main-text mean. The treated mean is 281.4 μM, about 2.38 times the recalculated reference mean, versus 1.50 times the prose mean. These are repeated observations from one treated individual compared to a separate age-heterogeneous reference group, not a fold change from that child's untreated CSF. Table S1 also gives a control plasma summary of 31.9 ± 18.9 μM; its 24 printed values yield 27.93 ± 11.58. Dates sufficient for a paired plasma-to-CSF ratio are unavailable. The comparison establishes that higher CSF values occurred during treatment, with no concomitant measurement of synaptic inositol, PIP2 recovery or KCNQ activity. The PLCB1 lesion differs from WWOX; concomitant therapy, lack of a control and changing seizure burden limit clinical transfer. The single case cannot outweigh the randomized premature-infant harm signal.
