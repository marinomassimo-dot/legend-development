**Question.** At comparable fast AMPA-current inhibition, does tezampanel reveal an additional kainate-receptor contribution to release and recurrent activity in a native juvenile Wwox-LoF preparation? The first requirement is a direct subtype-resolved concentration-response comparison. Record species, subunit and edit or splice configuration, agonist conditions, preparation, ligand and endpoint. Use within-assay potency ratios only where the endpoints and agonist conditions support them. Establish feasible local parent exposure before treating any nominal concentration as transferable.

**Orthogonal comparison.** Define fast postsynaptic AMPA loss as the matched condition. Record the first pre- and postsynaptic responses, direct glutamate output at comparable input or recruitment and the late recurrent response. Analyze the hippocampal kainate component and possible presynaptic GluK1 separately from the neocortical Wwox-null burst phenotype. Follow up an incremental effect with an independent GluK1-selective reference or a validated GluK1 perturbation. Measure the direction of the release change. A GluK1-dependent brake, if active before drug, predicts that removing it increases release even if fast postsynaptic current is smaller. A fall in synaptic event count alone is uninterpretable for release.

**Decision rule.** Retain as a conditional separation probe only if reproducible subtype evidence and exposure leave room for a kainate component after matching AMPA engagement. An incremental, GluK1-dependent release phenotype that covaries with the late response supports the presynaptic explanation. Equal response after AMPA matching, changed first recruitment, or comparable engagement across kainate subtype alternatives without a discriminating control lowers its priority as a separation probe. Receptor results do not establish disease specificity, CNS exposure or pediatric experience.
