# Tezampanel and the limits of AMPA-matched kainate separation

28 November 2026 UTC. Continuous compound #204, INV-05. context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. [Prespecified comparison](pretest.md) · [Opening evidence](first_comparison.md) · [Numerical replay](comparison.tsv).

**Disposition.** Retain tezampanel (LY293558) as a mixed AMPA/GluK1 comparator in calibrated preclinical assays. Defer pediatric WWOX priority and any claim that it supplies a clean GluK1-blocking window. Recombinant GluK1-containing receptors were more sensitive than the GluK2 homomer and the GluK2/GluK5 construct. The apparent margin against GluA2-flip fell from about 30-fold in the fixed-glutamate assay to 3-fold using the authors' agonist correction. Native NMDA current inhibition is an additional alternative in WWOX cortex. Juvenile regional unbound parent remains unknown.

## Where the apparent window comes from

Alt et al. 2006 Table 1 used glutamate-evoked calcium influx for recombinant human receptors in HEK293 cells (GluA3-flop was expressed in AV12). The default stimulus was 100 µM glutamate, with 1 µM for GluA4-flip. The table reproduces the kainate rows from Alt et al. 2004. These rows are shared evidence. The reported apparent Kb values correct the functional IC50 using the glutamate EC50 and the concentration in the assay. A calcium response EC50 is a functional quantity and the corrected value does not establish direct binding or a synaptic concentration-response. In the paper's legacy notation GLUK5 is modern GluK1, GLUK6 is GluK2, and GLUK2 is GluK5 (KA2). [Alt et al. 2006, pp. 242–244, Table 1 and Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00213-005-0292-0) · [Public author-posted table and methods](https://www.academia.edu/54656993/In_vitro_and_in_vivo_studies_in_rats_with_LY293558_suggest_AMPA_kainate_receptor_blockade_as_a_novel_potential_mechanism_for_the_therapeutic_treatment_of_anxiety_disorders) · [Alt et al. 2004](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15033339/) · [IUPHAR subunit nomenclature](https://www.guidetoimmunopharmacology.org/GRAC/FamilyDisplayForward?familyId=75)

The pre-existing GluA2 binding signal in the inventory is compatible with Sang et al. Table 1, which gives recombinant human radioligand Ki of 3.2 µM for GluA2, 4.2 µM for GluK1 and 32 µM for GluA3. This affinity table supports dual binding and does not establish native inhibition. O'Neill et al. independently used cloned GluA2 and GluK1 ligand binding with a separate cortical wedge functional endpoint. Full protocols and numerical results from O'Neill were not independently transcribed for this comparison. Alt's whole-cell Fig. 1 in primary cultured rat hippocampal pyramidal neurons gives **3 µM** for inhibition of the 30 µM AMPA-evoked current (95% CI 2–3 µM) and **13 µM** for inhibition of the 10 µM NMDA-evoked current (7–27 µM), with both agonist challenges selected as approximate EC80. This gives a distinct functional alternative when measuring recurrent network events. ### Reproducible stress test

With a reversible one-site Hill-1 scenario and equal maximal response, achieving 80% GluK1 inhibition while holding GluA2-flip inhibition at 20% or less requires an apparent potency ratio of at least **16**. The threshold is an illustrative assay criterion, without any biological validation. On the fixed-glutamate IC50 scale, 2.8 µM gives 80% modeled GluK1 and 11.8% GluA2-flip inhibition. If both receptors have equivalent fractional agonist challenge, the authors' apparent Kb ratio gives 57.1% GluA2-flip inhibition at 80% GluK1 inhibition. For the GluK1/GluK5 construct, even the raw ratio is only 6.5. The apparent margin depends on the agonist context and the GluK1-containing assembly. Slopes, subunit proportions, accessory proteins and glutamate pulse kinetics prevent treating the replay as a native response curve. [Inputs and replay](replay.py) · [Output](comparison.tsv)

### Parent exposure

A standalone pharmacokinetic arm in two-month-old male Sprague–Dawley rats measured parent by LC–MS/MS after intramuscular tezampanel. Whole-brain means were **318.7 ± 23.9 ng/g** at the first sample at 15 min (n=3) and **119.8 ± 76.1 ng/g** at 1 h (n=6); simultaneous plasma means were 18,435 and 1,758.3 ng/mL (n=6 at each time). The authors highlight potential residual vascular drug and use 10.3 µL plasma/g brain in their illustration. Subtracting plasma at that assumed volume yields about **129 ng/g** at 15 min and **102 ng/g** at 1 h. The converted totals on a 1 kg/L tissue-density scale are about **0.46 µM** and **0.36 µM**. The correction is a sensitivity scenario using group means and an external vascular-volume estimate. Neither whole-brain total nor the corrected estimate establishes unbound or receptor-site concentration. On that scale, reaching 0.2 µM at the early sample would require roughly 43% of the corrected total to be unbound. Regional distribution and native potency are unmeasured. Avoid transferring the ratio to human plasma or treating the earliest sample as the true peak. [Apland et al., PK arm only, Fig. 5, Table 2 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3533413/) · [Parent molecular mass](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/127894) · [Exposure sensitivity](comparison.tsv)

## One decision-changing comparison

**Held WWOX premise.** The processed P17 Wwox neuronal-deletion hippocampal series suggests a +17-percentage-point shift in the estimated Grik1 insert fraction (2 controls, 3 mutants). Combined library omission and adverse assignment of unclassified transcripts can reverse the sign slightly. Other regions and deletion models fail to reproduce the central estimate. The held native evidence for the presynaptic brake concerns the developing CA3-to-CA1 connection and defined age windows. These are different from mossy-fibre input to CA3. Neither the transcript estimate nor a native postsynaptic kainate EPSC establishes a functional GluK1c brake in WWOX. [Held isoform and physiology record](../../perampanel_kainate_20261123/README.md) · [Developmental primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22413061/)

**Experiment.** In age-matched Wwox neuronal-deletion and littermate CA3-to-CA1 preparations, first measure local parent concentration, incoming fibre volley and the first fast AMPA and NMDA currents. Match residual fast AMPA and NMDA current and initial recruitment between tezampanel and the reference conditions, using independently validated control agents in that preparation. Measure direct stimulus-aligned glutamate output, with a sensor-response control, together with early inhibition and late recurrent activity. Require the isolated release difference to fall away under an independent locally validated GluK1 perturbation. Establish splice dependence with an isoform-resolved test only if a GluK1-dependent effect is first reproduced. Treat recurrent cortical activity as a separate preparation with its own AMPA/NMDA controls.

- **Supports a persisting presynaptic brake.** After matching currents and recruitment, tezampanel increases CA3-to-CA1 glutamate output selectively in Wwox deletion, and the increase is occluded by an independent GluK1 perturbation. A decrease in release with the same dependency would favor a different GluK1 function in that preparation. A positive result needs age alignment and cannot assign the splice variant on its own.
- **Lowers the GluK1 interpretation.** The release difference disappears with current and recruitment matching, survives the GluK1 perturbation, or follows altered inhibitory recruitment. With no verified parent or GluK1 engagement, a null comparison gives no mechanistic decision.

## Pediatric feasibility and reopening

A small randomized acute IV migraine study had 44 evaluable adults, including 13 receiving tezampanel. At two hours its migraine response was 9/13 versus 4/16 with placebo. The adverse-event denominator was 2/13, with dizziness and drowsiness among the recorded symptoms. The discussion reports approximately one hour of adult plasma half-life, without a measured unbound CNS bridge. A separate completed adult acute-migraine registry uses subcutaneous tezampanel and an 18-to-65-year age range. The register records 306 enrolled and displayed no posted results when checked on 28 November 2026. Acute formulations, a pain endpoint and adult short-term exposure give no basis to infer chronic pediatric WWOX use, effects on development, or compatibility with background antiseizure therapy. No dose or clinical efficacy estimate transfers. [Sang et al., Results, Table 3 and Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2982.2004.00723.x) · [Adult SC migraine study](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00567086)

Reopen for measured, region- and age-matched unbound parent plus a native release effect independent of fast AMPA/NMDA suppression and first recruitment. An independent laboratory review would be decision-changing only if it supplies a relevant free-exposure measurement or a credible reason to reinterpret the subtype controls. The numerical checks and known gaps are recorded now.
