# A native-chloride counterexample to treating taurine as inhibitory

Sava et al. studied P2–P4 mouse cortex. In cell-attached recordings at 300 μM bath taurine, 20/35 putative pyramidal neurons met their criterion for increased firing (57.1%); 17/30 identified GABAergic interneurons increased firing (56.7%). The unadjusted difference is 0.48 percentage points, derived from separate cells; neither equivalence nor population prevalence follows. Firing decreased in three of 15 pyramidal cells with enough baseline spikes to evaluate suppression. That subgroup cannot supply an overall suppression fraction: low baseline activity makes inhibition difficult to detect. The text also says that 28 of 35 had spontaneous spikes and labels the remainder eight; 35−28=7. We retain the separately stated responder denominator of 35.

Low gabazine removed the firing increase in all 12 **previously responsive** pyramidal cells retested; the phasic current was suppressed while tonic current remained. This supports a network contribution, not a population-wide response or direct action on each pyramidal neuron. The current reversal of −56.7 mV came from whole-cell recordings with imposed chloride; it cannot substitute for the native reversal in the cell-attached cells. Neonatal control tissue and nominal 300 μM bath taurine leave WWOX, later developmental stages, and pediatric brain exposure unresolved.

**Derived prediction.** Two preparations with similar resting depolarization can have opposite firing responses depending on the fraction of cells already active and the direction of phasic GABA signaling. Measure changes in driven spike probability in initially silent cells as well as spontaneous firing in active cells, preserving endogenous chloride. Keep initial activity, cell type and maturation strata separate. Check whether network-mediated excitation persists in WWOX, reverses during maturation, or disappears with osmolarity-matched control. [Primary study, methods and results accompanying figures 7 and 8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3912439/)

**Prospective developmental alternative.** Test whether an intracellular transporter-dependent mechanism changes chloride extrusion and radial migration after extracellular taurine is washed away. Compare this with an acute receptor response that fades after washout, an osmotic response at matched osmolarity, and altered cell maturity or composition. The source lead for taurine, WNK signaling and KCC2 is [Inoue et al., PMID 22544747](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3375508/); use an independent [Wwox-dependent migration model](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6990504/) to establish which cell, stage and direction have been measured. A shared migration phenotype alone gives no direction of benefit. Those two sources await targeted verification for this comparison.
