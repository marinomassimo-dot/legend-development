# Taurine and the direction of chloride-dependent signaling

**Preregistered question.** In immature WWOX cortical circuits, does taurine reduce the probability of a spike in pyramidal neurons, recruit spikes through interneurons, or primarily change cell volume? The laboratory previously abstained on taurine as a redox supplement. The present comparison addresses chloride driving force and shunting in different cells. No taurine deficiency, native chloride gradient or supplement delivery to WWOX cortex has been established here. [Previous supplement screen](../../supplement_diet_it6_p11_20261029/README.md)

**Prediction before numerical source extraction.** At matched osmolarity and extracellular taurine, a receptor-mediated effect should depend on resting potential, native reversal potential and change in membrane conductance. If increasing conductance shifts the threshold-crossing probability in opposite directions between two cell types, extracellular or network recordings alone cannot assign the direction in the pyramidal neuron. A purely osmotic explanation predicts a similar volume change using a matched non-agonist osmolyte; volume, native chloride, conductance and spike probability should be measured separately. Stratify by maturation and whether baseline activity is matched. An artificially imposed intracellular chloride concentration or a conductance increase by itself will not identify the native sign.

**First sensitivity calculation.** For a toy passive membrane with fixed input and a conductance ratio r, the new steady voltage is (Vbefore + r × Ereversal)/(1+r). In the worked example Erest = −65 mV and the putative spike threshold is −50 mV. A chloride conductance reversing at −55 mV depolarizes a resting cell to −60 mV at r=1, yet the same conductance shifts a driven cell from −48 to −51.5 mV. This is a counterexample to identifying excitation from depolarization at rest. The model omits active channels, network feedback, time-dependent chloride transport and volume effects; all voltages are illustrative, not WWOX data. [Complete sensitivity grid](passive_grid.tsv)

**Next comparison.** Extract native-chloride measurements, measured firing, age, region, exposure and cell identity from [immature mouse cortex](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3912439/) and the [developmental shift study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15266648/). Compare them with what juvenile Wwox cortical recordings actually preserve. Check whether developmental migration or maturation changes survive matching activity. Evaluate pediatric exposure separately from experimental bath concentrations. Lower priority for supplementation if the functional direction or brain exposure is unsupported.
