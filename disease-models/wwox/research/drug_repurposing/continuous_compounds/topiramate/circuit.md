# Does antagonizing GluK1 increase inhibition in a WWOX network?

**The synapse and stimulus change the answer.** In Braga et al., rat basolateral amygdala interneuron responses to a GluK1 agonist were 71.9%, 54.2% and 12.3% of the agonist-alone response at 0.3, 1 and 10 µM topiramate. In recordings from principal cells, the same challenge raised spontaneous inhibitory event frequency to 895% of baseline. With topiramate it reached 697%, 466% and 108% respectively. Subtracting the original baseline before taking the ratio leaves 75%, 46% and 1% of the agonist-induced increment. These are ratios of published group summaries; blockers and added agonist define the preparation. They do not measure the effect on spontaneous inhibition in an unchallenged WWOX brain. [Braga et al., Results and Figs. 1–2](https://doi.org/10.1124/jpet.109.153908) · [Calculation](agonist.tsv)

With minimal electrical stimulation, the agonist increased the failure rate of inhibitory transmission from 48% to 88% (eight recordings). Topiramate at 10 µM prevented this increase. Topiramate alone increased evoked current amplitude to 172% of control without a detectable failure-rate change. Larger currents therefore cannot be interpreted as proof of greater release probability. The discriminating endpoints are evoked success probability and charge on successes, measured separately from interneuron firing. [Braga et al., Results accompanying Fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.1124/jpet.109.153908)

**A different circuit site.** Gryder and Rogawski isolated GluK1-mediated excitatory input to principal cells in slices from 20–30-day-old male rats. Half-block was interpolated at 0.46 µM; maximal inhibition required 40–50 minutes. At 10 µM both paired responses declined with no significant change in facilitation (four slices). This supports an additional postsynaptic action. In Fig. 4 the Results text gives 1 µM for the miniature-current experiment and its caption gives 10 µM. We do not use that trace for concentration calibration. GABA receptor blockade isolates this excitatory route but removes the inhibitory network response whose preservation matters in WWOX. [Original Results and Figs. 1, 3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6740668/)

**Measured exposure and a bounded sensitivity calculation.** FDA Table 4 for the open-label infant pharmacokinetic study gives mean total plasma troughs of 1.91, 3.25, 9.74 and 13.6 µg/mL in groups with 9, 9, 8 and 9 evaluable participants. At molecular weight 339.36 these are 5.63, 9.58, 28.70 and 40.08 µM. An adult study of 14 simultaneous trough pairs reported a total CSF-to-plasma ratio of 0.85. Applying it unchanged would give 4.78–34.06 µM across the infant group means. This scenario uses an unverified age-transfer assumption. The infant trial measured plasma, adult CSF differs from synaptic interstitial concentration, and bath exposure used a different matrix and timing. A numerical overlap with 10 µM does not establish exposure in the WWOX synapse. FDA explicitly states that concentrations were unavailable in the double-blind phase of the distinct pivotal efficacy trial. We therefore cannot label infants in that trial as exposed above or below a circuit threshold. [FDA review, pp. 10 and 26](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/pediatric/20505se5-037_20844se5-031_Toprimate_Clinpharm_BPCA.pdf) · [Adult paired CSF study, author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11591899/) · [Infant pharmacokinetic publication, author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23157581/) · [Molecular weight](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/topiramate)

**Decision and next comparison.** Retain topiramate for a narrow preclinical circuit question. The age-matched negative efficacy trial and lack of a measured WWOX response constrain trial priority. Next compare the proposed sign of inhibition with actual WWOX evoked GABA polarity or firing data at a matched developmental stage. If only spontaneous current frequency, chloride-transporter RNA or co-treated clinical seizure status is available, record the missing directional evidence. A meaningful rescue would suppress pathological recruitment while preserving evoked transmission under endogenous chloride conditions at a measured exposure.

**WWOX cross-check and a timing prediction.** In Breton et al., mouse neocortical sIPSCs were recorded at −70 mV with 140 mM KCl in the recording pipette. The mean peak was 57.3 pA in pooled controls and 27.5 pA in neuronal Wwox knockout (KO/control 0.48). This establishes an amplitude deficit under imposed chloride. It cannot determine whether GABA under native chloride at that age inhibits firing or by how much. The full-burst reversal was −31 mV, compared with about +6 mV for the initiating component and −36 mV during high-frequency recruitment (Fig. 2B, 30 bursts across five animals). Those values concern mixed synaptic currents and cannot substitute for isolated GABA reversal. Multiplying the mouse spontaneous current by the rat evoked amplitude gain would combine different endpoints. [Original mouse study, Methods and Figs. 2B, 4G–H](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34634460/)

**Predicted separation.** If the main WWOX action is amplification of effective GABAergic transmission, a larger effect is expected on late recruitment or burst duration than on the first evoked excitatory component, with preserved physiological propagation. If suppression of excitatory input to principal cells dominates, the early response or probability of initiating the burst should also fall. Measure stimulus-locked firing under native chloride and classify the early and late windows before comparing genotypes and drug conditions. Network suppression, sodium-channel block or pH shifts can yield superficially similar shortening of bursts. A positive late response would require survival of the physiological evoked response and concordant direct inhibitory measurements. Next inspect the direct WWOX treatment timelines for whether topiramate exposure, seizure type, background treatment and developmental outcome can actually be separated.
