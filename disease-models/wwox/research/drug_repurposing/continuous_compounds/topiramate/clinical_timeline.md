# What can the published WWOX treatment histories attribute to topiramate?

**Four informative narratives.** In AlBaradie et al., patient 1.2 had already experienced resolution of spasms at age five. Topiramate was documented with phenobarbitone and lamotrigine at age ten, when other seizure types were recorded. Earlier topiramate exposure cannot be dated from that narrative. Patient 3.1 had refractory seizures despite several drugs including topiramate; the text attributes a subsequent response to the ketogenic diet. Patient 4 had myoclonic and focal events and a history of multiple agents, with control reported on levetiracetam plus topiramate at last review. The narrative provides no isolated add-on or withdrawal estimate. Patient 5 was taking three drugs including topiramate when vigabatrin was added for spasms. The subsequent developmental status cannot be attributed to an individual agent. The four reports are selected exposure histories and provide no responder-rate denominator. [AlBaradie et al., case narratives 1.2, 3.1, 4 and 5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35792847/) · [Earlier laboratory audit](../../batch_sci_20261105/verify/audit_A1/audit_A1_README.md)

**A timed human signal with a within-child comparator.** You et al. report early focal seizures, followed by about three months without recurrence during levetiracetam plus phenobarbital treatment. After a tonic episode aged five months, topiramate was introduced; about three months without recurrence were reported again. At eight months spasms appeared, with tonic and myoclonic attacks also described. Vigabatrin was then added. At eleven months clinical observation and video-EEG diverged, with electrical events still reported. Withdrawal or continuation of every prior drug at each transition, a stable pretreatment rate of tonic episodes, and a matched EEG seizure count during the post-topiramate interval are not documented in this narrative. Thus the clearest temporal association in this check is compatible with a transient benefit, natural evolution or co-treatment. The earlier interval involved a different seizure phenotype. It cannot serve as a controlled baseline. [You et al., clinical history and Figs. 2–3 referenced in text](https://doi.org/10.1002/mgg3.2500)

**Checkpoint.** An unselected pediatric trial remains deferred. Keep the compound active only for a focused check of the remaining exact-name case leads for drug withdrawal, rechallenge or time-aligned EEG at stable background therapy. Prioritize a seizure-type-specific symptomatic effect; no existing observation here links topiramate to restored neural development. If the remaining records contain only polytherapy snapshots or group-level statements, close this clinical attribution question and retain the preclinical early-versus-late prediction.

**Remaining exact-name leads and disposition.** PMID 27495153 describes a partial response to several agents followed by worsening; PMID 30362252 reports a multi-agent regimen with phenobarbital, topiramate and pyridoxine; PMID 37974179 describes poor response on valproate plus topiramate; PMID 39507621 reports no sustained benefit across a longer medication history. None of the four topiramate passages documents a stable-background withdrawal, rechallenge or timed EEG response to the drug alone. Restrict that absence claim to the passages and held search scope. These observations cannot yield a patient-level response rate or exclude an individual symptomatic benefit. [W44X case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27495153/) · [Microdeletion case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30362252/) · [Deletion case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37974179/) · [Longitudinal adult history](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39507621/)

**Park topiramate for an unselected WWOX trial.** The temporally associated interval in You et al. is the strongest positive finding in this bounded check. It cannot establish causality or disease modification. Reopen for seizure-type-specific improvement attributable to a medication change with stable background treatment and EEG chronology, or for selective reduction of pathological late recruitment with preserved native-chloride inhibition and physiological propagation at measured neural exposure. The existing preclinical hypothesis survives; none of the six exact-name reports verifies its mechanism.
