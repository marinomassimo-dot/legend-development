# Alpha-tocotrienol exposure checkpoint

28 November 2026 UTC. context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. The [frozen comparison](pretest.md) preceded this targeted extract.

[Patel et al., primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22298568/), Table 1, p. 516, footnote 4. Total brain alpha-tocotrienol is **1.29 ± 1.06 nmol/g** in **four** mixed-tocotrienol-supplemented surgical specimens versus **0.04 ± 0.01 nmol/g** in **four** cadaveric comparator specimens (mean ± SD). The brain comparison uses different tissue sources, despite the general description of adult surgical assignment. Table 1 labels the comparator as tocopherol-supplemented. The brain-specific Methods identify autopsy tissue without dietary tocotrienol exposure, and give no evidence of tocopherol assignment for those controls. Other tocotrienol isoforms are also higher in the exposed group. No individual observations are transcribed.

Methods, pp. 514–515. The primary text lists **61.52 mg d-alpha-tocotrienol per 200 mg capsule**, alongside gamma and delta forms, with **two capsules per day** in the study. Nominal alpha content from the formulation is **123.04 mg/day**. [A later neuronal study's introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9081260/) describes 61.52 mg daily when citing this result. Keep the primary per-capsule and daily amounts distinct. The original report describes washed excised tissue and HPLC detection. This is total tissue analyte after long exposure to a mixture. Neither free neural parent concentration, intact parent-to-hydroquinone conversion nor alpha-specific functional efficacy can be inferred. Retain this as a tissue-delivery observation and extract functional comparisons at the next checkpoint.
