# Alpha-tocotrienol, Src and lipid protection in WWOX loss of function

28 November 2026 UTC. context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. Continuous compound #198, INV-06 with lipid-redox and neuronal stress controls. The [pretest](pretest.md) distinguishes an early parent-dependent signal, conversion to a quinone or hydroquinone, and chain-breaking membrane protection.

**Disposition.** Retain as a conditional cellular lipid-redox comparator. Defer pediatric WWOX priority. The low nominal cellular activity, independent liposome result and adult tissue observation justify measuring parent and products together. They leave the WWOX mechanism, unbound juvenile exposure and first-sheath or conduction benefit unresolved.

## Reproducible exposure comparison

Within the same cell assay, alpha-tocotrienol's rescue EC₅₀ is **30.9-fold lower** than alpha-tocopherol's in Q7 cells and **16.7-fold lower** in the separately run Pfa1 genetic model. The Q7 parent EC₅₀ is **3.88 times** either Q7 product EC₅₀. These ratios use central estimates and separate culture conditions. They supply neither a membrane-normalized rank nor an effect in WWOX. [Assay inputs](assay_inputs.tsv) · [Matched ratios](matched_ratios.tsv)

**Conversion-only counterfactual.** Under a one-product model, the parent-dose well at 0.167 µM must generate activity-equivalent product exposure matching a direct 0.043 µM product standard to explain the half-rescue result. If each mole formed has effective recovery identical to a mole directly administered, the required fraction is **25.7%**. Twofold higher relative recovery requires **12.9%**, and fivefold higher recovery requires **5.15%**. At one-quarter recovery, the requirement is **103%**, beyond a one-parent-to-one-product balance under this model. The calculation includes no estimated conversion rate, binding fraction or interaction between products. A weak or absent LC-MS signal constrains a conversion-only explanation only after recovery and redox trapping have been validated against authentic standards in the same assay. WWOX Tyr33 phosphorylation has been reported in stress-signaling experiments. A WWOX-null cell lacks that substrate, so any benefit would require an effect on an intact downstream or parallel mechanism. Separate neuronal Src activity from oligodendroglial Fyn. A mouse Fyn-null and kinase-inactive comparison shows forebrain myelin loss, with sparing of the sampled cervical spinal cord. This is a functional constraint on interpreting a nonselective Src-family phospho-signal, without evidence that alpha-tocotrienol itself inhibits Fyn. ## Prediction and decision

**One discriminating comparison.** In a stage-matched Wwox-null and corrected neuron–oligodendroglial preparation, first verify a native excess oxidized phospholipid burden per parent lipid or an early Src abnormality. Compare measured intact alpha-tocotrienol with authentic quinone and hydroquinone and an independently calibrated membrane-protection control. Measure recovered parent and products over 5–30 min and at later times with recovery standards and quenching that preserves redox state. At matched initial recruitment, read neuronal Src and oligodendroglial Fyn with separate kinase-specific endpoints, then regioisomer-resolved esterified lipids, survival, first sheaths per surviving cell and conduction. Verify cystine/redox stress separately from receptor-mediated excitation.

An early change in neuronal Src with verified intact parent, too little product to reproduce the effect and persistence after selective reduction of conversion would favor a parent-dependent signal. Restoration of early Src activity at matched analytes tests whether that signal mediates the benefit. If rescue persists, a parallel mechanism remains plausible. Reproduction by calibrated product exposure and loss of rescue with reduced conversion favors the metabolite route. If benefits track matched membrane protection with no separable Src response, favor the membrane route. Loss of Fyn function or first recruitment and a result confined to challenge survival both lower WWOX priority.

**Pediatric feasibility.** The adult evidence is a **mixed** supplement used before brain surgery, with **1.29 nmol/g total** alpha-tocotrienol in four exposed specimens and an independently sourced autopsy comparator. The primary text gives **61.52 mg per capsule**, not a measured daily intake or free concentration. The nominal alpha amount corresponds to two capsules in the published study. [Adult exposure audit](exposure.md)

A six-month pediatric liver study randomized **32** and analyzed **15 mixed-fraction versus 14 placebo**. Mean ages were 11.8 and 12.5 years. The formulation combined multiple tocotrienol isoforms and alpha-tocopherol. Authors report no compliance issues attributed to side effects in that small trial. Liver endpoints and this tolerability observation give limited transfer to an isolated stereochemically defined alpha preparation in children with WWOX. Free juvenile neural parent and products, interactions with background antiseizure drugs and spontaneous or developmental function remain unresolved. [Al-Baiaty et al., pp. 3–5, Fig. 1 and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12887-024-04993-8)

Reopen for measured exposure that separates parent and products, a repeatable WWOX lipid or kinase phenotype, and functional benefit with Fyn activity and physiological inhibitory recruitment preserved. An independent review would change disposition if it identifies a matched, speciated experiment or challenges the product-recovery comparison with direct data.
