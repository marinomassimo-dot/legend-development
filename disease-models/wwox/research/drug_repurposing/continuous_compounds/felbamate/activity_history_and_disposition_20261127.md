# Felbamate in WWOX loss of function

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · `context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`

**Disposition.** Retain felbamate as a conditional, nonselective circuit comparator and park it for pediatric WWOX trial priority. The best new discriminator is the joint effect on weak and prolonged excitation, with glutamate release measured independently. At pediatric-plasma-scale nominal exposure, native rat data allow facilitation of weak NMDA responses, suppression of late NMDA current, and changes in excitatory event frequency through different experiments. These effects can coexist. A fall in WWOX bursts alone would leave their contributions unidentified. The [existing boxed-warning qualification](../../qualification_it9_p4b_b_20261030/records/P3-17_felbamate.md) remains the feasibility baseline.

## Reproducible mechanism comparison

**Exposure anchor.** Three reported peak total plasma levels in children aged 4–12 years correspond to **54–56, 101–105 and 154–160 µM** estimated free parent using the label's in-vitro binding range. At 100 µM, both the native temporal current contrast and the entorhinal event-frequency effect are on this concentration scale. The comparison uses estimated free group plasma peaks against nominal bath drug. The human surgical-brain study is indexed without quantitative results, and open publisher routes examined here did not expose those data. Animal CSF or total resected tissue cannot supply a pediatric unbound cortical concentration. ## Presynaptic alternative and WWOX discriminator

Yang et al. at 100 µM found about **51%** lower mean spontaneous AMPA-EPSC frequency (5.1→2.5 Hz, four layer V entorhinal neurons), at nearly unchanged amplitude and with postsynaptic NMDA receptors blocked. The cell-normalized mean decrease reported separately is 55%. The reverse-order d-AP5 comparison involved three neurons. The mixed action-potential-dependent and miniature events, occlusion and the small number of biological recording units support a presynaptic hypothesis with residual uncertainty about release and firing. In a developmentally closer comparator, rat S1 L4→L2/3 responses showed a presynaptic component under conditions favoring glutamate accumulation (bursting or reduced uptake). In young mouse S1 L4→L2/3, Chiu and Carter (P11–22) found d-AP5 reduced peak EPSC to 82.4% of baseline (12 cells) and detected no paired-pulse ratio effect (11 cells, reported P=0.21). These recordings differ in species and conditions. They justify measuring release in the precise WWOX projection and stimulation state. Other direct alternatives are GABA_A modulation, potentiation in four of fifteen recombinant combinations in one screen, and adult rat somatodendritic dihydropyridine-sensitive calcium current inhibition. The calcium abstract's 0.504 µM IC₅₀ is an assay observation with barium as the carrier and no evidence for the relevant juvenile presynaptic current. [Yang et al., Methods and Fig. 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2496957/) · [Brasier and Feldman, Results and Figs. 1–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3071744/) · [Chiu and Carter, Fig. 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9345169/) · [GABA_A subtypes, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17056029/) · [Calcium current, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8613908/)

**Prediction.** In age-matched Wwox neuronal-deletion and control P13–17 cortex, apply the established AMPA/NMDA separation with measured free drug, physiological extracellular Mg²⁺ and monitored pH. Measure the native drive and co-agonist availability, then repeat with matched input and co-agonist conditions to separate genotype from environmental gating effects. Read the first AMPA response, early and late NMDA charge, evoked inhibitory charge and pulse recovery separately. Compare weak, isolated input with matched conditioning and a strong train. A drug effect that increases weak recruitment or lengthens recovery while lowering late charge would identify a physiological cost. Stable evoked glutamate release with stronger late inhibition after conditioning would favor a postsynaptic activity-history effect. Reduced release under fixed input firing with the postsynaptic NMDA component suppressed favors a presynaptic mechanism. A parallel, validated compartment-specific NMDA comparison can test occlusion while a standard postsynaptic agonist challenge separates reduced release from altered receptor responses. Calibrate the NMDA and calcium contributions before attributing a release change to one target. A reduction observed only in spontaneous AMPA event counts fails that inference. GABA_A charge and excitability at matched drug exposure give independent checks. Resume the mechanistic branch for a reproducible, genotype-preferential response with preserved brief recruitment, inhibition and recovery. ## Pediatric precedent and decision boundary

In the 70-day Lennox–Gastaut add-on trial, caregiver diary atonic seizure frequency fell 34% versus 9% among 28/22 participants with the endpoint. The 37/36 participants in the brief video/EEG comparison showed −11% versus +1%, with no detected group difference. The differences in reported group mean percentage changes are −25 and −12 points, respectively. They measure different seizure endpoints. A later tertiary pediatric retrospective series mixed clinical seizure responses and spike-wave-index responses. The study reports 37/73 responders at three months, or 37/75 if both omitted outcomes are counted as failures, and adverse reactions in 19/75 (three with elevated liver enzymes, one neutropenia). Polytherapy and concurrent interventions limit attribution, and a small series cannot estimate rare fatal risks. One public WWOX-related ketogenic-diet study source records concomitant felbamate exposure before and after diet initiation, while its endpoint concerns diet response. It provides no felbamate-specific outcome. [Lennox–Gastaut study, Table 2](https://www.nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/NEJM199301073280105) · [Pediatric retrospective cohort, Methods, Results and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9530252/) · [Primary ketogenic-diet cohort thesis, Table 4.1](https://escholarship.org/uc/item/8s773849)

Aplastic anemia and acute hepatic failure are boxed risks. The label describes aplastic-anemia presentations at 5–30 weeks where timing was available. The short randomized trial and mixed retrospective cohort do not clear this risk. Historical spontaneous-report adjudication confirmed 23 of 31 reports, using a sales-based exposed-user denominator. Concomitant therapy and differing attribution thresholds prevent a genotype- or regimen-specific estimate. The label also shows interactions with valproate, phenytoin, carbamazepine and its epoxide, and phenobarbital, which complicate any attribution of an add-on response. Independent review could change the mechanism if it found an exposure-matched result in native WWOX tissue with separate release measurements. The existing evidence does not advance clinical priority.
