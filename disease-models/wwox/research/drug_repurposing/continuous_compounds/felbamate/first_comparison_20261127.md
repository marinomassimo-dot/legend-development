# Felbamate first exposure and pulse comparison

**Status.** Preliminary checkpoint. The [opening test](pretest_20261127.md) addresses the compartment and activity-history question. The prior trial disposition remains on hold.

**Pediatric parent exposure.** The prescribing information reports steady-state peak total plasma of 17, 32 and 49 µg/mL across three studied exposure levels in children aged 4–12 years. With molecular mass 238.24 g/mol these are 71, 134 and 206 µM. Applying the reported in-vitro binding range of 22–25% estimates 54–56, 101–105 and 154–160 µM free plasma respectively. These are simple exposure brackets given albumin dependence, without a measured pediatric cortical concentration. Identified a primary report of drug measurements in resected human brain. The indexed PubMed entry provides no numerical results. The publisher identifier resolves the source for a subsequent access check (DOI 10.1016/S0090-9556(25)08294-7). [Label, Pharmacokinetics](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=2f522701-397a-11de-8a39-0800200c9a66) · [PubChem identity and mass](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/Felbamate) · [Human surgical-tissue report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8149879/)

**Matched concentration, different current histories.** At 100 µM nominal felbamate, Kuo et al. report normalized peak and late currents of 0.96 and 0.79 for the isolated 1 mM NMDA pulse (0.3 µM glycine, n=3 cells). With a 5-second 10 µM NMDA prepulse, the corresponding values are 0.88 and 0.79 (n=8 cells). At 30 µM glycine, the same pair is 0.97 and 0.83 without conditioning (n=7) and 0.90 and 0.78 with conditioning (n=8). Thus the point difference in early current between protocols is −8 or −7 percentage points, depending on glycine. The late difference is 0 or −5. Within each cell, normalization is to its drug-free current. Cross-protocol differences compare separate groups and have no paired uncertainty estimate. The 2-second, 1 mM agonist challenge and dissociated P7–14 rat CA1 neurons in nominally magnesium-free recording solution (usually −70 mV) impose a receptor-state history unlike fast cortical synaptic release. [Kuo et al., Figs. 2B–C, 5B–C and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1124/mol.65.2.370) · [Accessible article copy](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=da8945ad271a10072d765d469a059266f8c0a060&repid=rep1&type=pdf)

**Single-condition model check.** Replay Kuo's Eq. 3 with `a = NMDA/34`, `C = (1+a)**2`, `O = 2.6*a*a` and `D = 4*O` in concentration units of µM. For felbamate `F`, multiply each weight by `1+F/K` with `K = (200,110,55)` for C, O and D, then divide the new open fraction by the drug-free open fraction. At 100 µM felbamate the predicted late current ratios are 1.16, 0.98 and 0.75 at 4, 10 and 1,000 µM NMDA, respectively. The last value is 4 points below the published 0.79 mean for the 1 mM condition. This is a replay of a published fitted model, with no additional fit or inference about fast synaptic currents. A distinct Fig. 8 test uses 300 µM and double-exponential recovery fits. From the published constants, the predicted 1-s recovery fraction is about 56% in control and 45% with drug, a 10.4-point gap. This concentration exceeds the estimated pediatric free peak brackets above. Next compare low-agonist points and examine whether the recovery change persists closer to those brackets. [Kuo et al., Eq. 3 and Figs. 5D–8](https://doi.org/10.1124/mol.65.2.370)

**Competing presynaptic explanation.** In a separate rat medial entorhinal layer V slice study, 100 µM felbamate lowered AMPA-mediated spontaneous EPSC frequency from 5.1 to 2.5 Hz with postsynaptic NMDA receptors blocked through the patch pipette. Mean event amplitude was 11.2→10.7 pA. Subsequent 2-AP5 left group frequency at 2.6 Hz (n=4 neurons). The reverse-order arm used three neurons and also found occlusion. The 51% drop is a ratio of frequency means. The authors report a mean 55% cell-normalized decrease, a different denominator. The trial samples 200 events per cell under each condition, a mixture of action-potential-dependent and miniature events. Occlusion and unchanged amplitude support a presynaptic NMDA interpretation in that preparation. The indirect outcome leaves release versus altered spontaneous activity to resolve in WWOX. [Yang et al., Methods, Fig. 3 and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2496957/)

A separate primary abstract reports a 0.504 µM apparent IC₅₀ for a dihydropyridine-sensitive high-voltage calcium current in acutely isolated adult rat cortical and neostriatal neurons. Carry this as a competing hypothesis pending confirmation of the voltage protocol, partial maximal effect, analysis denominator and translation to juvenile presynaptic release. [Stefani et al., abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8613908/)

**Pediatric-inclusive clinical precedent.** The 70-day Lennox–Gastaut add-on randomized study included 37 participants on felbamate and 36 on placebo, ranging from 4 to 36 years across the study. During the treatment phase, the diary count of atonic seizures was evaluated in 28 versus 22 and fell by 34% versus 9% (reported P=0.01). The corresponding count during short video/EEG sessions used 37 versus 36 and changed −11% versus +1% (P=0.32). Different endpoints and sample windows support different interpretations. The duration and trial size cannot settle rare or delayed risks. [Primary randomized trial, Table 2](https://www.nejm.org/doi/pdf/10.1056/NEJM199301073280105)
