# Oral sodium pyruvate, sampling compartment and neural exposure
context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Culture baseline.** The GSE156243 organoid method uses a 1:1 basal mixture. The current Gibco DMEM/F-12 21331 and Neurobasal options contain 55 and 25 mg/L sodium pyruvate. At 110 g/mol the fresh basal mixture would contain 0.364 mM before supplements. This is a formulation scenario. The alternative supplier, lot, final dilution, consumption and concentration between feeds are unresolved. [Existing protocol audit](../riboflavin/medium.md) · [DMEM/F-12 formulation](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/technical-resources/media-formulation.329.html) · [Neurobasal formulation](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/technical-resources/media-formulation.251.html)

**Direct pediatric measurement.** Komaki et al. report pretreatment CSF pyruvate of 1.21 mg/dL (about 0.137 mM, using 88.06 g/mol pyruvic-acid equivalent) in one child with Leigh syndrome and complex IV deficiency. Blood changed from 1.13 to 0.88 mg/dL after starting oral sodium pyruvate. Timing relative to ingestion was unspecified. The 0.88 value belongs to blood. Exercise tolerance and cardiac measures improved while neurological signs showed no significant improvement at one year. Diarrhea occurred with concentrated preparations. [Primary study, journal p. 314, §2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19616603/)

**Independent baseline and patient overlap.** Fujii et al., patient 3, reported CSF lactate 5.2 mM and molar lactate/pyruvate ratio 20 before treatment, implying approximately 0.260 mM endogenous pyruvate. The GMFM-88 score rose from 13.6% to 16.0% at two months then fell to 12.8% at twelve. Patient 1’s early gains persisted, with deterioration during open withdrawal and recovery after restarting; there was no blinded comparator. Patient 4 is explicitly the earlier Saito case (p. 137, §3.4 and ref. 3). The early four-point NPMDS improvement in that child came entirely from the caregiver-rated quality-of-life domain, while sections I–III were unchanged. Serum/blood monitoring, background therapy and heterogeneous disorders limit inference. The two reports together describe four children. Komaki adds a fifth distinct child across these three reports. No time-matched post-dose CSF pyruvate value was identified in the examined records. [Fujii, Tables 1–3 and §§3.1–3.4](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24830361/) · [Saito, p. 634, Tables 1–2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21855607/)

**Controlled adult exposure.** In nine healthy adult men in a double-blind crossover, capillary blood pyruvate averaged 110 to 191 µM in the sodium-pyruvate period and 101 to 149 µM in the placebo period from rest to 60 minutes. The descriptive difference of mean changes is +33 µM. The authors report no significant treatment effect. This sample misses earlier peaks, has no CSF measurement, and provides no paired-variance estimate for this calculated contrast. Exercise later raised pyruvate in both arms. [Primary study, Methods and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4042581/)

**Decision.** Park for an unselected WWOX pediatric trial. The two diagnostic CSF observations, the nominal culture baseline and a single adult sampling time cannot define a therapeutic brain concentration or disprove CNS entry. The 2 mM cell-free peroxide experiment is a chemistry comparator. A functional WWOX comparison should start in measured fresh and spent medium and resolve neuronal uptake, pyruvate-derived mitochondrial oxidation versus labelled acetate recycling, and evoked neural recovery at an independently supported exposure. A fall in blood lactate alone would leave the decision unchanged. Replicate the arithmetic with `python3 exposure.py /path/to/exercise.xml`.
