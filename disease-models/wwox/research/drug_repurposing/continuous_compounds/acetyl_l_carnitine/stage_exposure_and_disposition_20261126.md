# Acetyl-L-carnitine disposition and the first-sheath comparison

**Data scope.** C2 and TMAO in adult venous plasma in µM, nominal C2 added to murine glial cultures in µM, and P17 mouse hippocampal counts per million assigned-class nuclear UMIs. Human WWOX cerebral and oligocortical models are distinct preparations. No WWOX treatment experiment is reported here.

`context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`. The fixed question was whether the glial result couples a C2-specific response to energy recovery at an oral exposure that could plausibly overlap the neural assay.

**Decision.** Retain acetyl-L-carnitine as a preclinical comparator. Defer pediatric WWOX trial priority. The post-oral human data are predominantly plasma measurements of total C2. They leave the size and duration of an intact central increment unresolved. Rodent and cell signals support a measurable carbon-routing hypothesis. None of the evaluated experiments demonstrates a WWOX-selective link between mitochondrial flux and functional myelin at an exposure that could bridge to pediatric research.

## Quantitative human comparison

- **Healthy adults, single oral administration, 2025.** The C2 capsules contained approximately 104% of the stated free-form C2 content. At 4 h after nominal 1.5 g, median plasma C2 rose from 9.4 to 12.5 µM (later IQR 9.8–14.4, n=11 in Fig. 2 although Table 1 lists 12). At nominal 0.5 g the values were 4.7 to 5.5 µM (n=6). The paper reports no significant increase for the higher C2 dose. After the higher C0 dose, the separate group went from 12.1 to 20.9 µM (n=12, Fig. 2A). At equal nominal grams the doses were neither equimolar nor matched for actual content. For C0 the paper measured 1,346 mg rather than the 1,500 mg dose label in Table 1. Differences of group medians are descriptive, with no participant-level estimates. Plasma C2 includes endogenous C2 and does not identify intact absorption of the oral molecule. [Krims-Davis et al., §§2.2–2.5, 3.1, Table 1, Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.1002/mnfr.70316)
- **Repeated C2 exposure in adults with treated HIV and toxic peripheral neuropathy.** The four regimen means at 2 h were 6.8–7.7 µM after at least three months in the initial twice-daily regimen and, for the once-daily arms, four weeks on the revised regimen. Predose-to-2-h mean changes span −0.9 to +0.4 µM (15 measured twice-daily, 4, 7 and 12 once-daily). Only two time points were sampled, and the controls were younger untreated adults without HIV. The groups cannot establish a ceiling for healthy or pediatric plasma, and they do not measure the CNS. [Herzmann et al., Methods and Table 2](https://doi.org/10.2174/1874613600802010039)
- **Scale and safety.** The Yang 100 µM nominal sheath assay and 2,000 µM differentiated respiration assay are 8 and 160 times the 12.5 µM adult post-oral plasma median. These cross-compartment ratios compare exposure scale, with no inference about a therapeutic threshold. The held infant diagnostic baseline CSF median of 0.19 µM is another compartment and was measured without a supplement challenge. The corrected adult clinical abstract for Parnetti reports a CSF rise after sequential IV and oral use and gives no absolute CSF values by route. Krims-Davis reports TMAO 2.2→47 µM at 16 h after the higher C2 dose. This is a separate metabolite signal with no demonstrated pediatric harm in that experiment. The seven-day study used C0 only. The age, concomitant antiseizure drugs and renal function relevant to WWOX trial feasibility remain unbridged. ## Does the glial comparison establish C2-specific OCTN2 or CRAT dependence?

**Membrane growth.** Yang Fig. 2F shows a larger MBP-positive area per mature oligodendrocyte under C2 in primary 2D cultures with control siRNA. The groups differ at baseline in the OCTN2 knockdown comparison. Fig. 2H shows residual significant within-arm C2 responses on axon-mimetic fibers under both OCTN2 and CRAT knockdown. Fig. 2H has no C0 arm under CRAT knockdown. The Fig. 2 legend specifies within-siRNA drug contrasts and baseline comparisons for F/H. It supplies neither the treatment-by-knockdown term nor a replicate file from which to fit the interaction. Comparing which arms meet a significance threshold cannot recover the interaction. In Fig. 2B, MBP is a separate immunoblot in differentiated Oli-neu, with n=3 independent cultures per group and unequal plotted baselines. In Supplement Fig. S4D, CRAT knockdown was checked at the RNA level. Residual protein and catalytic activity in the individual nanofiber cultures are unknown.

**Respiration.** Fig. 3D–F compares 24 h at 2 mM C2 with vehicle only under control siRNA in differentiated Oli-neu. Fig. 3J–L compares differentiated control versus SLC22A5 knockdown without a crossed C2 treatment. The acute 100 µM addition in 3G–I is in undifferentiated Oli-neu, and the two knockdown-condition traces both receive C2. The figure does not identify a treatment-specific interaction at the differentiated stage. Supplement Fig. S5D–F concerns CRAT knockdown alone in undifferentiated cells. The differentiated C2 arm increases the OCR indices labeled ATP production, basal respiration, proton leak and nonmitochondrial oxygen consumption. There is no significant increase labeled for maximal or spare capacity. An ATP-linked OCR change in one arm gives no estimate of carbon source, ATP reserve under driven activity or conduction. [Yang accepted PDF, figure images pp. 43–44, captions pp. 35–36](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-026-04214-z_reference.pdf) · [Supplement, captions for S4–S5](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41398-026-04214-z/MediaObjects/41398_2026_4214_MOESM1_ESM.docx)

## WWOX stage selection and result that would change disposition

No assigned oligodendroglial class in held P17 hippocampus passed the prespecified 20 CRAT UMIs per genotype arm. Totals are 22/19 in OPC, 2/3 in committed precursors, 1/11 in newly formed oligodendrocytes, 16/2 in myelin-forming oligodendrocytes and 3/3 in mature oligodendrocytes. This is pooled sequencing with one pool per arm, not a result about protein or flux. The WWOX neuronal knockout shows a callosal loss of mature oligodendrocytes with thinner myelin and glial WWOX retained. In human oligocortical spheroids the reported window includes loss of mature lineage cells and reduced MBP/CNP at week 30, with EM at week 37 based on 2 control and 3 knockouts. Use the period of first-sheath formation with stage markers and axonal contacts confirmed in each preparation. Early cerebral bulk RNA cannot establish cell-autonomous CRAT impairment at this stage. **Predictions.** In the same neuronal–oligodendroglial preparation, contrast C2 with C0, acetate and C0 plus equimolar acetate, using WWOX loss-of-function and matched controls. Use matched free parent exposure across genotypes, measured labeled acetate in the medium, and absolute mitochondrial M+2 acetyl-CoA and citrate in separately resolved stages. A C2-specific oxidative response predicts greater early label delivery than the acetate control together with improved ATP recovery and first sheaths per viable axon, preserved free CoA and viable counts, and later improvement in conduction with evoked inhibition retained after washout. Acetate recreating the response points to cleavage or convergent acetate use. Label without ATP or conduction recovery indicates transfer without the requested function. Sheath improvement without the early mitochondrial signature favors another route, such as signaling or anabolic support. Assign OCTN2 or CRAT dependency only from the change in the treatment contrast after a matched perturbation, with its untreated baseline and residual activity measured.

**Reopen** upon a genotype-selective preclinical flux–function response within a measured exposure window that could bridge to pediatric neural tissue. Independent review is useful if a measured exposure or a properly crossed experiment would change that decision. The existing microscopy brackets and plasma values cannot settle those questions. Seizure suppression and durable myelin function would remain separate outcomes.
