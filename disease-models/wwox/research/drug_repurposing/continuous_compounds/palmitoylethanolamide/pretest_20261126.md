# Palmitoylethanolamide · first comparative test

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · PEA (N-palmitoylethanolamine), endogenous N-acylethanolamine and supplement candidate · held WWOX tissue and cell-class expression

**Question.** Does added chemically identified PEA restore a glial fatty-acid and inflammatory response mediated by PPAR-alpha in WWOX neural tissue, with selective neuronal functional recovery? Rivals are faster hydrolysis and palmitate incorporation, altered composition or generic suppression of glial inflammatory output. This extends the WWOX glial activation and lipid turnover strands beyond the substrate synthesis and lysosomal contexts. Formulation can affect exposure. Free PEA, a defined oral formulation and precursor fatty acids require separate measurements; combined supplements are separate interventions.

**Before examining the signal.** Compare PPARA and RXRA, potential signaling controls PPARG and GPR55, candidate PEA synthesis or hydrolysis enzymes NAPEPLD, FAAH, and NAAA, and selective downstream or context genes ACOX1, CPT1A, ACADM, ACSL1 and IL1B. Assess matched P17 mouse cortex/hippocampus and week 15 human organoid libraries, then assess excitatory, inhibitory and astrocyte nuclear assignments and any explicitly assigned immune class. Do not extrapolate one pooled nuclear genotype to a biological sample effect. Species and cell types may differ. A consistent ligand handling plus receptor-response signal would favor a response test. Near-background receptor expression or mixed lineage effects prioritize receptor RNA and protein and endogenous lipid measurements.

**Prediction and stop condition.** In untreated and isogenic corrected neural-glial preparations, quantify baseline PEA and time-resolved intact drug after verified exposure, its hydrolysis products and a PPAR-alpha response, using the same medium and background lipid composition. Normalize to responding lineage number. A PPAR-alpha mechanism predicts a selective restoration of glial fatty-acid handling and a neuronal recovery phenotype at measured intact PEA. Pharmacological or genetic loss of PPAR-alpha signaling should remove the effect under matched lipid availability. A matched free palmitate control or matched carrier limits that explanation. If total isotope moves into generic phospholipids with low intact parent, or glial cytokines fall at equal magnitude in both genotypes without improved neuronal function, defer the mechanism. Seizure-like activity, glial response and evoked recovery are distinct outcomes.

**Pediatric feasibility.** Use defined, same-form oral formulation evidence. Document plasma and brain parent, age, chronic safety, comparison arms and concurrent antiseizure agents. Supplement status does not determine exposure or efficacy. An acute anti-inflammatory result in injured tissues cannot on its own support WWOX developmental rescue. No experimental result is assigned in this prespecification.
