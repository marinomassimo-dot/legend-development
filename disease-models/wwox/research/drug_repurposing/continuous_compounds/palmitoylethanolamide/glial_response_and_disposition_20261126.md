**Disposition.** Defer PEA for pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retain it as a mechanistic probe of ligand handling, PPAR-alpha competence, and glial–neuronal signaling. The held WWOX data leave intact PEA and responding-cell exposure unmeasured. A receptor-specific response in an inflammatory state, acute perinatal protection and later WWOX circuit recovery require different controls. ## A measured response at 100 nM, with different causal resolution by endpoint

- **Receptor-competence control.** In primary rat microglia, after 24 h the **100 nM** arm increases Cnr2 mRNA by approximately fivefold in Fig. 1A. The figure marks only 100 nM versus vehicle among the **1, 10 and 100 nM** arms (n = 6–8 as reported, without independent litter counts in the read methods). GW6471 and PPAR-alpha silencing each remove the CB2 protein increase. The paper reports about **81%** Ppara knockdown (Fig. 1B/E and E′, n = 6 for the silencing assays). The independent HEK293 promoter occupancy comparison uses ectopic PPAR-alpha and RXR expression. These data support receptor dependence of a CB2 expression endpoint in cultured glia. The same 100 nM also produces morphologic activation of cultured microglia (Fig. 1F). CB2 induction cannot be treated as evidence of quiet inflammation, restored glial oxidation or WWOX rescue. [Primary microglia study, Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-00342-1)
- **Glial challenge control.** In newborn-derived rat astrocytes mixed with embryonic rat neurons, simultaneous **100 nM** PEA and amyloid challenge for 24 h changes approximate GFAP-positive counts **62.9 → 35.4** and MAP2-associated apoptotic counts **14.0 → 5.1** on the plotted axes. With MK886 added, the estimates are **56.8** and **12.1**. The analogous PPAR-gamma antagonist arm is **41.5** and **6.0**. There were three independent experiments with at least nine fields per treatment. Fields are subsamples. The figure shows comparisons to amyloid challenge and lacks an amyloid-plus-MK886-without-PEA arm. MK886 also inhibits FLAP, so this is a pharmacological reversal compatible with PPAR-alpha, with less specificity than gene perturbation in the separate microglial model. The preparation exposes neurons and astrocytes together. The result provides neither an astrocyte-specific causal effect nor a native fatty-acid turnover readout. [Primary coculture study, Fig. 1B/C](https://doi.org/10.1186/1742-2094-9-49) · [Primary MK886 pharmacology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11389700/)
- **Exposure interpretation.** A 100 nM nominal glial bath is about **6.2–6.5 times** the density-based whole-brain scale from the maximum sampled labeled PEA in the ultramicronized oral rat arm. Neither is a free concentration. The excitotoxic hippocampal slice abstract gives a pooled **1–1,000 nM** range and antagonist-response result, without allocating endpoints to the minimum concentration. The identified publisher and indexed routes did not provide a validated minimum-effective value for this comparison. The exposure gap is unresolved. [Brain tracer](brain_tracer_and_controls_20261126.md) · [Primary slice abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20221904/)

## Perinatal developmental countercheck

Herrera et al. report 75 male pups (four initial groups of **21, 19, 18 and 17**) from 20 pregnant dams in a perinatal asphyxia experiment. Treatment was by injection within the first hour; the protocol subsection does not explicitly specify the injection route. Pups were fostered, and the behavioral assessor was reported as blinded. The analysis description does not model litter or document the independent litter counts for the individual contrasts. Some endpoint denominator degrees of freedom imply loss of observations under a simple individual-level model. The panel means and F audit cannot reconstruct litter assignment or attrition. [Neonatal primary study, Methods and Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnbeh.2022.953157)

The interaction contrast subtracts the treatment-associated shift in controls from the treatment-associated shift in the injury group. These are approximate bar estimates and carry no new inference. Forelimb placing **birth condition** was printed as F(1,67) = 3.09 with p = 0.008. Its recalculated p is **0.08334**. The independently reported interaction calculation still agrees with its printed rounded p. The pairwise p = 0.008 for vehicle birth groups is a different comparison. The gait onset subgroup comparison cannot substitute for the nonsignificant interaction. Gait and surface-righting longitudinal performance report no group effect or group-by-day interaction. Geotaxis performance shows a large early contrast on P11 and P12 (injured vehicle approximately **40.6 and 49.8 s**, injured PEA **20.6 and 16.9 s**), with similar arms by P13 and P14 as reported and no effect on geotaxis onset. The endpoint profile supports an early, selective injury result and leaves persistent general motor recovery after established WWOX dysfunction unresolved. ## Acute function and lipid redistribution in uninjured animals

Balaji et al. used adult female rats. Their behavioral, lipidomics and imaging arms are separate cohorts. At the high intraperitoneal exposure studied, open-field distance in Fig. 2 is approximately **20.4 → 5.2 m** compared with vehicle, about **75% lower** (six rats per treatment arm in the behavioral study). The region-summed anandamide signal in Fig. 5C is lower at 30 min than vehicle; 2-AG is higher in Fig. 5D. The lipidomics methods sum the six regions within each rat (5 per arm before analyte-specific exclusions), with unadjusted exploratory tests across numerous analytes and omission of values beyond group-based thresholds. The primary text describes the corresponding overall PEA contrast as significant, while Fig. 5B marks it with the stated trend symbol. Carry the direction without upgrading the panel to significant. This is a different sex, developmental stage, route, outcome and sampling scale than either the oral tracer or asphyxia study. Acute changes in movement and multiple lipids give reasons to require physiological evoked function at matched activity and to measure other N-acylethanolamines alongside PEA. They do not establish the mechanism of the lipid changes or seizure protection. [Primary adult study, Methods and Figs. 2 and 5](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnins.2024.1506352)

## WWOX reopening criteria

Advance the PEA mechanism if a matched WWOX/corrected neural preparation shows an interpretable intact-parent or receptor-linked functional deficit, followed by selective recovery of native fatty-acid handling and evoked neural function after verified exposure. Compare PEA with the corresponding free palmitate and carrier, test PPAR-alpha dependence, and record endogenous alternative N-acylethanolamines, locomotor or general suppression proxies, and receptor competence. If PPAR-alpha is engaged but fatty-acid handling and evoked function diverge, classify the compound as a receptor probe. If the benefit appears only at challenge onset or with broad motor and network suppression, defer a WWOX developmental inference. The small controlled pediatric adjunct experience covers a different indication and background medication. Chronic WWOX tolerability, drug interactions and central cellular exposure remain unknown. An independent review would change the disposition if it can supply a WWOX lipid and function data set, or identify low-exposure neural rescue with compound identity and age measured.
