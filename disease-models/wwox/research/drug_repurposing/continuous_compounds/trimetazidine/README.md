# Trimetazidine, substrate use and evoked recovery

28 November 2026 UTC. Continuous compound #207, INV-07. context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. [Prespecified comparison](pretest.md) · [Opening primary abstracts](first_comparison.md) · [Computed comparison](comparison.tsv)

**Disposition.** Retain trimetazidine as a conditional comparator of mitochondrial stress and substrate use. Defer pediatric WWOX priority. The best-known thiolase experiment leaves high-substrate inhibition unquantified. Independent adult human cardiac results place a limit on transfer of the rat cardiac flux result. Rabbit myocytes separate similar suppression of fatty-acid-supported respiration from substantially different pore responses. Neither a neural thiolase-engagement measurement nor an age-relevant free neural exposure is available in the checked sources.

**Reproducible counterexample.** In a Michaelis–Menten illustration, use the same treated rate curve in two models and an identical untreated rate at 2.5 µM. In the competitive model, treatment raises apparent Km from 1.25 to 5 µM with fixed Vmax. In the noncompetitive model, treatment halves Vmax at fixed Km of 5 µM. Both have treated rate 0.20 and untreated rate 0.40 at 2.5 µM, and both treated rates reach 0.45 at 15 µM. At 15 µM the **remaining inhibition versus the matching untreated condition is 18.75% in the first model and 50% in the second**. These values are chosen mathematical examples, without fitting figure points. In the limiting simple-competition case with exactly 50% inhibition at 2.5 µM, increasing substrate to 15 µM leaves at least **14.3% modeled inhibition** for nonnegative Km at fixed free inhibitor. Neither that bound nor the two example percentages estimate the laboratory result. The missing untreated curve and uncertainty are what prevent a kinetic conclusion. Two independent recombinant-protein reports further weaken a single competitive model. Liu et al. assigned MS/MS labeling of a Cys105-containing β-subunit peptide after exposure in a Mistic-fusion preparation. Fould et al. found no detected inhibition of any of the three activities in recombinant **human** trifunctional protein across their assays and observed immediate-substrate inhibition above **5 µM** even without assigning a drug effect. These abstract-level observations do not specify a comparable exposure and protocol for reconciling labeling with functional inhibition. Preparations, substrate inhibition and time dependence belong in any matched assay. An adduct and a functional rate at measured free parent are distinct engagement readouts. [Liu et al. primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18640292/) · [Fould et al. primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20825197/)

## A functional control for the alternative

In permeabilized rabbit myocytes, nominal trimetazidine and 4-bromotiglic acid lowered palmitoylcarnitine-supported state 3 respiration to a similar degree. Recomputed from Table 1 means, the changes are **−22.3% and −25.0%** (reported n=4 per condition). In the separate palmitate challenge, Fig. 4C gives a pore-associated calcein readout of **408 ± 46%** for palmitate alone (n=13), **101 ± 6%** with trimetazidine (n=9) and **387 ± 45%** with 4-bromotiglic acid (n=5). Those group means correspond to about −75% and −5% relative to palmitate alone. Similar suppression of this oxidation measure did not reproduce the pore effect. This comparison does not verify matched occupancy or locate a molecular binding site. It supports measuring pore-linked stress and coupling independently of fatty-acid oxidation. [Dedkova et al., Table 1 and Fig. 4A–C](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3670593/)

## Neural readouts and access

Two mouse PTZ kindling experiments reported lower provoked seizure **behavioral scores** under two higher exposures and no detected score improvement in the low arm. The 2025 paper observed behavior for 30 minutes after challenge, with eight mice per group in Fig. 1 and an exposure-stopping rule after repeated high scores. The low arm reduced hippocampal IL-6 by about **69.4%** versus the PTZ group in Table 1 despite the absence of a reported score difference. In the older study, the low arm also changed homogenate oxidative markers without a score improvement. In the 2025 study, rotarod latency improved in the two higher arms. Later rotarod measurements cannot rule out acute changes in motor expression. The low-arm result shows that a terminal inflammatory marker alone cannot establish mediation of the behavioral result. Challenge counts may diverge under the stopping rule. The published comparisons identified here lack electrographic burden, an unprovoked seizure endpoint and a same-preparation flux control. [Jain et al. abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21318336/) · [Ahmad et al., §§2.4, 3.1, 3.4, 3.6, Fig. 1 and Table 1](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2025.1621729/full)

A patent disclosure measured parent in total rat brain 2 h after single IV exposure. Table 68 gives **35.4–39.5 ng/g** for three rats, mean **37.23 ng/g**, equivalent to **0.140 µmol/kg wet tissue** calculated using the free-base molar mass. The mean of its three matched brain/plasma ratios is **2.33**. This is low-weight evidence of sampled brain-tissue exposure. Age, brain vascular correction, unbound fractions, region, oral steady state and mitochondrial concentration are unresolved. The patent text repeats the trimetazidine label for both groups; this calculation uses only the table explicitly labeled TMZ-treated. The late total ratio cannot support Kp,uu, a dose inference or an assay concentration match. ## One decision-changing WWOX comparison

**Held evidence.** WWOX work reports reduced MCT2 and MPC2 RNA in juvenile mouse brain and human organoids. The global juvenile knockout hypoglycemia is systemic, and high fructose-1,6-bisphosphate in Wwox-null fibroblasts is a pool measurement. Neither shows a glucose-oxidation reserve available to replace long-chain fuel in a juvenile circuit. A mitochondrial defect with limited carbohydrate reserve could reverse the benefit inferred from hearts. [Pyruvate entry dossier](../sodium_pyruvate/README.md) · [Systemic substrate dossier](../fenofibrate/README.md) · [Glycolytic pool comparison](../fructose_bisphosphate/README.md)

**Hypothesis and discriminating experiment.** At measured free parent, trimetazidine improves late ATP-linked evoked recovery through net substitution of oxidized carbohydrate for long-chain fuel. First establish measurable long-chain and glucose oxidation in juvenile WWOX-deficient neural material and matched controls. Compare trimetazidine and a separately validated thiolase perturbation at a matched measured decrease in long-chain substrate oxidation, with identical substrate access and first excitatory and inhibitory recruitment. Pair resolved carbon flux with a direct energy-recovery measurement and late electrophysiology. Include an orthogonal pore or coupling readout to test the rabbit alternative. Compare the same workload under baseline substrate supply and a glucose-dominant condition in which long-chain flux is demonstrably negligible. Treat an acute electrophysiological effect without matched workload as a competing explanation.

- **Supports the substrate-switch mechanism.** The verified thiolase comparator phenocopies the drug's recovery effect where long-chain fuel contributes. Both interventions lower long-chain oxidation, increase glucose oxidation, and improve late recovery while first recruitment is preserved. The benefit contracts when there is no meaningful long-chain flux to replace.
- **Lowers the mechanism.** Recovery or pore protection is drug-specific at matched flux suppression, or it persists when long-chain flux is negligible. A change that tracks reduced recruitment gives a demand explanation. A fall in long-chain flux coupled to weaker ATP recovery would count against the candidate in that preparation even with target engagement.
- **Inconclusive.** Free parent is below assay coverage, first recruitment differs, or the preparations cannot support the relevant fuel flux. In that case a nominally negative response does not test the mechanism.

## Pediatric feasibility and reopening

The European indication is adjunctive treatment for stable angina in adults. The chronic cardiac study used a modified-release formulation. European and French product information cite movement disorders including parkinsonian symptoms, caution for increased exposure with renal impairment and lack of established pediatric safety and efficacy. The French summary reports no identified drug interactions. It does not establish a pediatric CNS interaction study under the antiseizure polytherapy relevant to WWOX. Measured age-matched free neural exposure, juvenile tolerability and a repeatable EEG/functional endpoint remain necessary. Movement and vigilance are separate from seizure burden. [EMA referral](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/referrals/trimetazidine) · [French product information §§4.1–4.8](https://base-donnees-publique.medicaments.gouv.fr/medicament/62611660/extrait)

Reopen on a native substrate-use defect with a feasible free-parent window and function linked to matched flux. An independent reader could change the mechanistic classification by locating an untreated high-substrate curve or the original side-by-side MacInnes assay controls. A different reading of the old enzyme result alone would leave the pediatric decision dependent on neural exposure and function.
