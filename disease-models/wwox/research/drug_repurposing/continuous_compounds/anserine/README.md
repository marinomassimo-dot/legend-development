# Anserine, acrolein capture and intact exposure in WWOX loss of function

28 November 2026 UTC. Continuous compound #199, INV-06. context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. See the [frozen comparison](pretest.md) and [matched buffer kinetics](buffer_checkpoint.md).

**Disposition.** Defer oral anserine as a pediatric WWOX candidate. Retain anserine and its methylhistidine product as a paired mechanistic control if native lipid-aldehyde injury is demonstrated. Matched acrolein chemistry is close to carnosine. The adult plasma exposure to pure anserine gives less than 1% modeled interception even before accounting for competing sinks, and pediatric CSF contains low, cross-sectional concentrations. The priority changes with intact neural exposure or with a functional result that distinguishes the parent from its products.

## Exposure test and quantitative counterexample

With a pre-existing trace acrolein bolus, homogeneous mixing, parent in excess and no competing sinks, capture by anserine alone is `1 − exp(−k × AUC × 0.0036)` for AUC in µM·h. Fraction captured using the three adult plasma **mean AUCs** is **0.095%, 0.232% and 0.775%**. This is a scale comparison for those plasma observations. It is neither an estimate of treatment response nor a ceiling on tissue exposure under all formulations. It assumes parent and acrolein occupy the same accessible volume for the full interval and uses a buffer rate. A nonlinear transformation of a reported mean does not reconstruct the participant distribution. The model needs **50.46 µM·h** intact local exposure for 10% capture of that trace bolus. At equal capture, anserine needs at least **0.879** of the carnosine AUC using the matched buffer rate estimates. That small kinetic advantage makes a true exposure or retention difference more valuable than substituting the methylated analogue in a nominal-dose comparison.

The pediatric paired-detection CSF mean corresponds to **0.0116%** modeled capture over one hour if it stays constant. This is a baseline CSF scenario. In an independent buffer Esterbauer Table II reports a forward acrolein–GSH rate of **121 M⁻¹ s⁻¹** at pH 7.4 and 20 °C. Barski used pH 7.4 and 37 °C and a different assay. These settings support only a sensitivity example. At the CSF parent mean and a hypothetical **1 µM GSH**, the calculated anserine share of first capture is **0.027%** in a two-sink model, rising to **0.266%** with a tenfold smaller GSH rate. Actual colocalized GSH, enzyme-catalyzed sinks, localized parent enrichment and reversible adduct clearance were not measured. Match GSH and parent at the site before applying any of these numbers to tissue. ## WWOX-specific bridge and rejecting observation

Aldehyde production and detoxification can diverge from dipeptide availability. In [P17 mouse hippocampal bulk](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158681), GSTA4 went from 40.37 to 25.44 TPM, ALDH2 from 85.34 to 46.21, and both GCLC and GCLM fell. The directions held across every individual-library omission within this small set (2 controls and 3 neuronal knockouts). GSTA4 is approximately flat in [mouse cortical bulk](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158682), and GSTA4 and ALDH2 increase in [week-15 human cortical organoids](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE156243). In the [independent P17 hippocampal nuclear pools](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158684), Gsta4 rose from 5.32 to 9.41 UMI CPM and Aldh2 was 14.59 versus 14.29, with one pool per genotype. Carns1 declined there, while Carnmt1 was 29.83 versus 30.76. SLC7A11 fell across all three bulk comparisons and the nuclear pools. These observations nominate a hippocampal compartment for testing and weaken a universal deficit in aldehyde clearance or anserine synthesis. They measure neither free GSH nor aldehyde damage or product formation. The available matched disease data used here supply no native acrolein-specific adduct endpoint. **One discriminating comparison.** In prespecified stage-matched WWOX and genetically corrected neural preparations, require excess newly formed acrolein–protein adducts per parent lysine at matched survival and maturation before treating this as a native WWOX signal. Compare anserine, equimolar beta-alanine plus Nπ-methylhistidine, and carnosine, with actual recovered parent and product exposures. Resolve anserine from balenine and the free methylhistidine regioisomers. Pair a parent-derived acrolein conjugate with an independent measure of new protein adducts and GSH conjugates, and confirm preserved physiological inhibition and improved evoked output. The direct-trapping hypothesis predicts an intact-specific conjugate and less protein damage beyond the hydrolysis controls, with the effect scaling with measured intact exposure. If the product arm reproduces it without the intact conjugate, favor a product-mediated mechanism. If both peptides perform similarly after matching intact exposure and GSH, keep carnosine as the established comparator. Parent adduct without lower protein damage or improved evoked recovery warrants chemistry-only status.

**Pediatric feasibility.** The paired CSF study supports analytical detectability, with no intervention, WWOX genotype arm or safety follow-up. Neither small acute adult study establishes maintenance exposure or tolerability in children on background antiseizure therapy. Age-dependent serum CN1 activity measured with carnosine adds uncertainty to extrapolation. A clinical programme has no exposure or functional premise yet. A verified independently measured juvenile neural parent time course or native acrolein adduct phenotype would materially change the next comparison.
