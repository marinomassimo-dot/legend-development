**Research decisions.** Begin with the existing portfolio and active assignments to avoid duplicating a colleague's work. Before looking at the result, write which observation would favor each mechanism and what would lower priority. Prefer raw or processed primary datasets, matched comparisons and an independent system. Record provenance, units, exposure, replication unit and exclusions. Track seizure suppression, developmental change and adverse circuit effects separately. Distinguish tissue abundance, functional target engagement and organism-level response. Request a laboratory review when a contested assumption, denominator or sign could change the next experiment.

**Pediatric feasibility.** For each molecule assess the exact formulation, existing pediatric experience and age range, exposure at the intended site, interactions with background antiseizure medication, tolerability and an interpretable endpoint. Record a gap when evidence is unavailable. Food-supplement status or existing use in epilepsy earns an earlier look, never an assumed exposure, safety conclusion or simplified approval. These are experimental priorities.

**Resume.** Read the latest checkpoint below, the linked dossier and concurrent laboratory assignments. Continue unfinished work. When a further analysis could change the decision, run it. Otherwise record the stop/reopen condition, move to the next justified compound and advance immediately when running. The three-hour recall is a fallback for this same chat, effective around the clock until the operator changes it. Persist results on the shared repository, link the primary data and keep restricted source documents in their permitted location.

**D-β-hydroxybutyrate parked for pediatric trial priority.** The robust signal in human open data is stimulus-dependent evoked cortical gain on the native amplitude scale. WWOX-specific ketone delivery, oxidation and network stabilization remain unverified. **Rufinamide parked for WWOX trial priority.** The treated Dup15q cells started with a different intrinsic phenotype. **Citicoline parked for WWOX trial prioritization.** Mouse evidence supports remyelination in defined non-WWOX settings. Precursor proliferation can also raise myelin markers. **Riboflavin parked for WWOX trial prioritization.** The RNA signal failed independent adult transfer; compound-specific neural rescue was limited even in the positive-control disorder. **Lacosamide parked for WWOX trial prioritization.** Human tissue evidence concerns prolonged firing, with the waveform analysis restricted to persistent spikes under drug. **Triheptanoin parked for trial prioritization.** The tissue, even-chain comparator and ketogenic background determine what a biochemical change means. **Levocarnitine parked for trial prioritization.** A randomized pediatric valproate study supports a short-term difference in ammonia. CNS substrate-specific functional rescue in WWOX remains unmeasured. Three reported WWOX profiles and a CNS-specific control constrain how peripheral measures can be interpreted. **Acetazolamide parked for trial prioritization.** Prolonged discharges remain a mechanistic lead. Pediatric genetic epilepsy series have limited typical treatment duration, and the WWOX phenotype and free central exposure have yet to be bridged. **Thiamine parked for trial prioritization.** Juvenile hippocampal nuclei support lower mitochondrial carrier RNA within broad cell classes, but neither the mitochondrial cofactor nor a selective WWOX flux rescue has been measured. Positive-control disorders and rat brain demonstrate why whole-cell vitamin or PDHA phosphorylation can be misleading. [Compartment and flux analysis](thiamine/compartments.md)

**Clemastine parked for trial prioritization.** A neuronal genetic comparator supports improved thickness in already ensheathed axons. Its conduction rescue used a different drug. The required same-tract denominator for first-sheath rescue remains open in WWOX. Reopen for functional improvement at measured attainable exposure and compatible glial survival. **N-acetylcysteine parked for trial prioritization, retained as a mechanistic probe.** Across three untreated WWOX bulk datasets, SLC1A1, SLC7A11 and SLC1A2 all declined; the decline in SLC1A2 reversed in a pooled hippocampal astrocyte-enriched subset. The next decisive observation is neuronal GSH synthesis coupled to recovered function without sustained glutamate accumulation at matched exposure. [WWOX contrasts and competing mechanisms](acetylcysteine/mechanism.md) · [Pediatric oral and human central measures](acetylcysteine/exposure.md)

**Taurine parked.** [Native-chloride firing](taurine/native_chloride.md), [transient fetal arrival and discordant WWOX transcript contrasts](taurine/migration.md), and a [quantitative human CSF upper bound](taurine/exposure.md) leave a developmental trial unsupported. Reopen for a matched drug-by-WWOX functional response at measured attainable central exposure.

**Brivaracetam parked.** The WWOX series, target-occupancy comparison and astrocyte schedules did not identify selective absolute suppression of excitatory input. An independent direct-firing comparison lowers priority for an intrinsic firing correction inferred from simulation. Reopen for matched absolute excitatory/inhibitory drug responses on relevant patterns or an interpretable WWOX-specific response. **Creatine monohydrate parked.** The transcript screen did not meet its two-model criterion. The healthy-child trial measured water-referenced total creatine. In a separate older SSRI-treated population, an apparent +9.76-point increase versus placebo in resting PCr/total phosphate in the highest arm became −0.10 points with β-NTP as the reference. Direct absolute WWOX metabolite and recovery measurements would reopen the question. The independent review found a negative Gamt contrast under every single-class omission; the estimated share attributable to class weights depends on the weighting scheme.
