# L-asparagine in WWOX loss of function

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Disposition.** Park L-asparagine for pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retain the test for a regional, demand-dependent asparagine deficit. The WWOX data identify pyruvate carboxylase and aspartate supply as measurable candidates. The data do not establish an untreated neural deficit, a central exposure window after oral supplementation, or selective functional rescue. The ASNS deficiency reports include seizure worsening after supplementation and supply no WWOX efficacy estimate.

**Lineage countercheck.** In the same-age neuronal KO hippocampal single-nucleus deposit, one pool per condition, `Pcx` CPM was 24.8 to 15.8 in assigned excitatory and 36.9 to 17.6 in assigned inhibitory nuclei, and 73.9 to 89.5 in assigned astrocytes. `Asns` was higher in the two neuronal classes and nearly flat in assigned astrocytes. For example, excitatory counts were 124 and 119 at different total depths and corresponded to 10.0 and 11.5 CPM. These class contrasts use the same classifier as the held analysis and are technically descriptive. Nuclei are not independent animals, and nuclear counts do not measure active protein or support delivered from astrocytes to neurons. The discordance prioritizes a cell-specific metabolite and translation test over a blanket ASNS-deficiency interpretation. Adult human tissue evidence reports PC in astrocytes and in a subpopulation of neurons. ## Pediatric observations and CNS exposure

- In the two-sibling primary ASNS deficiency report, supplementation lasted 24 months. The authors report no change in clinical seizure frequency or severity. EEG changes were described, with no further improvement over later recording. The clinical course was described as stable, with subjective functional changes in one child. For one of two children, serum values were 3.0 µM before and 5.3, 4.4 and 3.5 µM at 6, 12 and 18 months. Baseline CSF was 2.91 µM without a local reference interval, and follow-up CSF was unavailable. The serum series is not a timed PK profile and has no on-treatment CNS pair. [Sprute et al., Oral L-asparagine supplementation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6531480/)
- The separate primary ASNS deficiency case reported a small initial change in responsiveness followed by irritability, sleep disturbance and worsening seizures requiring discontinuation. This is a relevant adverse observation without a denominator for estimating risk. A different series described low baseline plasma and CSF asparagine in two affected siblings. Such diagnostic observations establish neither the size of an oral CNS increment nor its causal mechanism. [Alrifai and Alfadhel, primary case abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27268761/) · [Baseline biochemical comparator, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25663424/)
- A pediatric leukemia study measured CSF asparagine during systemic enzymatic depletion. In 736 patients and 903 samples, about 28% of samples were at or below the quantification limit of 0.2 µM. Higher serum asparaginase activity was associated with lower CSF asparagine, with incomplete depletion even in the highest bracket. The circulating variable was enzyme activity, and the study tests depletion in a chemotherapy context. It does not calibrate uptake from oral Asn, establish bidirectional transport, or make CSF equivalent to intraneuronal availability. Pediatric reference data also identify medication associations with the CSF/plasma ratio. [Rizzari et al., Methods, Results and Figs. 2–3 captions](https://haematologica.org/article/view/9048) · [Akiyama et al., reference study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23287559/)

The sources examined provide an oral administration precedent in a different rare disease, no reliable post-supplementation CNS exposure calibration, no established effective clinical exposure and no controlled developmental benefit in WWOX. Age, formulation, time relative to exposure, dietary substrate, concomitant drugs, and reference assay matter. Report the seizure observation separately from efficacy. ## Prediction and counterproof

- **Discriminating prediction.** In age- and activity-matched WWOX and corrected preparations at replete starting nutrients, prioritize juvenile hippocampal neuronal and astrocyte-containing systems, with cortex as a regional comparator. A precursor limitation predicts low endogenous production from aspartate or glutamine and a smaller absolute asparagine pool during demand. At measured local exposure, labeled added Asn should increase both the total available pool and the fraction of charged tRNA-Asn before recovery of translation and physiological evoked output. Use separate tracers for added Asn and de novo synthesis and compare absolute labeled amounts alongside total pool size.
- **Counterproof.** A label enrichment shift with stable total Asn and tRNA charging is substrate substitution. An effect tracking other amino-acid influx and mTORC1 despite preserved charging favors exchange. Low charging despite normalization of the Asn pool shifts attention to charging capacity and ATP, for which NARS protein, aminoacylation and activity-linked ATP are independent checks. Suppressed translation despite adequate charging also warrants measuring proximal stress signaling. Record early inhibition and coherent output during the same assay to distinguish recovery from reduced network demand.
- **Reopening criterion.** Require an untreated, cell-specific deficit demonstrated in independently prepared WWOX models. Addition of Asn would then need an exposure-supported effect on charging and absolute function with physiological inhibition preserved. An independent review could change the decision if a source establishes positive on-treatment CNS exposure or a direct WWOX flux and charging deficit. Further review of the current bulk direction and uncontrolled oral outcomes would leave the disposition unchanged.
