# Ubiquinol-10 disposition in WWOX loss of function

**Decision.** Park ubiquinol-10 for a WWOX pediatric trial. Retain the excitatory-cell energy hypothesis and the separate membrane-redox hypothesis. Existing WWOX RNA and non-neural protein data cannot identify a reversible quinone limitation. No WWOX subject, neuron or organoid received ubiquinol in these comparisons. [WWOX data, imaging audit and prespecified alternatives](README.md)

**A secondary deficiency with different outcomes in each cell line.** In GEMIN5 patient dermal fibroblasts, all three lines had lower total CoQ10 and lower labeled precursor incorporation. After supplementation, intracellular CoQ10 reached control levels in P1 and P2; only P1 recovered the full oxygen-consumption response. P3 showed a small pool increase and partial respiratory recovery. P1 alone accumulated DMQ10. Among the two patient lines examined for enzyme activities, only P1 showed a significant reduction in combined II+III after normalization to citrate synthase. The mitochondrial mass markers were themselves lower. These observations distinguish the quinone pool, mitochondrial abundance, and respiratory recovery; the redox form supplied in the cell experiment is described as CoQ10. This is a comparator for mechanism, with one donor per patient and no WWOX intervention. [Cascajo-Almenara et al., pp. 428–430, Figs. 1A–B and 2A–K](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41431-023-01526-2.pdf)

**Pediatric feasibility and exposure.** The protocol of the randomized crossover trial NCT00432744 identifies the actual intervention as liquid ubiquinol. In 17 children with available exposure measurements at the interim report, mean total plasma CoQ10 was 5.44 versus 0.64 μg/mL during active versus placebo treatment (8.5-fold ratio of group means). This is a systemic exposure observation in primary mitochondrial disorders. The posted final motor endpoint included 14 of 24 enrolled children. Kendall’s tau-b was 0.015 (95% interval −0.44 to 0.48; two-sided p=0.95); the quality-of-life endpoint, also 14 children, gave 0.30 (−0.12 to 0.72; p=0.18). Thirteen completed both periods; the scoring rule allowed some disability events to contribute. Exposure and outcome denominators differ: the public summaries cannot pair plasma level with clinical response. The broad intervals allow benefit and harm, and an endpoint requiring motor participation may lose observations in a severely disabled cohort. [Design paper, §§1.2.2 and 1.2.6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3975832/) · [Registry results and participant flow](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00432744?tab=results) · [Reproducible extraction](clinical.tsv)

**WWOX protein evidence.** The held human endothelial WWOX-silencing TMT protein table contains 2,031 rows. Of the seven prespecified genes, only GPX4 (log2 fold change −0.27, adjusted p=0.006) and NQO1 (−0.22, adjusted p=0.00053) have entries by the tested symbols. COQ4 abundance remains unmeasured in this table. Tissue and perturbation differ from untreated WWOX-deficient neural cells. Fig. 2b of the healthy-mouse brain uptake paper also shows that individual values were not plotted, leaving the discrepancy between its stated SEM and test unresolved. [Protein-table extraction](protein.tsv) · [Mouse imaging](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5636788/figure/Fig2/)

**Observation that would change the decision.** Compare WWOX-deficient excitatory neurons and corrected counterparts at matched maturity and demand, with an inhibitory-cell comparator. Measure mitochondrial CoQ per cell and per mitochondrial mass, isolated complexes II and III alongside linked II+III, and ATP recovery after activity at measured ubiquinol exposure. A selective baseline deficit in the linked reaction with preserved individual activities, coupled with restoration of ATP and neural function on treatment, supports a quinone bottleneck. If the pool normalizes while ATP recovery remains impaired, that weakens respiratory replacement. If lipid oxidation falls with preserved neural function despite unchanged ATP, examine the membrane-protection hypothesis independently. Pediatric formulation precedent makes dosing studies conceivable; target engagement in WWOX and a measurable neurological endpoint remain open.
