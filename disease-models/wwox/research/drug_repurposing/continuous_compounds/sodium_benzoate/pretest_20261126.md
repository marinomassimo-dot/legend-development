# Sodium benzoate · prespecified co-agonist and DAO comparison

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · frozen before extracting the WWOX RNA panel

**Selection.** Sodium benzoate has pediatric metabolic-disease experience and tests a new question at the intersection of WWOX circuit excitation and redox handling. The prior glycine branch studied added co-agonist and transporter-dependent adaptation. This branch tests the function of inhibiting D-amino acid oxidase (DAO), an enzyme that consumes D-amino acids and produces hydrogen peroxide. Systemic benzoate can also consume glycine through hippurate formation. Direction at an excitatory synapse is therefore contingent on the local enzyme, free exposure and each co-agonist. The laboratory mentioned sodium benzoate in a prior review of combination treatment for glycine encephalopathy. That review did not isolate the molecule's effect or test WWOX DAO. [Earlier glycine disposition](../glycine/disposition.md) · [Laboratory combination review](../../advisor_pilot_20261113/brainstorm_20261116/it_ketamine_lanes_20261117/CORRECTIONS_AFTER_BLIND_VERIFICATION_IT_20261117.md) · [Mouse DAO-occupancy study, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28780732/) · [Rat hepatic/plasma amino acids, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1931156/)

**Fixed screen.** Compare DAO, SRR, SLC1A4, SLC1A5, SLC7A10, SLC6A9, GLDC, AMT and GLYAT in P17 mouse cortex and hippocampus and week-15 human organoids, with one-library omissions. Report mean abundance and number of libraries with a positive value. Inspect the same panel for absolute read depth and descriptive CPM in classified pooled hippocampal nuclei. The datasets are reuse of [held fingerprinted WWOX inputs](../d_mannose/inputs.tsv), with human shared lines and one nuclear pool per genotype. A positive expression comparison only motivates a flux assay. Persistent low or inconsistent detection in all available target preparations weakens local DAO as a near-term causal proposal. Transcriptomic absence alone cannot rule out sparse or long-lived protein, cerebellar activity, or untested stages.

**Discriminating prediction.** In developmentally matched WWOX-deficient and corrected preparations, first establish measurable D-serine turnover and suppressible peroxide production. At measured free benzoate, compare with a selective DAO inhibitor at matched inhibition of labelled D-serine turnover. Measure free extracellular D-serine and glycine, peroxide-related response, NMDA charge and evoked inhibitory recruitment. Optional mechanistic separation uses matched extracellular co-agonists and independently checked assay interference. A local oxidative benefit predicts less DAO-linked peroxide and selective functional rescue which persists under matched co-agonists. An excitatory cost predicts increased NMDA charge or recurrent firing as D-serine accumulates, with the difference shrinking when co-agonists are matched. Changes only with glycine depletion in a peripheral-to-neural comparison support a systemic mechanism. Matched sodium, osmolarity and evoked drive constrain nonspecific effects.

**Continuation criterion.** Keep trial priority low unless developing forebrain target engagement, an attainable parent exposure and selective functional benefit can be connected. Pediatric experience treating a metabolic indication is separately useful for assessing formulation and tolerability. A combination case cannot establish an independent effect in this disease.
