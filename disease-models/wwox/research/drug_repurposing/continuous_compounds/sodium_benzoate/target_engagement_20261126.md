# Sodium benzoate · target engagement, species and time dependence

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · targeted partial reads and arithmetic

- **Chemical counterexample.** Sershen et al., Table 3, compares D-serine alone to pretreatment with benzoate plus D-serine. Group brain D-serine was 0.45 ± 0.14 versus 0.45 ± 0.10 µmol/g, n=4 per group, at one time, with no benzoate-only group in Table 3. Using pooled sample SD under the independent equal-variance model, the approximate 95% difference interval is −0.210 to +0.210 µmol/g (`2.446912 * sqrt((0.14**2 + 0.10**2) / 4)`). Rounded summary data and assumptions matter. The value does not exclude a substantial change and the tissue measurement does not resolve synaptic D-serine. Brain D-serine was also tested under a different D-serine regimen from the behavioral combination in Fig. 4. [Sershen et al., benzoate Results and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4775346/)
- **Pediatric metabolic precedent.** Barshop's abstract reports intake-correlated urinary hippurate in both metabolic indications, and lower circulating glycine only in hyperglycinemia. An early neonatal-onset case showed serum normalization with persistent elevated CSF glycine. Van Hove reported carnitine deficiency in three of four tested children from five neonatal-onset cases, and identified benzoylcarnitine in CSF, urine and plasma. A measured conjugate is evidence of metabolism, with no inference of free parent in brain from that result. In a separate 14-adult crossover, Lennerz et al. Fig. 3 shows rapid plasma parent/hippurate changes. The reported ~446 µM is hippurate, and the 2/14 parent non-detects after the water drink and 8/14 after the glucose drink constrain interpretation. These are total circulating values during acute exposure with two beverage conditions. [Barshop et al., abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2501586/) · [Early NKH single case, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8165914/) · [Van Hove et al., abstract](https://doi.org/10.1002/ajmg.1320590410) · [Lennerz et al., Clinical Study and Process Measures, Fig. 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4289147/)
- **Time dependence worth distinguishing.** Caldinelli et al., Fig. 3 and pp. 1504–1506, report a kinetically estimated FAD dissociation constant of 10 versus 0.5 µM without versus with 1 mM benzoate, under their purified recombinant protein conditions. FAD off-rate fell from 0.0074 to approximately 0.0005 s⁻¹. In EGFP-DAO-overexpressing U87 glioblastoma cells at 10–100 µM nominal benzoate, the D/(D+L)-serine ratio shifted at 24 h and the difference was not significant at 48 h. They saw a small increase in active DAO at 48 h in the pLG72 cotransfection condition, Fig. 6C. This is a continuous-treatment transformed-cell comparison, without washout data or primary neural replication. The new test would measure parent, catalytic flux and protein through washout. Simple reversible inhibition predicts return of labelled D-serine turnover to baseline when parent falls. Persistent stabilization predicts a transient rise above baseline with more catalytically competent DAO after verified removal of parent, possibly with higher peroxide. If local activity is unmeasurable, this timing prediction is untestable. [Caldinelli et al., pp. 1504–1506 and 1509, Figs. 3 and 6](https://irinsubria.uninsubria.it/retrieve/handle/11383/1718832/1590/ProteinScience%202010.pdf)

No primary pediatric unbound parent in forebrain was recovered here. Howley publisher access currently exposes the abstract. The PubMed Central XML for Rojas has no body, so species-specific pharmacology remains an abstract-level lead. Continue with known primary regional DAO assays before committing to a phenotypic interpretation.

## Adult human activity is an alternative to rejecting from mouse RNA

Sasabe et al. reported **1.60 ± 0.44** in primary somatosensory cortex and **1.95 ± 0.81** in primary motor cortex versus **34.84 ± 1.03** in cerebellum (nmol D-alanine oxidized/min/mg protein, mean ± SEM; three elderly donors sampled across sites). The corresponding ratios of means are **4.6% and 5.6%**. They did not detect activity in whole mouse cortex (male, eight weeks, three per postmortem condition). The human homogenate assay used added FAD and D-alanine; the histochemistry used D-proline. Thus this establishes catalytic capacity under the assay conditions, not endogenous D-serine turnover. Adult human histochemistry described the fimbria and weak signal in the molecular layer with absent reactivity in pyramidal and granule layers. The mouse had only a slight fimbria signal. Non-identical cortical sampling and age further limit the cross-species comparison. [Sasabe et al., Methods and Figs. 1A/D, 2A–E](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnsyn.2014.00014)

Jagannath et al. found no significant age-adjusted within-region correlation between DAO RNA and protein in six postmortem regions and an age-group effect on frontal cortex protein. The cortical protein was compared across prenatal, 0–2 years and adult groups but there were no ages 2–20 years and the extraction precluded catalytic measurements. Gene detection is therefore an unreliable direct gate for human cortical capacity. Change the planned model choice: retain an early human astrocyte-containing or white-matter-relevant preparation with a confirmed DAO positive control, alongside the neuronal comparison. Require a direct catalytic baseline before ascribing any effect to DAO. [Jagannath et al., Results, Fig. 3F and Discussion](https://doi.org/10.3389/fnana.2017.00031)

## A developmental alternative within the same molecule

In primary astrocytes from human fetal tissue, Modi et al. Fig. 3F reported increased CNTF in culture supernatant by ELISA with sodium formate as comparator (three reported experiments, donor-level independence unestablished). Mouse oligodendrocyte experiments reported induction at nominal 50 µM, Fig. 2A–D. Fig. 7H/I and Fig. 8 report CREB perturbation and pharmacological PKA-associated evidence in mouse astrocytes. The Fig. 7 Results prose mentions aspirin in the CREB knockdown description although the caption and CNTF response indicate benzoate. Treat mechanistic attribution as provisional. These are expression and medium protein measurements. No functional sheath rescue is established by them. [Modi et al., Figs. 2, 3, 7 and 8 and adjacent Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4644097/)

Before extracting the WWOX counts, CNTF, CNTFR, LIFR, IL6ST, STAT3, SOCS3 and CREB1 were specified as the ligand, receptor and response panel. An activity-matched WWOX–corrected comparison would favor a glial mechanism if conditioned medium changes receptor-proximal signaling and functional first sheaths, and the change is lost on CNTF neutralization without a change in DAO turnover. A lineage marker shift or CNTF abundance without receptor-dependent sheath function leaves this branch low priority. A positive broad CNTF response common to both genotypes does not establish a WWOX-selective rescue. The established WWOX myelin phenotype is the choice of a functional endpoint. [Repudi et al., published WWOX models](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awab174)

**Next discriminating comparison.** Separate missing extracellular ligand from impaired receiver response using matched CNTF addition, benzoate-conditioned medium and CNTF neutralization at comparable electrical drive. Measure early receptor response and the later number and function of newly formed sheaths together. Improvement at matched free benzoate that follows extracellular CNTF and disappears when CNTF is neutralized would favor the developmental mechanism. Successful CNTF neutralization controls with no selective functional improvement would lower priority. Current evidence lacks the corresponding pharmacology and a pediatric forebrain parent-exposure bridge. ## Parent exposure anchors

Lin et al. Table II reports mean total-plasma benzoic acid peaks of **1.26, 6.78, 18.51 and 56.06 µg/mL** in four single-oral-exposure groups. Using 122.12 g/mol for the measured acid, these are **10.3, 55.5, 151.6 and 459.1 µM** (`1000 * Cmax_ug_mL / 122.12`). In the lowest group, six of ten participants had all samples below quantification. Mean terminal half-life was reported for the upper two strata (0.30 and 0.38 h). The paper reports all post-four-hour samples below quantification. The plasma means can be put in the same units as the nominal cell experiments, but they supply no regional free fraction or sustained exposure. For example, obtaining 50 µM free tissue from the highest mean total plasma peak would require a simultaneous free-tissue-to-total-plasma ratio of approximately **0.109**. This is a conditional ratio for the mouse oligodendrocyte assay concentration, with no implication that either the ratio or the time profile is achievable in a child. [Lin et al., Results, Table II and Discussion](https://www.syneurx.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/p_22_Oct.pdf)

Lin et al. cite a greater-than-50-fold CNS rise under an IV dose increase in the Discussion. Their cited Batshaw primary abstract describes one child with citrullinemia receiving **both** benzoate and phenylacetate, with reported parent in blood and CSF during escalation of the daily regimen. It separately describes mouse experiments. The accessible primary abstract does not give a quantitative free forebrain ratio, and the 2022 trial collected its own plasma data. The paper's strong general statement about CNS bioavailability does not supply the required pediatric oral exposure bridge. A separate 2025 study reports total whole-brain benzoate after systemic administration in mice by targeted metabolomics (Fig. 3F/G, four mice per group). Use the exact source data, units, perfusion status and matched plasma, if reported, before attempting a regional-free comparison. [Lin Discussion and ref. 42](https://www.syneurx.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/p_22_Oct.pdf) · [Batshaw et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3374991/) · [Li et al., Fig. 3F/G and brain-tissue method](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-025-03164-0)

The publisher's Fig. 3G labels the matrix **ng/mg** of brain tissue. Calibrating the raster by the 0 and 0.15 tick positions (approximately y=772 and y=583 on the 1,676×2,500 source PNG), the two benzoate-treated group bars (approximately y=692 and y=674) give **0.063 and 0.078 ng/mg**. These are digitized group means. Multiply by `1000 / 122.12` to obtain approximately **0.52 and 0.64 nmol/g** on a benzoic-acid mass basis. The salt-mass convention would lower both conversions. An illustrative tissue-volume conversion of 1 mL/g gives submicromolar whole-tissue equivalents, roughly **78–97 times lower** than the 50 µM nominal mouse oligodendrocyte condition. Whole-brain total at one collection cannot bound a brief peak, a local compartment concentration, or an intracellular free concentration. The tissue extraction and Figure 3 do not provide a matched plasma profile. This particularly weakens sustained-exposure extrapolation. It also makes a pulse/washout or accumulated-effect test more informative than a sustained high nominal bath comparison. [Li et al., publisher Fig. 3F/G](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-025-03164-0/figures/3) · [Modi et al., Fig. 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4644097/)

**Next decision.** Resolve collection timing and calibration for the whole-brain result and look for a paired regional or free exposure series. With no new exposure anchor, retain benzoate as a mechanistic comparator and close trial prioritization. A measured attainable short pulse paired with selective DAO turnover or CNTF-dependent functional recovery would justify advancing the corresponding assay.
