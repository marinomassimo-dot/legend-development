# Sodium benzoate · central exposure and decision

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Disposition.** Parked for pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retained as a mechanistic comparator. The checked evidence supports time-limited cerebellar target engagement in mice and permits substantial total cortical exposure in a high-exposure animal condition. The pediatric precedent and the WWOX datasets leave regional free exposure, developmental target engagement and genotype-selective function unresolved. [WWOX and assay rationale](target_engagement_20261126.md)

## Cerebellar engagement with measured circulating parent

Howley et al. Figs. 2–5 show that the lower benzoate arm included in the occupancy time course had **36 ± 18.2 µg/mL** reported plasma parent at **2 h**, when the visually estimated cerebellar occupancy was **about 72%** and total cerebellar D-serine was **about 11 versus 7 nmol/g** in vehicle. At 4 h plasma was below quantification and the occupancy point estimate was about 42% with wide error bars. In the higher arm, the 2 h plasma mean was **596 ± 24.5 µg/mL** and occupancy approximately **96%**. At 6 h D-serine in the higher arm was approximately **17 versus 5 nmol/g** in vehicle. The 2 h cerebellar D-serine contrast in the lower arm was marked significant, with no corresponding marked plasma D-serine contrast in Fig. 2. No inference is made about covariance or individual values because the available panes show group summaries. The public preview did not expose the sample sizes, type of dispersion or assay definitions. Fig. 7 has a cerebellar concentration curve for the distinct reference compound PGM030756. Fig. 5 reports plasma for benzoate. [Howley et al., abstract and publicly viewable Figs. 2–5 and 7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11064-017-2367-9)

The 36 µg/mL mouse plasma snapshot is in the same range as the highest adult healthy-volunteer group mean peak of **56.06 µg/mL**. Depending on whether the Howley assay expresses salt or acid mass, 36 µg/mL corresponds to approximately **250 or 295 µM total** (divide by 144.10 or 122.12 g/mol and multiply by 1,000). The adult value is reported as acid at approximately **459 µM total**. This overlaps in a circulating matrix and makes central engagement a plausible research question. It leaves duration, unbound exposure and target engagement in developing human forebrain unresolved. A cerebellar effect with a delayed biomarker argues for timed sampling in a neural pulse-and-washout comparison. [Adult phase I, Table II](https://www.syneurx.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/p_22_Oct.pdf)

## Reconstruction of the earlier pediatric citation

Batshaw et al. Methods specify two sampling occasions roughly a month apart, with CSF collected **about 4–5 h after an overnight gastrostomy infusion** containing both agents had stopped. Table 1 gives an approximately **58-fold** increase in the reported CSF benzoate number and a **2.83-fold** increase in the plasma number. The public text-rendered table is sufficient to check the within-matrix ratios. Absolute units for the benzoate subcolumns need verification against the printed table, so absolute pediatric values are withheld. The reference is a combined enteral regimen in one child with a different metabolic disorder and symptoms during escalation. Lin's description of the historical result as a single IV increase should be corrected in the laboratory record. [Batshaw et al., Methods and Table 1, pp. 369–370](https://doi.org/10.1203/00006450-198804000-00006) · [Public rendering of the primary tables](https://www.academia.edu/67546520/Effect_of_Sodium_Benzoate_and_Sodium_Phenylacetate_on_Brain_Serotonin_Turnover_in_the_Ornithine_Transcarbamylase_Deficient_Sparse_Fur_Mouse) · [Lin et al., Discussion and ref. 42](https://www.syneurx.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/p_22_Oct.pdf)

The separate Batshaw mouse study sampled plasma and cortical homogenate **1 h after intraperitoneal administration**. In its high benzoate condition, cortical total was **2,239 and 2,581 nmol/g** in the OTC-deficient and control groups, respectively, with concurrent plasma **6,362 and 5,627 µM**. The cortical-to-plasma ratios of group means were **0.352 and 0.459 mL/g**. The lower condition in the deficient group was **29 nmol/g** cortex with **85 µM** plasma (ranges included zero), and cortical benzoate was undetectable in controls. Consequently the high-to-low cortical concentration ratio was about **77** in the deficient group, with an undefined control fold change. This large change involves the same reported parent, a fivefold administered amount, one route, one time point and two distinct adult mouse groups. It establishes neither pediatric transfer nor a forebrain free exposure. Plasma free tryptophan and cortical serotonin turnover also changed under some conditions, giving a systemic alternative to interpreting a behavioral effect as DAO-specific. [Batshaw et al., Tables 5–6 and Discussion, pp. 371–373](https://doi.org/10.1203/00006450-198804000-00006)

A separate rat radiotracer study reports, for its sodium arms, **0.12, 2.34 and 791.65 µg-equivalents/g** of brain radioactivity at 6 h and **0.03, 0.33 and 1.84** at 48 h (three male Wistar rats per cell). The brain-to-blood ratios of group means span **0.308–0.536** across those rows. The tracer measures parent plus products and the plasma fractionation distinguishes hippurate from parent only in plasma. The homogenate values cannot be used to estimate a free parent concentration or a human partition coefficient. This and the timed Batshaw result prevent using Li's untimed low whole-brain result alone as a maximum. Li's targeted method and public Fig. 3G do not provide a paired plasma or a last-administration-to-collection interval. The supplementary file remained inaccessible through the checked public endpoints. [Kurebayashi et al., Tables 3–8 and plasma TLC](https://doi.org/10.1248/jhs.45.391) · [Li et al., Fig. 3G and methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-025-03164-0)

The conversions in [central_matrix_replay.tsv](central_matrix_replay.tsv) use group means. Cortical pmol/mg equals nmol/g, and dividing by plasma µmol/L gives mL/g. Tracer brain and blood concentrations use the same mass units, so their ratio is dimensionless. The measures describe different compartments and administration histories.

## Pediatric feasibility and reopening observation

A 12-week open-label pilot in a pediatric neurodevelopmental population combined oral benzoate with new daily communication training in six children. The abstract and Table 1 give ages about 3½–9½ years, while the Methods give a slightly different upper age. The authors described activation or disturbed sleep in **3/6**. Although nobody withdrew for that reason, the exact binomial interval for 3/6 is about **12–88%**, before considering the uncontrolled design, concurrent training and background treatment. These observations supply feasibility and interpretability considerations. Drug-specific effect, seizure benefit, WWOX applicability and interactions with background antiseizure therapy remain unresolved. [Yang et al., pp. 1–4, Methods, Table 1 and Discussion](https://doi.org/10.4172/2161-1025.1000192)

Reopen DAO prioritization for an exposure-supported alteration in labelled D-serine turnover in developing genotype-matched forebrain with a selective functional benefit and preserved inhibitory recruitment. Measure peroxide under matched co-agonists and check whether the effect reverses or overshoots after verified removal of parent. Reopen CNTF prioritization if measured extracellular CNTF and receiver response accompany functional first sheaths and neutralizing CNTF removes the effect at matched electrical drive. Quantitative central sampling would change the useful concentration window. No independent laboratory conclusion is claimed for these results.
