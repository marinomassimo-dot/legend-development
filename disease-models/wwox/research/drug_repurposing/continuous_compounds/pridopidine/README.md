# Pridopidine and productive ER-to-mitochondrial calcium coupling in WWOX loss of function

**28 November 2026 UTC.** Retain pridopidine as a conditional ex vivo SIGMAR1 perturbation and defer systemic pediatric WWOX priority. The [pretest](pretest.md) compares improvement in ATP recovery at fixed neuronal work with workload reduction, matrix calcium retention or a calcium-independent effect. ER is endoplasmic reticulum. S1R denotes the sigma-1 receptor encoded by SIGMAR1.

## Adult target engagement and pediatric feasibility

- In the open-label Grachev PET study, mean S1R occupancy at the highest tested single exposure was 91.2% in three healthy men and 87.4% in three adult men with HD. In a separate four-person healthy group, mean striatal D₂/D₃ occupancy under the same exposure was 3.3% (range 1.8–6.1%). This is evidence for adult central target binding across two interrogated receptor families. It does not measure WWOX S1R function, unbound neural parent, subcellular exposure or an effect on matrix calcium. [Grachev, Results Pharmacokinetics and PET, Tables 1–4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-020-05030-3)
- The adult total plasma Cmax of 589 ng/mL combines healthy and HD participants across study arms at that exposure (6 and 3). The same paper cites 618 ng/mL at steady state in an adult HD trial. Converting with parent mass 281.42 g/mol gives **2.093 and 2.196 µM** total plasma. This places the nominal 1 µM cell condition below, and the 5 µM condition about 2.28–2.39 times above those total-plasma means. Matrix, duration and protein binding differ. An occupancy result cannot bridge the 5 µM ATP-linked cellular finding to juvenile therapeutic tissue exposure. [PK source](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00259-020-05030-3) · [Parent molecular mass](https://www.guidetoimmunopharmacology.org/GRAC/LigandDisplayForward?ligandId=13833) · [Unit replay](exposure_replay.tsv)
- PROOF-HD randomized 499 adults. At week 65, the TFC difference was −0.18 (95% CI −0.49 to 0.14, p=.27, 250 active and 249 placebo ITT). The key secondary cUHDRS difference was −0.11 (−0.40 to 0.18, p=.454, Fig. 2b reports 242 active and 247 placebo). Serious adverse events were reported in 13.6% on active drug and 8.4% on placebo. These are observed arm percentages, with causality assessed separately by investigators. Analyses by absence of antidopaminergic medication during follow-up can condition on a post-randomization characteristic. The EMA did not find the subgroup sufficient to establish efficacy, and the company withdrew the application on 7 November 2025. [PROOF-HD Fig. 2, Safety and Extended Data](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-025-03920-3) · [EMA assessment summary](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/nurzigma)
- In the HEALEY ALS platform trial, the full analysis used 120 active and 164 shared-placebo adults. The 24-week joint function and survival disease-rate ratio was 0.99 (95% credible interval 0.80–1.21). PROOF-HD and HEALEY constrain claims of general functional benefit despite target binding. Their outcomes do not test WWOX or share a common endpoint suitable for pooling. [HEALEY, Fig. 1, Fig. 2 and primary efficacy](https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2024.26429)
- The EMA pediatric investigation plan describes an open-label PK and safety study for ages 10 to under 18 in juvenile HD. The plan describes future evidence, with a different disease and age range from early WWOX-DEE. No measured juvenile unbound brain exposure or WWOX target engagement was identified in this bounded comparison. The EMA adult assessment identifies CYP2D6 phenotype, renal impairment and QTc as exposure or safety considerations. Controlled pediatric work would require a distinct local-exposure and safety justification, and direct attention to functional readouts affected by background polytherapy. [EMA pediatric plan pp. 4–5](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/pip-decision/p-0364-2024-ema-decision-25-october-2024-acceptance-modification-agreed-paediatric-investigation-plan-pridopidine-hydrochloride-emea-003174-pip01-21-m02_en.pdf) · [EMA withdrawal assessment](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/nurzigma)

## A single discriminating comparison

**Observation.** The WWOX data contain a modest, model-dependent SIGMAR1 transcript change and discordant channel changes. Adult PET shows receptor binding. The adjacent primary models report contacts, bulk calcium and respiratory or potential readouts under different conditions.

**Inference.** The first comparison should estimate transfer efficiency at a measured source, removal and energy demand. A change in apposition alone is too far upstream to select the next experiment.

**Hypothesis.** A WWOX-LoF preparation with a baseline deficit in productive coupling retains enough functional S1R for pridopidine to improve early matrix uptake per measured ER release, followed by better ATP recovery without sustained loading. The effect should shrink with verified SIGMAR1 interruption and with an independent, verified reduction in matrix uptake. Baseline failure of transfer, excessive removal, impaired removal and reduced drive can yield different drug responses.

**Discriminating observation.** Use developmentally matched WWOX-LoF and corrected preparations and vehicle or verified local free parent at the same evoked demand. Preserve initial recruitment and match integrated activity over repeated challenges. Record source ER depletion/recovery, cytosolic calcium, matrix rise and decay, mitochondrial potential and subsequent ATP recovery in aligned windows. Account for mitochondrial mass, surviving cells and sensor dynamic range. Use time-matched thermodynamic and respiratory controls when interpreting matrix entry. In the same comparison, verify that the SIGMAR1 and matrix-entry perturbations leave enough initial recruitment, ER release and respiratory reserve for meaningful contrasts.

- Faster early matrix uptake at comparable source and recovery of ATP at equal demand support the hypothesis if the effect depends on both perturbation controls and closes a baseline WWOX deficit.
- The same improvement with matrix entry reduced supports a calcium-independent route if the perturbation engaged its target. A larger source with unchanged normalized transfer supports a source effect. Apparent ATP benefit lost on workload matching supports reduced demand.
- A normal or higher baseline transfer, delayed clearance with persistent matrix calcium, worsening potential or slower ATP recovery lowers priority. An increase in the matrix peak alone is ambiguous.

**Checkpoint and next step.** Ex vivo conditional probe only. If the model cannot establish a baseline deficit or reproduce aligned source, matrix and ATP measures at fixed work, stop this comparison. Reopen systemic research priority only with native rescue, a fit-for-purpose juvenile exposure bridge and a defensible safety margin. Independent review has a decision to test if it identifies actual juvenile neural exposure or a native WWOX calcium/ATP dataset at matched work. The next molecular increment starts after this checkpoint and a fresh overlap check against the roadmap.
