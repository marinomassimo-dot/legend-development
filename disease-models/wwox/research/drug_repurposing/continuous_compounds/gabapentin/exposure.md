# Gabapentin concentration, circuit and timing

**Prespecified extension.** A low-concentration effect in a TSP4-stimulated sensory–spinal circuit would weaken an exclusion based only on the concentration used in retinal cultures. To transfer that mechanism, first ask whether THBS4 RNA is consistently increased in the untreated WWOX datasets, whether the direction survives omission of a library and whether the separate hippocampal nuclei pool shows a corresponding source. A matched functional comparison would still need intact recurrent and inhibitory recruitment and a measured ligand concentration at the affected synapse.

**Human concentration benchmark.** The scanned sponsor tables in the PMDA submission give actual CSF and plasma concentrations in µg/mL for epilepsy studies in Sweden and Finland. In Sweden the 6 h single-dose CSF values span 0.14–0.44 µg/mL (0.82–2.57 µM, five observations). After 12 weeks the Swedish CSF range was 0.22–0.98 µg/mL (1.28–5.72 µM, 19 observations). The Finnish range was 0.22–0.78 µg/mL across nine values; excluding two deferred CSF collections marked by footnote d gives 0.22–0.56 µg/mL. Footnote a on the Swedish page specifies steady state for repeated administration. These ranges are sample observations in adolescent/adult epilepsy trials, not population concentration limits or continuous time above threshold. Lumbar CSF does not measure free synaptic exposure. [Sponsor Table 2.7.2.2, printed pp. 172–173, PDF pp. 9–10](https://www.pmda.go.jp/drugs/2006/P200600026/40007900_21800AMY10106_K106_2.pdf#page=9) · [Calculations](exposure.py) · [Results](exposure.tsv)

**Pediatric bridge.** In 48 healthy children aged one month to 12 years, single-dose peak *plasma* concentrations averaged 3.74 µg/mL in children under five and 4.52 µg/mL in the older group. This age comparison is available in the authors' abstract. Multiplying these peaks by a steady-state CSF/plasma ratio from other participants would mix time, age and regimen. Pediatric WWOX central concentration, duration of exposure and functional response remain unmeasured. [Haig et al., primary study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11361047/)

**Two different synaptogenesis experiments.** The retinal study used 32 µM, 5.59 times the highest Swedish steady-state CSF observation. The mouse sensory–spinal study used just 0.1 µM gabapentin from the start of TSP4 stimulation. After four days the induced excess in excitatory puncta was blocked, while baseline synapse formation was preserved in that preparation. Starting gabapentin two days later failed. Fig. 1E counts 20 neurons per group sampled across wells and cultures. Fig. 2 implicates the presynaptic sensory neuron. Reducing the receptor in the spinal recipient did not abolish the response; the interventions differed in knockdown efficiency. This low-concentration counterexample rules out rejecting the whole mechanism on the retinal concentration comparison. It does not show comparable sensitivity in cortical WWOX circuits or identify whether their inhibitory cells receive sufficient excitation. The nominal culture concentration was applied as a bolus and the four-day concentration profile was not measured. [Eroglu et al., Fig. 6](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19818485/) · [Yu et al., Figs. 1D–E and 2, Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5980510/)

**WWOX ligand countercheck.** Untreated THBS4 RNA increased in the human organoid (+0.72) and mouse hippocampus (+0.70) on the KO-minus-control mean log2(1 + normalized RNA) scale. Both signs persisted on library omission. Mouse cortex was −0.13 with an omission range crossing zero. In independent hippocampal nuclei, Thbs4 declined from 2.49 to 1.93 CPM in assigned cells. Among assigned astrocytes the signal fell from 18.48 to 14.53 CPM, with 17 nuclei positive in each group (417 control, 479 KO). Each genotype has one pool. This supports investigating a stage- or compartment-specific ligand change, with no quantitative estimate of extracellular TSP4 or identification of its cellular source. **Trafficking alternative and cortical check.** In a heterologous CaV2.1/β4/α2δ-2 system, 1 mM gabapentin did not significantly reduce peak barium current at 3–6 h (8 treated, 10 control cells); inhibition at 17–20 h was approximately 45% (12 treated, 13 control). The culture medium contained 800 µM each of leucine and isoleucine and 400 µM valine, which can compete with gabapentin uptake or binding. This preparation demonstrates delayed channel effects. Its concentration, bath composition and readout do not provide a neuronal potency estimate or a universal onset time. In the rat partial cortical injury experiment, miniature EPSC frequency in layer V **pyramidal** neurons was 2.3 versus 1.7 Hz (saline 10 cells, drug 11); amplitude was unchanged. This establishes a recipient-specific readout in acquired injury. Miniature frequency alone cannot distinguish fewer contacts from altered release or establish preserved recruitment of interneurons. [Heblich et al., Figs. 1–2 and Discussion](https://www.homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~ucklado/HeblichCHAN2-11.pdf) · [Li et al., Fig. 6C and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3461125/)

**Decision and next observation.** Keep gabapentin active as a conditional, low-concentration TSP4 probe. First establish whether excess TSP4 protein and newly formed functional contacts coexist in the same WWOX circuit. In parallel compare evoked excitation onto principal cells and onto identified inhibitory neurons, with release and surface channel measures to distinguish contact formation from altered transmission through established contacts. The critical new prediction is that early exposure selectively reduces excess new recurrent connectivity at concentrations within the human CSF benchmark while preserving recruitment of interneurons and pre-existing connections. A trafficking-only or non-selective result lowers trial priority. The immediate in-silico step is to check regional/developmental WWOX protein evidence, the source of the extra THBS4 RNA in organoid, and the independent review on recipient specificity. Pediatric trial prioritization remains unsupported.

**Organoid source sensitivity, specified before calculation.** GSE156243 consists of six week-15 WiBR3 bulk libraries (two WT and four KO). Compare the THBS4 signal with individual astrocyte-associated transcripts (GFAP, AQP4, ALDH1L1, SLC1A3), progenitor-associated transcripts (FABP7, HOPX) and the neuronal marker SNAP25. If THBS4 tracks increases in a set of these markers, a change in lineage abundance or state remains sufficient as an explanation. An increase relative to every marker would strengthen the case for a ligand-selective change. Ratios of mixed tissue do not identify the secreting cell. [GEO series](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE156243)

**Organoid result.** At the primary one-CPM offset, THBS4 increased relative to all seven prespecified markers, with the sign preserved on omission of each library. At offsets of 0.1 and 0.01 CPM six retained that direction. AQP4 reversed: its expression was only 0.22 CPM in controls and 0.74 in KO, making its contrast sensitive to the offset. THBS4 increased relative to GFAP, ALDH1L1, SLC1A3, FABP7, HOPX and SNAP25 under all three choices. This weakens a simple explanation involving a proportional rise in these six transcripts. A rare cell population, regional redistribution and changing expression per cell remain plausible. The source libraries share a cell line and one developmental age. Next seek an independent WWOX measurement that localizes TSP4 protein and newly formed contacts to the same developmental stage and pathway.
