# Gabapentin, new excitatory contacts and their recipient neurons

**Selection before extraction.** An antiepileptic with pediatric partial-onset seizure experience allows a direct test of the astrocyte thrombospondin–neuronal α2δ-1 mechanism. Existing laboratory work flagged a database group annotation that incorrectly suggested direct T-type pore targeting. Here the relevant target is the accessory protein encoded by CACNA2D1. A separate α2δ-2 target and rapid effects on release remain alternative explanations. [Prior annotation audit](../../proton_it18_20261101/README.md) · [Thrombospondin study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19818485/) · [US label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/021129s056%2C020882s057%2C020235s076lbl.pdf)

**Prediction before tissue extraction.** Stage-specific inhibition of new excitatory contact formation could reduce excess recurrent input onto principal neurons. The same mechanism could worsen seizures if recruitment of inhibitory interneurons already lags. Compare formation and survival of contacts, early release probability and delayed evoked transmission by postsynaptic cell class. A selective normalization of excess newly formed excitatory-to-excitatory contacts with preserved excitatory recruitment of inhibitory neurons, evoked responsiveness and survival would favor the developmental hypothesis. Equal suppression of both recipient classes, an already low contact-formation rate, or an exclusively acute release effect lowers priority. Count functional connections per available target dendrite and classify initially silent and AMPA-responsive synapses separately.

**Initial evidence test.** Check CACNA2D1, CACNA2D2, THBS1 and THBS2 across untreated WWOX cortex, hippocampus and human organoids. Contrast prespecified signs with recipient-class target RNA and astrocyte ligand RNA in the independent hippocampal nuclei pools. Concordant elevation would motivate direct temporal measurements. Opposite signs, low ligand counts or dependence on a single library would lower confidence in generalized excess synaptogenesis. Neither measured RNA nor total excitatory puncta identifies the recipient of an individual functional input.

**Untreated WWOX comparison.** CACNA2D1 fell in all three bulk systems (KO minus control mean log2[1 + normalized RNA]: cortex −0.25, hippocampus −0.45, organoid −0.36). The sign held on omission of each library. THBS1 fell in both mouse tissues but rose in human organoids. CACNA2D2 rose in hippocampus (+0.83) and organoids (+1.33); the small cortical change crosses zero on library omission. These results weaken the proposed generalized excess of ligand and α2δ-1. They do not measure extracellular thrombospondin, synaptic surface protein or contact formation. The organoid comparison has two WT and four KO libraries. [Twelve bulk contrasts](bulk.tsv)

**Recipient-class countercheck.** The independent hippocampal nuclei pools reverse the aggregate Cacna2d1 direction (679 to 716 counts per million among assigned classes). In assigned excitatory cells, Cacna2d1 was 819 to 834 CPM, and in inhibitory cells 602 to 750. The inhibitory detection fraction *fell* from 135/246 to 156/293, so a rise in normalized counts alone is a poor case for more target-positive interneurons. For Cacna2d2, the excitatory-class signal increased by both CPM (8.65 to 42.91) and fraction detected (70/1596 to 221/1262). Astrocyte Thbs1 and Thbs2 together account for two molecules in each pool. With one pool per genotype, these are descriptive. A class label identifies the nucleus producing the transcript. The presynaptic partner of any connection remains unknown. Reproduce both tables with `python3 compare.py /path/to/files /path/to/output_directory`. [Nuclear counts](cell_types.tsv) · [Within-class and composition differences](mixture.tsv)

**Primary pharmacological countercheck.** In retinal ganglion cultures, 32 µM gabapentin during thrombospondin stimulation reduced formation of synaptic puncta. When investigators added it for one day after five days of synapse formation, the existing count persisted. In neonatal wild-type mouse cortex, the large drop in colocalized VGlu2/PSD95 puncta was concentrated in half of treated animals. Neither experiment separated excitatory inputs onto interneurons from recurrent excitatory contacts. The 1.63-fold rise in miniature EPSC frequency in Fig. 5 was in α2δ-1-overexpressing layer IV pyramidal cells. It is a different intervention from the gabapentin exposure in Fig. 6. [Eroglu et al., Results and Figs. 5C–E and 6A–E](https://sites.duke.edu/eroglulab/files/2015/02/2-Eroglu_2009_Cell.pdf)

**Pediatric feasibility and decision.** The 2024 US label covers adjunctive treatment of partial-onset seizures from age three and includes an oral solution. It flags somnolence, pediatric behavioral effects, and additive respiratory depression with CNS depressants. Sedation can confound an activity-only outcome. The label reports a steady-state predose CSF-to-plasma ratio of about 0.2 in epilepsy; age-specific WWOX exposure and receptor-site duration are unknown. This ratio alone cannot establish that the 32 µM culture exposure is attainable during pediatric synapse formation. Keep gabapentin active for a bounded comparison of exposure, recipient-specific effects, and the alternative α2δ-2 signal. Next check measured paired CSF/plasma concentrations and a primary comparison separating receptor-dependent contact formation from calcium-channel trafficking. [US label §§1, 3, 5.7–5.8, 12.3](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/021129s056%2C020882s057%2C020235s076lbl.pdf) · [Single-dose CSF study for follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1373372/)

An independent laboratory review (SCI-GABAPENTIN-RECIPIENT, 24 November) was requested on the identity of recorded neurons and the evidence for preserved interneuron recruitment after treatment in cortical injury or genetic epilepsy models. The source question is whether any published experiment measures both arms of the proposed circuit effect. [Cortical injury comparator](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22766033/)
