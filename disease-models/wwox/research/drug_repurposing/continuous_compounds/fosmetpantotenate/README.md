# Fosmetpantotenate | Test of a CoA synthesis bottleneck upstream of phosphopantothenate

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Opening checkpoint.** The previous D-pantothenate dossier found lower PANK3, COASY and CRAT bulk RNA in two mouse neural preparations and human organoids. The pooled mouse neuronal assignments retained lower Coasy and Crat while Pank3 rose. Fosmetpantotenate is a distinct phosphopantothenate prodrug that can test whether bypassing pantothenate kinase improves CoA flux. It has lower clinical transfer priority than the decisive value of this controlled comparison. No primary PANK deficiency has been shown in WWOX. [D-pantothenate disposition](../d_pantothenate/decision.md) · [Native expression and sensitivity analyses](../d_pantothenate/README.md) · [Primary preclinical source](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192028)

**Prespecified prediction.** At verified intracellular availability of the active phosphopantothenate, a kinase-limited system should show more directly traced CoA and mitochondrial free CoA with the prodrug than with pantothenate, and recover substrate oxidation at matched initial work. Compare stage-matched WWOX-deficient and corrected neural preparations with a PANK2-deficient positive control for the bypass. Persistence of a low mitochondrial free CoA pool despite increased fully retained label in whole-cell CoA would favor downstream synthesis, import or acyl partitioning limits. Loss of phosphate label with retention of the backbone label is compatible with re-entry through native kinase or oxygen exchange. Use the retained phosphate signature and appropriate isotope controls to support bypass. Verify absolute pool and turnover with the tracer fractions. A response confined to the PANK2 control weakens the WWOX hypothesis.

**First comparison.** Recompute the partition of directly retained versus recycled tracer in published brain and liver data, with route, time, species, detection limit and unit of replication explicit. Then compare the preclinical exposure limit with the randomized FORT result and its missing or present evidence of human brain engagement. [Elbaum et al., primary tracer data](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192028) · [FORT, primary clinical report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8246547/)

## First result | Tracer route and randomized control

**Data.** In Elbaum et al., the CoA +6 and +4 signals were already corrected by the authors for 80% enrichment of the starting phosphate label. Retaining phosphate alongside the carbon and nitrogen labels supports entry of intact drug-derived phosphopantothenate. A +4 signal can represent pantothenate rephosphorylation or phosphate oxygen exchange. It does not identify the kinase route by itself. At 24 hours after intrastriatal administration, 50.7% of mouse whole-brain CoA carried the retained signature and 4.3% carried the +4 signature. Thus 92.18% of labeled CoA carried the retained signature. At day 10 in the higher ventricular arm, the two percentages of total brain CoA were 28% and 16%, equivalent to 63.64% of label retained. These are pooled wild-type samples, eight and six animals per time point respectively. They measure labeling of CoA across homogenized tissue with no separate mitochondrial free or acyl-CoA recovery estimate. There was no detected brain CoA labeling after oral tracer in mice. [Elbaum et al., Fig. 10, Tables 7–8 and isotope methods](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192028)

**Exposure countercheck.** The separate Table 6 dialysate experiments estimated parent below the 8.10 nM quantification limit after oral exposure in mice (8 animals). PPA was measured in mouse dialysate (estimated peak 177 ± 97 nM), even though the separate oral-tracer experiment showed no detectable brain CoA labeling. In monkeys after oral exposure, a lower arm showed parent at 221 ± 253 nM in striatal dialysate (4 animals) and the higher arm 1060 and 2060 nM (2 animals). The oral tracer and dialysate experiments used different exposures and therefore do not establish a concentration threshold or an exact reason for the discordance. Monkey dialysate supports extracellular access and does not establish mitochondrial incorporation. [Elbaum et al., Table 6 and brain dialysate methods](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192028)

**Pediatric feasibility and clinical boundary.** FORT randomized 84 people with biallelic PANK2-associated PKAN, including 14 pediatric participants on fosmetpantotenate and 16 on placebo. At 24 weeks the adjusted difference in daily functioning was −0.09 PKAN-ADL points (95% CI −1.69 to +1.51, p=0.9115). The pediatric subgroup also showed no statistically supported effect. The reported mean pediatric change was −0.6 versus +0.3 points. Treatment-emergent serious adverse events were 8/41 versus 6/43 across ages. These include illness-related events and the small study cannot establish rare event safety or pediatric WWOX tolerability. Healthy-child or infant exposure is outside the sample. The study collected sparse whole-blood PK, while the authors report that it lacked an appropriate brain engagement measure. The randomized result weighs against clinical extrapolation while leaving the exposure-versus-mechanism explanation open. [Klopstock et al., table 1, Fig. 2, Results and Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1002/mds.28392) · [Trial registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03041116)

**Inference and current priority.** Brain incorporation can follow direct exposure in a wild-type model. The same route from systemic human exposure to mitochondrial CoA or neural function in WWOX remains to be demonstrated. Retain the bypass comparison as a preclinical question. The progression criterion is a native WWOX free mitochondrial CoA or flux deficit and a genotype-selective rescue consistent with verified intracellular delivery and retained tracer. An independent review could change priority if human exposure data or a cell-specific CoA dataset established target engagement and a comparator. On current data a repeat interpretation would have little power to resolve either missing measurement.

**Cellular positive-control limit.** In a human neuroblastoma model with approximately 70% PANK2 protein knockdown, the high nominal exposure arms raised free cellular CoA by approximately two- to four-fold. In the separate repeated nominal 1 µM condition the authors reported little change in free CoA and a 1.6–2.6-fold rise in total CoA. Fig. 3 reports a triplicate experiment. Free and total CoA were assayed in cell lysates. This is a concrete within-model reason to require absolute free mitochondrial CoA rather than borrowing total-CoA rescue as the positive-control endpoint. The exposure protocols differ in duration as well as concentration. [Elbaum et al., cellular Methods and Results, Fig. 3](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0192028)

**Human exposure check.** The public FORT results record posts the functional and adverse-event endpoints and gives no parent PK outcome or CNS biomarker result. In the published single adult PKAN case, the drug was detected in whole blood for two hours after the observed administration and the reported half-life was 0.3 h. The estimate establishes systemic absorption in that person. Its single whole-blood series does not quantify pediatric brain phosphopantothenate. The registry reports termination after lack of benefit in the double-blind period. [FORT public results record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03041116?tab=results) · [Case study, §3.2 and Fig. 1b](https://doi.org/10.1155/2017/3247034)

**Disposition.** Retain as a conditional bypass probe. Defer pediatric WWOX trial priority. The sources establish different levels of evidence from circulating compound to whole-brain tracer incorporation to function. In the held WWOX record, COASY and CRAT RNA changes are compatible with a downstream limitation as well as a kinase-side deficit. A targeted public search found no absolute mitochondrial CoA pool or traced CoA production in stage-matched WWOX neural cells. Reopen for those data or a reproducible increase of absolute mitochondrial free CoA and oxidative function with verified intracellular bypass. The immediate decision is insensitive to the exact systemic parent peak because neither source measures the mitochondrial endpoint in WWOX.
