# Omaveloxolone | Exposure, recovered function and disposition
context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Decision.** Keep omaveloxolone as a conditional NRF2 probe. Defer pediatric WWOX clinical priority. Brain total parent, expression of downstream genes, recovery of ATP and improvement of network function define different steps. The available comparators establish some of these steps in other systems. The missing observations are free juvenile neural exposure and genotype-selective recovery at matched initial recruitment.

## Exposure and downstream response

Reisman et al. Fig. 3 (p. 1264) sampled cynomolgus brain and plasma 24 h after the final administration in a 14-day oral study, with two animals per sex per arm. The rendered log-axis bars give approximately **3.0, 4.4 and 3.7 mL/g** for total brain content divided by total plasma concentration across the three arms. The FDA nonclinical review reports **3.94–5.27** at 24 h. Timing, species, duration and dose groups are consistent with a closely related comparison. The lower digitized arm misses the FDA range even after the prespecified two-pixel allowance on each bar. No verified per-animal concentrations or explicit publication-to-study-ID mapping establish that the two ratio summaries are interchangeable. Do not count them as two independent replications. In the primary paper Fig. 4, the only statistically marked brain responses reported in the Results were GSR and AKR1C1 in the highest arm. The two-week whole-tissue mRNA results cannot locate the responsive cells or establish increased cystine import and ATP recovery. The EMA describes antioxidant enzyme activity across tissues in a separate regulatory study summary (RTA408-P-1206, p. 23) without enough tissue-by-assay detail there to assign those activity results to brain. [Reisman et al., Figs. 3–4](https://doi.org/10.2147/DDDT.S193889) · [FDA PK/ADME review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/216718Orig1s000PharmR.pdf) · [EMA §§2.5.2–2.5.3](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/assessment-report/skyclarys-epar-public-assessment-report_en.pdf)

The regulatory estimates are about **3.9 nM unbound plasma peak** in the established Friedreich ataxia exposure range. Published neuronal cultures used nominal 50–200 nM, about **13–51 times** the unbound plasma estimate. This division compares medium added to a dish with modeled plasma free drug. The free fraction in the medium and free brain exposure are unknown. As a sensitivity calculation, the primate FDA total ratios and separate EMA mean plasma binding would require approximately **0.99–1.32% unbound in brain** for a free brain-to-plasma ratio of one, treating 1 g of tissue as approximately 1 mL. This estimates a threshold, with no equilibrium, tissue binding or time-integrated CNS exposure assumed to have been measured. Whole-brain binding estimates alone would still not provide cell-specific engagement. [FDA QT review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2023/216718Orig1s000OtherR.pdf) · [EMA, pp. 23–26](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/assessment-report/skyclarys-epar-public-assessment-report_en.pdf) · [Replayed values](comparison.tsv)

The published **94%** refers to median weekly frequency in weeks 9–12, aligned to first spontaneous seizure. The **rounded printed medians** of 23 and 1 imply 95.65%. The **digitized plotted weekly means** average 20.78 and 1.55 over that window (descriptive reduction about 92.5%). Both checks corroborate a large late contrast. They cannot reproduce the unrounded median effect. Nine rats per arm is the study denominator. The figures and text inspected do not specify how the first-event alignment treated animals without a first event. Report the seizure-free contrast independently. The reported OR 4 (95% CI 0.3–49) and one seizure-free control imply 3/9 versus 1/9 if all nine were included in each arm. With those reconstructed counts Fisher's exact p is 0.5765. No per-animal seizure table was found in the checked publisher and university records. A verified event table could settle the analysis denominator, with no bearing on the missing WWOX exposure or genotype comparison. ## Discriminating WWOX comparison

- **Dato.** SLC7A11 RNA was lower across three untreated WWOX bulk profiles in the held screen, while GCLC and GCLM did not have a shared direction. This leaves baseline transporter protein, cystine import and glutathione synthesis unmeasured. [WWOX expression and sensitivity checks](../sulforaphane/README.md)
- **Inferenza.** A general NRF2 signal might precede useful recovery, or it might expose a substrate limit. Increasing system x<sub>c</sub><sup>−</sup> transport can also increase glutamate export, so a glutathione benefit may have a circuit cost when glutamate clearance is limited.
- **Ipotesi e verifica.** At measured free parent, compare vehicle and omaveloxolone in matched WWOX and control preparations during and after the same first patterned activation. Measure an NRF2-dependent response, stage- and compartment-resolved cystine-to-glutathione flux, extracellular glutamate dynamics, ATP recovery and a later evoked response with physiological inhibition preserved. Repeat the critical contrast with matched sulfur availability and an independent NRF2-dependence control. Selective recovery that follows target engagement and energy recovery supports the mechanism. Target-gene induction with unchanged flux and ATP favors substrate limitation or a downstream block. A reduction of first recruitment, a longer glutamate tail, or reduced physiological inhibition lowers priority even if redox reporters improve.

**Pediatric feasibility and reopening.** The US label covers immediate-release oral capsules for Friedreich ataxia at age 16 and over. Relevant limitations include hepatic enzymes, BNP and lipids, plus exposure shifts from CYP3A modulation and effects on CYP3A4 substrate exposure. Those interactions would complicate attribution in an antiseizure combination. The public BOLD and BRAVE records cover age 2–15 in Friedreich ataxia and showed no posted results on 27 November 2026 (BOLD 33 actual, BRAVE 255 estimated). They do not provide a WWOX clinical or juvenile free neural exposure estimate. Reopen for a functional WWOX comparison at measured free parent with baseline defect, NRF2 dependence, matched initial activity and preserved inhibition. A review of the same aggregate plots would refine a secondary number while leaving this decision unresolved. [US label §§5, 7, 8.4, 12.3](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/216718s014lbl.pdf) · [BOLD](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06054893) · [BRAVE](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06953583)
