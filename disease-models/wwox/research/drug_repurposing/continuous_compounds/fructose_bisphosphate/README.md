## Inference

- **The fast current effect has a falsifiable ionic rival.** The Shao study acknowledged extracellular calcium complexation. At fixed voltage, assume as a sensitivity exercise that the retained current is proportional to a power of the free calcium concentration. For the nominal 1.3 mM calcium current bath and 5 mM F1,6BP, the reported current ratio of 0.602 can be produced at free calcium 0.47, 0.78 or 1.01 mM for powers 0.5, 1 and 2. A one-site 1:1 mass balance would require apparent dissociation constants of 2.38, 6.79 or 16.33 mM. These are inverse scenarios. They assume baseline free calcium equals nominal, ignore Mg competition and other solution chemistry, and estimate neither the free concentration nor an affinity from the electrophysiology. Persistence after ion restoration would support an additional extracellular action while leaving direct binding and subtype unresolved.
- **A metabolic response depends on context and timing.** In the chronic pilocarpine mouse model, McDonald et al. measured enrichment from uniformly labeled glucose after five days of intervention. The abstract reports recovery of label in several hippocampal TCA-related pools and higher total GABA regardless of prior status. Whole-tissue GABA can increase while an evoked GABAergic current falls in another preparation. The isotope result admits changed energetics secondary to fewer seizures and fails to identify oxidative pentose-phosphate pathway (PPP) flux or intact F1,6BP entry. In a different model, Liu et al. reported that P7 rat cortical slices under hypoxia–ischemia showed neither high-energy phosphate preservation nor detectable lactate labeling above natural abundance from labeled F1,6BP. The latter constrains direct fuel use in that preparation and leaves other contexts open. [McDonald et al., abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.yebeh.2021.108223) · [Liu et al., abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18725216/)
- **Endogenous F1,6BP need not be deficient in WWOX.** In Abu-Remaileh and Aqeilan Fig. 2F, Wwox-null mouse embryonic fibroblasts have a higher F1,6BP pool (5/5 biological samples as reported). Reading median lines against the original figure axis gives about 2.26 vs 0.69 on the plotted fold scale, a 3.26-fold ratio (2.92–3.67 with ±2-pixel perturbations of the median lines). No sample-level data, absolute concentration or direction of flux was inferred. An elevated pool can coexist with low downstream ATP production. Fibroblast abundance cannot specify the neuronal or astrocytic bottleneck. [Fig. 2F](https://doi.org/10.1038/cdd.2014.95)

**Held WWOX models.** The ten predeclared transcripts mapped after using mouse G6pdx and human G6PD. P17 hippocampus (2 control/3 null), P17 cortex (4/3) and week-15 human cerebral organoids (2/4 libraries) were replayed separately. PFKFB3 falls in both mouse regions and rises in organoids, with every single-library omission preserving direction. PGD rises in cortex, is direction-sensitive to omission in hippocampus and falls in organoids. G6PD falls in organoids while mouse regional intervals span zero. These are differences of mean log₂(1 + native abundance), with mouse TPM and organoid CPM. Direction stability refers to a sensitivity check with both means at least one native unit. The pooled P17 hippocampal single-nucleus matrix gives a G6pdx difference of only +0.13 CPM, with opposing within-class and class-weight terms. There is one pooled control and one pooled null library. Neither these transcripts nor the pooled cells identify flux, glucose uptake, glutathione recycling or biological replication. No direct native WWOX neural flux pair was found in the held inputs. ## Hypothesis and discriminating observation

Use an age-matched Wwox-LoF and reference preparation with documented recurrent activity. Start in one region at a time. Measure free F1,6BP, ionized calcium and magnesium with and without F1,6BP, and match pH, counterions, osmolarity and first evoked recruitment. Compare ion-restored compound, carrier with the ion loss measured in the original compound condition, and matched carrier. Establish the calcium-current relation and quantify native GABAergic function and first recruitment alongside recurrence. If lowering divalents reproduces the early effect and restoring them removes it, the ion chemistry accounts for the rapid result in that preparation. Persistence of an early current-selective effect with stable ions favors another fast extracellular action. An effect on recurrence accompanied by impaired GABAergic function or reduced first recruitment fails the selectivity criterion.

Test a delayed metabolic contribution only if the acute control is interpretable. Compare time-resolved positional glucose labeling, using [1,2-¹³C₂]glucose with natural abundance correction and a dilution and recycling model, alongside NADPH/NADP, GSH/GSSG and ATP recovery. Measure baseline flux before treatment and match activity in a control arm over the labeling window. Run a separate intact-parent tracing arm if judging direct fuel use. If an early current change explains recurrence and tracer effects disappear when matched for activity, downgrade the WWOX metabolic hypothesis. A genotype-dependent correction of a measured flux deficit that persists at fixed ionic conditions and matched activity, with preserved inhibitory and evoked function, would move it forward. Both processes can occur.
