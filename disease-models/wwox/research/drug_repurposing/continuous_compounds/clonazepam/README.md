# Clonazepam and the durability of reticular gating in WWOX loss of function

**Units and preparations.** Juvenile rodent thalamic slice experiments report spikes per evoked oscillation, durations in milliseconds and recording-level SEM in reticular (TRN) and ventrobasal relay (VB) sites. Rat relay-cell experiments report postsynaptic potentials as percentage of control. Wwox-null mouse ECoG reports cortical spikes and spike-wave discharges per hour from approximately P14. Repeated-exposure animal evidence spans provoked seizure thresholds, behavioral seizure stages and motor activity. Pediatric clinical evidence uses child-level seizure and EEG endpoints, with serum or plasma parent in ng/mL.

**28 November 2026 UTC.** Retain clonazepam as a conditional ex vivo comparator for the persistence of reticular gating. Defer WWOX pediatric trial priority. The [prespecified comparison](pretest.md) separates attenuation within one burst sequence from attenuation of the drug effect over days of exposure.

## Durability and pediatric transfer

| Observation | Meaning for this comparison |
|---|---|
| In kindled rats the [Suzuki original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7969807/) reports a larger seizure-stage loss of protection after four weeks of repeated clonazepam. Vehicle-maintained rats receiving the same protective challenge also developed some contingent tolerance. The study reports no detected group difference in the single sampled peak plasma concentration or Bmax, Kd and GABA enhancement of binding. | Keep the stimulation history matched. One plasma sample and region-averaged binding do not establish equivalent unbound thalamic exposure, receptor occupancy or synaptic coupling. The full article and raw data were unavailable on the free routes reached. |
| In mice, the [Haigh original abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2880972/) reports incomplete tolerance on provoked thresholds without a detectable concurrent plasma change in two conditions with quantifiable concentrations. [Galpern](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1652144/) reports loss of motor suppression by day 7 through day 14 with no detected change in total cortical concentration, alongside reduced cortical benzodiazepine binding and chloride uptake. | Different phenotypes, compartments and assays. Motor adaptation can coexist with durable suppression of some seizure patterns. A nonsignificant concentration change is not an equivalence test. The old studies remain abstract-level mechanistic context. |
| In [Hosoda and Miura Methods, Results and Tables 3–5 pp. 117–119](https://doi.org/10.3805/jjes.14.116), 40 children aged 3–14 with cryptogenic focal epilepsy began monotherapy. Thirty were seizure-free the first year. Of those 30, Table 4 reports 25 controlled at maintenance, two uncontrolled and three lost after periods of observed control. Five had recurrence associated with missed treatment. Five of 21 children selected for discontinuation had episodes classified by the authors as withdrawal seizures, two during reduction and three after stopping. | The long-term report documents sustained control in a selected group without a contemporaneous comparator. It gives a counterexample to assuming universal clinical loss of effect. The Japanese text states a mean follow-up treatment duration of 5 years 2 months for the 30-child subgroup, while the English abstract calls this a median. Use the subgroup structure and range, not this conflicting summary. Maintain separate denominators for loss during treatment, rebound and delayed recurrence. |
| [Dreifuss original abstract](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.25.3.255) reports ten children with absence seizures and 12-hour EEGs before and after eight weeks. Total serum parent ranged 13–72 ng/mL. It reports seizure-frequency reduction in eight, no seizures in three and side effects in six. The [Specht original abstract](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1528-1157.1989.tb05326.x) describes 40 children with difficult-to-treat epilepsy and polytherapy undergoing discontinuation and reports mostly transient exacerbation in 19/40. | Both are small legacy cohorts with different designs and endpoints. Neither estimates durable efficacy in WWOX, and transient worsening during discontinuation cannot estimate benefit maintained during exposure. Full individual trajectories and paired initial and late exposure measurements were unavailable. |

At the parent molecular mass of 315.71 g/mol, the absence study's total serum range converts to 41–228 nM. Applying the US label's approximately 85% protein binding gives an **illustrative** 6–34 nM unbound serum range, compared with 100 nM nominal bath in the slice experiment. The protein-bound fraction was not measured in those children. Serum and the neural extracellular compartment are distinct. No age-specific free thalamic exposure or minimum effective concentration follows. The current oral label describes somnolence and ataxia in seizure treatment, secretion and respiratory concerns, additive CNS depression with concomitant depressants, effects of enzyme-inducing drugs on concentrations, development of tolerance, and dependence and withdrawal risk. Its “up to 30%” early-loss statement supplies neither a prospective denominator for WWOX nor an estimate of purely pharmacodynamic tolerance. ## Discriminating observation

A replay of the published aggregate WT retention illustrates the information loss. With five fixed windows each starting from 20 baseline spikes, both uniform suppression and predominantly late suppression give **87 of 100** total. In the latter scenario the first two windows retain **97%** and the last two **75%**. These are constructed sequences, not numerical estimates from Fig. 4. A separate fixed-duration charge toy model gives the same 50% loss from reduced engagement, reduced conductance per engagement or reduced ionic driving force. [Acute results replay](acute_replay.tsv) · [Temporal counterexample](temporal_counterexample.tsv) · [Charge counterexample](charge_counterexample.tsv)

Use the onset localization in a connected WWOX-LoF and corrected preparation as an eligibility observation. Compare acute and repeated exposure at matched local free parent, baseline vigilance or stimulation state, inter-event recovery and fixed first afferent input. Record first relay recruitment and subsequent population bursts separately. Independently track native TRN GABA reversal before and after a matched train, GABA-A conductance and reticular-to-relay late rebound, with site engagement where possible. Preserve the on-exposure comparison through the repeated window. Record off-exposure rebound in a different window. Treat animals or isogenic lines, rather than repeated sweeps, as the unit.

- **Durable reticular gating** predicts that the drug reduces late recurrence and preserves the first evoked response in both windows, with a reproducible genotype dependence.
- **Functional adaptation** predicts a smaller repeated response at matched exposure and engagement. Stable reversal but reduced added conductance supports a coupling change. Stable added conductance with reduced hyperpolarizing drive late in the train supports ionic limitation.
- **Broad suppression or an exposure difference** predicts early loss as well as late loss, or a change in verified local exposure sufficient to leave adaptation unresolved.

**Disposition.** Reopen with a measured juvenile WWOX reticular-to-relay phenotype and persistent late-versus-first separation at verified exposure. An independent review could change the disposition if it locates age-matched TRN evidence or paired acute and repeated thalamic measurements in a public dataset. The retrieved ECoG contrast and historical plasma and cortical data do not close those gaps. Keep the clinical endpoint set actionable only after localization and duration can be resolved.
