# L-ergothioneine · adult exposure and progenitor development

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · targeted source comparison

**Disposition.** Defer pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retain as a transport and redox probe and a stage-specific TrkB comparison. Redox-sensitive fluorescence changes at low nominal exposure, early lineage changes were measured at high exposure, and the in vivo functional result restores endpoints after dietary depletion. Each addresses a different rate limit. The review would change the decision if it recovered an exposure-matched WWOX functional record, or an ordinary-diet developmental experiment separating trophic benefit from loss of native inhibitory control.

## Human plasma and whole blood follow different time courses

Cheah et al. randomized 45 healthy men aged 21–35 across placebo and two pure-compound formulation arms, 15 per arm as reported in Methods. Capsules were taken for seven days. Samples were collected before clinic administration on days 1, 3 and 5, followed by days 8, 14, 21, 28 and 35. The primary assay used LC–MS/MS with labeled internal standards and separate sample preparation for plasma and whole blood. Fig. 3A reports absolute total plasma concentration. Fig. 3B subtracts each person's baseline whole-blood value. Parent in isolated erythrocytes, brain exposure and unbound plasma were not quantified in this study. The bars are group summaries rather than individual kinetics. [Primary study, Methods and Figs. 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1089/ars.2016.6778)

| Study day | Plasma, placebo / higher arm (µM) | Whole-blood change from baseline, placebo / higher arm (µM) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 1.06 / 1.19 | 0 / 0 |
| 8 | 0.99 / 2.91 | −5.0 / +16.6 |
| 14 | 1.02 / 2.31 | −2.3 / +21.0 |
| 28 | 0.95 / 1.55 | +0.8 / +33.4 |
| 35 | 0.99 / 1.58 | −2.5 / +32.9 |

The sampled plasma maximum is day 8. The highest displayed mean whole-blood increment in the higher arm is at day 28, with a near plateau at day 35. Plasma remains about 0.6 µM above placebo at those late visits. The largest unsampled plasma excursion and the red-cell exchange rate are unknown. Basal plasma in the larger study was reported as 1.28 µM across participants, versus 0.47 µM in an independent small analytical cohort previously used for the transporter sensitivity calculation. Baseline concentration therefore needs a cohort and assay attached to it. [PK source raster](https://journals.sagepub.com/cms/10.1089/ars.2016.6778/asset/d7f570c6-aad3-411f-9806-b01a97d4e3e7/assets/images/large/10.1089_ars.2016.6778-fig3.jpg) · [Independent analytical cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23494799/)

**Day-8 discrepancy.** The Results text estimates a whole-blood excess of about 125 µM that day. Panel B instead gives about **21.6 µM relative to placebo** from the baseline-subtracted means, a roughly **5.8-fold** difference. The printed estimate lies above the entire plotted scale. The approximately 2 µM plasma excess in the same sentence agrees with panel A. A different blood normalization or a typographical error could account for the divergence, but the source does not identify one. Use panel B for its own stated contrast. Panel C has creatinine-normalized urine units. A blood, plasma and urine concentration ratio cannot reproduce mass balance, and the authors' urinary fraction is itself estimated from typical adult urine volumes. The healthy-adult oxidative markers were mostly null or inconclusive. [Primary study, Results, Figs. 3 and 6–8 and Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1089/ars.2016.6778)

## Mouse cortical progenitors give two different effects

Ishimoto et al. used secondary cultures from embryonic day 15 mouse cerebral cortex. In Fig. 2F, at **5 µM** nominal parent, DCF-positive neurospheres give about **162% of time-matched control at day 3** without a reported significance mark, then about **56% at day 9** with a mark. Fig. 2C at day 9 gives MTT absorbance about **0.110, 0.091 and 0.077** for vehicle, 5 and 500 µM. Lower MTT can reflect cell number or metabolism. DCF fluorescence does not localize the source to mitochondria and may change with neurosphere size or probe chemistry. The nuclei, viability and functional endpoints need independent counts and native redox products. [Primary mouse study, Fig. 2 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089434)

At **500 µM** exposure during the nine-day expansion phase, Fig. 6E–F show βIII-tubulin-positive cells going from about **11% to 21%** at day 3 after differentiation and **12% to 18%** at day 6. GFAP-positive proportions fall about **89% to 79%** and **88% to 82%**. MAP2-positive proportions have no reported significant change at either time. These percentages are relative to DAPI nuclei, and changes in survival or pool composition can change their denominator. Edaravone and ascorbate reduce the oxidation-sensitive and neurosphere endpoints in the original study but did not reproduce the reported lineage-marker shift. This supports two competing mechanisms, with a direct fate conclusion still requiring lineage tracking and mature output. The literature bath for the lineage shift is approximately **172 times** the highest sampled adult total plasma mean. The 5 µM bath is about **1.7 times** that mean. Neither ratio equates the nominal cell-bath concentration to free brain exposure. [Primary mouse study, Figs. 2–3 and 6](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089434)

Primary mouse Octn1-null progenitors showed approximately **11 versus 37 µL/mg protein** in wild type for apparent radiolabel uptake at 240 minutes in Fig. 1E. The residual signal is about 30% of wild type. With a 500 µM bath, Table 3 LC–MS/MS measured **3,572 ± 629** and **10,687 ± 693 µg/g protein** after six and nine days. The paper's 1.6 and 4.7 mM estimates assume the same cell-volume-to-protein ratio for each condition and do not assign parent to a subcellular compartment. In a separate retinoic-acid-induced P19 carcinoma-derived progenitor-like model, siRNA reduces uptake and shifts lineage markers in the opposing direction. The primary study did not cross exogenous ligand versus vehicle with transporter loss and add-back in the same cortical cultures for the lineage outcome. Thus transporter dependence of primary uptake is stronger than transporter dependence of the whole lineage effect. [Primary mouse study, Fig. 1, Figs. 4 and 7, Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0089434)

## Barrier direction and the WWOX comparison

In Kato et al.'s author manuscript, Table I (PDF p. 39) reports **1.45 ± 0.30 µg/g** whole brain in wild type and **<1.07 µg/g** in systemic Octn1-null animals. The censor threshold is about 74% of the wild-type mean, so the table cannot localize the loss or establish complete brain depletion. WT and knockout plasma were 0.179 ± 0.024 and <0.15 µg/mL. The separate oral radiolabel experiment showed altered systemic persistence and urinary excretion, with renal vesicle evidence for transporter-dependent reuptake. Matching peripheral exposure is essential for a BBB-specific inference from this genotype. [Author manuscript, Results pp. 17–19, Table I and Fig. 3](https://kanazawa-u.repo.nii.ac.jp/record/15359/files/PH-PR-KATO-Y-832.pdf)

In a later mouse ventricular-start design, apparent CSF clearance of labeled ergothioneine was **3.62 versus 1.42 µL/min** in wild type versus knockout and labeled mannitol was **1.59 versus 1.25 µL/min** (Table 1, n=3, means ± SEM in the source). The descriptive genotype difference beyond mannitol is **1.86 µL/min**. The study does not provide the covariance needed to test that calculated contrast. This shows a route for tracer removal from CSF and is compatible with uptake into choroid plexus or other brain compartments. The peripheral-to-CSF entry rate and parent-specific interstitial exposure remain open. This gives a concrete counterexample to interpreting higher OCTN1 expression anywhere as greater extracellular brain availability. [Primary ventricular tracer study, Fig. 1 and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1248/bpb.b24-00451)

**WWOX inference and discriminator.** The held human organoid comparison records elevated radial-glial MYC across engineered and patient lines, whereas cell fractions and progenitor-phase evidence vary across lines. In the clinical-genotype models the prior held comparison identified a late-neuron maturation deficit. The high-exposure mouse shift toward βIII-tubulin without a measured MAP2 gain could shorten the progenitor expansion window and still leave mature neuronal output low. In WWOX and matched corrected organoids, a useful developmental effect should increase the absolute yield of lineage-traced late neurons and preserve the absolute progenitor pool across time, with tracked injury, alongside uptake and later evoked function. An early-marker rise with unchanged late neuronal yield, or loss of the progenitor pool, would reject that interpretation. Test the postmitotic redox hypothesis separately with intracellular parent, native damage products and functional recovery at matched basal activity. The working comparison still has no pediatric WWOX exposure, efficacy or co-medication evidence. [Held human WWOX analysis](../../../../analysis/tx007_genotype_class_ceiling_20260921.md) · [Primary organoid study](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag239)

## Nutritional repletion gives a functional TrkB comparator with a narrower interpretation

In a separate male-mouse experiment, Ishimoto et al. depleted dietary ergothioneine beginning at weaning. Fig. 1A–B after repletion shows plasma about **3.7 µM** and total hippocampus about **5.6 nmol/g**, close to the standard-fed vehicle group (**3.4 µM** and **5.2 nmol/g**). The deficient vehicle group remained near the bottom of both axes. The higher repletion arm improved object/location discrimination. This supports functional activity in a nutrition-depletion contrast at circulating concentrations of the same order as the adult human trial. No ergothioneine arm on the standard diet was shown in this comparison. Fig. 5D, in a distinct depleted cohort, shows the normalized DCX/NeuN-positive area ratio at about **1.0, 2.2, 1.0 and 1.0** in vehicle, parent, parent plus ANA-12 and ANA-12 arms. This is a pharmacological pathway control and an early marker area ratio with a variable denominator. It does not establish mature neuron production, direct TrkB binding or an effect on WWOX. [Primary repletion study, Figs. 1, 4–5 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41538-024-00250-5)

A secondary analysis of an adult food-extract tablet trial (23 placebo and 25 active) reports higher p-TrkB/TrkB in **serum extracellular vesicles** at week 12 (Fig. 7B). SNAP25-positive vesicles were demonstrated in a subset, while the arm comparison used the mixed serum EV preparation. No central tissue, vesicle-specific brain abundance or pure-compound contrast is established. The correlations across four repeated visits and many cognitive domains (Fig. 8, Table 1) are exploratory with within-participant dependence and multiple testing. A separate pure-compound MCI pilot enrolled only 19 adults aged at least 60. Its accessible abstract reports routine safety-marker results which cannot establish pediatric WWOX tolerability or treatment interactions. Adult pure formulation and food-extract results remain separate. Food-safety assessments support the feasibility of a standardized formulation under specific food-use conditions and give no WWOX CNS exposure or pharmacodynamic threshold. [Adult vesicle source](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41538-024-00250-5) · [MCI pilot abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39544014/) · [EFSA food-use assessment](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2017.5060)

The targeted WWOX RNA comparison gives **NTRK2 33.1 → 200.7 CPM** in the small week-15 human organoid series (log2(1 + CPM) contrast **+3.11**, single-library omission **+1.46 to +4.75**). Mouse P17 cortex and hippocampus both move down on their own TPM scales, and the pooled mouse excitatory nuclei move **473 → 337 CPM**. Mouse cortex and hippocampus show less **SLC12A5/KCC2** in bulk (**215 → 137** and **109 → 75 TPM**), while the pooled excitatory signal is near stable and human organoids rise (**17.8 → 30.1 CPM**). Human **SATB2** falls (**8.13 → 4.77 CPM**) despite the higher NTRK2 signal. The mouse deposit stores the NTF4 orthologue as Ntf5. Its reads are sparse in nuclei and low in bulk. Each nuclear class is one pooled sample per genotype, and the two human controls and four knockouts share a parental line. These gene-level values blend cell types and TrkB isoforms and do not measure phosphorylation, receptor function or chloride extrusion. [Bulk contrasts](development_contrasts.tsv) · [Nuclear counts](development_nuclei.tsv) · [GEO inputs and checksums](../d_mannose/inputs.tsv)

**Counterpredictions and reopening.** The trophic model predicts an exposure-linked maturation gain that is sensitive to TrkB perturbation after controlling intracellular parent and cell survival. A redox rescue alone could preserve function without that dependence. An opposing circuit effect is possible if trophic stimulation also reduces chloride extrusion and weakens recruited inhibition. Direct BDNF and NT-4 exposures lower KCC2 in rat hippocampal slices, whereas a neonatal study reports a different activity-dependent direction. The PTZ kindling report is an abstract-depth lead for another alternative, competition with the proconvulsant OCTN1 substrate homostachydrine. The predicted WWOX rescue should persist in defined medium without that substrate. Reopen for measured attainable parent in the relevant stage, increased mature neuronal output or improved evoked recovery, and preserved native chloride-dependent inhibition. Consider independent review if an exposure or functional result meets this threshold. [Mature slice primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12473684/) · [Neonatal primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25229715/) · [PTZ primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32844295/)
