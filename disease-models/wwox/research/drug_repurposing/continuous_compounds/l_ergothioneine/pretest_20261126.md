# L-ergothioneine · first comparative test

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · naturally occurring L-ergothioneine, not a mushroom mixture · WWOX tissue and cell-class expression for uptake and mitochondrial redox control

**Question.** Can exogenous L-ergothioneine reach a WWOX neural or glial compartment and selectively reduce redox damage with recovery of activity-dependent mitochondrial function? Compare uptake by SLC22A4 and SLC22A15 with systemic accumulation, extracellular antioxidant action and redox adaptation unrelated to ligand uptake. This extends the mitochondrial and lipid-peroxide strands. Ergothioneine is a candidate supplement with transporter-mediated entry, distinct from supplying glutathione precursor, selenium, ascorbate or a respiratory electron donor.

**Before examining the panel.** Compare SLC22A4, SLC22A15 and the related carnitine transporter SLC22A5, together with GPX4, SLC7A11, GCLC, GSR, AIFM2, SOD2, PRDX3 and HMOX1. Match P17 mouse cortex/hippocampal bulk and week 15 human organoid libraries and include assigned mouse excitatory, inhibitory, astrocyte and microglial nuclei. Establish whether either uptake signal is above the library floor. Pool-level absences are evidence about detection. They cannot rule out a low-level, developmentally restricted, endothelial or non-neural source. An elevated redox transcript without consistent uptake capacity does not predict delivery.

**Discriminating prediction.** In WWOX and corrected neural preparations, measure labeled intact ergothioneine in medium, blood fractions if relevant, cells and a validated mitochondrial fraction across the same physiological challenge. Require recovery of mitochondrial redox or ATP and evoked neuronal recruitment while keeping baseline activity and viability comparable. Test extracellular oxidant buffering, and compare direct intracellular uptake with relevant transporter blockade or matched transport-deficient control. If label enters whole cells but the mitochondrial or functional benefit remains absent, examine partition or a different rate-limiting lesion. Rescue without uptake or with comparable extracellular quenching supports a rival mechanism. Total blood enrichment can be driven by blood-cell retention and does not establish brain delivery.

**Pediatric feasibility.** Use exposure sources for purified L-ergothioneine and distinguish circulating plasma, erythrocytes, brain tissue and unbound neural exposure. Record developmental age, chronic tolerability, co-medications and evidence for a proximal biomarker. Adult food or supplement use cannot establish a dosing or efficacy bridge in pediatric WWOX. Defer if neither reproducible intracellular delivery nor selective functional recovery is plausible. No direction of a result is assigned before this first comparison.
