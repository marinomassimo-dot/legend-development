# Does zonisamide increase neuronal glutathione through astrocytic xCT?

**Checkpoint decision.** Retain zonisamide as a mechanistic probe. Defer a pediatric disease-modification trial in unselected WWOX loss. The available preparations support a cellular response to zonisamide, while the causal link from glial cystine exchange to functional recovery in WWOX remains open. Symptomatic use as an antiseizure drug is a separate question.

**Spinal injury and the correct control.** Kanbara et al., Fig. 5A, shows Slc7a11 means of approximately 1 in sham, 4 in untreated compression and 5 in treated compression. The significance brackets compare each injured group with sham. There is no marked direct treated-versus-untreated comparison. The Mt2a bracket also starts at sham. In Fig. 5C the drug comparison is marked and the mean GSH signal rises from about 1.0 to 1.6. Bar summaries and SDs are visible without individual data points. Supplementary Fig. S3 shows fields of the same type as Fig. 5B at two magnifications, without a rat-indexed table. The precise animal-level test therefore remains unresolved. [Original Fig. 5](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC7403578.1/41598_2020_70068_Fig5_HTML.jpg) · [Rat-level calculation](motor_neurons.md)

**Glial culture check.** The relevant culture panel is S2B in the actual supplement, despite the different numbering in the main Results. Oxidant alone raised astrocyte Slc7a11 to around 22 times the untreated baseline. With oxidant and 10 µM zonisamide the mean reached about 39. The marked comparison spans baseline and combined exposure. The additional rise over oxidant alone is descriptive, with three dishes per condition. In the adjacent S2A panel there *is* an annotated difference between stressed cultures with and without drug for Pcna. A change in cellular state or proliferation remains plausible. Neither of these RNA measurements quantifies cystine transport. [Supplement, p. 8, S2A–C and p. 9, S3](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC7403578.1/41598_2020_70068_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)

**Neuronal alternative.** Yagi et al. used embryonic mouse spinal preparations and a motor-neuron-like cell line. At 10 µM the primary cultures had longer neurites, about 113 to 127 µm, while the fraction of cells bearing neurites showed little change. Methods select the longest 200 neurites and specify a 25 µm bearing threshold; the Fig. 1D caption specifies 20 µm. In Fig. 4, nominal 10 µM increased the MTS signal under oxidative stress from about 84% to 92% of unstressed control. MTS measures metabolic reduction and the study reports n = 6 without clarifying embryo-level independence. Methods list 2–20 µM for this assay while the figure includes 1 µM; 10 µM is common to both. The primary preparation lacks a quantified glial-purity control. The separate NSC34 result supports a drug effect on neurite length in a cell line without an added astrocyte population. These experiments include neither isotope tracing into neuronal GSH nor restored conduction. The compression and neurite studies share investigators. [Yagi et al., Figs. 1 and 4 and Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4646494/)

**Prediction and falsification.** Compare stage-matched WWOX-deficient and corrected neurons under direct drug exposure with neurons receiving medium from an equal number of exposed astrocytes. Track carryover of zonisamide, sulfur incorporation into neuronal GSH, extracellular glutamate clearance and recovery of repetitive firing or conduction. A benefit that follows labeled glial substrate and disappears when its transfer is interrupted would favor the glial mechanism. Recovery under direct exposure without increased transfer would favor the neuronal alternative. More staining or longer already-established processes without functional recovery would leave trial priority low. A favorable reinterpretation of Fig. 5C would strengthen the compression-model observation and still leave donor cell, transfer and WWOX response unresolved.
