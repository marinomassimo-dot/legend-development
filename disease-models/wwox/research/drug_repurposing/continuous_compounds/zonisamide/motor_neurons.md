# Neuronal glutathione under zonisamide in a compression model

The cervical compression study moves the observation into identified neurons. In adult rats with spinal cord compression, zonisamide-treated animals showed a higher glutathione staining signal in ChAT-positive anterior-horn motor neurons at ten weeks (Fig. 5B–C). Fig. 2 reports improved stride length, with 11 treated and 11 vehicle-operated rats and six sham animals in the gait experiment. Fig. 5A measured transcripts in spinal tissue. These observations establish co-occurrence in the model. The relative staining signal does not identify astrocyte-derived sulfur, measure synthesis rate or exclude selection of surviving cells. The model differs anatomically and developmentally from WWOX. [Original Results, Figs. 2 and 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7403578/)

**Statistical control.** The Fig. 5C caption names three rats per arm and marks p < 0.05 by Mann–Whitney. Methods mention blinded observers and independent evaluation of both anterior horns. For exactly three independent rat-level values per arm, the 20 equiprobable allocations give a minimum exact two-sided p of 0.10 even with complete separation. The corresponding minimum one-sided p is 0.05. Asymptotic tests or treating horns separately can produce different values. The reported method does not specify enough to identify which explanation applies. This calculation audits the annotation and leaves the reported direction intact.
