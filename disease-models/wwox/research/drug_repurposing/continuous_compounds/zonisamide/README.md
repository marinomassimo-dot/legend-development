# Zonisamide and astrocytic support in WWOX loss

**Candidate selection.** The existing WWOX dossier covers the antiseizure drug's multichannel activity and a co-treated WOREE case with persistent seizures. A separate question is whether zonisamide can increase astrocytic cystine exchange and provide a sustained increase in neuronal glutathione synthesis. In untreated WWOX tissue, the exchanger and glutamate-clearance transcripts fall together. Exchange releases glutamate, so both a benefit and a cost are plausible. [Held clinical and channel record](../../repurposing_readiness_sci_20261116/sheets_tranche1.tsv) · [Held co-treated WWOX case](../pyridoxine/README.md) · [Existing substrate and clearance data](../acetylcysteine/mechanism.md)

**Discriminating prediction.** At matched viable astrocyte count, a genuine substrate-supply response should increase labeled cysteine incorporation into neuronal glutathione, persist beyond the acute channel effect, and improve neuronal recovery without sustained excess extracellular glutamate. More proliferating glia can raise whole-culture glutathione without increasing support per neuron. Measure astrocyte number, per-cell exchange, glutamate clearance, neuronal survival and network recovery separately. A short-latency reduction of firing with unchanged neuronal glutathione favors the acute mechanism.

**First comparator.** In a hemiparkinsonian mouse and glial cultures, zonisamide increased GSH and astrocytic xCT, and the authors also implicated glial proliferation. The lesioned striatum and levodopa-related quinone endpoint differ from WWOX developmental cortex. The immediate task is to extract the cell-count and per-cell xCT denominators from the source and compare them with measured free pediatric exposure and the existing WWOX redox record. [Primary study, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20225289/)

**What the original figure changes.** In Asanuma et al., printed p. 242, Fig. 1C–H, 24-hour exposure at nominal 100 µM increased GSH in C6 astroglial cells from about 3.2 to 7 nmol/mg protein. The dopaminergic CATH.a line had no marked increase. In C6 the xCT-positive fraction rose from roughly 4.5% to 13% (panel G), so increased cell number alone cannot explain this fraction. Panel H measured increased cell count in a *separate primary astroglial culture* (about 35 to 48). Dividing a C6 GSH result by that primary-culture count would create a spurious per-cell estimate. GSH was normalized to protein, and staining scored the proportion above threshold. Neither measures exchange flux or GSH transfer to neurons. Panel D marks only the 100 µM group as significantly different from control, and panel G also marks 50 µM. These are visually estimated bar heights, not raw data. The caption reports n = 5–6 for GSH, 9–11 for C6 immunostaining and 8 for primary counts without resolving donor independence. **Exposure checkpoint.** Pediatric group-mean scenarios span approximately 23–97 µM free serum across distinct cohorts and sampling conditions. Only the higher scenarios approach the nominal 100 µM glial GSH result. Next resolve actual paired CSF exposure and whether altered glutamate release or clearance accompanies neuronal redox recovery. [Source denominators, calculations and limits](exposure.md)

**Competing release mechanism.** In an independent rat astrocyte study, Figures 4 and 5 compare basal and stimulated glutamate and ATP accumulation at nominal zonisamide concentrations of 30, 100 and 300 µM (n = 6 reported). Neither acute nor seven-day treatment changed basal accumulation. During two challenges using high extracellular K and no added Ca, zonisamide reduced both glutamate and ATP accumulation in a concentration-dependent fashion. A common reduction is consistent with the authors' hemichannel mechanism. The sampling ACSF was a salt/glucose/HEPES solution with **no added cystine** after three washes. Resting glutamate in that experiment cannot adjudicate glutamate export driven by an extracellular cystine supply. The study also did not independently measure clearance. A priority WWOX comparison therefore pairs extracellular cystine availability with measured cystine uptake, neuronal GSH labeling, glutamate clearance and recovery at fixed astrocyte count. A reduction in stimulated glutamate without neuronal labeling would leave the redox hypothesis unsupported. [Fukuyama et al., Methods and Figs. 4–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7345221/)
