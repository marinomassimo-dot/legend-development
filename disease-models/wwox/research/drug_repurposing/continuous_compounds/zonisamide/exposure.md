# Does pediatric exposure approach the astroglial response?

In a 1994 monotherapy study of 47 children under 15, serum free fractions varied by age group and sampling period. Table 3 gives mean values of about 51–61%. Table 5 and the text report mean total serum zonisamide of 9.7 µg/mL (SD 5.4) in the 31 seizure-free children. Applying the endpoints of the group-mean free fractions to that selected mean gives about 23–28 µM unbound. These estimates are sensitivity scenarios, not measurements of paired values in those 31. The English summary miscounts the 2–<5-year group as 10; Table 3 gives 15, consistent with the total of 47. [Original Tables 3–5](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jjes1983/12/3/12_3_249/_pdf)

A second pediatric monotherapy study of 72 children reported mean total plasma trough and peak concentrations of 27.0 and 33.8 µg/mL. Applying the same external fractions gives approximately 65–78 µM at the mean trough and 81–97 µM at the mean peak. The groups differed in eligibility and timing. Neither estimate defines a child's exposure range. In a separate clinical protein-binding study, the average free fractions without and with valproate were 54.3% and 58.3%, respectively, among 68 patients with unbound measurements. Co-medication, binding conditions and selection change interpretation. **Implication.** The 50 µM nominal condition associated with a higher xCT-positive fraction is in the same order of magnitude as several serum scenarios. The GSH result was significant at nominal 100 µM alone. A higher pediatric peak scenario approaches this number, but the experiment held the nominal addition for 24 hours and did not establish the unbound concentration in the cell medium. Human CSF or brain extracellular exposure may differ. The Kumagai 1993 record names paired matrices but the accessible PubMed record provided no numerical CSF result. Quantifying that comparison remains open. Next prioritize actual paired CSF and free-serum measurements and an independent astrocytic release/clearance comparator under resting and stressed conditions.
