## Hypothesis and discriminating experiment

**Hypothesis.** A shallow, activity-dependent block of NMDA receptors decreases inhibitory output in the Wwox cortical circuit before achieving sufficient suppression of excitation. Rival explanations include a useful fall in recurrent NMDA excitation with inhibition preserved, or a change in the first AMPA response or Iₕ separate from the presynaptic deficit.

**Experiment.** Extend the existing ketamine–memantine–d-APV comparison in spontaneous P13–17 Wwox and sibling slices. Match partial postsynaptic late NMDA charge and extracellular magnesium using the same input pattern, verify local exposure and repeat the measurements at different activity histories. In the same preparation include direct inhibitory cell stimulation with first response, failures, paired-pulse ratio and **absolute inhibitory charge across the train**, and compare with disynaptic inhibition triggered by excitatory input. Check GABA reversal relative to rest/threshold and a postsynaptic inhibitory response to distinguish loss of release from changes in postsynaptic current. Measure the first AMPA response, sustained excitatory recruitment, sag and spontaneous initiation and spread with time-matched vehicle, and preserve the animal as the replication unit. Use a selective genetic or independent functional comparison only if a terminal contribution is supported.

- A drop in direct inhibitory output, with preserved postsynaptic GABA responsiveness and stable or increased recurrence despite lower NMDA charge, advances the presynaptic rival. Loss limited to disynaptic output instead suggests reduced recruitment upstream of the inhibitory terminal.
- If direct and disynaptic inhibition remain within prespecified bounds and the three arms reduce spontaneous recurrence similarly, advance suppression of NMDA-dependent excitation. A stronger effect confined to ketamine with altered first AMPA response or Iₕ instead supports investigating that parallel mechanism. A change only in a ratio with unstable paired responses stays unresolved.

This changes the frozen comparison by giving the first and summed inhibitory responses equal status. The adult primary supports a change in the first response and short-term plasticity. It provides no advance evidence for larger suppression late in the train. ## Pediatric and developmental limits

Short racemic IV induction, acute anticonvulsant dosing alongside midazolam, and multi-day intensive-care infusions represent different exposures. The previous public audit found an immediate clinical response in a combined regimen, uncontrolled cEEG observations during longer infusions and recovery measured over weeks in a different cohort. It found no joint comparison of induction-only exposure, baseline function and weeks 6–8 with an active induction comparator. Norketamine, other sedatives, background antiseizure medications, ventilation and recovery from the acute illness matter for transfer. None of the new native comparisons closes the clinical gap or measures functional myelin. Reopen the clinical attribution question for joint induction-only exposure, premorbid measurements and comparable timed follow-up. Reopen the circuit decision separately with native Wwox measurements of both NMDA charge and inhibitory output.
