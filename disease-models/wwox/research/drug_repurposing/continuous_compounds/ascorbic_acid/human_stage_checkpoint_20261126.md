# L-ascorbic acid · human transporter coverage and next decision

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Active compound: L-ascorbic acid.** The preceding [exposure comparison](exposure_bridge_20261126.md) makes a matched concentration–time profile a prerequisite for interpreting a formulation response. This increment checks whether human RNA data can localize the proposed transport constraint.

## Human data audit

- **WWOX organoids.** GSE156243 has bulk cerebral organoid libraries described as matched for age and batch. It cannot assign its positive SLC23A2 difference to a lineage stage. Steinberg et al. subsequently report single-cell data under E-MTAB-14792. Their supplemental methods (File009, *Single-cell data preprocessing*) document one library per condition in this comparison. The named high-quality downstream populations are radial glia, neuronal progenitors and neurons. The published oligodendrocyte gene-set signature in Fig. 6E refers to neuronal populations. It cannot estimate transporter expression within a stage-matched human oligodendroglial population. The public BioStudies API returned 403 at this checkpoint. No count-matrix reanalysis was run. Five group counts in the supplemental methods sum to 21,770, while the accompanying total of 18,007 equals the first four groups; neither total is an oligodendroglial denominator. [GSE156243](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE156243) · [Steinberg et al. and supplements](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awag239) · [E-MTAB-14792](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/biostudies/arrayexpress/studies/E-MTAB-14792)
- **Human fetal oligodendroglial reference.** The deposited GSE155488 fetal matrix has 359 cell columns and noninteger expression values. It has no cell header or donor labels. A positive-entry audit gives SLC23A2 in **27/359** overall, **7/107** in a PDGFRA-positive marker gate, **4/52** in a GPR17/TCF7L2-positive, MBP-negative gate, and **10/97** in the MBP-positive gate. At or above the median number of detected genes, the corresponding marker-gate counts are **2/33**, **4/39** and **8/62**. These are exploratory marker gates, not published stage assignments or cross-species homologies. The absolute positive counts are too sparse for a convincing stage gradient, and donor-level effects remain unknown. Positive entries demonstrate that the transcript is observable. Zero entries cannot distinguish low expression from sampling or prove absence of transporter protein. This normal developmental reference supplies no WWOX genotype contrast. [Original GEO deposit](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE155488) · [Counts and sensitivity check](fetal_detection.tsv)

**Decision.** Bulk human upregulation and pooled mouse stage-specific decreases remain compatible with differences in cellular composition. The decision to prioritize a supplement above a replete baseline needs a functional bridge. In stage-matched human WWOX-deficient and corrected cells, test whether the initial slope of intracellular reduced-ascorbate accumulation is smaller at the same measured extracellular profile. A smaller slope coupled to lower SVCT2 protein would favor limited entry. Comparable entry and protein with persistently reduced first ensheathment would shift weight downstream. Next check the primary Wwox reciprocal axon/OPC coculture against those alternatives to select the smallest informative functional comparison. Keep this compound active until that decision is recorded. No second compound initiated.

**Reproduction.** Download the original [fetal matrix](https://ftp.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/series/GSE155nnn/GSE155488/suppl/GSE155488_All_fetal_OL_lineage_subset.csv.gz) and give its path to `python3 fetal_detection.py`. The script verifies the source hash and reproduces the positive-entry table. No single-cell source data are redistributed.

## Axonal counterexample and first functional comparison

Repudi et al., pp. 3062–3063 and 3071–3072, compared WT OPCs placed on WT or Wwox-null DRG neurons. The two conditions differed in the abundance of premyelinating and myelinating morphologies (Fig. 6B–D, four cultures per arm across two experiments). The protocol lists N2/B27 and no separately added ascorbic acid in that medium; reduced ascorbate was not quantified. The human oligocortical spheroids and the rodent coculture are separate comparisons. This is an axonal-genotype comparison with fixed WT OPCs. It lacks the other two cells of a reciprocal genotype design and does not report first sheaths per eligible axon or evoked conduction. [Repudi et al., Fig. 6 and *Dorsal root ganglion oligodendrocyte progenitor cell co-culture*](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awab174)

This changes the experimental order. First use a single WT or corrected OPC population on paired control and WWOX-deficient axons, with replete and maintained-ascorbate conditions. Prespecify activity and surviving axon caliber and count before treatment, then record increased newly ensheathed fraction and evoked conduction together. Measure intracellular reduced ascorbate separately in the OPCs. If intracellular ascorbate rises equally on control and deficient axons yet first ensheathment remains impaired on deficient axons, lower the priority of an OPC substrate intervention. If only the deficient-axon condition improves beyond the replete baseline, follow up with the OPC-genotype arm to distinguish the responding compartment. This comparison is a proposed discriminator. Clinical prioritization remains open and no experimental response is claimed.
