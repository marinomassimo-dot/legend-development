# L-ascorbic acid · functional translation and disposition

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Decision.** Park L-ascorbic acid for prioritization of a WWOX developmental trial. Retain it as a mechanistic probe pending measured transport above a replete baseline and effects on first-sheath formation and axonal conduction. The decision concerns a developmental intervention. It does not address correction of nutritional deficiency. An independent review could reassess interpretation, but the missing WWOX tissue exposure and functional effects are the deciding gaps.

## Why length, thickness, and first ensheathment need separate measurements

Gess et al., sciatic nerve, germline SVCT2 heterozygous mouse. Figure 1D reports lower nerve ascorbate at P20, P60 and P300 (n=3 in each age comparison). Figures 2E–M report thinner sheaths and fewer wraps in established peripheral fibers at P60/P300, with unchanged axon diameters. Fig. 3 reports slower motor conduction and a nonsignificant CMAP amplitude contrast. This connects a genotype-associated tissue exposure deficit with function in the peripheral nerve. It provides neither first-ensheathment tracking nor a supplementation rescue. [Gess et al., primary results and legends](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6623846/)

In the same paper's Schwann-cell/DRG assay, addition of phloretin reduced MBP-positive segment **length** (Fig. 7J, n=5, p<0.001). The separate segment-number comparison was nonsignificant (7K, n=5). This small study cannot establish equivalence of segment counts. The authors' culture siRNA comparison confirmed an effect of reduced SVCT2 on collagen V (7B–C), without a genetic replication of the segment-length test. Phloretin affects other transporters, including glucose transporters. A length contrast can reflect growth of existing segments or a difference in initiation. For WWOX, the length or MBP effects under Guo's **As-2P** arm remain compatible with those alternatives. Schwann-cell collagen effects in the peripheral nerve cannot establish the oligodendroglial mechanism. [Gess et al., Fig. 7](https://doi.org/10.1523/JNEUROSCI.3457-11.2011) · [Guo et al., Fig. 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6001564/)

## Clinical countercheck

Verhamme et al. tested oral parent ascorbate in CMT1A, a **PMP22-related peripheral** neuropathy. Ages ranged 13–24 years in the placebo arm (n=6) and 14–24 in the treated arm (n=5). At one year the motor conduction-velocity contrast was **+1.3 m/s** (95% CI −0.3 to +3.0, p=0.11), from −0.8 and +0.5 m/s mean changes in Table 2. The study was planned for a +10 m/s between-group difference; the interval excludes an effect of that magnitude under these conditions. It does not rule out a small effect. The larger pediatric CMT1A trial reports an adjusted **+1.7 m/s** (−0.1 to +3.4, p=0.06), 81 randomized ages 2–16, in the primary abstract. It cannot establish CNS exposure, benefit in WWOX or infant feasibility. No pooled estimate across these differently analyzed trials was made. [Verhamme et al., eligibility, statistical methods and Tables 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2784478/) · [Burns et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19427269/) · [Reproducible table extraction](peripheral_translation_20261126.py) · [Extracted results](peripheral_translation_20261126.tsv)

## Reopening prediction

With an identical glial population on paired WWOX-deficient and corrected axons, sustained reduced ascorbate above verified repletion should increase intracellular ascorbate and selectively increase the proportion of previously bare viable axons that gain a sheath and conduct reliably. If only length/thickness on already wrapped axons changes, retain a growth effect and reject rescue of first ensheathment under those conditions. Reopen clinical prioritization when replicated human-line responses can be linked to attainable pediatric central exposure. The prior uptake, cell-stage and axon-genotype comparisons remain the basis for this discrimination.
