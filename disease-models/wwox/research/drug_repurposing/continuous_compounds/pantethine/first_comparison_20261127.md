The PKAN 5 h mean / 2 h mean is 1.04 / 1.56 = **0.667**. This is a ratio of time-point means, with no individual kinetics inferred. Converting each concentration with `µM = 1000 × (µg/mL) / (g/mol)` gives **6.47 and 4.31 µM** if the mass is anhydrous sodium pantothenate (241.22 g/mol), and **7.12 and 4.74 µM** if reported as free acid equivalents (219.23 g/mol). The nominal calibration basis was not further specified in the article; the original mass concentrations are the primary result. Neither molar conversion refers to pantethine or a WWOX target concentration.

In the 15-child PKAN pilot, the W24–W0 changes in total UPDRS I–III and Fahn–Marsden were approximately **+1.4 and +0.9 points** from the published means (Tables 2–3; paired tests p = 0.614 and 0.729). W−24 scores were assessed retrospectively at W0. The smaller subsequent increase compared with the preceding interval is vulnerable to ascertainment and disease-course confounding; the design lacks a contemporaneous control. Two children had mild nausea and poor appetite. Serum CoA was a colorimetric readout (p = 0.31), and the printed W24 median lies below its own range. No effect size from that median is used here. Serum CoA is also a different compartment from mitochondrial CoA in neural cells. The pilot establishes limited age-specific precedent with the studied capsule only; tolerability, antiseizure interactions and transfer to WWOX remain open. ## Interim inference and next test

The pediatric measurements favor starting with pantothenate and cysteamine as exposure-matched controls. They leave intact oral delivery to the neural compartment unresolved. The decisive model comparison remains the prespecified recovery of paired precursor labels in free matrix CoA, beyond products given separately at matched availability, with a concordant increase in pyruvate flux and preserved evoked function. Early intact intermediate measurement is needed to address label recycling or recombination. Keep pediatric priority on hold through this comparison. [Registered prediction](pretest_20261127.md)

**An extracellular route is a separate competing explanation.** Yu et al. recovered labeled 4′-phosphopantetheine from one fly-associated bacterial species after feeding labeled pantethine (Fig. 3H–J, at least four technical replicates). They detected the intermediate in culture supernatant in a separate experiment (Fig. 4C, at least four biological replicates), and the associated host outcome in Fig. 5 was pupation of Pank-null flies. This supports extracellular generation of a CoA precursor in the fly model. It supplies neither mammalian neural exposure nor endogenous neuronal conversion of the parent. The two models make different predictions. An exposure-matched 4′-phosphopantetheine reference can restore labeling and the matrix free CoA pool when verified pantethine exposure alone cannot; that would locate the missing step upstream of neural salvage. If verified pantethine works in the absence of a measured extracellular phosphorylated intermediate, early intracellular intermediates and moiety retention would provide evidence for a direct cellular route. Before disposition, compare Rana's fly and cell data, Brunetti's genotype and diet effects in mice and which enzymes or transport steps the mammalian route requires. [Yu et al., pp. 2656–2658](https://www.cell.com/article/S1097276522004403/pdf)
