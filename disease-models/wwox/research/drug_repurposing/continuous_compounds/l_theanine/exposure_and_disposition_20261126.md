## Countercheck from different seizure provocations

In male NMRI mice after 14 days with 4% w/v L-theanine as their only drinking solution, pilocarpine threshold for body twitch increased from 255 ± 26 to 315 ± 36 mg/kg (+23.5%, nominal p=0.047), and PTZ threshold for tonus decreased from 91 ± 10 to 60 ± 14 mg/kg (−34.1%, nominal p=0.014). The Fig. 1 caption reverses the letters shown next to the panels. Match the labeled drug axes (drawn B for pilocarpine and A for PTZ) to the actual Results and use the caption for the tests. Threshold arms were small (Fig. 1, n=5–6), multiple behavioral phases were tested and no EEG threshold is reported. The percentage changes describe differences of published means on different experimental readouts. [Schallier et al., Methods and pp. 79–80, Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1179/1476830512y.0000000033)

In the regional dialysis comparisons (Fig. 2, n=6–8), frontal GABA fell from 6.4 ± 2.0 to 1.5 ± 0.4 nM (−76.6%, nominal p=0.016). Hippocampal GABA had a nonsignificant increase (p=0.42), and neither regional glutamate contrast was detected. Basal dialysate is distinct from transmitter flux and evoked synaptic charge. The paired direction across the cortical and PTZ comparisons makes it important to monitor inhibition explicitly. The study cannot establish that GABA mediated the threshold change, that the phenotype translates to WWOX, or that comparable exposure occurs in children. [Schallier et al., pp. 80–81, Fig. 2A–D](https://doi.org/10.1179/1476830512y.0000000033)

## Pediatric sleep and vigilance

Lyon et al. randomized 98 boys aged 8–12 with ADHD and analyzed 93 completers (46 L-theanine, 47 placebo). Wrist movement on five nights before and near the end of six weeks favored L-theanine for actigraphic efficiency (Fig. 1, labeled p<0.05) and fewer bouts of nocturnal movement (Fig. 2, labeled p<0.029). The Statistical Analysis section sets α=0.01 because of multiple comparisons. Those p-value bounds do not demonstrate that either result meets the declared threshold. The authors reported no significant group difference in latency or total sleep duration and no significant correlation between actigraphy and parent questionnaire. Fig. 3 labeled wake after sleep onset as p<0.058. The trial excluded chronic medical conditions and serious learning disabilities. The Results account for stimulant status in 91 of 93 completers (27 plus 64). A tic was reported in the active arm in a participant with a history of tics, with attribution unresolved. The study measures rest and movement; it provides no WWOX efficacy, EEG staging, seizure assessment or comparable safety estimate. [Lyon et al., pp. 350–352, Methods, Statistical Analysis, Results and Figs. 1–3](https://nutritionalfrontiers.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/2012.lyon_.Sleep-Quality-ADHD.pdf)

A randomized four-way crossover abstract (five boys aged 8–15 with ADHD) reports a direction toward slower stop-signal reaction time with acute L-theanine alone (p=0.053). Caffeine and the mixture were separate conditions. Behavioral response inhibition and synaptic inhibition are different outcomes. [Kahathuduwa et al., abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32753637/)

## Discriminating observation and decision

**Data.** Lower bulk SLC38A1 RNA in all three untreated WWOX systems survives single-library omission. The pooled hippocampal nuclear data cannot establish surface availability or estimate biological replication. Existing glutamine work shows both Slc38a1-dependent inhibitory GABA replenishment and sustained excitatory responses with added glutamine in distinct preparations. [WWOX replay](README.md) · [Prior glutamine controls](../l_glutamine/uptake.md)

**Inference.** Normalizing total activity counts could obscure a loss of inhibition. The relevant competition may occur in a substrate-sensitive subset of neurons or glia. The prolonged developmental observation can coexist with an immediate adverse-direction synaptic effect. Neither RNA nor dialysate provides the link to functional recovery.

**Hypothesis and falsifier.** In matched juvenile Wwox neuronal-deletion and control preparations, test whether theanine during exposure reduces labeled glutamine incorporation into GABA and late evoked inhibitory charge during recovery from repeated input, at fixed measured free glutamine. Record absolute excitatory charge and late recurrent recruitment in the same preparations. In a separate exposure-and-washout comparison, delayed compensation predicts increased surface SNAT1 and recovered labeled GABA and inhibitory charge after free parent is below detection. A glutamine-only arm and a validated SNAT1-pathway control distinguish precursor limitation from an unrelated effect. Check membrane responsiveness, intrinsic firing and viability so that general suppression or a change in cell composition cannot masquerade as recovery. Stem-cell results require matched lineage composition, and organoid assays require measurement of free glutamine in the actual medium. A change without matched label flux, a worsening of late inhibition, or a larger effect on excitatory recovery falsifies the specific favorable interpretation. For any intact-animal extension compare EEG and behavior at matched vigilance. Quantify recovery as late evoked inhibitory charge at a fixed post-train interval, with the pre-train charge and the full recovery curve reported separately. At each measured exposure estimate the drug-minus-vehicle change in each genotype and their difference. A selective benefit must narrow a replicated vehicle-treated WWOX deficit even on the absolute scale. The independent replicate is the animal or organoid differentiation, with individual slices or libraries nested within it.

**Disposition.** Park L-theanine for unselected pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retain acute uptake competition and post-washout compensation as opposing mechanistic controls. The current study set contains no measured free, time-resolved neural parent exposure from which to infer engagement in WWOX. Reopen for an exposure bridge and a reproducible genotype-by-drug improvement in inhibitory recovery that preserves appropriately timed excitatory output and does not increase late recurrent recruitment.
