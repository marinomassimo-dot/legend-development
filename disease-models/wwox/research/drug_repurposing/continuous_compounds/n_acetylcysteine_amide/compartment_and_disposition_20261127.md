# N-acetylcysteine amide | Parent delivery, conversion and redox recovery

**Decision.** Retain N-acetylcysteine amide (NACA, NPI-001) as a conditional preclinical comparator for NAC delivery and thiol redox chemistry. Defer pediatric WWOX trial priority. The animal evidence supports an injured-cortex comparison. The advantage of intact parent at matched local NAC and cysteine exposure remains unresolved. A selective functional gain at measured local exposure would change the disposition.

## Discriminating observation

**Dato.** WWOX bulk comparisons found lower SLC1A1, SLC7A11 and SLC1A2 expression. The pooled astrocyte SLC1A2 comparison went in the opposite direction from bulk mouse hippocampus, and the broader glutathione-synthesis signature was inconsistent. Those data motivate a compartmental test. The native WWOX synthesis or recycling flux and the sign of the extracellular glutamate response remain unmeasured. [Held WWOX NAC evidence and replay](../acetylcysteine/mechanism.md)

**Inferenza.** The same rise in reduced GSH could come from new synthesis supported by converted NAC, from GSSG reduction by the intact thiol, or from lower oxidative demand. Relative parent enrichment in whole brain and protection after trauma are compatible with all three. A blood GSH rise cannot select the mechanism.

**Ipotesi e verifica.** At equivalent measured local NAC and free cysteine trajectories, compare NACA and NAC in juvenile WWOX-LoF and corrected neuron–glia preparations. Quantify chemically reduced intact NACA in the relevant compartment. Read out new GSH with an independent backbone tracer and follow both GSH and GSSG as reduced-glutathione equivalents (GSH + 2 × GSSG). Freeze the redox state at collection and use a post-harvest spike control to detect continued thiol exchange during processing. Early GSH recovery with falling GSSG, little additional label and an unchanged total pool would favor recycling or preservation. Increased labeled GSH and increased pool under matched redox handling would favor synthesis. A response shared by NAC at matched precursor exposure favors general delivery. An amide-specific gain raises priority only if it also improves post-activity mitochondrial or evoked recovery while preserving the first response, extracellular glutamate clearance and inhibitory recruitment. Falling clearance or blunted recruitment lowers priority despite higher GSH.

**Pediatric feasibility.** The NPI-001 tablet retinitis pigmentosa studies NCT04355689 (49 enrolled) and NCT07710196 (80 planned) specify ages 18 and older. The separately registered hereditary cystatin C amyloid angiopathy study includes ages 12 and older and reports 15 total enrolled. Its registry does not provide the adolescent denominator or clinical results. It concerns cerebral hemorrhage and skin and blood biomarkers, with a different population and outcomes. The public evidence used here leaves exposure and tolerability in younger children, WWOX CNS engagement and interactions with background antiseizure therapy unresolved. [NCT04355689](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04355689) · [NCT07710196](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07710196) · [NCT07716865](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07716865)

**Reopen.** Look for a native WWOX redox or post-activity recovery deficit with verified subcellular exposure and a matched-NAC functional difference. The key missing comparison determines whether further review can change the decision.
