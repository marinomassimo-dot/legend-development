# Nicotinamide | Exposure, NAD turnover and axonal function

**Decision.** Retain nicotinamide (NAM) as a conditional preclinical comparator for axonal NAMPT salvage and SARM1-dependent turnover. Defer pediatric WWOX trial priority. The first observation that could change the disposition is selective functional recovery in a native WWOX axon with verified local parent exposure, an interpretable NAD balance and the response to the first input preserved.

## What would distinguish the mechanisms

**Dato.** In the held WWOX comparisons NMNAT2 RNA is lower in cortex, hippocampus and human organoids. WWOX-silenced endothelial whole-cell NAMPT protein changes by −0.01 log2 (BH p=0.87). The published mouse NMNAT2 hypomorph comparison shows somatic NAD up and neurite NAD down under NR, with reversal of the neurite effect after SARM1 deletion. Its NAM-only arm uses an entire WT DRG explant. [Held WWOX and NR evidence](../nicotinamide_riboside/README.md) · [Compartment and protein checks](../nicotinamide_riboside/endogenous.md)

**Inferenza.** Separate net salvage from preservation or exchange of existing NAD. Angeletti et al. show that SARM1 can use pyridine bases in place of water in vitro. They explicitly raise re-incorporation of identical NAM as a possible contributor to apparent feedback in hydrolysis assays. If exogenous labeled NAM participates in this exchange, labeled NAD can appear without adding to the total pool. The observation has not been made in WWOX axons. [Angeletti et al., Figs. 2, 4–5 and Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2022.103812)

**Ipotesi.** When axonal NMNAT2 reserve is adequate, added local NAM can increase net NAMPT-dependent synthesis and improve recovery. When axonal NMNAT2 is limiting, increasing precursor supply may instead raise the NMN/NAD ratio, leave axonal function unchanged or impair it despite somatic improvement. A third possibility is preserved local NAD through altered SARM1-dependent turnover.

**Verifica discriminante.** Compare paired early and recovery measurements in juvenile WWOX-LoF and matched corrected axons at verified local parent concentrations. Trace labeled NMN and both light and labeled NAD with absolute pool sizes, monitor 1-methylnicotinamide, and measure ADPR/cADPR alongside an independent SARM1-dependence control. Match starting NAD and recruitment where they affect the comparison. A rise in labeled NMN followed by net axonal NAD and improved conduction or ATP recovery, lost with independent NAMPT constraint, favors salvage. Decreased light-NAD loss and increased pool retention with limited upstream labeling, persisting under matched NAMPT constraint and occluded when SARM1 activity is absent, favor altered turnover. Label appearing without net recovery remains compatible with base exchange or compensated loss. An increasing NMN/NAD ratio with worse axonal recovery lowers priority. Distinguish axons from somata and record first evoked response and inhibitory recruitment so generalized suppression does not count as rescue.

**Pediatric feasibility.** DeBoer et al. randomized 1,188 mother–child dyads. Direct infant exposure was oral powder at 6–18 months, preceded by assignment of a maternal pill or placebo from enrollment through month 6. The 1,084-child modified intention-to-treat set concerns the growth endpoint. From enrollment, Table 3 gives 147 serious events in the NAM arm and 145 in placebo, while the prose reverses these counts. They are events. The adjusted incidence rate ratio is 1.00 (95% CI 0.77–1.30). There were 6 versus 8 deaths in the narrative, with no reported time-to-first serious-event difference. Oversight paused and resumed the study. ENDIT separately studied modified-release oral NAM in 552 at-risk participants in a mixed child/adult cohort, reporting similar serious-event counts without providing values in the public abstract. The preparations, indication, exposures and outcomes do not resolve neural exposure, interactions with background antiseizure therapies, or safety in young WWOX patients. No pediatric WWOX efficacy, PK or tolerability conclusion. [DeBoer et al., Methods, SAE Results and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8478246/#pmed.1003617.t003) · [ENDIT primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15043959/)

**Reopen.** A native WWOX axonal NAD-balance defect or differential NMNAT2 reserve with stage-matched functional coverage would justify the comparison. A further review of the same injury systems would leave the local exposure and WWOX flux gaps unresolved.
