# Cenobamate · developmental separation of seizure onset and progression

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN · P7 rat behavioural events per animal, adult human motor threshold in % maximal stimulator output, adult rat radiolabel total tissue:plasma ratio, nominal bath exposure in µM

## A genuine early-life countercheck

The Witherspoon et al. P7 rat PTZ study observed latency to the first **behavioural** motor seizure (Fig. 1A) separately from progression to a stage-5 tonic-clonic event (Fig. 1C). The first-motor latency comparison gave **p = 0.66** across arms. Stage-5 outcomes during the 1,500 s window were **11/11** vehicle, **14/14**, **11/14**, **5/14** and **0/10** at the four ascending doses (7, 14, 28, 56 mg/kg). The two highest groups thus gave about **36%** and **0%** stage-5 events. These are counts of rats, with litter allocation unverified. A Wilson 95% interval for 0/10 under independent Bernoulli sampling is 0–28%; this illustrative interval does not account for litter clustering. It can be replayed at zero events with `1.96**2 / (10 + 1.96**2)`. Fig. 1B excludes the 56 mg/kg group from the conditional latency comparison and excludes ceiling observations within analyzed arms. Use Fig. 1C to assess progression and keep Fig. 1B descriptive of the subset observed to progress. Behavioural onset and progression may dissociate even if electrographic onset or propagation differs. [Witherspoon et al., Methods §§2.3–2.5 and Results §3.1, Fig. 1A–C](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11551878/)

The separate P7 toxicity cohort tested a 24 h PathoGreen endpoint in seven sampled regions. Phenobarbital was a positive control. The authors detected no increase for cenobamate in those readouts. This establishes a bounded histological countercheck for the exposure studied. Chronic neurodevelopment, functional conduction, inhibitory recruitment, and safety during polytherapy each need their own data. There are no paired regional free drug measurements in the PTZ comparison. [Witherspoon et al., Methods §§2.4–2.6 and Results §3.2, Figs. 2–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11551878/)

## Can tissue exposure identify the mechanism?

Palamanda et al. report approximately **1:1** total drug-related radioactivity for brain:plasma in adult male rats after labelled cenobamate. Parent was the principal species found in rat plasma. This experiment leaves the composition of brain radioactivity, brain binding and the free developing-forebrain partition unresolved. The FDA nonclinical review likewise reports a near-unity rat brain tissue:blood radiolabel ratio for its distribution study. The dossier and publication could represent the same sponsor study, so do not count them as independent exposure replicates. Neither tissue:blood nor tissue:plasma total ratio substitutes for a free partition coefficient. [Palamanda et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37921950/) · [FDA nonclinical review §5.1, printed p. 10](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2019/212839Orig1s000PharmR.pdf)

The EMA review reports potentiation of GABA-induced CA3 currents at **about 30 µM** nominal in a rat assay and persistence under 1 µM flumazenil. The Sharma group also reports significant evoked-current potentiation from 30 µM in the conference abstract with an EC₅₀ of 164 µM. The EMA and Sharma observations may report the same experiment. The EC₅₀ gives the half-maximal concentration and does not define the onset of an evoked effect. Treat tonic and evoked measurements as separate within-compartment outcomes. In WWOX use a brief natural waveform as a stimulus as well as a prolonged control. The P7 behavioural separation elevates progression as an outcome, with physiological onset and synaptic recruitment still measured independently. If the WWOX effect exceeds a sodium-matched comparator, the discriminating observation is whether the incremental suppression follows measured GABA reversal/tonic conductance while first-spike propagation is preserved. A phenotype that stays identical under independent matching of both perturbations has no need for a cenobamate-specific additional mechanism. [EMA assessment §2.3.2, p. 20](https://www.ema.europa.eu/system/files/documents/assessment-report/h-5377-par_en.pdf#page=20) · [Sharma et al., conference abstract P1.5-033](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.92.15_supplement.P1.5-033)

## Human pharmacodynamic comparator

Gefferie et al. tested add-on cenobamate in drug-resistant focal epilepsy, with **28 adults at baseline, 25 at T1 and 21 at T2**. The resting motor threshold slope was **+0.03 percentage points of maximal stimulator output per mg** (95% CI **0.02–0.05**, p < 0.001). In model units this equals about **+3 points per 100 mg**, with a **2–5 point** interval. Cortical silent period also lengthened, with stimulation intensity a plausible contributor. Short- and long-interval paired-pulse inhibition had **p = 0.81 and 0.87**. These null tests do not set an equivalence bound and do not measure tonic GABA current. Background medication changes and the uncontrolled add-on design restrict attribution. A higher threshold alone should never be scored as preserved interneuron recruitment or confirmation of preferential persistent-current inhibition. [Gefferie et al., §§2.2, 3.2–3.3 and 4, Fig. 1A–D](https://doi.org/10.1002/epi4.70255)
