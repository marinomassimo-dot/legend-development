## Primary kinetics and an exposure-dependent trap

Nakamura et al. examined mechanically dissociated rat P12–P17 CA3 pyramidal neurons at 22–25 °C. From −80 to −20 mV, a 200 ms command produced a persistent-current IC₅₀ of **53.1 ± 4.2 µM** and a transient-peak IC₅₀ above **500 µM** (Fig. 1D, seven animals). At 100 µM the persistent current was **25.6 ± 4.4%** of control and the transient peak was **94.6 ± 2.5%**. The six-second ramp from −80 to +10 mV gave **53.1 ± 4.8 µM** (Fig. 2D, six animals). The FDA review's earlier **41.8 µM** estimate remains a separate result. The 200 ms step supplies a timescale within the held WWOX field-event window, while neither this constant voltage step nor the six-second ramp reproduces a WWOX spike train. The field histogram contains **85–100 of 119** events under a second, depending on the treatment of the straddling bin. [Nakamura et al., Methods §§2.1–2.3, Figs. 1–2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ejphar.2019.05.007) · [Open article copy](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/332894399_Neuropharmacology_and_analgesia_effects_of_cenobamate_YKP3089_a_newly_developed_anti-epileptic_drug_on_voltage-gated_sodium_channels_in_rat_hippocampal_CA3_neurons) · [Held WWOX histogram](../../ganaxolone_chloride_recovery_20261123/wwox_burst_histogram.tsv)

After a 500 ms pulse to −20 mV, Fig. 5 recovery at −80 mV had fast and slow time constants of **4.7 and 25.7 ms** in vehicle and **11.7 and 134.6 ms** at 100 µM (five animals). Fig. 6 found **1.5 ± 0.3 mV** hyperpolarization at **10 µM** (seven cells reported in Results), a nominal bath within an adolescent plasma-based sensitivity scenario. Occlusion by riluzole or tetrodotoxin was tested at **100 µM**, where the experiment also found a rheobase rise from **65.3 to 130.6 pA** (n=9). The high-bath occlusion cannot establish causal attribution of the 10 µM effect. The rheobase increase challenges any presumption that the first physiological response is preserved whenever an isolated transient peak appears mostly spared. ## Matched comparison and decision

The public Sharma conference record reports GABA-evoked potentiation from **30 µM** in CA3 and a prolonged evoked IPSC decay in mouse CA1 with no peak change reported. FDA Table 4 lists a **42 µM** EC₅₀ for recombinant α4β3γ2 and **89 µM** for α5β3γ2. The table uses γ2 constructs. Native tonic currents and δ-containing receptors need their own measurement, and original agonist timing and concentrations remain unverified for the exact GABA comparisons. A negative flumazenil result cannot isolate the drug's GABA contribution from its sodium effects. [Sharma et al., conference abstract](https://doi.org/10.1212/WNL.92.15_supplement.P1.5-033) · [FDA review Table 4](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2019/212839Orig1s000OtherR.pdf)

Predicted discriminator: in the WWOX and gene-corrected preparations replay short native bursts with their observed **interspike intervals and inter-burst rest**, at matched and natural starting voltage. Measure free drug and pair the response of excitatory and inhibitory cells in the same experimental block. A sodium-only comparator must match both the persistent current and paired-pulse recovery across the sampled intervals. If it matches only the persistent current, extra burst suppression by cenobamate can still arise through sodium-channel recovery. After both sodium readouts are matched, a GABA contribution predicts additional reduction in late spiking that varies with tonic conductance and the measured GABA driving force. Expect the gain to disappear when the measured GABA component is also matched in the combined comparator. Preserve the first spike and physiological recruitment of inhibitory cells. Selective disruption of the latter, even if visible bursts fall, rejects the proposed functional advantage. If full pharmacological matching is unavailable, match the two outputs in the preparation or leave attribution unresolved.

**Park for an unselected WWOX pediatric trial.** Retain the compound as a symptomatic-circuit comparator. Published adolescents had post-dose plasma medians of **15.0–16.1 µg/mL total** (22.4–24.1 µM estimated free with adult binding), and the randomized adolescent PGTC subgroup had an imprecise response contrast. The prospective open-label focal-seizure trial aged 2–17 had no results posted in the checked registry. The public abstract of a 152-child DEE series has available-case responder rates of 64% and 79% at three and twelve months with **152 and 57 evaluable**, respectively. Do not read that trend as a fixed-cohort gain. Its genetic table could not be accessed through the publisher's public route. The existence of a WWOX case in the series is unresolved. The study's 10.5% reported seizure worsening, the distinct seizure types and the possibility of comedication effects make aggregate effects insufficient for WWOX attribution. [Adolescent exposure and age limits](adolescent_exposure_20261126.md) · [Aledo-Serrano et al., abstract and Table S4A description](https://doi.org/10.1002/epi.70460)

Reopen for a documented, attributable WWOX response with baseline seizure type and concomitant treatments, or a selective WWOX advantage after matching sodium kinetics at measured attainable free exposure. Use developmental endpoints only with persistent function and physiological recruitment assessed separately. A useful peer review would resolve the currently inaccessible genotype table or supply parent-selective juvenile free exposure. The recovery replay and the published transient/tonic controls suffice for the current comparator decision.
