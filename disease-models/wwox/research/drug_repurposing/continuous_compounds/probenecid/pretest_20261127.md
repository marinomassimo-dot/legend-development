# Probenecid, pannexin and purine feedback in WWOX loss of function

**Status.** Opening checkpoint for probenecid. A held Wwox-KO slice study reported divergent effects from carbenoxolone and a pannexin-directed peptide. Its plotted and tabulated peptide results disagree in size and direction, with neither supporting clear burst suppression. The probenecid question is narrower. At measured free parent, does suppressing pannexin-mediated purine release relieve feedforward excitation or remove a local brake, and can either effect be separated from organic anion transport? [Held WWOX source audit](../../../fulltext_dossiers/PMID34634460.md) · [Held target mapping and limitations](../../closed_loop_it3_20261028/README.md) · [Primary probenecid channel study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18596212/)

**Prediction.** At fixed initial neural recruitment and bath composition in juvenile cortical WT and Wwox-KO preparations, measure triggered extracellular ATP and its conversion to adenosine before later bursts. If PANX1 supplies an adenosine-mediated brake, exposure-aligned PANX1 inhibition predicts a smaller purine transient and more late recurrence with preserved first recruitment. If PANX1 instead amplifies excitatory purinergic signaling, reducing the ATP transient should reduce recurrence. The sign and timing of the effects should agree with an independent PANX1 perturbation and with receptor-specific counterchecks.

**Counterchecks.** Compare intact and occluded PANX1 responses at measured free drug and measure transporter-dependent substrate retention independently. Track pH, HCO3, baseline evoked inhibitory and excitatory recruitment, ATP recovery and purine breakdown. Neither broadly suppressing glutamate receptors nor changing circulating exposure of a second drug identifies PANX1. If the effect persists after independent PANX1 loss and follows altered organic-anion handling, attribute it to the alternative only after testing transporter dependence.
