# Cilostazol and first functional myelin in WWOX loss of function

28 November 2026 UTC. context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. Compound #194, INV-06.

**Decision.** Retain a conditional ex vivo PDE3/cAMP comparison and defer systemic pediatric WWOX priority. The [prespecified comparison](pretest.md) tests OPC differentiation, stage-specific survival and vascular protection as competing explanations. A first durable sheath and conduction through the same axons after washout are the functional outcomes.

## Exposure, pediatric availability and limits

- In a 30-person healthy-adult crossover, Table 3 reports average total parent Cmax **635.6 and 623.3 ng/mL** in the two tablet arms, approximately **1.72 and 1.69 µM** at an official parent mass of 369.46 g/mol. Applying the label's 95–98% plasma protein binding gives illustrative free parent **0.034–0.086 µM** across the two arms. The representative **3 µM nominal** cell exposure is approximately **35–89 times** that range. The comparison crosses sample matrices. Free drug in the OPC medium and in brain is unmeasured in these sources. Binding may differ by matrix, species and clinical state, so the ratio describes an exposure gap to resolve. [Adult crossover Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jpha.2014.08.001) · [Official molecular mass](https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000152646.pdf) · [Unit replay](exposure_replay.tsv)
- Adult systemic pharmacology includes active metabolites, especially 3,4-dehydro-cilostazol. The label reports potency relative to parent and predominantly CYP3A4 with a CYP2C19 contribution. Keep parent and metabolites separate when measuring free exposure and local engagement. Their brain distribution, local potency and simultaneous time courses would be needed before combining their contributions. The US label concerns tablets for adult intermittent claudication, has a boxed warning for heart failure, and identifies bleeding risk and inhibitor interactions. Pediatric safety and effectiveness are unestablished in the label. [US prescribing information §§4, 5, 7, 8.4 and 12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=77e77eaf-5556-43ac-844e-c0e29f02fe07&type=display)
- Published pediatric and young-adult moyamoya experience includes a two-institution retrospective **13-person**, **6-month to 21-year** perioperative series with aspirin and revascularization. Ten started before surgery and three afterward. It documents exposure in a vascular context with co-interventions and no comparator. The series reports one participant with intraoperative parenchymal hemorrhage and one with postoperative strokes, without establishing attribution. It does not bridge neural exposure, long-term developmental safety or WWOX efficacy. [Aggregate primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40974885/)

## One experimental comparison

**Inference.** The human organoid and single-pool OPC signals justify measuring local PDE3 activity as a baseline. The injury effects and the marker-denominator calculation leave transition versus retention unresolved. WWOX has a demonstrated neuronal contribution to the phenotype.

**Hypothesis.** At a developmental stage with slow OPC progression, inhibiting residual OPC PDE3 should elevate local cAMP before individual cells advance and form lasting new sheaths. The effect should depend on the OPC target when axon number, first recruitment and workload are comparable, and should close a deficit in WWOX.

**Experiment.** Compare neuronal WWOX loss and corrected controls in age- and stage-matched axon–OPC co-culture at controlled oxygen and measured free parent. Track a fixed cohort of initially identified OPCs over time and count transition, loss and division separately. Measure per-cell cAMP, verify engagement using a lineage-restricted PDE3 perturbation that tests occlusion and accounts for basal target effects, and record first compact sheaths per original viable axon and per originally tracked lineage. Pair the structural count with conduction measured after washout at matched axon recruitment. Retain time-matched oxygen-stress and no-stress measurements to determine whether a response requires the injury setting.

- Faster transition, stable first sheaths and better post-washout conduction at comparable survival support the developmental hypothesis if the change follows verified OPC engagement.
- More mature-labeled cells with unchanged transition time and reduced loss support survival. A marker-only change favors altered expression or cell composition. An effect confined to chemical stress, or a response that persists after verified removal of the relevant OPC engagement, lowers the proposed WWOX transfer.
- If the preparation lacks a baseline functional first-sheath deficit, or increased cAMP fails to improve durable first sheaths under matched neuronal activity, stop this comparison. Reopen systemic research priority only with reproducible native function, measured juvenile CNS exposure and a separate safety justification.

**Independent review.** A lineage-resolved WWOX dataset with timed transitions and sheaths, or a measured unbound juvenile PK bridge for the parent and metabolites, could change the decision and warrants targeted independent review if identified. The existing figure reconstruction changes the experiment by requiring directly observed fate and preservation of the original denominators.
