**Decision.** Retain valnoctamide as a conditional ex vivo comparator of phasic-current duration. There is an independent rat lipid-turnover result after repeated exposure. Hold it as a separate biological hypothesis with direct neural ACSL4 engagement unresolved. Defer pediatric WWOX priority given the missing free-exposure and native-inhibition data and the documented interaction with carbamazepine.

## Reproducible checks

Run `python3 -B replay.py` to regenerate [comparison.tsv](comparison.tsv). The 1 mM decay mean is **1.84×** baseline. A double-exponential decay with amplitude-weighted decay constant has area proportional to peak times weighted decay. The ratio of the printed group means yields a **1.75× decay-area proxy**. Cell-level peak–decay covariance and fitted traces are unavailable, and this estimate excludes rise and native chloride driving force. The flumazenil and diazepam comparisons support a functional distinction from canonical benzodiazepine-site modulation. They do not identify a physical site or prove which receptor population contributes.

On the rising limb, a standard idealized uncompetitive relation gives fractional reduction `(I/Ki)/(1 + Km/S + I/Ki)`. At 35 µM arachidonate and a nominal 0.5–0.6 mM valnoctamide, it gives **6.47–7.67%**. At 5 µM arachidonate the same range gives **3.91–4.66%**. These are illustrative, below the main test concentrations. They use total nominal concentrations and cannot predict free neural activity. The 2.595 AICc difference gives a relative two-model weight of **0.785**, conditional on the two fitted models. The PMC rendering of Eqs. iii and vi contains apparent symbol errors. This replay states the standard reduced model and relative AICc calculation explicitly. The 2014 slice paper's 0.455–1.26 mM brain range is an author estimate from dose and distribution assumptions and is not used as a direct measurement. ## WWOX-specific test

**Native inhibition.** The held analysis of P13–17 Wwox cortical slices found smaller spontaneous inhibitory events with imposed chloride. That dataset leaves native GABA reversal, input number and the postsynaptic contribution unresolved. Carry over its animal-level comparison and measure reversal under native chloride in the same preparation used for network responses. [Held WWOX inhibitory comparison](../../inhibitory_defect_sci47_20261112/README.md)

**Data.** Held bulk ACSL4 RNA is lower in P17 mouse cortex and week-15 human knockout organoids (log2(1 + abundance) differences **−0.52 and −0.69**). Hippocampus gives **+0.08** with a single-library omission range crossing zero. Two human pedigrees with pathogenic FACL4/ACSL4 variants and an independent juvenile-development hypothesis set limits on interpreting reduced ACSL4 as beneficial. A recent mouse *preprint* reports that neuronal Acsl4 deletion reduced initial excitatory transmission and LTP (Fig. 4m–o). Its mIPSC amplitude and frequency comparison did not test the valnoctamide decay effect. Functional genetic loss and an acute drug response are distinct interventions. [Held WWOX bulk contrasts](../tocopheryl_acetate/bulk.tsv) · [Human genetics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11889465/) · [Mouse preprint](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13142639/)

**Hypothesis.** In a WWOX preparation with preserved inhibitory driving force, valnoctamide could preferentially extend early phasic charge and interrupt late recurrence. A slower response could instead track arachidonate metabolism. Because the WWOX direction of ACSL4 activity is unknown and excitatory recruitment can be impaired by developmental loss of the enzyme, require a matched first response for both interpretations.

**Discriminating prediction.** Compare acute exposure, repeat exposure and verified washout in the same age- and region-matched WWOX and control preparation. Measure free parent by stereoisomer, native GABA reversal and absolute phasic charge, first excitatory and inhibitory recruitment, late recurrence, and time-resolved transfer of arachidonate into arachidonoyl-CoA and defined phospholipids. At matched workload, an early reduction in late activity that follows charge and reverses with parent washout before the lipid readout moves supports the phasic branch. A delayed effect that persists with measured parent cleared and phasic charge matched supports a separate mechanism if lipid flux changes and an independent ACSL4 control converges. If either effect accompanies weaker initial recruitment, the selective-recovery interpretation fails. If the GABA response is depolarizing at baseline, independently measure its effects on spiking before interpreting a larger current. Animals or donors are the independent units.

## Pediatric feasibility and checkpoint

Published studies distinguish a racemic oral product and individual preclinical isomers. The 173-participant adult mania monotherapy trial did not show benefit on its outcome and differs from a smaller positive adult risperidone add-on comparison. Neither informs spontaneous WWOX seizures. The published adult PK and epoxide interaction leave pediatric CNS exposure and developmental tolerability unresolved. No controlled WWOX pediatric study or age-specific neural free-exposure margin was located. Rodent developmental toxicity observations cannot establish pediatric safety. [Adult monotherapy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28605109/) · [Adult add-on](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20636634/) · [Interaction context](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8104783/)

Reopen for free juvenile neural exposure linked to reversible native phasic charge and selective recovery, or for a lipid effect with calibrated ACSL4 engagement that survives matched phasic activity. The independent genetic and repeated-exposure studies already constrain the interpretation. Further review of the same summaries cannot establish free neural concentration, native chloride, or target engagement. This dossier distributes published summary facts and original arithmetic.
