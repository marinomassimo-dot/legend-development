The comparison below uses aggregate published facts. It does not transfer either concentration or binding occupancy between compartments.

| Record | Source result | Limit for WWOX |
|---|---|---|
| Healthy older adults, 60–85 years | The published Schultz study reports 1.81 ng/mL mean TZ in lumbar CSF from three optional draws after one week on the stated study regimen. CSF is 23 ± 3% of **estimated free** plasma TZ. Plasma free fraction was calculated using about 92% binding. At the same regimen, whole blood ATP/Hb increased 6.22 ± 1.62% (n = 18), whole-brain FDG SUV fell 6.14 ± 2.15% (n = 18), and βATP/Pi changed 0.2 ± 1.4% (n = 16, p = 0.89). Methods and Figs. 2B and 3A–B. | Lumbar CSF and scan cohorts differ. The MRS peak-area ratio and FDG SUV are not production flux. The published text also shows lower arterial pressures at the relevant study exposures (Fig. 4), so adrenergic and delivery explanations remain open. No individual CSF values are carried here. [Published report](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718X251356503) |
| Purified mouse PGK1 | Chen Fig. 2d reports a small positive signal with nominal 2.5 nM terazosin and a positive range up to 0.5 µM, with inhibition reported at 2.5 and 25 µM (n = 4 trials). Coupled NADH proxy using recombinant mouse PGK1-His at 2 µg/mL, 1 mM ADP, 0.1 mM MgSO₄, pH 8.6 and GAPDH. | The 2.5 nM point is an assay condition, not an EC50 or a human intracellular activation threshold. Positive findings under conditions of the original assay do not establish an effect at similar nominal concentrations in native tissue. [Chen et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4412158/pdf) |
| Purified human PGK1 | Bütikofer reports an E318-based TZ binding estimate of 13 ± 7 µM for apo hPGK1. Separate titrations with ADP and a transition-state analogue support receptor-independent opening of the enzyme model at much higher nominal concentrations. Results around Figs. 1–2 and NMR Methods. | Binding affinity and conformation of purified hPGK1 are not catalytic potency. The titrations do not directly reproduce nanomolar turnover. No cellular occupancy can be estimated from lumbar CSF. [Bütikofer et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13714833/) |
| Primary hippocampal neuron culture under fuel limitation | Kokotos Figs. 1D–E and 2A–D report improved synaptic vesicle recycling and presynaptic ATP recovery at 10 µM nominal TZ after 12–16 h exposure. Assay glucose was 0.1 mM. Fig. 1E control/TZ n = 23/10. Fig. 2 control/TZ n = 12/17. Units are one neuron per coverslip. Results refer to loss of the recycling effect after PGK1 depletion and preservation with an analog with reduced alpha-1 potency (Fig. S2E–H, cited by the authors; supplement itself awaits inspection). | This is the closer functional precedent, but the exposure is nominal and much higher than reported CSF. ATP sensor kinetics show a net change in local content. The preparation does not measure WWOX function or directly balance ATP production against consumption. The separate PARK20 rescue in Fig. 3 uses PGK1 overexpression. [Kokotos et al.](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11338267/) |

On the parent basis (FDA SRS 387.43 g/mol), reported CSF mean is 4.6718 nM, 1.869 times the lowest positive concentration in the original mouse enzyme assay. Neither ratio is an exposure requirement, a pharmacokinetic margin or a disproof of activity. [FDA terazosin record](https://precision.fda.gov/ginas/app/ui/substances/8L5014XET7)

**Calibration check.** The published LC-MS methods report the lowest stated nonzero *plasma* standard as 15.6 pg/µL, numerically 15.6 ng/mL. This is 8.62 times the reported lumbar mean. It establishes a calibration question, not the CSF limit of quantification. The peer-reviewed supplement URL was inaccessible via Sage and PMC on this pass. The earlier report of the same cohort documents extraction and concentration of plasma, but those methods alone do not establish the CSF matrix calibration, handling of samples below the range or time relative to the last administration. No correction to the authors’ reported CSF number is justified. [Schultz published Methods and Fig. 2B](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718X251356503), [preprint LC-MS Methods](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2024.05.22.24307622v1.full)

**Interpretation and next step.** The positive nanomolar enzyme observation keeps the biochemical premise open. The neuronal experiment and adult fluid report leave a gap in functional exposure. A fit of adult FDG or resting ATP alone cannot discriminate greater production from lower demand or delivery. First retrieve the published CSF validation or a defensible lower-bound check, then seek a neural exposure–response with native metabolic demand that bridges toward measured free exposure. Test whether measured exposure improves early ATP production and subsequent evoked recovery at equivalent demand, survives alpha-1 matching, and is lost with a selective PGK1 dependence control. Recovery confined to higher exposure, unchanged recovery despite more lactate, or reproduction under matched alpha-1 block would lower priority. Continue as the only active molecule. No pediatric priority assigned at this checkpoint.
