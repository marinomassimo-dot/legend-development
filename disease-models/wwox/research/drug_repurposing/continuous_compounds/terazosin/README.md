# Terazosin and PGK1-dependent synaptic energy reserve

28 November 2026 UTC. Continuous compound #201, INV-07. context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. [Predicted comparison](pretest.md) · [Exposure comparison](first_comparison.md) · [Neural analysis plan](neural_analysis_plan.md).

**Disposition.** Retain as a conditional preclinical PGK1 comparator and defer pediatric WWOX priority. The positive cultured synapse assay used 10 µM nominal terazosin and severe glucose limitation. It shows better ATP-sensor preservation at the end of stimulation, with weaker evidence for a faster subsequent recovery independent of that preservation. The most accessible human central concentration is a lumbar CSF estimate from three older adults. Evidence of a functional PGK1 window at measured free juvenile neural exposure would change the decision.

## Replayed cellular response

[Kokotos et al. Figs. 1D–E and 2B–D, Results and live-imaging Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11338267/) used primary hippocampal neurons, 0.1 mM glucose and 10 µM nominal terazosin after 12–16 h preincubation. The publication used glutamate-receptor blockers and an imposed stimulus. ATP reporter values are fluorescence ratios. Reprocessing the [CC BY 4.0 authors’ data](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.11122117) by coverslip ID gives the following contrasts.

| Vesicle sensor fluorescence remaining 55 s after train 10 | 0.136 of the NH₄Cl-revealed signal (n=10) | 0.260 (n=23) | Lower residual fluorescence supports better retrieval/reacidification under repeated use in the recorded terminals. Initial recruitment was selected before analysis. |

Baseline reporter ratios were 2.51 with drug and 1.63 in controls. Normalization still leaves differences in sensor operating point, selected responding terminals, calcium or workload and the magnitude of the train-end deficit. The recovery increment is a sensitivity contrast, since recovery may depend on the starting deficit. It is neither an estimate of a rate constant nor evidence of equal recovery. Experimental day, litter and culture-preparation grouping are absent from the extracted tables. The bootstrap resamples coverslips conditionally and can be too narrow if records share preparations. It does not replace the original analysis.

**PGK1 and adrenergic checks.** The original supplementary data S2F compare treated cultures with and without PGK1 depletion. At the last train residual fluorescence is 0.404 versus 0.136 (n=13 vs 10), with one of 14 treated knockdown coverslips missing its last two trains. This panel has no concurrent vehicle × PGK1-knockdown factorial and knockdown can impair function by itself. S2H gives 0.070 for the reduced-alpha-1-potency analog versus 0.136 for terazosin (n=10 each, difference −0.065, interval −0.137 to +0.004). This supports a non-adrenergic route in this assay without establishing pharmacological equivalence. Parent coverslips recur across Fig. 1E and S2F/H. The comparisons share controls and are not independent replications. [Published article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11338267/) · [Supplementary result tables](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.11122117)

**Concentration coverage.** The earlier report of the same hippocampal preparation says 1 µM was ineffective on synaptic endurance and reports poor survival at 100 µM. This passage is absent from the corresponding peer-reviewed results and the final associated data provide no 1 µM group. Treat it as an unquantified same-study counter-observation. A distinct TDP-43 motor-neuron study used 2.5 µM and a 24 h exposure under arsenite stress. It reported higher glycolytic proxy values together with lower ATP-linked respiration. Main Fig. 4D had overall p=0.055 and the specific contrasts in the caption use stressed wild-type as reference. A lack of significant difference from wild-type is not a treated-versus-untreated mutant effect or proof of equivalence. The model supports a possible shift in ATP source, leaving total ATP turnover and evoked recovery open. Neither study tested near the reported single-digit-nM adult CSF. [Kokotos preprint Results and Fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.10.10.561760) · [Chaytow et al. author manuscript, pp. 20–22, Fig. 4C–I](https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/files/288865476/Chaytow_et_al._2022.pdf)

## Is PGK1 the limiting step in WWOX?

In juvenile loss-of-function hippocampus (GSE158684), reusing the existing nine-class assignments gives nuclear `Pgk1` 23.2→28.9 CPM in excitatory neurons and 27.2→25.1 CPM in inhibitory neurons. `Park7` goes from 23.4→29.5 and 23.6→31.5 CPM respectively. Across assigned classes, `Pgk1` is 28.7→34.9 CPM. The decomposition attributes +5.39 CPM to within-class differences and +0.77 to class weights. There is one pooled library per genotype. These descriptive counts do not support a universal loss of the target transcript and do not measure enzyme kinetics or DJ-1 function. An adverse possibility is that the intervention shifts fuel toward lactate while oxidative reserve remains constrained. A favourable possibility is that PGK1 becomes rate limiting during demand despite abundant RNA. The adult P47T profile has increased `Pgk1` in bulk forebrain, with a near-flat organoid contrast and lower contrasts in the distinct P17 bulk tissue sets. That variation supports testing the affected cell class rather than assuming the direction from bulk. [GSE158684](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158684) · [P47T and P17 comparison](../../p47t_it12_20261031/README.md) · [Cell counts](nuclear_cell_types.tsv) · [Mixture decomposition](nuclear_mixture.tsv)

**Falsifiable comparison.** In matched juvenile Wwox-loss and control preparations, first verify a reproducible failure during or after equal recruited demand at normal substrate delivery. Compare terazosin with vehicle and a functionally matched alpha-1 antagonist, and verify that the test still has reserve with the PGK1 control in place. Albumin-adjusted free parent and tissue/cellular exposure must be measured in that preparation. Support the PGK1 hypothesis only if exposure yields a PGK1-dependent gain in glycolytic ATP production and later evoked recovery after matching first firing, oxygen/glucose delivery and ionic work. Resolve the glycolytic contribution separately from oxidative ATP-linked flux and measure glucose carbon in lactate alongside pH. Compare demand and recovery windows separately. Persistence of higher lactate with lower oxidative ATP and flat evoked recovery, or disappearance of the effect after matching demand and adrenergic function, lowers the hypothesis. Read the alpha-1 comparison against actual receptor function, since neither class label nor the structural analog tests all alternative actions. Measure evoked excitatory and inhibitory recruitment independently of net ATP signal.

## Translational decision

In older healthy adults the published article reports an aggregate CSF terazosin mean of **1.81 ng/mL (4.67 nM parent, n=3)**. The 10 µM culture experiment is about 2,140 times higher on a nominal-compartment comparison. The earlier version of the adult article prints ng/mL for plasma where the final specifies µg/mL. The final cohort-mean total plasma (80 ng/mL), assumed 8% free fraction and reported free-plasma-normalized CSF fraction (23%) produce a rough 1.47 ng/mL CSF scale, versus 0.00147 if the earlier plasma units are taken literally. That checks unit consistency only. The means do not come from exactly the same group or averaging operation. The lowest plasma standard described by the journal is 15.6 ng/mL and is not a CSF limit of quantification. Accessible routes did not provide a CSF matrix-matched calibration and timing record to establish the uncertainty on 1.81 ng/mL. No assumption about the unbound neuronal concentration is justified from this. [Adult paper and Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.1177/1877718X251356503) · [Earlier report of the same cohort](https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.05.22.24307622) · [Replay](exposure_comparison.tsv)

The small randomized Parkinson pilot allocated 8 adults to terazosin and 5 to placebo. Five per group completed. Three stopped active treatment after dizziness and/or orthostatic hypotension. The reported brain βATP/Pi difference among completers (+0.19 versus −0.03 at the nominal 12-week visit, robust model p=0.02) is a spectral ratio with differential attrition. No selective target occupancy or WWOX benefit follows from it. The US label has no established pediatric safety and efficacy. A small pediatric urology report describes two children of ages 7 and 11 with additional management, with dizziness in one. It does not establish infant safety, dose, neurological effects, or central exposure. For pediatric research retain formulation and first-exposure cardiovascular effects as constraints. Concurrent medications affecting blood pressure and arousal may also confound exposure and neurological observation. No WWOX pediatric exposure or clinical efficacy cohort was identified. [Adult randomized pilot, §3.1–3.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8862665/) · [FDA label §5.1, §8.4](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/218139s000lbl.pdf) · [Pediatric primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15703097/)
