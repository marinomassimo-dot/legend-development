# Terazosin, ATP turnover and recovery of evoked activity

**Checkpoint #201, 28 November 2026.** Start after remacemide #200 closed on development/main. Earlier terazosin entries are screening and target-catalog rows. The present question differs from the pyruvate and dichloroacetate studies because the proposed intervention acts at glycolytic substrate-level phosphorylation, upstream of mitochondrial pyruvate oxidation.

**Question.** Does terazosin improve ATP turnover during a defined evoked demand in WWOX loss through phosphoglycerate kinase 1 (PGK1), or can a change in ATP content be explained by adrenergic suppression of demand, altered perfusion, or more glycolysis without durable functional recovery?

**Primary comparison.** First compare measured CSF terazosin in older adults with the published concentration response of purified human PGK1, carrying through CSF sampling times, analytical units, nucleotide and substrate conditions, and enzyme concentration. Check what the phosphorus MRS reports and whether the data measure concentration, a ratio, or turnover. Use the published human report and preprint as versions of one study. Evaluate the small randomized Parkinson pilot separately. Keep terms such as CSF, free plasma, parenchymal exposure and intracellular exposure distinct.

**Discriminating prediction.** Under equal imposed recruitment and oxygen/glucose delivery in a juvenile Wwox loss preparation and littermate controls, PGK1-dependent action should increase glycolytic ATP production during demand and accelerate recovery of ATP and evoked recruitment afterward. The change should track measured terazosin exposure and depend on PGK1 while surviving calibrated controls for alpha-1 antagonism. An alpha-1 comparator should be matched by functional receptor response, with subtype selectivity and exposure verified in that preparation. Measure labelled lactate routing, acidification, ATP production and utilization, and recovery of excitatory and inhibitory recruitment in the same preparation. Loss of the contrast after demand matching or reproduction by matched alpha-1 blockade weakens the proposed PGK1 mechanism. A larger resting ATP pool, lower FDG uptake, or more labelled lactate without a recovery gain would be insufficient.

**Decision rule.** First establish whether reported central exposure and a plausible functional PGK1 range overlap under comparable conditions. Support for a WWOX energy-recovery hypothesis requires the joint turnover and functional prediction. Adult measurements can bound feasibility. Reopening pediatric research priority requires a relevant juvenile exposure and native functional response. The next checkpoint should record which exposure or turnover premise survived and the comparison that could still decide it.
