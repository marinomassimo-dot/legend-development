# Phenytoin | What held juvenile WWOX recordings can constrain

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Unit and region.** Individual recorded cells (reported alongside animals) from acute L2/3 neocortical slices of neuronal Wwox-deletion mice and littermates, P13–17. Use the animal for genotype inference. For the effect sizes below, [table replay output](native_voltage.tsv) is simple subtraction of the original group means. [Breton et al., §2.6, Table 1, Fig. 6, Supplementary Fig. S4 and §§5.3 and 6.6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609180/)

- The resting potential of putative pyramidal cells was 5.63 mV more positive in KO than WT (−73.62 vs −79.25 mV, 40 cells from 15 animals vs 13 from 5). It was 1.89 mV more positive than heterozygote. The two values are distinct conditioning priors and preserve the littermate genotype distinction.
- For untyped interneurons, KO first AP amplitude at rheobase was 15.75 mV lower (45.03 vs 60.78 mV, 6 cells from 5 animals vs 5 from 4, with WT and heterozygotes pooled). The study did not detect a spike count difference in the 500 ms current steps. This sample does not establish high-frequency PV reserve or the effect of phenytoin on evoked inhibitory charge.
- Methods §5.3 exclude cells already spiking spontaneously. Fig. 6A shows example step responses and Supplementary Fig. S4 shows spike counts. They can motivate voltage and firing-rate scenarios. They do not supply the matched inhibitory and excitatory event-associated membrane histories, an identified presynaptic PV population, or estimates of late sodium charge. The article and supplement inspected and the local/raw-repository search did not yield downloadable traces. Fig. 3 uses voltage-clamp current at imposed holds during bursts and cannot supply these current-clamp voltage histories. Spontaneous inhibitory events with an imposed chloride gradient are a separate readout from absolute evoked charge and its preservation.

**Decision.** Retain the waveform comparison on native cells. Use recorded cell-class-specific histories when they exist, or label synthetic commands explicitly and vary starting potential and interspike recovery separately. At the same measured free exposure, separate first peak, late persistent charge and resurgent charge, and then check absolute evoked inhibition and PV recruitment. State dependence would follow the imposed history across genotypes. Loss of inhibition or the first response before suppression of late charge argues against a selective window. The Table 1 resting potentials alone cannot assign an effect size or a preferential therapeutic window. A methodological review could be useful once native curves exist, particularly for how resting-voltage and recovery differences are separated.

The first comparison provides a measured lower-concentration positive control for conditioning dependence. The second supplies a short-history countercheck on generalized preferential late-current block. Replay the WWOX and control histories in both genotypes, with a short recovered condition and a longer conditioned condition at each starting potential. This separates resting-voltage dependence from time spent depolarized. In each contrast show the absolute first peak beside subsequent sodium charge and evoked inhibition. Drug increments must be vehicle-corrected within the same history. Neither external tissue provides a WWOX-specific time constant or a presynaptic PV readout.

## Disposition

Retain phenytoin as a conditional comparator for controlled sodium availability and inhibitory reserve. Defer pediatric WWOX study priority until a native comparison shows preferential late-current or late-recurrence suppression at measured free exposure, with preserved first recruitment and evoked inhibition. The current evidence supports testing voltage history and duration. It supplies no native drug effect, no age-matched WWOX free CNS exposure and no attributable WWOX clinical response. The bulk-organoid SCN1A splice assay failed its prespecified information floor. [Clinical exposure and limits](pediatric_exposure_20261127.md) · [Human SCN1A pilot](scn1a_junction_pilot/README.md)
