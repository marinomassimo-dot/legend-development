# Phenytoin | Sodium-channel availability and developmental inhibitory reserve

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Selection.** Phenytoin is the only active compound. Its pediatric antiseizure use and an independently interpretable free-serum reference make it a useful comparator for the existing WWOX juvenile cortical excitability work. The held structural-spasm comparison used phenytoin as an acute negative comparator without matched neuronal sodium-channel engagement. Here the question is whether a WWOX circuit state preferentially exposes a late sodium-current component or instead makes fast-spiking interneurons vulnerable to use-dependent block. The scope is the active moiety. Oral free-acid suspension, sodium salt formulations and the fosphenytoin prodrug have separate exposure conditions. [Held WWOX cortical phenotype](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609180/) · [Held acute comparison](../carisbamate/exposure_and_disposition_20261127.md) · [Oral suspension label](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=560)

**Prespecified prediction.** If the useful phenotype is a late Na-current dependence caused by the WWOX circuit's voltage history, phenytoin should reduce post-recruitment sodium charge and later recurrence while preserving the first response, PV-cell high-frequency recruitment and evoked inhibitory charge. In age- and region-matched WWOX and control preparations, replay both measured voltage histories in both genotypes at the same verified unbound exposure. A drug effect that follows the replayed voltage history supports a state-dependent vulnerability. A genotype-specific difference surviving waveform matching prompts a separate channel availability, subunit or exon 5A/5N measurement. Loss of PV recruitment or evoked inhibition before preferential late-current suppression argues against a useful window. Separate persistent and resurgent currents from the first transient peak.

## Opening exposure comparison

The reference interval in the oral label is 1–2 µg/mL free serum phenytoin. Using molecular mass 252.268 g/mol gives **3.96–7.93 µM**. This is a serum reference for interpreting nominal in vitro measurements. Free brain concentration and binding or loss in the assay require separate data. The first accessible direct human comparison is from adults with acute brain injury. It is a context check on the bridge from serum to brain. **First countercheck on the serum bridge.** Tisdall et al. report 31 paired samples from eight adults with acute brain injury. The correlation calculated from eight participant means was r = 0.34 (reported p = 0.41). In vitro probe recovery was 2.1% in two tested catheters. Retain this result as evidence that a fixed free-plasma-to-brain conversion is unsubstantiated in that preparation. It supplies no estimate for pediatric WWOX. Distinguish dialysate concentration from the recovery-corrected extracellular estimate before any exposure comparison. [Tisdall et al., Results and Fig. 1B](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/2467/1/2467.pdf)

**WWOX transcript check.** SCN2A was modestly lower in mouse neuronal-deletion cortex and hippocampus (KO-minus-control log2(1+TPM) −0.21 and −0.38) and in one human organoid background (−0.17 log2(1+CPM)). Omitting any one library retained the direction, with one cortical omission close to zero. SCN8A was lower in both mouse bulk contrasts (−0.41 and −0.79). The human SCN8A contrast was near zero with sign changes on omission. In the separate pooled mouse hippocampal nuclei, assigned excitatory Scn8a moved from 433 to 476 CPM and assigned inhibitory Scn1a from 440 to 511 CPM. The latter contrasts each come from one library per arm. These abundance measurements prioritize comparisons across preparations and cell classes. They cannot establish which channel carries late current, a gain in sodium-channel function or an exon 5N fraction. **Pediatric feasibility.** Oral suspension has a pediatric label in specified seizure indications. The active compound is highly protein-bound, with saturable pharmacokinetics and clinically important interactions. Distinguish protein displacement from effects on clearance and include serum free phenytoin when comparing preparations. A serum reference from another clinical indication supplies no exposure or benefit criterion for WWOX. Evaluate interaction and adverse-event evidence by formulation and population before any move beyond the mechanistic comparison. [Oral label §§2.4, 2.5, 7 and 12.3](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=560)
