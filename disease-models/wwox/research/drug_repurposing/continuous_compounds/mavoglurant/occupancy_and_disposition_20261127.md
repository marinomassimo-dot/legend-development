# Mavoglurant in WWOX loss of function

`context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`. Adult healthy-brain PET tracer displacement (%), pediatric plasma mavoglurant (ng/mL), fragile X communication (log₁₀ WCS units) and WWOX expression (mouse cortical and hippocampal bulk TPM, human cerebral organoid bulk CPM, mouse hippocampal nuclear CPM).

**Disposition.** Park pediatric WWOX trial prioritization. Retain mavoglurant as a selective preclinical tool to test whether mGlu5 drives late recurrence in a particular WWOX cell state. Adult PET shows brain engagement. The fragile X trial found no pediatric language advantage and excluded recent seizures. Neither source shows that WWOX networks benefit at exposures that preserve adaptive signaling.

## Adult PET replay

Streffer et al. enrolled eight healthy men in total, including two replacements after radiotracer synthesis/injection problems. Six completed, with a two-cohort sequential design. The headline estimates, 27%, 59%, 74% and 85% at the four single-dose levels, reproduce from the published dose model `100 × dose / (69 mg + dose)` at **3–4 hours**. Table 1's PK counts are 5, 3, 4 and 3, while Fig. 3A shows three plotted points per dose. Cohorts and repeated measures share participants. They cannot be counted as independent adults. [Streffer et al., Methods §§2.3–2.7, Fig. 1, Table 1 and Results §3.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.117785) · [Dose replay](adult_pet.tsv)

The imaging measurement was reduction from baseline of plasma-referenced tracer binding potential. The fitted asymptote of about **65% tracer displacement** was normalized to 100% inferred occupancy. The two-tissue analysis used metabolite-corrected arterial input. The procedural description explicitly states that the lower-dose visits lacked arterial sampling. Details of how all lower-dose input functions were handled are limited in the paper. The pooled estimate excludes cerebellum and white matter. The regional concentration midpoints in the included areas span 35–58 ng/mL, with pooled estimate 43 ng/mL and reported standard error 20. These are assay- and model-specific estimates. [Streffer et al., §§2.4–2.6, Results §3.4 and Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.117785)

Digitizing the 12 Fig. 3A markers and fitting `D = Dmax × C / (K + C)` gives **K≈40.6 ng/mL** and **Dmax≈64.7%**, with residual RMSE about ten percentage points. Omitting a dose cohort in turn moves the midpoint from **23.2 to 65.8 ng/mL**. This is a sensitivity analysis of plotted markers from a small repeated-measures pilot. It supplies no participant-level confidence interval. Inserting the Table 1 mean plasma concentration in the published concentration model gives 78% at the 100 mg group mean, compared with 59% from the dose model at that dose. The dose and concentration curves are separate fits. The concentration of a PK group and mean of its nonlinear occupancy estimates also differ. The 400 mg points have an approximate average **64% tracer displacement**, consistent with the text's ~63% and distinct from the fitted occupancy estimate. [Fig. 3A](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.117785) · [Digitized markers](adult_PET_figure3_points.tsv) · [Fit sensitivity](adult_PET_figure3_fit.tsv)

## Mechanistic comparison in WWOX

| Untreated model | GRM5 Δ log₂(1+abundance) | ARC Δ log₂(1+abundance) |
|---|---:|---:|
| Juvenile mouse cortex, control/KO 4/3 | +0.027 [−0.232, +0.272] | −2.582 [−2.675, −2.483] |

| Human cerebral organoids, 2/4 | +1.039 [+0.877, +1.213] | −0.774 [−0.869, −0.667] |

The intervals are leave-one-library-out ranges, with TPM in mouse and CPM in organoids. Untreated ARC RNA fell in all three comparisons. GRM5 direction varies, and in the organoids HOMER1 fell (−0.374, omission range −0.431 to −0.316), even as GRM5 rose. HOMER1 total RNA combines isoforms. None of these comparisons measures stimulus-evoked translation, cell-specific receptor coupling or compensatory kinetics. Pooled mouse hippocampal nuclei supply one library per genotype. Excitatory nuclear GRM5 CPM were 2910 vs 2639 and inhibitory 2481 vs 2382, with no biological variance estimate. ARC counts are sparse in the inhibitory fraction. A bulk expression increase alone is inadequate evidence for mGlu5 pathway overshoot. [Bulk replay](bulk_mglur5_panel.tsv) · [Nuclei replay](hippocampal_nuclei_panel.tsv) · [GSE156243](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE156243)

The fragile X mechanism includes deregulation of translational control by FMRP. A published mouse CA1 result found that group-I mGluR-dependent synaptic depression persisted despite protein synthesis inhibition in Fmr1-null mice. Transfer the assay distinction to WWOX and require native receptor-dependent effects to be measured there. ARC at baseline and stimulus-induced ARC translation are different variables, and enhancing or suppressing plasticity could have different effects across stages. [Nosyreva and Huber, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16452252/) · [Held WWOX rationale](pretest_20261127.md)

**Discriminating observation.** In stage- and genotype-matched WWOX and control preparations, measure free parent and a native mGlu5-dependent proximal response independently of burst counts. Obtain the mGlu5-dependent component of a group-I response using a selective pharmacological or orthogonal target control, accounting for mGlu1. At matched first evoked recruitment, test whether modest engagement preferentially reduces prolonged post-input excitation or late recurrence while preserving inhibitory recruitment and inducible Arc protein or an orthogonal adaptation readout. Read basal ARC and induced translation separately, because a basal deficit could coexist with excess acute receptor signaling. An effect that vanishes on matching initial input, or a loss of adaptation despite reduced recurrence, weakens selective recovery. A larger effect in organoids is predicted only when their baseline mGlu5-dependent response is measurably greater.
