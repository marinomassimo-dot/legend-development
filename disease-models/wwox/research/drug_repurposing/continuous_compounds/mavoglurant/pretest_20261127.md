# Mavoglurant and activity-dependent mGlu5 function in WWOX loss of function

**Selection.** Mavoglurant (AFQ056) is an investigational mGlu5 negative allosteric modulator with measured adult brain engagement and a randomized study in children aged 3–6 years with fragile X syndrome. The latter offers a strict comparison of developmental function under an mGlu5 intervention. The held WWOX RNA results suggest context dependence. GRM5 increases in the human organoid comparison, falls in juvenile mouse hippocampus and has no stable direction in juvenile mouse cortex under single-library omission. Choose a native mGlu5-dependent readout before interpreting those expression shifts. [Adult PET study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2021.117785) · [FXLEARN results](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI171723) · [Held GRM5 table](../jbpos0101/bulk_receptors.tsv)

**Prediction.** If late WWOX recurrence depends on excess mGlu5 signaling, verified partial target engagement should reduce the sustained post-input response or delayed resynchronization at matched first recruitment and preserved activity-dependent translation, short-term adaptation and inhibitory recruitment. The relevant concentration-response should track measured mGlu5-dependent function in each model. If the RNA increase is compositional, compensatory or limited to a developmental state, late benefit should disappear when first recruitment is matched. Loss of adaptive signaling at an exposure that lowers burst counts would favor broad suppression. A stronger organoid effect is conditional on a measured baseline mGlu5-dependent functional excess and is not inferred from transcript abundance.
