# Brivaracetam, sequence of vesicle release and recruitment of inhibition

**Question fixed before numerical extraction.** Does brivaracetam plausibly damp recurrent excitatory recruitment more than it weakens inhibitory restraint in a WWOX-deficient network? A short cortical field burst, a recurring burst and an imposed high-frequency hippocampal train are different exposures. A quantifiable mechanism is that vesicle recycling and prior activity determine which terminals accumulate a drug effect first. The opposing hypothesis is that active inhibitory terminals become affected earlier, leaving little early excitatory selectivity.

1. Audit the direct brivaracetam source for first-response amplitude, train frequency, pulse number, cumulative response, recovery, drug concentration and incubation. Report a fall of late responses relative to the first separately from a fall of absolute train output. Seek a direct inhibitory comparator under the same drug. Use levetiracetam measurements as a class comparator with its own label. A result from levetiracetam cannot fill a missing brivaracetam response.
2. Compare the time window with the actual Wwox cortical recording conditions. At matched voltage and activity, prioritize the direct compartmental experiment if brivaracetam-selective loss of absolute excitatory charge exceeds inhibitory loss by at least 10 percentage points. A reversed sign gives priority to potential disinhibition. This is a research threshold. If native charge, inhibitory data or a matched exposure are missing, report that the sign is unidentified. Use equally plausible opposite outcomes as counterexamples.
3. Identify free exposure, formulation and age support for a contemplated molecular or electrophysiological comparison. Licensed pediatric use for a particular indication, chronic developmental outcomes and evidence for WWOX-specific benefit answer different questions.

**Competing mechanism for the next comparison.** Depolarized Wwox-deficient pyramidal neurons could show increased sensitivity to state-dependent sodium current suppression. Inhibition of an outward M-type potassium current could oppose that effect. At matched membrane voltage, cell type and free exposure, prioritize an intrinsic mechanism only if the net change in repetitive firing survives isolation from synaptic input. A change in one isolated current without a matching firing change is insufficient.
