# Do the effects on ion currents predict firing in WWOX cortex?

At 10 μM brivaracetam, measured mean M-type potassium current fell from 78 to 42 pA (46.2%) and hyperpolarization-activated current from 347 to 313 pA (9.8%) in pituitary-derived GH3 cells. The hippocampal measurement was different: at 3 μM peak sodium current in the mHippoE-14 cell line fell from 1.97 to 0.95 nA (51.8%). These are ratios of group means, without within-cell uncertainty. In the **simulation** labelled 10 μM the authors chose a 30% reduction in M conductance, a 50% reduction in sodium conductance and a doubling of calcium-activated potassium conductance, among other changes. The model borrows measurements from different preparations; its lower firing rate cannot identify the sign in a WWOX neuron. Current amplitude and maximal model conductance are different quantities. [Hung et al., Results 3.1, 3.3, 3.7, 3.9 and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8067033/)

The counterexample is a direct output measurement. In adult mouse CA1 pyramidal neurons one hour of 100 μM brivaracetam produced no detected reduction in sustained repetitive firing from a holding voltage of −60 mV. Carbamazepine changed firing in the same assay. Primary rat cortical neurons also retained repetitive firing despite a sodium-current effect under voltage clamp. Neither preparation tested WWOX loss, and failure to detect suppression is not an equivalence test. Phasic rebound, developmental age and the cell's natural resting potential remain plausible reasons for a different result. [Niespodziany et al., Results, Figures 2–5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4359682/)

**Decision.** Lower priority for an intrinsic firing or HCN correction inferred from the simulation. The discriminating comparison measures spontaneous resting potential, rebound spike probability and sustained firing in WWOX and matched control cortex. Compare at the natural resting potential and at matched voltage with synaptic input suppressed. Loss of the genotype-by-drug difference when voltage is matched favors state dependence. A response confined to network recordings leaves release or glial feedback as alternatives. Neither the cell-line percentages nor the PET occupancy connect exposure to developmental benefit.
