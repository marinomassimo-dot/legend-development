# Brivaracetam in WWOX disease when levetiracetam has already been used

**Decision.** Separate prior levetiracetam exposure, current exposure at the time of adding brivaracetam, and brivaracetam after clearance of levetiracetam. These comparisons estimate different effects. In a simple shared-site model, brivaracetam may replace levetiracetam on a substantial fraction of SV2A while adding relatively little *total* occupancy. Clinical evidence for specific WWOX benefit and preferential suppression of excitatory release is still missing. This distinction matters in an actual WWOX series and is testable under controlled conditions. [Previous cortical waveform analysis](README.md)

## Which comparison applies to the reported WWOX cohort?

Oliver et al. distinguished all medicines trialed from medicines current at reporting by **underlining** the latter in Table 2. We parsed the original markup for all 13 participants. Eleven had trialed levetiracetam, four were currently taking it, and one had trialed and was currently taking brivaracetam. That participant also listed levetiracetam among previous drugs, without an underline. The table therefore records **zero current BRV–LEV pairs**, while leaving historical overlap, order of starting drugs, plasma concentrations and individual drug response unresolved. Three participants were currently taking phenobarbital, six valproate and none carbamazepine. A total of eight had previously or currently received valproate. These are counts in a small ascertained cohort, not population prevalences. [Oliver et al., Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.17542) · [Reproduced exposure categories](cohort_exposure.tsv)

## Calibration with measured single-drug exposures

Finnema et al. paired plasma concentration with SV2A tracer occupancy in healthy adults. We parsed 17 BRV observations in nine people and seven LEV observations in seven people from Table 2. Given the single-ligand curve C/(K+C), least-squares fitting of those rounded observations gives K = 0.465 mg/L for BRV and 4.015 mg/L for LEV, reproducing the published 0.46 and 4.02. Equal total weight per person gives 0.481 and 4.015. Omitting one person at a time gives 0.470–0.500 for BRV and 3.822–4.628 for LEV. The mean absolute error on observations from omitted individuals was 3.1 and 5.0 occupancy points, respectively. All fits refer to total plasma concentration as an empirical predictor. These are apparent half-occupancy concentrations. They do not measure free synaptic concentration or the fraction of neurotransmitter release inhibited. [Primary PET measurements, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6532410/) · [Reconstructed rows](pet_rows.tsv) · [Fits and sensitivity](pet_fits.tsv)

The pediatric oral-solution pharmacokinetic study covered ages from one month to under 16 years, on background antiseizure medicines. Its protocol **excluded levetiracetam** and patients with hepatic impairment. It reported a model-predicted mean average BRV concentration of **2.10 mg/L** (SD 0.51) in the subgroup at the maximum scheduled study exposure without enzyme inducers. This is a comparator within the observed adult concentration range, not a measured pediatric brain exposure or a proposed therapeutic target. The study also estimated higher apparent clearance with phenobarbital and carbamazepine (+40.8% and +47.9%) and lower clearance with valproate (−10.1%). The proportional concentration sensitivity below assumes unchanged input, steady state and inverse clearance scaling. [Pediatric study, Data, Table 1 and metabolite results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5423986/)

## How much occupancy could an addition change?

At illustrative BRV concentration Cb = 2.10 mg/L, the adult single-drug curve predicts 82.0% occupancy. For simultaneous ligands at competitive equilibrium let x = Cb/0.46 and y = Cl/4.02. The BRV fraction is x/(1+x+y), the LEV fraction is y/(1+x+y), and the baseline on LEV alone is y/(1+y). The common-site assumptions include transfer of the adult single-drug plasma-to-site relationship and no cooperative or kinetic effects. Simultaneous occupancy was not measured in either source, the concentrations come from different participants, and each PET estimate averages heterogeneous neuronal terminals.

| LEV plasma comparator from adult PET | Predicted occupancy on LEV alone | Total with BRV | Extra total occupancy | Sites occupied by BRV in mixture |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 mg/L | 0% | 82.0% | 82.0 points | 82.0% |
| 3.65 mg/L | 47.6% | 84.6% | 37.0 points | 70.5% |
| 16.2 mg/L | 80.1% | 89.6% | 9.5 points | 47.6% |
| 23.6 mg/L | 85.4% | 91.3% | 5.8 points | 39.9% |

In this calculation, at a LEV comparator above 15.47 mg/L the BRV increment drops below ten total-occupancy points. This algebraic cut point is a sensitivity illustration. It does not prescribe a concentration or establish a clinical threshold. At 16.2 mg/L LEV, the increment becomes 9.2 points with person-weighted fits. Applying each pediatric clearance covariate separately yields 7.8 points with phenobarbital, 7.6 with carbamazepine and 10.0 with valproate using published K values. Population means cannot establish how an individual child would respond to these combinations. [All exposure and model scenarios](occupancy_scenarios.tsv)

## Which result could change the decision?

At matched measured total SV2A occupancy, compare LEV alone, BRV alone, and combined exposure in the same WWOX and control preparations. Measure first-pulse and cumulative absolute excitatory and inhibitory charge, recycling and recovery over repeated bursts. A persisting drug difference at matched occupancy would support a difference in release coupling, timing or an additional target. Similar responses at matched occupancy, accompanied by a small additional effect on an LEV background, would support a shared occupancy ceiling. Include exposure duration and firing history as independent variables. Regional PET occupancy alone cannot establish the membrane polarity, cell class, inhibition of release or clinical threshold.

An adult kinetic reanalysis of **a subset of the original PET observations** estimated BRV time to half occupancy at 1.9–4.1 minutes after intravenous administration. Only one or two volunteers contributed to each BRV exposure. The distribution and uptake times do not identify how an already-bound drug affects the next short WWOX burst. In the EU product information, the regulator describes limited concomitant-LEV subgroup data without an observed additional clinical benefit. That observation is compatible with several mechanisms and does not establish a ceiling in WWOX. The EU indication specifies adjunctive therapy for partial-onset seizures from two years, while the younger patients in the PK study inform exposure. Background medicines, timing, seizure type and age remain necessary for any interpretation.
