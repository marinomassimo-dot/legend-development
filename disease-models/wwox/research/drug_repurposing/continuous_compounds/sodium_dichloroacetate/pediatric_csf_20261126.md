# Sodium dichloroacetate · direct pediatric central exposure and disposition

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

## Direct observations

- **Pediatric oral exposure.** The free primary abstract from Kuroda et al. reports **125 µg/mL DCA in CSF** when blood DCA was **250 µg/mL** in a 15-year-old with mitochondrial encephalomyopathy and lactic acidosis after oral treatment with accumulation. The reported mass ratio is **0.50**. A separate treated newborn had **25 µg/g** in brain at autopsy. The autopsy measurement has a different matrix and patient and is excluded from the ratio. The abstract does not provide the analytical calibration, exact sample timing, free fractions or steady-state status. This is direct pediatric evidence of central access in a high-exposure case series. [Kuroda et al., freely accessible primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3099068/)
- **Adult toxicity with measured parent.** In a 46-year-old with metastatic melanoma, Brandsma et al. reported encephalopathy and severe sensorimotor axonal polyneuropathy after oral DCA and coexposure to vitamin A. CSF DCA measured by LC–MS/MS was **78 µg/mL** two days after withdrawal and **11 µg/mL** on day 16, with no matched serum measurements. A two-point monoexponential calculation gives an apparent decline half-time of **4.95 days**. Two CSF samples cannot identify clearance, and the coexposure and clinical setting preclude using 78 µg/mL as a toxicity threshold. [Brandsma et al., pp. 2099–2100, author-hosted copy](https://thomasdorlo.rbind.io/pdf/brandsma_severe_2010.pdf)

## Reproducible comparison

The pediatric case's CSF value corresponds to **0.83–0.97 mM** across the sodium-salt and unlabelled acid-equivalent mass conventions. The range reflects reporting basis and gives no estimate of free intramitochondrial DCA. The case's reported blood concentration of 250 µg/mL is substantially above the separate five-child study's **35 µg/mL mean plasma peak** after six months. The case abstract labels the sample as blood, and the sampling and treatment conditions differ. The CSF/blood ratio of 0.50 is a single high-exposure observation. Its applicability to a lower-exposure cohort remains untested. [Pediatric plasma comparison, Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2613565/) · [Code](central_exposure.py) · [Calculated results](central_exposure.tsv)

At a nominal plasma concentration of 35 µg/mL, a CSF concentration equal in molar units to the held **0.48 mM core-bound PDK2 apparent IC₅₀** would require a hypothetical CSF/plasma ratio of **1.77–2.07**. The enzyme value is a reference for designing a mechanistic comparison. It is not a required brain concentration or a therapeutic threshold. Target engagement may occur below it, and exposure in CSF, plasma, neural cytosol and mitochondria can differ. The case series and toxicity report cannot establish an exposure margin for chronic WWOX treatment. [Held kinase source, Table 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19833728/)

## Disposition and result that would reopen it

DCA remains a **reference comparator** for PDK-mediated effects. Park it for an unselected pediatric WWOX trial. The new case report resolves whether oral access has ever been measured in a pediatric patient. The therapeutic-exposure question remains open. The controlled PDCD trial's biochemical effect and inconclusive motor outcome, the age- and time-dependent exposure and the separately documented neuropathy limit clinical transfer.

Reopen for WWOX-selective recovery of labelled PDC flux and ATP after matched activity, at measured intracellular DCA compatible with a tolerable exposure. A fall in PDHA1 phosphorylation with persistently low flux would favour a downstream limit over failure to engage PDK. DLAT oxidation per unit total DLAT is one candidate to test. If DCA accumulation is low and an independently delivered PDK perturbation recovers flux, an uptake limit is more plausible. The present result leaves the experimental comparison intact. Independent review could change the decision if it supplies transferable pediatric PK or a WWOX-specific exposure–response dataset. A rereading of the single historical case alone cannot supply either. [Previous WWOX comparison](central_target_20261126.md) · [Pediatric phase III study](https://doi.org/10.1172/jci.insight.200149)
