# Does a direct KCNQ effect have room to grow?

**Primary comparison.** Manville et al. report a maximal 3.5-fold KCNQ2/3 current at −60 mV relative to vehicle in a frog oocyte expression system, with EC50 0.7 µM for BHB (Fig. 1H–I). Homomeric KCNQ2 lacked the corresponding response in the tested conditions. Their methods identify a Sigma BHB stock prepared in ethanol. The inspected methods provide neither a catalogue identifier nor an explicit enantiomer. The current compound is D-BHB; transfer of this curve to a chemically verified D preparation is an assumption. The reported EC50 pertains to augmentation of current at a specified voltage. It is not a binding constant or a direct measure of channel occupancy. [Manville et al., Figs. 1 and 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6994816/)

**Correction to the population used as the exposure reference.** The channel paper describes the fasting measurements in Pan et al. as pediatric. Pan et al. measured healthy adults, and distinguished their own cohort from earlier observations in children. The adult occipital 9-mL MRS voxel gave group means of 0.05 ± 0.05 mM before fasting and 0.98 ± 0.16 mM after three days. This is tissue BHB; the accessible concentration at a neuronal channel and the stereochemical composition cannot be inferred from that signal. The nonfasted mean is close to its reported dispersion. These data cannot establish saturation in an individual child. [Primary adult MRS report](https://doi.org/10.1097/00004647-200010000-00012)

**What difference does baseline make?** If the local concentrations were 50 and 980 µM, using the published 0.7 µM half-effect and 3.5-fold maximal current with an assumed unit Hill slope gives a **0.95% increase in current** between the two states. The modeled response starts at 98.6% of maximal *augmentation*. If local concentrations were one tenth of the tissue means the increase becomes 9.1%; at one hundredth it becomes 63.3%. These ratios are explicit sensitivity scenarios, not measurements of brain partitioning. With local concentrations equal to tissue and slopes 0.7–1.5 the estimated current increment is 0.12–3.09%. Voltage, local pH, access to the relevant side of the channel, native subunit mixtures, kinetics and disease state could alter the curve. A large response after adding BHB to a previously ketone-free bath would not by itself establish an incremental effect over a physiological baseline. **Next comparison.** Seek absolute BHB and stereochemical specificity in pediatric CSF and human interstitial measurements. Distinguish total ketone bodies from BHB, and relative metabolomics intensities from absolute concentrations. Compare native M-current and labelled D-BHB oxidation in WWOX with matched background exposure, pH and osmolarity. A reproducible current increase before fuel oxidation at physiological baseline would strengthen the channel hypothesis. Improved energy recovery without an incremental current increase would favor the fuel hypothesis. A clinical response to ketogenic nutrition would not separate these routes.
