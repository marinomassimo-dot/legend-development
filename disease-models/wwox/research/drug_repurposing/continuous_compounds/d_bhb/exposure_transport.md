# D-BHB exposure, SLC16A7 and M-current in WWOX loss
context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. Prior inputs were the Manville channel curve, the adult Pan voxel experiment and the WWOX diet ascertainment. This extension tests the assumptions used to connect them.

**Decisional result.** Retain D-BHB for a laboratory comparison. The first readout to add is transport into the WWOX neuron, upstream of oxidation. A physiological baseline and a measured concentration at the cell remain necessary to interpret any increase in acute M-current. The confidence in a pediatric trial of the isolated molecule remains low.

**WWOX reanalysis.** The eight-gene panel covers KCNQ2/3/5, MCT1/2 and BDH1/OXCT1/ACAT1. Compare mean log2(1 + expression) in KO minus control within each bulk dataset (TPM in mouse, CPM in human), omit one library at a time, and re-estimate direction using hippocampal nuclei assigned to nine broad classes. Reference helpers, counts and assignments are reused from the existing thiamine and ganaxolone analyses. The model_screen.py script accepts the archived files directory and writes bulk.tsv, cell_types.tsv and mixture.tsv. Datasets: GSE158682, GSE156243, GSE158681 and GSE158684. Small n; leave-one-out ranges are sensitivity checks, not confidence intervals. The human model contains two control and four KO libraries; bulk mixtures and maturation can differ. The single-nucleus comparison has one pool per arm; nuclei within it do not supply biological replication. CPM shifts are relative, and transcript is not membrane-localized protein or transport activity.

SLC16A7 stayed lower under each single-library omission in all three bulk datasets. Its pooled difference of −62.7 CPM decomposes into −60.6 from changes within assigned classes and −2.1 from differences in their weights (the symmetric two-factor decomposition). Excitatory nuclei shifted from 161 to 90 CPM and inhibitory nuclei from 101 to 66. Broad class labels leave maturation and subtype composition unresolved. The opposite bulk and nuclear directions for KCNQ3 challenge a uniform loss of the channel. The enzyme data do not establish global inability to oxidize ketones. Mouse tissue and pooled nuclei should not be counted as independent human replications. **Discriminating observation.** Compare WWOX-LoF and matched corrected neurons of the same maturity from a measured nonzero background, with identical extracellular labelled D-BHB, pH, lactate and glucose. Measure initial intracellular isotope appearance, then downstream labelled carbon and energy recovery during demand, alongside native M-current. A lower initial uptake with proportionate downstream label supports limited delivery. Equal entry with lower downstream label favors an intracellular metabolic limitation. Equal entry and oxidation across genotypes would weaken both WWOX-specific metabolic explanations. A fast incremental current at constant metabolic flux would support a separate channel effect; test dependence on KCNQ2/3 and quantify excitatory and inhibitory recruitment. Multiple mechanisms can coexist. Include aqueous-volume/cell-number normalization, external carryover blanks, and matched membrane localization of MCT2, since lower RNA cannot identify the step. The predicted transport difference is relative to matched control; raising systemic ketones above its baseline has no demonstrated WWOX benefit in these data.

**Next evidence.** Look for original calibrated CSF or brain extracellular BHB measurements with fed-state baseline, population and stereochemical assay. Candidate leads for fasting-only measurement: PMIDs 3665092 and 7641677. Prioritize direct local D-BHB measurements and a WWOX tracer or protein-localization dataset over additional transcript panels. Keep one active compound.

**A concentration threshold that can falsify the simple model.** Assume the published voltage and potency, I(0)=1, I(infinity)=3.5, EC50=0.7 µM and Hill slope=1. A measured baseline above 4.8 µM leaves less than a 10% relative M-current increase even at saturating exposure. This is obtained by solving I(Cbase) = 3.5/1.10. Ten percent is an illustrative size of detectable effect, not a clinical threshold. The other assumptions, including the identity of the assayed stereoisomer and applicability of the expressed channel to native WWOX neurons, are first-order uncertainties. Measuring a larger increase at a higher directly measured baseline would invalidate the transferred curve or its physical assumptions. See current_bound.py and current_bound.tsv for slope and effect-size sensitivity.
