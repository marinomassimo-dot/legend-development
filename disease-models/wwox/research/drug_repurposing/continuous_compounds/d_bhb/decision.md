# Park the isolated D-BHB molecule for WWOX pediatric trial priority

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. The previous checkpoint raised two competing explanations: acute M-current modulation and substrate delivery. The comparison below asks whether a human cortical readout can identify their direction or discriminate them.

**Acute human functional evidence.** Ten healthy adults received a ketone ester in a fixed-order, within-person study. At 60 minutes mean plasma BHB rose from 140 to 3036 µmol/L while glucose fell from 97 to 80 mg/dL. EEG at 60 minutes measured visually evoked responses; MRS at approximately two hours measured occipital metabolites. Neither measured local extracellular D-BHB, M-current or energy flux. This design offers no time-matched placebo comparison. It cannot separate the effects of ester ingestion from time, repeated measurement or accompanying metabolic changes. A physiological response in visual cortex does not estimate seizure risk. [Steinwurzel et al., pp. 3–6, Table 1 and Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.1111/ejn.70270)

**Reanalysis.** For each participant compute the post-minus-pre amplitude for a grating alone and for the same grating with a perpendicular mask. Eight of ten participants had a larger absolute increment without the mask. The paired interaction reproduces the published F(1,9) of 8.36; two-sided enumeration of all 1,024 sign assignments gives p=0.0234 under symmetry of the contrasts. Then compare proportional changes using within-person log ratios. Geometric post/pre ratios are 1.229 for the grating alone and 1.072 with the mask. Their ratio is 1.146, positive in seven of ten, with a sign-flip p=0.180. The estimate stays positive with any single participant omitted, but the proportional contrast is uncertain. This scale check is exploratory. The amplitudes start smaller under masking; a shared proportional gain could generate unequal absolute changes. Absence of significance on a second scale establishes neither equal gains nor equivalence. The published endpoint and the scale check both challenge a blanket assumption of acute cortical suppression.

**WWOX prediction and decision.** A useful functional rescue would reduce spontaneous pathological synchrony while preserving evoked recruitment and recovery during demand. At a measured nonzero baseline, compare WWOX-LoF and isogenic corrected neurons under matched substrate and pH. Record initial labelled D-BHB entry, downstream oxidation, native M-current, and spontaneous versus evoked activity. Proportionate amplification of both physiological recruitment and pathological bursts falls short even if energetic recovery improves. Preferential stabilization alongside preserved evoked responses, coupled to a reproducible uptake/oxidation or M-current change, would reopen this compound. Mouse SLC16A7 expression warrants this test; the adjusted human bulk RNA result gives little support for a transporter deficit. A concentration inferred from tissue MRS cannot decide channel saturation at the neuronal membrane. [WWOX RNA sensitivity](neuronal_reference.md) · [Exposure and model assumptions](baseline.md)

**Pediatric feasibility.** A case series includes three children starting an investigational D-BHB salt preparation from two months to ten years three months of age; two had MADD and one PDH deficiency. The PDH infant also received ketogenic nutrition. In one MADD case hypernatraemia on the preceding formulation resolved after the change. The three cases establish pediatric administration under monitoring; they cannot establish safety across preparations, efficacy in WWOX, interactions with background antiseizure drugs or a CNS concentration sufficient for that endpoint. The adult ester, pediatric salt and WWOX ketogenic-diet exposures are different interventions. [Original pediatric cases and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11231749/)
