# 2-Deoxy-D-glucose and the activity-dependent cost of glycolysis restriction in WWOX

**Status.** Opening checkpoint. Use 2-deoxy-D-glucose (2-DG) as a mechanistic comparison for whether glycolysis supports pathological recruitment or preserves circuit recovery under WWOX loss. The held genetic modifier screen flagged impaired glucose uptake or hexokinase dependence as a possible aggravator in a non-neural system. The original Wwox-null fibroblast study reported lower oxygen consumption per well. The held re-audit found no stated per-cell normalization. The WWOX neural direction remains open. [Held modifier result](../../modifiers_it12_p1b_20261031/README.md) · [WWOX metabolic assay and normalization caveat](../../../fulltext_dossiers/PMID25012504.md)

**Prediction.** Compare age-matched WWOX and corrected neural preparations at measured exposure, available glucose and the same initial stimulus. If excessive activity-linked glycolysis directly sustains recurrent excitation, burst recruitment or directly measured excitatory release should drop before ATP or phosphocreatine recovery changes, while evoked inhibition and patterned output remain within the matched baseline response. If glycolysis instead supplies compensation, verify whether poorer energy recovery and loss of inhibition precede the loss of sustained network output. Read immediate and delayed time windows separately.

**Counterchecks.** Match extracellular glucose, osmolarity and activity history and include an orthogonal glycolytic or fuel-supply comparison. Measure tonic and evoked inhibition separately from direct release. Preserve responses to low-burden physiological stimuli. Distinguish acute effects from longer-term redox, protein processing or transcriptional responses. A smaller pathological burst accompanied by less physiological recruitment and slower ATP recovery is evidence against advancing 2-DG.

**Next comparison.** Reconcile the contradictory model effects, the compartment and duration of the reported presynaptic and tonic mechanisms, and the available adult blood PK.
