# 2-Deoxy-D-glucose, activity load and glycolytic reserve in WWOX loss of function

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. The registered comparison concerns selective reduction of recurrent excitation versus loss of the energy that supports inhibition and recovery. The preparations below include induced-seizure models, rodent hippocampal and cortical slices, and aggregate human systemic exposure. WWOX evidence comes from held neural physiology, fibroblast metabolism and a separate human non-neural genetic screen.

**Decision.** Park 2-deoxy-D-glucose (2-DG) for pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retain glycolytic restriction as a conditional mechanism probe. The sign changes between seizure models, and the high-demand slice effects do not demonstrate preserved inhibition or an exposure bridge into juvenile WWOX. The informative comparison is an early selective change in firing or release with preserved energy and inhibition, measured before and after the same prespecified activity load. [Registered prediction](pretest_20261127.md)

## Which kind of activity changed?

| Preparation | Verified response | Boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Gasior et al., acute provoked seizure thresholds in mice and fully amygdala-kindled rats | At overlapping nominal treatment ranges the 6-Hz seizure threshold rose, while the electroshock, PTZ and kainate thresholds fell. Nonmetabolized 3-O-methylglucose did not protect in 6 Hz and lowered the PTZ threshold. The same paper found no detected effect on the tested established amygdala-kindled seizures. | The induction method, species, endpoint and treatment timing are part of each contrast. The glucose-transport control motivates a different mechanism for the proconvulsant effects but does not demonstrate pathway flux. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20491877/) |
| Janicot et al., P10–P17 rats with ongoing pilocarpine status epilepticus | The abstract reports cessation of electrographic and behavioral activity in approximately 10–15 minutes in stronger treatment arms during a short video-EEG observation period. | A treatment of established induced status is distinct from measuring induction threshold or later development. Neonatal age alone supplies no WWOX-specific or pediatric human exposure evidence. [Primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32558935/) |

The marginal medians were 7.3 to 2.0 nC/s at −70 mV (12 neurons), and 44.7 to 29.1 nC/s at 0 mV (9 neurons). A 20 mM glucose osmotic control did not reproduce those effects. | The recordings rely on different holding potentials and groups. The fractional remaining ratio of 0.483 and the ratio reconstructed from marginal medians (0.421) are two descriptive summaries. In injured slices, acute 2-DG changed the proportion of epileptiform evoked sweeps from 90% to 18% (10 slices from 6 animals). [Tonic study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27735054/) · [Kindling study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17041593/) |

The two rodent slice studies controlled different things. CA3 slices added 2-DG while retaining 10 mM glucose. The cortical assay partly replaced glucose and included a low-glucose/sucrose comparator. The high-K hippocampal and post-injury cortical preparations do not form a concentration-response curve. Their overlap supports testing demands on excitation across states, with physiological recruitment as an independent outcome. [Shao and Stafstrom](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00723.2018) · [Koenig et al.](https://insight.jci.org/articles/view/126506)

## Systemic exposure

The posted epilepsy PK study NCT05605301 included nine adults, three in each of three arms. The aggregate mean Cmax values are **1.26, 1.68 and 1.54 µg/mL**, or **7.68, 10.23 and 9.38 µM** using 164.16 g/mol. Cmax had three observations per arm. Reported half-life estimates were available in only one, two and two participants. The adverse-event record reports zero serious events in nine over the monitored interval, with small arms and short exposure. The highest arm mean is approximately **782–977 times below** the nominal 8–10 mM slice concentrations on the quoted scales. These are systemic means, not CSF, unbound brain concentrations or estimates of a target-engagement threshold. The trial registered PK outcomes and supplies no clinical seizure effect estimate. [NIH posted results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT05605301) · [Molecular mass](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/108223) · [Exposure replay](exposure.tsv)

A separate oncology study reported substantially higher systemic mean peaks (277, 449 and 744 µM). The 449-µM mean is still approximately 18–22 times below the two slice baths, and the study reported grade 3 QTc prolongation as dose-limiting at its highest level. This distinct population, study regimen and risk signal do not set an epilepsy exposure ceiling or establish the safety of chronic exposure. The comparison shows why the small epilepsy PK study alone does not define all human exposure, and why none of the available measures bridges to WWOX neural engagement. [Stein et al., primary phase I abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20687211/)

## WWOX inference and discriminating observation

The held Wwox-null embryonic fibroblast study reported higher glucose-related outputs and lower OCR and ATP per assay well. The lab's re-audit could not verify normalization by cell number or protein for the oxygen-consumption figure. The held HAP1 screen flagged glucose-uptake and hexokinase loss as possible aggravators but had one leukemia-derived knockout line, one screen and no re-expression arm. The juvenile WWOX cortical study supports delayed d-APV-sensitive recruitment. These are three separate observations. They leave whether activity-coupled glycolysis is excessive or compensatory in identified WWOX neural cells unresolved. [Metabolic assay with normalization caveat](../../../fulltext_dossiers/PMID25012504.md) · [Modifier screen limits](../../modifiers_it12_p1b_20261031/README.md) · [Held native cortical comparison](../../generation_it41_20261109/comparisons_it41.md)

**Prediction.** At matched available glucose, osmolarity, membrane state and the same initial input, record energy recovery (ATP or phosphocreatine), directly measured release, evoked inhibitory charge, tonic current and physiological patterned responses in age-matched WWOX and corrected neural preparations. Separate low and high activity loads. Measure local 2-DG and its phosphorylated product along with the time course of the physiological responses. A decline in excess excitatory recruitment that precedes any loss of energetic recovery, with preserved inhibitory and physiological output, supports a selective circuit effect. An early decline in energy or inhibition followed by smaller bursts favors energetic or recruitment failure.

**Countercheck.** In matched preparations, determine whether independent support for energy recovery removes broad network suppression without removing an initial selective release effect. If apparent benefit tracks reduced short-interval inhibitory recruitment, only appears in the tonic branch, or disappears when matched activity history is enforced, change the mechanism attribution. A reduction in seizure-like patterns alone is insufficient. Conditional benefit needs direct WWOX engagement at an exposure with a credible neural bridge.

**Pediatric feasibility.** The current human record is short-term systemic PK in adults. Bidirectional seizure threshold in animals and an oncology cardiac signal increase the value of the energy, physiological inhibition and exposure controls. Independent review would be decision-changing if it produces either a verified neural exposure-supported selective response in WWOX or a competing direct mechanism that alters the prepared assay. The current evidence leaves pediatric trial priority parked.
