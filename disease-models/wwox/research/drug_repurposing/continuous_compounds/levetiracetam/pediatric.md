# Levetiracetam and short-term focal seizures in young children
context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Pediatric comparator.** FDA Study 5 evaluated oral adjunctive treatment of focal-onset seizures at ages one month to under four years. Enrollment required at least two focal seizures during 48-hour video EEG on stable background treatment with one or two antiseizure drugs. Randomized groups comprised 60 and 56 children. During the final 48 hours of a five-day treatment period, 43.1% of the 58 evaluable children receiving levetiracetam and 19.6% of the 51 evaluable children receiving placebo had at least a 50% reduction in average daily focal-seizure frequency (reported P=0.013). The integer responder counts consistent with those rounded percentages are 25 and 10. Only four treated participants were under six months old. The comparison supports a short-term focal-seizure endpoint; it gives no separate effect estimate here for WWOX, spasms or development. Somnolence occurred in 13% versus 2% and irritability in 12% versus 0% of the safety groups. These can complicate assessments of alertness and development during polytherapy. The trial's stable background is essential for interpreting its result. [FDA label, Study 5, Fig. 5 and Table 6](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/021872s035lbl.pdf) · [Trial report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19243423/)

**Sensitivity to the analysis denominator.** Recalculate the modified-analysis difference as 100 × (25/58 − 10/51) = 23.50 percentage points. Assign both excluded treated children as nonresponders and all five excluded placebo children as responders for a deliberately unfavorable bound, 100 × (25/60 − 15/56) = 14.88 points. This descriptive missing-outcome exercise neither preserves a significance test nor estimates age-specific efficacy.

**WWOX case attribution.** Reuse the laboratory's Oliver Table 2 extraction (11 of 13 ever exposed, four current). In case 5 levetiracetam and valproate were listed together; the table supplies no isolated response. The You report links an interval without recurrent seizures to levetiracetam plus phenobarbital. AlBaradie reports control on levetiracetam plus topiramate at last follow-up. The A1 audit also records a history of multiple ineffective drugs and distinguishes later levetiracetam for other seizure types from preceding spasm resolution. The checked records do not quantify focal seizure change during isolated levetiracetam introduction on stable background with time-aligned EEG. These are bounded observations on previously audited histories. **Decision.** Park levetiracetam as a new priority for an unselected pediatric WWOX trial. Retain its symptomatic focal-seizure rationale and the separate CA3 mechanistic hypothesis. Reopen for attributable, seizure-type-specific WWOX outcomes on stable background, or a WWOX-selective restoration of direct GABA responses at measured local drug and free-zinc exposure with preserved native inhibition. A prospective symptomatic comparison could use the blinded EEG endpoint above; a developmental claim needs its own functional outcome, longer observation and control of seizure burden and sedation.
