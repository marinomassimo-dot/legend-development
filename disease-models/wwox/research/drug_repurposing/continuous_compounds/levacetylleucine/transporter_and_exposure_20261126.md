## Measured windows and surrogate limitations

The FDA integrated review for NDA 219132, §14.1, describes about **100% unbound in human plasma in vitro** by rapid equilibrium dialysis at 1, 10 and 100 µg/mL. Trial IB1001-201, Table 64, reports mean steady-state peak of **8.324 µg/mL** (n=17, SD 3.309, highest reported individual 15.84). At 173.21 g/mol the mean and maximum are **48.1 and 91.4 µM** total. The mean terminal half-life in the same group is **0.958 h**. The binding assay supports a similar order of magnitude for plasma-free drug and gives no human parenchymal measurement. The 24-hour AUC differs between Table 64 and the current label (**16.614** and **33.2 h·µg/mL**). The comparison here uses the peak only. [FDA integrated review §§14.1–14.2, Tables 63–64](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2024/219132Orig1s000IntegratedR.pdf)

Churchill et al., Fig. 4a/c, report **3.0 mM** apparent MCT1 transport Kₘ for the L enantiomer and **15 mM** half-inhibition of a 500 µM thiophene glyoxylate probe. The uptake result is vector-control-subtracted in transfected MDCK-II in a one-minute room-temperature assay. The inferred **4.3 mM** inhibition constant assumes competition and is for this probe system. This gives no measured inhibition of native lactate. Under a simple normalized transport calculation, a 48.1 µM substrate is at **1.58%** of the fitted transport capacity (`48.1 / (3000 + 48.1)`). This does not estimate absolute flux or human tissue uptake. Liver S9 Fig. 4f–h shows conversion to leucine, without identifying the delivery or conversion rate in human forebrain. [Churchill et al., Fig. 4, Results and Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8338929/)

The human induced-neuron article reports preservation of neurites during a concurrent **1 or 5 mM** nominal levacetylleucine and nocodazole challenge over 6 h (Fig. 4, reported N=3, n=15). Those two baths are roughly **21 and 104 times** the group mean plasma Cmax. Fig. 3 found no significant improvement when levacetylleucine was applied during recovery after injury. Fig. 5 uses AZD3965 at nominal 20 or 50 µM for 24 h before the challenge. Curtis et al. Table 1B reports **3.2 nM** MCT1 and **20 nM** MCT2 affinity in their matched assay. AZD3965 therefore supplies an MCT-sensitive counterexample with inadequate isoform assignment at the concentrations used. MCT2 could contribute to the background metabolic phenotype. Prefer paired transport/deacetylation measurements and isoform controls with a vehicle lactate baseline. [Induced-neuron article, Methods and Figs. 3–5](https://doi.org/10.3389/fimmu.2026.1866462) · [Curtis et al., Table 1B](https://doi.org/10.18632/oncotarget.18215)

## Functional and lysosomal counterchecks

The EMA assessment report §2.5.3 cites brain radioactivity after IV radiolabelled racemic acetylleucine in two monkeys, from Bénard et al. The signal supports exposure to labelled material. Its identity as intact L enantiomer, its free concentration and its delivery after the oral formulation remain unresolved. In a separate unilateral labyrinthectomy study, six rats per arm received L, D or racemic acetylleucine or vehicle. At day 7 the L arm's postural imbalance score was **4.8 ± 0.5** (mean ± SD, p < 0.01 vs vehicle), and the D arm did not differ significantly from vehicle at the measured times. The lower-dose L-arm experiment found a day-7 effect at the highest tested IV dose only (Fig. 3). FDG-μPET in Fig. 4 measures regional glucose metabolism. These findings establish a functional precedent in an acquired vestibular injury. There is no paired measurement of the parent, labelled products or native lactate transfer in that preparation. [EMA assessment, PDF p. 25](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/assessment-report/aqneursa-epar-public-assessment-report_en.pdf#page=25) · [Günther et al., Methods and Figs. 2–4](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0120891)

Davis et al. Fig. 1B–C reports an 18 h HeLa response. A graphical check puts the first two displayed submillimolar points beyond the leftmost point at **approximately 125 and 250 µM**. Panel C shows about **45% and 42%** of cells scored as nuclear TFEB, respectively, and about **58% at 500 µM**. These are approximate readings from log₁₀ molar tick spacing, not an EC₅₀ or significance threshold. The first two points are nonmonotone and already above the mean human plasma peak, before accounting for exposure duration. At 2 mM the study shows increased nuclear localization in wild-type HeLa and reduced nuclear localization in NPC1-knockout HeLa, with the NPC1 comparison using a 22 h incubation. LAMP1 abundance was lower after 44 h at 2 mM in the NPC1 contexts. A change in localization or abundance cannot establish autophagic flux or circuit recovery. The direction depends on the starting state, so apply an autophagic-flux comparator only if untreated stage-matched WWOX neural cells show a reproducible deficit. Use free leucine and acetate controls to distinguish that signal from the acetylated compound's effect. [Davis et al., Figs. 1, 4 and 5](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0353834) · [Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0353834.g001)

## Disposition and evidence needed to reopen

**Park for an unselected pediatric WWOX trial.** The pediatric oral-suspension precedents cover NPC neurological manifestations and ataxia in A-T down to 15 kg by the current US label. Applicability below 15 kg and to a WWOX developmental endpoint is unestablished. The US label flags concomitant acetylleucine racemate/D enantiomer and P-gp substrate interactions. The reviewed studies give no WWOX-specific comedication or tolerability bridge. [US prescribing information §§1, 6–8 and 14](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=professional&setid=0f248e55-d1bb-13f9-e063-6294a90a05ce)

Retain the molecule as a transport and fuel comparison. Start with measured free parent and a short exposure plausibly bounded by the human plasma observations, then quantify intracellular parent, labelled leucine and labelled acetate-derived products in the donor and receiver. Compare against free leucine and acetate separately, at matched pH, lactate and activity. A leucine-delivery advantage should disappear when intracellular free leucine is matched. An acetate effect should persist at matched leucine and track labelled acetate products. Reject the proposed functional rescue if parent uptake or product labelling rises without WWOX-selective ATP and evoked-function recovery, or if the apparent functional gain comes with a loss of native lactate transfer. Preserved transfer may remain below the healthy level if the drug bypasses the receiving-side deficit. A concentration-only rescue at sustained millimolar bath or an isolated TFEB shift is insufficient. Reopen trial priority for a coupled, exposure-supported WWOX response that also preserves control function. The denominator audit and independent endpoint comparison settle the immediate disposition. A review can add value if it identifies chemically resolved early CNS exposure or a matched low-exposure functional effect.
