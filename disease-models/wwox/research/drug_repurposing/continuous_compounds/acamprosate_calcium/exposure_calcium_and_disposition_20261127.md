# Acamprosate calcium, exposure dependence and cortical glutamatergic currents

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Decision.** Park acamprosate calcium for pediatric WWOX trial priority and as a primary NMDA probe. Retain the calcium and counterion comparison as a preclinical control. In the fixed-ratio alcohol self-administration model, calcium acamprosate and calcium chloride had a similar direction of effect, and sodium acamprosate retained the higher NMDA-to-AMPA current ratio. The effect of the calcium salt was small or undetected in other exposure histories. Adult PK provides only a semiquantitative lumbar CSF scale. Reopen for a genotype-preferential effect at measured neural anion exposure with an independently controlled ionic state and preserved physiological recruitment. [Registered prediction](pretest_20261127.md) · [Opening comparison](first_comparison_20261127.md)

## Exposure and pediatric denominator

Hammarberg et al. recruited **13** healthy adults. The primary abstract reports day-22 lumbar CSF values estimated **9–33 ng/mL**, below the assay's limit of quantification. Quantitative CSF assay limits, analyte calibration and the count of usable CSF samples could not be confirmed from available text. On a free-acid-equivalent reporting basis (181.21 g/mol), the estimated scale is **0.050–0.182 µmol/L**. The lowest reported recombinant anti-agonist screen concentration of **100 µmol/L** lies about **549-fold** above the upper estimate if reported per anion, or about **1,098-fold** when nominal concentration refers to the calcium-salt formula unit with two anions. These ratios place the negative test and the clinical marker on a scale. They are neither therapeutic exposure margins nor proof that a lower untested concentration has no activity. Lumbar CSF cannot stand in for free juvenile cortical interstitial drug. [Primary PK abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20592646/) · [FDA chemistry review](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/2004/21-431_Campral_Chemr.pdf) · [Recombinant study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3924515/)

**Pediatric boundary.** Erickson et al. enrolled 12 youths aged **5–17**, and **6/9** who received active drug met a composite global/social-withdrawal criterion. A separate randomized parallel registry lists **36 enrolled**, ages **5–17**, with ten-week social withdrawal and clinician global impression outcomes and **no results posted** in its public API record at review. Neither record measures WWOX seizure, evoked physiology or neural target exposure. The US delayed-release label indicates adult alcohol-dependence use, describes renal excretion of acamprosate and reports that pediatric PK, safety and efficacy are unestablished. Renal clearance, formulation and feeding therefore belong in a feasibility assessment, with comedication and clinical endpoints characterized independently. [Pediatric pilot, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24052275/) · [Randomized study registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01813318) · [Registry JSON, design and results flag](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT01813318) · [DailyMed §§1, 8.4 and 12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=91769a96-3182-4e38-90c3-b22c1beae398)

## What would change the WWOX comparison

The held juvenile neuronal Wwox-loss cortex shows larger miniature EPSCs and d-APV-sensitive bursts. The miniature-current experiment did not isolate AMPA and NMDA components. A ratio decrease can follow either lower NMDA current or a larger AMPA denominator. A synthetic example gives the same ratio **1.30** with NMDA/AMPA absolute currents **130/100** and **210/161.5** in arbitrary units. Relative to **210/100**, the first selectively changes the numerator and the second leaves it unchanged. The comparison therefore needs absolute early and late currents alongside the response of the network. [Existing WWOX evidence and preparation](../../perampanel_20261122/clinical_mechanistic_update_20261126.md) · [IT41 matched-current comparison](../../generation_it41_20261109/comparisons_it41.md)

**Discriminating observation.** In stage-matched WWOX and corrected juvenile preparations, separately vary measured free N-acetylhomotaurinate and ionized Ca²⁺, with the existing bath calcium as background. Compare matched vehicle, the sodium salt with balanced sodium and osmolality, added calcium with balanced counterion, and both factors. Compare the calcium salt with the ion-matched reconstituted mixture if composition or delivery remains relevant. Measure Mg²⁺, pH and free species, align stimulation, and measure absolute fast and late charge, fixed-agonist postsynaptic current, evoked inhibition and an independent measure of release. Use d-APV as a reference and animals as independent units. Measure the exposure and response over onset and washout if comparing an acute bath effect with pre-exposure. If acute ionic matching removes the contrast, a historical state effect requires a separate pre-exposure comparison.

- **Independent anion contribution.** At equal measured Ca²⁺, a calcium-free anion control changes the fixed-input late current and evoked late charge preferentially in WWOX while preserving early and inhibitory recruitment.
- **Ca²⁺ or circuit-state contribution.** Calcium reproduces the combined response when the measured ionized concentration and prior state match. An apparent effect limited to evoked responses also requires direct release measurements. Sustained benefit after washout with no change in acute fixed-input current would favor adaptation.
- **Interaction.** Estimate the joint-minus-additive contrast at matched exposures and the genotype dependency of that contrast. A difference in nominal significance between compound arms does not estimate this interaction. Worsened late recruitment, a change confined to the ratio, or an effect seen exclusively at clearly supraphysiological exposures would leave it parked.

**Checkpoint.** The risk of conflating a calcium-associated systemic effect with direct NMDA modulation is now bounded by a native circuit comparison, a recombinant countercheck and a separate non-calcium biomarker effect. A second reading could change the decision if it identifies a validated low-exposure direct result or animal-indexed data yielding a different calcium-versus-anion contrast. Reopen for those data or the matched WWOX experiment above.
