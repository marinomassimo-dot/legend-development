# L-tryptophan in WWOX loss of function

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · prespecified comparison · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Question.** Does higher extracellular L-tryptophan supply replenish a limiting neuronal product in WWOX loss of function, or divert substrate toward kynurenine products with opposing effects on NMDA current and redox state? The earlier L-leucine comparison covers shared transport and nutrient sensing, while fenfluramine and melatonin address different downstream mechanisms. L-tryptophan tests allocation of one substrate between those pathways and the kynurenine arm. Dietary availability gives it an earlier look. It does not establish central delivery or pediatric suitability. [Transport and nutrient-sensing comparison](../l_leucine/pretest_20261126.md) · [Program decisions](../README.md)

**First comparison.** Replay three held untreated WWOX bulk profiles and the single P17 hippocampal nuclear pool per arm. Prespecify SLC7A5 and SLC3A2 for shared transport, TPH2 and DDC for the neuronal serotonin route, IDO1 and TDO2 for pathway entry, AADAT for the kynurenate arm, KMO, KYNU and HAAO for the oxidative branch, and QPRT for quinolinate conversion. Retain missing transcript annotations and low-count bins as uncertainty. Compare KO minus control mean log₂(1 + abundance) within each model and omit each library in turn. In nuclei, report target counts, nuclei and total counts separately for relevant assigned classes. Even sign-stable RNA cannot diagnose deficient uptake, resolve enzyme activity or establish the direction of flux. [Held input fingerprints](../creatine/inputs.tsv)

**Discriminating prediction.** In a matched WWOX-versus-corrected neural system with a replete starting medium, measure isotope-derived products at the same extracellular free L-tryptophan and competing amino-acid concentrations. A substrate bottleneck predicts selective recovery of a deficient labeled product and evoked function when intracellular precursor rises. A transport bottleneck predicts that extracellular and intracellular increments diverge under the same measured amino-acid competition. Diversion predicts that added precursor raises kynurenine-derived exposure without restoring the deficient neuronal product, or worsens quinolinate, native lipid oxidation or pharmacologically isolated NMDA response. Measure those readouts independently. A neural-only model without measurable serotonergic capacity or microglia can test fewer branches. Include the pertinent cells or leave their contribution unresolved.

**Decision rule.** Advance a preclinical comparison only for a reproducible basal WWOX deficit with an attainable exposure and product-linked functional recovery that preserves evoked recruitment. Park a direct pediatric trial if the signal rests on blood abundance, RNA, nonspecific nutritional correction or a single pooled neural observation. Follow the measured products and function without treating their ratio as a substitute for flux.
