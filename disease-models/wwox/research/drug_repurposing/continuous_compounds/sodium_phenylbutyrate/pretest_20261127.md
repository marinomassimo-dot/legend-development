# Sodium phenylbutyrate opening checkpoint in WWOX loss of function

**Evidence scope.** Parent phenylbutyrate and phenylacetate in pediatric plasma and nonhuman-primate CSF, urinary phenylacetylglutamine, PDK/PDH enzymatic and tracer endpoints, and juvenile Wwox neuronal-deletion oxidative recovery and inhibitory transmitter replenishment.

`context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`. The [chemical-chaperone qualification](../../pharmacological_qualification_it8_p3_20261029/README.md) addresses the Q230P-class missense hypothesis and holds it as unresolved. This increment addresses a separate null question and does not assume recoverable WWOX protein. It joins the [PDK exposure comparison](../sodium_dichloroacetate/README.md) and [glutamine uptake comparison](../l_glutamine/uptake.md) to ask whether a parent-drug gain in oxidation would coexist with a substrate cost in inhibitory cells.

**Fixed comparison.** Use the sodium formulation and track parent phenylbutyrate (PBA), phenylacetate (PAA), and phenylacetylglutamine (PAGN) as separate chemical entities. Reconstruct the pediatric plasma, glutamine and urine measures, then bracket the neural parent and metabolite exposure with primary CSF or tracer evidence. One mole of recovered urinary PAGN represents a one-mole glutamine conjugation sink. That amount does not identify the size or direction of a brain free-glutamine change. Keep the parent-to-metabolite conversion and the systemic glutamine time course separate. Keep intravenous nonhuman-primate exposure distinct from oral pediatric UCD pharmacokinetics. Verify substrate and kinase isoform conditions in the PDK assays and separate reduced inhibitory PDHA phosphorylation, direct PDK engagement and improved oxidative flux. Use dichloroacetate from the prior work only as a matched-PDH-change comparator. The prior lesion hypothesis and q230p chaperone result do not supply efficacy or central exposure in null tissue. **Discriminating prediction.** If a direct parent action matters, at measured free exposure and fixed glutamine, parent PBA should increase labeled pyruvate oxidation, ATP recovery and appropriate evoked output with preserved inhibitory recruitment. Compare a separately measured metabolite exposure and a controlled reduction of free glutamine compatible with any observed systemic shift. If PAGN formation reduces available glutamine, a peripheral conjugation model and a matched glutamine-only decrease should reduce labeled GABA replenishment and late inhibitory charge. Glutamine clamping should restore inhibitory recovery without eliminating a genuine oxidative effect. An apparent benefit confined to phospho-PDHA or lactate, or a loss of late inhibition despite better oxidation, lowers priority. Treat development and background anticonvulsants as explicit comparisons. An isolated brain preparation cannot model hepatic or renal nitrogen scavenging by itself.
