# Sodium phenylbutyrate in WWOX loss of function

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · `context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`

**Disposition.** Park for pediatric WWOX trial priority and keep dichloroacetate as the reference PDK probe. Sodium phenylbutyrate is useful as a conditional mechanistic comparison. The parent inhibits recombinant PDK2 on a concentration scale comparable to the higher observed pediatric total-plasma concentrations. Its main circulating metabolite has a different PDH result in preclinical assays and can remove glutamine as urinary phenylacetylglutamine. No comparison measured free parent at neuronal PDK or established a loss of the glutamine available for inhibition in WWOX tissue. This null-circuit result is separate from the [unresolved Q230P-class chemical-chaperone hypothesis](../../pharmacological_qualification_it8_p3_20261029/README.md).

Across the two pediatric NaPBA cohorts, the PAA-to-PBA molar AUC ratio is 3.64 and 3.95. This compares integrated systemic exposure. The PAA and PBA peaks in the older cohort can occur at different times. One conjugated glutamine arrives in each PAGN molecule, which contains two nitrogen atoms. ## What the PDK comparison establishes

The apparent PBA inhibition constants are **0.33 ± 0.08 mM for PDK2** (competitive with E1α), **0.98 ± 0.19 mM for PDK3** (uncompetitive) and **7.1 ± 0.15 mM for PDK1** (uncompetitive). PDK4 inhibition was undetected in the tested conditions. PDK1's estimate extends beyond the tested 0.25–1 mM inhibitor series. These are initial phosphorylation rates on isolated E1α at fixed ATP (0.2 mM), with the core scaffold and E1β absent. The PDK2 value in the 2015 table comes from the 2013 study. At a reported saturating 4.2 µM E1α in the earlier study, assay activity fell only about 7% in the inhibitor condition. The 2015 Table 1 labels the E1α Km in mM, while the stated 0.5–8 µg of 47-kDa E1α in a 50-µL reaction corresponds to approximately 0.21–3.40 µM. The substrate scale merits confirmation from assay records before assigning absolute substrate concentrations to the fitted model. [Ferriero et al. 2013, PDK inhibition Results and Fig. 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4102924/) · [Ferriero et al. 2015, kinetics Methods, Table 1 and Fig. 3 caption](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4551558/)

The total plasma parent mean divided by apparent PDK2 Ki is **0.37** in the younger group and **0.18** in the older group. The older observed peak mean divided by the same Ki is **0.69**. These numbers describe an exposure scale. Peaks of individual children and variable free mitochondrial exposure remain unresolved. For a sensitivity check only, if free assay PBA were 0.228 mM, simple competitive kinetics would imply approximately **39%, 26% or 6%** inhibition at E1α concentrations of 0.1, 1 or 10 times Km, respectively. The same estimate for 0.123 mM would be **25%, 16% or 3%**. The scenarios fix ATP, use the nominal fitted Ki, and use deliberately hypothetical substrate/Km and free inhibitor values. They estimate an isolated reaction under those assumptions. The average plasma concentration is AUC/24, so the result evaluated at that average is distinct from the time-averaged fractional inhibition of a fluctuating exposure.

Ferriero et al. measured higher PDHC activity in perfused WT mouse brain mitochondrial fractions after oral PBA and reported no significant effect for the matched PAA arm (PBA n=10, PAA n=5, saline n=20). The direction also differed in 24-hour parent versus metabolite fibroblast conditions. The fractions were assayed with excess substrate and cofactors. This gives a parent-with-metabolite comparison for enzyme capacity, without native neural substrate oxidation, free parent measurements or a Wwox-null comparison. [Ferriero et al. 2015, Mouse studies, PDHC enzyme assay, Results and Figs. 1–2 captions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4551558/)

## WWOX inference and discriminating comparison

**Held finding.** The [PDK comparison](../sodium_dichloroacetate/README.md) leaves low native PDC flux and its kinase-dependent fraction unresolved. The [glutamine comparison](../l_glutamine/uptake.md) finds lower bulk SLC38A1 and GLS RNA in three knockout systems. The single pooled P17 hippocampal inhibitory nuclear sample was 72.7 vs 68.2 CPM for Slc38a1 in control and KO. Together with published tracer and stimulation experiments, these observations justify measuring whether supply limits inhibitory replenishment at the selected age and activity level. They do not identify a WWOX glutamine deficit.

**Prediction.** In matched juvenile Wwox-deficient and control preparations, verify free PBA and PAA in the measured exposure range, then compare PBA versus vehicle under identical extracellular glutamine and activity. Use a prespecified dichloroacetate comparison with a similar reduction in inhibitory PDHA phosphorylation. Seek increased early pyruvate-derived M+2 acetyl-CoA (relative and absolute label, controlled precursor enrichment), faster ATP recovery and preserved late evoked inhibitory charge with matched initial recruitment. A phosphorylation change without flux recovery favors another bottleneck, such as discordant DLAT oxidation or substrate utilization. A flux rise explained by lower evoked workload, or an equal response in control with no correction of the deficient phenotype, does not support a WWOX-selective rescue.

**Counterprediction.** In a separate arm, impose any measured glutamine change from a peripheral conjugation and repletion comparison while holding free PBA/PAA and workload constant. Measure glutamine-derived labeling into GABA in a separate tracer pulse and compare late inhibitory charge, fast excitation and recovery. If peripheral substrate diversion matters, restoring glutamine should recover labeled GABA and late inhibition while preserving the parent-dependent oxidation result. Test PAA with glutamine held constant to identify direct metabolite effects. Bulk plasma glutamine changes supply no neural clamp value. An isolated brain preparation can test sensitivity to glutamine availability and a direct metabolite effect, while peripheral PAGN formation and transport across compartments need separate measurements.

**Reopen condition.** Measured attainable free parent exposure with increased labelled oxidation, ATP recovery and physiological evoked recovery while preserving inhibitory replenishment in the same genotype and developmental comparison. A PDK response restricted to higher exposure or a reproducible worsening of late inhibition closes the null-rescue branch under the tested conditions. A second reviewer could check transcription or kinetics, while the decision currently hinges on unmeasured neural exposure and function. The smallest decision-changing next step is paired exposure, flux and inhibitory replenishment in the existing matched preparation.
