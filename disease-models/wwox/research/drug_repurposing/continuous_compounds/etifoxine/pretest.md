# Etifoxine opening checkpoint

28 November 2026 UTC · SCI_CODEX · INV-10 · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. One compound. The recorded ganaxolone and pregnenolone comparisons already constrain neurosteroid replacement and downstream conversion. Etifoxine tests a different premise: enhancement of endogenous flux versus direct GABA-A modulation. No evidence of a duplicate active task. Treat the human parent, any measured metabolite, the administered salt and assay-stated parent as different exposure fields.

**Rivals.** A delayed effect could require a measurable increase in flux to a GABA-active steroid. The mechanism could be TSPO-dependent or mediated by a downstream process. An immediate effect could instead follow direct GABA-A modulation in particular receptor assemblies, while sedation, arousal, body temperature, or chloride-dependent loss of physiological recruitment alter circuit readouts. The WWOX-specific hypothesis requires a basal limiting step, an achievable local exposure and a favorable native inhibitory response.

**Comparison.** Recompute human unbound-parent concentrations and the central enrichment required for hypothetical engagement without inferring brain exposure. Compare separate rat steroid content, labeled precursor conversion, TSPO-independent counterevidence and direct GABA-A current timing and assembly. Preserve provoked versus spontaneous endpoints and sex/age where reported. In native WWOX compare Tspo, Star, Cyp11a1, Srd5a1, Srd5a2, Gabrb1, Gabrb2 and Gabrb3 on deposited scales, full-arm abundance floor 1 and individual-library omission directions. Pooled single-nucleus classes are descriptive. Check human clinical and pediatric limits on primary or official sources.

**Predictions.** At unchanged chloride and first recruitment, the steroid branch should follow new isotope-labeled GABA-active product and disappear with downstream pathway interruption, with response retained to measured exogenous product. A near-immediate effect at constant labeled flux, persisting after downstream interruption and tracking receptor assembly, would favor direct modulation. Loss of a delayed response after an orthogonal TSPO perturbation at matched parent exposure is required for assigning that branch to TSPO. An effect only at unattainable free exposure keeps etifoxine a secondary in vitro comparator.
