# Vatiquinone | Neural exposure and phospholipid specificity

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Units and systems.** Parent vatiquinone in human plasma, ng/mL and converted nmol/L. Total radiolabel in six-week male Long-Evans rat whole brain and other tissues, µg equivalents/g. Oxidized phosphatidylethanolamine (PE) in cell-free liposomes with human 15-LOX-2 or a chemical radical initiator, pmol product per nmol precursor. The existing WWOX contrasts use individual mouse neural RNA libraries and human cerebral organoid RNA libraries.

**Decision.** Retain vatiquinone as a conditional preclinical redox probe and defer pediatric WWOX trial priority. The exposed patient studies establish systemic parent exposure in other disorders. Pharmacologically relevant free neural hydroquinone and an endogenous WWOX neural substrate burden remain unverified. The two conditions govern reopening independently. ## What to measure in a WWOX comparison

**Data.** Kahn-Kirby Fig. 3B uses a peroxide-dependent FOX readout with free arachidonic acid and human 15-LOX-1. The 4.4 µM hydroquinone IC50 uses that reaction and detector. A reductant could also alter peroxide recovery or the color reaction, so use a product-only control and an orthogonal measure of product if reusing it. Hinman Fig. 6C gives orthogonal active-site iron evidence for α-tocotrienol hydroquinone by EPR in soybean lipoxygenase at 1 mM hydroquinone and approximately 100 µM enzyme. Kahn-Kirby Fig. 3C–D and Fig. 4 add cellular LC-MS on free HETEs and partial protection with ALOX15 knockdown after RSL3 in PCH6 fibroblasts. The EPR, cellular and human enzyme studies cover different preparations and exposure ranges. [Kahn-Kirby et al., Fig. 3–4 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0214250) · [Hinman et al., Fig. 6C and EPR Methods](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0201369)

**Reanalysis.** Tyurina et al. source sheet `Fig. 2`, J3:M5, yields alkenyl:diacyl 15-HpETE-PE ratios of **1.40 at 2.5 min and 1.71 at 5 min** in liposomes prepared from HT22 lipids with human 15-LOX-2, from three experiments per condition. In the PE half of Fig. 2g (J9:M11), the DOPC plus radical initiator AMVN comparison yields **1.04**, or **1.01** after subtracting corresponding control means. These descriptive ratios use each substrate's own precursor denominator. The different membrane matrices preclude a formal enzyme-versus-AMVN interaction estimate. They do justify retaining both alkenyl and diacyl PE in a targeted WWOX panel. Free arachidonate oxygenation, RNA abundance, and total lipid fluorescence cannot cover both classes. PEBP1 is especially relevant for diacyl substrate handling and cannot serve as a universal proxy for PE oxidation. [Tyurina et al., Fig. 2f–g and membrane substrate Results](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-71869-z) · [Primary workbook from PubMed Central](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC13276253.1/41467_2026_71869_MOESM11_ESM.xlsx) · [Ratios recalculated from the authors’ data under CC BY 4.0](ether_bias_comparison.tsv)

**Comparator.** ML351 is a structurally unrelated reference with a reported nonreductive mixed inhibition mechanism for purified human 15-LOX-1. Rai Table 5 estimates a mouse brain:plasma total AUC ratio of 2.8 after intraperitoneal exposure in young adult male CD1, with about 1 h brain half-life. This is a scaffold comparator once a species-specific lipid response is verified in the same assay. Rakib treated surviving adult C57BL/6 mice after diazepam had terminated pilocarpine status, then measured glial, histological and behavioral outcomes. In the separate behavior experiment, post-status allocation was 15 per exposed group and final counts were 13 vehicle and 12 ML351. Improved memory and less gliosis support a secondary injury mechanism after a prolonged insult. The selected results do not establish suppression of spontaneous seizures. Ferrostatin-1 can itself reduce esterified product formation in a 15-LOX-2/PEBP1 assay and is unsuitable as the sole mechanistically orthogonal radical-trapping control. [Rai, Fig. 1 and Table 5](https://doi.org/10.1021/jm401915r) · [Rakib, Methods and Fig. 7–8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41401-025-01743-z) · [Anthonymuthu et al., Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.redox.2020.101744)

**Inference and discriminating prediction.** At equal stimulation, a high late hydroperoxide pool could follow faster production, slower disposal or greater preceding recruitment. In the WWOX and matched control neural preparations, measure the intact PE substrates, their positional hydroperoxy products and their corresponding reduced hydroxy products before activity, just after the same evoked input, and during recovery. Include absolute amount and amount per matched precursor. Interpret the early rise and recovery jointly, and keep early recruitment and viability as requirements rather than post-hoc covariates. Compare vatiquinone with ML351 at independently verified reductions in the relevant species-specific product, with vehicle and measured parent/hydroquinone. A shared decrease in early oxidized-product formation and later WWOX-specific functional recovery, with preserved initial recruitment and no loss of native inhibition, supports a production-linked mechanism. Loss of hydroperoxide accompanied by a corresponding rise of the hydroxy product favors reduction or disposal. An effect confined to a stronger initial burst, global suppression, or pharmacological GPX4 inhibition cannot establish a selective endogenous WWOX mechanism. A drug effect appearing only after days with reduced inflammatory injury belongs to a separate time course.

**Reopen.** First establish a tissue-validated measurement of the endogenous oxidized PE excess or delayed clearance in WWOX under native activity, reproducible at animal or organoid-line level. Then show pharmacologically relevant neural hydroquinone or a preclinical preparation with independently verified local exposure and the predicted rescue. Prioritize an independent redox-lipid assay review if lipid-isomer assignment, formation versus disposal, or the applicability of the CNS exposure limit changes interpretation. The MIT-E estimate and SAE uncertainty remain a separate clinical constraint. [Clinical adjudication](README.md)
