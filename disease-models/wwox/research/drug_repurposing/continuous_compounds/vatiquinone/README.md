# Vatiquinone | Lipoxygenase-dependent phospholipid oxidation
context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Opening checkpoint, 27 November 2026.** Vatiquinone (EPI-743/PTC743, α-tocotrienol quinone) follows the pregnenolone disposition. The question is whether WWOX-LoF recovery is limited by a measurable, target-dependent oxidized phospholipid burden that can be reduced without losing physiological recruitment. The MIT-E trial gives a controlled epilepsy comparison in a distinct genetic disease class. [Pregnenolone disposition](../pregnenolone/assay_feasibility_and_disposition_20261127.md) · [WWOX redox comparator](../omaveloxolone/exposure_and_disposition_20261127.md) · [MIT-E trial](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04378075)

**Data anchors.** After nominal 1 µM vatiquinone for 3 h, RARS2-associated PCH6 fibroblast experiments detected 45–62% of the dosed compound as hydroquinone (Fig. 3A, six replicates for each of three cell lines). For purified human 15-LO, hydroquinone IC50 was 4.4 µM (Fig. 3B, n=2); parent gave about 20% inhibition at 100 µM. In one PCH6 fibroblast line under RSL3, 200 nM parent prevented the 15-HETE rise but not 12-HETE (17 replicates from three independent experiments, Fig. 3C–D). The direct hydroperoxide was below consistent quantification. This is acute chemically provoked fibroblast biology. Enzyme IC50, reported nominal cellular concentrations and free neural concentrations each require separate interpretation. In another laboratory, vatiquinone reproduced some acute cell protection, failed against two other oxidative stress insults and did not significantly improve survival in the inducible Gpx4 or Ndufs4(-/-) mouse models. P30 rotarod-provoked seizures were absent in six treated Ndufs4(-/-) mice, with Fisher p=0.156 against combined controls (n=24). It is a small provoked signal. Spontaneous seizure effects remain open. [Kahn-Kirby et al., Figs. 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0214250) · [Kayser et al., Figs. 1, 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13023-025-03582-x)

**Clinical countercheck.** MIT-E enrolled 68, with 34 per arm in the posted intent-to-treat comparison. Baseline mean ages were 8.7 and 6.6 years for vatiquinone and placebo. At week 24, median changes in the 28-day frequency of observable motor seizures were −12.74% and −0.33%. The registered treatment estimate is −8.31 percentage points, 95% CI −31.3 to +16.4. The statistical analysis plan §5.1.2 assigns the point estimate and interval to Hodges–Lehmann and the p-value (0.173) to rank ANCOVA. The arithmetic difference of arm medians is −12.41 points. The endpoint uses valid diary days across the double-blind period, with seizure frequency scaled to 28 days. Sensitivity analyses for diary completeness, baseline therapy changes and discontinuation were planned in §5.1.3. This aggregate reconstruction cannot recompute the model or adjudicate the sensitivity analyses. The US registry records primary assessment completion and lists sponsor decision as the overall termination reason. The FDA complete response for Friedreich ataxia concerns different primary and key secondary outcomes in MOVE-FA. [MIT-E results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04378075?tab=results) · [EU results](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2020-002100-39/results) · [MIT-E statistical analysis plan, pp. 22–25](https://cdn.clinicaltrials.gov/large-docs/75/NCT04378075/SAP_001.pdf) · [FDA complete response](https://download.open.fda.gov/crl/CRL_NDA220049_20250818.pdf)

**Safety across registries.** In the double-blind safety groups, both registries show serious adverse events in 18/34 vatiquinone participants and 9/34 placebo participants (+26.5 percentage points). Both list 3 versus 0 fatal events as reasons for noncompletion of the 24-week period. Their all-cause death summaries for the same double-blind group labels report 3 versus 0 in the US results and 1 versus 0 in EudraCT. EudraCT lists additional deaths under extension group labels. The SAP §§3.2 and 6.2 allows different time windows for blinded and on-vatiquinone summaries. The public aggregate rows do not reconcile event timing, reporting conventions or causes. Preserve each count with its source and defer a comparative mortality estimate. The SAE imbalance is descriptive, in a small, severely affected population. Age-stratified safety, genotype mix and background antiseizure therapy balance are unavailable in these extracts. **Prespecified first comparison.** Screen the held WWOX cortical, hippocampal and organoid RNA for ALOX15, ALOX15B, ALOX12, PEBP1, ACSL4, LPCAT3, GPX4, SLC7A11 and AIFM2, plus NQO1 as a candidate reduction-pathway marker. Retain developmental stage and model, normalized abundance, detection counts and single-library omissions. Neither RNA nor a broad oxidative stress readout can identify endogenous lipid peroxide production. Identify whether a public WWOX preparation already measures pre- or post-activity oxidized phospholipids by species and compartment. In matched WWOX and control neural preparations, at measured quinone and hydroquinone concentrations, a selective improvement in post-activation recovery should track a fall in prespecified endogenous oxidized phospholipids and converge with a target-selective reference. An equivalent change from a broad antioxidant, a primary decrease in first recruitment, or protection only after direct GPX4 inhibition would weaken the WWOX-specific mechanism. Separate human and mouse lipoxygenase regioselectivity before choosing the product panel. [Recombinant comparative lipid products](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13246792/)

**Next step.** Verify central free exposure to each redox form, then check whether held neural lipid data justify an endogenous pre-activity or recovery-linked measurement. Keep chemical identity, compartment, developmental age and assay sensitivity explicit in the exposure comparison.

**Initial WWOX comparison.** The edited bulk screen finds consistent SLC7A11 decreases in P17 cortex and hippocampus and week-15 human cerebral organoids (mean KO minus control log₂(1+normalized expression), −0.37/−0.73/−0.45). GPX4 is −0.14/−0.74/−0.42, with one-library omission crossing zero in cortex. The neuronal-deletion hippocampal ALOX15 row is detected in one control and one KO library (2 controls and 3 KO total), close to the measurement floor. In human organoids (2 controls and 4 KO), ALOX15 has a small descriptive increase (+0.23) at low normalized abundance. PEBP1 decreases in the two mouse regions and increases in organoids. Mouse Alox15b is mapped via Alox8 in the archived Ensembl reference and decreases in both mouse contrasts. NQO1 decreases in P17 hippocampus (−0.81) and human organoids (−0.43), raising the conditional possibility that parent-to-hydroquinone conversion itself may limit response. Neither the contribution of NQO1 nor actual reduction capacity is assigned by this screen. These signals support measuring peroxide disposal and active metabolite alongside target-derived production. They leave baseline peroxide burden and the cell source unresolved. [Replay](replay_wwox_lipid_peroxide_expression.py) · [All transcript rows](wwox_lipid_peroxide_expression.tsv)

**Species control.** Recombinant human ALOX15 on free arachidonate yielded about 10:1 15-HETE to 12-HETE in Chen et al. Fig. 1I, and mouse Alox15 about 2:10 in Fig. 1L–M. Human ALOX15B and mouse Alox15b also had different major products. The first animal experiment should therefore not use a human 15-HETE-only signature, and either marker can have contributions from other enzymes. For the proposed mechanism prioritize identified oxidized esterified phospholipids, with free eicosanoids as separate context. A next calculation should ask whether the same active-metabolite exposure can align the lipid and circuit effects in both species. [Chen et al., Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42003-026-10233-9)
