# Allopurinol in WWOX loss of function: CNS transfer, functional evidence and disposition

**Data scope.** LC-MS fractional isotope abundance in young-adult mouse blood, whole brain and kidney, normalized MAP2, GFAP and synapse-marker ratios per well in human MPS IIIA cortical cultures, IBA1-positive cells and process morphology in P14 mouse cortex, neonatal plasma purines and drug concentrations in mg/L, age-specific patient outcome counts after hypoxic-ischaemic encephalopathy (HIE), and adult oral total plasma peak concentration.

## Neonatal clinical and model evidence

- **Plasma engagement.** The first Chu model pooled 46 treated neonates across ALBINO and two earlier datasets. It estimated a combined plasma IC50 of 0.36 mg/L with 98% of observed concentrations above it. The extension analyzed 20 allopurinol and 17 mannitol recipients from the ALBINO PK substudy and estimated a combined IC50 of 0.68 mg/L (95% CI 0.48–0.92). They observed clearly different xanthine profiles but described broadly similar hypoxanthine and urate trajectories. The revised concentrations are plasma summaries. The first and second fits partially overlap and differ in structure and population. Neither directly measures pathway flux in the brain. [Chu first PK/PD fit](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40262-021-01068-0) · [Chu extension, Fig. 1, Table 1–2 and Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1007/s40262-022-01164-9)
- **Audit of the model report.** The extension reports 75% versus 68% of hypoxanthine clearance assigned to salvage in the treatment and comparator groups. Eq. 1 defines EFF as positive inhibition increasing from zero with drug concentration, while Eqs. 3–5 and 8–9 multiply the degradation flux directly by EFF (printed pp. 1547 and 1549). Substituting the Table 2 rates k40 = 0.5 and k45 = 0.2 per hour yields an instantaneous salvage fraction of 100% at zero drug under the printed equations, declining to 73.5% at an illustrative 9× IC50. Using residual activity 1−EFF yields 71.4% and 96.2% at the same points. Both give 83.3% at IC50. This is a sign check, with no claim about the authors' implementation, refit or population averaging. The supplementary file carries diagnostic figures without control-stream code, and the reported fraction is conditional even with the sign clarified. It was excluded as a quantitative input for WWOX. [Chu extension, original PDF Eqs. 1, 3–5 and 8–9](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s40262-022-01164-9.pdf)
- **Controlled clinical outcome.** In the small interim Benders trial after severe term asphyxia, death occurred in 13/17 allopurinol and 10/15 vehicle recipients (risk difference +9.8 percentage points, two-sided Fisher p=0.699). Normal aEEG recovery within 24 h was 7/17 versus 7/15, and status epilepticus 8/17 versus 5/15. Most received anticonvulsants. Dosing began within four hours and the population had substantial injury. The counts leave uncertainty and do not establish chronic neurological benefit. The uncontrolled association between timing and effect in the Discussion is an explanation for testing. [Benders et al., Table 1 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1136/adc.2005.086652)
- **ALBINO status.** The latest public registry record inspected on 26 November 2026 has no posted results, and the trial consortium reports primary-endpoint documentation in 440 of 497 children who had reached the 2-year visit age, out of 506 enrolled as of its February 2026 update. These are follow-up denominators with no treatment-arm efficacy estimate. The ClinicalTrials.gov metadata still list an estimated enrollment of 760. The dated consortium count is used for progress. [Official registry record](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03162653) · [Trial consortium update](https://www.albino-study.eu/news-events-publications.php)

## Pediatric feasibility

The US tablet reference provides pediatric use in cancer-therapy-associated hyperuricemia and identifies 100 and 300 mg scored tablets. The neonatal HIE experience used brief IV exposure, not chronic oral therapy. The adult tablet label’s approximate total parent peak of 3 mg/L after one oral dose converts to 22 µM. The 2,200 µM single-agent nominal culture assay is about 100× that adult total parent value. This comparison has no pediatric CNS exposure, free concentration or efficacy threshold. Parent and oxypurinol have different kinetics, and a two-drug or four-drug bath cannot set single-agent exposure. Serious cutaneous hypersensitivity, renal clearance and xanthine calculi are material to feasibility. The label identifies a substantial thiopurine interaction, and a UK product summary identifies possible inhibition of phenytoin oxidation with unestablished clinical significance. A chronic WWOX study would require formulation, parent and metabolite exposure, and tolerability evidence alongside a functional endpoint. [US label §§1, 5, 7, 8.4, 8.6 and 12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5dca2d4f-0e93-4d1a-8d9f-b9b72867c242) · [UK SmPC §4.5](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/15032/smpc)

## Inference and discriminating observation

**Inference.** A peripheral rise in hypoxanthine can coexist with much weaker label transfer to brain, and the isotope fraction can improve without restoring the size of the ATP pool. WWOX has a plausible downstream limit because ADSL is lower across bulk comparisons and HPRT1 is lower in mouse hippocampus, with an opposite HPRT1 direction in human organoids. The available expression measurements leave tissue XOR activity, native extracellular base concentration and availability of PRPP unresolved. The human neural counterexample from the [held Sekine results](../inosine/salvage.md) already separates precursor incorporation from expansion of the ATP pool.

**Hypothesis and test.** In a stage-matched WWOX/isogenic neuron–astrocyte comparison, first match the initial evoked workload, substrate resting concentrations and viability. Cross drug exposure with a peripheral or barrier-side XOR-active source and a verified partial HPRT perturbation. Include a matched hypoxanthine supply without drug and distinguish allopurinol from oxypurinol. Trace labeled native purines during recovery, quantify absolute IMP, adenylosuccinate, AMP, ADP and ATP and test evoked inhibitory and excitatory responses after washout. Record oxidant or redox measures at the same workload and extracellular adenosine to separate a transient A1-mediated firing brake. A preferential net WWOX ATP and evoked recovery that disappears with verified loss of salvage, while oxidant protection remains, supports the pathway. Increased blood-side base with no local delivery, labeling without net ATP and durable recovery, or a response that tracks reduced firing before repletion lowers that mechanism. Apparent rescue surviving partial HPRT loss with lower isotope incorporation requires a separate oxidant or alternate target explanation.

**Disposition.** Park for unselected pediatric WWOX trial priority and retain as a comparator for peripheral substrate retention. The decisive gap is measured exposure and preferential WWOX neural ATP and evoked recovery. The printed model sign inconsistency limits use of the neonatal fraction, and adjudicating it alone would not change the priority. Reopen for matched neural delivery plus HPRT-dependent restoration of net adenylates and evoked function, or a separate verified beneficial mechanism at transferable exposure.
