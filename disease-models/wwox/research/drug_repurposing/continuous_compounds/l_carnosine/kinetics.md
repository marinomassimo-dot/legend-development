# Which aldehyde could oral L-carnosine intercept?

**Disposition.** Park oral L-carnosine for an unselected pediatric WWOX trial. Retain acrolein as a specific biochemical probe. The missing observation is excess endogenous acrolein-derived damage in a defined WWOX compartment, followed by reduced damage and better neural function at measured local exposure. Lower CARNS1 RNA alone supplies neither result. [WWOX tissue and cell contrasts](README.md) · [Independent myelin review and circuit predictions](specificity.md)

**Rate and exposure.** In Table 1 of Barski et al., the carnosine rate constant is 0.51 M⁻¹ s⁻¹ for acrolein and 0.048 for 4-HNE. In the adult oral study, Table 4 reports mean baseline-subtracted plasma carnosine AUC of 450.6 µM·h (SD 610.5) and histidine AUC of 1256.0 µM·h (SD 1482.5) in the same 10 g arm. For a trace bolus of aldehyde present before the exposure, with continuous mixing and a single irreversible reaction, the fraction intercepted by the incremental exposure alone is 1 − exp(−k × AUC × 0.0036). This gives 56.3% for acrolein and 7.5% for 4-HNE under the carnosine plasma scenario. At one tenth that AUC the corresponding scenarios give 7.9% and 0.8%. Site exposure, aldehyde lifetime, continuous production, competing sinks and individual variation are unresolved. Applying a nonlinear function to a group mean does not give the average participant response. [Buffer kinetics, Table 1 and Fig. 2 caption](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3869200/) · [Incremental plasma AUC, statistical analysis and Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12252343/)

**Two competing explanations.** Applying the same calculation to incremental plasma histidine alone gives 46.9% for acrolein and 0.77% for 4-HNE. This is histidine measured after oral carnosine. It supplies neither a separately randomized histidine arm nor an additive percentage. A carnosine-specific effect needs a peptide-specific adduct and matched free-amino-acid controls. For 4-HNE the predicted half-time at a constant 1 mM carnosine in the first buffer is 241 min. An independent experiment with 1 mM glutathione and 100 µM 4-HNE reports a half-time of about 3 min. Different assays and buffers preclude assigning these rates to a living neuron. GSH and its conjugates should be measured in the same compartment. [Independent study, reaction methods and Fig. 1A–B Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6924506/)

**Methylglyoxal cross-check.** Neither lipid-aldehyde rate licenses a methylglyoxal (MG) estimate. The renal paper's Fig. 6 reports that 0.5 mM carnosine did not reduce the MG-H1 immunoblot signal in human tubular cells exposed to 0.25 or 0.4 mM MG for six hours. This more directly challenges our MG-H1 prediction than the previously examined viability data. The blot uses a loading control and measures accumulated immunoreactivity. Newly formed MG-H1 per arginine, local free MG and neuronal function remain untested. In the adult plasma scenario above, a standalone bimolecular MG reaction would need k≈0.065 M⁻¹ s⁻¹ for 10% incremental interception; this is a sensitivity threshold with hypothetical irreversible chemistry, not a measured rate. [Weigand et al., Fig. 6 caption and accompanying Results](https://d-nb.info/1157006043/34)

**Reopen criterion.** At matched survival and maturation, test whether newly formed acrolein–protein adducts fall as intact carnosine–acrolein conjugate appears and evoked neural recovery improves, beyond beta-alanine plus histidine at matched measured exposures. Measure the aldehyde burden independently of the carnosine conjugate. Prespecify tonic and evoked activity because MG could alter both. For a myelin claim, pair this chemistry with conduction in the same tract. CNS exposure in infants, interaction with background antiseizure medication and WWOX-specific efficacy are unresolved.
