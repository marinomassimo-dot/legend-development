# L-carnosine, carbonyl damage and the direction of GABA signaling

**Decision after independent review.** Keep L-carnosine as a mechanistic probe. Deprioritize generalized myelin rescue. In the 2023 authors' preprint, carnosine rose 3.3-fold in mouse corpus callosum during cuprizone injury, while homocarnosine stayed unchanged. Reported myelin basic protein and oligodendrocyte outcomes did not improve. The 2024 genetic study removed both dipeptides. It found fewer mature oligodendrocytes at baseline and greater susceptibility to injury, with a similar baseline g-ratio in constitutive knockouts. Tissue delivery, loss of a biosynthetic enzyme and recovery of conduction answer different questions. For a glial claim, require a reduction in a prespecified carbonyl adduct together with better conduction in the same tract, accounting for axon availability and oligodendrocyte maturation. This interpretation incorporates the independent Codex strategy reply SCI-CARNOSINE-MYELIN. Its quantitative 2023 details come from the authors' preprint, Figs. 7 and S9, and its 2024 comparison from Figs. 1–2 and the Results on S3. [2023 preprint](https://doi.org/10.1101/2023.03.30.534899) · [Published abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37774767/) · [2024 original](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11661932/)

**Pediatric comparison.** Ghodsi et al. randomized 44 children with autism aged 4–14 years. Eighteen per arm completed two months. Plasma methylglyoxal was measured by ELISA. Table 2 gives baseline and follow-up medians of 50.44 and 45.00 ng/mL with carnosine, and 43.26 and 43.14 with placebo. The comparison of changes gave P=.323, or .832 after adjustment. The sample size calculation used pentosidine. This leaves an imprecise negative result on systemic methylglyoxal. The trial excluded epilepsy and measured neither brain exposure nor MG-H1 per arginine. It establishes oral study feasibility for a different pediatric population. [Publisher, Participants, assays and Table 2](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/0004563218796860)

**Receptor comparison.** In cultured hippocampal neurons, methylglyoxal (MG) alone activated GABA-A currents with an EC50 of 9.5 ± 0.9 µM. Its maximal current was about one-third of the GABA response. With 100 µM of each ligand, the current was about 60% of the response to GABA alone. The paper's approximately 5 µM brain estimate comes from tissue measurements. Free extracellular MG at a developing WWOX synapse remains unknown. The high-concentration coapplication demonstrates a possible direction reversal. Its magnitude should not be extrapolated to endogenous conditions. [Distler et al., Fig. 3B–C, Fig. 6A and accompanying Results](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/61319)

**Discriminating prediction.** If attainable carnosine lowers receptor-accessible MG, in low ambient GABA it could reduce tonic current. During strong GABA transients the same change could relieve competition and increase the evoked current. Distinguish both predictions with separate tonic and evoked measurements at native chloride and with measured extracellular MG. In parallel compare intact carnosine, its two free amino acids and vehicle in WWOX-deficient and matched control neural preparations. The carbonyl-protection explanation requires less newly formed MG-H1 per arginine and better recovery of neural activity without increased synchronous firing. An effect restricted to lipid-aldehyde adducts would redirect the chemical hypothesis. Failure to alter relevant chemistry at measured exposure would lower priority further.

**Next checkpoint.** Assess whether primary kinetics and attainable exposure permit appreciable MG removal on the relevant timescale, alongside direct reactivity against lipid aldehydes. If neither chemical engagement nor functional benefit can be supported at relevant exposure, park the candidate for trial prioritization. No pediatric WWOX efficacy or central exposure has been established.
