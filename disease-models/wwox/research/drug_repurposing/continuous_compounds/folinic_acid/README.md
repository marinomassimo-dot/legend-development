# Folinic acid and cerebral folate availability

**Opening prediction.** Explore folinic acid (5-formyltetrahydrofolate, leucovorin). Keep specific stereoisomers and salts as reported in each source. A folate delivery deficit could contribute to abnormal WWOX white-matter development if folate availability is low on the CNS side despite adequate peripheral availability and limits folate-dependent metabolism in maturing oligodendroglia. Alternatively, developmental disruption might reduce the representation of choroid plexus cells or mature glia, generating a transporter RNA or myelin marker change without folate deprivation. Distinguish correction of a folate-limited pathway from increased precursor proliferation. Do not equate folinic acid administered with 5-methyltetrahydrofolate measured in CSF.

**Rationale and scope.** WWOX myelin abnormalities make this hypothesis testable. Three FOLR1-deficient children provide a receptor-specific positive comparison; a child with a mitochondrial DNA deletion provides a secondary transport comparator. Neither genotype establishes cerebral folate deficiency in WWOX. The earlier laboratory catalog queried leucovorin in ChEMBL, without establishing this mechanism. [FOLR1 primary study, author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19732866/) · [Secondary comparator, author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16365882/) · [Earlier laboratory survey](../../advisor_pilot_20261113/brainstorm_20261116/it_adv156_20261121/README.md)

**Prespecified first comparison.** Check Folr1, Slc46a1 (PCFT), Slc19a1 (RFC) and Mthfs (intracellular utilization of folinic acid) in three archived WWOX bulk contrasts. Compare with Ttr, Aqp1 and Otx2 to flag choroid plexus representation; these are context genes, not a pure epithelial cell count. Report raw means, unmapped genes and leave-one-library-out direction. A change in one transcript from unsorted cortex does not measure transport across a blood–CSF barrier. A more discriminating observation would pair age-matched peripheral and CNS-side folate species with labeled folinic-acid passage across a relevant WWOX barrier model, then examine one-carbon metabolism and myelination in a fixed population of mature glial cells. Bypassing the barrier should preferentially rescue a delivery defect; improvement after direct provision alone cannot localize the lesion.

**Result in WWOX.** SLC46A1 (PCFT) is lower in mouse cortex (KO minus control mean log2[1 + expression] = −0.69, four control and three KO libraries) and higher in human cerebral organoids (+0.55, two control and four KO). FOLR1 also changes in opposite directions (−0.42 cortex, +1.02 organoids). All four signs survive every single-library omission. Hippocampal FOLR1 is sensitive to individual libraries (−0.31, omission range −2.86 to +2.24). In organoids, three choroid plexus context transcripts rise more strongly than FOLR1: TTR +2.57, AQP1 +2.25, OTX2 +2.38. Exploratory subtraction of the marker's log-expression from FOLR1 in each of the same libraries gives negative genotype contrasts for all three markers. That sensitivity check shows the conclusion depends on the denominator; it is not cell-type deconvolution or a measure of transporter abundance per epithelial cell. Folate levels, barrier polarity and transport were not measured in these data. [21 gene contrasts](bulk.tsv) · [Within-library sensitivity](organoid.tsv)

**Prior evidence and next step.** The October review identified a gap in quantitative disease-specific CNS folate data, and independently checked a FOLR1 case series that lacked CSF 5-MTHF. The new contribution is the cross-model direction test and explicit organoid compartment control. Next extract the *paired* blood and CSF measurements, age reference, formulation and time relative to treatment from primary transport comparators, then check whether suitable WWOX samples exist. An independently replicated deficit of CNS-side folate with adequate blood folate would strengthen the transport hypothesis. If a compartment-matched WWOX model shows normal transfer but reduced myelination despite adequate intracellular folate, direct the interpretation back to glial maturation.
