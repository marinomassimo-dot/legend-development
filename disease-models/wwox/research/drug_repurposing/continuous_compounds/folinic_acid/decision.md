# Folinic acid disposition in WWOX loss of function

**Decision.** Park folinic acid for WWOX-specific pediatric trial priority. A direct human case weakens a presumption of a general severe cerebral folate deficiency. The evidence still allows an affected subgroup or localized downstream defect. Restore priority if there is reproducible evidence for the lesion in WWOX and productive folate flux and neural function improve with folinic acid, at a feasible exposure.

**Correction to the October evidence inventory.** In Riva et al., Results, Clinical Features, the authors explicitly list folate in a metabolic work-up and state that results in CSF, blood and urine were within normal limits. This is a disease-specific qualitative report in one child with genetically confirmed WOREE and delayed myelination. The sentence lists analytes and specimens together. It does not assign individual analytes to matrices or give folate species, concentrations, collection dates, assay, reference limits or a paired ratio. The phenotype comparison in Supplementary Table 1 supplied no additional folate measurement. An unspecified normal work-up should not be counted as a quantified normal CSF 5-MTHF. The earlier discovery ledger described an evidence gap on the basis of substitute records. Its broad folate-absence wording needs this qualification. [Riva et al., Clinical Features](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9100683/) · [Earlier inventory, DL-MECH-169](../../../discovery_ledger_current.md)

**Scope of the negative search.** The reproducible local scan considered 579 top-level XML files in the held fulltext directory. It identified 94 distinct PubMed-indexed WWOX-titled JATS articles with a body; one had a folate and CSF term in the same paragraph (Riva). Title and section filtering, English vocabulary, non-JATS sources and missing supplements limit recall. This is a bounded metadata/text screen, not 94 completed readings or proof of a universal absence. A numerical age-referenced paired measurement is unverified in the sources examined. **Same administered folate, different lesions.** In Steinfeld et al., a child with FOLR1 deficiency had CSF 5-MTHF of 1.4 before and 41 nmol/L on folinic acid; the separate age 2–5 reference range quoted was 43–159 (35 children). The series was selected for low CSF and normal blood MTHF. Patient-level blood concentrations were not available in the section examined. In Rodan et al., a child with MTHFS deficiency had CSF 5-MTHF of 53 at 13 months before and 35 nmol/L at 2.5 years on folinic acid (study reference 40–150). Fibroblasts from this patient contained about 30 times the control concentration of the upstream substrate, 5-formyl-THF. Age, different primary diseases and lack of concurrent controls preclude a comparison of treatment effects. The counterexample gives a reason to measure conversion to downstream folates even when the administered substrate reaches cells. [Steinfeld et al., CSF metabolites](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2771535/) · [Rodan et al., patient 1 and Fig. 5A,B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6557438/)

**Biochemical response and pediatric feasibility.** In the Kearns-Sayre follow-up, six of eight participants started folinic acid, and three had repeat CSF sampling. In these three, 5-MTHF rose from 7 to 48, 5.6 to 60 and 8 to 82 nmol/L over 0.8, 3.7 and 1 years, respectively (study range 35–124). All three had higher disability scores during clinical follow-up, although some individual findings improved. CSF and clinical follow-up times differed. Case 8 had low plasma folate and case 2 lacked a plasma measurement. Oral pediatric administration is documented; repeat sampling selected a small subset and biochemical normalization did not ensure global functional recovery. The series cannot estimate benefit or safety in WWOX. [Quijada-Fraile et al., Tables 2–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4302586/) · [Paired readouts](KSS_pairs.tsv)

**Discriminating observation.** The WWOX organoid transporter signal changes with compartment-marker normalization. A matched comparison should separate transfer across choroid plexus epithelium from conversion after direct delivery to glia. Uptake with substrate accumulation and poor downstream labeling would weigh against simple replacement. Reduced transport with recovery of downstream flux after bypass, followed by improved myelination and conduction at comparable cell maturity, would support it. In human observational material, age, paired blood/CSF species, nutritional status and sampling time are necessary to interpret any ratio. The comparison can first be done with existing data or residual material. [WWOX expression and context sensitivity](README.md)
