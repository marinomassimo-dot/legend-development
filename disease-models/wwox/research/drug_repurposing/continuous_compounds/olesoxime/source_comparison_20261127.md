**Reproduction.** Use `python3 -B replay_source_comparison.py`. The script transcribes the two threshold rows by nominal concentration, the two reported relative OCR decrements and the two clinical adjusted means. It performs arithmetic on summary data. There is no fit, individual-level record, pooling across systems or uncertainty estimate. The three comparators differ in preparation, insult and exposure.

**Interpretation.** The positive-control separation in liver weakens a general direct block of calcium-induced permeability across tissues. It does not settle the intact neural system. The discovery abstract's association with VDAC and TSPO concerns outer-membrane proteins, and the intact cortical OCR effect is compatible with protection through either membrane or downstream injury signaling. A drop in permeability events under a smaller initial load could arise through reduced activity or calcium uptake. Neither total tissue olesoxime nor nominal culture concentration supplies the free local parent concentration. [Bordet discovery abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17496168/)

**Next comparison and decision.** Follow the prespecified matched-load prediction in the [opening checkpoint](README.md). First look for existing juvenile WWOX data with time-resolved matrix calcium, direct inner-membrane permeability and post-burst recovery in compatible biological units. Characterize independent pore and outer-membrane injury controls in the same window. A documented absence of excess permeability with a sensitive matched-load assay would lower priority. If WWOX shows the expected deficit, a reversible olesoxime effect with preserved first recruitment and an orthogonal cyclophilin D comparator would justify a focused follow-up. A review could change the interpretation if it identifies a matched neural pore measurement at verified exposure or resolves a conflicting direct assay. No such dataset has been confirmed in this opening comparison.
