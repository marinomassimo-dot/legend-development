# Olesoxime | Developmental transfer, exposure and disposition

**Decision.** Park olesoxime as a conditional preclinical mitochondrial-stress probe and defer pediatric WWOX trial priority. In the WWOX sources checked, neural activity and metabolic proxies are available, while native mitochondrial loading, inner-membrane permeability and functional recovery have not been measured together. The age-dependent counterexample makes a higher pore-opening threshold insufficient as a success criterion. A focused review would be decision-changing if it identifies such WWOX neural data or measured free olesoxime exposure linked to a preparation-matched response. ## Why the sign and the exposure each matter

**Developmental counterexample.** Wang et al. report roughly doubled calcium retention capacity after loss of cyclophilin D in brain mitochondria isolated at both P9 and P60. After hypoxia–ischemia, mean tissue loss at day 7 was **20.6 vs 10.5 mm³** in P9 KO versus WT (19/16 animals, reported p=0.017), and **2.3 vs 14.3 mm³** at P60 (13/18, p=0.035). The KO−WT contrasts are **+10.1 and −12.0 mm³** respectively. This is a descriptive sign comparison under age-adjusted hypoxia protocols. It does not estimate a clean age interaction, olesoxime toxicity, or a WWOX effect. Together the two assays show an experimentally verified dissociation between greater calcium retention and later protection. Use the cyclophilin D comparator to identify assay dependence. Require preserved early recruitment and better recovery to interpret an olesoxime response as beneficial. [Wang et al., Methods, Results and Figs. 1 and 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3049447/)

**Electrical and pore readouts.** Kovács et al. report mitochondrial depolarization during seizure-like discharges in hippocampal slice cultures. Calcium-cycling inhibitors affected that signal and cyclosporin A did not in that assay. Barsukova et al. report that cyclophilin D deletion can attenuate mitochondrial calcium efflux during short stimulation in adult cortical neurons. These results distinguish physiological release from sustained permeability and flag the possibility of greater matrix calcium despite a delayed pore event. The scope of the Kovács conclusion is the measured depolarization in the tested preparation. [Kovács et al., Results excerpt and Fig. 5 legend](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15858052/) · [Barsukova et al., Fig. 5 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3183752/)

**Exposure sensitivity.** In Bordet et al.'s adult mouse whole-brain row with highest exposure at day 42, the total parent mean 2.66 µg/g equals **6.66 µmol/kg wet tissue**. The other quantifiable rows correspond to 0.48–3.08 µmol/kg. Sample times are four hours after the last subcutaneous exposure, n=3 per row. The descriptive total brain/plasma ratios are 0.23–0.51 mL/g. They do not specify the free interstitial or membrane-adjacent concentration. To compare the highest row with the **30 µM nominal** embryonic-culture OCR observation, assume a uniform tissue density of 1 kg/L and equal effective free concentrations. Under those assumptions the brain free fraction would have to be at least **4.51 times** the culture-medium free fraction. For an illustrative brain free fraction of 1%, the culture-medium free fraction would have to be no more than **0.22%**. These are equality conditions, with both fractions unknown. Distribution into the relevant membrane and time-dependent effects can change the comparison. The adult BACHD study measured early regional total parent in a separate cohort from the 13-month mitochondrial OCR assessment. Its citrate-synthase-normalized respiratory result provides an intact-exposure comparator, with engagement of the pore unresolved. [Bordet et al., §5 and Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4033913/) · [Gouarné et al., Fig. 6B](https://doi.org/10.1111/bph.12094) · [Clemens et al., Figs. 1H–I and 5C](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awv290)

**Pediatric transfer.** The exact oral suspension has experience in type 2 or nonambulatory type 3 SMA at ages 3–25. Its randomized 104-week motor interval crossed zero. OLEOS is an open-label extension (131 treated, 120 with an adverse event and 36 with a serious event, zero deaths). Sponsor termination accounts for 76 study discontinuations. The week-52 external natural-history comparison was non-significant and motor scores declined over later follow-up. These are observed SMA tolerability and motor outcomes. They offer no estimate of WWOX antiseizure effect or interactions with background WWOX therapy. Product access, unbound neural exposure and a function endpoint specific to the WWOX mechanism remain feasibility requirements. [RCT comparison](source_comparison_20261127.md) · [OLEOS, §§3.2–3.3, Table 3 and Discussion](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.nmd.2020.10.008)

## One discriminating comparison and condition to reopen

**Hypothesis.** In a juvenile WWOX neural preparation, repeated recruitment could accelerate sustained inner-membrane permeability and delay recovery even when the starting mitochondrial potential and initial matrix calcium load are comparable. The rival is ATP limitation or altered calcium loading upstream of permeability, with outer-membrane injury as a separate later route.

**Comparison.** First establish the untreated WWOX and control time courses under one predeclared common input. Track first electrical recruitment, early matrix calcium, a permeability-sensitive tracer, ATP recovery and a distinct late outer-membrane injury endpoint. Use independent controlled-load calibration and an orthogonal cyclophilin D comparator at the same developmental stage. Preserve all randomized traces in the common-input comparison. Selecting traces after treatment according to observed calcium could introduce bias. A selective delay of sustained permeability with faster ATP and circuit recovery at measured local free parent and preserved initial recruitment would advance olesoxime. Smaller early recruitment or calcium load would favor an upstream effect. Better respiration without a permeability change would favor the bioenergetic branch. Pore delay with unchanged or worse recovery would lower priority. Reopen on a replicated native WWOX excess permeability phenotype and traceable local exposure. A well-powered sensitive comparison that finds similar permeability despite impaired ATP recovery would move the research to the rival pathway.
