# Clemizole in WWOX loss of function

`context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`. Follow-up to the [registered prediction](pretest_20261127.md). The test is whether an exposure-relevant neural action can be assigned to clemizole or a defined circulating metabolite and separated from cardiac channel effects.

**Decision.** Defer pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retain clemizole as a conditional mechanism comparator and replace a single serotonin-receptor test with a parent/metabolite concentration-response panel before choosing the native circuit experiment. The available 5-HT2 binding screen does not give functional potency. Functional hERG block and TRPC5 inhibition are directly measured, at different concentration scales. Neither supplies a pediatric clinical risk estimate.

**Exposure replay.** The EPX-100 section of the EILAT XVIII report gives approximately **0.01 µM** peak unbound parent in one repeated adult healthy-volunteer context and 98% plasma protein binding. It is a programme report with no participant concentration records here. The ratio of in vitro midpoint to this approximate parent value is **7 for hERG**, **100–130** for the earlier TRPC5 assays and **307** for the human TRPC5 assay. If brain and plasma free concentrations are equal, these are the illustrative neural exposure ratios. Allowing a hypothetical ten-fold higher free brain concentration still leaves human TRPC5 about 31-fold below its half-inhibition concentration. Actual neural partitioning, effect kinetics, metabolites and free concentrations under the channel-assay conditions are unresolved. The cardiac ratio is an assay comparison and provides neither a safety margin for children nor event risk. No corresponding calculation is valid for 5-HT2 signaling because the functional midpoint is unavailable. [EILAT XVIII §§5.4–5.5 and 5.7](https://doi.org/10.1002/epi.70346) · [Exposure scenarios](exposure_comparison.tsv) · [Reproducible assay data](trpc5_source_curve.tsv) · [Interpolation](trpc5_midpoint_check.tsv)

**Metabolites change the comparison.** The human arm in Nishimura Fig. 1A enrolled 10 for the single-exposure study. Table 2 reports 54.9% and 22.0% of total LC–MS response-based AUC as M1 and M6 versus 4.7% and 2.4% in conventional mice. The calculation assumes equal analytical response among analytes and does not establish their molar fractions or neural exposure. The small adult interaction pilot reported increased parent exposure with a CYP3A inhibitor, and the paper's erratum only corrects the author's given name. The modern programme instead names M6 and M6′ among primary metabolites. Do not merge historical M1 and M6′ without an analytical identity crosswalk. Include verified species in the functional test when exposure and reference standards are available, and record which remains unknown. ## WWOX inference and discriminating observation

**Observation in held data.** In GSE156243 HTR2B is near the detection floor (0.060 and 0.020 CPM, detected in 2/2 control and 2/4 KO libraries). The juvenile mouse cortical baseline Htr2b was undetected in the four controls and measured at low abundance in 3/3 KO libraries. HTR2C shifts in opposite directions in human organoids and mouse hippocampus. TRPC5 also rises in organoids and falls in both mouse regions, with directions that survive each single-library omission. Neither cross-species pattern supports a common receptor reserve. Candidate receptor expression is sparse or varies by preparation. The per-library omissions assess numerical leverage. Bulk RNA cannot establish receptor location, function or endogenous ligand competence. [Readout with background targets](wwox_receptor_context.tsv) · [Human organoid matrices](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE156243) · [Mouse cortex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158682) · [Mouse hippocampus](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158681)

**Inference and hypothesis.** The current receptor screen cannot choose a serotonin subtype and the measured human TRPC5 midpoint is far from the approximate adult free plasma anchor. A potent, exposure-relevant action of the parent or a metabolite remains possible. At much higher test concentrations, a broad reduction in excitability could result from TRPC5 or other ion-channel inhibition. This can coexist with a lower-concentration receptor-mediated response.

**Decisive comparison.** First require functional concentration-response readouts for authentic parent and structurally confirmed human metabolites, using human 5-HT2A/B/C signaling, H1 antagonist function and hTRPC5 inhibition at measured free concentrations. Pair with hERG where relevant and document assay controls and limits. The result selects a native slice experiment only if a plausible exposure band and a mechanistic contrast emerge. In juvenile Wwox-deficient and WT tissue, a serotonergic component predicts fewer or shorter late bursts, preserved first excitatory and inhibitory recruitment, and selective loss of the late effect under an independently validated receptor perturbation. A matched H1 ligand or selective TRPC1/4/5 tool provides distinct comparators where its target is demonstrably functional. Match first recruitment, track the timing and amount of parent, metabolites and serotonin, and check drug-by-genotype-by-perturbation at a prespecified endpoint. If the effect requires high concentrations with first-response depression or persists after the relevant receptor contrast, the selective recruitment hypothesis loses priority. If the preparation cannot respond to its positive receptor control, the null result is uninterpretable.

**Reopen** for validated free pediatric exposure plus a concentration-linked selective native response and credible cardiac assessment, or a randomized epilepsy result that changes the transfer question. An independent review could improve an analyte identity crosswalk or a decision-level exposure and functional comparison when these are available. It was deferred at this checkpoint.
