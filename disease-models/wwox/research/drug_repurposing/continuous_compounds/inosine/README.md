# Inosine and ATP recovery in WWOX-deficient neural systems

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Selection.** The previous adenosine work raises a distinct substrate question. Inosine is an oral purine nucleoside. Its ribose and purine base can take different metabolic routes. A shortage of precursors during recovery from neuronal activity could limit ATP replenishment even if baseline ATP appears adequate. Conversely, inosine could chiefly supply carbohydrate during glucose deprivation or undergo peripheral degradation to urate. The WWOX observations motivating the comparison are reduced ENT2 RNA across three bulk datasets and incomplete evidence for an energy-buffering deficit. Neither establishes an inosine deficiency. [WWOX transporter comparison](../cannabidiol/exposure_and_flux.md) · [Held energy-buffering analysis](../creatine/README.md)

**First comparator.** In ATP-depleted primary astrocytes with glucose and adenine present, addition of inosine accelerated recovery. The paper reports about 80% of initial ATP by 40 minutes with 300 µM inosine and 30 µM adenine; with adenine plus glucose the cells attained about 75% by 120 minutes. These are different times, not an estimated threefold rate. The extra benefit disappeared with purine nucleoside phosphorylase inhibition. In separate comparisons nucleoside alone produced only partial recovery. Availability of the co-substrate therefore matters. This experiment measured ATP content in a stressed glial preparation. It does not establish ATP restoration by oral inosine in WWOX neurons. [Original study, Figs. 6–8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12374869/)

**Discriminating prediction.** At matched activity burden, glucose, developmental state and baseline adenine availability, inosine should accelerate recovery of absolute adenylate pools and evoked function more in WWOX-deficient than corrected neuron–astrocyte cultures if a salvage bottleneck is reversible. Separately follow the purine base into IMP/AMP/ATP and ribose carbon into lactate and ATP ribose. Fractional labeling must be interpreted alongside absolute pools and cell viability. Improvement confined to glucose-free medium with ribose carbon entering lactate would prioritize a fuel route. A rise in urate without neural parent or adenylate recovery would prioritize peripheral breakdown. Dependence on externally added adenine would reduce the relevance of single-compound supplementation.

**Next step.** Compare chemically identified parent inosine versus urate in human blood and CSF after oral administration. Check whether the available exposure can support an inosine-alone effect in neural tissue at physiological glucose, and whether purine base salvage or ribose utilization predicts WWOX-specific recovery. Establish pediatric formulation, age-specific safety including renal stone risk, and interpretable endpoints before considering trial priority. The adult urate-elevation trial is a retrieval lead.
