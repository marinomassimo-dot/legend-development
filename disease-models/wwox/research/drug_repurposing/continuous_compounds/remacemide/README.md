# Remacemide, desglycinyl-remacemide and native recruitment in WWOX loss of function

28 November 2026 UTC. Continuous compound #200, INV-05. context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. [Prespecified comparison](pretest.md) · [Primary reading receipts](reading_receipts.md).

**Disposition.** Hold pediatric WWOX priority. Keep the resolved enantiomers of desglycinyl-remacemide (DGR) as conditional probes of NMDA-dependent recurrence. At the same nominal concentration, bulk sodium influx in cortical synaptosomes can be similar for parent and metabolite while hippocampal burst frequency differs. This discrepancy motivates a state- and exposure-matched comparison. The existing experiments do not establish an attainable free juvenile neural window or a selective circuit effect at native magnesium.

## Exposure and pediatric feasibility

In the [adult interaction thesis, pp. 134, 146 and 155](https://theses.gla.ac.uk/75299/), group-mean DGR **total plasma** peaks in the repeated-exposure comparisons were 64.8, 52.7 and 104 ng/mL on carbamazepine, phenytoin and valproate, respectively, in groups of ten. Converting with the 211.30 g/mol free-base molar mass gives **0.307, 0.249 and 0.492 µM**, or **0.262, 0.213 and 0.420 µM** if the analytic mass were expressed as the hydrochloride. The sources inspected did not settle the assay reporting basis, so retain both. The 10 µM onset in the slice was about **20–47 times** these group-mean total plasma values across the two mass conventions. The selected three valproate completers reached a mean of 0.75 µM on a base basis (0.64 µM as HCl mass), a 13–16-fold difference from the slice and a selected group. These are comparisons of nominal concentrations and total plasma means. The fold differences do not estimate free neural exposure. The 0.7 µM isolated S-DGR condition is closer to total plasma, but the S fraction, local binding, conversion, stereoselective disposition and Mg²⁺-dependent block remain unmeasured. [Input definitions](exposure_inputs.tsv) · [Reproducible exposure comparisons](exposure_comparison.tsv)

In a separate [12-adult phenobarbital comparison, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11298071/), mean DGR AUC changed from 1532 to 533 ng·h/mL. The ratio is 0.348 and shows why a pooled transfer across background medications is weak. The [pediatric study, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11516608/) reports 11 children aged 9–14 years in an open-label escalation with different exposures by induction status. CNS and gastrointestinal events predominated. One child died following a suspected seizure, judged unlikely treatment-related by the authors. A small uncontrolled study cannot establish efficacy, infant safety or a causal adverse-event rate. The pediatric concentration table was not verified in an accessible original and was excluded from the calculations. No WWOX clinical cohort or validated pediatric free neural exposure was identified.

In adults with refractory epilepsy, a twice-daily add-on trial reported 50% responder counts of **18/60 versus 9/60** in the highest studied arm and placebo. A separate trial with a different schedule and cohort reported **23% versus 7%** in its highest arm and placebo. Both enrolled adults on background therapy including enzyme-inducing drugs. These estimates are from primary abstracts and do not imply developmental benefit or transfer to WWOX. A separate developmental rhesus study reported persistent learning-performance impairment in its higher remacemide exposure group. Its exposure distance to children and its molecular cause are unresolved. [Adult trial 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11945097/) · [Adult trial 2](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11945098/) · [Developmental primate study, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11485835/)

## Discriminating prediction in WWOX

In the existing [P13–17 neuronal Wwox-deletion neocortical preparation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609180/), d-APV reversibly suppresses spontaneous bursts. This demonstrates NMDAR dependence in the preparation. Reducing first excitatory drive can also remove NMDA recruitment, and inhibitory reserve may already differ by genotype. The held [AMPA/NMDA comparison](../../perampanel_20261122/clinical_mechanistic_update_20261126.md), [memantine timing model](../../memantine_burst_kinetics_20261123/README.md) and [native sodium benchmark](../phenytoin/native_comparison_20261127.md) constrain both alternatives without establishing DGR potency.

**One experiment.** In Wwox-deletion cortex and littermate controls, compare S-DGR and R-DGR at measured free stereoisomer exposures and matched NaV availability during the same waveform. Record early and late isolated NMDA charge at physiological Mg²⁺, and repeat under zero-added Mg²⁺ only as a perturbation of receptor access. Use d-APV as the pathway reference and an independently verified sodium comparator. In the same preparations verify first excitatory and inhibitory recruitment, evoked transmitter release, and spontaneous burden as count, duration and seconds occupied per minute. Treat the animal as the experimental unit. Keep resting sodium separate from response amplitude where measurements permit.

If S-DGR retains greater suppression of late NMDA charge and recurrence while first recruitment and release remain stable at matched sodium engagement, favor the NMDA route. Loss of the separation after adding Mg²⁺ reduces the relevance of the historical zero-Mg²⁺ result. If changes follow evoked release or sodium state and the enantiomer ordering disappears after the matching, favor those routes. Even a fall in event count fails this comparison if duration increases enough to offset total burden or first inhibitory recruitment is lost. Dividing the published nominal firing IC50 values by their respective NMDA IC50 values gives an illustrative **8.06-fold S/R exposure-index contrast** at equal firing-normalized concentration. Its only use is to motivate measuring both mechanisms, because the protocols, regions and molecular states were not matched.

Reopen for a measured juvenile free parent/metabolite window in which the enantiomer contrast survives physiological Mg²⁺ and controls for first recruitment. Review could change the next decision if it identifies a directly matched magnesium–voltage result or credible juvenile free stereoisomer exposure. Neither is established in the sources inspected.
