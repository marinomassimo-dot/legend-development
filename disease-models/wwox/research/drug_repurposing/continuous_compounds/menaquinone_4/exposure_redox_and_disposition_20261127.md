# Menaquinone-4 · tissue delivery, quinone reduction and WWOX disposition

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · adult human serum/plasma (ng/mL and nmol/L), whole mouse brain (pmol/g and isotope fractions), cell-free human FSP1 and challenged mammalian cultures (nominal µmol/L and relative oxidized phospholipids), mouse OPC/rat DRG coculture (MBP-positive segments/field), P17 mouse cortex/hippocampus (TPM and assigned hippocampal nuclear UMI), week-15 human cerebral organoid (CPM).

**Disposition.** Retain menatetrenone (MK-4) as a conditional probe of phospholipid protection and keep a separate GAS6/TYRO3 control in the myelination comparison. Defer a direct pediatric WWOX trial. A native neural oxidation defect, exposure to the reduced compound and functional benefit at that exposure remain unverified. The mammalian respiratory comparator is unfavorable to replacing coenzyme Q. [Prespecified predictions](pretest_20261127.md) · [Opening comparison](first_comparison_20261127.md)

## Mechanisms that could produce apparent rescue

- **Membrane lipid protection.** Mishima et al. detected MK-4 hydroquinone after incubating MK-4 with recombinant human FSP1 and NADH (Fig. 2b). The liposomal radical-trapping comparison responds to MK-4, FSP1 and reductant availability (Fig. 2d–f). In mouse Pfa1 fibroblasts, **3 µmol/L** nominal MK-4 decreased relative oxidized PE/PC signals in the challenged lipidomics series (Fig. 1e and Extended Data Fig. 2a). The knockout and inhibitor survival comparisons needed FSP1 for full protection in tested ranges, with residual protection at high exposure. Yang et al. identify VKORC1L1 as a parallel reductase in cancer-cell contexts. Carrier density, enzyme localization and NAD(P)H availability can therefore matter independently of total MK-4. In developing rat oligodendroglial cultures, the approximately **2 nM** nominal endpoint was survival during cystine withdrawal. The tested peroxide challenge remained unrescued. [Mishima Figs. 1–3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9402432/) · [Yang, parallel reductase](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cmet.2023.06.014) · [Developing rat cultures](first_comparison_20261127.md)
- **GAS6 carboxylation.** FSP1 also contributed to MK-4 epoxide formation in HepG2 cells with the canonical carboxylation pathway active (Mishima Fig. 4a). FSP1 dependence alone can fit both the lipid and carboxylation hypotheses. Asadian et al. report that GAS6 carboxylation is required for its promyelinating effect and that direct central GAS6 improved remyelination in control mice following cuprizone, with a weaker result in Tyro3-null mice. The distinct developmental study used postnatal rat DRG axons and genotype-varied mouse OPCs in the 10-day assay. With **100 ng/mL** exogenous GAS6, mean MBP-positive segments/field increased **57.29 → 87.52** with WT OPCs and **35.13 → 39.50** with Tyro3-null OPCs. The descriptive difference of changes is **25.86 segments/field**. Separate within-arm tests are insufficient to infer uncertainty on that contrast. The same study's P7 optic-nerve count comparison for Tyro3-null had adjusted P = **0.1186**. GAS6-null counts showed a trend in the early optic nerve, and Protein S was proposed as a competing ligand. These are target and assay controls. They leave the abundance of undercarboxylated GAS6 and responsiveness to MK-4 in WWOX open. [Mishima Fig. 4a](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9402432/) · [Adult remyelination, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38572807/) · [Developmental Figs. 1 and 4D, manuscript pp. 11, 15–16](https://minerva-access.unimelb.edu.au/server/api/core/bitstreams/5c2bfc7f-c41d-5762-9bc0-914c4e58237d/content)
- **Respiratory electron transfer.** MK-4 was detected in a mitochondrial-enriched fraction of HeLa cells, whereas coenzyme Q-deficient human cells failed to recover CII+III activity or the tested ATP deficit with the same nominal **5 µmol/L** bath. This favors interpreting an ATP increase downstream of reduced oxidative damage as a functional outcome until direct respiratory flux at measured inner-membrane exposure is demonstrated. [Mammalian exposure and functional panels](first_comparison_20261127.md)

**Inference.** One mouse-bulk pattern raises the possibility that precursor rebuilding, reductant capacity or receptors could limit an MK-4 response. The same changes are absent or smaller in the human organoids and the nuclear stratification does not support a uniform loss of capacity. A measured high fraction of undercarboxylated GAS6 with responsive TYRO3 would be a distinct substrate-limitation case. Low receptor function even when challenged with already active GAS6 would place the relevant control downstream of the vitamin. Neither case can be decided from RNA or from total vitamin K.

**Falsifier.** First verify absolute native oxidized PUFA phospholipids at a matched developmental stage, activity level and cell composition. Track reduced MK-4, oxidized/unoxidized parent phospholipids and NAD(P)H recovery at measured membrane exposure. In parallel, quantify carboxylated over total GAS6 and measure TYRO3 activation with an already carboxylated GAS6 reference. In a controlled cellular comparison, an early lipid response that persists when GGCX/TYRO3 signaling is interrupted, followed by improved sheath formation or evoked recovery, favors membrane protection. A verified GAS6/TYRO3-dependent functional effect without an early lipid change favors the signaling route. Verify baseline effects of perturbation, exposure and viable-cell counts in all arms. A direct carrier hypothesis requires acute linked CII+III and ATP recovery with inner-membrane exposure and a coenzyme Q positive control. Require native lipid and functional readouts in the same preparation, with evoked response and new sheath formation scored separately. A signal confined to extreme cystine withdrawal, dye reduction or general survival keeps MK-4 as a challenge comparator. Reopen a trial discussion only with measured neural exposure and a selective functional response. An independent review would change the disposition if it recovered these missing measurements.

**Pediatric feasibility.** Japan's Kaytwo oral syrup documents MK-4 use in neonates and infants for vitamin K deficiency bleeding and neonatal hypoprothrombinemia. The PMDA describes a blinded 148-neonate study using coagulation criteria. The product label's **325 ng/mL** mean plasma peak is from three healthy adult men, not the neonatal study. In the retrospective 6,075-neonate syrup cohort, the hepaplastin endpoint was measured in the first month and second-week retesting was selected. That evidence establishes a short, indication-specific precedent without chronic neural efficacy, exposure or WWOX co-medication data. The adult Glakay capsule label documents food effects, states that pediatric trials were not conducted for that product and lists a warfarin contraindication. Kaytwo has a separate warfarin interaction statement and notes no trials in low-birthweight infants. [Kaytwo official label, §§4, 9.7, 10.2, 16.1 and 17.1.1](https://www.pmda.go.jp/PmdaSearch/iyakuDetail/ResultDataSetPDF/530258_3160002Q1040_3_02) · [Glakay official label](https://www.pmda.go.jp/PmdaSearch/iyakuDetail/ResultDataSetPDF/170033_3160002M2028_1_15) · [Neonatal cohort](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11682687/)
