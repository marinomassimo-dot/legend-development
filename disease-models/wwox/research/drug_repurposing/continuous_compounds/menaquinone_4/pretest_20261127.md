# Menaquinone-4 in WWOX loss of function

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · `context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`

**Question.** At measured membrane exposure, does menaquinone-4 (menatetrenone, MK-4) preserve developing WWOX neural and oligodendroglial function through a reduced quinone pool that limits phospholipid oxidation? Compare this with a respiratory electron-transfer effect and a separate vitamin K-dependent carboxylation effect. This is the MK-4 homologue. MK-7 efficacy or blood residence, synthetic menadione and phylloquinone delivery cannot establish MK-4 neural exposure. [Held lipid oxidation comparison](../edaravone/lipid_response_and_disposition_20261127.md) · [Held respiratory quinone comparison](../idebenone/exposure_and_disposition_20261126.md)

**Fixed comparison.** Examine primary mammalian developing oligodendroglial and neuronal concentration-response, biochemical MK-4 hydroquinone and membrane-lipid results, a mammalian coenzyme Q-deficient control with measured mitochondrial uptake, and species- and formulation-matched pharmacokinetics. Separate a defined oxidative challenge from untreated WWOX, viability from first sheaths or evoked recruitment, exogenous parent from precursor conversion and total tissue abundance from membrane-accessible parent or hydroquinone. Record baseline cell composition, maturation, lipid substrate, and AIFM2/FSP1 and alternate vitamin K reductase context.

**Discriminating prediction.** If phospholipid protection drives a WWOX-selective response, reduced MK-4 should increase and native oxidized PUFA phospholipids should fall before first-sheath or evoked-function recovery. The effect should track the ability to reduce MK-4 under matched cell state, even if acute complex I/II-to-III flux does not improve. An electron-carrier effect instead predicts near-term respiratory and activity-linked ATP recovery after MK-4 reaches the inner membrane, with a coenzyme Q reference and unchanged native phospholipid oxidation over the same early window. A later change in carboxylated substrates without either acute signature would motivate a separate carboxylation mechanism. Control for the vitamin formulation/vehicle, baseline carboxylation substrate sufficiency, stage, total NAD(P)H consumption, redox recovery, and cell selection. Stop for lack of a reproducible WWOX baseline lesion, failure to reach the active compartment or apparent rescue supported only by survival or a generic membrane dye.
