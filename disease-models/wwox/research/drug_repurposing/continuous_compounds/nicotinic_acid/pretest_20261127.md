# Nicotinic acid in WWOX loss of function

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · prespecified comparison · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Question.** Does nicotinic acid preferentially restore WWOX neural function through NAD synthesis, or through an HCAR2–CD36 response to myelin debris? The candidate is pyridine-3-carboxylic acid. The held nicotinamide riboside lane probes a different NAD entry route. The held WWOX first-sheath analysis provides a developmental endpoint that can be distinguished from clearing debris after induced adult demyelination. [NR decision](../nicotinamide_riboside/oral_exposure.md) · [WWOX functional sheath comparator](../triacetin/bridge.md)

**First comparison.** In the held juvenile cortex, hippocampus and human organoid bulk datasets, prespecify NAPRT, NMNAT1–3 and NADSYN1 for acid-derived NAD production, and HCAR2, CD36, TREM2, APOE and ABCA1 for myeloid response and lipid handling. Examine nuclear microglia and oligodendroglial pools separately, with target molecule counts and total depth. Record missing and near-zero coverage. RNA comparison can support where to measure flux and engagement. It cannot show availability of usable NAD, debris burden or a receptor response. Independently retain the direct mouse and human microglial intervention evidence and distinguish culture from adult injury.

**Discriminating prediction.** At matched extracellular free nicotinic acid, the precursor hypothesis predicts a genotype-selective increase in labeled nicotinic acid mononucleotide, compartmental NAD and evoked recovery after activity, with dependence on NAPRT and interpretable recovery of native substrate flux. The receptor hypothesis predicts an HCAR2-dependent shift in CD36 and measured debris disposal before any necessary correction of neural NAD. A debris-matched comparison with and without microglia, and a mature-axon control alongside first-sheath formation, distinguishes repair after injury from development. More engulfed material can reflect slower processing or loss of intact myelin. Measure actual disposal, formation of new functional sheaths, axon survival and conduction. A mixed response needs selective controls at matched exposure.

**Decision rule.** Keep pediatric WWOX trial priority parked unless a defined preparation shows selective functional recovery at a verified exposure and tolerability is interpretable. Dietary deficiency correction establishes nutritional need in a different setting. Record an adverse-direction result if uptake of myelin material increases while intact myelin, first-sheath formation or conduction worsens.
