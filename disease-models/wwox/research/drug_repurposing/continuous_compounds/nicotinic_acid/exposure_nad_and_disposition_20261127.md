# Nicotinic acid exposure, NAD capacity and disposition

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Disposition.** Defer direct pediatric WWOX trial priority. Keep nicotinic acid as a test of acid-derived NAD flux, with an independent HCAR2-linked debris compartment. The mouse comparator shows a brain NAD response under a specific oxygen condition. The pediatric comparator shows systemic biomarker improvement despite clinical progression. The strongest WWOX data are untreated RNA and identify no measured NAD deficit, free brain substrate level or remyelination substrate. [Registered prediction](pretest_20261127.md) · [Untreated WWOX comparison](first_comparison_20261127.md)

## Formulation and brain exposure

| Comparison | Result and bounds |

| Neuvonen et al., randomized crossover, **7** healthy adults, three historical products with the same **500 mg** single dose | Parent serum mean Cmax in rapid-release **I**, sustained-release **II** and sustained-release **III** was **7.4**, **2.1** and **0.08 µg/mL**. At **123.11 g/mol** these are **60.1**, **17.1** and **0.65 µmol/L**. I/III ratio of Cmax means is **92.5-fold**. Mean peak times **0.8**, **0.8** and **1.1 h** describe the sampled groups. The historical products gave different parent and nicotinuric-acid kinetics. The parent ratios are neither individual paired ratios nor estimates for all extended-release products. Dose, formulation, conditions and serum versus plasma differ across studies. The comparisons are of measured concentration scales and do not give a pediatric exposure, time above threshold or free parenchymal delivery. [Primary study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17725178/) · [Rawji culture comparison](first_comparison_20261127.md) |
| Hankes et al., historical adult PET | Rawji's reference for human brain access reports lower cerebral signal with injected carbon-11 nicotinic acid than with carbon-11 nicotinamide. At 30 min the cortical activity/plasma ratios were **1.9** and **5**, respectively, and the acid tracer showed greater red-cell association. The earlier report described limited transfer under its imaging conditions. Detectable cortical radioactivity is compatible with some delivery or labeled products. It cannot specify intact unbound parent concentration in a developing brain, and multiplied PET and oral plasma values would not supply that quantity. The **100 µmol/L** value is a nominal culture exposure from the debris study, rather than an HCAR2 EC₅₀ or a threshold for NAD production. Pulsed oral delivery, protein and red-cell association, receptor reserve and local formation of metabolites remain unbridged. Comparing two historical formulations at the same dose supports form-specific exposure work before an in vivo extrapolation.

## Orthogonal counterexamples

- **Brain NAD changes with context in a mitochondrial mouse comparator.** Grange et al. administered intraperitoneal acid in Ndufs4-null mice. At **17% oxygen**, the treatment and vehicle groups had trough brainstem NAD⁺ **2.06 vs 1.26**, cerebellar **5.50 vs 4.33**, and cerebral **5.77 vs 3.96 nmol/mg protein**, respectively, at **12 h** after the previous exposure. Corresponding changes of tissue means are **+63%**, **+27%** and **+46%**. NADH did not show the same differences. Median survival was **104 vs 72 days** in the 17%-oxygen arms, with better rotarod latency at the two later reported time points and no improvement in body weight. The authors reported no neurological or survival benefit from the acid arm in room air. There is no comparison of matched oral and intraperitoneal parent kinetics here. Separate oxygen settings and available measurements do not establish a treatment-by-oxygen interaction or a causal mediation by NAD. Basal regional Naprt RNA in Ndufs4-null mice in air was higher than WT, opposite to the bulk sign in the held WWOX datasets. [Primary Results, Fig. 2G–I and Fig. 3B–H](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8489256/)
- **Peripheral rescue boundary.** Pirinen et al.'s open-label adult-onset mitochondrial myopathy pilot reported blood NAD rising up to eightfold and patient muscle NAD reaching the comparison level. Intervention windows differed for patients and treated healthy comparators (**10 and 4 months**, respectively). The paper's selected muscle-metabolite comparison uses **5 patients and 8 controls**, and its blood analyte denominators vary. Muscle and blood engagement do not identify neuronal precursor source or myelin formation, and no untreated randomized comparator establishes the function effect. A separately published pediatric mitochondrial myopathy case reported improvement of the systemic NAD marker but continued disease progression. It supplies a useful dissociation with no transferable estimate of WWOX benefit or safety. [Adult primary Results and Figs. 1, 5](https://www.brennerlab.net/files/Pirinen20.pdf) · [Pediatric primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39173541/)
- **Cofactor synthesis and extracellular receptor engagement can dissociate.** Human-cell work shows dependence of nicotinic-acid-driven NAD synthesis on NAPRT. In Rawji et al., HCAR2-dependent phagocytosis was shown in mouse marrow-derived macrophages, with human adult microglia represented in the separate exposure comparison. Better debris uptake and electron-microscopic repair after adult focal injury leave first-sheath formation, phagolysosomal processing and conductance unresolved in WWOX. The bulk HCAR2 signal here is sparse across models and its hippocampal nuclear coverage is uninformative. [NAPRT cellular primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17604275/) · [Held receptor and RNA check](first_comparison_20261127.md)

## Prediction and counterproof

**Inference from the replay.** In an illustrative saturating NAPRT step, the maximum flux is proportional to enzyme capacity. With **half** capacity and a reference flux at **20%** of the control maximum, increasing available intracellular substrate **2.67-fold** can restore the target in this model. A reference demand at **50%** reaches the reduced maximum only asymptotically. At **60%** demand no concentration can restore it. These assumed fractions illustrate why a stable decrease in NAPRT RNA is consistent with both substrate responsiveness and non-responsiveness. They do not equate RNA and enzyme capacity or establish the WWOX baseline. NAPRT also depends on its cosubstrate and subsequent NaMN/NaAD processing. [Arithmetic and saturation counterexample](exposure_capacity.tsv)

**Discriminating observation.** In stage-matched WWOX and corrected neural compartments, measure actual free parent, intracellular substrate and a time course of acid-derived label in NaMN, NaAD and NAD. Independently quantify labeled nicotinamide in the source and neural compartments. Label in final NAD can also arrive through an indirect precursor route. Distinguish direct local acid use with a NAPRT-specific control and distinguish an entry block from later conversion by the placement of the labeled intermediate. Match bioenergetic demand and compare baseline and evoked recovery, including axonal NAD and conduction. A verified increase in early intermediates with improved compartmental NAD and recovery supports substrate-limited synthesis. Higher substrate with a plateau in NaMN formation supports reduced entry capacity, while higher NaMN/NaAD with no downstream recovery supports a later limit. A receptor effect should change HCAR2-dependent CD36 and actual debris disposal at matched exposure even when local incorporation is unchanged. A parallel debris-free first-sheath comparison and a check of intact myelin distinguish needed clearance from unwanted loss.

**Pediatric feasibility.** The extended-release label states that safety and effectiveness are unestablished at **16 years or younger**. It flags formulation switching, hepatic effects, increased blood glucose, and other monitoring considerations for the adult drug. Dietary requirement, historical products and exposure in one pediatric mitochondrial disorder cannot settle WWOX pharmacological tolerability. Polytherapy, baseline liver and glucose status, formulation behavior and sensitive functional outcomes remain prerequisites for a trial decision. [US label §§2, 5 and 8.4](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6e604319-e164-4056-ab80-b78b98c519c7)

**Reopen** when exposure enables selective improvement of labeled NAD production and functional recovery in a WWOX neural compartment, or a matched HCAR2-dependent debris response that preserves intact myelin and improves functional sheaths. An independent review could change the decision with relevant free-neural exposure or a route-resolved functional dataset. Current peripheral measurements and lesion geometry leave the main transfer question open.
