# A smaller half-block concentration does not guarantee preferential block at brain exposure

**New calculation.** Bucchi et al. expressed mouse HCN1 and human HCN4 in HEK293 cells. Fig. 1C reports fitted half-block concentrations of 0.94 and 2.0 µM and Hill coefficients of 1.2 and 0.8, respectively. Their fits use a particular repeated voltage protocol. For these parameter pairs the positive crossing of the two curves is 0.208 µM: below it the fitted fractional block is smaller for HCN1, despite its lower half-block concentration. This is a property of the fitted functions. It is not a measured selectivity reversal in brain. Fig. 1 explicitly shows example traces at 0.3 and 30 µM. Fig. 4B also reports an example in which 1 µM produced about 51% block with repetitive pulsing and no detectable block during sustained opening. Channel state and pulse history are part of the exposure comparison. [Original figure legends 1C and 4B](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16484306/)

**Cross-system sensitivity.** Iacone et al. and the existing laboratory figure audit give a two-hour free brain estimate of 4.7 nM after oral ivabradine in GAERS (two animals). At this concentration the projected blocks are 0.17% for HCN1 and 0.78% for HCN4. At 184 nM, a four-animal mean in a separate combination arm, they are 12.4% and 12.9%. Changing both Hill slopes to one, purely as sensitivity analysis with the midpoints fixed, reverses the order at 4.7 nM (0.50% and 0.23%). Thus the exact low-concentration selectivity is fragile; all four estimates for parent alone remain below one percent in these scenarios. This does not put a quantitative upper bound on block in individual WWOX neurons. Whole-brain estimates, one time point, different species/isoforms, and lack of a measured low-nanomolar WWOX curve limit transfer. Applying the same concentration to the two channels isolates a model feature and does not compare cardiac and cerebral tissue exposure. **Decision and next test.** Keep ivabradine active for the narrow question of whether an independent native preparation or active metabolite materially changes this exposure comparison. Compare parent and the N-desmethyl metabolite by measured free concentration, with identical waveform histories and a matched vehicle. A cardiac rate response in pediatric heart failure supplies a peripheral pharmacodynamic endpoint. By itself it provides no neural concentration or cortical target engagement. Prioritize a low-concentration cortical current measurement and explicitly track whether an apparent effect vanishes when hyperpolarizing voltage excursions are matched. Account for the opposite possibility that blocking HCN raises input resistance and increases responsiveness to excitatory input.
