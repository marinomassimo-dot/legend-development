# Ivabradine, developmental voltage sag and central target engagement

**Why this compound now.** WWOX superficial cortical pyramidal neurons have increased voltage sag and input resistance, a pattern with several possible causes. Ivabradine is a non-AED candidate with measurable cardiac target engagement in a pediatric trial. The laboratory already examined a rat absence-seizure model, its major brain-exposure limitation, and the uncertainty about the contribution of the hyperpolarization-activated current to juvenile layer II/III resting resistance. We reuse those results here. The new question is whether channel state and attainable free exposure permit a cortical HCN effect of meaningful size. [WWOX and existing rat comparison, rows O05/T01/T02](../../membrane_hypotheses_20261108/output/observations.tsv) · [Prior layer- and age-specific search](../../ih_rin_juvenile_l23_it_20261121/output/RESULT.md) · [Pediatric cardiac trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28859790/)

**Opening discriminating prediction.** Repeated hyperpolarization and release can engage a blocker differently from a maintained hyperpolarization. If excess HCN current drives WWOX rebound, physiologically attainable free ivabradine should reduce isolated HCN-dependent tail current and excessive rebound in susceptible cells, with the size of the effect depending on channel state. Record at matched voltage excursions and after matching passive input resistance; check whether loss of the HCN conductance reproduces or occludes the drug response. If excess sag disappears after excursion matching, or only micromolar compound changes cortical current while ordinary systemic exposure changes heart rate alone, lower priority for systemic WWOX repurposing. Distinguish inhibitory versus pyramidal cells and interpret the HCN1/4 recombinant comparison with its species and stimulation protocols. No strategy to change barrier permeability is proposed.
