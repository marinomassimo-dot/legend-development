# Ivabradine: parent, metabolite and the case for a central effect

**Decision.** Park systemic ivabradine for WWOX-specific pediatric trial prioritization. Retain the hyperpolarization-activated current (Ih) as an experimental mechanism. Developmental WWOX mouse cortex shows increased rebound and voltage sag with a concomitant change in passive resistance. No targeted native WWOX measurement links parent or metabolite exposure to recovery of this phenotype.

**Independent parent-drug comparison.** Che et al., Fig. 1A–B, found a half-block concentration of 2.94 ± 0.61 µM for wild-type human HCN1 expressed in HeLa cells at −140 mV (six independent experiments). In the FDA pharmacology review of study NP15864, isolated adult mouse rod photoreceptors had an Ih half-block concentration of 2.7 µM. These are about 626 and 574 times, respectively, the 0.0047 µM two-hour free brain estimate from two orally treated GAERS rats. They provide scale comparisons between distinct systems. Half-block is not a minimum effective concentration. Human recombinant HCN1 and native retinal rods both differ from juvenile cortical neurons; the 3 versus 30 µM discrepancy between text and figure legend for the rat cortical experiment remains unresolved. **The active metabolite changes the channel ordering.** In regulator-reviewed study NP08442, parent and N-desmethyl metabolite S 18982 were tested in the same mouse HCN1 assay. Half-block required 16.1 and 26.6 µM, respectively. In NP07905, the metabolite was more potent than parent on human HCN2 (6.9 vs 10.2 µM) and HCN4 (2.6 vs 3.6 µM). The within-assay potency ordering therefore depends on the isoform. In NP15182, direct apical-to-basolateral transport at 10 µM was classified as high for parent and low for S 18982 in cultured bovine brain endothelial cells. This finding weakens the idea of preferential delivery of metabolite to rescue cortical HCN1 blockade. The summaries give neither a measured free human cortical concentration for S 18982 nor a time course of local metabolite formation. The parent’s high in vitro permeability alongside its low net rat brain exposure illustrates that this assay cannot predict net brain disposition by itself. **Clinical feasibility and reopen condition.** Pediatric randomized experience with ivabradine concerns reducing resting heart rate in dilated cardiomyopathy. In native rabbit sinoatrial tissue a cardiac rate effect was measurable at 0.1 µM after prolonged exposure. Cardiac engagement and its safety implications would therefore need to be considered even if cortical benefit remained undetectable. Reopen for a directly measured, attainable parent or metabolite exposure that changes native WWOX Ih and excessive rebound with matched starting voltage, pulse history and voltage excursion, while preserving recruitment and conduction. The current evidence favors testing that mechanism over extrapolating cardiac or retinal responses to WWOX.
