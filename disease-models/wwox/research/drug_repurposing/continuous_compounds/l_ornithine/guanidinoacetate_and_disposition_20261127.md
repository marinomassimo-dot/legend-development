# L-ornithine | Guanidinoacetate, chloride state and creatine reserve

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Decision.** Park L-ornithine for pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retain an AGAT substrate-competition comparison only if a mammalian WWOX model shows a reproducible local guanidinoacetate (GAA) excess. The human mechanism comparators show that lower GAA can coexist with limited cerebral creatine. No accessed sample cohort establishes an excess in age-matched WWOX neural tissue.

## Human observations and counterevidence

| Evidence | Observation and transfer limit |
|---|---|
| [Stockler-Ipsiroglu et al., pp. 19–22, §§2.9 and 2.11, Figs. 2–4](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymgme.2013.10.018) | In GAMT deficiency, plasma or urinary GAA fell in all 35 individuals with at least one follow-up under variable treatment combinations. Four of eight had a repeat CSF assay, with residual elevations. In one adolescent on creatine, adding L-ornithine preceded a plasma GAA change from 19.1 to 12.2 µmol/L (−36.1%) over six months without observed clinical improvement. The pediatric CSF trajectory in Fig. 4 overlaps the [Mercimek-Mahmutoglu case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22019491/) and counts once. |
| [Marten et al., pp. 4–5, Table 1 and Fig. 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10926185/) | One GAMT-deficient child began creatine and ornithine together. The baseline cerebral total creatine peak was undetectable by the authors’ interpretation despite a fitted 0.1 mmol/L in Table 1. At three months it was 3.4 mmol/L in parieto-occipital white matter. The 13 numeric post-start visits had median 2.70 and range 2.30–3.70 mmol/L, remaining below the age reference range over the longer term. Eight visits had a numeric cerebral GAA and total creatine pair. Descriptive r was +0.103 and changed sign on leave-one-visit-out (−0.381 to +0.394). The visits come from one person, and GAA was measurable only in high-quality spectra. The association supplies no estimate of ornithine’s separate effect or of phosphocreatine recovery. |
| [Sipilä et al., p. 686, Table I, Figs. 2–3](https://www.jci.org/articles/view/109905) · [Balfoort et al., pp. 8–10, §3.5.2 and Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1002/jimd.12842) | Six OAT-deficient patients versus six controls had mean fasting ornithine 733 versus 38.2 µmol/L (19.2-fold), similar mean fasting arginine and a smaller urinary GAA response after arginine challenge. This is systemic with renal handling. In a separate OAT cohort, brain creatine was ever low in 15/20 with MRS, including 10 using creatine. Serial scans and mixed treatment make the normal and low categories overlap. Plasma and MRS were obtained on different days. Chronic OAT deficiency is a counterexample to assuming preserved reserve under sustained high ornithine, without defining a WWOX exposure threshold. |

The enzyme and structure support a direct mechanism. Rat crude kidney AGAT shows competitive inhibition by ornithine (Ki 0.253 mM with apparent arginine Km 9.21 mM). Recombinant human AGAT shows active-site product binding and induced fit. Purified human kidney arginine Km was 2.5 mM in another assay. The measurements do not calibrate human neural AGAT. [Rat inhibition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7378422/) · [Human structure](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9218780/) · [Human kidney kinetics](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3800397/)

## What the WWOX evidence can support

**Dato.** In the fly supplement and MetaboLights MAF, the relevant pathway has no exact annotated GAA signal. A blinded-to-genotype mass screen in 38 held positive-mode mzXMLs gave three pooled quality-control maxima near 485, 798 and 1219 s for m/z 118.061102942 [M+H]+. The strongest feature had signal in all tested pools and all experimental samples. Raw five-versus-five geometric knockdown/control ratios were 0.789 in LacZ and 0.430 in Aβ42. Both directions persisted across integration offsets and total-ion normalization. The other features had sparse data or a leave-one-out interval crossing unity. The three peaks lack a GAA identity standard and may be different analytes. Drosophila lacks orthologs of both mammalian GATM and GAMT. The raw data constrain the annotated fly result and do not test the human synthesis hypothesis. [Primary fly paper](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcag385) · [Deposit](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/metabolights/MTBLS12344) · [GAA mass](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/763) · [FlyBase orthology](https://flybase.org/reports/FBgg0002176)

**Inferenza.** Lower GAMT RNA in earlier juvenile mouse and human organoid comparisons leaves net GAA direction open. Both upstream production and downstream consumption can fall. Tissue concentration and the short activity-evoked trajectory have priority over additional bulk RNA contrasts. [WWOX precursor expression comparison](../l_arginine/precursor_fate_and_disposition_20261127.md)

**Controprova circuitale.** GAA can inhibit mature neurons. In the 2026 Cell study, acute adult mouse slices showed increased firing after 20 µM GAA in tumor-infiltrated slices, with no reported increase contralaterally (three slices per condition, Figs. 5O–P). Separate perforated-patch experiments showed a more depolarized GAA reversal potential in tumor-adjacent cortex (four neurons per condition, Figs. 5Q–T). The patch concentration at the pipette and the bath concentration in MEA are different experiments. Contralateral and tumor conditions cannot establish a WWOX effect. The evidence changes the comparison by requiring measurement of the native GABA reversal potential as well as spike output. Receptor activation can be inhibitory, shunting or depolarizing with a context-dependent effect on recruitment. [Cell, Fig. 5I–T and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2026.08.037) · [Mouse pallidal inhibition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12505422/) · [Culture counterevidence](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36671459/)

**Ipotesi ed esperimento.** First confirm GAA excess with an authentic standard and isotope-dilution MS in matched WWOX-LoF and corrected mammalian neural tissue, before and after a matched activity challenge. Resolve intracellular from extracellular GAA, and phosphoguanidinoacetate from phosphocreatine. Then compare vehicle and chemically specified L-ornithine at verified local exposure, matching intracellular arginine enrichment and initial recruitment. An AGAT-side effect predicts an early drop in newly formed labeled GAA at fixed precursor enrichment. A smaller arginine pool or lower labeled precursor entry favors transport competition. Test whether measured-level GAA add-back restores a selective circuit phenotype with a direction consistent with native chloride and preserved creatine and phosphocreatine recovery. A fall in ATP or reserve, failure of add-back to restore the circuit effect, or uniform weakening of initial recruitment lowers priority. The proposed outcome is conditional and supplies no predicted clinical efficacy.

**Pediatric feasibility.** GAMT cases support oral formulation feasibility in children, including use starting in the second year of life, with coadministered creatine and sometimes diet or other agents. The series included aspartate and hydrochloride formulations. It reports tremor and gastrointestinal effects in separate high-exposure cases and raises a chloride load concern for the hydrochloride. Taste limited adherence in the longitudinal case. Formulations, tolerability, pediatric WWOX brain exposure, background antiseizure interactions and long-term energy outcomes would each need separate evidence. Prior pediatric use in GAMT deficiency alone does not warrant advancing a WWOX study. An independent review of existing case series would not supply the missing WWOX metabolite measurement.
