# L-ornithine | Lowering guanidinoacetate while preserving creatine reserve

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Opening checkpoint.** L-ornithine is the sole active compound after L-arginine. Earlier WWOX analyses support testing a bottleneck in guanidinoacetate (GAA) conversion, with native GAA still unmeasured. Ornithine tests the complementary direction. It is an AGAT reaction product and may lower GAA formation, but competition for cationic amino acid transport may instead lower local arginine availability. Pediatric GAMT deficiency reports have direct GAA outcomes under combined interventions. Keep exact salt, creatine coadministration, diet and nitrogen scavengers separate. The WWOX rationale is conditional on a verified excess of local GAA or a demonstrable accumulation under endogenous activity. **Discriminating prediction.** In a stage-matched WWOX preparation with verified local GAA excess and matched arginine entry, a selective AGAT-side action should lower newly formed GAA and late recurrent activity while preserving absolute creatine and phosphocreatine recovery. A GAA decrease that covaries with declining intracellular arginine or reduced first recruitment favors transport competition or suppression. Loss of creatine reserve or worsening of recovery lowers priority even if GAA falls. Keep methylation and efflux distinct by following substrate, product and compartments over time.
