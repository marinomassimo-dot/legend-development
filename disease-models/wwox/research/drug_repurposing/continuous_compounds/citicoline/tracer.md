# Which cells account for more myelin after citicoline?

**Decision.** Park citicoline for WWOX pediatric trial prioritization. Keep it as a probe of developmental sheath formation. The drug improved myelin in a glial choline-transporter knockout, but a second primary model offers a competing causal explanation for more myelin in a tissue section.

**A precursor proliferation control.** Skripuletz et al. studied acquired demyelination in mice. In Fig. 4A–D (p. 408), treatment spanning weeks 4–5 increased myelin proteolipid staining and mature oligodendrocyte counts in corpus callosum, while treatment restricted to weeks 3–4 or 5–5.5 failed on these endpoints (six mice per group). In cultured precursors, incorporation of BrdU and the Ki-67 comparison supported increased proliferation. The authors reported unchanged differentiation and migration. Phosphatidylcholine itself failed to increase proliferation (Fig. 5D). Three PKC inhibitors abolished the additional proliferation, but the authors detected no increase in PKC phosphorylation. These results identify a drug-sensitive process in an injury model. Whether developmental WWOX glia share it is open. [Primary study, pp. 408–411](https://d-nb.info/1113291133/34)

**What a tracer measures.** Böckmann et al. compared four enteral choline sources in 32 premature neonates (eight per compound). We reconstructed the 64 plasma samples from Supplement 1. Each infant supplied either the 1- and 24-hour pair or the 12- and 60-hour pair. At 1 hour median free labelled choline was 1.825 µM after choline chloride and zero after labelled palmitoyl-oleoyl-phosphatidylcholine (POPC). At 12 hours median labelled plasma phosphatidylcholine was respectively 8.085 and 14.42 µM. Four infants contributed to each group at each time. The published four-group comparison at 12 hours was p = 0.14. Fatty-acid remodeling can retain the labelled head group after POPC. A plasma signal therefore has several origins, and none of these infants received citicoline. [Primary article, Fig. 3A](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10030424/) · [Original supplemental data, Böckmann et al., CC BY 4.0](https://media.springernature.com/full/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1007%2Fs00394-022-03059-8/MediaObjects/394_2022_3059_MOESM1_ESM.docx)

**Reproducibility limit.** The concentration medians reproduce the paper. Individual trapezoidal area under the curve (AUC), with zero concentration at baseline and an assumed zero at 60 hours for infants last sampled at 24 hours, gave group medians of 234, 318, 284 and 359 µM·h for chloride, glycerophosphocholine, phosphorylcholine and POPC. Substituting each group's measured terminal median for that assumed zero yields 293, 353, 325 and 426 µM·h. The publication describes an enrichment AUC of 15.5, 21.9, 17.5 and 19.5, p = 0.38. Neither concentration nor either of two percentage-enrichment denominators reproduced all four summaries. The precise scale or processing remains unresolved. **Reopening test.** In an oral rat radiotracer study, 62.8% of the radioactivity recovered *in brain* was in phospholipids. The percentage uses brain label as its denominator and measures neither absorbed dose reaching brain nor myelin-specific deposition. In a maturation-matched WWOX/corrected comparison, follow the same viable axons and the same cohort of postmitotic oligodendrocytes. Measure labelled precursor and product alongside absolute lipid per cell, new sheaths per cell, percentage of axons acquiring a sheath by caliber, and evoked propagation. An improvement confined to a proliferating culture, explained by increased cell counts, would favor precursor expansion. A functional gain after restoration of lipid synthesis at fixed cell number and matched firing would reopen the substrate hypothesis. Human neonatal exposure data concern other precursor forms or an intravenous hypoxic injury trial. The combination of reachable neural exposure, WWOX-specific flux and functional recovery remains to be demonstrated.
