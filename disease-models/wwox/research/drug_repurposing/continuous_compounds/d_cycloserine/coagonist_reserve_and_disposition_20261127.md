# D-cycloserine, co-agonist reserve and WWOX circuit disposition

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Decision.** Retain D-cycloserine (DCS) as a reference for preclinical co-agonist-site pharmacology. Park pediatric WWOX clinical priority. US cycloserine labeling lists epilepsy as a contraindication. The useful mechanistic question is whether state-matched WWOX currents show a response sign or late-charge dissociation after co-agonist reserve is measured. Prefer that comparison over inferring a receptor mixture from RNA. ## Same-assay recombinant comparison

Dravid et al. used Xenopus oocytes, two-electrode voltage clamp at −40 mV, fixed 100 µmol/L glutamate, and normalized each response to that with 100 µmol/L glycine. Values below are source-table means and reported uncertainty. Efficacy denominators differ from some concentration–response denominators. [Primary Methods, Results, Table 1 and Fig. 1 caption](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2862277/)

| GluN1 partner | Glycine EC₅₀, µmol/L (n) | DCS EC₅₀, µmol/L (n) | DCS maximum, percent of glycine maximum (n) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| GluN2A | 1.3 ± 0.1 (9) | 19 ± 1.5 (7) | 90 ± 2 (7) |
| GluN2B | 0.5 ± 0.1 (11) | 8.2 ± 0.9 (12) | 65 ± 2 (17) |
| GluN2C | 0.4 ± 0.02 (21) | 3.3 ± 0.2 (19) | 190 ± 6 (22) |
| GluN2D | 0.2 ± 0.03 (8) | 2.9 ± 0.2 (8) | 94 ± 2 (8) |

The maximal relative response for the GluN2C pair is **2.92 times** that for the GluN2B pair in this assay. GluN2D has a slightly lower DCS EC₅₀ than GluN2C and lower relative efficacy, so effect magnitude cannot be inferred from potency alone. Glycine concentration dependence and displacement demonstrate glycine-site competition under the GluN2C conditions. The EC₅₀ values are functional assay parameters and are unsuitable as binding constants for pediatric occupancy.

The same paper's fast whole-cell HEK293 assay gave **140 ± 8%** relative DCS peak for GluN2C (4 cells). In six paired single-active-channel patches, GluN2C mean open time was **0.46→0.61 ms** and mean open probability **0.018→0.029**. The rounded probability means give a ratio of **1.61**. The Results text reports 163% and the figure caption reports 194 ± 33%. The paired data and aggregation details are needed to reconcile those different summaries. Current magnitude, open probability and gating rates therefore retain their own units and denominators. Cerebellar GluN1/GluN2A/GluN2C triheteromers also have distinct single-channel properties. A GluN2C transcript or a diheteromer response does not determine the sign of a native mixed receptor. [Dravid et al., Figs. 1C and 7 and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2862277/) · [Cerebellar triheteromer primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30056832/)

## Reproducible sign counterexample

A compact two-component interpolation separates initial reserve from a possible receptor mixture. Let r be the baseline current relative to a saturating glycine current under matched glutamate, and let e be the relative current after full replacement by DCS in that component. In the illustration both components have the same baseline r and replace the same fraction q of their functional contribution. For a fraction c of reference current from a C-like component, the change in normalized current is q × ((1 − c) × e_B + c × e_C − r). The coefficients 0.65 and 1.90 use the oocyte maxima. The effective response fraction r and replacement fraction q are illustrative and separate from microscopic occupancy. The interpolation is not a fitted two-site NMDAR gating model.

- At a baseline of **0.40**, the B-like component increases by **0.25** under full replacement. At **0.90** it decreases by **0.25** even with the same receptor composition.
- At baseline **0.90** the sign changes at a C-like reference contribution of **20%**. Contributions of **10% and 30%** give changes of **−0.125 and +0.125**, respectively.
- When effective replacement is 0.50 in B-like channels and 0.80 in C-like channels, the illustrative threshold moves from 20% to **13.5%**. This is sensitivity to unknown competition. These fractions cannot be interpreted as receptor stoichiometry or derived from WWOX transcript counts.

The WWOX recordings already support NMDAR dependence of juvenile cortical bursts with d-APV, and the held IT41 comparison separates fast recruitment and delayed currents. Held Grin2c log₂(1 + abundance) contrasts are **−1.02** in neuronal-deletion P17 cortex and **−1.02** in hippocampus, with negative leave-one-library ranges. The other mouse hippocampal designs are also negative. In week-15 human cerebral organoid the contrast is **+0.51**, retaining a positive omission range, with a low-abundance transcript and few libraries. Held SLC6A9 RNA decreases across systems, with extracellular glycine unmeasured. These observations motivate checking receptor composition and endogenous reserve independently. The model does not supply either. **Prediction to test.** In stage-matched Wwox and corrected preparations, measure the increment to a saturating glycine or D-serine reference under fixed glutamate input with Mg²⁺, pH, stimulation history and initial voltage controlled. Read DCS effects at matched measured free exposure against both baseline and saturated co-agonist conditions. Record absolute peak, late charge, acute release and evoked inhibition in separately identified excitatory and inhibitory cells. Use an independent receptor-composition control before assigning a subunit. Compare bursts at matched magnitude of late-charge change using the held d-APV reference. A matched-reserve sign reversal favors a receptor/state contribution, while a sign that tracks baseline reserve across matched receptor measures favors co-agonist availability. A decay change with unchanged peak is a separate outcome. Loss of evoked inhibition or stronger late recruitment stops advancement of the WWOX intervention hypothesis. ## Human exposure and pediatric interpretation

- **Adult CSF access.** Upton et al. studied **27 adults aged 20–60** receiving terizidone in a pulmonary-TB cohort, with **216 plasma and 27 CSF** samples. Their population model estimated a steady-state CSF-to-total-plasma exposure ratio of **0.69** for measured cycloserine and a **4.7-hour** equilibration half-life. These measurements concern a different formulation, single CSF sampling per participant and model-linked average exposure. They do not establish the peak, free juvenile cortical concentration or target occupancy after an intermittent pediatric exposure. [Primary Methods and Results](https://journals.asm.org/doi/pdf/10.1128/aac.00931-25)
- **Timing and denominator.** In the pediatric ASD skills-training study, the original paper reported **34 vs 33 randomized**, with one placebo assignment excluded before medication. Registered week-11 SRS means changed by **−13.39 vs −17.00** and the comparison had p=0.45. The same cohort's week-22 publication reported **60 completers**, with a robust adjusted difference **−6.4** (SE 3.1, p=0.042). The registry lists **34 and 33** for its outcome summary at week 22 and a different analysis/test. Use each report's own denominator. The delayed result is a signal within one cohort and a different endpoint/time from immediate change. Neither analysis measures WWOX excitability or separates learning during training from neural pharmacodynamic engagement. [Initial primary report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4712595/) · [Follow-up Methods and Results](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13229-017-0116-1) · [Trial registry results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01086475) · [Registry API](https://clinicaltrials.gov/api/v2/studies/NCT01086475)
- **Clinical decision.** The clinical priority remains parked given the US labeling and absence of WWOX engagement or benefit. The same label notes central nervous system toxicity and renal clearance considerations. Reopening the mechanistic comparison requires an exposure-matched genotype interaction with preserved evoked inhibition and patterned output. Independent review would change the mechanistic choice if it identifies held native co-agonist reserve, a directly matched triheteromer pharmacology comparison or physiology that separates immediate current from plasticity in juvenile WWOX.
