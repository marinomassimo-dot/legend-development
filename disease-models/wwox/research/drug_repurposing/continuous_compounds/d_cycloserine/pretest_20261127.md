# D-cycloserine, opening prediction

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Selection.** The existing I11 work nominated D-cycloserine for a co-agonist-site test and left its sign open. Target-first work already audited identity and broad pharmacology. This increment asks whether two opposing native responses can be explained by the local co-agonist reserve or by a change of receptor composition. This is a preclinical pharmacology probe. The current US antibiotic label lists epilepsy among contraindications. [Held I11 mechanism](../../advisor_pilot_20261113/brainstorm_20261116/it_i11_coagonist_persistent_na_20261116/I11_dossier_v1.md) · [Held target audit](../../nmdar_pharmacology_it6_p9_20261029/README.md) · [DailyMed contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f7ad9d0d-3f3e-4116-8bc4-8d76da4b75ae)

**Prediction.** With low starting co-agonist-site occupancy and a large response reserve to saturating reference agonist, adding a site agonist can increase a late NMDA response. At high starting site occupancy, and when the probe replaces glycine at sufficient occupancy, the same probe should lower the isolated response where a lower-efficacy GluN2B-like component dominates and can raise it where a higher-efficacy GluN2C-like component contributes. A different sign between cells at independently verified, comparable endogenous occupancy would motivate a component comparison. Diheteromer responses are reference bounds and do not assign triheteromers or native composition.

**Decision rule.** At fixed glutamate input and matched free probe, measure the response increment to a saturating reference as an operational measure of reserve, then compare absolute currents in excitatory and inhibitory cells and quantify release separately. Track early recruitment, late charge, bursts and evoked inhibition. If no native sign separation survives measured reserve and exposure, retain only a receptor assay control. A switch in evoked or spontaneous circuit activity with altered inhibitory recruitment would be a functional conflict.
