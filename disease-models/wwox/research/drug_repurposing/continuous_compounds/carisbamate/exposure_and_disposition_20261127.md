# Carisbamate in WWOX loss of function

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Decision.** Retain carisbamate as an exposure-conditional comparator for late circuit recruitment. Park pediatric WWOX trial priority. The neonatal rat study shows selective acute suppression of electroclinical spasms. It leaves sodium-current inhibition unmatched between drugs. Pediatric absolute plasma exposure and free neural exposure could not be quantified from the public records examined. The adult comparison makes a synaptic or sodium kinetic contribution testable and puts direct CaV3.1 engagement at a substantially higher nominal assay concentration.

## The neonatal comparator has an unmatched mechanism

Ono et al. used a structural multiple-hit rat model. In male P4 pups, carisbamate reduced behavioral spasms versus vehicle in the first three hours in its 30 and 60 mg/kg groups. In a different P6–7 group, the 60 mg/kg arm reduced **electroclinical** spasms versus vehicle for the first two hours. The later groups received an additional model-inducing intervention and had video-EEG and EMG. The authors detected no reduction in electrographic ictal-like events without a clinical spasm or interictal discharges in the monitored comparison. The P4 phenytoin comparison used its own vehicle and a behavioral readout. The Results report **one death among 17 high-dose pups observed for 24 hours**, without established cause, despite the abstract's summary on mortality. The Results describe no significant sedation on their sampled assessments. There was no prolonged developmental follow-up. [Ono et al., Methods, Results and Figs. 1A, 1C and 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3169712/)

The authors took the phenytoin difference as evidence for a sodium-independent mechanism. For this decision, it supports a comparison worth repeating with measured drug exposure and matched sodium-channel effects. Neither free neonatal brain concentration nor matching of steady-state and use-dependent sodium current inhibition was established. The finding also cannot extend a P4 behavioral null to a direct P6–7 electroclinical comparison. The distinction matters because two sodium-active compounds can produce different inhibition within the same burst even when final inhibition is matched.

**Pediatric data boundary.** The phase 1 oral-suspension report included 7 participants aged 6–11, 3 aged 12–17 and 8 adults. Sixteen of 18 completed. The planned 2–5-year group enrolled none. The accessible primary abstract and same-cohort conference report describe dose proportionality and approximately eight-hour half-life in the youngest enrolled group after a single dose, versus about 12 hours in the older groups. The abstract mentions 12/18 treatment-emergent adverse events (66.7%), one severe affective event, and one discontinuation for sedation. These reports are the same cohort. Neither accessible abstract gives absolute cohort Cmax or AUC. The free publisher article's tables were inaccessible through the publisher routes checked. The US registry has no results posted, so the pediatric exposure estimate remains open. Adult means below are an independent comparator. [Vashi et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42431071/) · [Same-cohort AES abstract](https://aesnet.org/abstractslisting/carisbamate-treatment-of-adult-and-pediatric-patients-with-lennox-gastaut-syndrome-a-phase-i-pharmacokinetic-dose-escalation-study) · [NCT03731715](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03731715)

**Adult benchmark.** In the first of two focal-seizure trials, the early 0–2 h sample-bin mean at steady state in the 400 mg/day group was **6.74 mg/L** without and **4.53 mg/L** with enzyme-inducing background drugs (bin counts 83 and 100). These are descriptive bins, not maximum concentrations or a randomized comparison of enzyme induction. At molecular mass 215.63 g/mol and assumed free fraction 0.56 they correspond to **17.5 and 11.8 µM estimated free plasma**. A higher-exposure healthy adult comparison had measured mean total Cmax of 15.8 and 17.1 mg/L in two study cohorts under carisbamate monotherapy, or about 41.0–44.4 µM estimated free. Estimates depend on plasma binding and contain no measured brain partition. At the 17.5 µM reference, free brain/free plasma ratios required to equal nominal 30 and 100 µM are **1.71 and 5.71**, respectively. The 100 µM value produced only 16.8% CaV3.1 inhibition in its assay. In reverse, if brain/free-plasma ratio were one and free fraction were 0.56, nominal 30 and 100 µM would require total plasma of 11.55 and 38.51 mg/L. These are scale comparisons, with sensitivity over free fraction and partition, and no prediction of a pediatric dose or occupancy. [Sperling et al., Table 2B, printed pp. 340–341](https://ptacts.uspto.gov/ptacts/public-informations/petitions/1477817/download-documents?artifactId=g8o7bf8OBqSxWb4sOrktpn_9olv2AeXuvbqXJvz-CIFL3H9VnpCbD5M) · [Chien et al., Table 4](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/j.1528-1167.2007.01037.x) · [Source for the adult binding scenario](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1016/j.nurt.2006.11.016.pdf) · [Exposure comparison](exposure_sensitivity.tsv) · [Inverted comparison](required_plasma.tsv)

## Control the voltage trajectory and the first spikes

**Within-train counterexample.** Whalley et al. describe piriform cortical action-potential amplitude fits for carisbamate at a nominal 400 µM bath and lidocaine at 160 µM. At 25 and 100 Hz, their fitted onset constants were 399 versus 59 ms and 55 versus 9 ms, respectively. The adjoining Results specify milliseconds even where the Discussion describes different absolute time units. The caption and Results give different counts for plotted points and summary fits. The replay uses the explicitly printed fit constants and makes no sample-level uncertainty estimate. It scales each shape to 40% suppression at pulse 15, a counterfactual normalization. At 100 Hz, pulse 5 still retains **77.6% versus 60.5%** of initial spike amplitude. This shows why matching only the final spike or a single voltage-step IC50 misses a sodium-compatible explanation for differential suppression. The replay describes spike-amplitude shape under high-bath conditions, with no inference about low-exposure WWOX waveforms or about direct channel block from amplitude alone. [Whalley et al., Fig. 4 and adjoining Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2697724/) · [Calculation](pulse_train.tsv)

**Voltage dependence.** A sensitivity calculation uses the published 300 µM recombinant CaV3.1 current and midpoint shift, normalizes each availability curve at −110 mV, and varies the unreported slope for this calculation from 4 to 8 mV. At an illustrative 6 mV slope, drug/vehicle current at the same conditioning voltage goes from 0.70 at −90 mV to 0.50 at −70 mV. These are simulated consequences of holding voltage, not measured responses. In the held WWOX mouse P13–17 cortical series, resting potential shifts from about −79.3 mV (WT) to −73.6 mV (neuronal KO), with higher input resistance and sag. The conductance interpretation therefore requires both common voltage commands and native starting voltages. Held CACNA1G RNA is lower in neuronal-KO mouse cortex, weakly higher in human organoids, and omission-sensitive in mouse hippocampus. The data give no evidence for a general CaV3.1 excess. The separate fetal cortex check also supports keeping progenitor fate separate from mature circuit physiology. **Discriminating test.** Record the native burst waveform, then compare WWOX and corrected/WT preparations using matched free drug, initial excitatory and inhibitory recruitment, and matched transient and late sodium charge over the full train and after recovery. Matching sodium function means matching the current waveform and recovery at the relevant intervals. If residual suppression persists, compare low-threshold CaV3 current and Ih tail current at the same voltage trajectory and measure terminal spike invasion and glutamate release separately. If the late recurrence and rebound effects track CaV3 current and are occluded by an independent CaV3 perturbation while Ih and release are preserved, the CaV3 branch gains support. If only the measured terminal output/release changes at matched somatic and terminal recruitment, test the synaptic branch. If voltage-excursion normalization or matching Ih explains the change, the CaV3 assignment loses priority. If the residual disappears after sodium matching, a separate target is unnecessary for that response. Reduced initial recruitment, impaired physiological inhibition, or dependence on high nominal exposures without a local exposure bridge weakens translation. Compare each age and region separately, with donor or animal as the replication unit.

## Clinical transfer and reopening criterion

- **Focal-seizure efficacy was inconsistent.** The two lower-dose randomized trials reported a 400 mg/day effect only in one study. In the separate 547-participant trial the primary publication reports no significant combined-dose contrast for frequency reduction or responder rate (P=0.20 and 0.18). The EU tabulation separately lists 48/183, 49/176 and 66/181 for a ≥50% response in placebo, 800 and 1,200 mg/day, with nominal P=0.043 for its higher-dose pairwise contrast. The trial randomized 547 participants, and the EU endpoint table accounts for 540 analyzed. The pairwise and combined-dose tests warrant separate reporting. This is focal epilepsy, with age eligibility 16+, and no WWOX or infantile-spasm efficacy estimate follows. [Sperling et al., primary publication](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19863578/) · [Halford et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21320109/) · [EU register primary endpoint results](https://www.clinicaltrialsregister.eu/ctr-search/trial/2008-007687-41/results) · [Denominator replay](adult_focal_response.tsv)
- **Pediatric trial feasibility and endpoint transfer.** NCT05219617 lists a phase 3 Lennox–Gastaut drop-seizure trial, ages 4–55, recruiting as of the 24 November 2026 registry version, with 252 as planned enrollment and no posted US results. The 15-person open-label safety extension lists eligibility from two years, but has no posted US results or verified age-specific outcomes. The FDA infantile-spasm orphan record is designation without approval for that indication. A future WWOX comparison requires seizure-type and EEG definitions, baseline and treatment attribution, separate vigilance and motor measures, and free drug and co-medication monitoring. Adult controlled interaction data include differences in valproate and lamotrigine systemic exposure at the tested co-exposures, while focal trial strata show lower carisbamate concentrations with background induction. These are not pediatric interaction estimates. [Registry snapshot replay](clinical_registry.tsv) · [Phase 3 registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05219617) · [Extension registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04062981) · [FDA orphan designation](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=362511) · [Adult interaction study, Tables 4–6](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/j.1528-1167.2007.01037.x)

**Reopen** for absolute age-stratified exposure and a plausible free CNS bridge, or a specific functional WWOX effect at measured relevant exposure that survives waveform and recruitment matching. A review of underlying exposure tables or a reproducible genotype-specific target-engagement result could change the decision. A second interpretation of the unmatched phenytoin arm would leave the key measurements missing.
