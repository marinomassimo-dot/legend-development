# Carisbamate and burst scaling in WWOX loss of function

**Selection.** Carisbamate is an investigational antiseizure compound with published oral-suspension pharmacokinetics in children aged 6–17 with Lennox–Gastaut syndrome and a stage-relevant infantile-spasm animal comparison. The FDA lists orphan designation for infantile spasms and identifies the indication as unapproved. In the provoked multiple-hit rat model, carisbamate reduced spasms acutely while phenytoin did not in a separate comparison. The effect does not identify its target without matched exposure and functional sodium-current inhibition. Existing WWOX comparisons already provide sodium-current and evoked recruitment controls. [Phase 1 primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42431071/) · [FDA orphan record](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/opdlisting/oopd/detailedIndex.cfm?cfgridkey=362511) · [Rat primary abstract and figure captions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21770922/) · [Held WWOX sodium-current comparator](../cenobamate/kinetics_and_disposition_20261126.md)

**Prediction.** At measured free parent and matched early recruitment, sodium-current availability and recovery across the actual burst waveform, carisbamate could still reduce late burst propagation while preserving inhibition. A residual effect that tracks loss of low-threshold rebound would favor testing native CaV3 dependence. A residual effect that tracks diminished evoked glutamate release, with rebound preserved, would favor a synaptic explanation. If the advantage disappears once sodium-channel use dependence and recruitment are matched, a separate target hypothesis loses priority.

**Counterchecks.** Match excitatory and inhibitory recruitment separately. Examine evoked versus miniature glutamatergic currents and independent measures of release, low-threshold rebound, membrane excitability, and delay to re-engagement. Assign a residual effect to CaV3 only with a relevant native current and an independent perturbation. Use age- and circuit-matched preparations. Separate correction of a disease-selective network phenotype from sedation or global suppression. [Primary voltage-gated calcium comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28230232/) · [Primary dentate synaptic comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21824485/)
