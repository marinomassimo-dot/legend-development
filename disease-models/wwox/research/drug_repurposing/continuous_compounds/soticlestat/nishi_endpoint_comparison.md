# Soticlestat | Primary comparator for late recruitment

SCI_CODEX · 27 November 2026 · Further checkpoint on the same active molecule.

**Data.** Nishi et al. (2022), Methods §2.2–2.5, Results, Tables 1–2 and
Figs. 2–4 compare different models, regimens and endpoints. After three days of
pretreatment, Frings audiogenic seizure protection was 7/8 with soticlestat
versus 0/8 with vehicle. Mouse electroshock, acute scPTZ and both 6 Hz
intensities had 0/8 protected in each arm (Table 1). A separate acute PTZ cohort
after two weeks of infusion gave 1/9 protected with vehicle, 0/8 and 2/9 with
two active exposures, with approximately 50% and 90% lower bulk 24S-HC,
respectively (main Results referring to Table S4). Supplement S4 was not
opened. This small arm had weak power to establish equivalence. In rat
amygdala kindling, the mean stimulations to first focal seizure were 2.7 and
2.8, and mean focal afterdischarge was 72.8 and 65.2 seconds for vehicle and
soticlestat. Neither difference was reported significant. First generalized
seizure required 8.9 and 13.2 stimulations. Mean generalized seizures were 8.2
and 4.4, a descriptive 46% reduction from rounded group means
(`1 - 4.4/8.2`). Rats reaching full kindling were 9/12 and 5/11, untested in
the source. Endpoint rules capped values for animals that failed to reach a
stage. Mouse PTZ kindling Figure 4C–E reported a 75% lower cumulative Racine
score and a 92% fall in bulk 24S-HC at the highest exposure. Its pooled
correlation r = .682 is a joint change with exposure, without mediator
isolation. [Primary article](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.17232)

**Inference.** A 24S-HC decrease can coexist with little protection in an
acute provocation. The separation of focal onset and spread in a different
model lends design support to measuring first and late WWOX responses
separately. The generalization endpoint remains distinct from late cortical
recruitment in a WWOX slice. These provoked models cannot establish a WWOX
benefit or set a portable biological threshold from bulk 24S-HC.

**Hypothesis and decisive observation.** At matched juvenile age, test
whether soticlestat-induced lowering of available cortical 24S-HC precedes
a selective fall in the late NMDA-sensitive response in Wwox-deficient
tissue. Test reversal by bringing 24S-HC back toward its measured baseline
while the parent compound is still present. Quantify early recruitment and
inhibitory output separately. A fall in late recruitment without add-back
reversal at verified available exposure favors another mechanism. A fall
without measured 24S-HC reduction is inconclusive for the pathway.
Focal/spread precedent favors the existing cortical comparator. It leaves
native cell-class dependence and the compound-specific kinetics unresolved.

**Cell-class counterproof.** Shu et al. measured bulk hippocampal 24S-HC by
LC-MS after global or VGLUT1-Cre loss of Cyp46a1 (Methods §2.4 and Fig. 1). The
excitatory conditional deletion substantially reduced tissue 24S-HC while
leaving a residual pool. The Fig. 1 caption/Results use cohort-matched WT and
give the conditional cohort as P100–120 and global series as P100–550; Methods
gives an assay age range of 58–128 days. Their results pool some
global animals across ages. Conditioned-freezing changes in global loss were
absent from the VGLUT1 conditional comparison in Fig. 6, with separate ages
and cohorts. The Allen expression overlay in Fig. 1A points to both
glutamatergic and GABAergic sources. These data leave both lower overall
depletion and relevant cell class as possible explanations for the conditional
result. No inhibitory-selective deletion or juvenile WWOX physiology was
tested there. This motivates recording cell-specific responses in the same
WWOX setting rather than inferring preserved inhibition from total spiking.
[Primary article](https://doi.org/10.3390/biom14030254)
