# L-valine | Substrate supply versus impaired mitochondrial disposal

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Disposition.** Retain L-valine as a conditional preclinical substrate and isotope-tracing comparator. Defer pediatric WWOX intervention priority. The held WWOX RNA signal supports testing the pathway. Direction depends on measured compartmental substrate availability and functional flux. In the primary ECHS1-deficient mouse comparator, the supplement increased measured brain valine and coincided with worse outcomes after a seizure challenge. The restriction arm produced little change in mean brain valine in mutant mice. [Prespecified comparison](pretest_20261127.md) · [Printable research brief](l-valine-research-brief.pdf)

## Reproducible exposure and two directions

Eller et al. used an Echs1 F33S/null mouse model. Fig. 4 used supplementation from gestation, with litter exposure. Fig. 6 used an amino-acid-defined control and restriction starting at weaning. Separate three-month old cohorts provided the whole-brain chemical measurements (5–7 mice per group in Supplementary Tables S2–S3). Food delivery, brain parent, tissue chemistry and provoked seizure outcomes have different denominators.

| Within-study intervention | WT brain valine, control → intervention | ECHS1D brain valine, control → intervention | Boundary |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Supplementation, Table S2 | 54.1 → 160.8 nmol/g (+197%) | 42.9 → 110.1 nmol/g (+157%) | Parent rose in both. The genotype-by-diet difference in absolute change is −39.5 nmol/g, with an illustrative independent-arm SEM of 33.8. |
| Restriction, Table S3 | 61.2 → 35.8 nmol/g (−42%) | 42.7 → 42.1 nmol/g (−1%) | The authors report p = 0.001 in WT and p = 0.94 in ECHS1D. The exploratory absolute interaction is +24.8 nmol/g (independent-arm SEM 9.7). Cell and regional exposure under restriction remains unresolved. |

The estimates use reported means and SEMs. Litter correlations and covariance were unavailable. The contrast of simple effects does not itself establish a diet-by-genotype interaction, and cross-arm reporting does not show equivalence. Table S1 also shows that the high-valine and standard foods differ in leucine, isoleucine, lysine and macronutrients. The restriction comparators have matched formulation leucine/isoleucine and substantially different valine. These cohorts cannot define a continuous exposure–response curve. [Eller, Figs. 4–7, PMID 42947772 and publisher supplement](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13710032/) · [Calculated contrasts and source units](quantitative.tsv)

**Toxic-product branch.** In Fig. 4F, circulating SCPC rose approximately twofold within ECHS1D under supplementation. Table S5 gives genotype-by-diet interaction p = 0.2843 despite a significant simple comparison. This supports a within-mutant signal with limited precision for specificity. Fig. 4J shows 0% at the last observed step of the supplemented mutant group and 50% in the control-diet mutant group, with small groups (5–6 animals per arm for the challenge). The original x-axis uses injection number up to 12. The Methods also say 12 injections, whereas the Fig. 4 legend says 12 days. Interpret the survival and provoked-event endpoints by event number. Baseline survival and spontaneous electrographic seizure benefit cannot be estimated from this challenge. [Eller, Fig. 4 and Table S5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13710032/)

**Restriction branch.** Fig. 6 and Table S10 do not show reduced seizure severity or latency at the measured exposures. The within-mutant diet main-effect p values were 0.556 and 0.490, respectively. Injection-number effects describe a different factor. Circulating SCEC rose within the restricted mutant comparison, with genotype-by-diet p = 0.0706 in Table S9. SCEC can be generated from acryloyl intermediates shared with other substrates. It should not stand in for valine flux. Fig. 7 body text reports reduced energy charge for WT (p = 0.009) but no significant reduction in the mutant (p = 0.181). This is a counterexample to assuming stronger dietary reduction is benign. [Eller, Figs. 6–7 and Tables S9–S11](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13710032/)

**Reanalysis of the pyruvate-dehydrogenase proxy.** In the supplement cohort, the WT ratio of arm means for alanine/lysine rises 18% despite nearly stable alanine. Lysine falls 17%. In the ECHS1D group the ratio rises about 70%, dominated by a 64% alanine increase with lysine down 3%. These ratios of group means differ from the paper's average of within-brain ratios. The paper reports an omnibus interaction for the latter (Table S7 p = 0.0018). The denominator explains much of the WT shift and little of the mutant shift. This measurement still leaves direct PDC activity and pyruvate oxidation unresolved. Whole-brain energy charge in the supplement cohort was preserved within the reported uncertainty. [Eller, Fig. 5 and Tables S2, S7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13710032/) · [Log-ratio decomposition](quantitative.tsv)

**Pediatric feasibility.** L-valine is an essential amino acid. Defined oral nutritional products and medically supervised formula adjustments in enzyme-confirmed disease have a systemic monitoring precedent. They do not establish a pediatric WWOX target concentration or a clinically useful direction. Insufficient exposure can compromise growth and protein incorporation. Supra-baseline exposure may redistribute other large neutral amino acids or overload clearance when enzyme reserve is low. No verified WWOX neural PK or interaction with background antiseizure polytherapy was identified. Preserve separate growth, motor, metabolic and electrographic seizure endpoints in any future research decision.

ECHS1, BCAT2 and DLAT preserve the sign in all single-library exclusions. HIBCH and ACAD8 do not imply a conserved downstream block. Lower upstream formation through BCAT2/ACAD8 is a competing explanation for low productive flux and could limit the supply of intermediates to ECHS1. In the single pooled hippocampal nuclear library per genotype, **Echs1** falls in excitatory and inhibitory assignments (15.6→12.4 and 18.2→16.5 CPM), and is stable or modestly higher in astrocyte and OPC assignments. RNA and descriptive mixtures do not establish enzyme activity, transport, residual substrate handling or charging. Tissue type and development differ across systems. **Inference.** The mouse diet experiments establish that delivered substrate and measured neural parent can move differently across genotypes. A WWOX test has to establish whether the affected compartment is substrate-limited, clears valine slowly, or has lower input to the pathway. The held WWOX pyruvate study identifies a separate carbonyl/redox explanation for PDC impairment. Measure independent pyruvate oxidation rather than infer it from an amino acid ratio. [Held pyruvate-flux design](../sodium_dichloroacetate/README.md)

**Discriminating experiment.** Compare stage- and lineage-matched WWOX-deficient and corrected neural preparations at several measured local valine levels, matching intracellular exposure across genotypes within each comparison and fixing competing neutral amino acids and demand. From uniformly carbon-labeled valine, quantify parent M+5 enrichment and intact label in charged tRNA and new protein. Compare early M+4 valine-catabolic products, later M+3 propionyl-derived products and label in the valine-derived four-carbon moiety of separately identified SCPC. Normalize product appearance to intracellular precursor enrichment and report absolute together with fractional labeling. Track SCEC separately because an unlabeled rise can reflect compensation from other sources. Countercheck direct ECHS1 activity in the same material, verify chromatographic separation (including C4:1 isomers), and measure pyruvate oxidation and respiration in matched aliquots. Use an enzyme-defined positive control to verify that the assay detects diversion at equivalent substrate exposure.

- **Supply limitation** predicts recovery of charging or absolute downstream labeled products and improved function without disproportionate conjugate accumulation.
- **Overflow** predicts a higher share of label in SCPC coupled to reduced productive labeled flux, impaired pyruvate oxidation or poorer recovery. A response to a change in valine would need to preserve charging and protein incorporation.
- **Low upstream production or unrelated PDC stress** predicts little valine-derived label reaching either productive or overflow branches, or a PDC phenotype that fails to track changes in partitioning.

These signatures are proposed tests. Reopen when native brain-cell parent and isotope partitioning can be linked to a functional change at relevant exposure. A review could alter the decision with new native data or better estimates of cohort and assay covariance. The present study's denominators and interactions are explicit and cannot settle the missing native observations.
