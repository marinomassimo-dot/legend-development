# Miglustat · lipid identity, developmental response and disposition

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · QUESTION_DRIVEN · [Prespecified question](pretest_20261126.md) · [Opening comparison](README.md)

**Disposition.** Defer miglustat for a pediatric WWOX trial. Retain it as a mechanistic comparator for opposing glucosylceramide synthesis and clearance. CSF penetration is measured in a different neurological disorder. WWOX remains missing a native lipid indication for substrate reduction and a functional window that preserves development. The new acute-neuron result and the limits of the human lipid readout both favor checking turnover before assuming synthesis correction.

## Developmental counterproof at a nearby concentration scale

Woeste et al. report shorter neurites in juvenile mouse cerebellar cultures exposed to **5 µM nominal miglustat for 48 h** (Fig. 4G–H, nominal **p=.002**, n=3 per condition in the panel, replication unit not stated there). A separate 30 pM AMP-DNM arm also had shorter neurites and reduced the GBA2 activity reading from **0.30 ± 0.10 to 0.03 ± 0.02 rfu/min** (n=3). In the same paper, lifelong Gba2 deletion abolished activity while neurite length in the isolated cultures remained similar. Acute pharmacology, lifelong adaptation and additional drug targets therefore provide distinct explanations. The age range in the figure caption is P6–9, while the acute Results paragraph specifies P7–8. The H-panel reports a normalized length comparison. The analysis uses the reported concentration and direction. An individual-neurite dataset and a concentration–response threshold remain unavailable. An independent human SPG46 report shows complete GBA2 activity loss in the evaluated variant's blood cells and progressive motor disease starting in childhood. This establishes a developmental context where loss of the enzyme is adverse, while partial reversible inhibition can have a different outcome. [Juvenile neurites, Woeste Fig. 4 and Results](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.ra118.006311) · [Human genetic comparator](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ajhg.2012.11.021)

The **5 µM culture input** is **1.74–5.45 times** four measured human type 3 Gaucher predose CSF values (**0.917–2.87 µM**). The ratio compares nominal culture concentration to CSF sampled before a dose. It cannot establish a safety margin or an intracellular exposure. TGA's NPC review reports geometric-mean total **plasma** peaks of **12.30 µM** in an older six-person group and **15.00 µM** in one younger two-person subgroup. A separate repeated oral monkey radiotracer experiment reports CSF **0.752 and 0.732 µg/mL** at two 8 h observations, equivalent by molecular mass to **3.43 and 3.34 µM**. The TGA excerpt identifies the plasma tracer and does not resolve the molecular composition of all radiolabel. Species, timing and compartment prevent combining these values into a human CNS range. Reports in infantile Tay–Sachs and a pediatric analytical demonstration name CSF detection. The accessible abstracts leave the timed numerical concentrations unavailable. [Direct GD3 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2605167/) · [TGA pp. 9, 20–21 and 52](https://www.tga.gov.au/sites/default/files/auspar-miglustat.pdf) · [Infantile detection](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16434676/) · [Pediatric LC-MS description](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19095507/) · [Replay](developmental_replay.tsv)

The GBA2 assay result is assay-dependent. With artificial 4MU substrate, Körschen et al. report **18.2 ± 0.3 nM** at pH 6 in mouse brain lysate without detergent (Fig. 4F). In the liver comparison at the same pH, the corresponding apparent IC50 was **12.0 nM** without detergent and **11.0 µM** with detergent (Fig. 4H), roughly a **917-fold** shift. GBA1 and GBA2 contribute overlapping activity with detergent, so the shift includes possible enzyme mixture as well as altered pharmacology. A nominal CSF value cannot identify either enzyme's occupancy. The opener's **0.326 µM human GBA2 Ki** and **50 µM in-situ GCS IC50** remain useful as distinct assay scales. [Original assay and controls, Fig. 4F–H](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3561557/) · [Human overexpression and synthesis assays](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5677758/)

## What the lipid observations resolve

- **Human treatment-associated signal by acyl chain.** In Fan 2013 NPC1, Fig. 7 reports increasing CSF monohexosylceramides by normalized peak area after starting miglustat. The group panel uses eight people and the individual paired panels use nine. The paired effects are reported for **18:0, 20:0, 24:0 and 24:1**. Because the published endpoint is the monohexosylceramide family, infer neither the GlcCer direction nor intracellular retention from those panels. A separate feline NPC intervention that mobilizes lipids also increased the CSF family signal (Fig. 8). Extracellular release can therefore contribute a distinct explanation. The exact sampling intervals and the Fig. 7 denominators beyond the caption remained unverified in this pass. [Fan Results, Figs. 7–8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3770093/)
- **Identity and matrix controls.** Xu et al. separate the glucose and galactose headgroup isomers using LC plus differential ion mobility and independent standards. In a single human CSF extract, with four technical replicates, they quantify six GalCer species and two GlcCer species. GalCer d18:1/24:1 was **242.80 ± 19.72 pmol/mL** and GlcCer d18:1/24:0 **8.25 ± 0.83 pmol/mL**. These are different acyl chains and method examples from one extract. They provide neither a population reference nor an NPC or WWOX effect size. The authors also identify M+2 overlap from a more abundant 24:1 species when measuring 24:0. Validate that separation in the actual WWOX matrix with standards before interpreting a headgroup or chain. [Xu Results, Table 1 and Fig. 5F](https://www.jlr.org/content/60/1/200.full.pdf)
- **WWOX material and scope.** GSE156243 is bulk high-throughput RNA expression from 15-week cerebral organoids. GSE158681/2 are mouse brain RNA and GSE158684 is pooled nuclei RNA. The 14 authenticated metabolite rows in the held fly MTBLS12344 annotation table contain no candidate glucosyl-, galactosyl-, hexosylceramide or sulfatide species. This is a negative on the inspected annotation table, with unmatched features and unassayed compounds unresolved. Separate human oligocortical spheroids do show developmental myelin defects at later stages. They are a better lineage assay and a different preparation from the early cerebral bulk RNA. The October lipid wave's chain-specific table is from CerS2-deficient comparator animals. Across these sources, the native WWOX GlcCer/GalCer direction remains unmeasured. ## WWOX maturity sensitivity

In the GSE158684 data, symmetric decomposition of the UGT8A pooled change of **−36.97 CPM** assigns **−19.58** to changing rates within assigned classes and **−17.38** to changing class weights. For GAL3ST1, **−19.54 CPM** splits into **−15.51** and **−4.03**. These are weights of captured UMIs. Across the three score bands of the mature assignment, both transcripts have lower pooled CPM in KO. In the highest band, Ugt8a CPM is **936.83 → 210.11**, with detection **47/59 → 4/12**, and Gal3st1 CPM is **310.17 → 84.04** with detection **30/59 → 2/12**. The marker score means are 3.52 and 3.35 and median depth 2449 and 3341. The strongly imbalanced maturity distribution and one nuclear pool per genotype leave fine stage, sampling and expression differences unresolved. The target genes were excluded from the original classifier and the stratification score. The data motivate measuring GalCer and sulfatide supply, while activity and absolute lipid pools remain to be measured. **Reopen** on a reproducible isomer-resolved native excess in a relevant WWOX lineage, with a measured-response comparison at the relevant free exposure. In the same WWOX and corrected cells, measure newly synthesized GlcCer, loss of prelabelled GlcCer during chase, recycled ceramide and chain-resolved GalCer and sulfatide. Couple the paired biochemical result to absolute mature neurite length, viable first sheaths per axon and evoked recovery after washout. At preserved early synthesis, slower GlcCer clearance with less tracer appearing in ceramide and GalCer favors inhibited clearance. Reduced initial GlcCer incorporation with preserved clearance and preserved or improved GalCer output favors synthesis competition. Include independently measured GBA1, GBA2 and UGCG activity or target-specific controls. A lipid signal with reduced viable neural or myelin output is a failed developmental comparison. Tissue and medium lipid gains should be tracked separately.

**Pediatric relevance.** Miglustat has regulated pediatric precedent for neurological NPC, with limited experience in younger children. Gastrointestinal effects, growth, renal function and baseline motor differences complicate interpretation of a developmental WWOX endpoint. The human storage-disease evidence provides clinical context. A WWOX trial choice still requires age-appropriate exposure, background antiseizure-drug assessment, and direct preservation of development in a relevant model. Independent review would change the disposition if it identifies actual stage-matched WWOX chemistry, a relevant native-flux exposure window or an acceptable developmental selectivity comparison. The remaining external dose reconstruction alone would leave the disposition intact. [EMA §§4.2, 4.4, 5.2](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/product-information/zavesca-epar-product-information_en.pdf)
