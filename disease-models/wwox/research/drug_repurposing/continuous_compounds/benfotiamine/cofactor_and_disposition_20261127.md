# Benfotiamine in WWOX loss of function

`context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`. Use the [prespecified comparison](pretest_20261127.md) and distinguish S-benzoylthiamine O-monophosphate, free thiamine, thiamine monophosphate (ThMP) and thiamine diphosphate (ThDP, also called TDP or TPP). Concentration in blood, concentration in brain homogenate, active cofactor in neural mitochondria and functional recovery are separate outcomes.

| Volvert et al., adult mice, Figs. 2–4 | At one hour, the acute Sv129 comparison reports whole blood ratios of 822-fold for free thiamine, 14-fold for ThMP and 4.7-fold for ThDP. C57BL/6 time-course sampling through six hours showed no significant whole-brain variation. A repeated Sv129 experiment, sampled 24 h after the last exposure after 14 days, had 8 vehicle and 10 benfotiamine animals. Approximate liver ThDP was 173→239 pmol/mg protein (+38%) and whole brain 80→76 (−6%). The reported multivariate p values were .0061 in liver and .48 in brain. The brain comparison is a counterexample to mandatory whole-brain gain after peripheral repletion and cannot exclude brief or regional effects. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1186/1471-2210-8-10) |
| Hurt et al., adult WT and acid-phosphatase-null mice, Figs. 7–8 | At one hour after gavage, the authors report a small but significant dose-dependent increase in whole-brain free thiamine, reaching 2.3-fold in the highest animal exposure group (five animals per dose per genotype for Fig. 7). Brain ThDP did not show the corresponding dose-dependent increase described for blood. The genotypes had similar measured vitamer concentrations but loss of PAP removed the spinal thermal phenotype. This dissociates exposure and one form of function. Pain behavior does not measure epilepsy, mitochondrial occupancy or WWOX rescue. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0048562) |
| Tapias et al., P301S tauopathy, Figs. 3–4 and 8 | Animals received control or benfotiamine diet from age 1 to 10 months for the biochemical cohort. Fig. 3 labels seven controls and eight active animals per genotype. **Graph versus caption:** digitization gives cortical ThDP in P301S of ~68→81 pmol/mg protein (+18%), with an explicit pairwise significance bracket. The WT cortical pair moves ~118→74 (−37%), also bracketed. The caption generalizes an increase in WT and no change in P301S. Hippocampal P301S is ~50→49, without a pairwise bracket. Blood P301S ThDP rises ~2.94-fold. The graphical contrasts remain in the same directions with ±2 px bar-top uncertainty, which is not statistical uncertainty. Cortical transketolase activity increases 7.4→9.1 mU/mg in P301S, while the Results describe the α-KGDH contrast as a nonsignificant trend. Subcellular ThDP and intact oxidative flux were not measured by these enzyme assays. In separate fibroblasts, the compound induced selected NRF2 targets and the response was absent in Nrf2-null cells (three biological replicates). That dependence is specific to the transcriptional response in those cells. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1093/hmg/ddy201) |
| de Moraes et al., adult rats with intracerebroventricular streptozotocin, Figs. 2–4 | After 30 days, hippocampal homogenate ThDP was higher in supplemented healthy and STZ groups relative to their respective controls (Fig. 2 caption n = 6–8). The entorhinal treatment main effect includes a significant within-condition pair in healthy rats. The text does not identify that same pairwise significance for STZ entorhinal cortex. Hippocampal PDH and recognition outcomes also changed, with distinct comparison denominators and an activity/locomotion change in the model. PDH was a detergent-homogenate kit assay. Ratios of endpoint to baseline means in the active arm were **161.2**, **6.98** and **3.05** for free thiamine, ThMP and ThDP. The last two differ from the prose's approximate fivefold and twofold. The corresponding placebo ratios were 2.49, 1.20 and 1.23. Visit timing relative to the last capsule was variable. At 12 months, mean ADAS-Cog changes were +1.39 and +3.26 under ITT; model p values ranged from .071 to .137 for the three reported longitudinal/rank analyses. Post-treatment blood vitamer levels did not correlate with ADAS-Cog or CDR under the reported analysis. A nonsignificant correlation provides weak evidence about mediation because the individual sampling intervals and true neural exposure are unknown. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.3233/JAD-200896) |
| Ziegler et al., BOND adult diabetic polyneuropathy, Tables 1–2 and 5 | 57 randomized and the outcome tables label 24 active and 27 placebo. The reported within-participant change differs from a subtraction of the two scheduled means. Dividing that reported change by the displayed baseline mean, 191.091/182, gives approximately 105%. **Transporter identity check.** The de Moraes Results attach the mitochondrial carrier name to *Slc19a3*, the plasma-membrane free-thiamine transporter. In the selected rat RefSeq transcripts, the printed mTPPTr forward oligo matches *Slc25a19* and neither tested *Slc19a3* transcript. The closest candidate reverse site in the matched *Slc25a19* transcripts has three mismatches, including at the primer's 3′ terminal base. The printout therefore supports the intended *Slc25a19* interpretation, with amplification identity and efficiency unverified. It cannot support an inference of restored mitochondrial transport. This is a directed reference comparison, with no search for genome-wide specificity. ## WWOX inference and counterproof

Across juvenile mouse cortex, mouse hippocampus and week-15 human WWOX organoids, TKT bulk expression changed by −0.309, −0.471 and −0.356 respectively in mean log₂(1 + TPM/CPM), with 4/3, 2/3 and 2/4 control/LoF libraries. Omitting one library retained the directions. SLC25A19 was clearly negative within those omission sensitivities in hippocampus (−0.533) and organoids (−0.182), while cortex was direction-sensitive to omission. TPK1 fell substantially in hippocampus (−0.712) and was near zero in the other two. NFE2L2 was lower in all three, but NQO1 and other downstream genes did not give a uniform readout across preparations. These are descriptive bulk differences. They justify measuring hippocampal conversion and transport and including a separate cortical response, with no estimate of cofactor reserve, enzymatic capacity, post-translational activation or response to drug. [Bulk replay](wwox_cofactor_and_redox.tsv) · [Held compartment study](../thiamine/compartments.md)

**Discriminating prediction.** In thiamine-replete WWOX and matched control neural material, separate free thiamine and benzoyl intermediates at relevant exposure, total intracellular ThDP and recovery-corrected neural mitochondrial ThDP. Compare the derivative with ordinary thiamine at matched free-thiamine input. If ThDP delivery is the cause of rescue, a drug-by-genotype increase in intact pyruvate and 2-oxoglutarate oxidative flux and post-activity recovery should follow increased matrix ThDP and native cofactor occupancy. Ordinary thiamine should reproduce that rescue if brought to the same actual matrix occupancy. If the parent-derived compound instead acts through an NRF2-linked branch, functional improvement and pathway engagement can persist at matched matrix occupancy and should lose the NRF2-dependent component with an appropriate loss control. Test cytosolic TKT separately and keep the first recruited response and physiological inhibition measurable. A larger TK signal from changes in enzyme abundance can occur without a larger fraction of enzyme occupied by ThDP. For the native-versus-supplemented mitochondrial assay, verify endogenous ThDP retention and enzyme integrity during extraction.

**Disposition and pediatric feasibility.** Retain an exposure-conditional preclinical comparator and park pediatric WWOX trial priority. Reopen for measured exposure of the exact product and its active species in age-relevant neural material, selective WWOX rescue and an interpretable adverse-effect and interaction bridge. In healthy adults, Sheng et al. measured single-exposure whole-blood peak timing of 1–2 h for free thiamine versus 8–24 h for ThDP, making a single unmatched blood draw inadequate for comparison. The assessed 2026 food-supplement application targeted adults. EFSA could establish human thiamine bioavailability and concluded that the safety of the proposed novel food under those conditions of use could not be established. That opinion concerns the assessed product and does not provide a pediatric trial safety bridge. No directly relevant pediatric central exposure, controlled WWOX efficacy or background AED interaction data were found in the examined sources. A review of the Tapias panel can refine reporting but cannot validate the required matrix-to-function bridge. [Whole-blood PK](https://doi.org/10.2147/DDDT.S296197) · [EFSA §§3.8.4–5](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2026.10301)
