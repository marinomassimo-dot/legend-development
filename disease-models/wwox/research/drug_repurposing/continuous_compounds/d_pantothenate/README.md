# D-pantothenate and mitochondrial coenzyme A availability

**Prespecified before opening the WWOX results.** Examine the D-pantothenate supplied by calcium D-pantothenate separately from pantethine and downstream phosphorylated derivatives. The WWOX datasets already implicate altered oxidative metabolism and an unstable direction for the shared vitamin transporter SLC5A6 across species. D-pantothenate is a precursor for coenzyme A (CoA). The hypothesis is that deficient free mitochondrial CoA limits oxidation of pyruvate or fatty acids in WWOX-deficient neural cells. Alternatives include limited pantothenate phosphorylation, altered mitochondrial CoA import and sequestration of CoA in acyl-CoA species. A change in the total CoA pool could conceal any of these. [Held oxidative-flux analysis](../thiamine/README.md) · [Transporter comparison](../d_biotin/screen.tsv)

**Discriminating prediction.** Compare fractional and absolute incorporation of labeled pantothenate along the CoA pathway, free versus acyl-bound mitochondrial CoA and pyruvate-derived oxidative flux at matched substrate and neural workload in WWOX-deficient and corrected cells. Monitor viability and major acylcarnitines. Increased extracellular precursor or total cellular CoA with persistent mitochondrial free-CoA depletion and impaired flux would weaken a substrate-only mechanism. Rescue of mitochondrial free CoA and oxidative function in a genotype-by-exposure comparison would raise priority. A rise in fractional labeling alone could reflect a smaller starting pool. A positive control with an identified transport or kinase defect would test assay specificity. No WWOX pantothenate deficit is established.

**Transcript test.** Before model comparisons, select shared substrate transporter Slc5a6, pantothenate kinases Pank1–3, downstream pathway enzymes Ppcs, Ppcdc, Coasy, mitochondrial transport candidate Slc25a42 and context markers Crat, Pdha1, Acly and Acss2. Lower synthesis/transport genes concordant across all three untreated datasets would support investigation of reduced capacity; conflicting signs or stable expression cannot measure cofactor supply and would shift attention to direct pool and flux measurements. Pediatric formulation, brain exposure and human data remain to be assessed.

**Initial result.** Of the 12 prespecified transcripts, PANK3, COASY and CRAT all fell in both mouse tissue comparisons and in human organoids. Each decrease survived single-library omission. Coasy went from 28.9 to 22.0 TPM in cortex and from 17.5 to 10.5 TPM in hippocampus. Organoid COASY went from 129.6 to 119.5 CPM. Controls/KO libraries are 4/3, 2/3 and 2/4, respectively. In the archived release-92 mouse transcript annotation, the old symbol Ppat represents *two* stable loci. Summing by that label would mix genes. Four transcripts at ENSMUSG00000001755 specifically represent Coasy; the code verifies their stable locus and excludes ENSMUSG00000029246 from the Coasy estimate. SLC5A6 falls in both mouse tissues but rises in organoids. A shortage of pantothenate itself remains unmeasured. Reduced synthesis capacity or reduced acetyl-group buffering by carnitine acetyltransferase could each limit the response to more substrate. **Next comparison.** Check whether the coordinated signal persists within neuronal or glial classes. Compare free and acylated CoA species and labeled pantothenate under independent genetic deficiencies in CoA synthesis versus mitochondrial import. Compare SLC25A16 and SLC25A42 as potential alternative carriers after the initial screen; SLC25A16 was not part of the prespecified 12. Do not rank precursor supplementation without direct exposure and functional data. [Independent cellular transport study identified for comparison](https://doi.org/10.1038/s42255-025-01358-y)

**Within-class sensitivity.** In pooled hippocampal nuclei Coasy and Crat fell in both excitatory and inhibitory neuronal classes. For excitatory neurons Coasy was 5.49 versus 4.25 CPM (68 and 44 counts) and Crat was 18.18 versus 15.17 CPM. In contrast Pank3 rose in both neuronal classes, from 24.97 to 27.64 CPM in excitatory neurons and from 21.19 to 26.12 in inhibitory neurons. Astrocytic Coasy rose, with only 9 versus 16 counts. Across assigned nuclei, both mitochondrial transporter transcripts rose. There is one pooled library per genotype. These data weaken a uniformly diminished neuronal pantothenate-kinase mechanism and give priority to measuring CoA synthesis capacity and free/acyl buffering within cells. They leave mitochondrial import unmeasured. Next compare the substrate challenge against primary transport and synthesis controls and against attainable neural exposure.
