# A compartment benchmark for D-pantothenate

Liu et al. provide a quantitative control for two different CoA-pathway defects in human K562 cells. In original source-data Fig. 3f, free CoA after COASY loss was 56.3% of the matched whole-cell control and acetyl-CoA was 52.8%. In the separate transporter-double-loss experiment (SLC25A16 and SLC25A42), whole-cell free CoA was 42.3% of matched control (Fig. 5c), whereas mitochondrial free CoA was only 6.4% of its matched control (Fig. 5a). Acetyl-CoA was comparatively preserved (81.8% whole-cell and 37.0% mitochondrial). Each ratio uses the mean across three reported values in each arm. The two bulk free-CoA changes have the same sign; their magnitudes alone cannot identify the limiting step. Raw mass-spectrometry peak intensities cannot be compared directly across analytes, fractions or experimental series; concentrations, absolute mitochondrial/cellular mass balance and free/acyl molar ratios do not follow from these peak signals. The synthesis arm does not measure a matched mitochondrial endpoint in Fig. 3f.

This supports testing compartment-specific substrate delivery and free versus acyl pools alongside oxidation. K562 cells derive from leukemia, the experiments tested large genetic lesions, and they did not test a WWOX genotype or rescue by D-pantothenate. Changes in pooled WWOX nuclear RNA do not establish this mechanism. Next compare pantothenate exposure-dependent rescue in neural models or patient cells with residual kinase before assigning a probability of benefit. Original data at DOI 10.1038/s42255-025-01358-y; original publisher workbooks remain held for local analysis.
