# Dantrolene · mitochondrial calcium countercheck and mouse exposure audit

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN · native striatal-neuron calcium in nM, whole mouse brain parent drug in ng/g and plasma in ng/mL

## Effect after comparable bulk cytosolic stimulation

In saponin-permeabilized cultured striatal neurons, Jakob et al. evoked ER release through IP₃ receptors and used **10 µM nominal dantrolene** before the second challenge. In Fig. 1A Results the authors give **490 ± 51.2 versus 550 ± 47.3 nM** cytosolic calcium peaks with and without dantrolene, respectively, and report no detected difference. In Fig. 1B they report a reduced mitochondrial response despite broadly similar bulk cytosolic peaks. In the control the text places mitochondrial calcium at **110 ± 0.6 → 700 ± 59.6 nM**. After washout they report partial return (**90 ± 7.8 → 250 ± 19.6 nM**). The figure caption calls these representative time courses. The text gives no separate numerical mitochondrial peak under dantrolene from which to derive an inhibition percentage. Similar bulk peaks leave local ER–mitochondria microdomains, membrane potential and other effects on uptake unresolved. ATP recovery and a free-concentration series were outside this comparison. [Jakob et al., §Results and Fig. 1A–B caption](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4122666/)

The observation strengthens the adverse-direction prediction for WWOX. A decrease in mitochondrial calcium can occur with no obvious large change in the bulk cytosolic stimulus. In intact stage-matched WWOX neural cells, test the early and late ER signal, matched evoked cytosolic input, mitochondrial calcium and ATP recovery together. An apparent reduction in excess late release should preserve physiological coupling and recruitment. The pharmacological observation in the striatal preparation cannot identify which RyR isoform controls an effect in WWOX.

## Cross-check of the mouse concentration and AUC workbooks

Wang et al. used terminal sampling cohorts of mice. The source curves in Fig. 1A and 1C report plasma **ng/mL** and whole-brain **ng/g**. At 20 minutes the intranasal brain mean was **369.38 ng/g** (8 mice). The oral brain mean peaked at **278.20 ng/g** at 50 minutes (5 mice), where the intranasal mean was **112.13 ng/g** (4 mice). The highest sampled means correspond to **1.18** and **0.89 nmol/g** total brain parent, respectively, using 314.25 g/mol. The data do not measure unbound concentrations. For order-of-magnitude context only, assuming tissue density of 1 g/mL gives total-tissue concentration equivalents of roughly **1.18** and **0.89 µM**, below the **10 µM** nominal striatal assay point. The assay point gives neither a potency estimate nor a threshold. Independent lower-exposure effects remain possible. [Wang et al., Methods and Fig. 1A/C/D](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229156) · [Original Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229156.g001)

**Five-of-five source mismatch.** The archived Fig. 1D `A3:A7` cells labelled nasal brain AUC are **67,720; 57,565; 45,955; 40,905; 54,605**. These are exactly the five 10–120-minute trapezoidal integrals of columns `K:O` in the **oral plasma** time-course workbook Fig. 1A. The five oral brain entries in Fig. 1D `B3:B7` exactly match columns `K:O` of the oral **brain** workbook Fig. 1C over the same window. These numerical identities flag a likely source mix-up in the nasal brain AUC arm. The approximately **4.08-fold** ratio implied by the two Fig. 1D workbook means therefore cannot support the magnitude of a brain exposure advantage. [Published source archive](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229156.s001) · [Row-by-row replay](wang_pk_check.tsv)

These are descriptive integrations of cross-sectional group means. Spreadsheet columns spanning terminal time points do not give longitudinal individual animals, so no individual-animal AUC test is reproduced. Intranasal brain has later time points and integrates to **27,858.38 ng·min/g** between 10 and 180 minutes. The oral brain values were recorded as zero at 120 minutes, with no subsequent oral observations in this workbook. The common-window data support different persistence and greater absolute exposure in the measured mouse tissues, with a systemic exposure difference of the same order. They do not establish preferential brain delivery, free neuronal exposure or pediatric chronic feasibility. The paper also acknowledges possible residual vascular signal and formulation-specific effects. [Wang et al., Fig. 2 and limitations](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229156)

The caption labels both groups **5 mg/kg**. The drug-administration section gives a **5 mg/mL** preparation and **5 µL/g** for the oral volume, which would be **25 mg/kg** if the same concentration was used. The text examined gives no separate oral concentration. Actual oral dosing remains ambiguous, so the route ratios here describe the recorded groups without dose normalization. Dryad lists the corresponding 2020 workbooks, but file downloads returned HTTP 403 during this audit. The checked source is the publisher archive. [Wang et al., Drug administration and Fig. 1 caption](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229156) · [Dryad dataset](https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.jdfn2z37k)

**Next decision point.** Dantrolene stays active for the exposure-bound comparison. Treat Fig. 1D AUC magnitude as unreliable until independently reconciled. Check a lower, plausibly attainable free exposure against native-neural effects and an orthogonal control on mitochondrial uptake. The decisive outcome is reduced pathological late release with preserved mitochondrial calcium and ATP recovery in intact WWOX neural material. Current evidence supports mechanistic work and leaves trial priority low.

The next comparator is the WFS1 program, where a primary study reported dantrolene protection of patient-derived neural progenitors and a six-month open-label clinical trial reported no improvement in visual acuity or neurological function. A paired reading can separate cellular rescue, pediatric tolerability and measurable CNS target engagement. A second input is the transporter-controlled rat brain study, which can help distinguish total from unbound exposure and clarify why CSF may diverge. The abstracts leave the exposure denominators open.
