## Exposure and pediatric feasibility

Foster Table 3 reports rat EAE trough total active phosphate of **0.23 ± 0.17 ng/mL in CSF** (about 0.59 ± 0.44 nM, n=6), 7.35 ± 0.69 ng/mL in plasma (about 19.0 ± 1.8 nM, n=17), and 398 ± 186 ng/g in brain (n=6). Plasma, CSF and extracted brain remain distinct compartments. Neither the high tissue stock nor the CSF value directly calibrates the unbound extracellular concentration near receptors in WWOX. The newborn study did not link targeting to a measured active exposure. The clinically used oral formulation is parent fingolimod hydrochloride. The US label reports a median systemic phosphate concentration of 1.10 ng/mL in MS at ages 10–17 (about 2.84 nM total) and >99.7% protein binding for both species. A carbamazepine interaction lowered observed blood AUC for both by about 40%, with clinical impact uncertain. The label also flags potential reductions with phenytoin or phenobarbital and clinically relevant cardiac, hepatic, ocular and immune risks. Reported terminal half-lives near 6–9 days matter for attribution. [Foster original Table 3](https://ptacts.uspto.gov/ptacts/public-informations/petitions/1482195/download-documents?artifactId=3WbQ4YFDzTEz-CYP8eTsGEctH5Ha_Re5s_m4vJqWr1dTyN47kwQRfAA) · [Active enantiomer molecular mass](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/11452022) · [FDA label §§5, 7, 8.4, 11–12.3](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2025/022527s044lbl.pdf)

The direct WWOX search did not identify a treated native WWOX preparation or an attributable pediatric WWOX clinical comparison. The controlled adolescent MS study has safety sets of 107 per arm and standardized convulsion reports of 6/107 versus 1/107 on interferon. The endpoint and older population leave WWOX safety unresolved. The label reports persistent neurobehavioral and immune findings in juvenile rats and gives no pediatric efficacy or safety basis below ten years. A developmental readout in WWOX would require functional confirmation alongside age-appropriate clinical measurability. Symptomatic shifts or changes in MBP alone would be difficult to attribute. ## Inference, hypothesis and discriminating observation

**Inference.** The strongest positive developmental result needs transplanted cells, and the injury studies support preservation, reduced damage or altered debris as context-dependent alternatives to first-sheath formation. WWOX-specific bulk and pool data motivate an activation hypothesis but do not establish reduced glial SPHK2 capacity. Endogenous sphingolipid homeostasis and fingolimod conversion may vary independently. Direct tissue evidence supports widespread drug delivery in other models rather than WWOX local pharmacology. [WWOX panel and assay context](central_activation_checkpoint_20261127.md) · [Mouse SphK2 deletion primary, abstract-level](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34399014/)

Kihara Fig. 5F–G gives a mechanistic control in 8–12-week-old female mice with EAE. Untreated genotype curves were similar (12 double-knockout and 14 WT). In the treated comparison (12 and 13), the WT course improved more than the astrocytic Sphk1/2 double knockout. The panels use different risk sets and do not report a formal genotype-by-drug interaction. The measured lower spinal analyte was **endogenous S1P**, with no local active drug or active-metabolite add-back measurement. This supports measuring an upstream astrocyte component alongside receptor response, without attributing juvenile WWOX effects to glial SPHK loss. In another primary study, human SPNS2 exported phosphorylated drug in a heterologous system. [Kihara Fig. 5](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncel.2022.918041) · [Hisano primary, abstract-level scope](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21084291/)

The added SPNS2 replay gives log₂ differences of **+0.286** in P17 mouse neocortex, **−0.796** in P17 mouse hippocampus and **−0.118** in week-15 human organoids. In the pooled assigned hippocampal nuclei, SPNS2 goes from 28.1 to 33.1 CPM in astrocytes (41 versus 57 UMIs), but from 174.5 to 89.4 in myelin-forming oligodendrocytes (125 versus 20). One pool per genotype, class proportions and different measurement units preclude a cell-specific functional conclusion. The signs weaken a broad astrocyte export deficit and make it important to distinguish drug generation from extracellular delivery in the relevant cell compartment. [Bulk replay](wwox_s1p_context.tsv) · [Nuclei replay](hippocampal_nuclei_s1p.tsv)

**Hypothesis.** A WWOX background with reduced conversion could generate less active phosphate from parent or deliver less of it to extracellular receptors, while retaining a recruitable receptor response. Equal exposure to active phosphate could bypass that limit. An independent shortage of competent axon–glia interactions or a deficit downstream of receptor engagement could remain after matching exposure. Preservation of existing axons and formation of new sheaths need separate measurements because untreated WWOX pathology can affect both.

**One comparison.** In isogenic or age-matched WWOX and control preparations forming their initial sheaths, estimate the genotype-specific incremental response to parent and to directly supplied active phosphate, with paired vehicle and an injury-only comparator. Keep initial recruitment and viability matched. Quantify parent, appearance and clearance of intracellular active phosphate and unbound extracellular phosphate over time, then measure early receptor function and later surface availability before measuring new compact axon-linked internodes and evoked conduction in the same preparation. Slower formation of intracellular phosphate at comparable parent exposure and similar phosphate clearance, followed by functional rescue with matched active phosphate, favors an activation constraint. Preserved intracellular accumulation with low extracellular phosphate and rescue after add-back favors a release or retention constraint. Equal phosphate with diminished receptor response points downstream of conversion. Receptor engagement or better axonal health with no gain in internodes and conduction supports a preservation or cell-maturation action. Use a positive control to verify that a structural and functional gain remains detectable in the juvenile preparation.

**Disposition.** Retain fingolimod as a conditional S1P and injury-response research comparator. Defer WWOX pediatric trial prioritization. Reopen the first-sheath branch for replicated, WWOX-selective structural and conduction correction at measured attainable local phosphate with preserved physiological recruitment, or a validated juvenile WWOX measurement that demonstrates a pharmacologically reversible activation defect and ties it to that functional outcome. A different analysis of Fig. 3C could change the size of the descriptive contrast, but would leave the independent exposure and conduction gaps. An outside review is most informative after raw litter-level data or replicated native-stage results become available.
