# Can oral D-ribose restore neural function in WWOX loss?

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Disposition.** Defer oral D-ribose for a pediatric WWOX trial. Direct entry into rat brain is possible under defined experimental conditions. In primary granule-cell cultures, high-concentration pretreatment reduced death during oxygen/glucose deprivation. These findings make a blanket claim of poor penetration or lack of biological activity untenable. Neither provides the paired evidence needed for a trial: neural parent exposure after oral intake and a functional advantage over an energy-matched control in WWOX. An independent source check on oral parent exposure has been requested.

## Route, age and the identity of the measured sugar

* Gaitonde and Arnfred detected labeled ribose in rat brain after intracarotid or intravenous labeled ribose with hepatic and renal blood flow interrupted. They reported no significant labeled glucose in brain or heart in this condition. In the separate subcutaneous experiment, 40–70% of brain radioactivity was in amino acids and 2–6% in the free-sugar fraction at 30 min. This is strong qualitative evidence for direct utilization under the first condition. The second experiment cannot distinguish intact parent from glucose using total tissue radioactivity. Assessed from the publisher-deposited abstract. [Original rat study](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1471-4159.1971.tb09603.x)
* In young rats studied after subcutaneous injection, 46–57% of acid-soluble brain label in the first week was in glucose, fructose and sedoheptulose combined. Between ages 10 and 15 days the free-sugar share decreased from 24% to 3% and the amino-acid share increased from 11% to 44%. The denominator is acid-soluble radioactivity. These percentages supply neither parent concentrations nor a pediatric dosing relationship. In the rabbit carotid indicator study, D-ribose extraction was slower than D-glucose. Even the intravascular marker mannitol showed first-pass loss, illustrating possible endothelial retention. Both primary abstracts were checked. [Developing rat](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1471-4159.1972.tb01347.x) · [Rabbit capillary comparison](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1748-1716.1967.tb03612.x)
* In one adult after intravenous position-labeled D-ribose, 96.8% of the label **within isolated blood glucose** was on carbons 1 and 3 at 15 min and at 2 h. This is a distribution within recovered glucose, not a percentage of the administered ribose dose. A separate human metabolism study compared urinary radioactivity with an orcinol-reactive sugar measurement. The measurements need not identify the same molecules. [Hiatt, Table I](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI103649) · [Segal and Foley](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI103658)
* In the six-month oral mouse study, high-dose ribose produced hippocampal and behavioral changes. The examined methods measured blood glucose and insulin; the reported brain outcomes included tau phosphorylation and amyloid-like products. These endpoints do not quantify timed parent ribose in neural interstitial fluid. Chronic gavage in healthy male mice and acute ischemic cultures answer different questions. [Oral study, Methods and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4741441/)

## Human oral exposure and the glucose comparator

Gross et al. studied continuous administration over at least five hours in eight healthy volunteers and five people with myoadenylate deaminase deficiency. The primary abstract reports a mean steady-state serum ribose of 4.8 mg/dL for one oral rate, equivalent to **0.320 mM**. Its 81.7 mg/dL (5.44 mM) example was intravenous at a different rate. Neither value is an oral bolus peak. The 10 mM concentration associated with cell protection below is about 31 times the one oral serum example. This comparison illustrates scale without estimating a brain/plasma ratio or an upper exposure limit. [Gross et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2514319/)

Twelve healthy adults participated in three fasting oral solution periods and a fourth fed period with six per meal type. Mean time to peak was 18–30 min. The high-fat and high-carbohydrate meals left 57.4% and 30.9% of the fasting peak, respectively, and 59.2% and 35.1% of fasting AUC. Serum glucose fell by up to 26.3 mg/dL in the first hour across the doses studied. A fed/fasting comparison can therefore change both parent exposure and the competing glucose supply. Chronic ketogenic feeding, pediatric developmental stages and background antiseizure medication require their own measurements. The inherited ADSL case reports include discordant clinical outcomes; they do not supply a WWOX glucose or exposure threshold. [Oral PK, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24272966/) · [ADSL clinical comparisons](README.md)

## A single-agent positive result depends on timing and endpoint

Following three hours of pretreatment, 10 mM D-ribose reduced cell death after six hours of oxygen/glucose deprivation from 58 ± 5% (N=7) to 37 ± 2% (N=6) in primary cerebellar granule-cell cultures. The difference is −21 percentage points. At the six-hour biochemical endpoint, the author found no improvement in total adenine nucleotides, energy charge or ATP/AMP. The exploratory three-hour nucleotide measurement had N=1. In a separate post-insult experiment, 1–5 mM ribose alone did not establish protection during reperfusion. Pretreatment and post-treatment differ in timing, insult history, follow-up and, for those comparisons, concentration. A direct timing-by-treatment effect has not been estimated. The survival result supports a preconditioning hypothesis even when net nucleotide preservation is unproven. Activity-evoked recovery at physiological glucose remains a different endpoint. [Zur Nedden thesis, chapter 8, Figs. 8.4–8.7](https://doi.org/10.82444/warw.33664381)

In hippocampal slices, ribose **plus adenine** lengthened the two inter-burst intervals versus control by 31.3 and 29.7 seconds. Burst duration did not differ significantly among the three treatments for bursts 1–3. The first-burst inter-spike comparison reached significance against creatine only. Denominators in Fig. 5 dropped from 24–32 slices per group at the first burst to 15–22 by the third; the authors say slice counts usually matched animal counts. Later intervals necessarily condition on observing another burst. There was no ribose-alone seizure arm. This circuit experiment cannot assign seizure suppression to D-ribose. [Hall and Frenguelli, statistics and Fig. 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6220757/)

## WWOX and the observation that would reopen the question

The untreated three-model panel adds ribokinase (RBKS), the entry step for free ribose. KO minus control mean log2(1 + normalized RNA) was +0.28 in human organoids (2 control/4 KO libraries), +0.39 in mouse cortex (4/3), and +0.025 in mouse hippocampus (2/3). The last omission range includes zero. A separate pooled juvenile hippocampal nuclei dataset showed 22.5 → 13.2 CPM overall, 18.5 → 10.4 in excitatory-marker nuclei and 21.8 → 11.7 in inhibitory-marker nuclei. This single-pool-per-genotype comparison does not provide biological replication. RBKS RNA is low in the bulk data. ADSL RNA was lower across bulk models in the previous screen. These patterns justify measuring intracellular ribose phosphorylation and conversion of adenylosuccinate to AMP independently. They do not prove that either enzyme limits flux. [Bulk results and library omissions](pentose_bulk.tsv) · [Nuclear counts](cell_types.tsv) · [Composition sensitivity](mixture.tsv) · [Earlier ADSL result](../inosine/salvage.md)

**Discriminating prediction.** In WWOX-deficient and corrected neural preparations, compare the same measured parent concentrations before activity and during recovery. Match osmolarity, energy input, glucose and stimulus burden, and include D-ribose alone with a separate fixed-adenine comparison. At a credible neural exposure, a D-ribose-specific salvage response should increase PRPP, absolute AMP/ADP/ATP and recovery of evoked transmission above the matched glucose condition. Labeled IMP or adenylosuccinate with little net AMP would instead identify a downstream constraint worth testing. A viability advantage confined to pre-exposure and severe starvation would support preconditioning. For systemic tracing, measure circulating labeled glucose as well as intact ribose and compare glucose-derived brain labeling. Prioritize a trial only if intact oral exposure, net functional rescue and the glycemic margin all hold in a relevant developmental setting.

**Independent laboratory review, 24 November.** The reviewer reread the human intravenous tracer Results (Hiatt, pp. 652–653). The authors estimated that at least 16% of administered label had entered circulating glucose by 15 minutes in one subject. This uses a different denominator from the fraction of isolated glucose label at C1 and C3. The reviewer did not independently verify the table or the rat publisher abstracts. No reviewed evidence established chemically intact oral parent in a neural compartment. This strengthens the case for measuring labeled circulating glucose alongside intact ribose and preserves the parked disposition. [Hiatt, original report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/13539206/)
