# D-ribose and precursor limitation in WWOX loss

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Selection.** D-ribose tests a specific finding from the completed inosine comparison. Purine base delivery can use up phosphoribosyl pyrophosphate (PRPP) without increasing ATP. Free D-ribose can enter pentose metabolism after phosphorylation by ribokinase. In WWOX, lower ADSL expression across three bulk datasets makes a downstream conversion limit an explicit alternative to a shortage of pentose. [WWOX comparison and data](../inosine/salvage.md)

**First comparison.** Checked the original plots in Fig. 7A–C of the human iPSC-neuron study. In post-stress cultures receiving hypoxanthine, significant comparisons for total adenylate are marked for **L-ribose** and several other sugars. The D-ribose bar lacks a significant bracket versus hypoxanthine alone. The paper does not report a direct statistical comparison of the two enantiomers. A whole-class statement about pentoses would give unwarranted support to the supplement. [Primary neuronal study, Fig. 7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11342100/)

**Independent neural comparison.** In freshly cut rat hippocampal slices, total adenine nucleotides after three hours were 25.8 ± 0.7 with 1 mM D-ribose plus 50 µM adenine versus 19.1 ± 1.2 nmol/mg protein in standard medium (reported n = 3–5). The difference from the rounded means is 6.7 nmol/mg, or 35%. The authors also report that D-ribose alone at 2.5 or 10 mM and adenine alone at 50 µM produced no significant increase. Single-agent values are referenced to supplemental Table 4 and have not yet been extracted. The 35% contrast applies to the combination; statistical evidence for a formal interaction at matched concentrations requires the individual groups. [Original Results adjoining Fig. 4B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6632980/)

**Matched substrate concentrations.** The same investigator’s thesis gives the single-agent comparison in Table 5.1 (printed p. 109), with a sucrose control. At 2.5 mM D-ribose alone, mean total adenine nucleotides were 17.1 nmol/mg, versus 17.8 in control and 24.7 with the same ribose plus 50 µM adenine (reported N = 3, 4 and 3, respectively). At 10 mM D-ribose alone, IMP increased to 0.25 versus 0.14 nmol/mg in control, while total adenine nucleotides were 17.9 versus 17.8 (N = 5 and 4). From the rounded means this is approximately +79% upstream IMP and +0.6% total adenine nucleotides. The significance annotation applies to IMP at 10 mM. The table does not resolve flux, exposure in vivo, or the identity of a limiting enzyme. This is overlapping work from the same laboratory as the journal article. [Zur Nedden thesis, Table 5.1](https://doi.org/10.82444/warw.33664381)

**Pediatric countercheck.** The original abstract of one ADSL-deficient case reports fewer seizures during D-ribose exposure. Substitution with an equivalent amount of glucose did not lead to increased seizure frequency. In a separate four-person series, twelve months of D-ribose failed to reduce seizures or improve the clinical outcome. Another series describes deterioration after withdrawal in one treated child; timing cannot establish causation. The patients had inherited ADSL deficiency. These observations cannot establish WWOX efficacy or safety and have been checked at abstract depth only. [Single case](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9989253/) · [Four-person series](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18649008/) · [Additional clinical report](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21903433/)

**Discriminating prediction.** At matched energy input, glucose and hypoxanthine availability, D-ribose should raise PRPP and absolute adenylates and improve recovery of evoked transmission preferentially in WWOX-deficient cultures if insufficient pentose supply limits recovery. Compare labeled D-ribose, vehicle and an energy-matched glucose condition, separating ribose incorporation from net pool growth. If PRPP rises but the label accumulates in adenylosuccinate without AMP recovery, the downstream step would gain priority. If glucose works as well, a D-ribose-specific effect loses priority. Compare D-ribose alone with a fixed base background to distinguish sufficiency from co-substrate dependence.
