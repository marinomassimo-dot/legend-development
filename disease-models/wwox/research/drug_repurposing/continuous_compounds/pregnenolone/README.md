# Pregnenolone | Neurosteroid conversion and opposing circuit effects
context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Opening checkpoint, 27 November 2026.** Pregnenolone follows the completed omaveloxolone analysis. It tests a precursor-supply hypothesis that is distinct from direct GABA-A modulation by ganaxolone. In adult studies, administered pregnenolone has produced measurable serum changes in multiple downstream neurosteroids. The compounds are separate analytes and peripheral measurements leave local production and exposure unknown. Published pharmacology of the sulfate includes inhibition of GABA-A and NMDA effects that depend on subunit and assay history. Some measurements of endogenous adult rodent brain sulfate report levels below analytical detection. The first question is therefore chemical identity and availability at a developmental stage, before inferring a circuit effect. **Fixed first comparison.** Locate public tissue- and age-resolved data that distinguish unconjugated pregnenolone, progesterone, 5α-dihydroprogesterone, allopregnanolone and intact pregnenolone sulfate. Record the analytical recovery, limits and steroid isomer assignment. Screen the held untreated contrasts for compartment and stage-dependent availability of the relevant conversion enzymes only if the chemical data support a test. Keep C21 precursor routing separate from the WWOX-linked C19 androsterone-to-androstanediol question, which has no demonstrated brain flux result in the existing dossier. Compare serum conversion with neural exposure and report formulation and age boundaries.

**Discriminating prediction.** In age-matched WWOX and control preparations at measured free parent, follow precursor-derived products in time and compare physiological inhibitory recruitment at native chloride with separately recorded NMDA responses. A selective precursor limitation predicts baseline loss of a defined C21 product and labeled allopregnanolone recovery before improved native inhibition. A sulfate-heavy exposure or a delayed increase in NMDA drive without selective C21 product recovery would weaken that interpretation. Test whether measured product mixtures reproduce the response and whether a direct GABA-A modulator does so independently of conversion. Preserve the activity window and distinguish absent parent entry from failure of a downstream enzyme.

**Next step.** Verify which solvent extraction, isotope control and blood contamination checks support the competing tissue measurements. Establish which exposure and analyte differences could change a WWOX experiment. Adult psychiatric results are peripheral pharmacology references and supply no pediatric WWOX efficacy estimate.

**Latest checkpoint, 27 November 2026.** The age-matched hippocampal transcript replay weakens the inference that sulfate production increases. The published stereoselective assay needs a lower quantification limit for the juvenile comparison.
