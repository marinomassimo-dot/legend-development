# Docosahexaenoic acid in WWOX loss

**Molecule and question.** The existing S11 screen nominates docosahexaenoic acid (DHA) through several potassium channels. Its neuron-specific attribution is unresolved. The myelin strand instead predicts a slower effect of DHA incorporation into membrane phospholipids. Select the nonesterified fatty acid as the index molecule. Keep lysophosphatidylcholine-DHA (LPC-DHA), triglyceride DHA and mixed marine oils as separate exposures. **Prespecified before opening the data.** Compare MFSD2A with endothelial markers CLDN5, PECAM1 and SLC2A1 across untreated cortex, hippocampus and human organoids. Evaluate ACSL6 and LPCAT3 as neural lipid-handling candidates, then KCNK2, KCNK10 and KCNK4 from S11 with matched gene reporting. A persistent transport signal in vascularized tissue requires a within-endothelial comparison, particularly if endothelial marker abundance moves in parallel. Treat a low-baseline organoid as a weak vascular comparator. Inspect all single-library omissions. These data only test expression and composition.

**Discriminating prediction.** At matched free DHA exposure, an acute potassium-current response should precede substantial labeling of membrane phospholipids, reverse on washout and depend on the implicated channel. A membrane supply mechanism predicts incorporation into a defined lipid species before durable structural or conduction improvement. Compare routes at matched circulating nonesterified and LPC-DHA exposure and normalize lipid incorporation to precursor enrichment. Increased total tissue DHA alone does not resolve either mechanism. Priority depends on WWOX-specific rescue with preserved inhibitory recruitment or improved conduction at a defined developmental stage.

**Untreated WWOX comparison.** Across nine genes and three bulk datasets, MFSD2A decreases in mouse hippocampus (46.8 to 34.0 TPM) and human organoids (87.3 to 66.0 CPM), but increases in mouse cortex (33.3 to 39.1 TPM). Each direction survives every single-library omission. Cortical PECAM1 and SLC2A1 decline, and the CLDN5 sign is unstable. The existing hippocampal nuclear classification lacks an endothelial group, precluding a within-endothelial comparison. None of these measures establishes reduced DHA transport. ACSL6 also changes in opposite directions between mouse bulk tissue and human organoids. [All 27 contrasts](bulk.tsv) · [Bulk analysis](dha.py) · [Input fingerprints](../creatine/inputs.tsv)

**Channel contrast.** All three prespecified potassium-channel transcripts decline in both mouse tissue datasets. Of the three, only Kcnk10 also declines within both pooled excitatory (86.5 to 70.4 counts per million) and inhibitory neurons (37.5 to 30.9). Kcnk2 rises in both. Kcnk4 has only one control and three knockout counts in the inhibitory group. Human organoid KCNK10 increases, from 12.4 to 26.5 CPM. These pooled nuclei give one observation per genotype. An acute conductance response should be tested separately in each neuronal class at matched starting voltage. A common reduction in firing could impair inhibitory recruitment. **Formulation and exposure.** In adult rats, Chen et al., Fig. 3, found greater raw brain tracer radioactivity after five minutes with LPC-DHA. After accounting for the area under each plasma tracer curve, the coefficient favored nonesterified DHA by 3.00-fold (0.18/0.06 µL/s/g). Including endogenous precursor pool size, estimated brain incorporation favored nonesterified DHA by 9.79-fold (46/4.7 nmol/g/day). Each infusion arm had four animals. Raw retention, uptake per exposure and physiological contribution answer different questions. This acute adult measurement leaves long-term dietary accretion, developmental transfer and unbound DHA at the channel unresolved. The term nonesterified includes protein-bound fatty acid. [Chen et al., Fig. 3b–g](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4625162/)

**Checkpoint.** DHA remains active. Compare equal-molar oral free DHA and LPC-DHA in a primary developmental or chronic model, with the diet, time window, enrichment versus absolute brain DHA and peripheral exposure documented. Then check whether an attainable unbound concentration affects a native potassium current before membrane incorporation. Pediatric feasibility depends on exact formulation and developmental safety. Adult tracer uptake cannot set a pediatric exposure target.

Source locator for the uptake comparison: Chen et al., Results preceding Fig. 3, “After correction for plasma exposure”.
