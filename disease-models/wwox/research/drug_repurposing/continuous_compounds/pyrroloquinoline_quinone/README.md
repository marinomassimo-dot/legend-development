# Pyrroloquinoline quinone and mitochondrial reserve

28 November 2026 UTC. Continuous compound #203, INV-07. context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. [Prespecified comparison](pretest.md) · [Opening comparison](first_comparison.md) · [Numerical replay](comparison.tsv).

**Disposition.** Keep pyrroloquinoline quinone disodium (PQQ, track the parent quinone and its reduced form) as a conditional preclinical comparator of respiratory reserve and NMDA redox modulation. Defer pediatric WWOX research priority. The best neural function result is an improvement in respiratory reserve in adult amyloid-model rat hippocampal synaptosomes. The study leaves ATP-linked rescue, washout and pathway dependence unresolved. The best parent-specific brain exposure lead is in a separate mouse neuroinflammation model after injection. It gives no usable neural exposure window for juvenile WWOX by the oral route.

## Discriminating prediction

**Data in WWOX.** The held human WWOX-deficient cerebral organoid data report oxidative-phosphorylation RNA changes. That does not identify a specific respiratory bottleneck. Juvenile Wwox-deletion neocortex has NMDA-dependent bursts in the held slice study. Thus a drug-induced fall in energy demand through NMDA modulation is plausible even if mitochondrial capacity does not improve. The hippocampal transgenic rat result and the juvenile WWOX cortical result concern different preparations and pathologies. [Organoid primary record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8350905/) · [Juvenile slice primary record](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609180/) · [Held WWOX energy audit](../methylene_blue/coupling_and_disposition_20261127.md)

**Inference.** The APP reserve result justifies measuring demand-dependent reserve in neural material. It does not establish the mechanism proposed from liver cells. A receptor effect could reduce NMDA charge, calcium load and ATP consumption. An apparent improvement in net ATP recovery or a reduction in recurrent bursts could follow reduced work. The rinse result makes this a real counterexample to using persistence alone as a mechanism test. A rise in total OCR can also coincide with flat or falling ATP-linked OCR. The replay gives one algebraic example with identical measured basal and uncoupled OCR.

**Single comparison.** First establish a reproducible native Wwox-LoF deficit in recovery after defined work in a juvenile cortical preparation. Compare PQQ and vehicle with a control that matches the NMDA current change, using littermates or isogenic rescue and measured extracellular and tissue parent and metabolites. Record early NMDA charge and calcium load, then compare ATP-linked respiration and ATP recovery after matched evoked work, with activity, viable-cell count and mitochondrial mass tracked. Include the immediate and later windows using analytical washout in the later window. Use a respiratory assay that partitions oligomycin-sensitive OCR, a separate ATP measure and a cell-free reporter blank. Test pathway dependence with a PGC-1α perturbation only once the functional effect and exposure are reproducible. Run any reductant check in a separate current assay with reductant-alone and vehicle controls because the reductant itself changes NMDA and quinone chemistry.

- **Favors mitochondrial adaptation.** At an attainable measured local exposure, ATP-linked respiration and evoked ATP recovery improve together after parent clearance at matched spiking and NMDA charge or calcium load. Viable-cell and mass normalizations support the result. The delayed difference depends on the nominated pathway without simply reducing the stimulus or baseline viability.
- **Favors reduced demand or redox chemistry.** Acute NMDA changes explain the ATP signal or network effect, matched receptor modulation reproduces it, or the remaining signal follows probe chemistry, loss of cells or increased uncoupled OCR. A short post-rinse effect alone is insufficient. A spare-capacity change with no ATP-linked recovery leaves the adaptation claim unverified.

## Feasibility and threshold to reopen

The cited oral human evidence is from adults. The EU novel-food entry for PQQ disodium lists supplementary use in adults and excludes pregnancy and lactation. This does not establish pediatric tolerability, interactions with background antiseizure medicines, exposure in the juvenile CNS or a WWOX outcome. No clinically actionable comparison follows from the evidence here. [EU consolidated implementing list, PQQ disodium entry](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A02017R2470-20260810)

Reopen when both a measured, chemically resolved exposure range is available in a relevant age and compartment and a WWOX preparation has a reproducible coupling or post-work deficit. The highest-value external check would be access to the Mostafa PK tables with timing, perfusion, quantitative range and control data, or a publicly reusable oral brain parent dataset. A review of the present numerical contrasts without those missing observations would leave the disposition unchanged.

The replay uses a few reported numerical inputs from public primary sources and does not reproduce figures or article text. No new biological estimates are fitted.
