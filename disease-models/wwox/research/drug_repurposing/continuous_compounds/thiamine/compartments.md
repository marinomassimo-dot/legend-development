# A mitochondrial cofactor deficit can be invisible in total-cell measurements

**Evidence in WWOX.** The neuronal datasets do not measure free thiamine or mitochondrial thiamine diphosphate (ThDP). The Wwox-null embryonic-fibroblast study documents less oxygen consumption, more extracellular lactate and altered glucose metabolism, providing a rationale for testing pyruvate handling. These effects have multiple explanations, including HIF-linked regulation. PDHA1 transcript increased in human organoids and declined in mouse hippocampus, whereas PDK1 RNA declined in both. The RNA provides no kinase-activity or PDH-phosphorylation measurement. No direct mitochondrial ThDP or cofactor-response measurement was identified in the WWOX sources examined. [Original WWOX fibroblast study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4211377/) · [All RNA contrasts](screen.tsv)

**A quantitative counterexample.** In two patients' SLC25A19-mutant lymphoblast cultures, Table 1 of Lindhurst et al. reports mitochondrial ThDP of 0.10 versus 1.03 in controls and postmitochondrial supernatant ThDP of 4.63 versus 3.23 ng/mg total cellular protein. Both fractions share a denominator. The sum of the two recovered fractions is 4.73 versus 4.26, **11% higher despite 90% lower mitochondrial ThDP**. The fraction in mitochondria among the measured pools falls from 24.2% to 2.1%. This sum is reconstructed from reported means and does not correct for incomplete recovery or contamination. The units are per cell protein rather than per mitochondrion. In the mouse knockout, mitochondrial ThDP was below detection while postmitochondrial ThDP rose. The authors recovered enzyme activity by adding ThDP to an isolated mitochondrial assay. That response bypasses cellular delivery of ordinary thiamine. This is a comparator with a primary transporter lesion, not an estimate of the effect of WWOX loss. [Lindhurst et al., Table 1 and Fig. 7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1595310/)

**Counterexample in a different lesion.** Ortigoza-Escobar et al. studied children with SLC19A3-related disease. Five untreated children had CSF free thiamine of 0.6–9.3 nM, and a different child already on treatment had 179 nM. These are cross-sectional measurements. One untreated patient had normal blood vitamers but a CSF/blood free-thiamine ratio of 0.22 compared with 0.9–3.6 in nine paired controls. The measurements demonstrate compartment-specific exposure. Mildly low CSF free thiamine also occurred in unrelated epileptic encephalopathies. This cohort cannot establish a WWOX deficiency, pediatric WWOX tolerability or interactions with concurrent antiseizure medication. [Primary study, Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awv342)

**Independent calculation on the source's supplemental values.** In the author's archived table, the patient-1 fibroblasts and parallel control experienced a change from low-thiamine to replete medium (2.8 to 304.3 nM, about 109-fold). Oxidation of carbon-1-labeled pyruvate changed from 18.5 to 19.0 in the patient and 11.6 to 15.9 in the control (nmol/h/mg). The change attributable to the comparison is +0.5 minus +4.3 = −3.8. For carbon-2-labeled pyruvate the contrast was (4.5−4.7)−(4.2−2.0) = −2.4. One patient and one parallel control supply these substrate measurements. There is no confidence interval, both low-medium patient values fall within the quoted control ranges, and the same paper's biomarker result across several cell lines does not supply independent replication of the flux measurement. Measurements at two label positions probe different carbon fates. Neither supports selective improvement of pyruvate oxidation in this patient culture under these conditions. [Author-deposited table, “Substrate oxidation rates in fibroblasts”](https://diposit.ub.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/6faa60ed-abdf-4934-99b9-927e2102abe0/content)

**The organoid medium already contains thiamine.** For the GSE156243 week-15 cerebral organoids, Steinberg et al. specify a 1:1 mixture of DMEM/F12 and Neurobasal, with two possible sources for Neurobasal. If the Gibco 21103049 option was used, its 4.0 mg/L thiamine hydrochloride equals 11.87 µM, so that component alone contributes nominally about 5.93 µM before the small dilution from additions, excluding the other base and supplements. This is about 19.5 times the 0.3043 µM replete fibroblast medium. The actual bottle, measured concentration after incubation and intracellular pools are unknown. Repudi et al.'s oligocortical protocol has separate media and is not the source of the RNA comparison. Persistent disease phenotypes in enriched media weaken a simple lack-of-extracellular-vitamin explanation, while leaving mitochondrial availability unresolved. [Steinberg et al., cerebral organoid generation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34268881/) · [Gibco formulation](https://www.thermofisher.com/us/en/home/technical-resources/media-formulation.251.html)

**Conditional transport calculation.** If the mitochondrial carrier has simple saturable uptake with a constant counter-substrate and driving force, let r be KO/control capacity, and s the control cytosolic free ThDP divided by its apparent Km. Matching control flux would require a fold increase of 1/[r−(1−r)s] relative to the control substrate level. No finite amount can match it when s ≥ r/(1−r). As an illustration only, inserting the hippocampal mean RNA ratio of 0.719 for r gives a required 2.28-fold at s=1 and no finite solution at s=3. In human organoids, r=0.867 gives 1.36-fold and 2.14-fold, respectively. Neither r nor s was actually measured as a transport parameter; this exchanger also depends on the internal counter-substrate. Substrate means cytosolic **free ThDP**, not oral dose or free thiamine in CSF. This identifies the observation that changes the decision: if actual mitochondrial import capacity is limiting and already saturated, simply increasing extracellular vitamin cannot correct that step. If cofactor-limited and unsaturated, intact WWOX cells should jointly recover mitochondrial ThDP, functional substrate oxidation and a neural endpoint at measured exposure. **A result within WWOX cell classes.** In nine marker-assigned classes of juvenile hippocampal nuclei (GSE158684), Slc25a19 fell from 12.75 to 10.85 counts per million. Symmetric decomposition gives −1.99 CPM within classes and +0.10 from changing class weights. In excitatory neurons the means were 12.12 and 9.76 CPM, and in inhibitory neurons 17.56 and 14.93. Tpk1 instead rose in the pooled nuclei (37.64 to 41.31), opposite to its bulk hippocampal difference. These nuclei came from one pooled library per genotype. Their cell counts do not supply animal-level replication, cell assignment is approximate and nuclear RNA is not total RNA. The direction merits checking mitochondrial transport in neural cells; the cofactor itself remains unmeasured. **A functional counterexample in thiamine-sufficient brain.** In a separate rat study, adult male animals received a single high intraperitoneal exposure in the morning or evening. In the evening groups, cortical total ThDP increased from about 3.2 to 5.7 nmol/g fresh weight, while maximal PDH-complex capacity changed from about 0.095 to 0.065 µmol/min/g. In the morning the approximate ThDP means moved in the opposite direction (6.1 to 4.0), while maximal capacity also moved down (0.14 to 0.11). These are approximate figure-derived group means. Two-way ANOVA supports a daytime-by-treatment interaction for cortical ThDP (p<0.01) and a treatment main effect for PDHC capacity (p=0.03); the latter is not a separate significance test of either pair of bars. Cortex Ser293 phosphorylation relative to PDHA was also lower with supplementation. Dephosphorylation, vitamin delivery and usable enzyme capacity need separate measurements. The experiment is cross-sectional, 9–10 animals in each plotted group. No WWOX genotype, pediatric exposure, individual metabolite–capacity covariance or intact-cell flux was measured. Nominal group counts sum to 38; Methods also say 36 animals were purchased. [Primary study, Figures 2A, 3A, 4B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12428021/) · [Calibration and approximate bar coordinates](rat_figure.tsv)

**Refinement of the original prediction.** In this cortical homogenate, omitting assay ThDP left under 10% of the measured PDHC activity; extraction and assay conditions can make an apparent cofactor deficit. A native-versus-supplemented comparison must first preserve or independently verify endogenous occupancy. Pair matrix ThDP with intact-cell substrate flux and OGDH, control mitochondrial mass and assay timing, and compare neural function at matched activity. A response confined to isolated-enzyme rescue, serum vitamers or PDHA phosphorylation would leave the case for pediatric WWOX repurposing weak.
