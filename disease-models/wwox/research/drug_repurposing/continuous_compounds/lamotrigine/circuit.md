# Does lamotrigine preserve inhibition at an exposure that changes cortical activity?

**Decision, 24 November 2026.** Park lamotrigine for WWOX-specific pediatric trial prioritization. A juvenile cortical preparation responds within the adult surgical exposure interval. Preservation of recruited inhibition has been measured in a different preparation at a higher concentration. Neither experiment demonstrates selective suppression in WWOX, and the independent clinical audit found no attributable treatment contrast. **What the array can distinguish.** Divide total retained amplitude by the retained fraction of detectable sites. At 3 µM the ratio is 0.835/(28.5/34) = 0.996. The magnitude of the aggregate change is compatible with fewer detectable sites and little difference in this summary per detected site. Uniform modest weakening can also push marginal sites below threshold. An anatomical spread effect, a synaptic effect and a change in signal detectability remain unresolved. The ratio of published group summaries cannot recover a paired within-electrode response, identify lost sites, or give a confidence interval. At 30 µM it exceeds one, illustrating selection among the remaining sites. Reproduce all five rows with `python3 cortical_field.py` and compare against [cortical_field.tsv](cortical_field.tsv).

**Discriminating observation.** In a genotype-matched juvenile cortical preparation, distinguish a fixed baseline set of electrodes from the reselected responsive set. Retain all baseline sites, report missing/undetectable responses as censored with a sensitivity bound, and compare spatial extent and amplitude. Use the same input both to identify interneuron spikes and to record inhibitory charge in principal cells. Sporadic and sustained inputs address the competing sodium-state explanation. WWOX cortical spontaneous inhibitory currents already have smaller amplitude in the held record. Losing additional evoked inhibition would invalidate a selective benefit even if overall activity fell. Reopen for measured extracellular exposure near the adult 2.23–7.03 µM benchmark and relative preservation of evoked inhibition with abnormal propagation reduced, or for an attributable human WWOX comparison.
