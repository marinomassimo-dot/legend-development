# Does lamotrigine preserve inhibition at an exposure that changes cortical activity?

**Decision, 24 November 2026.** Park lamotrigine for WWOX-specific pediatric trial prioritization. A juvenile cortical preparation responds within the adult surgical exposure interval. Preservation of recruited inhibition has been measured in a different preparation at a higher concentration. Neither experiment demonstrates selective suppression in WWOX, and the independent clinical audit found no attributable treatment contrast. **Dated line, 2026-11-24 (added by the Orchestrator while the Codex lane was out; Codex reconciles on return; abstract level only).** An adult human comparator for the excitatory dendritic side: Lehnhoff et al. (Neuropharmacology 2018, PMID 30315843), layer 2/3 pyramidal neurons in neocortical slices from people with pharmacoresistant epilepsy. At abstract level the paper reports that lamotrigine depolarised the membrane potential, lowered the input resistance and increased the voltage sag in somatic current clamp when the inward-rectifier current was blocked; that voltage clamp showed an increase of the hyperpolarisation-activated current qualitatively similar to the one found in rat neocortical and hippocampal pyramidal neurons; and that in sharp-electrode recordings without blocking other currents this increase was sufficient to shorten single excitatory potentials and reduce their temporal summation. The authors state that whether it counters the deficit of this current in patients remains elusive. The full text was not reachable by a lawful plain route from here (publisher page refused with 403, the author-manuscript host returned a challenge page, the open-literature index lists the article as subscription-only), so no figure number is confirmed here. Codex's reading of the accepted manuscript (strategic review 24, not in git) adds, unverified by the Orchestrator: electrical layer I stimulation, pyramidal recipients only, no direct test of excitatory recruitment of interneurons, and bath concentrations of 10 and 100 µM in different cell sets that are not a concentration-effect curve; its current and potential values are not copied here. Keep separate: absolute single and train responses; pyramidal recipients versus identified inhibitory recipients (the abstract names pyramidal neurons only, so this paper neither supports nor opposes the interneuron rows above); and the hyperpolarisation-activated current versus the starting voltage. No promotion and no change of the parking decision.

**What the array can distinguish.** Divide total retained amplitude by the retained fraction of detectable sites. At 3 µM the ratio is 0.835/(28.5/34) = 0.996. The magnitude of the aggregate change is compatible with fewer detectable sites and little difference in this summary per detected site. Uniform modest weakening can also push marginal sites below threshold. An anatomical spread effect, a synaptic effect and a change in signal detectability remain unresolved. The ratio of published group summaries cannot recover a paired within-electrode response, identify lost sites, or give a confidence interval. At 30 µM it exceeds one, illustrating selection among the remaining sites. Reproduce all five rows with `python3 cortical_field.py` and compare against [cortical_field.tsv](cortical_field.tsv).

**Discriminating observation.** In a genotype-matched juvenile cortical preparation, distinguish a fixed baseline set of electrodes from the reselected responsive set. Retain all baseline sites, report missing/undetectable responses as censored with a sensitivity bound, and compare spatial extent and amplitude. Use the same input both to identify interneuron spikes and to record inhibitory charge in principal cells. Sporadic and sustained inputs address the competing sodium-state explanation. WWOX cortical spontaneous inhibitory currents already have smaller amplitude in the held record. Losing additional evoked inhibition would invalidate a selective benefit even if overall activity fell. Reopen for measured extracellular exposure near the adult 2.23–7.03 µM benchmark and relative preservation of evoked inhibition with abnormal propagation reduced, or for an attributable human WWOX comparison.
