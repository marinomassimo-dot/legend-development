# Lamotrigine, frequency-dependent dendritic integration and inhibitory recruitment

**Question before calculation.** At each published WWOX somatic time constant, compare the fifth-to-first subthreshold voltage response to five identical instantaneous inputs at 5, 10, 20 and 40 Hz, normalized to the same first response. Five pulses are an illustrative common train. A passive exponential can produce frequency-dependent excess summation in KO at baseline. If the KO–WT difference continues to increase from 20 to 40 Hz, that pattern alone cannot identify the intermediate-frequency drug effect reported in hippocampal dendrites. Separately compare the actual direction of spontaneous inhibitory events onto CA1 pyramidal neurons across experiments, preserving the concentration and developmental stage. No drug effect is assigned to WWOX from these separate comparisons.

## What the WWOX baseline can explain

The mean membrane time constant of juvenile layer II/III putative pyramidal neurons was 18.55 ms in WT, 18.79 ms in heterozygotes and 21.25 ms in knockout mice. For an exponential decay and a common first pulse, the model gives fifth/first responses of 1.072 and 1.105 in WT and KO at 20 Hz and 1.349 and 1.442 at 40 Hz. The KO–WT difference increases over this grid. This is a somatic passive baseline. It provides a control for a change in temporal summation before attributing it to a dendritic current. The source supplies group means, without the cell-level covariances needed for individual predictions. KO mean resting potential was 5.63 mV more depolarized and input resistance was higher. Equal current steps therefore need a companion comparison at matched voltage. [Held observations O01–O05](../../membrane_hypotheses_20261108/output/observations.tsv)

## Which observations travel across preparations?

In CA1 pyramidal dendrites of 6–10-week-old rats, 100 µM lamotrigine shifted the midpoint of the hyperpolarization-activated current (Ih) by about +11 mV without increasing its maximum. The response to a short train of inputs fell significantly at 10 and 20 Hz. Fig. 7B/C used actual dendritic current injections or synaptic stimulation. Fig. 7A was simulated. At 100 µM, an independent study in HEK293T cells found no significant shift in midpoint or maximum tail-current density for HCN1, HCN1 with TRIP8b, or HCN2. These results lower the priority of a direct channel-activation explanation. A native-cell effect mediated by another component remains possible. [Poolos et al., Figs. 5 and 7](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12118259/) · [Merseburg et al., Fig. 9A/B](https://elifesciences.org/articles/70826#fig9)

The preparations differ in strain, baseline network activity, age and cell sampling. A pooled mean would conceal the reversal. In paired dentate recordings, forcing five presynaptic basket-cell spikes at 25 Hz gave a first unitary inhibitory current of 128 ± 27% of control (6 pairs, p = .44), while an independently stimulated compound inhibitory response fell to 51.6% of control. This comparison supports measuring interneuron recruitment and transmission conditional on a spike separately. The six pairs do not establish equivalence. ## Exposure changes the order of the tests

In the human intraoperative study, 12 cortical catheters from five adults aged 20–54 yielded recovery-adjusted extracellular lamotrigine estimates of 0.57–1.80 µg/mL, or 2.23–7.03 µM as parent. Four catheters in one participant spanned both extremes, a 3.16-fold range. One probe used imputed recovery. The temperature-adjusted recovery estimates exceeded 100% in two probes. The rat exposures of 50 and 100 µM are about 7 and 14 times the highest cortical estimate in these participants. The surgery, local sampling and recovery correction limit generalization. Pediatric exposure at the relevant neurons remains unknown. [Rambeck et al., Tables 1 and 3B](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16650134/)

Dissociated CA1 neurons from P7–14 rats gave apparent affinities of approximately 7–9 µM for inactivated sodium channels and over 200-fold weaker affinity at rest relative to the 7 µM estimate. The nominal external concentrations tested began at 10 µM. The inference at the adult cortical concentrations is an extrapolation. For a channel *continuously held in the inactivated state*, the one-state calculation gives 24–50% occupancy at equilibrium with Ki = 7 µM (20–44% at 9 µM). Starting unbound and using the reported association rate gives only 0.22–0.70% at 100 ms and approximately 15–38% at 10 s for Ki = 7 µM. It assumes constant accessible concentration and a dissociation rate derived from that affinity. Any estimate for a firing neuron needs the changing state occupancy and recovery between spikes. A separate cultured rat cortical study also began at 10 µM. [Kuo and Lu, methods and binding results](https://bpspubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1038/sj.bjp.0701221) · [Cortical preparation, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7687190/)

A low-concentration primary comparator supplies a positive network control. In guinea pig hippocampal slices with magnesium omitted from the bath, the investigators reported a concentration-dependent fall in epileptiform field-event rate at 2 and 5 µM. Their results paragraph gives 0.51 and 0.22, respectively, without specifying the denominator for those two numbers. We use direction and concentration only. At 1 µM the investigators reported no detectable change. The single-cell recordings in the same paper used rat CA1 and higher concentrations. Low-dose network activity is therefore plausible in this provoked preparation. Transfer to a juvenile WWOX cortex, preservation of inhibition, and the molecular target remain open. [Grunze et al., pp. 132–134, Fig. 1 and extracellular results](https://epub.ub.uni-muenchen.de/16555/1/10_1159_000026528.pdf)

## Decision, discriminating prediction and next step

Prioritize a **history-dependent sodium-current comparison** within the lamotrigine dossier. At matched measured free drug, predict that prolonged depolarization in WWOX excitatory neurons will reduce available sodium current and sustained firing more than an isolated input after full recovery. First compare genotypes at their physiological resting voltages, then impose the same voltage history in both. Attenuation of the genotype difference after matching would support dependence on electrical state. Record identified inhibitory neurons and evoked inhibitory charge in parallel. Earlier loss of interneuron firing or of inhibitory charge would defeat the proposed selective circuit benefit, even if total network spikes fall. Relatively preserved isolated responses and inhibition, together with a native dendritic Ih shift and selective reduction of 10–20 Hz subthreshold summation after voltage and passive-response matching, would reopen the HCN mechanism.

The next evidence search targets native cortical-cell recordings at or below 7 µM with subthreshold and prolonged depolarization, explicit recovery intervals and identifiable interneurons. Screen the [identified inhibitory microcircuit study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6608323/) first, then the [entorhinal conductance study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2877872/). These are retrieval leads. Check the actual lamotrigine arm, extracellular concentration, recorded cell type and spike history before assigning either study evidential weight. A within-person WWOX exposure/withdrawal comparator would change the clinical interpretation. ADV has since returned its independent review. Targeted verification in three primary records found a combined-drug response in biallelic disease, a clinically informative response in a multigene deletion, and exposure during polytherapy. The missing comparator persists. [Independent review and primary checks](clinical_review.md) The adult extracellular interval is a benchmark for comparative experiments. Pediatric trial priority remains on hold. Existing pediatric use includes a dispersible formulation for defined epilepsy indications. A primary series under age 20 had 342 serum measurements in 102 individuals, with exposure varying by co-medication. Valproate co-therapy and serious rash matter to feasibility. These records supply neither pediatric cortical exposure nor a WWOX-specific response. Seizure reduction and developmental benefit would require separate evidence.
