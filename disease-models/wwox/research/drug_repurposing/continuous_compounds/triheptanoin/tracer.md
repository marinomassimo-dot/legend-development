# Odd-chain carbon reaches brain metabolites. Does it replenish a deficient pool?

Marin-Valencia et al. tracked the three terminal labeled carbons of heptanoate. Table 1 shows preferential appearance in glutamine M+3: 4.31 versus 1.30 mole percent excess in glutamate in controls and 3.40 versus 0.88 in glucose transporter 1 deficient mice. Ratios of the published group means are 3.32 and 3.86. The study did not detect a between-genotype difference in enrichment. In the contralateral hemisphere, post-infusion succinate averaged 0.15 and 0.14 µmol/g respectively, while glutamine averaged 4.50 and 6.00 µmol/g. Both groups received heptanoate. Table 2 does not establish a treatment-driven net increase in either group's cycle pool. Glial metabolism is a plausible contributor to glutamine enrichment. Direct heptanoate, liver-derived C5 ketones, and peripheral labeled glucose need to be accounted for before assigning the tissue route. [Primary tracer study, Tables 1–2 and Fig. 4](https://doi.org/10.1038/jcbfm.2012.151)

The randomized Glut1DS trial compared triheptanoin (25) with safflower oil (11) for eight weeks. Median overall baseline seizure rates were markedly imbalanced (96.6 and 2.1 per four weeks). Median individual changes were 12.6% and 0% reductions. The between-group estimate was 13.45 percentage points (95% CI −38.63 to 80.95, p=.5812). The frequently cited 62.2% fall in the absence-only subgroup involved eight treated participants; the one comparator participant had a 100% fall. The eight-week trial misses its primary endpoint and gives no interpretable advantage in that subgroup. Safflower oil matches appearance and supplies calories; it is not an even-chain medium-chain substrate control. Barbiturates were prohibited, material to transfer given the WWOX phenobarbital exposure history. The study excluded ketogenic-diet treatment.
