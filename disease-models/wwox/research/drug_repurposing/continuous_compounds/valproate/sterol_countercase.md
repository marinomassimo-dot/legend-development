# Does a plausible sterol benefit outweigh a stage-dependent myelin liability?

Two mouse cuprizone studies supply competing observations. In hippocampus one study reports restored cholesterol and diminished demyelination under valproate during injury (PMID 34461265). In a callosal study, after six weeks of cuprizone and two weeks of recovery, the proportion of axons >0.3 µm with a complete wrap was 48.1% with saline and 22.2% with valproate (−25.9 percentage points, six mice per group; Fig. 4F). Ages, schedules, tracts, denominators and outcomes cannot be equated across studies. Preservation of existing sheaths or sterol content could coexist with impaired generation of new sheaths. No WWOX treatment response is established. Source coverage: abstract and indexed primary-results/methods excerpts, no new full-paper coverage. [Hippocampal study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34461265/) · [Callosal study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2656679/)

An exploratory screen of six sterol pathway genes in untreated neuronal Wwox-deletion cortex and hippocampus and human WWOX-null organoids found lower SQLE, LSS and DHCR24 in all three comparisons. The negative direction survived each single-library omission in each dataset. DHCR24 KO-minus-control mean log2(1+expression) differences: −0.83 cortex, −0.99 hippocampus, −0.33 organoids. Upstream HMGCR was lower in both mouse tissues but near zero with unstable sign in organoids. The tissues and developmental stages differ; cortex and hippocampus come from the same mouse study. These are exploratory bulk contrasts, sensitive to lineage composition and maturation. RNA cannot establish enzyme activity, substrate limitation, cellular synthesis versus uptake, or the action of valproate. Discriminator: follow existing and newly formed sheaths separately at matched initial stage and activity suppression. Compare incorporation of labeled precursor into cholesterol, desmosterol pool and conversion, numbers of surviving mature oligodendrocytes, new wraps per eligible axon and conduction. Improved sterol availability and preservation of old sheaths together with reduced formation of new wraps would support coexisting protection and developmental liability. If the bulk sterol contrast vanishes within stage-matched oligodendrocytes, lower priority for a cell-autonomous sterol bottleneck.
