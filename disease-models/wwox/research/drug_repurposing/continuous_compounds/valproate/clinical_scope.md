Oliver et al., Table 2, records valproate exposure in 8 of 13 WWOX-DEE cases; 6 of those 8 have valproate underlined as current at the report. Seven exposed cases were classified drug-resistant. The one case classified non-drug-resistant had valproate as the only current agent listed and a previous levetiracetam trial. This is a reason to preserve heterogeneity in the clinical interpretation. Drug resistance is a patient-level history across regimens; neither 1/8 nor 7/8 is a valproate response rate. Retention can reflect partial benefit, combination effects or prescribing decisions. The narrative independently reports eight exposures. Table 2 gives serial MRI for some participants, including delayed and poor myelination in an exposed participant; it provides no paired drug dates, free exposure or activity-matched comparator for attributing MRI change. [Primary clinical series](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36779245/)

U.S. product labeling identifies greater risk of fatal hepatotoxicity below two years, particularly with other antiseizure drugs and in other high-risk settings. Known POLG-related mitochondrial disease, and suspected POLG-related disease in children under two, are specifically contraindicated. WWOX-LoF alone does not establish a POLG diagnosis. These constraints materially affect feasibility for an infant adjunctive trial. [Official oral solution label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=7d5cb52e-37dd-4dfa-a6f4-09f9548846c6)

Disposition: deprioritize repurposing valproate as a WWOX developmental or myelin-restoring intervention. The disease-specific observational data can document symptom-directed use but cannot estimate a comparative effect. Reopen the developmental hypothesis if a stage-matched WWOX preparation at measured clinically relevant free exposure shows improved new ensheathment and conduction independent of seizure suppression, without loss of existing sheaths or progenitor survival. A separate evidence opportunity would be longitudinal review of already-treated patients with exposure timing, baseline burden, concomitant drugs and serial age-normalized white-matter readouts. No patient treatment change follows from this analysis.
