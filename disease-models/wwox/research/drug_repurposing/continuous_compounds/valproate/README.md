# Valproate: suppression of seizures and stage-dependent myelination

**Decision at entry.** Existing LEGEND HDAC5 ranking and promoter-IV analyses provide no selective HDAC5 potency estimate for valproate. The present question concerns a possible developmental liability during seizure suppression. Lower firing can reduce excitotoxic load yet also change activity-dependent myelination. A non-WWOX rat comparator reports hypomyelination after exposure within an early postnatal window; later exposure gave no reported change in myelin gene expression. Neither timing nor rodent exposure directly maps to a child's clinical exposure. [Source indexed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15897262/) · [Prior LEGEND comparison](../../alternatives_it27_p5_20261104/README.md)

**Untreated WWOX baseline.** In GSE158684 hippocampal nuclei assigned to five oligodendroglial stages, progenitors comprised 242/731 (33.11%) of controls and 352/668 (52.69%) of neuronal-Wwox-deletion samples. Myelin-forming oligodendrocytes comprised 186/731 (25.44%) and 49/668 (7.34%), respectively. One pool per genotype. Sampling, recovery and marker assignment can shift these fractions. They neither measure transition rates nor demonstrate how valproate acts. Run stage_mix.py to reproduce from the held nuclei table. [Stage table](stage_mix.tsv) · [Source data](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158684)

**Discriminating prediction.** If the WWOX network remains enriched in a valproate-sensitive stage, delayed formation of new sheaths should persist after matching free exposure and seizure suppression to a mechanistically independent comparator. Test at matched chronological age and at matched initial lineage stage. Disappearance of the genotype difference after stage matching favors altered stage composition. A residual genotype-by-drug effect would warrant testing altered cellular susceptibility. Track newly myelinated axons, conduction and glial survival separately. The next comparison is to check stage-specific effect sizes and unbound exposure in the primary developmental study against feasible human exposure. This is a research comparison and supplies no reason to change an individual's existing antiseizure therapy.
