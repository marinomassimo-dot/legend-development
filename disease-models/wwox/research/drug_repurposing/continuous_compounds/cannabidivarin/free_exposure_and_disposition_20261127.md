# Cannabidivarin | Free-exposure boundary and a competing CB2 mechanism

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Disposition.** Retain CBDV as a conditional pharmacology comparator. Pediatric WWOX trial priority remains deferred. Direct TRPV1 desensitization at the published slice exposure has a large exposure gap. CB2-linked Gi signaling is a plausible competing low-concentration test, with cell-type assignment and native target engagement unresolved. The adult focal-seizure trial did not show a difference from placebo. In the five-participant pediatric Rett study, drug and metabolite exposure, polytherapy, adverse events and EEG have different denominators and cannot establish WWOX benefit. ## Reproducible exposure comparison and WWOX context

Let Cmax be the held mean total first-exposure plasma peak in four pediatric participants, 0.02575 µM. If fup is an assumed free fraction in plasma, the calculated free plasma concentration is Cmax × fup. The numerical comparison to a nominal reference T requires `Kp,uu / fu_assay ≥ T / (Cmax × fup)`, with Kp,uu the matched unbound brain-to-plasma partition and fu_assay the free fraction in the assay. This simply sets the unbound brain concentration equal to the free assay benchmark. It does not predict a clinical threshold, account for temporal hysteresis or establish an equilibrium during the initial peak.

At fup=0.232, the scenario is **5.97 nM** free plasma. The required ratios are approximately **0.84** for the reported CB2 cAMP estimate, **70** for rat TRPA1 activation, **603** for human TRPV1 activation and **1,674** for the 10 µM TRPV1 challenge or slice condition. Even setting fup=1 leaves a ratio of approximately 16 for TRPA1 and 140 for TRPV1 activation. These are sensitivity values with no measured human brain overlap. The lower benchmark for CB2 is a reason to compare native routes, not to assign clinical causality.

The held organoid CNR2 RNA means were **2.37 versus 3.47 CPM**, with descriptive log₂(1 + CPM) effect +0.405 and omission bounds +0.365 to +0.467 (two WT and four KO libraries in one parental background). Juvenile mouse cortex and hippocampus had means below 0.18 TPM and omitting one library reversed the sign. The assigned microglial nuclei in the pooled hippocampal comparison had **two versus six** Cnr2 UMIs from 167 and 171 nuclei. These counts are below the designated interpretation floor and cannot support a WWOX microglial increase. Bulk organoid expression cannot assign a cellular source. ## Discriminating observation

**Inference and hypothesis.** A low-exposure action may be mediated first through native CB2 Gi signaling, while a separate TRPV1 component may emerge with sufficient free channel exposure. Their consequences for excitability could differ. Neither low CB2 transcript nor high organoid TRPV1 transcript establishes exposure or receptor mediation.

**Countercheck and reopening criterion.** In a stage-matched WWOX and corrected human neural preparation with verified cellular representation, measure free parent and each metabolite and look for CB2-dependent cAMP change alongside a direct TRPV1 current/challenge readout. Compare identical initial recruitment and track late burst duration and amplitude separately. A receptor-dependent cAMP effect at exposure-supported levels, with a coherent change of late activity and preserved first recruitment, warrants a CB2 follow-up. A TRPV1 challenge change with selective loss of the duration effect under verified TRPV1 interruption would redirect the comparison toward TRPV1. Widespread suppression or isolated amplitude change without either direct engagement supports a different explanation. Reopen pediatric evaluation only if measured exposure and a genotype-selective native response align in an age-appropriate preparation. Trial formulations, co-medication effects and seizure outcome transfer remain separate requirements. Independent review is warranted if an exposure-matched signal in either route is found.
