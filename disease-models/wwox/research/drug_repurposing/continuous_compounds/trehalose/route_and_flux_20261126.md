# Trehalose · compartment, flux and disposition

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · [prespecified WWOX question and RNA data](README.md)

**Disposition.** Defer pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retain trehalose as a laboratory comparator for delayed lysosomal adaptation, glucose transport and late clearance. The strongest measured exposure is low micromolar in macaque CSF, and the lowest identified neural benefit comes from an acute-injury preconditioning assay at a higher concentration. Evidence of impaired chronic hippocampal function in wild-type mice makes preservation of evoked function a condition for a useful WWOX effect.

## What the exposure studies actually measured

In Howson et al. Fig. 2C, the terminal-sampling rat PK experiment gave peak plasma **8,900 and 8,336 ng/mL** and brain **87 and 73 ng/g** on days 1 and 7. Both reported peaks occur at the same 15-minute sampling time. The curves use five rats per time point. The whole brain was rinsed and surface vessels drained. The time-course method does not describe intravascular perfusion. Its plotted brain axis is ng/g wet tissue, although the result prose renders ng/mL. Using the plotted units, brain-to-plasma apparent volumes are **9.78 and 8.76 µL/g**. A separate rat study measured native residual plasma spaces of 7.99 µL/g for a plasma protein marker and 10.3 µL/g for a plasma water marker. Applied illustratively to the Howson plasma values, these would account for **71–92 ng/g** and **67–86 ng/g**, ranges that include both reported peaks. This is a sensitivity calculation with different animals and collection methods. It cannot establish how much signal was vascular, and it does not adjust for trehalose partition into erythrocytes. [Howson primary, Methods pp. 366–368, Fig. 2C p. 370](https://jpet.aspetjournals.org/content/jpet/369/3/364.full.pdf) · [Fridén primary, Results on vascular spaces and Fig. 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2949109/)

The **separate rat efficacy cohort** had saline perfusion before cerebellar collection. It reports a measurable brain signal of **53 ± 17 ng/mL** in the single daily oral arm and terminal plasma **6,383 ± 890 ng/mL**. The tissue normalization behind the prose unit and washout recovery are unclear. This supports detection after a more stringent collection and does not identify the concentration in striatum or neural interstitial fluid. The **three-macaque PK series** reports **81.3 ± 13.8 ng/g brain** and **562 ± 346.5 ng/mL CSF** at the terminal sample, the latter **1.64 ± 1.01 µM** (mean ± SEM), after the two-day phase. The method describes chromatographic separation from the sucrose internal standard despite a shared mass-spectrometric transition. CSF documents low-level molecular exposure with substantial uncertainty and is a separate compartment from free neural or lysosomal parent. No validated unbound concentration follows. [Howson primary, rodent and macaque Methods and Results](https://jpet.aspetjournals.org/content/jpet/369/3/364.full.pdf)

Tanaka et al. are the direct contrast for alternative hexose delivery. Fig. 1B and Supplementary Table 1 show **one 61-week-old female mouse per chromatogram** (untreated, IV, nasal). A parent standard and a reported transition identify the expected parent peak. Only the nasal sample has the reported hexose signal, and all three lack the assigned parent peak. Recovery, perfusion, per-sample detection limits and sampling time for that mass-spectrometry comparison were not explicit in the supplement section inspected. Without labeled parent, the hexose peak cannot distinguish peripheral hydrolysis, local hydrolysis or altered native hexose abundance. In a separate MSA cohort the combined oral/nasal arm improved spatial exploration and preserved LTP. Individual routes had four animals each in the behavioral panel. A significant combined-arm result leaves interaction and route necessity unresolved. [Tanaka primary, Fig. 1](https://doi.org/10.1093/braincomms/fcae193) · [Supplementary Methods pp. 1–2 and Table 1 p. 15](https://pmc-oa-opendata.s3.amazonaws.com/PMC11334933.1/fcae193_supplementary_data.pdf)

## The reporter, cargo and function can diverge

Mayer et al. Fig. 2F/G give approximately **0.63** and **0.58** of control radiolabel uptake after GLUT8 shRNA in HepG2 and germline Glut8 loss in mouse primary hepatocytes. The intact mass-spectrometry exposure comparison in Fig. 2B is a separate arm. The hepatic GFP-cleavage endpoint in Fig. 5F/G supports delivery to a degradative compartment and shows less cleavage in deficient cells/tissue. Neuronal surface transport and magnitude remain unresolved. Jeong et al. report nuclear TFEB at **100 µM** in macrophages and an endocytic lysosomal accumulation response with mild alkalinization. This gives an alternative to a common GLUT8 mechanism and makes a time-dependent stress response plausible. It is a targeted primary text comparison, with incomplete figure and lysosomal cargo coverage. [Mayer primary, Figs. 2 and 5](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep38586) · [Jeong primary, selected Results](https://doi.org/10.1080/15548627.2021.1896906)

Castillo et al. Fig. 2C gives an approximate **1.3 → 3.6** rise in red/yellow puncta per cell after a combined oral/parenteral regimen in mouse spinal cord. The comparison includes 40, 112 and 86 cells from three vehicle, five trehalose and four rapamycin animals. The plotted ratio can change with acidification, entry or residence time. Cells are nested within animals and the available figure does not resolve that covariance. The opposite high-concentration neuronal comparator from Yoon shows more autophagosomes, fewer autolysosomes and membrane damage indicators. The two studies differ in compartment, duration, tissue and exposure. An early pH shift followed by compensatory lysosomal biogenesis could produce some of these differences. Dynamic native cargo degradation is needed to distinguish it from persistent accumulation. [Castillo primary, Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.1038/cddis.2013.421) · [Yoon primary, Figs. 1–3](https://doi.org/10.1038/cddis.2017.501)

The closest identified low-concentration neuronal observation is Kulbe et al. Fig. 4. **100 µM** nominal trehalose given before transient glutamate exposure lowered the fraction of dying neurons at 24 h in rat hippocampal cultures (n=4 coverslips). Trehalose-specific cargo flux and delayed functional rescue were not measured in that panel. This nominal bath is about **61 times** the reported mean macaque CSF concentration. The 100 mM high-bath clearance counterexample is about **61,000 times** that CSF mean. These are scale comparisons across assays and do not assume free CNS concentration equals CSF or bath. [Kulbe primary, Fig. 4 and Results](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.brainres.2013.10.032)

## Wild-type function and pediatric feasibility

Camazzola et al. randomized male and female mice to water or 2% trehalose in water from weaning to P90–120. In dentate granule cells, treated males fired **5.75 versus 9.69 spikes at a fixed 100 pA** (three animals per group, multiple cells per animal). Cell capacitance increased from **35.46 to 46.81 pF**, lowering the nominal stimulus from **2.82 to 2.14 pA/pF**. A group-mean sensitivity check at a common **2.56 pA/pF** recovers about **9.2 versus 6.3 spikes** from Fig. 3B. This aligns the treatment group at 120 pA with interpolated control response at about 91 pA. The residual difference is descriptive because individual current–capacitance pairs and animal-level data are absent. The voltage-clamp panel also reports lower normalized inward and outward currents in males. The spatial memory change was −0.18 in males and +0.02 in females. The descriptive treatment-by-sex contrast is **−0.20 ± 0.13** (independent-group SE) from rounded paper values. The two separate within-sex tests do not provide an interaction estimate. The regimen also altered fluid intake, and the water-only control leaves hydration, sweetness and glucose delivery as alternatives. [Open-access primary published online 18 November 2026, Figs. 2–3](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202603043R)

The same mouse study detected no assigned cortical or hippocampal parent under its endpoint LC–MS procedure. A cortical spike control separated the analyte and supported identification, while the peak-window sensitivity and compartment exposure remain unresolved. Steady-state hippocampal LC3 and p62 markers were similar across arms. The relevant inference is that function can worsen under a regimen whose static autophagy markers do not change, so preserving physiological firing and memory matters alongside a cargo endpoint. [Camazzola primary, Figs. 6–7](https://doi.org/10.1096/fj.202603043R)

The pediatric ASMD feasibility pilot reports **five boys aged 2–12**, all of whom completed 12 weekly IV infusions of a pharmaceutical-grade solution. The single-arm design and small age-heterogeneous sample limit the QoL result. The preschool questionnaire was designed for ages 2–48 months. The mean change was **+6.0 ± 2.45 SEM** and was reported nonsignificant. The assayed serum sphingomyelins concern peripheral biochemistry. The post-hoc metabolomics analysis explicitly reuses that cohort and contains incompatible sex summaries in its own Results (five male and 3:2). The age and infusion completion here come from the parent report. The MPS III report is abstract-depth here, with a 12-week single-arm IV exposure. In adult ALS, the official registry gives a composite disease rate ratio **0.87 (95% credible interval 0.665–1.102)** with the regimen and platform placebo structure. Reported serious events were 19/120 in active and 3/41 in concurrent placebo. This is a tolerability and efficacy boundary in a different disease, without causal attribution of individual events. Pediatric WWOX formulation-specific free CNS exposure, efficacy and interactions with antiseizure co-medication remain unknown. [ASMD primary](https://doi.org/10.3390/jcm11010247) · [ASMD follow-up](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41501824/) · [MPS III publisher abstract](https://doi.org/10.2174/0929867330666230406102555) · [Official ALS results](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05136885?tab=results)

**Discriminating observation.** At measured intact parent, compare WWOX and corrected cells for early pH, later washout and complete native cargo turnover, with osmotic and glucose-flux controls. Delayed adaptive recovery predicts transient pH stress followed by faster substrate disappearance, preserved lysosomal acidification after washout, and improved ATP and evoked response. Persistent clearance block predicts increased LC3-positive structures together with unchanged or slower cargo loss. A matched hydrolysis-product arm should reproduce glucose-mediated effects without intracellular labeled parent. Reopen trial priority only for stage-appropriate exposure and preferential WWOX functional rescue with preserved input–output function. An independent review would change that decision if it could establish a measured early neurological exposure or a matched native-cargo/physiology result near the measured concentration.
