# Meclizine, phosphoethanolamine disposal and membrane reserve in WWOX loss of function

**Units and preparations.** Concentrations are nominal meclizine in cell medium, estimated intracellular phosphoethanolamine in fibroblasts, phosphoethanolamine in isolated mitochondria assay medium, or measured parent in plasma. These four measurements use different matrices. Mouse stroke studies use adult animal-level PET, depolarization and tissue injury endpoints. WWOX bulk RNA uses library TPM in P17 mouse cortex and hippocampus and library CPM in week-15 human cerebral organoids. Nuclear CPM values describe cell assignments within one hippocampal pool per genotype.

**28 November 2026 UTC.** Retain meclizine as a conditional ex vivo metabolic perturbation. Defer systemic pediatric WWOX priority. The [pretest](pretest.md) distinguishes temporary protection during an energy insult from impaired ethanolamine-lipid replenishment under repeated demand. PEtn denotes soluble phosphoethanolamine. PE denotes the membrane lipid class phosphatidylethanolamine.

The parent compound did not measurably inhibit isolated mitochondria in the earlier study. ## Native WWOX test of the assumed direction

PCYT2 is lower in each series and after each single-library omission. The two mouse bulk series use neuronal Wwox deletion. Means are **75.1→49.5 TPM** in mouse cortex (4/3), **42.3→20.6 TPM** in mouse hippocampus (2/3), and **93.2→57.4 CPM** in week-15 human organoids (2/4). ETNPPL moves in opposite directions in mouse cortex (**4.18→13.32 TPM**) and hippocampus (**8.06→4.97 TPM**), with each sign retained on omissions. Both organoid ETNPPL means are below 1 CPM. The pooled mouse hippocampal astrocyte gate gives Pcyt2 **247→183** and Etnppl **44.5→19.2** UMI CPM, with 417/479 assigned nuclei and one pool per genotype. These are not independent replicate counts. Mouse Pisd is lower in cortex and hippocampus, while the organoid contrast changes sign under omission. Tests of developmental cell composition, absolute enzyme activity and pools remain necessary. The **two-sink sensitivity model** asks what can be inferred from relative transcript changes even if they were proportional to rates. For fixed input, PEtn relative to WT equals 1/[a(1−f)+bf], where a and b are KO/WT catalytic and disposal ratios and f is the baseline fraction removed by ETNPPL. Under the cortical transcript-ratio scenario, the direction of the soluble pool flips at f=0.135. At f=0.05, the soluble pool is 1.272-fold and the Kennedy flux 0.839-fold. At f=0.40, they are 0.598-fold and 0.395-fold. Under the hippocampal ratios at f=0.15, the modeled pool is 1.975-fold, with flux near 0.961-fold. Reducing remaining PCYT2 capacity by half in that illustration produces a 3.340-fold pool and 0.813-fold flux. These results assume two first-order sinks, unchanged input, no feedback and arbitrary disposal fractions. They estimate neither target engagement nor true native flux. They show why a direction for cortical soluble PEtn cannot be read from PCYT2 and why an accumulated intermediate can coexist with diminished membrane synthesis. ## Pediatric exposure and feasibility

The open-label 14-day phase Ib achondroplasia trial enrolled 12 children aged 5–10, six per cohort. Unadjusted day-14 means were 167 (95% CI 83–250) and 266 (118–413) ng/mL total plasma parent. The separate weight-adjusted mean in cohort 2 was 229 (132–327) ng/mL. Using parent mass 390.95 g/mol gives 0.427 and 0.680 µM for the unadjusted means. The earlier primary E14–15 mouse cortical neuron assay tested 5 and 10 µM nominal and shows reduced OCR at both concentrations within roughly two hours (Supplementary Fig. 9, n≥3 per curve as reported). Its 5 µM condition is about 12 and 7 times the unadjusted pediatric means, closer than the 25 µM HEK condition (59 and 37 times). These are matrix comparisons, with tested concentrations rather than minimum effect thresholds. Formulation salt mass is distinct from circulating parent. No unbound juvenile brain concentration or metabolic engagement was provided. The trial's phase Ia predecessor used short exposure and modeled repeated PK. The small open-label phase 2 abstract reported nine children aged 5–10 over 26 weeks and no serious adverse events. Its growth endpoint and correction to Fig. 1 do not resolve central exposure or neurologic efficacy. [Embryonic neuron assay, Supplementary Fig. 9](https://doi.org/10.1038/nbt.1606) · [Phase Ib Table 5 and Fig. 3B](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0283425) · [Phase Ia](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0229639) · [Phase 2 abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00223-025-01449-8) · [Correction](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00223-026-01528-4) · [NIST parent mass](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C569653&Mask=2280&Units=SI) · [Unit replay](exposure_replay.tsv)

Three of 12 children reported adverse events in phase Ib, including one instance of somnolence. No serious event attributed to the study drug or withdrawal for adverse events was reported. The US prescription label concerns adult vertigo and describes possible additive CNS depression, anticholinergic effects and variation with CYP2D6 status or inhibition. The cerebral ischemia (S)-enantiomer evidence cannot inherit the pediatric exposure and safety evidence for conventional racemic tablets. In a WWOX study sedation could reduce measured activity or alter functional endpoints independently of metabolic rescue. ## One comparison that can change the decision

In age-matched WWOX-LoF and corrected neuron–glia preparations, compare vehicle with verified local free meclizine in nominally identical fixed-workload stress and recovery windows. Measure early PEtn and CDP-ethanolamine and distinguish new ethanolamine-derived PE from serine/PS-derived mitochondrial PE with pathway-specific isotope tracing and precursor enrichment. Use absolute PE species and verify separation of mitochondrial and membrane pools. Track ATP recovery at comparable surviving cell number, mitochondrial mass and first recruitment. Measure oxygen consumption and glucose-to-lactate flux with the same workload. Use matched PCYT2 engagement as a mechanistic control and a route-specific downstream lipid replenishment that is independently confirmed to leave PEtn elevated.

- Improved ATP recovery at fixed demand with preserved new PE formation supports stress adaptation. A signal erased by matching workload supports a demand effect.
- Lower oxygen consumption with worse ATP recovery and depleted new PE supports reduced metabolic reserve. Rescue of late ATP by downstream PE replenishment despite sustained PEtn separates the membrane deficit from the early intermediate effect.
- If increased PEtn and lower ATP persist after the relevant PE pools are restored, the soluble-intermediate mechanism remains a stronger candidate for the residual response.

**Disposition and review.** Defer systemic priority, including any inference from ischemic protection or peripheral pharmacokinetics to WWOX. Use the comparison as a conditional ex vivo probe when the native PEtn and lipid reserve can be measured at supported local exposure. Independent review could change the decision if it identifies a public WWOX target-activity or species-resolved lipid dataset that distinguishes the two competing directions, or directly measured juvenile CNS exposure near the effective local range.
