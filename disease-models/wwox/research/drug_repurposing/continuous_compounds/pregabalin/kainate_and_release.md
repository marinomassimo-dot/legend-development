# Pregabalin | Calcium entry versus α₂δ-2-associated GluK1
context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Checkpoint, 27 November 2026.** The new evidence changes the assay. Separating early from delayed effects remains informative, but loss of surface GluK1 can occur within the early window. Test a direct GluK1 response with input release bypassed alongside presynaptic calcium and transmitter output. Clinical and exposure boundaries are in the [public comparator](public_timing_and_exposure.md). This compound remains active.

## Independent primary comparisons

- **Release can change below the concentrations used in the calyx.** Quintero et al. measured extracellular glutamate after a second 70 mM K⁺ challenge in male rat neocortical slices (source ages 2–8 weeks, pp. 241–243, Fig. 3). The fitted IC₅₀ was 5.3 µM. The 1 µM contrast was not significant and the 10 µM response was 54% lower than the normalized control. The nominal 5.3 µM fitted estimate is approximately 2.35 times the adult lumbar CSF value at 6 h and 2.26 times the digitized rat ECF point, with no age or anatomical exposure bridge. Strong depolarization, net extracellular glutamate and the normalized second-to-first response differ from direct release at a defined synaptic terminal. In separate male mice aged 2–5 months, at 100 µM the published S2/S1 ratios were 0.92→0.59 in WT (13, 14 observations) and 0.74→0.63 in R217A (9, 9). The difference in mean drug changes is −0.22 on this ratio scale. The paper reports a main treatment effect and a WT-only significant post hoc contrast, without a reported genotype-by-drug interaction in Fig. 5. The observation count does not establish independent animal replication. [Quintero et al., Figs. 2–5](https://doi.org/10.1124/jpet.110.178384)
- **Functional α₂δ isoform evidence tilts toward α₂δ-1 for a provoked seizure endpoint.** Lotarski et al. report that pregabalin's protection and seizure latency after maximal electroshock were attenuated in their Cacna2d1 R217A mouse comparison and similar between Cacna2d2 R279A and its littermate control. Phenytoin had activity across groups. This is abstract-level evidence with no verified effect interval or exposure matching. It constrains transfer from binding to seizure protection in that model and leaves native developmental α₂δ-2 effects open. [Lotarski et al., abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24698052/)
- **A distinct postsynaptic α₂δ-2–GluK1 mechanism works in a system without native voltage-activated calcium-channel subunits.** Zhou et al. reported 12.49 ± 1.27 pA/pF for GluK1(Q) alone (14 cells), 34.88 ± 5.42 with α₂δ-2 (13), and 38.33 ± 8.34 with binding-site mutant α₂δ-2 R282A (12). With 20 µM pregabalin for 30 min, the augmentation was lost with α₂δ-2 and retained with its R282A mutant. Drug exposure did not significantly change the current in cells without α₂δ-2 or the separate recombinant CaV2.1 plus α₂δ-2 comparator (Fig. 5C, 16 cells per vehicle and drug arm). The authors specify GluK1 Q/R editing and myc tagging in biochemistry. The C-terminal splice isoform is unspecified in the inspected main Methods and Fig. 1 text. This leaves GluK1c and NETO1 dependence untested. Fig. 4 also reports lower surface GluK1 and α₂δ-2 after the same exposure in HEK293 cells (eight independent assays) and lower GluK1 relative to PSD-95 in paired adult Cacna2d1-null cerebellar halves (10 mice). In Fig. 2, the puff-evoked GluK1 response fell (15 and 13 recorded Purkinje neurons), with no significant change of paired-pulse ratio. This is evidence for a predominantly postsynaptic cerebellar mechanism in that preparation. The genotype, location and drug concentration differ from juvenile WWOX hippocampus. The R282A control preserves augmentation in this GluK1 assay. Its baseline functionality with CaV or native release still requires separate verification. [Zhou et al., pp. 1272–1279, Figs. 1–5](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awae333)
- **A lower-exposure presynaptic receptor mechanism is a third explanation.** Li et al. report a nominal 1.97 µM midpoint for the fitted reduction of the first evoked EPSC during local pregabalin application at the parallel fibre–Purkinje synapse in C57BL/6J mice. This is a midpoint of the measured modulation and does not mean half the baseline EPSC was abolished. At 10 µM, bath-applied parent lowered evoked iGluSnFR peak to 78.3 ± 5.8% and area to 77.4 ± 6.5% of control (8 recordings from 4 mice). Extracellular NMDA receptor blockade and disruption of α₂δ-1–NMDAR complexes altered the EPSC drug response in separate comparisons. The authors infer a presynaptic α₂δ-1–GluN2A-associated component. The fluorescence reports extracellular glutamate with possible clearance contributions. The study did not measure the blocker-by-pregabalin interaction on that fluorescence readout or address juvenile WWOX. It shows that a fall in release alone cannot distinguish direct CaV modulation from a presynaptic ligand-gated receptor pathway. [Li et al., §§2.1–2.3, Figs. 1–6, and Methods §4.1](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijms27114660)
- **The inhibitory direction can differ in human tissue.** At 100 µM in human neocortical synaptosomes, Brawek et al. report 47% lower high-K⁺-evoked GABA exocytosis when transporter reversal was blocked pharmacologically, with no detected effect on glutamate exocytosis under their assay conditions. At the same high nominal concentration, the ratio of excitation to inhibition can therefore move in either direction across preparations. The abstract gives neither the unit-level data nor a native inhibitory response at preserved chloride. [Brawek et al., abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19002437/)

## Prediction and decision

**Inference.** The α₂δ-2 signal in excitatory nuclei supports measuring channel partners. The data weaken an excitatory GluK1 enrichment claim, but retain a much larger co-detected population in inhibitory nuclei. Its postsynaptic effect on interneuron recruitment could be different from that at a presynaptic pyramidal terminal.

**Discriminating observation.** At matched age, drive and measured local pregabalin, measure presynaptic calcium and directly evoked glutamate output along CA3→CA1, alongside GluK1 agonist responses when presynaptic firing is bypassed. Use age-matched control and Wwox tissue and measure native inhibitory recruitment in parallel. A fall in calcium coupled to lower glutamate with a preserved direct GluK1 response supports an upstream release branch. To attribute it to CaV, test whether it survives independent NMDAR control while preserving initial drive. Loss of the drug effect only when the presynaptic NMDAR branch is controlled would favor the α₂δ-1-associated alternative. A smaller direct GluK1 response with preserved presynaptic calcium and reduced receptor surface signal supports a receptor branch. If native Wwox terminals retain the immature GluK1c brake, reduced GluK1 function can instead increase glutamate output despite smaller receptor currents. That predicted sign requires direct release measurement and an independently verified brake in the baseline preparation. Compare against an earlier developmental reference and measure the local effect at low exposure anchored by available PK; 20 µM supplies a published mechanistic comparator with uncertain central attainability. A drug effect restricted to the high comparator, or loss of inhibitory recruitment, weighs against a disease-targeted advance.
