# Felodipine in WWOX loss of function

**Decision.** Keep as a conditional laboratory comparator for cargo clearance versus altered Tau phosphorylation. Defer pediatric WWOX study priority. The strongest transfer is an assay design for a tractable question. Establish that old native Tau turns over slowly in a defined WWOX neural setting before assigning benefit to a clearance mechanism.

## Exposure and age boundary

- The 28-day dedicated mouse PK cohort had mean terminal **33.43 nM total plasma** (12.845 ng/mL, eight mice; day 10 mean **74.49 nM**). The separate Huntington reporter cohort's mean was **46.84 nM** (18.0 ng/mL, seven retained mice; **14.76–77.03 nM**). These represent separate designs and terminal samples. In Table 5, the mean of five quantified whole-brain totals was **43.42 ng/g**. Substituting zero or the listed limit for the three censored samples gives **27.14 to <42.14 ng/g** across all eight. These are arithmetic bounds on total homogenate and yield no local free fraction. The isolated injection peak, **2,995 nM**, belongs to another experiment. [Siddiqi et al., Tables 3 and 5, Supplementary Table 9](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-019-09494-2)
- The clinical reference used by Siddiqi was an average adult total plasma peak of **30.5 nM** at ages 60–80, about **1.54-fold below** the retained Huntington reporter cohort terminal average. A separate label reports **7 nM** peak and **2 nM** trough in healthy young adults under its extended-release condition (reporter cohort terminal average **6.69-fold** above that peak). With reported binding above 99%, the latter label peak implies **less than 0.07 nM free plasma** if that binding applies at that concentration. These are anchors for populations and matrices, without an estimate of pediatric neural free parent. In a separate primary study, 10 people with enzyme induction during chronic antiseizure treatment had Cmax **1.6 vs 8.9 nM** and AUC **2.0 vs 30.0 nM·h** relative to 12 matched controls. That is **18.0% of peak and 6.7% of AUC** in that comparison, with individual background drugs and pediatric transfer unresolved. [DailyMed, Description, Pharmacokinetics and Anticonvulsants](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=2e4298cf-02e9-49c7-9803-887161e1989a&type=display) · [Capewell et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2900404/)
- The pediatric essential hypertension trial randomized **133** and **128** completed three blinded weeks. The primary abstract describes half the cohort as age 6–12 or Tanner stage 3 or lower. One arm showed a modest trough diastolic contrast, with no detected systolic reduction. The tested extended-release tablet formulation and short follow-up constrain applicability to younger children. The US label states that pediatric safety and effectiveness are unestablished. Systemic hypotension, edema and CYP3A-sensitive exposure matter for any future pediatric feasibility assessment. No CNS exposure or developmental effect can be inferred from the BP endpoint. [Trachtman et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12700955/) · [DailyMed, Pediatric Use and Pharmacokinetics](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=2e4298cf-02e9-49c7-9803-887161e1989a&type=display)
- Open-label adult early Huntington FELL-HD reports **3/16** with a drug-attributed adverse event and **0/16** with a drug-attributed serious adverse event in its analyzed population. The flow diagram reports 19 allocated including one replacement, two intervention discontinuations in the third cohort, two moved from the third to the second cohort by received exposure and one undetectable exposure excluded for a drug interaction. This selected denominator and as-exposed allocation constrain tolerability inference. The FELL-HD-s lay results report no detected significant changes in NF-L, mHTT or GFAP during the study. This summary supplies neither a numerical central felodipine exposure nor direct neuronal cargo flux. The small, uncontrolled setting cannot establish efficacy or equivalence. ## WWOX detectability and the discriminating comparison

**Data.** The fixed RNA replay found **MAPT** down in P17 mouse cortex (200→127 TPM) and higher in week-15 human organoids (443→581 normalized counts per million). **CACNA1C** also moved in opposite directions (8.86→6.25 TPM and 6.19→10.23 CPM). Both signs survive one-library omissions in their respective datasets. **ATG7** fell in both mouse regions and was directionally unresolved in organoids. **SQSTM1** rose in cortex, fell in hippocampus and was unresolved in organoids. At the fixed detectable floor (both arm means ≥1), **CAPN1** was lower across cortex and organoids with hippocampal direction unresolved. Bulk TRAPPC6A cannot resolve the shorter protein isoform implicated by Chang. The hippocampal single-nucleus comparison is one pool per arm, with class-specific detectability and composition effects. These patterns support selecting a region and measuring covariates. They neither establish a shared channel direction nor measure protein phosphorylation or turnover. **Inference and hypothesis.** A reduced terminal pTau readout can follow decreased formation, increased dephosphorylation, removal of pTau, or loss or release of the cells or cargo being measured. The WWOX kinase precedent makes altered formation at least as credible as slower disposal. Animal huntingtin data make the disposal route worth testing when native cargo is slow to clear. A separate intact-versus-limited lysosomal challenge determines whether the WWOX defect is at delivery or degradation.

**One comparison.** In a developmentally matched WWOX-deficient and corrected cortical preparation, first check whether pre-existing endogenous Tau decays more slowly and whether the result precedes functional decline. Test felodipine at measured free parent against vehicle and one independent L-type perturbation matched on the measured early calcium current. Quantify pulse–chase turnover of absolute total Tau and site occupancy (S396 and S404 plus the relevant PS19 epitopes where observed), including new synthesis, soluble and retained fractions, intact released cargo, and survival. Check whether a validated acute interruption of lysosomal degradation removes the drug-dependent increment in old-cargo loss. Pair with axonal transport and preserved first evoked recruitment. Acute calcium block can alter both independently of proteostasis. Include blocker alone and a post-washout function measurement.

| Discriminating observation | Research action |
| --- | --- |
| Both calcium-matched interventions lower pTau occupancy rapidly and old total Tau clears at the same speed. | Prioritize the phosphorylation or calcium mechanism. Use the WWOX GSK3 comparator to separate site production from dephosphorylation. |
| Felodipine selectively accelerates loss of old Tau across cells plus medium, the increment requires lysosomal degradation, and axonal transport improves with early recruitment preserved. | Prioritize the turnover branch at the demonstrated free exposure. Calibrate a route-selective comparator and confirm in a second developing neural preparation. |
| Marker movement tracks cell loss, cargo release or acute suppression, or cargo clearance is normal in both genotypes. | Lower priority for felodipine as a WWOX cargo lever. |

**Checkpoint.** The next decisive inputs are turnover of endogenous Tau with function at measured local exposure and a second reading of the original cortical blot mapping. The latter could restore or further weaken the general cargo precedent. A corrected cortical worksheet alone would not establish native WWOX responsiveness. The independent brainstem and Huntington outcomes support retention as a conditional laboratory comparator.
