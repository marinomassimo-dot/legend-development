**Primary causal comparison.** In Jaber's separate preparations from rat cortex, the first tested **10 µM** bath depressed maximal neuronal oxygen consumption despite an unchanged basal readout. Astrocyte respiration after complex I inhibition increased at **20–80 µM** and the added oxygen consumption disappeared with complex III inhibition. The separate basal astrocyte comparison used oligomycin to identify an ATP-linked component. In permeabilized neurons, both recombinant NQO1 and NADPH reversed respiratory inhibition together (p=0.0014) at **80 µM** idebenone. Individual additions did not. This is a strong mechanistic control at those concentrations and does not define intact-neuron efficacy or central exposure. Wells were averaged within independent preparations. [Primary Results and Figs. 1–5 captions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7275857/)

Aleo et al. report that human NQO1 variants reduced protein strongly without a corresponding consistent fall in total mRNA and affected the idebenone response in LHON cells. The clinical association was retrospective and varied with the mtDNA mutation. It supports measuring reductase activity and reduced parent in WWOX, while LHON retinal anatomy and genotype do not establish a brain effect in WWOX. The EMA indication covers LHON-related visual impairment from age 12. WWOX pediatric use, brain target exposure and antiseizure co-medication remain separate questions. [Human primary](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2023.101383) · [EMA assessment](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/raxone)

**First exposure boundary.** In the pooled rat IV samples in Torii Table III, unchanged parent at 10 minutes was **1.61 µg/mL plasma, 1.20 µg/g brain and 0.01 µg/mL CSF**. At 30 minutes, brain parent was **0.02 µg/g** against **0.57 µg-equivalent/g** total radioactivity. The mitochondrial fraction in Table IV contained 0.65 of 1.89 µg-equivalent/g parent at 10 minutes in a separate IV cohort. The roughly **34%** fraction is not an oral exposure, an unbound concentration or a measurement of idebenol. In one specific high-repeat-dose adult study, the Australian regulator quotes **19.2 ng/mL (57 nM)** parent in plasma and **12.6 µM** for QS-10 plus its conjugates. These are different analytes. Applying the report's 98.5% human plasma binding gives an illustrative **0.86 nM** free plasma parent estimate if binding transfers to that cohort. This does not measure brain or membrane exposure and other formulations and cohorts need independent alignment. [Torii primary, Tables III–IV, printed p. 462](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bpb1978/8/6/8_6_457/_pdf) · [Australian assessment, pp. 18–19, footnotes 19–20](https://www.tga.gov.au/sites/default/files/auspar-idebenone-200206.pdf)

**Predicted split.** If astrocytes reduce the quinone while neurons cannot, an apparent gain in a mixed culture can coexist with lower neuronal respiratory reserve. Measure oxidized and reduced parent, NQO1 catalytic capacity and coupled ATP recovery by cell class. Increasing reduced parent with little recovery and impaired complex III substrate-supported capacity would favor a downstream limit. Restoration in WWOX of evoked function and ATP-linked respiration, lost when reductase activity is interrupted, would favor bypass. Lower lipid peroxidation with little ATP change would support a distinct antioxidant action. The next decision-bearing analysis is to bracket reduced parent and metabolites at observed exposures and check genotype-dependent effect sizes against the primary rodent and patient-cell experiments.
