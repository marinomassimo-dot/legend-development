# Idebenone · exposure, metabolite controls and disposition

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. The opening contrast held P17 WWOX mouse hippocampal/cortical abundance in TPM and week-15 human cerebral organoid abundance in CPM. New measurements below include human plasma and CSF, mouse/rat CSF, recombinant human NQO1 kinetics and nominal cell baths. They describe different compartments and endpoints. [WWOX prespecified comparison](pretest_20261126.md) · [Opening result and causal comparison](README.md)

**Disposition.** Park as a pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retain idebenone as a quinone-reduction and electron-transfer comparison with an explicit delayed low-concentration arm. The acute respiratory effects need substantially more parent than the central fluid measurements reviewed here. A delayed ATP signal near rodent fluid exposure survives as a hypothesis for a different assay and timing. Neither result demonstrates exposure-matched functional recovery in WWOX.

## Human and rodent exposure boundaries

- Artuch et al. measured idebenone in plasma and CSF of five people with Friedreich ataxia. Three CSF results were below detection and the study reports no demonstrated association between plasma and CSF idebenone. The approximate 300-fold plasma–CSF difference in the same abstract refers to **endogenous CoQ10 in 15 controls**. The idebenone concentrations of the two detected cases, assay limit, individual ages and timing are unresolved at open abstract depth. The earlier study from the same group reports **0.66–3.63 µM** in serum 90 minutes after administration in nine pediatric patients. That abstract describes electrochemical HPLC, with sample processing and redox specificity unresolved here. The separate young mitochondrial encephalomyopathy PK study reports a mean **452.2 ng/mL** Cmax in an abstract discussing parent and major metabolites. Its exact analyte definition needs the primary methods. None of these numbers establishes pediatric WWOX brain interstitial concentration. [Artuch CSF primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15127307/) · [Artuch serum primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11897364/) · [Pisano primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8911883/)
- Di Prospero Table 3 reports child-cohort peak **7090 ng/mL** for the selected higher oral regimen, as **total idebenone** in the Results. The laboratory analysed reported free and total parent and QS10 by LC–MS. The article gives fractions without tabulating their values or documenting the physical separation of unbound ligand from protein. Its 0.25–0.5% and 2% figures would imply **52–105 nM** reported-free parent and **0.40 µM** reported-free QS10 if applied to that total peak. These are conditional arithmetic, with timing and whether “free” means unconjugated or independently protein-unbound unresolved. Do not use these as measured free peaks. Four mild gastrointestinal events were reported in the open repeat phase. One child had recurrence on rechallenge and discontinued. [Di Prospero primary, Methods, Table 3, Phase 1B](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamaneurology/fullarticle/793937)
- In the healthy adult SNT-I-003 repeat study the TGA quotes **19.2 ng/mL, 56.7 nM** plasma parent and **12.6 µM** combined QS10 plus conjugates. The Puldysa assessment names separate validation studies for unconjugated and total analytes. Measured human plasma binding of **98.5%** by equilibrium dialysis gives an illustrative **0.85 nM** unbound parent in that cohort, if binding transfers. The older ultrafiltration range (93–99.7% bound) would give **0.17–3.97 nM**. QS10 binding of **98.8%** gives an illustrative **0.151 µM** even if the whole 12.6 µM analyte pool were unconjugated QS10, an intentionally extreme scenario under fixed binding. It is about **20-fold** below the embryo bath and **331-fold** below the pre-reduced cell bath described below. None of these calculations measures free CNS drug. In the direct regulatory interaction study, repeat idebenone increased midazolam Cmax **28%** and AUC **34%**, so interaction and exposure assessment with background antiseizure drugs remains relevant. The EMA LHON product has adolescent experience and a separate indication from age 12. It supplies no infant WWOX efficacy result or infant-specific CNS PK. [TGA assessment pp. 18–19, nn. 19–20](https://www.tga.gov.au/sites/default/files/auspar-idebenone-200206.pdf) · [EMA Puldysa pp. 31–33](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/documents/withdrawal-report/withdrawal-assessment-report-puldysa_en.pdf) · [EMA Raxone indication](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/medicines/human/EPAR/raxone)
- A coauthor's institutional thesis reproduces the separate oral EAE mouse experiment. Fig. 9B reports CSF **1.7, 2.7 and 5.0 ng/mL** by LC–MS/MS in three samples with different EAE scores. The parent is about **5–15 nM** and the **10 µM** acute rat cortical-neuron bath is approximately **677–1991 times** higher. The severe EAE sample had the highest CSF concentration. Disease-dependent barrier permeability, timing and assay handling limit transfer of this result. Pooled rat IV CSF from Torii adds **29.5 nM** at 10 minutes as a separate route. Neither CSF total nor a culture bath establishes intracellular or membrane concentration or the fraction present as idebenol. [Bros Esqueu thesis Methods and Fig. 9B, pp. 8, 16](https://refubium.fu-berlin.de/bitstream/handle/fub188/5622/diss_bros.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1) · [Torii Tables III–IV](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/bpb1978/8/6/8_6_457/_pdf) · [Jaber Fig. 2D](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7275857/)

## Compare timing and redox state before attributing rescue

Heitz Fig. 1C shows a delayed low-exposure counterexample. After two days of pretreatment and a 24-hour postchallenge interval, **10 nM** nominal idebenone recovered roughly **7% of the rotenone-lost signal** in RGC-5 wells, about **4 percentage points** of the unchallenged ATP-luminescence scale using Fig. 1B's 59% loss. The 10 nM one-day arm did not carry a significance mark. The groups contained six wells each. The ATP readout mixes cell number and ATP per cell. The mechanistic conclusion is further limited by RGC-5 authentication work identifying tested stocks as mouse and implicated in cross-contamination with 661W. This comparison supports a timing-sensitive test and cannot assign retinal ganglion identity, acute bypass or preferential WWOX activity. The separately measured ocular fluid after oral mouse dosing concerns a different compartment. [Heitz primary Methods and Fig. 1A–C](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0045182) · [Primary RGC-5 authentication](https://doi.org/10.1167/iovs.13-12085) · [ICLAC record](https://iclac.org/education/rgc-5/)

Haefeli found that both idebenone and QS10 were reduced by recombinant NQO1. At fixed NADPH, the dilute-substrate slopes (Vmax/Km) are **1.780** and **1.792 µmol/mg/min per µM**, approximately equal per available molecule in that purified assay. In HepG2, **5 µM** idebenone plus rotenone left **71±6%** of vehicle ATP versus **2±1%** with QS10 or rotenone alone. Thus capacity to accept electrons from NQO1 is insufficient to infer ATP rescue. With the measured idebenone Km of 30 µM, the enzyme-only model gives just **0.033% and 0.332% of Vmax** at 10 and 100 nM versus 25% at 10 µM. This is a substrate sensitivity calculation at fixed cofactor and enzyme, with no estimate of membrane accumulation, reoxidation, time integral or a cellular efficacy threshold. [Haefeli primary Table 1, Figs. 2A and 3A](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0017963)

Giorgio et al. then found that **pre-reduced 50 µM QS10** supported antimycin-sensitive membrane potential after oligomycin and rotenone and increased rotenone-insensitive OCR in two complex-I-deficient cell models. The figure uses chemically pre-reduced substrate, and in the control cybrid the rotenone effect weakened when assayed later. The endpoints and preparations differ from Haefeli. **3 µM** unconjugated QS10 added to dechorionated zebrafish embryo water before rotenone partly restored whole-embryo respiration and survival. The human aggregate plasma assay leaves the unconjugated QS10 fraction and its neural exposure unresolved. The results place QS10 in the metabolite-control arm, without assuming that its conjugates are equivalent or that QS10 is an inactive chemical species in all assays. [Giorgio authorized repository copy, §§2.6, 3 and Figs. 2–5](https://arts.units.it/bitstream/11368/2934405/4/2934405_1-s2.0-S000527281830080X-main-PostPrint.pdf)

As a clinical countercheck, the four-week randomized MELAS study's posted cerebral-lactate MRS change was **−0.49 IU** with placebo, **−0.09** and **+0.16 IU** on idebenone, with seven participants per arm. Subtracting placebo gives **+0.40 ± 0.57** and **+0.65 ± 0.72 IU** (unadjusted SE from the posted group SDs). Of 27 randomized participants, 26 were in the registry’s 18–65 age category. The small, predominantly adult cohort and indirect MRS endpoint permit uncertainty, with no favorable hyperlactatemia point estimate. [Official NCT00887562 primary outcome](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00887562?tab=results)

**Decision-changing observation.** In matched WWOX and corrected excitatory neurons and astrocytes, compare acute response and delayed response with matrix-validated measurements of oxidized and reduced parent, unconjugated QS10 and deconjugated total. Use measured intracellular exposure and verify the species survives collection. Compare NQO1 competence and downstream complex III capacity within the same preparation. A bypass predicts an early increase in coupled ATP-linked respiration followed by improved evoked recovery, with dependence on NQO1 at a clean perturbation and retained downstream capacity. Accumulated reduced parent with weak complex III-dependent ATP recovery favors a downstream limit. A delayed fall in phospholipid oxidation or improvement in viable-cell ATP without an acute coupling effect is compatible with adaptation or survival. Use cell-normalized ATP and preserved basal neuronal and inhibitory responses to distinguish these accounts. Reopen priority for an exposure-matched WWOX-specific evoked recovery with coupled mitochondrial evidence and no material decrease in normal neuronal reserve. An independent reading could change the disposition if it retrieves a verified stage-appropriate CNS exposure or an authenticated low-exposure cortical replication with separable respiration and survival. Further interpretation of total plasma would not resolve either.
