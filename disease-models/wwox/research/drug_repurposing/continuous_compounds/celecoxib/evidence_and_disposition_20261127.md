# Celecoxib, post-activity prostanoids and neural exposure

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. The prespecified WWOX hypothesis and prior glial and drug records define the comparison. Units and preparations are explicit below. Coverage receipts describe targeted full-text sections and captions.

**Decision.** Park celecoxib for pediatric WWOX trial priority. Retain it as a preclinical probe of an activity-dependent COX-2 response. The adult exposure observation is in the low-nanomolar range, and a WWOX-specific prostanoid response at that exposure has yet to be measured. The most informative experiment starts with endogenous product dynamics after matched activity, then asks whether a supported free exposure changes both the product and circuit recovery. ## Counterchecks on timing and seizure effects

- **Time and cell type.** In Serrano's adult pilocarpine status model, forebrain principal-neuron Cox-2 deletion gave no detected difference in initial EEG seizure intensity (four mice per group, Fig. 2F, p = 0.44). At one day the CA1 injury result was an adverse-direction trend with the deletion (p > 0.05, Fig. 4A). At four days the deletion reduced Fluoro-Jade-positive CA1 injury (p = 0.0286, Fig. 4B), and a later Nissl count supported reduced loss. Some somatostatin interneuron counts already favored deletion at one day (Fig. 5). An early marker's direction therefore depends on which population is measured. The strongest transfer is the need for matched early activity and separate later outcomes. Constitutive absence of neuronal enzyme in this model and acute inhibition in juvenile WWOX carry different exposure and timing assumptions. [Serrano et al., Results and Figs. 2–5, 8–9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4126152/)
- **Direct neural activity and washout.** Berdichevsky et al. found no detected count or duration change in the acute low-Mg preparation at nominal 10 µM celecoxib (five hippocampi, Fig. 4A–B). In early organotypic cultures with spontaneous recurrent activity, frequency and power declined relative to vehicle during exposure (eight slices per group, Fig. 4D, p = 0.01 and 0.047). Seizure-like activity recurred after washout in a separate chronic-exposure preparation (seven slices, Fig. 4H–I). Nominal bath exposure was about 1,589 times the observed adult CSF mean maximum. The rat crossover added an in vivo chronic model and reported reduced recorded seizure frequency. A possible carryover effect and lack of a concentration bridge limit conclusions about persistent modification. None of these comparisons isolates a prostanoid-mediated effect in WWOX. [Berdichevsky et al., Results and Figs. 4–5](https://doi.org/10.1002/acn3.364)
- **Adjunct-exposure explanation.** In Schlichtiger et al., four selected prior phenobarbital nonresponders completed all phases. Their mean seizures/day were 7.68 on repeat vehicle, 6.46 during celecoxib pretreatment, and 2.20 during the following phenobarbital course. The overall celecoxib period had no detected seizure-frequency difference and mixed individual directions. Three of four completers had complete suppression during the repeat phenobarbital phase. Completer ratios of 0.84 and 0.34 for those consecutive phases are descriptive. Selection on initial resistance, loss from seven to four, sequential order, pantoprazole coexposure, lack of a contemporaneous no-celecoxib repeat arm, and a single terminal tissue phenobarbital measurement prevent attributing the change to a direct celecoxib action or quantifying the contribution of altered brain drug delivery. P-glycoprotein staining and free regional antiseizure drug concentration are different readouts. [Schlichtiger et al., Table 1, Results and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2936016/)

## Discriminating result and disposition

**Hypothesis.** An inducible COX-2-linked prostanoid response could prolong recovery after the same initial activity in juvenile WWOX hippocampus. Match the initial input and recruitment by design, record the early response before delayed compound assignment, and compare blinded Wwox and littermate preparations within each exposure condition. Measure PGE₂ and another prespecified prostanoid over time with free parent exposure, evoked inhibition, and patterned recovery in the same compartment. Product suppression preceding preferential Wwox recovery, with initial recruitment and evoked inhibition preserved, would favor a downstream contribution. A matched downstream add-back or receptor perturbation should test mediation of the recovery response. If recovery changes only with concomitant antiseizure drug availability, the barrier or exposure explanation takes precedence. Product suppression alone supports engagement. A functional response without product change shifts the mechanism search. These are prospective criteria.

**Pediatric feasibility.** Juvenile arthritis use from age two provides experience in a different indication. The label's pediatric study boundaries and cardiovascular, gastrointestinal and renal risks matter for a chronic trial. Measured low-range neural engagement and a replicated benefit in native WWOX would be needed before revisiting this compound; current evidence does not supply either. Independent review would be decision-changing if a new WWOX product-plus-function dataset or a matched pediatric neural exposure measurement becomes available. [US label §§1.3, 5, 8.4 and 14.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=45816eae-ad83-4d2f-a035-c3f9f24708f9)
