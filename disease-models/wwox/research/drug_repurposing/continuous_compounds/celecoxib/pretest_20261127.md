# Celecoxib and post-activity prostanoid signaling in WWOX loss of function

Status on 2026-11-27. First comparison complete. Preclinical hypothesis under evaluation. No pediatric priority assigned.

Hussain et al. observed hippocampal gliosis, increased inflammatory markers and reduced inhibitory interneuron markers in approximately two-week-old global Wwox-null mice. The joint observation permits several causal orders. A developmental inhibitory deficit could drive aberrant activity and secondary gliosis. An activity-dependent prostanoid response could also worsen function during recovery. Glial staining, cytokines and interneuron counts alone cannot establish either direction. The preparation and age matter because the response to activity and the distribution of vulnerable interneurons can differ across regions and models. [Primary study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30290271/) · [Existing functional comparison](../../generation_it41_20261109/comparisons_it41.md)

**Prediction.** At matched initial evoked activity in juvenile Wwox-deficient hippocampus and age- and litter-matched wild-type controls, look for a larger or longer post-activity prostaglandin E₂ (PGE₂) response followed by slower recovery of patterned evoked transmission. Compare compound and vehicle within each genotype. In the same preparation, record early recruitment and evoked inhibitory charge and measure local free celecoxib, PGE₂ and a second prespecified prostanoid. A delayed celecoxib effect on the excess prostanoid signal that precedes preferential Wwox functional recovery, with preserved initial recruitment and inhibition, supports a functional contribution of the prostanoid pathway. An orthogonal comparison at the proposed prostanoid receptor or a downstream add-back would test whether product suppression mediates the functional effect. Treat the experiment as a proposed comparison.

**Competing explanations.** If early recruitment changes first, compare recovery at the same initial activity before assigning a downstream effect. If PGE₂ drops and recovery remains unchanged, retain pathway engagement only. If recovery changes without prostanoid engagement or disappears at fixed free concentration of a concomitant antiseizure drug, favor an independent mechanism or altered drug exposure. Analyze pretreatment and post-activity comparisons separately because suppressing the pathway could remove an early protective effect. Any loss of evoked inhibitory function is a reason to stop advancement. [Temporal functional comparator](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22016518/) · [Adjunct-exposure comparator](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20590600/)

**Decision.** Start with the target-engagement and exposure comparison in the same prepared neural compartment. A larger Wwox-specific post-activity response and exposure-matched functional recovery would justify an independent review before any prioritization. Stage-appropriate functional benefit, confirmed mechanism and a pediatric exposure bridge would each require distinct evidence. [Initial quantitative comparison](first_comparison_20261127.md)
