# Does depolarization or pediatric exposure close the concentration gap?

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. Chang et al. had been read for the native EPSC data. This extension examines the previously unused subunit-by-voltage panel and compares the reported assay concentrations with the specified formulation in a feasibility trial.

**Channel subtype and voltage.** In Xenopus oocytes Fig. 6B reports IC50 values of 2.09 mM for GluA1, 1.16 mM for GluA1/2 and 0.52 mM for GluA2/3. In a separate GluA2/3 voltage comparison, the IC50 shifted from 1.11 mM at −80 mV to 0.43 mM at −40 mV (n=6 per voltage). Depolarization increases apparent potency about 2.6-fold. A 430 µM half-effect is still 215–430 times the 1–2 µM adult dialysate range, across different preparations. Fig. 6A–B and G–H include a 1 µM point with current close to the normalization reference; the next plotted concentration is 50 µM. Small effects between these points, their cell-type dependence and compounded network consequences remain unresolved. The plot does not support a precise WWOX percentage at 2 µM. Neither an IC50 nor the 300 µM CA1 bath result defines the minimum effective concentration. The subunit comparison does not establish native AMPA composition at cortical synapses onto inhibitory interneurons. [Chang et al., Fig. 6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4805082/) · [Human interstitial comparison](README.md)

**Pediatric feasibility and inference.** In an uncontrolled study of an 80:20 C10:C8 triglyceride food with dietary advice, 23/35 children and 18/26 adults completed. Gastrointestinal symptoms were the main reason for discontinuation. In children the visit-C plasma C10 median was 165.1 µM (range 18.3–407.4), with C8 also elevated. Assays measured plasma free medium-chain fatty acids; no unbound cortical C10 was reported. The highest plasma value overlaps the nominal 300 µM slice bath. To equal that bath, extracellular brain C10 would have to be 74% of that highest value, or 182% of the pediatric median; these are required compartment ratios and do not estimate actual penetration. Adult traumatic injury with continuous feeding cannot provide a reliable fixed conversion for that population. Only 9/23 pediatric completers returned quantifiable diaries, clinicians' records were used for others, four children had insufficient counts and some recorded paroxysmal events across the cohort were nonepileptic. C8 and C10 covaried strongly (reported R²=0.87). A correlation with outcome cannot isolate C10 from the mixture or establish AMPA blockade. [Schoeler et al., biochemical methods, Fig. 3 and seizure/event analysis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8557697/)

**Next decisive comparison.** Seek independent chemical measurements of the unbound fraction, probe recovery or the time course of extracellular C10 after comparable feeding. Separate transient peak effects from steady exposure. Test whether any compartment correction and voltage dependence can coexist with a reproducible native effect at low exposure, using an energy-matched comparator and matched inhibitory recruitment. Inspect whether C8 changes C10 oxidation/availability even when it is itself a weak AMPA blocker. This gives a specific alternative interpretation of the clinical correlation without treating either mechanism as demonstrated in WWOX.

**WWOX expression comparison specified before analysis.** The most C10-sensitive tested heteromer contained GluA2/3. Ask whether Gria3 increases relative to the alternative partner Gria1 specifically in the excitatory pool versus the inhibitory pool. Compute the fraction Gria3/(Gria1 + Gria3) using sums of nuclear molecules within assigned classes. Also condition on detectable Gria2 as a separate dropout-sensitive check. Use the same class assignment and ≥500-UMI filter as the held WWOX analyses, and compare within genotype and cell class before interpreting their difference. An opposite sign or a similar shift in both classes weakens the hypothesized preferential excitatory effect. RNA fractions are neither receptor stoichiometry nor surface occupancy.

**Observed result.** The fraction decreased from 24.48% to 23.48% in excitatory nuclei and from 21.27% to 19.27% in inhibitory nuclei. Thus the excitatory-versus-inhibitory contrast widened by about one percentage point because the inhibitory fraction fell more; neither class showed the absolute shift toward Gria3 posited for increased sensitivity. Conditioning on detectable Gria2 gave 24.48% to 23.47% and 21.44% to 19.26%, respectively. One biological pool per genotype limits replication. Different interneuron subtypes, receptor editing, auxiliary proteins, protein trafficking and membrane potential can change the functional ordering.
