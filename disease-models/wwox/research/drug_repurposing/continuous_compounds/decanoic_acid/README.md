# Decanoic acid and cortical AMPA receptor exposure

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. Open C10 as a defined molecule after the L-serine disposition. Its connection to AMPA-mediated currents and its presence in some pediatric dietary formulations give a testable comparison to perampanel and to the ketogenic-diet observations. C8/C10 triglycerides, liberated C10, systemic ketones and a direct application of decanoic acid to neurons represent different exposures.

**Prediction.** Direct AMPA modulation should appear rapidly at demonstrably attainable unbound local C10, before major changes in fuel oxidation. In WWOX and matched corrected networks, compare excitatory input to inhibitory as well as excitatory neurons and evoked recruitment alongside spontaneous bursts. If useful AMPA suppression requires exposure far above measured brain concentrations, test nutritional or metabolic explanations separately. Suppressing AMPA input to inhibitory neurons may offset a reduction in recurrent excitation. Changes in GRIA transcripts do not identify receptor surface composition or pharmacologic sensitivity.

**First exposure check.** The registered Chang slice comparator found a mean 38.9% reduction in evoked CA1 excitatory current at a 300 µM bath exposure in six recordings. Bernini et al. measured nonesterified C8/C10 with brain microdialysis during feeding in ten adults with traumatic brain injury. Both brain analytes were reported in the 1–2 µM range. Median plasma C10 was 7.9, 18.7 and 15.2 µM during fasting, intermediate feeding and stable feeding, respectively. Calling extracted plasma fatty acid “free” does not measure its albumin-unbound fraction. The brain dialysate value is a different compartment with a recovery uncertainty of its own. Comparing the slice bath to the reported brain range gives a 150–300-fold separation. A 300 µM true interstitial level would yield 1–2 µM in dialysate only with 0.33–0.67% recovery if probe loss alone explained the gap. This is a sensitivity calculation, not a measured recovery. Do not transfer a recovery estimate for ketone bodies to C10. A single measured slice concentration does not establish a minimum effective concentration. The human study involved brain injury and enteral nutrition; it supplies no upper bound for WWOX or a C10-enriched pediatric formulation.
