# C10 disposition, plasma binding and metabolic alternatives

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. Park isolated decanoic acid for pediatric WWOX trial priority. The reported exposures from the pediatric blend do not establish free brain C10. Native AMPA sensitivity at attainable exposure and functional metabolic rescue in the disease model remain unverified.

**What albumin adds.** Among pediatric completers on the mixed triglyceride formulation, Table 4 gives visit-C albumin of 44 g/L (range 39–50). At a rounded albumin mass of 66.5 kDa that mean represents about 662 µM protein, compared with a reported visit-C median of 165.1 µM nonesterified C10 and maximum of 407.4 µM. These are aggregate statistics; individual values cannot be paired. Nonesterified acid includes protein-bound acid. A dialysis study in defatted human albumin at 37°C and pH 7.4 described a high-affinity component of about 65% and measured first stoichiometric association constants for C10 of 11 and 0.14 per µM for the two components. Chloride competed at the high-affinity component. These are purified-preparation results. [Pediatric feasibility study, biochemical results and Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8557697/) · [Honore and Brodersen, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3407921/)

**Sensitivity calculation.** At 44 g/L and full availability of the two assumed first binding steps, a mass balance gives 0.056 µM free for 165.1 µM total and 0.76 µM free for 407.4 µM total. With only low-affinity albumin and all protein available, these increase to 2.33 and 10.69 µM. If only one tenth of the protein is available in the mixed-affinity model, the calculated free concentrations become 101 and 342 µM. The deliberately extreme availability scenarios are unmeasured. The calculation truncates the binding polynomial after its first step for each component. With fixed first constants and available albumin, including additional occupied states can only increase mean binding at the same free concentration. Matrix, competing lipids/drugs, ionic strength, albumin heterogeneity and membrane partition can change the assumptions. These numbers illustrate why the plasma cohort alone cannot identify unbound C10. They are neither pediatric PK estimates nor an upper bound on brain exposure. Pairing actual free/total plasma measurements with calibrated brain sampling would resolve more than another receptor curve at high nominal exposure. **Distinct carbon endpoints resolve an apparent contradiction.** The SH-SY5Y cell study reports C10 oxidation to carbon dioxide at about one fifth of the C8 rate; C8 further decreased C10 oxidation. In intact mouse cortical slices, 200 µM of each acid in the presence of glucose gave preferential utilization of C10 in the mixed-substrate experiment, judged by relative carbon labeling. These experiments assess different tissues, endpoints and fates. Neither provides a direct concentration of C10 at the synapse. In eight primary astrocyte culture batches, C8 stimulated respiration coupled to ATP production while C10 increased proton leak. In the slice glutamine-synthetase comparison, C10-derived carbon reached glutamine and GABA. Label in GABA does not establish an increase in evoked inhibitory transmission. A beneficial energetic interpretation of higher oxygen consumption would fail if oxygen use mainly increased proton leak. [Neuronal comparison, author abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28682459/) · [Andersen et al., Figs. 1–3 and 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8414667/)

**Reopening observation.** In WWOX and matched corrected preparations at measured free substrate, test direct AMPA current and inhibitory recruitment first, then ATP-linked respiration, leak, labelled substrate in transmitter pools and evoked inhibitory current. A WWOX-selective reduction of pathological firing with preserved physiological recruitment, or a functional energetic benefit that survives matching AMPA current, would reopen the respective mechanism. Analyze isolated C10 and the mixed preparation separately. Pediatric mixed-food administration documents a formulation precedent, with gastrointestinal intolerance and attrition. It does not establish chronic tolerability, drug interactions or efficacious exposure for isolated C10 in WWOX. The absence of a demonstrated therapeutic window in these comparisons gives no reason to infer absence of all C10 effects.
