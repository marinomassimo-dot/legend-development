# Carglumic acid in WWOX loss of function

`context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`. Comparisons use blood ammonia and plasma labeled urea (µmol/L), precursor enrichment (atom % excess), recurrent emergency visits per child, and juvenile mouse cortical and hippocampal bulk TPM and human cerebral organoid bulk CPM.

**Decision.** Park carglumic acid for an unselected pediatric WWOX trial. Retain a specific research question for documented secondary hyperammonemia, including in the setting of valproate exposure. Human PA tracer experiments support an effect on labeled ureagenesis. The controlled episode trial and retrospective valproate series do not quantify a WWOX electroclinical benefit or identify a hepatic WWOX defect. The experiment that would change prioritization measures hepatic nitrogen disposal and functional recovery in the same causal comparison.

**Missing outcomes stress test.** The rounded ER means and analyzed sizes uniquely imply 101 and 217 observed visits. The crude ratio of means is **0.4945**. At equal planned follow-up, the five omitted children would have to average **33.4** visits each for the crude mean ratio to reach 1.00. Assigning each the retained active arm's maximum of 16 gives **0.675**, and assigning the retained control maximum of 41 gives **1.142**. These are sensitivity scenarios, not imputed values or replications of the offset-adjusted analysis. Informative loss, variable follow-up, and open care decisions remain plausible sources of bias.

**Valproate dispersion audit.** Table 1 recomputes the published mean drops, but the paired sample SDs are **62.03 and 66.35 µmol/L**, versus the reported **29 and 47.5**. Table 1 gives 2 h mean **98.32** and SD **35.21**, alongside the text's **98.8 ± 35.2**. Median paired drops are **42 and 81 µmol/L**. The 16 with no additional listed nitrogen-directed agent have mean drops of **42.44 and 79.94**. The nine with a listed co-intervention started higher (166.67 vs 142.63 µmol/L), with mean drops **71.67 and 104.11**. Their 24 h means are similar (62.56 vs 62.69). This is a descriptive trajectory. It cannot isolate adjunct use, background care, valproate PK or changes in ammonia production. Only 12 and nine respectively had 24 h and 48 h valproate measurements. The aggregate replay avoids assuming that published measures of dispersion are paired SDs.

## What the tracer supports

Seven people with propionic acidemia were studied metabolically stable in the same pre/post order. At three days, modeled peak plasma **[¹³C]urea rose 2.2 to 3.8 µmol/L**. The plasma precursor curve (labeled CO₂/bicarbonate after [1-¹³C]acetate) stayed similar. Grand mean ammonia changed **59 to 43 µmol/L** (Results p=0.018) and glutamine **552 to 331 µmol/L**. Bulk plasma urea (reported as BUN) changed **4.04 to 4.61 mmol/L** (p=0.061), with concentration also sensitive to clearance. The human study separates new product appearance and pool concentration. It lacks a placebo period and a functional endpoint. [Ah Mew et al., Methods, Results and Figs. 2A–B and 3](https://doi.org/10.1542/peds.2010-0008)

I digitized the *group model lines* in Fig. 2 to test a precursor-only explanation. On the 0–240 min window the labeled urea concentration area was **1.74-fold** after versus before and the systemic precursor enrichment area was **0.96-fold**. As a sensitivity index, dividing the two ratios gives **1.80**. Starting at minute 15 or 30 changes the index to **1.80** or **1.79**. These are graphical areas and a quotient, not units of urea flux. The systemic precursor did not rise in proportion to the observed product. Unmeasured hepatic mitochondrial precursor enrichment, urea distribution/clearance and sequential time remain possible modifiers. In WWOX, acetate oxidation can itself change, so an uncorrected [¹³C]urea response would be especially ambiguous. [Digitized points](propionic_tracer_plot.tsv) · [Window sensitivity](propionic_tracer_sensitivity.tsv)

Yudkoff et al. supplied labeled bicarbonate directly, bypassing acetate oxidation, in **six sessions among five healthy adults**, one studied twice. At 300 min, collapsing repeated sessions by person, the mean observed/predicted labeled urea ratio is **1.16**, with **4/5** people above the early-line prediction. At 270 min the mean is **1.19**. The extrapolation uses the early time course in the same sessions and lacks a time-matched placebo. Basal ammonia or total urea did not shift consistently, and a biochemical response in healthy subjects cannot by itself identify NAGS insufficiency. [Yudkoff et al., Methods, §2.1, Fig. 2 and Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ymgme.2009.07.010) · [Repeated-session replay](healthy_bicarbonate.tsv)

## WWOX transfer and discriminating observation

**Data.** Held P17 mouse bulk cortex and hippocampus show lower GLUL (Δ log₂(1+TPM) **−0.30** and **−0.49**) and lower SLC1A2 (**−0.63** and **−0.75**), each direction stable to single-library omission. Human organoids have higher GLUL (**+0.46**) despite lower SLC1A2 (**−0.30**, log₂(1+CPM)). Organoid CPS1 is detectable (6.97 to 14.77 CPM). OTC is sparse (0 to 0.32 CPM) and ARG1 was unobserved in the held organoid samples. None of these is a hepatic assay. Published Wwox hepatocyte-directed deletion supplies lipid and glucose readouts, with no ureagenic challenge established in the held analyses. The literature's rat-liver valproyl-CoA assay supports NAGS inhibition as a candidate explanation for valproate-associated nitrogen handling and leaves human WWOX translation open. [WWOX bulk replay](wwox_bulk_nitrogen.tsv) · [Held hepatic Wwox deletion study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30755385/) · [Held liver lipid study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24871327/) · [Rat hepatic NAGS primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21147182/)

**Inference and hypothesis.** A reversible increase in ammonia could compound impaired inhibition in a susceptible WWOX developmental stage. A separate mouse hyperammonemia experiment connects astrocyte potassium buffering to neuronal chloride and GABA reversal. This supports a downstream signal to measure. It cannot establish a WWOX ammonium burden, astrocytic enzyme activity or the direction of circuit response. Low neural OTC together with absent organoid ARG1 weakens a hypothesis of direct recruitment of a complete neural urea cycle. Prespecify a hepatic intervention and test the brain consequence at a controlled ammonia exposure. [Rangroo Thrane et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24240184/)

**Verification and counterproof.** In a defined hepatic WWOX comparison, quantify new labeled urea production relative to measured precursor enrichment, total urea export, ammonia handling and ATP across matched metabolic states. Include background valproate state explicitly and a nitrogen tracer in the hepatic preparation as a countercheck to acetate oxidation. If reserve is NAG-limited, labeled urea output should rise before ammonia/glutamine burdens fall while ATP and downstream pathway capacity are preserved. A fall in ammonia without increased labeled urea redirects attention to production, nitrogen shunting or clearance. For circuit attribution, compare WWOX and controls at the same pH and measured extracellular ammonia trajectory, using evoked inhibition or chloride/potassium readouts with an independent function endpoint. Restoration should track reduced ammonia exposure and lose its apparent drug dependence once ammonia is matched. A persistent effect at fixed ammonia would require its own direct-mechanism and exposure evidence.

**Pediatric feasibility and checkpoint.** The oral suspension tablet has clinically documented use down to birth in specific indications. In the trial label, adverse reactions use **episode** denominators, such as vomiting 3/42 vs 1/48 and lethargy/stupor 2/42 vs 1/48. Renal function changes systemic exposure (label AUC geometric mean ratio **2.8** for moderate and **6.9** for severe impairment versus matched controls). Product formulation and pediatric precedent support measurement feasibility. They do not bridge to routine WWOX antiseizure polytherapy, direct neural exposure or long-term benefit. Reopen the restricted question if a reproducible ammonia burden coincides with a change in labeled ureagenesis and standardized, temporally linked function after controlling acute recovery and co-interventions. The evidence limits present interest to this secondary phenotype. A further review would be decision-relevant on availability of time-indexed valproate withdrawal, episode clustering details or a hepatic WWOX flux dataset.
