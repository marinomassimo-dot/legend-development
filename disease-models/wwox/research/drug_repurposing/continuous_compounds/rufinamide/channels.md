# A voltage shift in NaV1.1 and recovery kinetics across four isoforms

**Parent compound, 100 µM.** Recalculation of Table 1 from Gilchrist et al. uses the authors' matched DMSO control. Rufinamide shifted half-activation by +7.6 mV for human NaV1.1, +2.2 for NaV1.2, +0.2 for NaV1.3 and −1.4 for NaV1.6 in frog oocytes. Only the NaV1.1 activation shift carried the authors' significance marker. For NaV1.6 half-inactivation shifted +5.1 mV. Fast-inactivation recovery time constants increased by 34%, 49%, 42% and 48%, respectively. Thus the parent compound has a selective activation effect alongside a less selective recovery effect. These distinct voltage- and interval-dependent effects need not affect synaptic recruitment in the same direction. Rodent seizure protection and the neuronal firing threshold shift studied with the more selective compound B are evidence about a different molecule. **WWOX context.** In juvenile hippocampal nuclei assigned to broad inhibitory and excitatory classes (GSE158684), control→KO Scn1a was 440→511 and 231→262 counts per million. Scn8a was 288→309 and 433→476, respectively. The direction of Scn1a in either nuclear class differs from the earlier whole-hippocampus contrast. One pool per condition and nuclear RNA cannot determine membrane channel density or susceptibility. In adult P47T hippocampus, Scn1a effects were +0.06 and −0.48 in females and males (within-sex mean log2-expression contrasts); age and variant differ. The high relative Scn1a abundance in the broad inhibitory class in both juvenile pools motivates measuring interneuron recruitment directly. The data do not establish that rufinamide preferentially disables these cells. **Exposure bridge.** A reviewer analysis in the FDA pediatric file reports model-predicted median concentrations of 16.5, 19.6 and 19.3 µg/mL for ages 1–<4, 4–12 and 13–18 in its maximum-dose scenario. Using 238.2 g/mol and an assumed free serum fraction of 0.66 gives approximate free plasma values of 45.7, 54.3 and 53.5 µM (µg/mL × 1000 × 0.66 / 238.2). Matching a nominal 100 µM protein-free bath in free plasma alone would require about 36.1 µg/mL total under those assumptions. These are modeled medians; they do not bound an individual peak or establish unbound brain exposure. May et al.'s primary analysis of 292 serum samples from 119 patients found a concentration-dependent association of valproate with higher rufinamide trough concentrations. In the native mouse hippocampal primary study, the estimated dissociation constant for an intermediate inactivated state was about 40 µM; this statistic and a test at a single 100 µM concentration describe different experiments. **Case evidence and decision.** The held extraction contains three rufinamide exposures across two WWOX reports. The report listing ineffective therapies includes rufinamide alongside six other agents. The other two entries have no stated individual outcome. The addendum annotates these same three exposures. The sources cannot furnish a drug-specific response rate. Retain the compound as active. Next compare interval-dependent effects in native mouse neurons and the rufinamide response in human Dup15q neurons against the specific physiological defect measured in WWOX. Test the interaction at matched voltage and free exposure; record any deficit in inhibitory firing concurrently. A reference genotype whose sodium inactivation is displaced at baseline might respond differently from WWOX.
