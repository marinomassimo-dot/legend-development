# Rufinamide, burst duration and recruitment of inhibitory neurons

**Why now.** The clinical trial in Lennox–Gastaut syndrome measured tonic-atonic drop attacks, and direct WWOX records include previous exposure. Neither establishes selective efficacy on WWOX seizures or development. This candidate asks a different question from sustained-firing suppression by lacosamide: can the duration of a depolarizing burst and the interval between bursts predict a selective effect on recruitment of WWOX circuits? [Primary syndrome trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18401024/) · [Existing WWOX case extraction](../../advisor_pilot_20261113/brainstorm_20261116/it_i2_semiology_20261116/extraction_v1/exposures.tsv)

**Opening discriminating comparison.** Native mouse hippocampal sodium currents and recombinant human isoforms offer potentially competing results. Compare the parent rufinamide with its own vehicle at equivalent free concentration. Retrieve the channel activation and recovery curves by isoform and the duration dependence of inactivation in primary hippocampal cells. Keep parent results distinct from those of the structural analogue in the same paper. In WWOX and matched corrected networks predict preferential suppression of recurrent excitation only if, for activity patterns observed in WWOX, reduced recruitment of excitatory cells exceeds concurrent loss of inhibitory firing. Match starting voltage, pulse count and duty cycle where appropriate, vary the temporal organization of bursts and record each cell class' absolute evoked spikes, recruitment latency, inhibitory charge and conduction. Reversal of the selectivity when intervals change would favor state-dependent gating; disproportionate loss of interneuron firing across patterns would weaken the intervention. A shorter population event alone cannot establish recovery of circuit function. These are proposed tests. [Native-current study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35063442/) · [Parent and analogue study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24635129/)
