# A neuronal rescue in Dup15q changes the WWOX countertest

**Decision.** Park rufinamide for WWOX-specific trial prioritization. Its pediatric antiseizure experience makes syndrome-specific seizure control a plausible clinical comparator. The human neuronal result tested here does not establish a WWOX sodium-inactivation defect or developmental rescue. The three recorded WWOX exposures supply no attributable response rate. [Parent channels, exposures and direct case records](channels.md)

**What the drug corrected in the human model.** Elamin et al. studied one Dup15q iPSC line and its isogenic corrected line. The culture protocol yields predominantly glutamatergic neurons. The study did not present a drug response separately for inhibitory and excitatory cells. At week 21, Fig. 4A and accompanying Results give a steady-state sodium inactivation midpoint of −36.6 mV before and −42.5 mV after acute 100 µM rufinamide (seven Dup15q cells). The corrected, vehicle-treated comparator was −40.7 mV (nine cells). The shift was −5.9 mV and crossed the comparator. Absolute deviation fell from 4.1 to 1.8 mV, about a 56% reduction. This is a calculation on fitted group values. Fig. 2 gives different numerical midpoints for its baseline comparison, which must not replace the Fig. 4 reference. No drug-treated corrected group is shown in Fig. 4. Neither normalization nor a genotype-by-drug interaction follows from a nonsignificant difference against the untreated comparator. In Fig. 4F–G at week 22, drug reduced the elevated spike amplitude and firing and altered fourth-to-first burst firing with 10 ms gaps. The Results report no such burst effect with a 1 s gap and no drug effect on persistent sodium current. Main Fig. 4 inspected directly; the supplemental document for the latter results was not available at the checked routes. [Primary study, Results and Fig. 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10529833/) · [Derived contrasts](transfer.tsv)

**What WWOX actually shares.** In the neuronal Wwox knockout, Breton et al. measured superficial neocortical pyramidal cells. More spikes occurred at fixed injected current. After aligning current to each cell's rheobase, the frequency–current slope difference was nonsignificant (WT 0.184, KO 0.204 Hz/pA, reported p = 0.3669). In Table 1, resting voltage shifted +5.63 mV and input resistance increased 40.15 MΩ. First-spike amplitude at rheobase was already lower (76.30→68.67 mV), the opposite within-model direction from Dup15q. Interneuron amplitude was also lower (60.78→45.03 mV), but its reference pools WT and heterozygotes and contains only five cells from four mice, compared with six from five KO mice. Pyramidal WT and KO groups contained 13 cells from five animals and 40 from 15. The amplitudes measured in the two papers are not directly calibrated to each other. Breton did not measure the sodium-inactivation midpoint in this comparison. [WWOX primary Table 1, Fig. 6 and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609180/) · [Prior laboratory figure audit](../../../deepdive_manifests/PMID34634460.json)

**A quantitative alternative.** Dividing threshold-minus-rest by input resistance gives a passive current proxy of 345→226 pA for pyramidal cells and 178→241 pA for the interneuron comparison. These are ratios of group means under a linear passive approximation. They are neither observed rheobases nor estimates of animal-level effects. They illustrate how the same test current can bias recruitment in opposite directions between cell classes, even without a genotype difference in sodium gating. Collect absolute spike amplitude, recruitment, conduction and inhibitory charge at both native and matched starting voltage, and at current relative to rheobase. Compare short and long interburst intervals and drug versus vehicle in *both* genotypes at directly measured unbound exposure. A selective normalization of a demonstrable WWOX kinetic defect with preserved inhibitory recruitment would reopen the question. Fewer bursts alongside smaller already-attenuated spikes or failing inhibitory propagation would weaken it. [Reproduction from the two original articles](transfer.py)

**Clinical feasibility.** The original Lennox–Gastaut trial used tonic-atonic drop frequency. Its result has limited transport to WWOX cohorts with other seizure types, and supplies no developmental endpoint. An oral suspension exists. Valproate co-treatment can change exposure, and the US label identifies QT shortening and a contraindication in familial short-QT syndrome. Molecular classification, ascertainable seizure type, background therapy and measured exposure would condition interpretation of a future WWOX study.
