# Calcitriol | Oligodendrocyte VDR response and the first myelin sheath

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Disposition.** Retain calcitriol as a conditional VDR comparator and defer pediatric WWOX study priority. Positive assays use oligodendrocyte differentiation markers or axon–myelin colocalization at high nominal concentrations, with a clear medium-history dependence in one study. The next feasible decision is to qualify active-hormone measurement near endogenous concentrations in the juvenile preparation and then test a selective glial response. ## Direct WWOX baseline and RNA comparison

Repudi et al. describe developmental hypomyelination after neuronal Wwox deletion, with P18 myelin immunostaining, P17 ultrastructure and impaired callosal conduction, and structural deficits in WWOX-null human oligocortical spheroids at week 30. Fig. 6 directly varied the genotype of sensory DRG neurons with WT OPCs on both sides. At matched imaging area, reported counts came from two experiments and four cultures per group. Fig. 6D gives approximate medians of **45% myelinating OL morphology on WT DRGs and 28% on Wwox-null DRGs**. This is the fraction of scored cells, not the fraction of initially eligible axons that acquired a first stable sheath. Oligodendrocyte-specific deletion in a separate in vivo comparison showed no major P17 MBP staining change. The published comparisons do not constitute a complete neuron-by-OPC genotype factorial. ## Which exposure comparisons are informative

**Measured human reference.** The nine-child paired pharmacokinetic subset of Jones et al. included seven crossover participants aged 1.8–16 years and two additional children. The measured oral mean peak was **116 ± 23 pmol/L** (0.116 nM total serum) during peritoneal dialysis, with oral half-life 27.4 h. The FDA review's Table 9 lists nine patients and sex totals for only seven. The primary abstract resolves why the counts differ. Table 9 describes a separate single-dose pediatric renal cohort (Klaus et al., n = 10), peak approximately 125 pg/mL or 0.30 nM. Against the Jones measurement, the nominal culture conditions 10, 50 and 100 nM are **86-, 431- and 862-fold** larger. Protein binding, tissue partition and the constant exposure window differ. These are scale checks rather than efficacy or safety margins. [Jones primary abstract, PMID 7923966](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7923966/) · [FDA review, PDF p. 12, Table 9](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/nda/98/021068a_clinphrm.pdf) · [Label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d1013494-bf62-4e68-9f0c-e6571137f20f)

**Tissue assay check.** In older adult postmortem tissue, Xuan et al. measured mean calcitriol of 30.7 pg/g in anterior watershed, 19.2 pg/g in middle temporal cortex and 18.5 pg/g in cerebellum (153 donors, each region sampled once per donor). The values correspond to approximately **44–74 pM total-tissue equivalent** if wet brain density is set to 1 kg/L solely for a dimensional comparison. Their 100-mg extraction reports a 2.5 pg/g detection limit and its lowest reported precision control at 10 pg/g, with 13.6% interday RSD and 51% mean extraction recovery at that control. At unchanged absolute detection mass and effective recovery, a 10-mg input would have a detection equivalent of **25 pg/g**, and require **100 pg/g** for the same analyte input as the low QC. Thus a 10-mg sample at the published temporal-cortex or cerebellar mean would be below the illustrative detection equivalent. The 10-mg input and adult means are scenarios, and the QC equivalent is not a validated lower limit of quantification. [Xuan, Fig. 2 and Table 1, PMID 39224141](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11367554/) · [Mass and density replay](quantitative.tsv)

**Independent rodent assay control.** Stephenson et al. resolved the 3-epimer in a single pool of whole-brain extracts from twelve seven-week-old mice. Their conservative Table 3 gives standard LLOD 1 **pg/mL** and LLOQ 12.5 **pg/mL**, whereas the Discussion gives lower figures. Use Table 3 for planning. At the stated approximately 0.15 g/mL extraction ratio and 400 µL stacked loading, the latter corresponds to 5 pg loaded and at most 60 mg of original tissue per stack before extra dilution. With perfect recovery, that is about 83 pg/g, or about 500 pg/g if a 10-mg sample must be diluted to fill the same stack. These bounds depend on method geometry and not biological concentrations. Neither this pooled validation nor the adult human assay establishes performance in small juvenile white-matter samples. Require small-input recovery, a mix of authentic calcitriol and 3-epimer standards, isotope dilution and a compartment-appropriate blank. [Stephenson Methods, Table 3 and Fig. 4, PMID 36705732](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9928823/)

## Inference, discriminating observation and disposition

**Inference.** The existing evidence supports a context-dependent transition to an MBP-positive state. Faster transition can coincide with a smaller precursor reserve or normal lineage markers on axons that still fail to acquire a stable sheath. The WWOX neuron-side constraint and pharmacological glial response can therefore coexist. There is no measured juvenile free exposure or demonstrated glial VDR functional deficit.

**Hypothesis.** A calcitriol-responsive subset of stage-matched OPCs could bypass part of the Wwox-deficient axonal constraint. At matched available axons and measured calcitriol, it should improve first persistent sheath formation on deficient axons while conserving the precursor pool and conduction reserve. Higher VDR or MBP expression can accompany a persistent neuron-side failure. Mineral changes, developmental timing and survival remain competing routes.

**Experiment.** First qualify detectability and quantification in weighed, unpooled juvenile WWOX and corrected samples, separating endogenous baseline and administered exposure where appropriate. In a crossed axon-genotype by glial-genotype comparison, fix carrier protein, baseline calcitriol or other contributing steroids, thyroid-hormone content and extracellular calcium and phosphate. Qualify an inducible, VDR-dependent early response such as CYP24A1 if it is measurable in the relevant glia. Compare calcitriol and vehicle at matched local exposure, and include a lineage-restricted VDR perturbation with its own baseline and dynamic-range control. Track axon availability and caliber, survival, Olig2 stages and renewal, nascent and persistent sheaths per initially unmyelinated eligible axon, and patterned conduction. A response confined to markers or colocalization, or lost once precursor reserves or mineral changes are matched, lowers priority. Persistent structural and functional recovery on deficient axons with dependence on glial VDR raises priority. An independent review would be decision-changing when the juvenile assay or lineage-specific endpoint is available and its recovery or denominator is disputed.

**Pediatric feasibility.** Rocaltrol 0.25 or 0.5 µg capsules and 1 µg/mL oral solution have experience in defined renal and parathyroid indications. The label distinguishes pediatric predialysis evidence from the lack of established safety and effectiveness in pediatric dialysis, despite small measured dialysis PK. Hypercalcemia, hyperphosphatemia and renal effects constrain interpretation. Older phenytoin and phenobarbital interaction information distinguishes loss of endogenous 25(OH)D substrate and uncertain active-hormone exposure. It cannot predict the magnitude of interactions with antiseizure polytherapy in WWOX. Assay-defined glial response and myelin or conduction endpoints precede any judgment on pediatric WWOX feasibility.
