Calcitriol is the active vitamin D hormone, evaluated here as a defined molecule for a developmental myelin comparison. The existing WWOX neuron–oligodendrocyte lineage swaps give a baseline to test a glial bypass. Existing compound dispositions and active assignments have no dedicated calcitriol task. **Hypothesis.** If VDR response in a stage-matched WWOX oligodendrocyte can bypass part of an axonal developmental bottleneck, exposure-matched calcitriol should restore first stable sheaths on Wwox-deficient axons with preserved axon survival, recruitment and conduction. A receptor-specific perturbation in the glia should remove that gain.

**Countertest.** Differentiation-marker induction can coexist with persistent failure to wrap Wwox-deficient axons. In developmental systems with axonal or mineral perturbations, an apparent benefit can also reflect altered survival, calcium or lineage composition rather than a glial VDR bypass.

**Prespecified comparisons.** Audit receptor dependence in purified or lineage-defined OPCs and separate developmental myelination from injury repair. Prefer ultrastructure, eligible axon denominator and functional outcomes over positive marker counts alone. **Decision.** The minimum change in priority requires local calcitriol exposure, glial VDR dependence, a native first-sheath effect at fixed extracellular minerals and a functional effect with the first response preserved. A response limited to OPC differentiation or an adult injury model retains calcitriol as a conditional comparator.
