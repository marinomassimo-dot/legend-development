# Sodium selenite, selenium supply and phospholipid hydroperoxide reduction

**Opening question, before the screen.** Could inorganic selenium supply limit the ability of surviving WWOX neurons to reduce phospholipid hydroperoxides through GPX4? The existing glutathione and ubiquinol dossiers provide comparisons for availability of reducing equivalents and lipid-radical protection. They do not measure selenium incorporated into GPX4. Compare impaired selenium transport (SELENOP/LRP8), incorporation (SEPSECS), antioxidant capacity (GPX4 and TXNRD1) and availability of peroxidizable lipids (ACSL4). Inspect selenium in baseline medium and background antioxidant supplements before interpreting any response. [Glutathione comparator](../acetylcysteine/mechanism.md) · [Prior medium audit](../../myelin_premises_20261108/verify/audit_b.md) · [Brain delivery study lead](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24760755/)

**Discriminating prediction.** In an initially selenium-replete, matched WWOX and control system, a selective supply bottleneck predicts lower active selenoenzyme followed by greater incorporation of tracer selenium into GPX4, recovery of hydroperoxide-reducing activity, a fall in oxidized phospholipids and improved neuronal function after repletion. Simple rescue of both genotypes from an experimentally depleted medium supports a nutritional rescue common to both. A fall in bulk oxidant signal without recovery of GPX4 activity leaves other mechanisms open. Distinguish exposure of brain from dietary intake, total selenium from its protein-bound form, and toxicity from a therapeutic window. No WWOX response is claimed at opening.

**Screen rule.** Compare the sign of each untreated bulk contrast across two mouse systems and a human organoid, including leave-one-library-out ranges, and preserve differences in reference symbols. Use class-specific neuronal measures if they would change interpretation. Transcript directions nominate the next measurement and cannot identify deficient selenium or loss of enzymatic activity.

## First WWOX comparison

GPX4, SEPSECS and TXNRD1 RNA were lower in untreated WWOX organoids, mouse cortex and mouse hippocampus. Leave-one-library-out contrasts retained the negative TXNRD1 sign in all three. A cortical GPX4 omission and a hippocampal SEPSECS omission could erase their signs. SELENOP was higher in cortex and organoids and lower in hippocampus. ACSL4, which influences the pool of susceptible phospholipids, fell in cortex and organoids. Reduced peroxide clearance and reduced peroxidizable substrate have competing consequences. The RNA alone does not establish net lipid oxidation. [Bulk values, accessions and omission bounds](bulk.tsv)

In the separate hippocampal nuclear preparation, excitatory Gpx4 was 29.0 versus 29.8 counts per million in control and knockout, and inhibitory Gpx4 was 46.6 versus 41.6. Across assigned classes the aggregate rose from 36.9 to 40.7. Within-class and class-mixture contributions were +2.71 and +1.11, respectively. Strong bulk downregulation cannot be assigned to all surviving neurons. Nuclear RNA and whole tissue sample different compartments and the nuclear dataset has one biological pool per arm. **Medium check.** The cerebral organoid protocol names Gibco N-2 17502048 at 1% in induction, then 0.5% N-2 in differentiation. The maturation recipe retains that supplement and changes B27, CHIR, ascorbate and buffer. The current supplier formulation for 17502048 lists 0.52 mg/L selenite at 173 g/mol in the 100× concentrate. Multiplying the stated stock molarity (3.006 µM) by 0.01 and 0.005 gives nominal N-2 contributions of 30.1 and 15.0 nM, assuming use of the same stock formulation. Other medium components, historical lot, speciation, tissue uptake and depletion between feeds were not quantified. A simple zero-selenium baseline would mischaracterize this comparator; adequacy is still a functional question.
