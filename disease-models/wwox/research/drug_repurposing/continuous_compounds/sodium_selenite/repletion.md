**Cell protection.** Immortalized N27 cells of rat mesencephalic origin received selenium before and during either of two oxidative challenges. Table 1 gives selenite EC50s of 38 nM for erastin and 15 nM for RSL3, against 925 and 489 nM for selenate. These are nominal additions in RPMI with 10% serum. The 16-hour pretreatment preceded a further 24 hours with challenge and compound. The endpoint was MTT reduction (five independent experiments with four replicates each). The chemical distinction and timing matter. Neither the unmeasured selenium in the serum nor a rescue begun after established cell dysfunction is resolved by this comparison. The 15 nM organoid-medium contribution and the 15 nM RSL3 EC50 describe different quantities. Subtracting them would have no biological interpretation. **Mechanism test.** At 1 µM supplementation the N27 study measured GPX1 and GPX4 protein and assayed lysate activity with tert-butyl hydroperoxide. That concentration is about 26 and 67 times the two selenite viability EC50s. Increased mixed GPX activity at that exposure cannot identify the enzyme mediating the lower-concentration benefit. Rescue during RSL3 exposure could reflect residual or newly synthesized GPX4 or another route; it cannot establish complete target blockade. An independent assay validated a phosphatidylcholine hydroperoxide readout for GPX4 against a second peroxide readout for GPX1. Its paired activity plateau was obtained with selenomethionine in cancer cell lines; that numerical exposure cannot be transferred to selenite-treated neurons. Compare substrate-specific activity at the same exposures and times as the functional change. **Delivery versus incorporation.** In Burk et al., global loss of Sepp1 or its receptor in selenium-deficient mice was accompanied by severe neurological dysfunction at whole-brain selenium near 35 ng/g. With Sepp1 removed specifically in hepatocytes and the brain delivery machinery retained, brain selenium reached 12 ± 4 ng/g at 40 weeks (n=5). Hind-limb weakness appeared from 22 weeks, while the severe global-knockout phenotype was absent. Ages, genotype and deprivation duration differed. A single tissue concentration cannot rank neuronal sufficiency across these animals. In a separate family study, three affected siblings (ages 9, 11 and 18, homozygous SECISBP2 R540Q) and two unaffected heterozygous siblings received an oral selenite phase after a washout from selenium-rich yeast. In affected siblings, selenoprotein P increased slightly while circulating GPX3 activity and the thyroid abnormalities failed to improve. The serum selenium rise with yeast was a separate result. The human study measured extracellular GPX3, not neural GPX4. Taken together these comparators separate cellular delivery, incorporation and peripheral biochemical response. [Burk et al., Figs. 6–8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4101661/) · [Schomburg et al., Fig. 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2858371/)

**Pediatric feasibility and decision.** Enteral sodium selenite has a nutritional precedent in preterm formula. The published abstract of one three-week comparison reports no end-of-study difference across groups in blood selenium or peroxidase activity despite different intakes. A separate epilepsy cohort reports falling serum selenium on ketogenic diet. It selected 12-month diet completers and excluded children deficient at baseline. These abstracts identify exposure and nutritional confounders; neither is evidence of neural rescue. A five-sibling experiment has little ability to bound rare or chronic harms. Total intake from formula, food and all supplements, age-dependent upper intake levels and background ketogenic treatment would matter in a pediatric study. Seizure medication interactions and age-specific unbound CNS selenite exposure remain unresolved here. [Preterm feeding study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1908895/) · [Ketogenic-diet cohort](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27873289/) · [EFSA selenium assessment](https://doi.org/10.2903/j.efsa.2023.7704)

Park sodium selenite for an unselected WWOX trial. Reopen for **a selective increase in GPX4 phospholipid hydroperoxide turnover coupled to improved neuronal function** at a documented selenium-containing baseline, with oxidized-to-parent lipid ratios, parallel GPX1 and a matched control genotype. A measured secondary dietary deficiency is a separate clinical phenotype. Whole-brain or serum selenium, a single surviving-cell signal or rescue from an intentionally selenium-depleted medium would leave the WWOX-specific question open.
