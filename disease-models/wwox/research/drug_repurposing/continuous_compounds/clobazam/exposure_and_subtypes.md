# Does a higher norclobazam fraction preferentially potentiate α2-containing receptors?

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Decision.** The clinical interaction shifts the blood mixture toward norclobazam. Two recombinant assays predict a small increase in α2 current after the change when α1 potentiation is held fixed. The sign reverses under some parameter choices within the published dispersions. This weakens the case for subtype-selective rescue of the WWOX circuit. Keep clinical antiseizure activity, sedation, preserved evoked transmission and WWOX-specific rescue as separate outcomes.

## Matched-current comparison

Ralvenius Fig. 1C–D directly resolves the previous audit's open normalization question. The vehicle point is zero percent potentiation on both curves. The means in Table 1 therefore describe an increment over GABA alone. In Hammer, subtracting 100 converts the total responses to increments. Ralvenius used rat α1β2γ2 and α2β3γ2 receptors and GABA EC10. Hammer used human α1β2γ2S and α2β2γ2S receptors and GABA EC20. Differences in β subunit, agonist level and assay limit attribution to α alone. [Ralvenius, Fig. 1C–D and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4981430/) · [Held independent comparison and transcribed Hammer data](../../gabaa_subtype_adv004_20261114/verify/audit.md)

Use a two-ligand shared-site model with Hill slope one. For each receptor let x and y be the parent and metabolite concentrations divided by their respective functional EC50, with fitted maxima Ep and Em. Predicted percent potentiation is (Ep·x + Em·y)/(1+x+y). This interpolates individual responses under assumed competition. Functional EC50s are empirical scales and the single-drug assays did not fit this mixture model. For each composition, scale both concentrations until α1 gives the same increment. Apply those concentrations to the α2 curve. Relative partition is the ratio of CNS-to-free-plasma partition for metabolite versus parent. Use 1 as the reference and 0.25 and 4 as unmeasured sensitivity scenarios. The quantities matched here are receptor responses. Native network inhibition and physiological GABA release remain independent readouts.

| Matched α1 increase | Hammer α2, before → after | Ralvenius α2, before → after | α2 difference, Hammer / Ralvenius |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 20% | 33.03 → 34.39% | 38.29 → 39.41% | +1.37 / +1.12 percentage points |
| 40% | 65.13 → 67.68% | 74.58 → 76.95% | +2.56 / +2.36 percentage points |

At a fixed 20% α1 response, the two central estimates span +0.32 to +3.08 points across partition assumptions. We then perturbed each of eight fitted parameters up and down by its published dispersion, independently, for 256 combinations per assay and condition. With equal partition the difference ranges from −0.61 to +5.33 points for Hammer and −1.91 to +8.94 for Ralvenius. These bounds describe chosen parameter combinations. They do not estimate confidence, likelihood or a minimum biologically relevant difference. Hammer reports SEM (on the logarithmic scale for potency) and Ralvenius SD. The parameter correlations and mixture-specific Hill slopes are unavailable. The small central differences warrant a direct mixture measurement before claiming preferential recruitment.

**Counterevidence and WWOX prediction.** In cultured rat cerebral neurons, parent and metabolite produced similar concentration-dependent potentiation with 10 µM GABA. In a separate recombinant α1 preparation, cannabidiol directly increased currents when added to either benzodiazepine at 10 µM per agent. A post-cannabidiol change in a patient cannot isolate metabolite composition. In WWOX models the held data show reduced spontaneous inhibitory current amplitude and frequency, with open explanations involving cell maturation, synaptic input and chloride handling. A decisive result would be a reproducible difference between mixtures in evoked pyramidal spike probability at preserved physiological transmission and equal reference inhibition, specific to the WWOX-deficient versus corrected cells. Compare with cannabidiol alone and add-back, measure actual bath concentrations, preserve native chloride, and measure interneuron recruitment alongside principal-cell firing. A matching fall in spontaneous and evoked activity, or a mixture effect of similar size in controls, would lower priority. [Native neuronal experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8764810/) · [Anderson et al., Fig. 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6900043/) · [WWOX inhibitory-current audit](../../inhibitory_defect_sci47_20261112/consumer_delta.md)

## Pediatric feasibility and remaining clinical question

In Oliver et al., Table 2, six of 13 WWOX medication histories list clobazam. Four mark it current and two mark both clobazam and cannabidiol current. These are counts of recorded exposure. The table gives no longitudinal seizure denominator or parent/metabolite measurements for attribution. A prior laboratory reading identified a separate WWOX publication reporting partial response to several antiseizure agents. It cannot isolate clobazam. [Oliver et al., Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.17542) · [Held primary-source attribution check](../topiramate/clinical_timeline.md)

In a separate uncontrolled pediatric drug-resistant epilepsy cohort, 43 of 90 children initially became seizure-free or had fewer seizures. The authors reported subsequent tolerance in 28 of these 43 (65.1%). Treatment selection, co-medication, baseline severity and escalation can confound associations with higher concentrations. The seizure-free and reduced-seizure subgroups had different starting counts. Serum and salivary sampling did not yield a validated therapeutic target in that cohort. Prior pediatric use supports feasibility of observational follow-up. It does not establish a free-exposure target or sustained response in WWOX. [Bardy et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1928610/)

**Durability question examined below.** Reconcile the pediatric cohort with the published clobazam extension in Lennox–Gastaut syndrome, keeping initial responders, all entrants, ongoing participants, co-medication changes, dose escalation, withdrawal and drop-seizure definitions separate. That comparison can change whether loss of effect is an interpretable endpoint. If it yields no attributable WWOX response or durable genotype-specific circuit signal, park the selective-mixture hypothesis for pediatric trial priority. [Extension study for the next comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27683846/)

## Durability comparison and final disposition

The LGS extension changes the interpretation of the pediatric 28/43 figure. Gidal et al.'s lead-in enrolled ages 2–60 years. Entry into the extension selected trial completers without investigator-attributed probable or definite clobazam adverse events. The extension allowed changes to other antiseizure therapies. Of 267 extension participants, 200 had lead-in responder data for the post hoc analysis. Table 1 then uses a narrower denominator of 85 who had a weight-adjusted dose increase of at least 40% in the responder groups shown. Ten also lost their drop-seizure response category (11.8% of 85). The table shows 1/18, 7/41 and 2/26 in the three displayed rows. Loss of benefit without dose escalation falls outside this definition. The 67-person difference between the extension enrollment and the post hoc analysis does not measure attrition, and the 85 do not comprise all initial responders or all children. A common follow-up risk set and reasons for withdrawals among those at risk cannot be recovered from Table 1. Stable group means do not establish preservation of response within each individual. [Gidal et al., Methods, Results and Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5089527/)

The pediatric cohort's 65.1% and the extension's 11.8% answer different questions. Neither study identifies a WWOX-specific pharmacodynamic tolerance rate. For disease-specific follow-up, define a seizure type and baseline observation window, follow all initial responders including withdrawals, track co-medications, record loss of response and sedation separately, and collect time-matched exposure before attributing loss to tolerance. There is no common denominator for pooling these estimates.

**Disposition.** Park subtype-selective clobazam repurposing for pediatric WWOX trial priority. The direct mixture estimates are small and sign-sensitive, native neuronal currents provide counterevidence, and the WWOX histories establish use without attribution. Reopen for a genotype-specific evoked-circuit advantage at measured free parent/metabolite concentrations, or a time-resolved WWOX response with interpretable co-medications and follow-up. This disposition addresses the new WWOX-selective mechanism. Existing clinical prescribing decisions are outside the comparison.
