# Sodium selenate and tau phosphoregulation in WWOX loss of function

**Selection.** Sodium selenate is the Se(VI) salt under evaluation in adult drug-resistant temporal lobe epilepsy. The prior sodium selenite dossier addressed Se(IV) delivery to selenoenzymes. That dossier supplies a comparator for selenium-dependent redox effects. The new question is whether selenate can alter a WWOX-linked PP2A–GSK3β–tau pathway under verified exposure. Developmental WWOX loss and acquired adult epilepsy require separate evidence. [Adult SeLECT registry](https://anzctr.org.au/Trial/Registration/TrialReview.aspx?id=385697) · [Held sodium selenite analysis](../sodium_selenite/README.md) · [Primary WWOX/GSK3β study](https://doi.org/10.1038/cdd.2011.188)

**Prediction.** If selenate compensates for a WWOX-linked tau phosphoregulation defect, matched-stage WWOX preparations should show a PP2A-dependent fall in the relevant phospho-tau/total tau ratios before recovery of axonal function and late network recruitment. Compare selenate with selenite at matched elemental selenium and characterize the actual local species. A shared improvement that follows GPX4 activity and lipid oxidation with similar shifts in both salts favors a selenium-supply mechanism. Reduced firing with unchanged or increased phospho-tau, impaired axonal function or suppressed initial recruitment favors a separate excitability effect. Normalize phosphosite measurements to total tau and assess baseline protein, maturity and cell state. [Primary counterexample in SH-SY5Y cells, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30781361/)

**Next step.** Reconcile human CSF selenium speciation and molar units. Compare the strongest acquired-epilepsy study and the opposing SH-SY5Y result at verified exposures. Audit held WWOX phosphosite, stage and tissue evidence, then identify which measured exposure and endpoint could separate PP2A-mediated compensation from redox or general circuit suppression.
