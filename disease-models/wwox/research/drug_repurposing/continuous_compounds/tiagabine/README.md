# Tiagabine hydrochloride and activity-dependent GABA clearance
context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Selection.** The laboratory's earlier inhibitory-defect dossier already records tiagabine’s database target. The present analysis adds class-resolved WWOX contrasts and tests what target inhibition does to timed spike transmission. [Existing laboratory record](../../inhibitory_defect_sci47_20261112/output/levers_notes.md) Tiagabine inhibits GAT-1 (SLC6A1) and asks whether the lifetime of *released* GABA limits inhibition. This question is distinct from potentiating a receptor directly. Reduction of uptake could prolong effective inhibition after short recurrent discharges, yet tonic conductance in thalamic relay cells can favor a synchronous rebound. Reuse earlier chloride analyses for developmental context. The marketed hydrochloride is a pediatric epilepsy precedent in older children. [Existing chloride comparison](../taurine/native_chloride.md) · [Regulatory label](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=72b7f357-8827-4a8b-b55a-01fbce47ae80)

**Prediction before tissue extraction.** At matched presynaptic recruitment and physiological intracellular chloride, a clearance-limited WWOX circuit should show prolonged evoked inhibitory charge with tiagabine, preserved or improved suppression of a timed test spike, and recovery of ordinary evoked transmission between bursts. The peak IPSC can remain unchanged. If residual uptake capacity is already small, or GABA release insufficient, the incremental effect of GAT-1 inhibition should be small at matched free drug. In a separate thalamic readout, increased tonic current with increased synchronous rebound would argue against a general benefit even if a hippocampal burst falls. Match stimulus trains and quantify absolute evoked charge, extracellular GABA decay, test-spike probability and post-inhibitory rebound. A change in the sign of a voltage deflection alone cannot determine whether a spike is inhibited.

**Opening comparison.** Check SLC6A1 and the alternative GABA transporter SLC6A11 against inhibitory-terminal markers SLC32A1 and GAD1, and extrasynaptic receptor subunits GABRD and GABRA4. Use the existing three bulk WWOX datasets and the independently prepared hippocampal nuclear pools. Concordant lower bulk target and lower terminal markers can reflect reduced inhibitory tissue. Within-class signals can challenge that interpretation. These data cannot identify transporter current or supply a thalamic readout. [Matched hippocampal primary study lead](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1629773/) · [Absence EEG primary study lead](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7588592/)

**Opening result.** The KO-minus-control difference in mean log2(1 + normalized SLC6A1 RNA) was −0.58 in mouse cortex, −0.95 in mouse hippocampus and +0.74 in human organoids. Each direction survived omission of individual libraries. In organoids, GAD1 and SLC32A1 rose more (+3.03 and +2.49). Thus an increase in bulk transporter RNA can coexist with an even larger change in inhibitory cell or terminal markers. In the separate mouse hippocampal nuclei pool, Slc6a1 fell from 312 to 256 CPM within assigned inhibitory nuclei and rose from 233 to 245 in assigned astrocytes. Aggregation across all assigned classes gave a rise of 8.66 CPM: within-class changes contributed −3.57, while altered shares of RNA depth contributed +12.22. Astrocytic Slc6a11 fell from 162 to 115 CPM. This analysis has one pooled preparation per genotype, with no protein, uptake or thalamic measurement. **Marker sensitivity analysis.** We then calculated the KO-minus-control difference in the within-library log2 ratio of SLC6A1 to each inhibitory marker, after adding 0.1, 1 or 5 normalized units to both genes. With offset 1 the organoid contrasts were −2.29 against GAD1 and −1.74 against SLC32A1, despite the absolute SLC6A1 increase. Both marker ratios fell in all three preparations for every offset and every single-library omission. These transcript ratios describe relative abundance. They neither count inhibitory terminals nor measure uptake per GABA release event, and the magnitude in the organoid depends strongly on the offset. This weakens the inference that higher organoid target RNA favors a larger response to GAT-1 blockade. Direct clearance kinetics after matched release become the discriminating readout. Reproduce with `python3 marker_balance.py /path/to/files marker_balance.tsv`. [18 contrasts and matched omission bounds](marker_balance.tsv)

**Pharmacological countercheck.** The rat hippocampal CA3 primary abstract reports that 10–25 µM tiagabine prolonged evoked monosynaptic inhibition at unchanged IPSP peak amplitude. The mean GABA-A current decay time increased from 16 to 250 ms (15.6-fold); spontaneous miniature events were unaffected. The observed time constant alone cannot specify a 15.6-fold increase in inhibitory charge or spike suppression. In a separate study, eight adult male WAG/Rij rats per group received saline or tiagabine; generalized spike-wave events increased in number and duration, most at the higher exposures. Behavioral observation was unblinded. The authors counted 7–11 Hz events lasting at least one second with cortical EEG. These rat data provide a counterexample to a circuit-independent benefit and do not establish that WWOX seizures are typical absence seizures. [CA3 primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/1629773/) · [Absence EEG study, §§2–3, Figs. 1–2](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0920121195000153)
