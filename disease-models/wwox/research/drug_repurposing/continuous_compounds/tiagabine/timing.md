# Tiagabine, inhibitory timing and free exposure
context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Adult motor cortex.** Werhahn et al. compared healthy adults before and approximately two hours after oral tiagabine. At 3 ms the subthreshold conditioning pulse left a larger test motor-evoked potential (MEP) after drug: 50.4% of the unconditioned response versus 37.1% at baseline, a 13.3 percentage-point reduction in inhibition (all eight participants, P < 0.01). At 10 ms facilitation increased on average, with six of eight going in that direction; the group change was nonsignificant. The cortical silent period lengthened from 158 to 180 ms in eight participants. The reported 160-ms inhibition increased in all three subjects in the dose experiment, where both pulses were suprathreshold. The different conditioning intensities, denominators and subject subsets prevent identification of a pure time-dependent reversal. Presynaptic GABA-B feedback is an interpretation; the TMS readout cannot assign a receptor. No placebo-adjusted effect or WWOX-specific response is available. [Primary study, Methods and Results for Figs. 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2269337/)

**Timed population response.** Jackson et al. compared single pulses with a 100-Hz, 200-ms conditioning train in rat hippocampal CA1. At nominal 20 µM tiagabine, the unconditioned population spike was preserved. After a train, the 200-ms response fell from 35.4% of unconditioned amplitude in control to 7.8% with drug (−27.6 points). At 800 ms it fell from 98.8% to 34.4% (−64.4 points). This measures delayed recovery as well as burst suppression. It is a population response, so neither single-cell spike probability nor human seizure benefit can be recovered from these means. The lowest bath concentration reported in the study range was 2 µM. [Primary paper, field recording methods and Fig. 1 results](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.1999.81.3.1192)

**Exposure and the tracer denominator.** Frankle et al. report 223 ± 152 ng/mL total plasma tiagabine in 22 adult healthy comparators, averaged across samples at 30, 50 and 90 minutes. Using active-moiety mass 375.56 g/mol and the label's 96% human plasma binding gives 0.594 µM total and an estimated 0.0238 µM unbound. The 2 and 20 µM baths are about 84 and 842 times that estimated free plasma mean. These contrasts mix a nominal slice bath and estimated human free plasma; neither is measured human synaptic drug. Plasma binding was not measured for tiagabine in these participants. The separate eight-person PET study reports a change in hippocampal flumazenil binding and a plasma free fraction for the **radiotracer**. Using that fraction for tiagabine would materially overestimate exposure. PET supports a central pharmacodynamic effect, without supplying GAT-1 occupancy or seizure benefit. In a separate rat study the reported CSF/total-serum ratio (0.008–0.010) also differed from free/total serum (0.045); species, sampling and compartment rule out a direct human brain conversion. [Human plasma and PET, Table 2 and protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5070491/) · [Tracer study, Tables 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2754778/) · [FDA label, pp. 1–3](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2016/020646s021lbl.pdf) · [Rat primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15519917/)

**Disposition.** Park for an unselected pediatric WWOX trial. Human pharmacodynamic signals warrant keeping a focal-circuit hypothesis available. To reopen, require matched GABA release and free exposure in a WWOX comparison that shortens pathological recurrent activity while preserving both early test-spike inhibition and recovery of normal evoked transmission. Check for synchronized thalamocortical rebound in a separate relevant preparation. An increased tonic current, longer IPSC or positive PET change alone cannot resolve these opposing effects. Age, background enzyme-inducing medication, and seizure phenotype matter for feasibility. Existing sources support older-child partial-seizure precedent and leave the infant WWOX exposure-response unestablished.
