# Riluzole · free exposure, glutamate handling and WWOX disposition

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · pediatric SMA plasma, pediatric OCD and adult ALS serum, adult traumatic spinal injury CSF (ng/mL or µmol/L), rat astrocyte radiotracer uptake and release, rat medullary currents (µmol/L nominal bath and normalized amplitude), human cortical glutamate-sensor fluorescence, mouse P17 tissue TPM and assigned hippocampal nuclear UMI, human week-15 cerebral organoid CPM.

**Disposition.** Retain riluzole as a conditional comparator for glutamate clearance and activity-dependent suppression. Defer priority for a direct pediatric WWOX trial. Acute uptake and stimulus-dependent astrocytic release can move in the same direction. A smaller synaptic peak alone can reflect reduced neuronal activity, reduced release or faster removal. The held WWOX data leave baseline clearance and a selective, exposure-matched rescue unresolved. [Prespecified prediction](pretest_20261127.md) · [Opening comparison](first_comparison_20261127.md)

## Three outcomes that can look alike

- **Uptake and secondary astrocyte release.** Yoshizumi et al. observed higher radiotracer uptake at low glutamate input and higher provoked radiotracer release in a separate, higher glutamate challenge. The exchanger inhibitor reduced the enhanced calcium response and shifted basal calcium on its own. This supports a possible contribution to the calcium response while leaving the mechanism of tracer release unresolved. TBOA increased extracellular tracer on its own and could not assign the release effect. The recombinant rat EAAT1/2/3 study reports an increase in apparent affinity for all three transporters, which does not establish selective EAAT2 activation. The long-exposure GLT-1 result follows withdrawal of a growth-factor supplement at a different concentration and timescale. Thus low-to-high input and early-to-late phases need separate tests. [Rat astrocytes, Results and Figs. 1, 2 and 7](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3268950/) · [Recombinant primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18036519/) · [Long-exposure results](first_comparison_20261127.md)
- **Reduced synaptic release or postsynaptic gain.** In human epilepsy-surgery cortex, the separate paired optical exposure in two slices from two donors showed a **55%** lower normalized peak with riluzole. The method applied **7 µL of a 200 µmol/L stock**, equivalent to **1.4 nmol** deposited next to the electrode. Sixteen stimulations were averaged within each trace. The stock concentration gives no local free concentration, and the peak is a joint release-and-clearance readout. In rat cortical synaptosomes, riluzole reduced 4-aminopyridine-evoked calcium-dependent release, with an ionomycin control that remained responsive. In P10–23 rat hypoglossal slices the **20 µmol/L** comparison includes decreased miniature EPSC amplitude and a smaller glutamate-evoked inward current, compatible with reduced postsynaptic gain. A current evoked by an externally applied glutamate pulse therefore cannot stand in for the measured extracellular concentration after that pulse. [Human slice sensor study, Fig. 3 and Methods](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-022-07940-8) · [Synaptosome primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15051158/) · [Hypoglossal primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23741042/)
- **Activity-dependent sodium current inhibition.** Ptak et al. measured a **2.4 µmol/L** fit for peak transient sodium-current inhibition for a step from −80 to −20 mV. The **3 µmol/L** comparison reduced both transient and persistent current in the illustrated native rat medullary preparation. The separate **0.3 ± 0.1 µmol/L** estimate (n = 9) describes apparent affinity for the inactivated state, rather than a measured dose-response for intact respiratory rhythm. The authors did not measure the transient concentration-response below 0.1 µmol/L. An unqualified persistent-current-selective interpretation is unsafe across holding voltage, cell type and stimulus history. The repository's **139.4 µmol/L** K2P comparator likewise comes from a native sympathetic-neuron response, so it does not justify a low-exposure astrocytic K2P mechanism. [Ptak Fig. 7, Results pp. 5163–5165](https://www.jneurosci.org/content/jneuro/25/21/5159.full.pdf) · [Held K2P comparison](../../it44_k2p_primary_it55_20261115/README.md)

## WWOX premise and a replayable counterexample

The held bulk SLC1A2 contrasts, KO minus control in mean log₂(1 + expression), are **−0.63** in P17 mouse cortex, **−0.75** in P17 mouse hippocampus and **−0.30** in week-15 human cerebral organoids. Every one-library omission retains the sign. In the mouse hippocampal astrocyte-assigned nuclear pool, Slc1a2 instead rises **4,910 → 5,392 UMI CPM**. Slc1a2 itself is an assignment marker there. In the separate Aqp4+ and Aldh1l1+ gate, which does not select on Slc1a2, the result remains **5,330 → 7,823 CPM** (**1,106 → 2,124** target UMIs, **35 → 43** nuclei). Slc1a3 also rises in that gate. One pool per genotype and different bulk/nuclear denominators forbid a cell-autonomous conclusion. The transcript results support direct measurement of surface transport and baseline clearance. [Held bulk data](../acetylcysteine/bulk.tsv) · [Nuclear counts and independent marker gate](../acetylcysteine/nuclei.tsv) · [Source datasets and regional limitations](../acetylcysteine/mechanism.md)

The synthetic perturbation model has first-order removal `k`, an equal calibrated input `Q`, and a potential delayed release `B`. It compares concentration traces rather than a measured WWOX phenotype. Total area is `(Q + B) / k` after return to baseline. With `Q = 1` and `k = 5 s⁻¹`, baseline area is **0.200** normalized concentration·s. Increasing clearance to `7.5 s⁻¹` yields **0.133** without late release. A separate reduction of the evoked input to `0.7` yields a similar area of **0.140**, while leaving the fixed-input trace at **0.200**. With faster clearance and delayed additional release of `0.6 Q`, area becomes **0.213** even though the 50-ms signal is exactly the same as uptake alone. In this example any addition above `0.5 Q` erases the integrated benefit. It takes both fixed input and late sampling to tell these scenarios apart. These values illustrate identifiability and neither set assay timings nor predict doses or kinetics in WWOX. [Model output](comparison.tsv)

**Discriminating observation.** First establish slowed glutamate clearance or excess late spillover in a matched WWOX cortical neuron–astrocyte preparation against corrected controls, with comparable maturation, stimulus history and localization of the input. Measure the calibrated extracellular concentration trace after a fixed local pulse separately from the synaptically evoked response. Check local free riluzole and sensor dynamic range, recovery and kinetics. Measure early decay, late glutamate exposure and astrocyte sodium/calcium over repeated stimulation. In parallel track presynaptic output or spike failures, persistent and transient sodium current and early/late evoked recovery. A true uptake mechanism must shorten fixed-input decay and lower late exposure under a validated transporter-dependence comparison. Include an inactive baseline and measure the control's own effect on glutamate handling. A smaller evoked peak with no change in the fixed-input trace favors source suppression. A smaller postsynaptic current with preserved optical glutamate favors altered receptor gain. A rapid first decline with higher late exposure or reduced recruitment rejects an unqualified uptake benefit. Reconsider trial priority after measured free neural exposure and a selective matched response that preserves physiological inhibition and evoked function. An independent review could change the judgment if it finds measurements of these missing inputs.

**Pediatric feasibility.** The current TIGLUTIK label provides an oral suspension with adult oral and gastrostomy PK precedent and states that pediatric safety and effectiveness have not been established. Its CYP1A2, liver, food-exposure and respiratory adverse-effect context matters for interpretation in a polytherapy population. The early SMA trial randomized **7 versus 3** and reported no motor improvement. Survival comparisons are underpowered with baseline and respiratory-support differences. ASIRI lists **141** enrolled and no results in its primary registry. A 12-week adjunctive OCD trial randomized **60**. The Results report **59** in the intent-to-treat analysis and **51** completing double-blind treatment, with no detected effect on the prespecified outcomes. One reported case of pancreatitis and five transaminase elevations in the riluzole arm warrant retaining tolerability as an empirical question, without deriving WWOX risks from a single small trial or counting the later serum subcohort as independent. [TIGLUTIK §§5, 7, 8.4 and 12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=75d68f19-4208-109e-e053-2991aa0ac857) · [SMA trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14623733/) · [ASIRI registry](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT00774423) · [Pediatric OCD trial, Results and Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3988548/)
