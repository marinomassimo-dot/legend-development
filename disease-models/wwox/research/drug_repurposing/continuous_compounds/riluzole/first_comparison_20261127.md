# Opening comparison for riluzole

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · P1–2 rat cortical astrocyte culture and primary mouse striatal astrocytes · nominal µmol/L, tritiated glutamate uptake or release and GLT-1 protein relative to study control.

**Early uptake and later release can move together.** In Yoshizumi et al., Fig. 1 used 1 µmol/L nominal riluzole and 1 µmol/L extracellular glutamate under low-input uptake conditions. The increase in radiotracer uptake was blocked by nonselective DL-TBOA. The Fig. 1 caption and Results text list 50 and 100 µmol/L DL-TBOA, respectively. The transporter control therefore supports an uptake contribution without identifying a subtype or settling the inhibitor concentration. In Fig. 2, 0.1–1 µmol/L riluzole was tested during a 10 µmol/L glutamate challenge and the authors report increased evoked radiotracer release, with basal release unchanged. TBOA alone altered extracellular tracer and could not isolate the drug's effect on release. This cultured-glia result establishes a sign ambiguity under challenge. It does not quantify net clearance in WWOX tissue. [Primary Results §§3.1–3.2 and Figs. 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3268950/)

**Prolonged culture response.** In Carbone et al., nominal **100 µmol/L** riluzole for three days following growth-factor withdrawal increased tritiated glutamate uptake to **138 ± 8%** of vehicle and the WAY-213613-sensitive component to **140 ± 13%** of control (both n = 9, Fig. 3b and 4b in Results). The accompanying GLT-1 protein increase is a separate endpoint in this culture state. These results do not establish that the low-micromolar acute effect raises GLT-1 abundance or that prolonged transporter engagement is attainable in pediatric brain. [Primary Results §3.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3430367/)
