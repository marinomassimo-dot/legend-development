# Riluzole in WWOX loss of function

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · `context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`

**Question.** At a measured free exposure, can riluzole shorten the extracellular glutamate tail from a standardized input through astrocytic uptake in WWOX neural preparations? Compare that with a reduction of presynaptic release or persistent sodium current, and with sodium-dependent secondary glutamate release from astrocytes. The held WWOX bulk analyses place SLC1A2 among reproducibly lower transcripts without directly measuring dynamic uptake, cellular localization or receptor recruitment. The current comparison adds the uptake-versus-release distinction to the laboratory's existing K2P and sodium-channel work. [Held WWOX glutamate and redox comparison](../acetylcysteine/mechanism.md) · [Held sodium comparator](../cenobamate/README.md) · [Held K2P evidence](../../it44_k2p_primary_it55_20261115/README.md)

**Fixed comparison.** Audit native astrocyte studies and recombinant transporter assays, separating background culture state, acute redistribution or altered kinetics, prolonged expression and activity-dependent release. Compare fixed glutamate input, uptake inhibitor/genotype controls and persistent-sodium experiments using known free compound exposure where available. Tabulate pediatric formulations and pharmacokinetics separately from adult ALS, net clearance versus peak release, and assay-specific concentrations affecting physiology. Treat riluzole-sensitive current as an operational effect unless independently isolated.

**Discriminating prediction.** At matched stimulation and recorded cell state, true uptake enhancement should shorten the glutamate transient after an equal calibrated input, reduce late extracellular glutamate and preserve early evoked recruitment. A release reduction should mainly lower the synaptic transient amplitude and corresponding release or firing readout while leaving the response to an equal exogenous input substantially intact. A secondary glial-release mechanism predicts a faster early removal phase with a later glutamate rebound under repetitive stimulation, accompanied by changes in astrocytic sodium or calcium. Require a transporter-specific dependence control, correction for baseline uptake and sensor kinetics, and independent pre/post-synaptic and persistent-current observables. Verify that shorter bursts leave evoked recovery and physiological inhibition intact. Stop for an effect confined to generalized inactivity, native exposure beyond the achievable range, or absent uptake deficit and function in untreated WWOX.
