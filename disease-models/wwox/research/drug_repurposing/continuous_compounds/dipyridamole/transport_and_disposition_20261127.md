# Dipyridamole in WWOX loss of function

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Decision.** Keep dipyridamole as an exposure-conditional preclinical probe of adenosine transport. Park pediatric WWOX trial priority. Rat neuronal data support drug effects on both outward and inward transport. The sign of the net effect depends on how adenosine is generated and cleared in the preparation. Human free brain exposure and WWOX functional target engagement are unmeasured in the sources examined.

In P12–18 rat medial entorhinal slices, nominal 1 µM dipyridamole left evoked AMPA EPSCs at 70 ± 6% of control in 10 cells. With A1 antagonist DPCPX present, dipyridamole left them at 103 ± 15% of control in five cells. Each slice supplied one cell. These are separate arms with within-arm comparisons, no drug-by-antagonist interaction estimate, and no adenosine measurement. The induced epileptiform arm tested exogenous adenosine, and the isolated IPSC comparison tested adenosine as well. A separate juvenile hippocampal primary abstract reports attenuation of interictal and ictal discharges with dipyridamole over a nominal 1–40 µM range and reversal by adenosine receptor antagonists at high concentrations. These experiments support a functional A1 comparison while leaving transport direction and normal inhibition during dipyridamole exposure open. [Wang et al., Methods, Fig. 2F–H and Fig. 7](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0062185) · [Tancredi et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9732405/)

A separate embryonic rat CNS culture study found transient survival under serum-free stress with dipyridamole. Its tested transport-blocker comparators did not reproduce the survival effect, while iron manipulation and antioxidants supplied competing explanations. Survival alone would therefore be ambiguous in a WWOX culture. [Farinelli et al., Table 1, Fig. 8 and Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6793500/)

## Can the compound separate ENT1 from ENT2?

Ward et al. measured human ENT1 and ENT2 nominal IC50s of **5.0 and 356 nM**, respectively, for uptake of 10 µM radiolabelled uridine in transporter-deficient PK15 cells. Kiss et al. measured corresponding mouse recombinant oocyte values of **75 and 2,204 nM** with broad reported intervals, and a separate mouse Ehrlich-cell comparison reported **Ki** estimates of 66 and 371 nM. Human and mouse assays differ in system and timing. These measurements describe uridine influx and do not map directly to adenosine efflux or protein occupancy in a neural preparation. [Ward et al., Fig. 6B](https://doi.org/10.1074/jbc.275.12.8375) · [Kiss et al., Fig. 5 and Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1042/bj3520363)

The [sensitivity replay](assay_window.tsv) uses an illustrative Hill curve with a common slope and unit maximum. At slope 1, the human recombinant values permit **20–39.6 nM** for at least 80% ENT1 inhibition and at most 10% ENT2 inhibition. The mouse oocyte thresholds do not overlap at that slope (minimum 300 nM, maximum 245 nM). At slope 0.75 the window also disappears in the human scenario. At slope 1.25 both recombinant pairs permit overlap, whereas the mouse native Ki scenario still lacks one. These are assay-design thresholds. A neural isoform claim requires measuring both contributions at the actual free concentrations.

The adult extended-release dipyridamole/aspirin label lists steady-state total parent plasma of 1.98 µg/mL at peak and 0.53 µg/mL at trough. With a 1% free-plasma assumption these become **39.2 and 10.5 nM**. Across 0.5–3% free, the calculated free peak is 19.6–117.7 nM. The 1% scenario would require unbound brain/free plasma ratios of 0.13 to equal the human ENT1 assay IC50, 0.51 to reach the nominal window start, 1.63 to equal the rat neuronal efflux assay IC50, and 9.07 to equal the human ENT2 IC50. These are required ratios to nominal assay scales, with no assumed measured brain partition or binding comparison across media. The label describes limited penetration in animals and gives no quantitative pediatric brain partition. Human water PET observes perfusion and the baboon substrate PET observes P-glycoprotein tracer behavior. Neither directly quantified neural dipyridamole. [Exposure sensitivity](exposure_sensitivity.tsv) · [US label §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/getFile.cfm?setid=f07c8535-f9fb-4697-9b63-1ab22210efc9&type=pdf) · [Irish product information §5.2](https://assets.hpra.ie/products/Human/30284/Licence_PA22865-001-001_16052024165428.pdf) · [Primary human PET abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10436110/) · [Primary baboon PET abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27283486/)

## WWOX inference and a discriminating observation

In reused public WWOX bulk data, low **SLC29A2** RNA spans all five genotype contrasts (KO minus control −0.23 to −0.41 in mean log₂(1 + normalized abundance)). The neural-KO hippocampal sign crosses zero when one library is removed. **SLC29A1** is higher in the three hippocampal contrasts and human organoids, with an uncertain direction in neuronal-KO cortex. **ADORA1** is lower in all five leave-one-library-out analyses. Pooled nuclei show an opposite all-class SLC29A2 direction in the held analysis, with only one pool per arm. These layers warrant testing residual ENT1-dependent efflux and A1 responsiveness, and cannot supply transporter weights, functional receptor reserve or WWOX drug sensitivity. [Five-contrast input and replay](../probenecid/bulk_purine_pathway.tsv) · [Held single-nucleus comparison](../cannabidiol/exposure_and_flux.md#untreated-wwox-transport-and-receptor-rna)

**Mechanistic result in silico.** A linear one-compartment calculation varies a finite source pulse and the effective removal rate independently. With source duration equal to the vehicle time constant and a 20% slower removal rate, a 15% reduction of the source gives a **7.4% smaller peak**, **6.3% larger integrated exposure** and **25% longer half-life of a matched external pulse**. An 8.2% smaller source in that scenario would leave the evoked peak unchanged. The replay is illustrative and separates observations that can have opposing signs. Peak, integrated signal and A1-dependent functional recovery can disagree even within one preparation. Measured baseline, local gradients, saturation and the sensor kinetics can break the model assumptions. [Pulse model and sensitivity table](pulse_identifiability.tsv)

**Prediction.** Compare initially matched recruitment in WWOX-deficient and isogenic corrected neural preparations. Within each preparation, estimate effective removal from matched low-range extracellular adenosine pulses and independently record ATP, early evoked adenosine, and electrical recovery after the same activity train. Verify bath and local free dipyridamole, characterize adsorption and sensor response, and measure separate ENT1 and ENT2 contributions with orthogonal acute controls and genetic attribution. If residual ENT1 supports the early WWOX signal, a perturbation with confirmed ENT1 engagement should **slow external-pulse removal yet depress the early evoked adenosine signal** at matched initial drive, with preserved early ATP if extracellular ATP conversion is unchanged. A smaller early A1-dependent functional brake under those conditions would resolve the sign of the effect on recovery. If the pulse slows and the early evoked signal and brake are preserved or prolonged, removal dominates within that preparation. If purines change while a calibrated A1 challenge shows weak receptor responsiveness, prioritize the receptor-side explanation. Check pH, glucose, extracellular ATP conversion and physiological excitation and inhibition before assigning genotype selectivity. Compare effect directions within donor/isogenic line pairs or animals with slices as subsamples.

## Pediatric feasibility and reopening condition

An observational Kawasaki cohort provides an age-relevant peripheral precedent. Seventeen children received dipyridamole with IVIG. Eight met the study's ADP-channel thromboelastography threshold (>30% platelet inhibition). Groups were selected by clinical condition, while the separate warfarin comparison was the one with random allocation described. The authors reported zero observed bleeding events across all 82 children during three months of follow-up, with a small dipyridamole subgroup and no neural endpoints. This does not estimate safety in WWOX or interaction with background antiseizure drugs. The separate US single-agent tablet label describes peripheral vasodilation, including hypotension, and pediatric safety and effectiveness below 12 as unestablished. Adult combination-product bleeding estimates belong to that formulation and population. [Hao et al., Methods, Results and Table 3](https://tp.amegroups.org/article/view/75110/html) · [US single-agent tablet label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f7ffd78a-e93a-40f4-8193-2d6ab4dafd00)

A separate two-month course comparison retrospectively included 51 children receiving dipyridamole plus IVIG and aspirin and 44 receiving IVIG and aspirin (mean ages 3.29 and 3.50 years, respectively). It examined coronary injury, symptoms, inflammation and coagulation. Its coagulation comparison does not measure a platelet-inhibition threshold, CNS exposure or neurological outcomes. This exposure history leaves formulation-specific WWOX safety open. [Du et al., Methods and Tables 1, 2, 5–6](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11826173/)

Reopen for a replicated WWOX-specific functional benefit with preserved ordinary transmission, measured local free drug and independent transport/A1 attribution. Before considering a pediatric trial, connect that active exposure to a measured age-relevant exposure and a tolerable peripheral hemodynamic and platelet profile. An independent review could change the priority if a relevant free CNS exposure dataset or a disease-specific functional contrast becomes available. An additional review of the same recombinant ratios would leave those prerequisites unresolved.
