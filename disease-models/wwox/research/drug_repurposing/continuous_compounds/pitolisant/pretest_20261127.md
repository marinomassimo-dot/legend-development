# Pitolisant, opening prediction

27 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Selection.** Pitolisant is a histamine H3 inverse agonist with pediatric narcolepsy randomization data, a separate small human EEG photosensitivity experiment, and exploratory focal-seizure follow-up. This permits a direct test of how an arousal-associated drug can change electrographic recruitment without assuming a broad clinical antiseizure effect. It examines a different receptor route from the held melatonin and guanfacine comparisons. [Photosensitivity primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23665640/) · [Focal-seizure primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27223666/) · [Pediatric trial abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36931805/)

**Prediction.** At matched vigilance, stimulus intensity and measured free exposure, a circuit-selective pitolisant effect would reduce late pathological recruitment with preserved initial evoked output and inhibitory charge. A change that disappears across vigilance strata while altered alertness persists would favor an arousal explanation. A change accompanied by altered fixed-input release, or opposing effects across cortical and hippocampal preparations, would favor a local H3-associated presynaptic mechanism worth testing independently. In a slice, control histamine tone and background state, and treat H3 specificity as an experimental comparison. The trial evidence does not identify autoreceptor and heteroreceptor contributions.

**Next comparison and stop rule.** Reconcile acute provoked PPR, spontaneous seizure counts over weeks and pediatric narcolepsy outcomes without sharing denominators. Audit phase follow-up, drug form, temporal window and exposure, including background antiseizure therapy and QT/CYP constraints. Compare adult circuit data with the established WWOX juvenile preparation and check whether it is possible to preserve physiological output at human-relevant exposure. Retain only an exposure-supported hypothesis and defer pediatric WWOX priority if the apparent improvement travels with state, is restricted to provocation, or lacks preserved physiological function.
