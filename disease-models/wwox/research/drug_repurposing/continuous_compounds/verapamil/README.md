# Verapamil in WWOX-LoF

28 November 2026 UTC · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. [Opening checkpoint](pretest.md)

**Disposition.** Defer systemic pediatric WWOX prioritization. Retain verapamil as a conditional laboratory comparator for barrier delivery versus direct calcium-current effects. In a WWOX system, the barrier hypothesis first needs a demonstrated co-drug exposure deficit attributable to ABCB1. Cardiovascular and systemic drug interactions are competing effects.

**Serum-only sensitivity.** For a fixed 15–180-minute ECF-to-unbound-serum AUC ratio, increases of 25.5% and 15.0% in unbound circulating phenytoin and carbamazepine, respectively, would produce the reconstructed ECF changes. Those are derived sufficient changes under an explicit fixed-ratio assumption, not measured serum effects. In a separate rat systemic-exposure study the direction of the phenytoin plasma change reversed according to treatment day. In human-MDR1 concentration-equilibrium assays, directional transport was observed for phenytoin and for some other co-drugs, but not for parent carbamazepine. Substrate behavior depends on assay conditions and the exact parent or metabolite. Use an independently validated transport-positive control and verify the actual WWOX background co-drug rather than assigning a whole class to ABCB1. [Time-dependent systemic interaction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24074542/) · [Human ABCB1 assay comparison](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuropharm.2008.08.032)

**Parent, metabolite and voltage.** The official adult oral extended-release formulation reports mean steady-state R/S peak totals of 77.8/16.8 ng/mL. Their sum is 0.208 µM on the free-parent mass basis. Separate mean peaks can overstate the simultaneous mean peak. Applying an 8.08% adult serum free fraction from a separate monotherapy study gives 0.0168 µM in that cross-study scenario. This is about 65-fold below the nominal 1.1 µM parent IC₅₀ for P-gp-mediated digoxin transport and about 112-fold below the nominal 1.89 µM recombinant CaV1.2 IC₅₀ in the conditioning assay. Summed parent totals alone are about fivefold below the digoxin assay IC₅₀. These are preparation and analyte scale checks. They do not predict juvenile luminal free concentration, membrane accumulation, target engagement, or clinical magnitude. Norverapamil had a 0.3 µM IC₅₀ in the same digoxin assay and can have substantial systemic exposure. Its unbound site level and combined effect remain unresolved. An independent seven-adult study measured parent and metabolite in CSF, a different compartment from juvenile cortical ECF. The two-pulse CaV1.2 protocol shifted nominal parent IC₅₀ from 35.3 to 9.1 µM, with the 1.89 µM value from a separate conditioning protocol. Activity-dependent gating is testable. It makes a single quoted IC₅₀ conditional on waveform and assay context. ## WWOX inference and discriminating observation

Held untreated P17 mouse cortex (4 control/3 null libraries) and hippocampus (2/3) showed lower Abcb1a RNA in both, with all single-library omissions preserving direction. In cortex, the change in mean log₂(1 + TPM) was −0.38. It was −0.43 relative to the within-library CLDN5 signal and −0.05 relative to PECAM1, with the latter omission range crossing zero. Hippocampus showed a decrease relative to all three tested markers, with all single-library omissions preserving direction. Human organoid ABCB1 means (0.48 and 0.67 CPM, 2/4 libraries) were below the predeclared detectability floor. CACNA1C moves down in both mouse regions and up in organoids. These contrasts are anatomical and compositional sensitivities. SLC2A1 is not restricted to endothelial cells, and a marker-scaled contrast is not a cell-fraction correction. The two hippocampal single-nucleus pools show a different within-class Abcb1a pattern and lack a separately resolved endothelial class in this held output. The sign of native luminal capacity in WWOX remains unverified. [Mouse cortex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158682) · [Mouse hippocampus](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158681) · [Human organoids](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE156243) · [Pooled nuclei](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158684)

**Hypothesis.** Persistent activity could induce a functional efflux bottleneck in a subset of WWOX barriers despite lower baseline mixed-tissue Abcb1a RNA. A pre-existing ionic defect could instead change drug response at fixed co-drug concentration. Peripheral metabolic interaction or a change in perfusion could shift apparent efficacy.

**Experiment.** In an age- and activity-matched WWOX and corrected barrier-plus-neural comparison, establish baseline net transport for a verified co-drug, a transport-positive calibration substrate and a non-substrate control. Verify endothelial localization, barrier integrity and matched free luminal co-drug. Measure unbound neural-to-systemic co-drug exposure, free R/S parent and norverapamil, then compare verapamil with an orthogonal ABCB1 perturbation. The delivery model predicts restoration of a depressed WWOX co-drug ratio, occlusion after removing ABCB1 activity, and a network effect that can be reproduced by matching the increased co-drug on the neural side. Raising both systemic and neural levels with a stable ratio supports a systemic explanation. A residual effect at matched free neural co-drug and abolished ABCB1, accompanied by measured state-dependent current loss, supports a direct ionic component. Match first recruitment, inhibitory output and perfusion-related effects. A change in barrier leak invalidates the selectivity inference.

**Pediatric feasibility and checkpoint.** Oral extended-release hypertension formulation, oral epilepsy research use and acute pediatric cardiac experience are different settings. The exact adult ER label does not establish pediatric safety or effectiveness. Hypotension, bradycardia and AV conduction, and interactions with carbamazepine and enzyme-inducing drugs limit the feasibility of an interpretable pediatric evaluation. Native WWOX seizure outcomes and age-specific free endothelial and neural exposure remain unresolved. An independent review would change priority if it obtained paired free systemic and ECF exposure from the relevant model or clarified the Dravet counting windows with protocol and participant-flow denominators, without individual records. Current evidence supports retaining a comparison, with clinical priority deferred. Next increment checks ownership before selecting one distinct bottleneck.
