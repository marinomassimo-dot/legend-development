# Methylene blue chloride | Coupled mitochondrial electron transfer versus redox cycling

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Opening checkpoint.** Methylene blue chloride is the sole active compound after the ademetionine disposition. WWOX-deficient cerebral organoid RNA implicates oxidative phosphorylation, with the complex-specific defect unresolved. An alternative electron carrier has an interpretable test because both bypass and hydrogen peroxide production have been documented in distinct preparations. The methylthioninium chloride exposure and its metabolites must be resolved separately. The acute intravenous pediatric context concerns methemoglobinemia. [WWOX organoid primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8350905/) · [Electron transfer study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3091255/) · [Pediatric label context](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4f6848e5-35ed-4046-b13c-3032b5ba3232)

**Discriminating prediction.** At matched first recruitment, an alternative electron route in a verified WWOX bottleneck should increase coupling-dependent ATP recovery and membrane potential while avoiding disproportionate peroxide production. Higher oxygen use without ATP recovery, or with more peroxide, favors redox cycling, uncoupling or another nonspecific effect. Separate a complex I-side effect from a claim that blocked complex III is bypassed and include blank controls for optical interference by the dye.

**Next comparison.** Reconcile the 2011 bypass report, 2014 ATP/peroxide comparison, 2019 antimycin counterexample and 2026 simultaneous oxygen/peroxide study. Record preparation, substrates and inhibitors, measured parent and metabolite concentration, and denominators. Ask which of those comparisons is possible from held WWOX respiratory data.
