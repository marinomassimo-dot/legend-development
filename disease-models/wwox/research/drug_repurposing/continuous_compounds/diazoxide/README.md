## First result | Exposure and a circuit counterexample

**Drug exposure.** Kishore et al. reported rat CSF diazoxide of 0.26 ± 0.06 µg/mL at 1 h and 0.78 ± 0.03 µg/mL by 4 h. The rat time course used 13 animals overall. The per-time-point sample sizes are unresolved, and the main text's list of sampling times omits 4 h despite the 4 h statement and bar in Fig. S1. Using 230.671 g/mol, the mean values are 1.13 and 3.38 µM. Burkart et al. used nominal 300 µM in acute WT mouse CA1 slices, about **89 times** the latter rat CSF value. The routes, species, compartments and free fractions differ. This comparison marks the test scale and supplies neither a human CSF prediction nor a minimum effective concentration. [Kishore et al., Results heading Oral diazoxide crosses the BBB](https://doi.org/10.1172/JCI58035) · [Original Fig. S1](https://dm5migu4zj3pb.cloudfront.net/manuscripts/58000/58035/JCI58035sd.pdf) · [NIST molecular mass](https://webbook.nist.gov/cgi/cbook.cgi?ID=C364-98-7&Mask=200)

**Protecting physiology and interpreting fewer events.** In WT slices from Burkart et al., median physiological sharp waves fell from 77 to 34 per minute at nominal 300 µM diazoxide. The kainate-evoked peak frequency shifted from 40.3 to 28.1 Hz. The comparison measured network rhythms, with a different preparation and endpoint from spontaneous seizures. With the activating Kcnj11 allele restricted to PV interneurons, 7/9 mice showed electrographic seizures over the reported post-surgical observation window versus 0/8 controls. This genetic result demonstrates a risk direction of PV-specific channel activation. It does not establish acute diazoxide proconvulsant activity in people or in WWOX. In a separate rat slice study, the primary abstract reports mean hyperpolarization of −18.6 mV in stratum radiatum interneurons and −4.0 and −7.9 mV in CA1/CA3 pyramidal neurons. This independent contrast strengthens the requirement to measure the inhibitory response during a purported rescue. [Burkart et al., pp. 4207–4208 Figs. 1 and 4](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awae157) · [Rat cell-type study, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12355256/)

**Pediatric boundary.** The original population PK work studied serum in 22 children with hyperinsulinemic hypoglycemia, including five with antiseizure co-medication. Their total exposure and fitted one-compartment clearance do not locate a pediatric neural effect. The oral suspension label describes pulmonary hypertension reported in infants and neonates. Together these motivate a formulation- and age-specific feasibility assessment, with no proposed pediatric WWOX administration. [Kizu et al., Methods, Table 1 and Results](https://doi.org/10.1159/000478696) · [FDA label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2026/017453s025lbl.pdf)

## The most sensitive signal is a loss of inhibitory events

**Data.** In mechanically dissociated rat CA1 cells with retained terminals, Matsumoto et al. found an IC50 of 0.935 µM (±0.128, fit uncertainty as reported) for reducing mIPSC frequency. Amplitude showed no significant change. Tolbutamide opposed the frequency effect. The concentration series reports 3–9 neurons per point, with the animal denominator unresolved in the extracted results. The same article reports a somatic outward-current EC50 of 51.1 µM in its prose, while the Fig. 6Ba caption gives 5.11 ± 0.63 µM in molar units after conversion. Both 5.11 and 51.1 are therefore retained as sensitivity cases. Somatic measurements used nystatin perforation (four neurons per concentration), whereas the mIPSC series used conventional whole-cell recording. This is an assay contrast, with neither a cell-intrinsic selectivity ratio nor preserved network connectivity. [Matsumoto et al., Comparative pre- and postsynaptic effects, Figs. 6Ba and 7Ac](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2290338/)

**Inference.** Either reading of Fig. 6Ba places the somatic half-effect above the inhibitory-frequency half-effect, by about 5.5 or 55 times. The 0.935 µM result falls below both reported mean rat total CSF concentrations. This overlap concerns concentration scale and leaves free drug at the neuron, evoked inhibition, WWOX response and pediatric CNS exposure unmeasured. Treat fewer miniature events as consistent with a presynaptic effect in that preparation. A KATP-dependent reduction of GABA release at lower exposure than the conductance needed for excitatory rescue is now an explicit rival hypothesis.

**Independent network contrasts.** In P10–P13 rat medial entorhinal cortex with spontaneous developmental events, 400 µM nominal diazoxide reduced field burst duration from 7.7 to 4.6 s (eight slices). At 100 µM, intrinsic bursts with synaptic transmission blocked shortened from 7.8 to 3.1 s (six cells). These comparisons used separate preparations and endpoints. In a different P8–P16 rat model with CA3 epileptiform bursts provoked by zero Mg and 4-aminopyridine, the rate was 3.6 before and 3.9 bursts/min during diazoxide, with no significant change in the three-condition comparison (ten slices, reported p = 0.95). In the same slices the authors observed cessation after 2-deoxyglucose. Target response during the diazoxide network recording was unverified, and the primary endpoint was frequency. [Lemak et al., Figs. 1D–E and 2A–B](https://doi.org/10.3389/fncel.2014.00255) · [Shao et al., Methods and Fig. 6C](https://doi.org/10.1152/jn.00124.2023)

**Independent metabolic contrast.** In cardiac mitochondria from WT and Abcc8-null mice, 100 µM diazoxide reduced succinate dehydrogenase activity in the 20-minute succinate/DCPIP assay. The diazoxide arms had 10 WT and eight null preparations. The effect persisted without SUR1. This isolates a plausible alternative at high nominal concentration in a non-neural tissue. Neural complex II response and the lower-concentration threshold remain undetermined. [Anastacio et al., Methods and Figs. 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3660462/)

**Target availability in WWOX.** In RNA from neuronal-deletion mouse cortex and hippocampus, both transcripts change down with stable leave-one-library-out direction. Cortex log2(1+TPM) KO-minus-control contrasts are −0.91 for Abcc8 and −0.62 for Kcnj11 (four control and three KO libraries). The hippocampal contrasts are −0.98 and −1.19 (two control and three KO libraries). Human cerebral organoids in one parental background diverge, with ABCC8 +0.64 and KCNJ11 −0.22 on log2(1+CPM) (two control and four KO libraries). In hippocampal nuclei pooled into one library per arm, assigned excitatory Abcc8 falls from 34.02 to 25.61 CPM (421 and 265 UMIs), while assigned excitatory Kcnj11 is 4.61 and 4.74 CPM (57 and 49 UMIs). Assigned inhibitory Abcc8 is 25.43 and 26.12 CPM (42 and 49 UMIs), and inhibitory Kcnj11 is below the count floor in the KO pool (11 and six UMIs). The mouse bulk direction and excitatory Abcc8 contrast justify matching tissue and cell class for a functional comparison. They leave conserved subunit direction, the PV-specific ratio and functional recruitability unresolved. [Mouse cortex](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158682) · [Mouse hippocampus](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158681) · [Hippocampal nuclei](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE158684) · [Human organoids](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/query/acc.cgi?acc=GSE156243)

**Discriminating observation and current decision.** Keep diazoxide as a conditional mechanistic probe. In the same age- and region-matched Wwox-deficient and control preparation, obtain exposure–response curves for pyramidal KATP conductance, PV recruitment, evoked inhibitory charge and late-burst duration. Preserve early recruitment and native metabolism in the primary comparison, and measure the unbound concentration. Rescue is supported if a shared exposure restores the abnormal excitatory-cell conductance, shortens late bursts and preserves early recruitment and inhibitory charge. Loss of inhibitory transmission before excitatory-cell engagement disfavors a useful window. Persistence of a network effect after a cell-type-specific KATP perturbation, alongside altered succinate-supported function, favors an alternative mechanism. Advance clinical consideration only after a native window, credible pediatric CNS exposure and population-specific safety are established.
