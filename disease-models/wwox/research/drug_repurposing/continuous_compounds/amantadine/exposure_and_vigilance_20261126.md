## What the pediatric EEG establishes

Soto-Insuga et al. measured EEG before treatment and at three months in a retrospective add-on series. Four of five participants with D/EE-SWAS improved in the authors’ pattern-level assessment. In the detailed EEG descriptions in Table 3, only **one of five** explicitly had no epileptiform activity on the follow-up recording. **Three of the four** who improved still had sleep discharges and one participant showed no change. The summary Table 2 uses a stricter-sounding absence label for two participants with residual discharges in Table 3. The endpoint carried forward is **resolution or marked attenuation of the SWAS pattern**, with residual discharges documented. In the separate refractory-absence group, **one of seven** had a >50% clinical seizure-frequency response. These groups are descriptive and should not be pooled. Other antiseizure drugs and, in some cases, other treatments were present. Methods and tables do not give matched N2/N3 minutes, sleep-cycle timing, stage-resolved counts, the standardized scoring window or blinded central adjudication. The [Wilson et al. abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29754107/) independently describes 20 patients with mixed ESES-associated diagnoses, with a reduction of **23 percentage points between cohort medians** (76% to 53%, p=0.01). It adds a signal for further EEG measurement, with no verifiable stage-matched estimate from the accessible abstract. This report should not be combined with the 2026 series or taken as confirmation of a direct pharmacological mechanism. [Soto-Insuga et al., pp. 3–7, Tables 2 and 3](https://www.elsevier.es/es-revista-neurologia-295-pdf-download-S0213485325001021)

**A bound on the sleep-denominator explanation.** One published paired comparison gives >80% pre-exposure and 12% post-exposure within NREM. Assume the two values use the same epoch definition and local scoring. Write the whole-NREM spike-wave proportion as `sum(w[s] * p[s])`, where `w[s]` is the fraction of comparable scored epochs in a stratum and `p[s]` the fraction with discharges within it. If all `p[s]` are constant, the magnitude of any change in the total is bounded by `0.5 * sum(abs(w_after[s] - w_before[s]))`. This follows because `p[s]` lies in [0,1] and the positive and negative changes in the weights have equal mass. The **>68-point** change would therefore require a **>0.68** shift in the distribution of sampled NREM strata to be explained by mixture alone. The denominator already conditions on NREM, so a change in wake time alone cannot explain it if scoring is comparable. N2/N3, cycle, clock time, and scoring differences remain unresolved. This is a bound for one reported contrast under stated assumptions, with no reconstructed patient data and no estimate of the treatment effect. [Soto-Insuga et al., Tables 2–3](https://www.elsevier.es/es-revista-neurologia-295-pdf-download-S0213485325001021) · [Calculation inputs](inputs.tsv)

**The vigilance comparator.** The McMahon outcome report matches the site, design, seven enrolled and five completing both periods in the Vargus-Adams pharmacokinetic report. Count the pair as one crossover. The separate recovery scales did not detect a difference in slope (p=0.24 and p=0.28), while physician ratings of consciousness did (p=0.02). No seizure burden or within-stage EEG contrast is established by the public outcomes. The injury setting also admits spontaneous recovery over time and period effects. [Outcome abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19404190/) · [PK abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20129511/)

## Discriminating prediction and disposition

**Dato.** Juvenile Wwox neuronal-deletion neocortex showed d-APV-sensitive spontaneous bursts. Existing human organoid analysis holds no stable bulk change in combined CHRNA7 plus CHRFAM7A RNA. The latter is bounded by paralog assignment and developmental cell composition. [WWOX cortical design](../../ampa_nmda_discrim_20261108/README.md) · [Held human organoid controls](../../memantine_nicotinic_fate_20261123/README.md)

**Inferenza.** An EEG change can survive a broad NREM denominator without identifying the target. Current data make a low-exposure nicotinic contribution an assayable rival to direct deep GluN1/GluN2A block. The direction of the nicotinic effect on inhibitory recruitment, and whether dopamine-sensitive changes in vigilance contribute independently, remain open.

**Ipotesi e verifica discriminante.** In matched juvenile Wwox cortical preparations, record the initial NMDA and nicotinic currents separately at the same measured free amantadine concentration. Use recorded Mg²⁺, voltage and activation history, and monitor fast AMPA and evoked inhibitory recruitment in the same state and compartment. A current-matched d-APV arm should reproduce the effect on late activity if a reduction in NMDA current is sufficient. A low-exposure circuit response with negligible direct NMDA-current change and a measurable α7-sensitive current change warrants a selective α7 antagonist comparison for phenocopy and occlusion at non-saturating inhibition, with matched excitatory and inhibitory recruitment. An isolated-tissue null with adequate target-control responses would lower priority for either direct explanation. For the separate sleep branch, a true EEG response should survive blinded comparison of equal windows stratified by N2/N3, sleep cycle and clock time with stable background therapy and prespecified artifact rejection, and should preserve clinically relevant waking function. Documentation requires a reproducible sleep-activated EEG phenotype in WWOX before extrapolating the syndrome cohorts.

**Decision.** Keep amantadine parked for an unselected WWOX pediatric trial and as a primary NMDA-blocking probe. The pattern-level EEG finding is a bounded rationale for a stage-controlled measure, with no efficacy estimate in WWOX. Reopen if a comparable WWOX sleep phenotype is shown and the within-stage signal survives controls, or if verified attainable free exposure produces reproducible, genotype-informative circuit engagement without worsening inhibition. A second article review could clarify ambiguous wording in the tables, but the decision now depends on measured exposure and matched-state data.
