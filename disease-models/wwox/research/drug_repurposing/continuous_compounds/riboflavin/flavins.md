# A change in the synthesis rate can exceed the change in total FAD

**WWOX discovery data.** The riboflavin transporter SLC52A2, riboflavin kinase RFK and FAD synthase FLAD1 all declined in the human KO organoid and both juvenile mouse bulk tissues. Their organoid differences in mean log2(1+CPM) were −0.45, −0.45 and −0.24. Across the three genes, all nine within-model contrasts retained their sign after omitting any one library. Mouse tissues use TPM; compare direction, not absolute values between assays. SLC52A1 was unmapped in both mouse tables. This targeted screen lacks genome-wide calibration and its cohorts share biological context. [Panel and omission results](screen.tsv) · [Code](screen.py) · [Input checksums](../creatine/inputs.tsv)

**Two challenges to transfer.** In GSE158684 juvenile pooled hippocampal nuclei, RFK rose by 1.07 CPM, mostly attributable to within-class rates (+1.15 CPM). In the excitatory-enriched class only 33 control and 29 KO nuclei had an RFK count. FLAD1 fell by 1.45 CPM, including −1.36 from within-class rates. In adult P47T hippocampus (GSE215425, ten libraries, equal weighting by sex), RFK rose in both sexes; the overall SLC52A2 contrast also rose and the FLAD1 omission range crossed zero. None of the three genes met the prespecified adult transfer criterion. Age, allele, tissue and preparation vary together. Pooled nuclei provide no independent animal replication. The juvenile bulk results cannot establish a generalized deficiency in flavin synthesis. **Human positive control.** In one child with biallelic FLAD1 variants (Olsen et al., S4a), the fibroblast FAD synthesis rate was 25% of the unweighted mean of the three control lines (1.04/4.13). Whole-cell FAD was 86% of the control mean and lay within the range of the three control means, whereas mitochondrial FAD was 66% of the corresponding mean. The mitochondrial FAD/FMN ratio still fell inside the span of the three controls. These are three distinct estimates in a genetic flavin disorder, derived from the published line means in Tables 2–3. Multiple lysates do not increase the number of people. The authors report mild improvement of hypotonia in this child after riboflavin, and differing outcomes in other genotypes. They do not demonstrate that riboflavin restored the measured flux in this child. No post-treatment paired neural measurement appears in these tables. [Olsen et al., Tables 1–3 and clinical response paragraph](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4908180/)

**Next comparison.** Measure new FMN and FAD formation relative to labelled intracellular riboflavin, alongside mitochondrial flavins, a specific enzyme’s activity and neural recovery at matched demand. Pair with baseline vitamin-replete controls and a lower-vitamin sensitivity arm. This separates failure of entry from failure of conversion and from a change caused by culture conditions. A normal total FAD concentration or a normal FAD/FMN ratio alone would miss the defect in the positive-control case. Lower RNA in a WWOX organoid, alone, cannot tell whether substrate supplementation, a downstream bottleneck or a change in neuronal composition accounts for the observation. The next analyses address oral pediatric exposure, central measurements and whether nutrient level in the original cultures was sufficiently documented.
