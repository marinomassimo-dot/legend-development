# Racemic baclofen · prespecified compartment comparison

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · order 76 · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN · WWOX mouse P17 cortical and hippocampal TPM, human week-15 cerebral organoid CPM, pooled hippocampal nuclear counts

**Question.** Can GABA-B engagement promote oligodendroglial differentiation and first functional myelin sheaths in WWOX, while preserving evoked inhibitory recruitment and avoiding delayed network rebound? Racemic baclofen is the sole active compound. Assess oral and intrathecal exposure separately, as well as the experimental cell exposure and how the active enantiomer is reported.

**Why this compound.** It links two existing LEGEND observations with opposing experimental predictions. The neonatal lineage can respond to GABA-B signalling in held glial evidence. In the independent reticular-thalamic model, baclofen worsened spike-wave discharges. Neither preparation is a WWOX functional assay. The held WOREE phenotypic audit has one account of poor spasticity/dystonia response. It establishes exposure history at report depth and supplies no attributable electroclinical comparison. The vigabatrin card uses baclofen as an external comparator without testing the acute/chronic separation for baclofen. The phenylbutyrate family already has a separate LEGEND card and direct WWOX missense docket. **Before computing.** Compare GABBR1 and GABBR2, GIRK coupling (KCNJ3, KCNJ6, KCNJ9), the recovery counter-readouts CACNA1G, CACNA1H and CACNA1I, and myelin lineage markers PDGFRA, OLIG2, SOX10, MYRF, MBP and PLP1. The RNA question is a stage/cell-type screen. Receptor and GIRK RNA cannot identify surface heterodimers, functional coupling or which presynaptic cell is affected. Keep the omissible small-library comparison and one pooled sample per genotype explicit. Preserve the developmental/electrophysiological context behind each comparison.

**Discriminating observation.** At measured free parent and matched background activity in WWOX and corrected cells, record acute evoked excitatory and inhibitory strength and post-hyperpolarization recovery, then measure later mature ensheathment and conduction after drug clearance. Direct glial engagement predicts increased first sheaths per mature oligodendrocyte and preserved conduction gain after washout. Reduced neuronal activity alone can change glia. An activity-matched control and a glial GABA-B dependence comparison distinguish it from direct glial signalling. A decline in inhibitory recruitment or increased delayed burst synchrony at the same exposure rejects a favorable mixed-compartment claim. Consider concentration, exposure time, developmental age, route and co-medication before interpreting pediatric feasibility.
