# Losigamone, persistent sodium current and enantiomer-dependent release

28 November 2026 UTC · Continuous compound #212 · INV-15 · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN. Preparations include P15–25 rat CA1 sodium recordings (mV and nominal µM), Wistar medial entorhinal layer II neurons (pA and percent of TTX-sensitive current), mouse cortical slice effluent and magnesium-free wedges (% of first pulse or baseline), healthy adult plasma (ng/mL, µg/mL, and mL/min), and adult focal epilepsy trials (frequency reduction and percentage points). Held WWOX context uses region-specific mouse bulk TPM and human organoid library CPM. [Prespecified comparison](pretest.md) · [Opening checkpoint](first_comparison.md)

**Decision.** Retain losigamone as a conditional ex vivo comparator of near-threshold persistent sodium current and glutamate release. Defer pediatric WWOX priority. The adult adjunctive efficacy signal and higher adverse-event rates coexist. Measured adult plasma exposure is below the main nominal concentrations of the mechanism studies. Sodium-current suppression raises the threshold for first-spike recruitment in the most directly quantified comparator.

**Reanalysis.** The entorhinal mean rheobase rose **81.8 pA**, about **49%**. In the separate wedge preparations at 25 µM, afterpotentials were **35%** of the concurrent control-normalized value and the spontaneous frequency measure was **95%**. These are ratios of reported group means. The low-concentration wedge result gives a reason to measure late activity before the larger-concentration near-threshold block. It does not imply that the initial response was preserved.

## Did enantiomer identity change release?

Srinivasan et al. 1999 exposed P21–50 BALB/c cortical slice preparations to the compound before and during the second of two stimulation pulses. In the original plots, `S2/S1` percentages compare second and first pulse-evoked effluent. Each figure includes its own stimulus-specific and analyte-specific control, with 6–8 slice preparations per plotted comparison. Multiple slices could share a preparation. Mutually calibrated animal-level sample sizes were unavailable.

- In Fig. 1a, potassium-evoked glutamate at 100 and 200 µM S(+) was approximately **52% and 69% lower** than its plotted control after dividing by the control S2/S1. In Fig. 1b, veratridine-evoked glutamate at the same concentrations was about **48% and 65% lower**. The S(+) preparation also lowered evoked aspartate.
- In Fig. 2, R(−) showed no statistically detected release change in the reported range up to 400 µM. The comparable graphical reductions in glutamate are smaller, roughly 12% under potassium and 5–6% under veratridine at 100–200 µM relative to its own controls. There was no detected basal amino-acid or stimulated GABA release effect in these conditions.
- The Methods say pulse 1/pulse 2, while captions and the Results describe **S2/S1**. The plotted Fig. 1b vehicle bars are approximately 88% for glutamate and 83% for aspartate, while the narrative 2nd/1st-pulse means are 72.4% and 72.8%. The digitization uses same-panel controls throughout. The original between-preparation data are required to reconcile the summaries. Values are approximate, read from the official 359 × 640 figure rasters, with axis and bar-top rows in [comparison.tsv](comparison.tsv). Reported tests do not provide matched free exposure or a formal effect-size difference between enantiomers.

The potassium challenge directly depolarizes the preparation and supports testing a pathway beyond spike-driven release. It does not verify that sodium dependence was eliminated in that exact comparison or isolate a vesicular or receptor mechanism. In an independent hippocampal culture abstract, the drug reduced spontaneous activity with no detected miniature-current change. In a GABA-receptor comparison, parent through 100 µM did not measurably displace the three reported radioligands. The functional chloride-flux findings in that study use a separate preparation. The safer causal interpretation is a release or excitability phenotype that can be separated from direct GABA-A binding. ## Adult exposure and controlled clinical outcomes

**Exposure.** In Peeters et al. (five healthy adult men, crossover), Table I measured pure-isomer plasma Cmax values of 1.69 versus 0.44 µg/mL for S(+) AO-242 and R(−) AO-294. AUC values were 10,460 versus 1,260 ng·h/mL and half-lives were 4.85 versus 2.24 h. Thus S(+) had **8.30×** the R(−) AUC after equal individual administrations. Table II also shows **8.21×** after racemate. No chiral inversion was detected at the reported sampled times. In vitro binding to adult plasma was 54.7% and the ex vivo radiolabel fractions were time dependent. An illustrative plasma free fraction of 0.50 is used only as a scenario. A single-dispersion AUC may reflect bioavailability, clearance and protein binding. These sparse adult plasma comparisons cannot assign central free concentrations to either isomer. [Primary PK](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF03189826) · [Accessible original Tables I–II](https://www.academia.edu/80190528/Pharmacokinetics_of_14C_labelled_losigamone_and_enantiomers_after_oral_administration_to_healthy_subjects)

The largest reported single-dose plasma Cmax in the three values given by Biber and Dienel is **4.4 µg/mL**, approximately **17.3 µM total** using 254.66 g/mol or **8.64 µM** with the illustrative free fraction. On this scale, 25, 100 and 200 µM nominal bath concentrations are **2.89×, 11.58× and 23.15×** the illustrative adult unbound Cmax. These comparisons use different study products, matrices, ages and timepoints. The ratio does not establish a pediatric tissue exposure limit or a required mechanism concentration in humans. ## WWOX-specific hypothesis and experiment

**Data.** Held WWOX bulk RNA does not show uniformly increased sodium-channel abundance. SCN2A is lower in P17 mouse cortex and hippocampus and week-15 human organoids. SCN8A is lower in both mouse regions and inconclusive in the organoids. In separate P13–17 Wwox cortical slices, passive properties, membrane potential and spontaneous bursting differ from controls. Voltage history or recruitment drive can alter persistent sodium dependence even with lower bulk RNA. The RNA and passive measures cannot determine native persistent current per excitatory or inhibitory cell. [Held sodium RNA](../relutrigine/wwox_sodium_bulk.tsv) · [Held WWOX cortical recording audit](../../inhibitory_defect_sci47_20261112/README.md)

**Hypothesis.** Late WWOX recruitment could be disproportionately supported by persistent sodium current while the first volley remains usable. A distinct release contribution would show an S(+) effect after current and free exposure are independently matched. Equal systemic or nominal concentrations cannot ensure equivalent free exposure for each isomer.

**Discriminating comparison.** Compare vehicle, each measured free isomer and a reference sodium-current perturbation in age- and region-matched WWOX and controls. Quantify persistent and initial transient currents after the same holding and activity history. Match the first afferent volley and initial excitatory and inhibitory recruitment. Measure late recurrence and fast glutamate release under an independent fixed-input or depolarization control. If late activity follows independently matched persistent-current suppression and the release difference vanishes, the sodium explanation gains support. A residual S(+) release and recurrence effect with current, native inhibition and first recruitment matched supports a parallel mechanism. Independently isolate an evoked NMDA current before attributing that residual response to direct NMDA modulation. Loss of first recruitment, especially inhibition, weakens both selective-recovery readings. Use animal or donor as the replication unit.

**Checkpoint and feasibility.** Historical adult oral formulations and adjunctive trials supply an exposure and tolerability reference. Juvenile neural free exposure, developmental tolerability, effects under current background antiseizure medicines and efficacy in WWOX remain unmeasured. Keep the molecule as a comparator for the matched-current experiment. Reopen the clinical-priority question only for a verified juvenile exposure-to-effect relationship with selective circuit benefit. A further review of the current summaries would not fill the free-exposure or matched-input gaps. No source figures or full texts are redistributed.
