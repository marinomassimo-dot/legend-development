# Bromide can silence spontaneous discharges while evoked abnormalities persist

**Decision.** Park potassium bromide for a WWOX-specific pediatric trial. Retain the halide hypothesis for a direct, exposure-matched comparison of spontaneous activity, delayed cortical recruitment and physiological transmission. Among the sources examined there is no demonstration of bromide-specific recovery of these outcomes in WWOX. The pediatric epilepsy series supports feasibility of an antiseizure study, with phenotype, sedation and long equilibration time affecting interpretability. [Clinical denominators and halide-gradient analysis](README.md)

## Two outcomes in the same developing preparation

In juvenile rat hippocampal slices challenged with pentylenetetrazole, Fueta et al. compared untreated tissue (10 slices from nine pups) with an independent series exposed to sodium bromide (10 slices from seven pups). Spontaneous epileptiform discharges occurred in 6/10 and 0/10 respectively. Evoked epileptiform responses occurred in 10/10 and 8/10. Thus even among the ten bromide-exposed slices without spontaneous events, eight still displayed evoked pathology. The descriptive changes are −60 and −20 percentage points. Neither a paired before/after effect nor an animal-level interval can be estimated from these counts. Preparations were selected for an initial evoked population spike above 3 mV. Selection and the acute pharmacological challenge limit transfer to WWOX. [Published study, methods and Figs. 2–4](https://doi.org/10.2486/indhealth.2020-0098) · [Extracted counts](pup_slice_counts.tsv) · [Calculated contrasts](evoked.tsv)

The bath contained 2.4 mM bromide. The authors report about 1.2 mM in the whole slice. Their Fig. 3 calibration gives 0.545 × 2.4 = 1.31 mM. Neither quantity resolves intracellular versus extracellular bromide. The washout graph approaches baseline by 30 min without providing a quantification limit on the plot. A separate washout cohort had spontaneous events in four of eight slices from four pups. The text subsequently gives ten as the evoked denominator for this group. That comparison is excluded from the calculation. The description available here does not establish a matched replacement of bath chloride. Keep potassium, sodium, total halide, bicarbonate, pH and osmolality matched or measured in a WWOX comparison. [Original article, printed pp. 244–245](https://www.jniosh.johas.go.jp/en/indu_hel/doc/IH_59_4_239.pdf)

## A WWOX endpoint and a preservation control

In the neuron-specific Wwox mouse knockout, stimulation from cortical layer V to layer II/III elicited a delayed negative field response peaking at 97 ± 31 ms (mean ± SD). The authors report six animals for the latency. The separate between-genotype amplitude curve used six knockout slices from three animals and seven control slices from four animals. At the strongest stimulus (100 µA), the delayed peak reads approximately 0.46 mV in knockout and 0.12 mV in wild type from the plotted means. NMDA receptor blockade reversibly suppressed the delayed response. The plotted means are visual estimates. This identifies a stimulus-locked measure for bromide even when spontaneous bursts disappear. [Breton et al., Results and Supplementary Fig. S3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609180/)

Hippocampal circuit direction supplies a preservation control. In the CA3-to-CA1 direction, the authors found no significant genotype difference in either the population spike or the field excitatory postsynaptic potential. In the CA1-to-CA3 direction they report larger knockout population spikes at 40–100 µA. The figure itself labels the maximal CA1-to-CA3 spike comparison p=0.0312 and the reverse direction p=0.7164, while the Fig. 5D caption includes a blanket statement of no significance. The pathway-specific Results and panel support using direction as a prespecified contrast. The absence of a significant difference in the reverse direction cannot establish equivalence. Measurements must retain absolute amplitudes, matched input and animal-level replication. [Original study, Fig. 5C–D and caption](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8609180/)

**Reopening observation.** At measured exposure, the delayed cortical response should move toward the untreated wild-type input–output curve without a parallel loss of the early response or of CA3-to-CA1 transmission. Record both directions, spontaneous burden, train-dependent changes and recovery. Subtract the drug response in controls to test genotype selectivity. If delayed and early responses both fall, or evoked multispikes persist after spontaneous activity subsides, a general antiseizure effect is still plausible but the WWOX-specific claim loses support. This test addresses circuit function. Developmental rescue would require a separate outcome.

**Halide assay interference matters for that prediction.** In a child already receiving potassium bromide, a routine electrolyte result gave chloride 171 mEq/L. The independently reported serum bromide was 691 µg/mL (8.65 mmol/L as bromide). The analyzer and mixed-halide response matter when assigning a concentration to chloride. A routine chloride result or a single anion-reversal measurement cannot by itself yield both intracellular concentrations. The case does not estimate an exposure–benefit relationship or indicate a universal correction factor. [Primary pediatric report](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4766335/)
