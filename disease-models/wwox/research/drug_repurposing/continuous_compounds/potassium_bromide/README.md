# Potassium bromide and activity-dependent inhibition in WWOX loss

**Why this AED next.** The existing WWOX program has examined native chloride, bicarbonate, tonic inhibition and synaptic recruitment. An earlier laboratory review found reduced event frequency without shorter bursts in a scn1lab zebrafish pharmacology screen. Bromide asks a distinct question about the ion carrying the current. In an immature network with altered anion gradients, can a larger GABA-evoked current suppress a test spike during repetitive input and preserve the subsequent evoked response? [Earlier fish comparison](../../phenotype_first_it29_20261105/p3_D/README.md) · [Native chloride analysis](../taurine/native_chloride.md)

**Prior prediction.** At matched chloride replacement, potassium, osmolarity, baseline drive and central exposure, bromide may transiently shift the anion reversal potential or strengthen shunting. At higher intracellular bromide fractions the shift may disappear or reverse. If a repeated train then increases rebound spiking or compromises normal transmission, a larger current in a voltage-clamped cell would overstate utility in WWOX. Measure native reversal relative to spike threshold and probability of a test spike during and after the train in both WWOX-deficient and corrected cultures. Match the baseline evoked response and assess inhibitory-cell recruitment separately.

**First quantitative comparison.** Suzuki et al. found a bromide/chloride permeability ratio of 1.51 in cultured rat cortical neurons. Substituting bromide for extracellular chloride at 10 and 20 mM increased the whole-cell GABA current to 1.28 and 1.36 times control at −20 mV. The paper's abstract reports current-clamp hyperpolarization at 140 mM substitution. The lower-concentration voltage-clamp measurements cannot by themselves determine spike suppression under native ion gradients. [Original rat-neuron study, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7843172/)

A two-anion Goldman calculation illustrates the dependence on intracellular bromide. With 159 mM total extracellular halide, 37 °C and iso-halide substitution, the predicted change in reversal is about −0.84 mV for 10 mM external bromide and zero intracellular bromide. Equal fractional replacement on both sides yields zero shift. A higher intracellular replacement fraction changes its sign. This is a sensitivity calculation, not an estimate of brain exposure. It holds total halide constant on each side and omits bicarbonate, transport kinetics and conductance changes. Shunting can change spike probability with little change in reversal. Reproduce with `python3 halide_shift.py`. [Calculated scenarios](halide_shift.tsv)

**Pediatric feasibility.** A retrospective KBr cohort included 42 patients aged four months to 19 years. Three were seizure-free and 21 had a greater-than-50% reduction. The authors found excellent or moderate responses in 13/18 patients with tonic seizures and 2/10 with spasms. Thirteen patients had two seizure types, precluding interpretation as disjoint trial groups. Adverse effects were reported for 13/42, including drowsiness, excitement and rash. No genotype-matched comparator or developmental benefit was established. [Original cohort, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31111774/)

**Next checkpoint.** Seek simultaneous central and plasma bromide/chloride measurements, their time relative to treatment, and an electrophysiology experiment during sustained exposure or after washout. Check halide assay specificity, formulation, slow accumulation, dietary chloride and background AEDs before translating the bath experiment. No WWOX trial priority assigned.

## Central sampling site and exposure time

Hunter et al. measured both ventricular and lumbar CSF bromide in six selected patients with diverse diagnoses. In every case ventricular bromide was lower. Recalculation of Table 7 gives ventricular/lumbar ratios from 0.447 to 0.709. In case 39X (described as having fits), lumbar and ventricular concentrations were 1.51 and 0.89 mM, respectively. Neither measures the intracellular halide fraction or interstitial concentration at a synapse. Table 6 also documented slower ventricular equilibration in a participant with hydrocephalus. A historical neurological diagnostic challenge cannot establish chronic pediatric exposure during epilepsy treatment. Reproduce the site and unit comparison with `python3 csf.py`. [Hunter et al., Tables 6–7](https://doi.org/10.1042/bj0560588) · [Six paired observations](csf.tsv)

A formulation distinction affects the bridge to the bath data. Six responding children among eleven given a mixed bromide elixir had a mean serum bromide level of 14.1 mM (range 4–30.5 mM). This response-selected serum measurement belongs to a different cohort from Table 7. In another cohort, 113 patients aged 1–20 years given potassium bromide reached steady-state blood levels at a median of 28 days (range 5–95). A rapid change in an acute bath and long-term clinical outcomes therefore require separate comparisons. The next decision depends on sustained exposure and native-gradient spike suppression with repeated input. [Mixed-elixir study, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/2299142/) · [KBr study, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17621520/)

**A dynamic comparator already changes the concentration question.** In rat hippocampal–entorhinal slices with reduced magnesium, 3 and 5 mM NaBr decreased the frequency of entorhinal seizure-like events and 7 mM blocked them. Late recurrent discharges were reduced at 10 and 15 mM and blocked at 30 mM. A separate paired-pulse test found increased inhibition at 5 mM. At that concentration resting extracellular pH showed no detectable change. These endpoints depend on discharge pattern and on when the measurement is taken. Record early and late events separately, and test physiological transmission after each pattern at the same achieved exposure. An increase in paired-pulse inhibition alone leaves the sustained mechanism open. Sodium bromide in these experiments and oral potassium bromide share the bromide ion. Their salt loads require separate controls. [Meierkord et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10651143/)

**Why WWOX model choice changes the prediction.** In the held untreated datasets, the knockout-minus-control differences in mean log2(1 + expression) for SLC12A5/KCC2 were −0.68 in mouse cortex, −0.57 in mouse hippocampus and +0.66 in week-15 human organoids. Each direction survived individual library omission. SLC12A2/NKCC1 also declined in both mouse regions, so the KCC2 change alone cannot determine the sign of native halide transport. These data motivate measuring native reversal and intracellular halide recovery separately in matched developmental cell types in each model. There is no direct estimate of a bromide response from the untreated transcript counts. [Held WWOX readouts and omission bounds](../acetazolamide/screen.tsv)

**Disposition after the developmental slice comparison.** Park for WWOX-specific trial priority. Juvenile slice data show that spontaneous and evoked endpoints can dissociate. [Observed counts, WWOX-specific stimulus comparison and reopening criterion](evoked.md)
