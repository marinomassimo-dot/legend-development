# Carbamazepine | Inositol-dependent cargo disposal and neuronal reserve

context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

**Disposition.** Retain as a conditional mechanistic comparator for inositol-dependent cargo disposal and activity suppression. Defer pediatric WWOX study priority. The positive cargo-loss comparison with a defined nominal exposure uses 50 µM in a rat PC12 reporter system. An independent non-neural comparison at 10 µM has an early autophagy signal without demonstrated completed disposal. The WWOX transcript results and the CSF comparison do not establish a usable neural exposure window. [Prespecified comparison](pretest_20261127.md)

**Reproducible counterexample.** In a synthetic two-step model, steady reporter pools are both 2 arbitrary units when entry is 3 and both steps run at 1.5 per unit time. They are also both 2 when entry is 0.5 and the steps run at 0.25. With an identical pulse of labelled cargo, 44.2% versus 2.6% is degraded at time 1. The reference (entry and rates all 1) degrades 26.4%. The example is analytical and has no fit to the published cells. A static increase in early and late reporter pools can coexist with either direction in measured cargo degradation. **Why HMIT RNA cannot close the bypass question.** Di Daniel et al. found predominant Golgi localization, no detectable somatic currents in tested acute rat preparations and reversal of CDP-DAG accumulation by exogenous inositol even in HMIT-null mouse cortical cultures at pH 7.4. Uldry et al. saw approximately 40 to 200 pmol/mg protein/min after stimulated exocytosis in primary brain-cell aggregates under sodium-free acidic conditions, and reported neuronal process localization in a different preparation. The studies support a test of compartment and state. The WWOX RNA pattern does not identify a compensating surface uptake route. ## Exposure and pediatric feasibility

- The Tegretol label's total parent reference of 4–12 µg/mL converts to 16.93–50.79 µM. Applying its reported 76% binding gives an illustrative free plasma scale of 4.06–12.19 µM. This scenario does not account for age, formulation, binding changes or background treatment. In Johannessen et al., CSF before the morning dose in a predominantly adult cohort (CSF measured from age 17 up) contained 1.0–3.1 µg/mL total parent, **4.23–13.12 µM**, with mean 8.89 µM. The reported mean epoxide CSF value was **2.77 µM**. These are CSF samples, with no free interstitial or intracellular measurement. [Official label](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=8d409411-aa9f-4f3a-a52c-fbcb0c3ec053) · [Johannessen et al., Tables 1 and 4, 10 paired parent measurements](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdfdirect/10.1111/j.1365-2125.1976.tb04878.x)
- **Direct measured pediatric systemic exposure.** Riva et al. (Clinical Pharmacokinetics) sampled at three hours after the morning administration in 39 children aged 3–10 years on long-term treatment. HPLC after filtration at 25 °C measured free parent means of **5.17 ± 1.89 µM** with carbamazepine alone (n=22) and **4.41 ± 0.88 µM** with phenobarbital (n=17). Free epoxide means were **1.84 ± 0.88** and **2.08 ± 0.52 µM**, respectively. Mean within-child free epoxide/parent ratios were 0.34 and 0.48. This was a nonrandomized medication comparison. The assay temperature and age range matter. An independent analytical series in 55 children reported free serum means of 2.59 µg/mL parent and 1.05 µg/mL epoxide (10.96 and 4.16 µM); its available abstract does not establish sampling times or an age-specific range. These studies establish measured systemic exposure scales only. They do not imply a shared activity or potency scale for parent and epoxide. [Riva et al., Tables I–II and Methods](https://www.academia.edu/89641125/Free_Concentration_of_Carbamazepine_and_Carbamazepine_10_11_Epoxide_in_Children_and_Adults) · [Liu et al., author abstract](https://utsouthwestern.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/determination-of-total-and-free-carbamazepine-and-the-principal-m/)

- Pediatric retrieval leads report paired CSF-to-plasma fractions of **33% parent and 41% epoxide in nine children**, and a separate binding study includes 31 children between 4 months and 11 years in three age groups. These two sources were consulted at abstract depth. Applying the 33% pediatric aggregate to the separate total reference gives a sensitivity scenario of 5.59–16.76 µM parent in CSF. That scenario is about threefold below nominal 50 µM at its upper end and cannot estimate an age-specific measured range. The measured older cohort maximum is 3.8-fold below the same nominal bath point. The comparisons do not bound unbound intracellular exposure. [Pynnönen et al., abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/837966/) · [Riva et al., abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4006891/) · [Conversions](exposure.tsv)
- In the 13-person WWOX multicenter cohort, three histories list carbamazepine. There is no attributable response or timed exposure for this compound. A separate adult WWOX report documents exposure during epilepsy management with several medicines, without a sustained treatment response attributed to carbamazepine. These observations supply neither a causal negative nor a response rate. The licensed suspension, immediate-release and extended-release tablets give pediatric formulation experience in specified seizure types. The label reports different peak behavior by formulation, enzyme induction, parent/epoxide confounding with valproate or brivaracetam, potential worsening with mixed atypical absence patterns, and serious cutaneous and hematologic risks. For a future mechanistic study, eventual clinical readouts would need to distinguish EEG-defined seizure types, vigilance and developmental function. [Oliver et al., Table 2](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/epi.17542) · [Adult WWOX exposure report](https://doi.org/10.3389/fgene.2024.1477466) · [Label, indications, boxed warning and interactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=8d409411-aa9f-4f3a-a52c-fbcb0c3ec053)

## Inference, hypothesis and reopening experiment

**Inference.** Increasing autophagosome entry could help when entry limits total disposal. When late lysosomal processing is already limiting, the same input change can accumulate cargo or leave disposal unchanged. Depleting available inositol could independently limit the recovery of membrane PIP2 and Kv7 current in a circuit with a small metabolic reserve. Either consequence can coexist with acute sodium-dependent burst suppression.

**Hypothesis and prediction.** The post-audit design adds an add-back-alone arm to identify its independent effects. In a WWOX isogenic contrast at matched developmental stage, first establish a native disposal deficit. Then cross parent versus vehicle with myo-inositol add-back versus its vehicle in both genotypes. Measure free parent and epoxide. Keep epoxide matched between add-back conditions and measure its effect separately if it is present. Record absolute first recruitment, then match the conditioning input across arms for the cargo comparison. Track labelled inositol input, recovery of PIP2 and Kv7 current after identical stimulation, decay of a prelabelled native cargo with and without a short validated lysosomal block, and after-washout evoked recovery. Prefer a pulse–chase readout that separates loss of old cargo from new synthesis, dilution, death and pH-mediated reporter changes. A drug-dependent increase in native cargo decay that is lysosome-sensitive and lost with inositol add-back supports the proposed route only if PIP2/Kv7 recovery and physiological inhibition are preserved. If add-back reverses slow Kv7 recovery while LC3 rises with no additional cargo loss, the reserve-cost branch gains weight. If only acute burst suppression remains after matching initial activity, there is support for circuit pharmacology but no identified cargo benefit.

**Reopen** for increased completed native neuronal disposal at measured, age-relevant free exposure with preserved Kv7 and inhibitory reserve. An independent assay review could change the decision when a paired turnover and local exposure comparison is available. The current public evidence supports a bounded comparison and no clinical reprioritization.
