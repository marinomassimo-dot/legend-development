# Caffeine citrate · neonatal seizure and motor endpoints

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN · neonatal aEEG event incidence and cumulative seconds, randomized preterm developmental outcomes by participant

## High versus standard early exposure

Vesoulis et al. Table 3 gives seizures in **17/29** high and **12/30** standard among interpretable recordings. These are **+18.6 percentage points** in the evaluable contrast (Table 3 p = 0.19; abstract and body narrative print p = 0.10 with the incidence, so carry Table 3). Of 37 infants randomized per group, eight high and seven standard did not enter the EEG comparison. Extreme imputation over all 74 bounds the binary incidence difference from **−5.4 to +35.1 points** (`(17−19)/37` to `(25−12)/37`). This addresses missing outcomes only and is not a confidence interval. EEG monitoring covered the first 72 hours with two channels, and some interpretable recordings did not span the full window. The post hoc analysis excludes short and corrupted records. [Vesoulis et al., study flow, EEG methods and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5031091/)

Mean cumulative burden was **170.9 vs 48.9 seconds** (3.49-fold from rounded means; p = 0.10), and the upper maximum was 2,174 s in the high-dose group. Removing one observation at that maximum leaves approximate mean **99.4 s** (`(29*170.9−2174)/28`), about **2.03 times** the standard group mean. This is a descriptive sensitivity check, with no adjusted p value. It separates a possible sign from the exact magnitude. The randomized contrast compares two timing/dose regimens. Paired concentrations for the EEG window were not reported in Table 3, and the comparison does not estimate a concentration threshold or the effect of standard caffeine against placebo. [Vesoulis et al., Results and Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5031091/)

## Adult human cortical physiological comparison

Kerkhofs et al. used temporal neocortex resected outside the seizure focus from six adult surgical donors (five with mesial temporal epilepsy and one with dysplasia). Fig. 3 compares spontaneous excitatory inputs to deep-layer pyramidal neurons. Under **50 µM caffeine**, adding **20 µM adenosine** left the reported mean event amplitude close to its starting value (38.9 pA control and 36.7 pA combined, n=9 cells, p=0.49), while event frequency still declined (1.1 to 0.2 Hz, n=9, p=0.007). With caffeine alone the corresponding means were 40.1 pA and 0.6 Hz. The ratio of the combined and caffeine-alone group means is thus **0.92** for amplitude and **0.33** for frequency. These ratios are descriptive and use rounded means, with no paired interaction or equivalence test. Multiplying amplitude and frequency group means would miss kinetics and cell-level covariance and cannot establish charge transfer. In the same preparation **100 nM DPCPX** raised frequency alone (2.0 to 3.0 Hz, n=6, p=0.01) and the adenosine challenge with DPCPX yielded 1.9 Hz versus 2.0 Hz baseline. At 50 µM caffeine, adenosine still increased rheobase in Fig. 2E (n=11). These outcomes support separating the synaptic amplitude, event probability and intrinsic excitability arms. Spontaneous frequency does not itself establish presynaptic localization. [Kerkhofs et al., Methods and Figs. 2E and 3A–G](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2017.00899)

Fig. 1B gives apparent caffeine Kᵢ **11.53 µM** for human synaptosomal membranes and **33.02 µM** for total membranes, a **2.86-fold** ratio (five subjects per plotted condition). Total membranes are a mixture. This contrast cannot assign pre- versus postsynaptic affinity or predict occupancy during an endogenous adenosine transient. Human cortical pharmacology independently favors testing an endogenous ligand transient and separate pre/post perturbations. Under a WWOX matched-ligand challenge, a loss of adenosine-mediated amplitude suppression with event probability and first-evoked inhibition preserved would support a component-specific effect. Test release probability with paired evoked pulses and separate local glutamate responses, alongside inhibitory input and the actual total excitatory charge. [Kerkhofs et al., binding methods and Fig. 1B–C](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2017.00899)

## Exposure transfer and receptor separation

The original preterm plasma–CSF articles by Türmen and Somani have no abstract on PubMed, and public publisher routes did not yield paired values. Keep the qualitative preterm label statement and carry no numerical CSF ratio forward. An independent paired adult study sampled 12 hospitalized adults undergoing lumbar puncture one hour after oral caffeine. Group mean total plasma and lumbar CSF were **5.9 and 2.9 µg/mL**, with paired correlation **0.77**. The ratio of means is **0.49**, and the mean lumbar CSF concentration is **14.9 µM** (`2.9 * 1000 / 194.19`). A nominal 50 µM slice bath is **3.35-fold** that adult CSF mean. This comparator is total lumbar CSF at one time point and cannot calibrate preterm brain interstitial concentrations or infer equilibration. The reported paired association and ratio of group means describe different properties. [Türmen et al., bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/480049/) · [Somani et al., bibliographic record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7189555/) · [Soto et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8160248/)

A mouse Schaffer–CA1 slice study using the same nominal **50 µM** caffeine concentration reported **+40% basal transmission and −35% LTP amplitude**. Its abstract reports that removal of extracellular adenosine blunted both, while A1 antagonism occluded the transmission effect and A2A genetic deletion or pharmacological blockade occluded the LTP effect. These are different outcomes measured in a mouse hippocampal circuit. Human cortical event frequency and amplitude cannot be mapped directly onto the mouse field response. The two papers share an author. Treat the mouse comparison as a receptor discriminator rather than an independent replication of human cortical physiology. [Lopes et al., primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31199895/)

## Disposition and reopening criteria

**Defer WWOX pediatric trial priority.** Keep caffeine as a receptor comparison in the adenosine strand. The respiratory and motor signals in prematurity leave the WWOX mechanism unresolved. The higher early-exposure EEG contrast is imprecise but prevents screening only on basal evoked gain. The adult human experiment separates synaptic amplitude and event frequency under fixed ligand, while the mouse receptor controls separate greater basal excitation from lower plasticity. Neither determines the direction in young WWOX tissue.

**Discriminating prediction.** In stage-matched WWOX and corrected preparations, assay extracellular adenosine, matched evoked release and total postsynaptic charge at the same measured free caffeine. Compare endogenous activity and fixed ligand. A loss of the early A1 brake predicts a larger or longer evoked glutamatergic response after the burst, with loss of physiological recruitment a separate rejection signal. An A2A-dependent effect can instead change activity-matched plasticity or axonal recovery without reproducing the A1-occluded acute response. An endpoint that improves only while resting excitability rises is insufficient. Reopen for preferential WWOX recovery after washout at attained free exposure, with preserved early inhibition and no prolongation of post-burst activity, or for paired clinical and mechanistic WWOX evidence that addresses those measurements. Review could change priority if it supplies such evidence. The paired preterm concentrations alone would narrow exposure without demonstrating the required response.
