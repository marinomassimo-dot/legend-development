**Deferred for WWOX pediatric trial priority. Retained as an adenosine receptor comparator.** The prescribed neonatal citrate formulation offers measured systemic exposure and a human brain target-engagement comparator. This strand asks whether persistent adenosine suppresses activity or maturation in a WWOX preparation with metabolic stress, and whether removing that signal also removes protection immediately after a burst. The retained CBD and inosine studies address transport and salvage. The new question is the receptor-side tradeoff at the same extracellular adenosine. ## Reused disease measurements and a new exposure comparison

Untreated WWOX **ADORA1** RNA was lower in week-15 human cerebral organoids (KO minus control **−0.52**), P17 mouse cortex (**−0.30**) and P17 mouse hippocampus (**−0.50**), with direction preserved on omitting one library at a time. In the pooled hippocampal nuclei, the excitatory-marker class went from **44.3 to 21.0 CPM**. These observations describe transcript abundance. Cell composition and a single nucleus pool per genotype limit inference. Lower RNA can coexist with high extracellular adenosine and gives no receptor reserve measurement. [Held bulk contrasts and omissions](../cannabidiol/transport_bulk.tsv) · [Held nuclei counts](../cannabidiol/cell_types.tsv)

The US caffeine-citrate label reports a **6–10 mg/L** caffeine peak in preterm neonates (about **31–52 µM**, calculated as `mg_per_L * 1000 / 194.19`), with CSF concentrations broadly approximating plasma. The report does not supply a region-specific free concentration or binding in this population. In adult A1 PET, the group fit used serial total plasma as the predictor. It found plasma **12.5 mg/L** for half-displacement (fit SE **1.5 mg/L**) and **5–44%** actual displacement. The study describes 15 adult men and 16 scans, including a repeat and one vehicle experiment. Applying its Eq. 1 to the neonatal systemic window gives **32–44%** as an adult-calibrated sensitivity comparison (`100 * C / (12.5 + C)`). The upper end modestly exceeds the observed adult concentration range of 0–9 mg/L. Neither neonatal nor WWOX occupancy can be read from this calculation. Formulation mass and measured caffeine base concentration are distinct. [US label, clinical pharmacology](https://www.dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=5f38c395-0093-4afd-89ec-f96e5dc0934a) · [Elmenhorst et al., pp. 1724–1727, Eq. 1 and Figs. 2, 5–6](https://doi.org/10.2967/jnumed.112.105114)

A receptor-selective immature rat comparison supports an A1 component of protection in cortical afterdischarges. A separate neonatal hypoxia model implicates A1 in ventricular enlargement and white matter injury. The age, injury and outcome differ from WWOX. These experiments motivate measuring acute network protection independently of chronic tissue effects and require direction to be established in the disease model. [Immature rat afterdischarge study, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24824175/) · [Hypoxia and A1 knockout, primary study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12975523/)

## Prediction to discriminate

Hold oxygen, glucose, age and stimulus burden fixed. In WWOX and corrected cells, measure an adenosine-resolved trace and evoked currents before and immediately after matched bursts. Compare a brief caffeine exposure, an A1-selective antagonist and an A2A-selective antagonist at individually measured engagement. Fixed extracellular adenosine is a separate arm. If a smaller response to antagonist persists in WWOX at the same adenosine level, reduced functional A1 reserve becomes plausible. If a genotype difference disappears under ligand matching, availability or clearance better explains the effect. If reducing tonic A1 signalling improves baseline conduction or maturation, the advantage must persist when acute activity is matched and after washout. Reject the WWOX benefit if improved resting recruitment comes with prolonged post-burst firing or reduced physiological inhibition at the same exposure. Directly distinguish excitatory and inhibitory input before attributing a net change to glutamatergic release.

**Pediatric disposition.** Existing oral and IV caffeine citrate experience concerns apnea of prematurity. Neonatal EEG and the adult cortical-slice study distinguish seizure burden, presynaptic-like event frequency and post-burst activity from resting recruitment. Reopen for preferential WWOX recovery at measured free exposure with preservation of the post-burst brake, or for attributable clinical WWOX evidence.
