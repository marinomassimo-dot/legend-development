# Does KARS1 provide an independent reason to prioritize lysine?

**Decision.** Park L-lysine hydrochloride for a WWOX pediatric trial. Fly heads retain a reproducible relative depletion, supporting a preclinical question. Mouse RNA does not establish a shared shortage of the enzyme that attaches lysine to tRNA. Reopen for reduced neural free lysine together with reduced absolute lysyl-tRNA and recoverable protein synthesis in a mammalian WWOX model, with competing arginine uptake and total protein intake controlled.

**Exact-sequence comparison.** Five reviewed mouse RefSeq mRNAs describe two protein products. We matched each complete annotated coding sequence, including its stop codon, against the six Kars cDNAs present in the Ensembl release 92 archive used to quantify mouse GEO libraries. Two archived transcripts contain the full coding sequence of the longer product, one the shorter product. The fourth transcript annotated as protein-coding (407 nt) contains neither full sequence. The two remaining transcripts are annotated as processed transcript and retained intron. Keeping these unresolved categories preserves the original abundance accounting. Protein identity does not measure subcellular localization, maturation, enzyme activity or tRNA charging. We summed transcript TPM by product **within each library** before taking log2(1 + TPM) and computing the KO–control difference of means. Omission ranges test single-library influence. They do not quantify uncertainty in assigning reads to the transcripts. In the archived cDNAs only 4.1–4.2% of possible 75-nt start positions in the long transcripts distinguish their product class from the other Kars transcripts outside that product class; the short reference product gives 5.2%. These are possible read starts within the single gene, without genome-wide specificity, positional bias, or actual supporting read counts. The state of the enzyme cannot be read from either the aggregate or a low-count isoform estimate. **Can the mouse estimates be independently re-counted at junctions?** On 24 November 2026 ENA public read-run queries for all 14 hippocampal and 7 cortical experiment accessions returned zero rows; the same query for six human organoid experiments returned six rows. This independently checks the earlier accession audit. The public mouse TPM files support the estimates above but cannot verify their supporting junction reads. The available human organoids cannot validate the regional mouse comparison. Additional review of the same abundance table would not resolve this missing measurement.
