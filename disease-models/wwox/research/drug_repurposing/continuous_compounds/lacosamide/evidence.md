# Lacosamide in WWOX deficiency: seizure ascertainment and the sign of a developmental effect

**Direct WWOX experience.** In Oliver et al., Table 2, three of 13 individuals had tried lacosamide (patients 6, 7 and 11). All three had drug-resistant epilepsy, with multiple agents listed. The table gives no individual seizure trajectory attributable to lacosamide. Seizure onset is not drug start. This verifies the pre-existing laboratory extraction and yields neither a failure rate nor a responder rate. [Oliver et al., Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1111/epi.17542) · [Held exposure records](../../advisor_pilot_20261113/brainstorm_20261116/it_i2_semiology_20261116/extraction_v1/exposures.tsv)

**Pediatric comparator.** In SP0967 (age one month to under four years), the Table 3 efficacy population included 116 taking lacosamide and 120 taking placebo. The adjusted placebo-relative reduction in daily electrographic focal seizures was 3.2% (95% interval −13.6% to +17.5%, p=0.69). Seven maintenance days provide little information about development. Of the first seven recordings that qualified at local review, five failed the central reader's baseline seizure criterion. The protocol subsequently used local counts. This small, selected reading check cannot estimate error across all recordings or explain away the null result. Somnolence was recorded in 15/128 vs 4/127 during treatment. A separate trial in ages four to under 17 found a 31.7% relative reduction in focal seizure frequency versus placebo (p=0.0003, 170 vs 168 in the comparison). Age, endpoint and observation window changed together. Neither trial reports a WWOX subgroup. In a future WWOX comparison, predefine the seizure type, keep focal events separate from spasms and background slowing, use repeat blinded reads, and record alertness alongside any change in behavioral function. [Makedonska et al., Methods and Tables 3–4, CC BY 4.0](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10963285/) · [Farkas et al., primary trial abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31462582/)

**Pediatric feasibility.** A US oral solution carries a focal-seizure indication from one month. Its cardiac conduction precautions cover other conduction-active medicines, relevant to background polytherapy. The availability of that formulation addresses one practical constraint. WWOX-specific central exposure, developmental benefit and a reliable attributable drug-response denominator remain open. [US label §§1.1, 5.3, 7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=458e80da-8fc1-4715-9b4b-bc4f388f933f)

**Contradictory binding measurements.** Wilson and Khanna reported a thermophoresis experiment for the marketed R enantiomer in a review. Labelled soluble wild-type CRMP2 gave an apparent affinity of about 1 µM, with three trials for the concentration curve. The five-substitution comparator showed no detectable response through 1 mM. Wolff et al. reported no specific signal with 0.1–1.45 µM free radioligand or with unlabelled drug at 0.39–100 µM by surface plasmon resonance. The concentration windows overlap. For a hypothetical single accessible site with Kd 1 µM, 1.45/(1+1.45) and 100/(1+100) give 59% and 99% fractional occupancy at equilibrium. These calculations are conditional. Protein presentation, labels, amount of accessible protein and signal-to-background can change detection. They give no estimate of occupancy in a living brain. No claim about the opposite enantiomer enters this comparison. [Wilson and Khanna, original measurement in Fig. 2B–F](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24944082/) · [Wolff et al., primary negative study, abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/22672303/)

**WWOX expression and alternative mechanism.** In untreated human WWOX organoids DPYSL2 increased (mean 650 to 908 normalized units, delta +0.47 in mean log2(1+x)). Juvenile mouse hippocampus showed the opposite sign (55.5 to 34.8 TPM, delta −0.67). Each contrast retained its sign on deleting one library. Mouse cortex was equivocal (delta −0.13, omission range −0.42 to +0.07). SCN2A fell in all three preparations, although the cortex omission range came within 0.002 of zero. SCN8A fell in both mouse tissues but its small positive organoid contrast changed sign on omitting libraries. Within-model sensitivity does not address biological replication across disease genotypes, developmental timing, cellular composition or post-translational state. **Discriminating observation.** The proposed anti-sprouting effect in acquired epilepsy has an uncertain sign in a developing, hypomyelinated system. Reducing new branches could limit recurrent excitation *or* remove functional connections. In WWOX-deficient and corrected preparations at the same developmental stage, compare lacosamide with a sodium-channel comparator matched for acute burst suppression. Count new and lost branches longitudinally per surviving neuron and check stimulus-evoked propagation and inhibitory recruitment after activity and exposure equilibrate. A drug-specific, persistent recovery of propagation with selective normalization of excess branching and orthogonally verified CRMP2 engagement would support the developmental hypothesis. Fewer branches with weaker propagation would count against it even if burst duration falls. Reproduction by the activity-matched comparator favors an activity-dependent explanation. Baseline excessive branching in the selected WWOX preparation is itself an unverified prerequisite. Continue the acute circuit arm by asking whether native WWOX discharge intervals allow block to accumulate at measured attainable unbound exposure while preserving the first evoked response.
