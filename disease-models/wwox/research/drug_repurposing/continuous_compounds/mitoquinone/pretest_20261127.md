# Mitoquinone mesylate and potential-dependent mitochondrial delivery in WWOX loss of function

`context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`. Planned comparisons are potential difference in mV, local redox species per mitochondrial mass, ATP-linked respiration and evoked neural recovery. No WWOX mitochondrial membrane-potential value is currently assumed.

**Selection.** Open mitoquinone after adjudicating mavoglurant. This returns to the supplement pathway with a test that differs from the earlier ubiquinol and idebenone experiments. The conjugated, monovalent C10 triphenylphosphonium cation makes enrichment sensitive to mitochondrial voltage. The starting chemical identity is oxidized mitoquinone mesylate, PubChem CID 11388331. Study records use several forms, including mitoquinone or mitoquinol bromide and oral mitoquinol mesylate. Keep the administered form, measured quinone/quinol species, and mass of salt versus active cation distinct. [Chemical identity](https://pubchem.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/compound/11388331) · [Cellular uptake and reduction, primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18294140/) · [Held ubiquinol comparison](../ubiquinol/)

**Prediction.** At matched extracellular unbound cation, selective recovery requires that WWOX cells accumulate usable mitochondrial mitoquinol and lose excess membrane lipid oxidation before respiration and evoked recovery improve. Orthogonal controls should distinguish a local quinone effect from linker/cation effects and the signal from a change in mitochondrial mass or recruitment. If depolarization dominates, the least polarized cells should show the weakest local enrichment at fixed free exposure. If the cation drives an apparent antioxidant response through reduced respiratory work, a chain-length-matched decyltriphenylphosphonium control should reproduce the change in respiration or proton leak, and native functional recovery should fail. Ubiquinol is a separate redox comparator with its own measured delivery. The sign of native WWOX mitochondrial voltage is an empirical question and may vary by cell type and activity.

**Reproducible pretest.** At 37 °C in an ideal monovalent equilibrium model, a 10, 20 or 30 mV drop in the magnitude of the matrix-negative potential multiplies the unbound matrix/cytosol ratio by 0.688, 0.473 or 0.325. These sensitivity values use `exp(-96485.33212 * drop_mV / (1000 * 8.314462618 * 310.15))`, holding cytosolic exposure and partitioning fixed. They refer to unbound ion. Membrane binding, reduction, uptake kinetics, plasma-membrane potential and ligand-dependent changes in voltage require separate observations. Whole neural tissue amount cannot specify a matrix concentration or the direction of neuronal rescue.
