# Mitoquinone mesylate in WWOX loss of function

`context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN`. Source scales are Jurkat cell and isolated rat liver mitochondrial concentrations and radioactivity, mouse whole-brain wet-weight chemistry, mouse hippocampal complex I activity and sucrose preference per litter, adult myocardial PCr/ATP, and WWOX mouse cortical and hippocampal bulk TPM, human organoid CPM and mouse hippocampal nuclear CPM.

**Decision.** Retain the oxidized mitoquinone mesylate as an exposure-conditional preclinical assay comparator. Park pediatric WWOX trial priority. The direct mouse comparison separates an enzyme result from a partly shared behavioral effect. BBB delivery, neuronal mitochondrial uptake and formation of mitoquinol remain separable, unmeasured gates in WWOX. The reliable next test is local active species, ATP recovery and evoked function against the chain-matched cation at matched recruitment.

## Clinical transfer

The MitoDCM registry reports actual enrollment **53** for the study and identifies oral **mitoquinol mesylate**, a reduced formulation. Its eligibility floor is 16 years. The paper gives arm age medians of 60 and 50 years and supplies no age-specific adolescent result. The study lists three registered primary outcomes, including the vanguard myocardial energy and circulating oxidative endpoints and the 12-month ventricular-volume endpoint. The article reports the 34-person vanguard comparison, 16 active and 18 placebo randomized, with **14 and 18** analyzed for the three-month myocardial PCr/ATP result. The adjusted difference is **−0.09** (95% CI **−0.24 to +0.07**). Article text gives p=0.27 and Fig. 1D p=0.25. Skeletal muscle PCr recovery time shows **−1.31 s** (Fig. 1D), with no clear separation and **14 and 17** retained. The reported 26 vs 49 adverse events are event counts, with 3 vs 2 at least possibly related and graded mild. The vanguard oxidative marker contrast was not reported in the letter, and the checked registry has no posted results. This documents adult capsule use and a functional readout without reconciling overall study efficacy or locating target engagement. The Pham primary abstract reports lower leak-state net peroxide emission with both MitoQ and CoQ10 and no clear change in adult muscle respiration. It supplies a redox-versus-function comparison without verifying a neural dose or response. [Halliday Fig. 1A–D and pp. 74–76](https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjci/jeaf310) · [NCT05410873](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05410873) · [Replay](heart_trial.tsv) · [Pham primary abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32458156/)

No age-relevant human CNS exposure, exact-form pediatric efficacy or compatible polytherapy comparison was identified in these sources. Earlier pediatric ubiquinol experience cannot fill that gap for a C10-TPP conjugate. A pediatric research design would first require a defined redox form and formulation, age-relevant exposure and tolerability and compatible background medications, with distinct EEG and functional outcomes. ## Discriminating test in WWOX and disposition

**Data.** P17 mouse hippocampal SDHA and SDHB RNA both fall (Δ log₂(1+TPM) −0.349 and −0.457, respectively, each retaining its sign on leave-one-library-out). The same two genes lack a consistent direction in cortex or human organoids. Gpx4 falls in hippocampus and in human organoids, with Prdx3 falling in mouse hippocampus and cortex and rising in organoids. Abcb1a falls in both mouse bulk tissues. Abcg2 falls in cortex only, and human organoid ABCB1 and ABCG2 levels remain below 1 CPM in both arms. The held mouse hippocampus nuclei supply one pooled library per genotype and no assigned endothelial fraction. These observations do not calibrate BBB efflux, lipid oxidation, complex II catalysis or mitochondrial mass. Wwox-null muscle's lower mtDNA signal supplies a non-neural example of the importance of the mass denominator. [Bulk RNA](wwox_bulk.tsv) · [Hippocampal nuclear RNA](wwox_nuclei.tsv) · [Held Wwox muscle comparison](../vigabatrin/decision.md)

**Inference and hypothesis.** If the untreated WWOX stage has lower mitochondrial voltage or lower capacity to reduce the quinone, the most impaired preparations may form less usable mitoquinol at the same extracellular exposure. Bulk Abcb1a direction provides a separate delivery hypothesis, with a potential change of the opposite sign before neuronal uptake. Among preparations with verified local uptake, a true quinone effect should precede native recovery and outlast matching for the chain/cation effect.

**Verification and counterproof.** Within a region and developmental stage, compare matched WWOX and corrected neurons with a glial comparison. At equal extracellular free cation, determine whether differences are upstream (barrier in an appropriate preparation), at mitochondrial uptake (calibrated plasma and matrix potentials and mitochondrial mass), or in reduction (intact quinone, quinol and major conjugates with species recovery). Then compare at matched local mitochondrial cation amount per mitochondrial mass, independently of extracellular dose. Track native oxidized membrane lipid alongside basal and ATP-linked respiration, ATP recovery after matched evoked work, and preserved initial evoked recruitment and inhibition. If the redox signal follows lower work, or matched decylTPP reproduces the ATP and functional response, the hypothesis of selective quinone rescue weakens. Ubiquinol is a separate comparator for membrane-redox rescue with its own measured uptake.

**Reopen** when a defined WWOX stage shows preserved physiological recruitment and selective local redox-to-ATP and functional rescue with verified free compound, or when age-relevant exposure and safety data change the feasibility of an interpretable study. An independent check would be decision-relevant at that stage or if the mouse preparation's treatment ordering and paired target exposure can be resolved. The current raw analysis gives no reason to treat enzyme recovery as an established circuit or pediatric effect.
