## Which chemical species reaches the developing CNS?

No direct oral-to-brain parent concentration in children was identified in the targeted search. A dog oral-distribution paper sometimes retrieved under “squalene” tested the hydrogenated molecule squalane (PMID 2744688). The radiolabelled mouse vaccine study used intramuscular emulsion (PMID 27498239). Neither is a basis for estimating dietary CNS exposure. A primary rat oral-tracer abstract (Tilvis & Miettinen, PMID 6855483) flags intestinal conversion before systemic distribution. Full-text verification of its denominators remains open. Any paired plasma and neural tracer analysis must identify intact parent and each labelled sterol separately and account for vascular carryover.

## Downstream conversion control

Shah (1972), Table 1, p. 77, representative experiment with duplicate determinations. With the same developing rat brain microsomes, replacing brain soluble fraction with liver soluble fraction increased recovery of squalene label in the lanosterol fraction from 0.86% to 6.62%, and in the cholesterol fraction from 0.53% to 1.21%. Difference in percentage points: +5.76 and +0.68, respectively. Conditions were separate fraction mixtures; these are recoveries of total label, not cholesterol yield from a molecule of lanosterol. Chromatographic fractions limit species assignment, duplicate assays do not estimate biological variance, and tissue disruption can change substrate presentation. The donor of a soluble fraction is not evidence that a specific carrier is deficient in WWOX. [Primary study](https://doi.org/10.1016/0014-5793(72)80020-6)

WWOX prediction: if parent uptake and early ring formation occur but later conversion limits the response, labelled lanosterol can increase while new distal sterols, first sheaths and conduction remain low. Match intracellular parent and carrier across genotypes and determine absolute distal pools as well as isotopic enrichment. Whole-cell tracer can partition into droplets, making equal total uptake insufficient to establish equal access to microsomal enzymes. Quantify subcellular parent distribution or show robustness across delivery conditions. Genotype differences that disappear on matching accessible substrate weaken a catalytic explanation. The current evidence favors retaining squalene as a probe, with low trial priority pending central exposure and selective rescue.

## Human positive control and disposition

Coman et al. studied three affected people in two families with recessive FDFT1 variants. The primary results narrative reports plasma squalene 0.17–0.93 μmol/L against a 0.36–1.04 reference interval. At least one value lies inside that interval despite a genetically established upstream defect. Total plasma farnesol (free farnesol plus farnesyl pyrophosphate as defined in the paper) was 1.5–3.9 μmol/L against a reference below 0.12. Even the lowest reported value is 12.5 times the reference cutoff. This is a comparator for substrate/product interpretation; individual paired values and sensitivity are unverified. A normal peripheral parent concentration would be a poor exclusion rule for a WWOX neural bottleneck. No WWOX plasma phenotype or supplementation response follows from this series. [Primary results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6037199/)

Disposition: park squalene for a pediatric WWOX developmental trial. The discriminating preclinical observation would combine verified neural exposure, tracer incorporation into distal sterols, first ensheathment of previously bare axons and conduction in stage-matched WWOX tissue. If upstream metabolites alone rise, examine substrate accessibility and distal conversion before interpreting the result. If myelin swellings fall without new ensheathment, classify the effect as sheath protection. Research lead retained.
