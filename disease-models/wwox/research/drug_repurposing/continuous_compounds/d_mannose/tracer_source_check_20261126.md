# D-mannose · tracer source and total glycan output

26 November 2026 · SCI_CODEX · targeted evidence · context_policy: QUESTION_DRIVEN

Ichikawa et al. differentiated mannose-derived from glucose-derived carbon in N-glycans using distinct carbon tracers at 5 mM glucose and 50 µM mannose. They report **25–30%** from exogenous mannose in normal human fibroblasts and **about 80%** in MPI-deficient fibroblasts. The larger exogenous fraction in MPI deficiency accompanies reduced glucose contribution and does not itself report total N-glycan occupancy or synthesis. In a separate experiment with deuterated water, about one-third of mannose-derived glycan mannose carried the exchange label, consistent with an MPI-dependent fructose-6-phosphate detour. The detour signal was absent in Mpi-null mouse embryonic fibroblasts. The fraction came from a different exposure/comparison than the 50 µM assay. [Ichikawa et al., Abstract, Results, Figs. 5, 6 and 9 captions](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3945336/)

**Comparison fixed for WWOX.** At a replete glucose baseline, distinguish three outputs for the same stage-matched preparation: absolute GDP-mannose and N-site occupancy, the fraction of glycan mannose supplied by each extracellular tracer, and the fraction progressing to mature glycoforms. Compare absolute amounts per viable cell or same cargo. A larger upstream label fraction with unchanged total occupancy and mature output indicates source substitution in this assay. Increased pool and occupancy without mature readout recovery favors a downstream processing bottleneck. Recovery of absolute occupancy and maturation at attained exposure is necessary to extend the substrate hypothesis. If mannose-derived carbon routing itself needs explanation, include the isotope-exchange comparator and downstream products and avoid treating origin label as direct passage. The fibroblast concentrations calibrate an assay control and provide no pediatric neural concentration.

A residue mass balance illustrates the distinction. When glycan-bound mannose stays at 100 arbitrary units, changing exogenous contribution from 25% to 50% increases the exogenous contribution from 25 to 50. If glycan-bound mannose increases to 140 units at the same change in fraction, the exogenous contribution is 70 units. Even the second pattern needs an independent measurement of N-site occupancy. Changing the number of mannose residues per glycan or the amount of cargo can change total glycan-bound mannose.
