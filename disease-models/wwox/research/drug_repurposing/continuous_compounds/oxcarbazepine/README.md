Oxcarbazepine is parked for an unselected trial after the [pediatric evidence assessment](pediatric.md). It is an AED previously used in WWOX-DEE with measurable human CNS metabolites. The discriminating question is whether the circulating stereoisomer mixture during oxcarbazepine therapy can preserve recruitment of inhibition while limiting recurrent firing during clustered bursts in WWOX neural circuits.

Oliver et al. Table 2: 2/13 have OXC in their trialled-medication list; neither has the drug underlined as current. Both have drug-resistant epilepsy. Exposure duration, seizure-type-specific response and reasons for stopping are unknown. This does not provide a response rate. Reproduce the extraction by running clinical_count.py on the original Oliver XML (PMID 36779245); output clinical_count.tsv reproduces the table column order.

The [adult CSF reconstruction](exposure.md) resolves parent and both licarbazepine enantiomers. Adult exposure is an initial bracket, not pediatric free brain interstitial exposure. At matched total free metabolite concentration and matched burst suppression, compare the measured mixture with the predominant S-enantiomer alone and vehicle. During the same stimulus train measure excitatory-cell action potentials, interneuron spike fidelity and evoked inhibition in WWOX and controls. A lower burst count accompanied by deterioration of inhibitory recruitment weakens the hypothesized circuit benefit. Persistence of a genotype-specific effect across mixtures would weaken a stereoselective explanation. Establish attainable concentrations and pediatric applicability before trial prioritization.
