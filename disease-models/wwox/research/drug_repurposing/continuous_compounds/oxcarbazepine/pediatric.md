## Pediatric seizure-type and age boundary

Protocol 2440 compared high against low oxcarbazepine exposure as adjunctive therapy for focal seizures with a behavioral correlate in children aged 1 month to under 4 years. FDA statistical review, sections 3.1.2.6–8 and Table 13. Of 128 randomized, 116 contributed to the ITT efficacy analysis. The primary absolute change in seizures per 24 h favored the higher exposure (p=0.043). After adjustment for baseline burden, the nominal within-age treatment contrasts were p=0.9762 (under 6 months, n=17), 0.8218 (6 to under 12 months, n=24), 0.8962 (12 to under 24 months, n=34) and 0.0204 (24 to under 48 months, n=41). In the oldest group the respective low/high median changes were −0.57/−1.97 events per day. The adjusted analysis used residuals and cannot be reproduced by subtracting these two medians. Small strata and exploratory subgroup comparisons do not establish an age interaction or exclude efficacy in younger children. The pooled result supplies limited support for extrapolation to WWOX infantile spasms or developmental outcomes. [FDA reviewer analysis](https://www.fda.gov/media/92267/download) · [Trial](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16275822/)

Decision: retain a seizure-phenotype-specific mechanistic probe; low priority for an unselected pediatric WWOX trial. Reopen for a selective effect on pathological bursts at measured attainable exposure with preserved evoked inhibition, or interpretable longitudinal WWOX focal-seizure responses with timing and co-medications. Open research question: does native low-threshold calcium current at the relevant age respond to the observed mixture independently of sodium-channel train suppression?
