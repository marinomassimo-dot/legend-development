# Suvorexant pretest, 28 November 2026 UTC

**Context policy:** SYNTHESIS. This question was frozen at 07:07 UTC after discovery abstracts and before extracting figures and tables. The compound is distinct from the SV2 branch and current lab assignments. Treat rodent exposure, receptor antagonism, spontaneous seizure rates and sleep-state composition as different observables.

**Alternatives.** (1) Orexin signalling directly raises the hazard of seizure recurrence even within the same vigilance state. Antagonism should reduce seizures per at-risk hour within matched state and circadian bins at verified local exposure. (2) Antagonism changes only how much time the animal spends in high- and low-risk states. A composition-only model should reproduce the aggregate change with control-state hazards and treated-state times. (3) Behavioural scoring, generalized depression or unrelated pharmacology explains an apparent benefit. Electrographic counts and recruitment, especially the first evoked response, should separate these explanations. Retain transitions as a separate bin if the data resolve them.

**Test.** Start with same-compound spontaneous video-EEG in a genetic rodent epilepsy model. Recover exposure, elapsed time and animal denominators. Compare reported marginal seizures with the composition-only expectation from separate control-state rates and treated-state durations. If the joint data are missing, bound what the available marginals can identify and keep within-animal rates unresolved. Use provoked seizures only as a separate countercheck. Compare the OX1R and OX2R exposure margins against independently measured pharmacology in their native assay units. Put total, free, plasma and brain concentrations into separate columns; do not assign occupancy from concentration alone. Check WWOX expression only in represented regions and developmental stages. An orexin source outside the sampled regions cannot be ruled out by these data.

**Decision rule.** Reopen as a WWOX functional candidate only for a state-matched electrographic gain, at measured local exposure, that preserves recruitment and survives state-reweighting. A gain limited to altered time in state retains a sleep-state comparator role. A sleep-state shift without replicated electrographic gain limits the claim to state perturbation. No clinical efficacy inference follows from a rodent model or a change in sleep alone.
