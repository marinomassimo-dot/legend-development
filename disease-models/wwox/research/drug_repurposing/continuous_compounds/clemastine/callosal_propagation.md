## Reproducible matched comparison

**Data.** The horizontal bars in the published Brain Fig. 5D read approximately 2.43 to 3.17 ms for N1 and 6.64 to 8.29 ms for N2, S-Control to S-KO (this note's own readback of the plotted bars, not numbers the paper states: slice-level recordings, 8 slices per group from five control and six S-KO mice aged P13 to P17; the legend does not say whether the bars are means or medians and states no millisecond unit). The plotted summary of the N1/N2 amplitude ratio falls from about 2.76 to 0.85. Absolute N1 and N2 values are unavailable at the group level. The 0.31 ratio-of-ratios is compatible with an illustrative 69% loss of absolute N1 if N2 were fixed, or about 3.23 times absolute N2 if N1 were fixed. These examples apply a common scaling to individual N1 or N2 amplitudes in a reference group. They illustrate non-identifiability and do not estimate individual or group amplitudes.

**Inference.** A later peak at one geometry can reflect slower travel, a distance-independent loading or relay offset, or a different mixture of activated fibers. Two limiting models share the published N1 gap (0.74 ms) and can also share the observed myelinated profile and amplitude ratios. If the gap comes entirely from slower travel, at half the reference path it is predicted to be 0.37 ms. If it comes entirely from a fixed offset it remains 0.74 ms. At twice the reference path the corresponding gaps are 1.48 and 0.74 ms. A ±2-pixel readback sensitivity for each plotted line gives 0.28–0.46 versus 0.56–0.91 ms at half distance. This is measurement-readback sensitivity within the two illustrations. Sampling error and physiological variability are unestimated. The reference path is normalized and the vertical offset supplies no absolute velocity. The models are deliberately limiting cases. Recruitment and dispersion can produce a slope change when the mixture of fibers changes across recording positions.

## One discriminating comparison for INV-06

**Hypothesis.** If clemastine restores myelin-dependent propagation in Wwox-deficient axons, the knockout-by-treatment contrast should improve the slope of tracked early callosal response latency versus measured path length at comparable absolute recruitment. If the main change is recruitment, timing offset or a switch in the dominant peak, the improvement will concentrate in the intercept, absolute fiber volley or waveform shape and may disappear when timing is tracked for the same response at matched recruitment.

**Test and stopping rule.** Use the same juvenile callosal tract in matched Wwox and control animals with and without clemastine, with verified local exposure and measured initial axonal excitability. Record early onset, peak latency and absolute volley amplitude or area across multiple measured along-fiber distances and overlapping recruitment levels. Preserve the input–output curves, since a failure to reach comparable recruitment can indicate axonal loss or dysfunction. Analyze animal-level differences with slices nested within mice. Pair function with total and myelinated profiles from the same tract within prespecified caliber bins, alongside survival and g-ratio of already myelinated fibers. An increase in the cross-sectional myelinated fraction can support structural recovery. Identifying first-sheath acquisition or persistence additionally requires tracking the same axons over time. A slope change with stable recruitment and preserved survival, together with structural improvement, would justify reopening the compound. A marker-only response, an intercept shift, or apparent rescue confined to a changing waveform mixture keeps clemastine a preclinical comparator. No WWOX treatment priority follows from the current figures.
