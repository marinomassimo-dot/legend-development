# What change in axon density could explain the WWOX callosal result?

The existing [WWOX full-text and figure audit](../../../../research/deepdive_manifests/PMID33914858.json) already extracted the relevant mouse counts. Repudi et al., Figure 5B, report about 180 and 55 myelinated profiles per callosal field in neuronal controls and knockouts at P17. The simultaneous unmyelinated counts, 55 and 270, come from the **optic nerve**. A fraction constructed from these two tracts has no anatomical interpretation. Fields are nested within three animals per genotype. [Primary study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33914858/)

Let r denote the knockout/control ratio of **all eligible callosal axons per sampled area**. The knockout/control ratio of fractions myelinated is (55/180)/r. If axon density is unchanged, it is 0.306. At r=0.5 it is 0.611. At r=0.306 the fractions are equal despite a large decline in counts per field. For r=0.25, the ratio of fractions is 1.22, possible only where the absolute control fraction permits this value. This is a sensitivity calculation, not an estimate of r. A reduction in first-sheath probability is supported by this comparison if eligible axon density is shown to exceed 30.6% of control. Recover the denominator in the same field and diameter bins; pooling across fields requires comparable area and animal-level weighting.

The human organoid analysis instead supplies an explicit proportion from two control and three knockout organoids, without the per-organoid counts needed for a precision estimate. Its endpoint supports a distinct follow-up in a human system. The laboratory's prior Tcf4 assessment measured the density of already ensheathed profiles and compaction. Improved compaction is compatible with a persistent deficit of first sheaths. In the WWOX co-culture, replacing the genotype of neurons while preserving the glial genotype implicates a neuronal contribution, but the morphology endpoint alone does not distinguish an altered axonal contact signal from fewer eligible axons. These contrasts provide two ways to reverse the direction of an apparent drug benefit: normalize evoked current to an unknown maximum, or normalize myelinated profiles to an unmeasured population of eligible axons. In a WWOX rescue comparison both absolute propagation and caliber-matched sheath initiation must be measured, with glial survival evaluated separately.

**A pediatric comparator.** Qilu Hospital's randomized Williams-syndrome crossover study enrolled 28 participants, eligible ages 3–12, and is registered as completed on December 30, 2025. Its March 19, 2026 record has no results posted. Recent barbiturate or sedative use was excluded, limiting transfer to children on sedating antiseizure combinations. This is a feasibility precedent for studying developmental myelination in another genetic syndrome; the registry supplies no estimate of benefit or tolerability. [ClinicalTrials.gov, NCT06315699](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06315699)

**A closer genetic comparator changes the interpretation.** In Barak et al., excitatory forebrain neuron-specific Gtf2i deletion induces a myelin phenotype although the oligodendrocytes retain their Gtf2i. At baseline Figure 3B measures the fraction of all axons myelinated (three mice per group), and Figure 3D/E measures evoked latency and amplitude. After two weeks of clemastine, Figure 5 instead reports oligodendrocyte counts, g-ratio in already myelinated axons and social preference. Figure 5B samples 281 axons from three vehicle mutant mice and 419 from four treated mutant mice. Recovery of thickness in this neuron-driven model provides evidence that some neuronal myelin phenotypes remain drug-responsive. It leaves the change in first-sheath fraction under clemastine undetermined. The acute latency rescue in Figure 4 was obtained with 4-aminopyridine. Attribution of that rescue to clemastine would change the ranking without evidentiary support. [Barak et al., Figures 3–5](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31011227/)
