# JBPOS0101 and exposure-linked electroclinical recovery in WWOX loss of function

**Selection.** JBPOS0101 is the single investigational antiseizure agent identified in the infantile-spasm registry by that code. The open-label phase 2 study enrolled children eligible at 6–36 months and posted aggregate plasma and safety results. This is a distinct evidence opportunity after carisbamate, for which the pediatric publication did not supply accessible absolute exposure values. The WWOX relevance is an endpoint and exposure question. A direct target, a molar conversion that depends on verified identity, and disease-specific response remain to be established. **Prediction.** If free parent preferentially interrupts late pathological recruitment, a matched-drive WWOX preparation should show fewer or shorter late recurrences with early excitatory and inhibitory recruitment and post-train recovery preserved. If apparent suppression reflects generalized activity reduction, these physiological readouts should decline alongside the late events. An improved behavioral count with persisting electrographic events in a controlled comparison would weaken the first interpretation.

**Counterchecks.** Select molecular comparators only after confirming compound identity, analyte, assay exposure and binding or functional evidence. Distinguish ordinary firing, motor or vigilance suppression, electrographic background, and electroclinical spasms. Keep missing and unevaluable follow-up distinct from observed unfavorable outcome. Treat a change accompanying titration and time in an uncontrolled cohort as non-attributable without a suitable comparator. Use confirmed analyte and age-dependent sampling to evaluate a free brain exposure bridge.
