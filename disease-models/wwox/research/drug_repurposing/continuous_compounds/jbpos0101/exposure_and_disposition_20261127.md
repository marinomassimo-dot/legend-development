# JBPOS0101 in WWOX loss of function

**Disposition.** Park pediatric WWOX trial prioritization. Retain an exposure-conditional preclinical comparison. The infant trial establishes systemic exposure and reports adverse events. The planned electroclinical outcomes and their evaluable denominators are absent from the posted results. There is no demonstrated exposure window for selective circuit benefit in WWOX.

Values are aggregate plasma concentrations, with no free fraction reported. ## Inference, hypothesis and discriminating observation

**Inference.** The adult PK paper describes proposed mGlu4 activation and mGlu1/5/7 inhibition in its introduction. Its experiment measures drug interactions. No public concentration-response for JBPOS0101 at those targets was verified here. An independent primary counterexample to assuming that mGlu7 inhibition always suppresses excitation comes from surgical human tissue. A different selective mGlu7 negative allosteric modulator, ADX71743, increased action-potential-independent spontaneous EPSC frequency from 1.0 to 2.3 Hz in six responding cells (five cortical and one amygdala) with unchanged amplitude at peak. The responding-cell estimate gives no responder fraction. Adult mouse SC–CA1 experiments also support activity-dependent mGlu7 suppression of GABAergic transmission. These observations permit opposite network directions depending on the synapse and stimulation. [Hwang, Introduction](https://doi.org/10.1111/cts.13892) · [Ciobanu et al., Fig. 6C–F](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41380-025-03202-x) · [Klar et al., SC–CA1 experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4429158/)

**Hypothesis.** At verified free exposure, mGlu4 engagement can reduce evoked glutamate and late recruitment while preserving initial drive and inhibitory transmission. mGlu7 inhibition could instead increase spontaneous excitatory release or relieve an activity-dependent brake on GABA release during stronger trains. mGlu1 inhibition offers a distinct postsynaptic explanation through lower dendritic calcium at fixed glutamate input. The KO cortex and human organoid expression results give no common direction for weighting these effects.

**Discriminating observation.** First verify free parent concentration and response across recombinant human mGlu1, mGlu4, mGlu5 and mGlu7, testing drug alone and modulation of glutamate-evoked signal. If engagement is measurable, compare vehicle and JBPOS0101 in stage-matched KO and control preparations. At matched first evoked recruitment, separate spontaneous glutamate, train-evoked glutamate, inhibitory charge, postsynaptic calcium and late recurrence. Test subtype-specific occlusion at concentrations with verified selectivity. A fall in train-evoked glutamate and selective loss of late recurrence that disappears with mGlu4 occlusion supports the mGlu4 branch. Increased miniature EPSC frequency or preserved/increased inhibitory charge after stronger trains, sensitive to mGlu7 occlusion, distinguishes compartment-specific mGlu7 effects. Calcium reduction at fixed synaptic input supports a postsynaptic branch. Reduced late recurrence with attenuated physiological recruitment, or benefit only at unverifiable free exposure, fails the registered comparison. [Registered prediction](pretest_20261127.md)

Reopen clinical feasibility on a public release of the planned efficacy tables with evaluable denominators and background treatments by summary group. Such data would support a descriptive estimate. A WWOX-directed development decision separately requires a target-specific functional effect within a verified free exposure window and preservation of physiological recruitment. Any pediatric design would need interpretable electroclinical ascertainment and separate vigilance and respiratory safety measures. Independent review could change the decision if a new efficacy table or primary receptor exposure-response becomes available. The current checkpoint retains the circuit comparison as conditional and closes routine searching for this compound.
