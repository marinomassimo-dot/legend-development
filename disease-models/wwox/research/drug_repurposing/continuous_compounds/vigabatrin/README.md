# Vigabatrin, inhibitory transmission and mitochondrial nucleotide salvage

**Opening prediction.** Revisit this antiepileptic through a second function of its target, mitochondrial GABA transaminase (ABAT). Sustained impairment of nucleotide supply could limit mtDNA maintenance during repeated activation in energetically vulnerable WWOX cells even if GABA breakdown and spontaneous bursts fall. A rival is reversible network suppression with preserved mitochondrial function after activity is matched. Test GABA-T activity, dNTP pools, mtDNA per nuclear genome and per mitochondrial mass, evoked recruitment and respiratory recovery at successive time points after a measured S-vigabatrin pulse and washout. Include proliferation, viability and cell-type composition as alternative causes of altered mtDNA per nucleus. A WWOX-specific liability would require a genotype-by-exposure difference after matching initial ABAT inhibition and workload; a proportional response in both genotypes would suggest a general drug effect. Determine whether the tested WWOX neural models show a broad salvage deficit, a substrate-side disturbance only, or no consistent pattern. Use ABAT, GAD1/2, ALDH5A1, SUCLG1/SUCLA2, NME4, TK2, DGUOK, RRM2B, POLG and TFAM. RNA alone cannot identify depleted nucleotides or altered mtDNA copy number. **Untreated WWOX evidence.** ABAT means fell in juvenile mouse cortex (102.4 to 75.4 TPM) and hippocampus (83.4 to 40.4 TPM), but rose in human organoids (90.3 to 140.0 CPM). The sign in each model survived single-library omission. Three other prespecified genes, NME4, DGUOK and RRM2B, fell in all three models under every single-library omission. TK2 rose in the organoids. Nucleotide availability, ABAT protein activity, cell fractions and mtDNA were unmeasured. Cortex used four control and three KO libraries, hippocampus two and three, organoids two and four. This nominates reduced nucleotide reserve as a modifier worth measuring even when ABAT RNA increases. A global salvage failure is unsupported. **Paired pharmacology.** Besse et al. exposed a murine retinal 661W cell line in low serum with daily medium replacement for ten days. The strongest comparison is within the experiment: at 200 µM nominal vigabatrin mtDNA was about half of control, and adding deoxynucleoside triphosphates prevented the decline. The prevention experiment does not demonstrate that intact triphosphates entered mitochondria. A subsequent study from the same group measured GABA-T activity and mtDNA in the same human T98G glioblastoma cell model under daily resupply for ten days. At 100 µM GABA-T activity was about 50% of control. At 200 µM residual enzyme activity was about 25% while mtDNA was about 50% of control. This yields a separation of endpoints within one condition. It gives no WWOX genotype effect, temporal order or proof that human MRI changes originate from mtDNA depletion. The two publications share investigators. [Retinal source, Fig. 6 and methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4757431/) · [T98G source, Fig. 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5131463/)

**Pediatric plasma versus brain.** Mean active S-vigabatrin plasma peaks in six neonates and six children aged five months to two years were 14.0 and 13.9 mg/L respectively, approximately 108 µM in each group using molecular mass 129.16 g/mol. In six older children the corresponding mean was 23.8 mg/L (184 µM). Each report gives separate R and S plasma measurements after the racemic formulation. Four-day morning plasma measurements cannot determine irreversible enzyme inhibition, recovery of ABAT protein or chronic mitochondrial exposure. The retinal experiment used nominal vigabatrin in culture without a chiral concentration measurement. Neither plasma S nor racemate alone supplies a retinal or neuronal mitochondrial exposure. US pediatric use for infantile spasms includes a risk of permanent visual loss. **Human brain reanalysis.** Shannon et al. reported extracellular vigabatrin after severe traumatic brain injury in people older than 16. The assay pooled enantiomers. An unknown compound co-eluted with vigabatrin and was present before exposure. Using individual values from S2–S3, the first-dose raw median catheter peak was 2.41 µM (11 catheters, nine people). Subtracting each catheter’s own mean predose signal under a constant-background assumption gave a median 1.46 µM. Giving each person equal weight by averaging their two catheter peaks when two were present gave 1.94 µM. At the third dose the corresponding values were 5.22, 3.78 and 3.23 µM (ten catheters, eight people). The largest third-dose peak was 72.9 µM in an abnormal brain region in one patient, with no measured baseline interference. Among the eight with paired observations, seven had a higher average extracellular signal after the third dose (median paired change +2.02 µM); a constant additive background cancels in the within-catheter difference. Probe recovery was calibrated in vitro only and the predose background could vary with time. These data describe extracellular fluid after injury, not the intracellular active enantiomer in an infant with WWOX loss. The marked variation and progressive exposure argue for measuring intracellular S, ABAT recovery and organelle function under repeated pulses, even when plasma troughs remain stable. **Human WWOX and next decision.** In one child with two null alleles, spasms resolved after vigabatrin was added to prednisolone, followed by other seizure types and persistent profound developmental impairment. Two published WWOX cases had new symmetrical thalamic and pallidal MRI changes during treatment. Neither observation measures mitochondrial nucleotide pools. A previously audited TSC/comparison cohort also found similar imaging in seven of 52 unexposed children. No WWOX denominator for excess susceptibility follows from the case pair. The next comparison is whether a postmitotic WWOX cell with low nucleotide reserve experiences a delayed mtDNA/respiration difference at matched GABA-T inhibition, cellular exposure and network activity. Examine the native neuronal versus glial uptake evidence and check whether the bulk WWOX nucleotide signal survives cell composition. Preserve seizure, MRI, retinal and developmental endpoints separately. **Within-class check.** In the GSE158684 hippocampal nuclei, aggregate Abat fell 14.71 CPM, with −19.78 CPM from within-class differences and +5.08 from changing class weights. Rrm2b fell from 18.91 to 12.56 CPM in the excitatory neurons and from 21.19 to 20.79 in inhibitory neurons; astrocytes moved in the opposite direction (25.32 to 32.54). Nme4 also rose in astrocytes, with only 9 control and 23 KO counts there. Dguok decreased in all three classes. Keep excitatory and inhibitory neurons and astrocytes separate when interpreting nucleotides and evoked recovery. Each genotype supplies one pooled library. Counts cannot show an animal-replicated genotype effect or an exposure response. [Counts and denominators](cell_types.tsv) · [Decomposition](mixture.tsv) · [Code](nuclei.py)

**Active uptake changes the meaning of extracellular exposure.** Schousboe et al. found active uptake of S-vigabatrin in cultured neurons and astrocytes, with a higher-affinity component detected only in neurons (Km 78.2 ± 20.3 µM). This estimates uptake kinetics in that system, not irreversible target inactivation or a therapeutic threshold. In an independent mouse experiment, continuously exposed for 12 days, enantiomer-specific S retina/plasma partition was 6.4 and 5.2 in the two exposure groups; corresponding brain/plasma values were 0.42 and 0.60. The implied S retina/brain concentration ratios are 15.2 and 8.7. For R the corresponding ratios are 1.13 and 0.86. Samples were eight pools of three mice per group. These are total tissue estimates calculated by the authors with an assumed plasma S/R ratio of 0.75. They establish tissue-specific accumulation under those conditions, but provide no unbound mitochondrial concentrations or measurements in infants. A retinal mtDNA response at nominal 200 µM cannot be assigned to cortex by correcting only for plasma. Next test how long ABAT activity remains suppressed after a pulse, whether mtDNA loss emerges at equivalent inhibition across cell types, and whether a compensatory copy-number change affects respiration.
