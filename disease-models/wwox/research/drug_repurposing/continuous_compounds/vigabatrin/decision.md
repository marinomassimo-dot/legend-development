# Vigabatrin: GABA synthesis, active enzyme and integrated exposure

In six people with refractory complex partial seizures, occipital magnetic resonance spectroscopy after the first exposure showed an increase of 2.0 µmol GABA/g within 81 minutes. The median edited signal stayed essentially stable for two days, and after the second exposure it increased by only 0.5 µmol/g within 72 minutes. This compares two responses in the same treatment sequence; accumulation and substrate feedback confound any inference of receptor adaptation. A separate serial CSF series of 11 patients found neurochemical changes outlasting detectable vigabatrin in blood and CSF: analyte concentrations rose by six hours and remained elevated for up to five to seven days. The two studies sampled different compartments. Neither measured ABAT recovery in brain mitochondria. Selective cell cultures reported maximal GABA-T activity within six days of withdrawal, and greater endogenous GABA release at a nominal 25 µM. Do not equate time to maximum recovery to a fitted enzyme half-life. In rats, time and repeat exposure changed how brain GABA related to brain and platelet GABA-T inhibition. In that study the association between platelet enzyme and brain GABA was present at 4/24 h and undetectable at 48/72 h. A peripheral enzyme assay at late times cannot substitute for a neural turnover measurement. All four items read at author-abstract depth. PMIDs 10403220, 3143561, 2757904, 10448817.

**A metabolic rival to nucleotide depletion.** At 24 h in anesthetized rats (PMID 8897466), mean cortical GABA concentration rose 1.3 to 2.7 µmol/g, residual GABA-T activity was 40%, and model-estimated GABA synthesis fell 0.14 to 0.04 µmol/g/min. A simple stock/flux scale moves from 1.3/0.14 = 9.29 min to 2.7/0.04 = 67.5 min (7.27-fold). These are ratios of reported estimates. They do not measure washout half-life, and the fitted synthesis result depends on a two-pool model. The same study reported no detectable difference in total tricarboxylic acid-cycle flux (treated 0.47 vs control 0.52 µmol/g/min). In a second experiment at 5–6 h (PMID 16517019) isotope-derived synthesis from labeled glucose was 0.031 vs 0.037 and from labeled acetate 0.078 vs 0.084 µmol/g/min (control vs vigabatrin, p=0.30 and 0.42 respectively). A constant GABA pool with reduced residual enzyme can be maintained when more substrate drives that enzyme. These experiments used different time points, models, tracer designs and absolute control rates. They cannot establish a longitudinal switch in one animal. The second's null tests do not establish equivalence. Taken together, an elevated GABA pool alone leaves both net shunt flux and carbon availability unresolved. Moreover, the earlier study demonstrates that reduced modeled GABA synthesis need not manifest as a fall in the whole cortical TCA rate.

**Prediction to discriminate mechanisms.** Measure early GABA amount, labeled turnover and residual ABAT together. Subsequently measure nucleotides, mitochondrial DNA per nucleus and mitochondrial mass, and respiration on defined non-GABA substrates in the same cell types under comparable work. Early altered GABA turnover with preserved nucleotides bounds the early metabolic cost; a later nucleotide-specific rescue of mtDNA *and* oxygen flux despite continued inhibition would strengthen an additional salvage mechanism. Compare cells and media for nucleoside breakdown and uptake before ascribing extracellular dNTP rescue to a specific mitochondrial step. Record nucleotide interventions as experimental discriminators. No biomarker in these studies demonstrates WWOX hypersensitivity. The denominator check below compares the original cellular assays with independent evidence from Wwox-deficient tissue. **Direct Wwox evidence outside the brain.** Abu-Remaileh et al., PMID 30755385, Fig. 3J: conditional muscle deletion of Wwox, four mice per group. The plotted relative mitochondrial DNA signal in skeletal muscle fell to about 0.6 against a control near 1.0; this is a visual approximation. The adjacent Fig. 3I shows lower expression of several slow-twitch muscle markers and Fig. 3K lower expression of several oxidative enzyme transcripts. The paper describes Fig. 3J as quantitative PCR. The supplemental primer table at the end of mmc1.pdf is actually headed Table S2 (the main RNA methods point to S1); it lists mitochondrial and nuclear B2m targets together. A mitochondrial-to-nuclear ratio is consistent with those reagents, but the panel and RNA methods do not explicitly define its calculation or normalization to independent mitochondrial mass. The authors interpret it as lower mitochondrial quantity. At the tissue level, more glycolytic fiber composition, less mitochondrial mass within fibers and fewer genomes per unit mitochondrial mass all remain possible. This is direct evidence of a Wwox-linked mtDNA signal in peripheral muscle. It supplies neither a neural mtDNA phenotype nor a vigabatrin interaction. It strengthens the case for a denominator-matched comparison while arguing against interpreting a fall in the neural mtDNA/nuclear ratio as selective nucleotide failure. DOI 10.1016/j.molmet.2019.01.010.

The mild ABAT paper's T98G day-10 measurements (Fig. 2) used an mtDNA/nuclear DNA ratio. Its oxygen-consumption measurements (Fig. 3) concern inherited-ABAT patient fibroblasts, normalized to post-assay cell counts across five wells per sample. They cannot supply a missing respiratory readout in the drug-exposed retinal/T98G arms. In the 2015 paper the respiratory-complex studies are normalized to citrate synthase and concern other specimens; one patient muscle biopsy had elevated citrate synthase (~240% of mean control). The strongest control for a drug-induced mtDNA-specific effect is still missing at the level of matched treated neural cells.


**Disposition.** Park vigabatrin for a disease-modification trial. Preserve the observed symptomatic responses in the separate epilepsy record. Retain the mitochondrial liability as an experimentally testable question. A selective deficit in nascent mtDNA per unit organelle mass and sustained respiratory capacity after washout, corrected by restoration of nucleotide availability in WWOX-deficient neural cells at matched residual ABAT and GABA turnover, would reopen the question. A genotype difference explained by mitochondrial biomass, cell composition or persistent changes in GABA turnover would weaken that interpretation. The existing pediatric experience concerns seizures. The WWOX-specific frequency of imaging events, visual risks relative to comparable treated children and developmental benefit remain unknown. No adverse-event attribution follows from the present analysis. Codex Strategy supplied a substantive independent review in STRATEGY_21_CODEX, read by SCI_CODEX on 24 November. In quiescent genetic ABAT-deficient fibroblasts, Fig. 5B–C adds lower phosphate-state and salvage-defect controls to the retinal pharmacology in Fig. 6A–B. Technical PCR triplicates and the MTND1/B2M ratio still leave organelle mass and cell selection unresolved. A downstream nucleotide bypass can mask an upstream carbon-flux impairment.
