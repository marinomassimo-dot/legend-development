# Acetazolamide in WWOX-related epilepsy

**Decision.** Park pediatric trial prioritization. Retain the hypothesis that intracellular carbonic anhydrase activity helps sustain long discharges in a subset of developing WWOX networks. The strongest comparator changes cumulative time in prolonged discharges while shortening the interval between them. The exposure and the clinical phenotype determine whether this observation transfers. [Slice data and juvenile WWOX counts](burden.md)

## Two pediatric precedents

For CHD2 epilepsy, 13 children received acetazolamide and one was lost to follow-up. All 12 analyzed had photosensitivity. In two (patients 6 and 12), spontaneous seizures had already stopped on their background medicine. Six were seizure-free at the final assessment, including those two. Patient 2 lost complete control after six months. Seven of the 12 reported treatment durations in Table 1B are six months and three are at least a year. Their median is six months and their maximum 45 months. The results narrative gives a median follow-up of 13 months and maximum 3.5 years. These descriptions cannot be substituted for each other without information about treatment and assessment dates. No controlled comparison or infant WWOX response is available from this series. [CHD2 study, Tables 1A–B and Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11450644/)

For SLC6A1 epilepsy, all six received add-on therapy. Three were seizure-free at last assessment. The six treatment durations total 180 months, of which 132 come from one patient. The median is 7.5 months, falling to six months when that case is removed. Two cases reached a year. One endpoint entry is exactly 50% reduction: five of six exceed 50%, while six of six reach it. Photosensitivity could be followed in only two affected children. Different syndromes, ascertainment and therapies make a pooled efficacy proportion inappropriate. [SLC6A1 study, Tables 1A–B](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12716284/) · [Recalculated denominators](cohorts.tsv) · [Calculation from source tables](clinical.py)

In the 13-person WWOX series the median onset was five weeks. Eleven had focal seizures and ten had spasms. All had optic atrophy. An evoked photosensitivity endpoint requires a measurable baseline. Blind assessment of cumulative electrographic seizure duration plus event count, duration and preserved evoked responses would be more interpretable for the specific late-burst hypothesis. The treatment histories list phenobarbital in 6/13, topiramate or zonisamide in 4/13, and valproate in 8/13. These are counts of ever-listed medicines. Their timing relative to a hypothetical intervention is unknown. The latter two carbonic anhydrase inhibitors complicate target attribution and tolerability. ## What free exposure would match the slice comparison?

One pediatric epilepsy **abstract**, pending full-text verification, reports mean total plasma acetazolamide of 6.2 µg/mL. At molecular weight 222.25 this is 27.90 µM. Four younger adult volunteers had plasma unbound fractions of 0.032–0.057, with a reported mean of 0.040 (Table 2). Applying this adult fraction to the pediatric mean gives a *scenario* of 1.12 µM free plasma (0.89–1.59 across those adult fractions). A free brain-to-free plasma ratio near 9 (range 6.3–11.2) would be required to match the single 10 µM rat bath condition. This assumes equilibrium, comparable timing and negligible bath binding. These ratios are requirements under the stated assumptions. They are not observed pediatric ratios or a minimum effective concentration. Cytosolic free drug at the enzyme also need not equal extracellular fluid concentration. Lower exposure could affect prolonged discharges. That part of the concentration-response curve is unmeasured here. [Pediatric abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11934510/) · [Reproducible exposure sensitivity](partition.tsv) · [Calculation](partition.py) · [Adult binding study, Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1463727/) · [Rat preparation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4884091/)

**Competing explanation.** In one adult taking phenobarbital and phenytoin, the mean of two paired phenobarbital CSF:serum ratios was 0.515 before acetazolamide, 0.585 during use, and 0.523 after withdrawal. That is a 13.7% relative increase during coadministration. Absolute phenobarbital CSF remained high after withdrawal while serum varied. Phenytoin showed no similar increase. This is a reason to compare concomitant drugs and their free concentrations when assigning mechanism. A single adult cannot establish an interaction size for WWOX. The CSF data measured the other medicines. [Sato et al., Table III](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/yakushi1947/102/11/102_11_1067/_pdf)

**Reopen** for preferential shortening of late WWOX electrographic discharges at a directly supported free exposure, with normal evoked activity preserved, matched pH and co-medications, and results reproduced across independent preparations. To advance a developmental claim, also require a durable independent functional outcome, such as conduction in an age-matched preparation. The opposing hypothesis is that suppressing residual glial carbonic anhydrase could diminish pH recovery during repeated activity. The decline in glial Car2 RNA motivates testing that consequence but does not establish its sign.
