# Acetazolamide and bicarbonate-dependent excitation

**Question before measuring.** Revisit the laboratory's pH work from the perspective of GABA currents and developmental stage. Can acetazolamide reduce the late depolarizing response to repeated GABAergic stimulation in WWOX-deficient neurons without simply suppressing the entire network through acidification? The laboratory has already tracked changes in extracellular pH after the drug. The new comparison asks whether intracellular bicarbonate regeneration, chloride loading or bulk acidification limits the response. [Earlier pH measurements](../../ph_k2p_sci45_20261111/output/decisive.tsv) · [Earlier chloride comparison](../taurine/native_chloride.md)

**Discriminating prediction.** At matched extracellular pH, CO2, temperature and initial activity, a larger drug effect on the late response during repeated activation than on the first GABA response would be consistent with bicarbonate depletion. If the WWOX phenotype instead reflects a persistently high intracellular chloride concentration, the first response can remain depolarizing and suppressing bicarbonate regeneration need not normalize it. If matching extracellular acidification reproduces the full network effect, generalized pH action remains sufficient. Absolute spike probability and charge transfer matter: a depolarizing response can still shunt excitation. Clinical seizure suppression and correction of a developmental WWOX phenotype need separate evidence.

**First check.** Examine CA7, CA2, neuronal chloride transporters and the age dependence of the relevant carbonic anhydrase activity. Tissue RNA may mix glial and neuronal CA2 and does not measure intracellular catalytic activity or bicarbonate current. Seek evidence from developmentally matched recordings, inhibitor-versus-genetic comparisons, achievable exposure and pediatric experience with concurrent antiseizure medicines. Regulatory labels flag metabolic acidosis and overlapping carbonic anhydrase inhibition. The WWOX metabolic phenotype supplies a reason to compare baseline acid handling, not a prediction that every child has acidosis. [Acetazolamide label](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2024/008943s053%2C009388s039lbl.pdf)

**First WWOX comparison.** CA2 and the chloride exporter SLC12A5/KCC2 fell in both mouse cortex and hippocampus but rose in human week-15 organoids. Omitting any individual library preserved those directions. CA7 was low in organoids, with mean normalized signal 0.13 in controls and 0.21 in knockout, and showed a small decline in mouse cortex. The hippocampal CA7 decline lost sign in one omission. Mouse bulk CA2 could partly reflect oligodendroglial composition or maturation, while net GABA currents depend on neuronal chloride and bicarbonate and firing history. The data favor separating cell classes and examining the late GABA response in already-recorded neural experiments before inferring the direction of acetazolamide's effect.
