# WWOX, GSK3 beta and eIF2B: the chain tested link by link (label gsk-p, receipts suffix 70)

Non-canonical research material, not medical advice, no dose. The OpenScientist report was a question generator and was not used as evidence. Tables: `chain.tsv`, `evidence.tsv` (E01 to E28), `residues.tsv`, `hypotheses.tsv`, `experiment.tsv`, `queries.tsv`, `receipts/`.

## Answer
The chain is not established. Only L2 has support, and none of it is in WWOX. L1 rests on one single-lane Ser9 blot, L3 is split, L4 and L5 are unmeasured in any WWOX system. "Reduced eIF2B activity without a rise in phospho-eIF2 alpha" was tested by no source read. The path does give hypotheses that differ from "inhibit the ISR", but they are tests of the premise first.

## DATO
- L1 (WWOX observation): Wwox-null mouse brain, postnatal day 20, Ser9 phosphorylation of GSK3 beta falls with total unchanged (E01, `FTR-20260804-32000863-01`, cited not re-read). One lane per genotype per region, no activity assay, heterozygote not intermediate on the printed densitometry (E02; the manifest's 'in every region' sentence is false in two of three regions); P20 follows seizure onset in a malformed brain, so the change may be secondary. In SK-N-SH, WOX1 knock-down "enhanced phosphorylation" of GSK-3 beta, residue and direction not stated, abstract only (E04).
- L2 (external precedent): GSK3 inhibition dephosphorylates eIF2B epsilon Ser535 in CHO cells (12133000, abstract) and in rat cardiac myocytes (15001529, abstract); in mouse retinal ganglion cells Gsk3b deletion lowers phospho-Ser535 and Gsk3a deletion does not (26974342, partial full text; E13, E14).
- L3: 12133000 says the dephosphorylated site did not give significant eIF2B activation ("additional inputs are required") and the inhibitors also alter protein synthesis by other routes (E06, E07). Against it, in retinal ganglion cells the phospho-resistant epsilon raised new-protein labelling and a catalytically dead epsilon removed the Gsk3b-deletion gain (E15, E16): a labelling assay, not an exchange assay.
- L4 and L5: S535A gave axon regeneration but no survival benefit (E17); cardiac S535A reproduced the hypertrophy only in part (E11, E12). Lithium in Wwox-null mice acts also in wild-type and heterozygote (E03). No WWOX translation or eIF2B measurement found (queries Q03, Q-E1, Q-E3, Q-E4).
- ISRIB: its action depends on phosphorylated eIF2 and is small without it (E20 to E22, E24); it restores stability and exchange of destabilised mutant eIF2B (E25; every rescue assay there included 5 nM phospho-eIF2, and the epsilon mutants rescued are not near Ser535) but not pocket mutants or all other lesions (E26). The ISRIB authors name regulation by subunit phosphorylation as an untested context (E23). No source tests ISRIB against a phospho-epsilon lesion.
- Residues: the papers' "Ser535" is rat numbering. Human Ser540, mouse Ser536, rabbit Ser540, hamster Ser535; priming serine four residues downstream (rat 539, human 544, mouse 540; UniProt annotates DYRK2 there). Local window identical in 24 of 32 positions across human, mouse and rat; the two serines are identical in all. GSK3 beta Ser9 equals GSK3 alpha Ser21; kinase domains 260 of 285 identical by my alignment, tails about half (`residues.tsv`).

## INFERENZA
- GSK3 and phospho-eIF2 alpha are not interchangeable inputs: phospho-eIF2 binds the regulatory cleft and ISRIB acts through opposing conformations of the same cleft (E24), whereas the GSK3 serine sits in a predicted disordered segment next to the catalytic W2 domain. ISRIB compensating a GSK3-site defect is therefore a prediction to test, not a premise.
- A mutation labelled "S535A" in mouse needs the species sequence checked (mouse numbering is 536).
- Phospho-Ser535 is higher in adult than in developing retina (E18): the brake may be stage dependent, relevant to a developmental disorder.

## IPOTESI (compound, action, context, benefit, what refutes it: `hypotheses.tsv`)
H1 two GSK3 inhibitors of different class as tools (no repurposing candidate named; the IT15 abstention stands; Ser535 dephosphorylation is documented for LiCl, SB-415286 and SB-216763 only, not for LY-2090314, laduviglusib or TDZD-8). H2 GSK3B versus GSK3A knock-down. H3 phospho-resistant and phospho-mimetic epsilon with a catalytically dead control. H4 ISRIB as a discriminating probe. H5 the null: the GSK3 route is not engaged.

## NON RISOLTO
Whether GSK3 beta activity, phospho-epsilon, exchange activity or translation change at all in WWOX-deficient human neurons or glia; whether site phosphorylation alone changes exchange activity (the one source that tested it, 12133000, was read at abstract only); the isoform in these cells; the dependence on phospho-eIF2 alpha.

## Priority question and next experiment
Priority: is phospho-epsilon at the GSK3 site raised in WWOX-deficient neurons at matched stage, with phospho-eIF2 alpha measured beside it? If not, the chain breaks at L1 or L2 and H1 to H4 stop. Minimum design (`experiment.tsv`): isogenic WWOX-deficient and corrected neurons; baseline phospho and total GSK3 alpha and beta, epsilon at both serines, eIF2 alpha, ATF4; then lysate exchange assay and per-cell labelling with S-to-A, S-to-D and catalytically dead epsilon, isoform knock-down, two inhibitors, ISRIB, a rapamycin arm. In silico, LEGEND can do now: the public-dataset search for Wwox-null phosphoproteome or translatome (not run here) and the residue table (done). Everything else needs a laboratory.

## Existing consumers to update (proposals; nothing edited, nothing promoted)
