## 0. For the operator
**What this is.** A design brief for a laboratory that already works with Wwox-knockout mice: knockout, heterozygous and wild-type pups given a standard seizure-provoking test, with and without the two drugs Cheng 2020 reported to reduce those seizures in knockout pups (ethosuximide and lithium), plus reference drugs and a wild-type age series. **It cannot show** whether any drug would help spontaneous seizures, whether a drug works after seizures start, how a drug works, or anything about people.
**What may not be ready.** Whether any laboratory has the line and capacity is unknown. Knockout pups are scarce (about one in four from a heterozygote cross) and fragile; a test of "different between genotypes" on the full-hour area summary is **not attemptable** at the published variability, unless the pilot finds a far less variable stimulus or summary (for example the incidence of generalised seizures).
**[OPERATOR DECIDES]** Whether to approach any laboratory (none is named for this experiment), which and in what order, what if anything to offer, and whether to attach the full brief or only this page. A laboratory will ask who pays, who owns the data and what credit or authorship it has: this brief promises none of it and no agent can; the laboratory should be told that nothing is promised until the operator says so. **Not to be confused with the slice package** (`outreach_adv042_sci_20261118/`): a different brain-slice experiment with its own priors; no shared animals, priors or dependency; the slice experiment comes first or in parallel.

## 1. The question, in one sentence
In Wwox-null pups, are the seizure-suppressing effects reported for ethosuximide and for lithium specific to the knockout, or are they the same proportional effect any anticonvulsant of that kind would have in a pup of that age, and does lithium act through GSK-3?

**Why this question.** The only in vivo drug data in a Wwox-null animal that the repository holds are one experiment (Cheng 2020, PMID 32000863, global knockout, "preweaning" pups, pentylenetetrazol-provoked seizures scored 0 to 6, one laboratory): lithium lowered the stage in all three genotypes; ethosuximide lowered it in the knockout and was not significant in wild-type and heterozygous pups. **No genotype-by-treatment test was reported or can be computed from the figures** (mean ± SEM curves, one-way ANOVA; no per-animal values; the test age is not stated; the knockout PTZ-alone curves of the two drug panels differ in height by eye, so different cohorts appear to have been used; wild-type stage is about 1 on 0 to 6, close to the floor). A claim of knockout selectivity rests on a difference in significance. By eye the proportional reduction by lithium looks similar in the three genotypes, which fits a general or regimen effect better than a knockout-enhanced one.
**Readings to separate for ethosuximide:** (E1) knockout-selective; (E2) age-limited, with a wild-type floor (rat pups lose the PTZ effect of ethosuximide below about 25 days in Prague abstracts; mouse ontogeny not found by two routes; one mouse pup datum for a different seizure type, Cortez 2004, PMID 15582027, abstract only); (E3) a general anticonvulsant effect, proportional in every genotype. **For lithium:** (L1) general, against (L2) specific to GSK-3 in the knockout (p-Ser9 is lower in untreated knockout brain, Cheng 2020 Fig. 7c, one representative blot per region; lithium engagement was not measured; lithium has other targets).

**Timing limit: state the age and the structural state beside every effect.** In the germline null report, of the structural findings **only the E16.5 cortical thinning precedes seizure onset** (seizures "commonly observed after postnatal day 12"). The doublecortin increase is at P14 (n = 5), brain-size and weight reduction at P20, and the apoptosis ratio (about five times control) and cerebellar vermis fusion at about P19 to P20 (per IT's reading): concurrent with or after seizure onset. The paper states no causal sequence, and "brain-wide" is not its word. A preventive or early arm cannot be read as a reversal in respect of the E16.5 finding only.

**Sizing flag, read this first.** IT's eye read of Cheng 2020 Fig. 7b (knockout PTZ-alone curve, N = 6; SEM bars of about a third to a half of the mean) suggests an SD of S of 0.7 to 1 times the mean, about 0.6 to 0.8 on the log scale, which puts a **simple** effect of ratio 0.7 at roughly 44 to 79 animals per arm; the SD of 0.3 used in section 3 is the **optimistic** case, and at the pessimistic scale the **interaction is out of reach**. The interaction contrast needs **four equal cells** (treated and vehicle, in knockout and wild type): at 44 per cell about 176 animals for one drug against vehicle, 88 of them knockouts; **75 litters is about 525 pups** (plus the panel and the age series) and about 132 fragile knockouts. (Corrected in the ADV-061 paragraph of section 3, kept beside it; see `ORDERING_PAGE.md`, conflicts.) The brief plans a pilot first and promises no interaction test.

## 2. Design frame (fixed before the first animal)
1. **All three genotypes (+/+, +/-, -/-) in the same session, at the same postnatal age** (**a session = one assay day at one assay age; cells are filled across litters, with litter as a block for the vehicle groups where numbers allow; a litter yields only about 7 pups, so all nine genotype-by-arm cells cannot come from the same litters**; heterozygote crosses, expected 1:2:1; knockouts die by about 4 weeks and are fragile). Vehicle per genotype in every session; **within a session the ethosuximide, T-type, lithium, second GSK-3 and comparator arms share one vehicle group per genotype**; the panel shares the vehicle of its age (item 4). **Allocation:** block randomisation of arm within genotype and litter, scarcest genotype first, unequal cell sizes accepted and reported; n per cell reported in cells, animals and litters. **Blinding:** drug syringes coded by a person who does not score; **genotype read before the test by a person who does not score**; the scorer blind to genotype and arm; **every trial video recorded and scored from the coded video by a scorer who was not present**. Complete blinding to genotype may be impossible where knockouts are visibly smaller; blinding to arm is then asked and the limit stated. Litter is recorded as a cluster; sex is recorded.
2. **Assay age defined by the laboratory** (Cheng 2020 does not state it) as the age at which knockout vehicle pups reliably show provoked seizures and can still be tested. **Documented ages in the sources read:** behavioural spontaneous seizures commonly after P12 (Cheng 2020; no EEG); cortical ECoG interictal-like spikes and generalised spike-wave discharges in null pups from about P14 (a 2026 report; n = 5 per group; nothing recorded earlier, so onset may be earlier); slice bursting at P13 to P17 (Breton 2021); death at about three to four weeks; no EEG before P14 exists in the sources read; the laboratory confirms from its own colony. **State the chosen age against the chloride-gradient window:** no Wwox system has a measured E_GABA; the one mouse study that suggested a P11 to P14 window (Kasahara 2019, full text read, groups of 4 to 5) is confounded (clonic incidence at P11 not raised, 5 of 5 in saline; the worsening is in latency, threshold temperature and LFP events; bumetanide did not block the latency effect and alone lowered incidence; 5 of 5 deaths on high-dose diazepam at both ages; near-normothermia in the co-treatment arms), and no other source supports it. An NKCC1-blocker arm is **not** proposed (no brain-engagement control, narrow pup age effect, ototoxicity and diuresis signals in neonatal trials); the age series straddling P11 and P14 covers the cheap functional read.
3. **A wild-type age series** (no null animal needed): wild-type pups, same stimulus and scale, at the assay age and **at least two older ages inside the window to about P28**, vehicle against each test agent; knockouts at the assay age only. Purpose: to see whether a wild-type non-response is age.
4. **An assay-sensitivity panel in separate wild-type pups at every age, sharing that age's vehicle group** (a null is read only at an age where the assay can show a drug effect; mouse valproate effect unknown, so validity is established empirically per age and a failed panel is an assay failure, not a negative): a **flat-profile** agent (valproate protects against PTZ at every rat age tested), one **rising-profile** agent (carbamazepine, or cannabidiol, efficacy rising with age in rat PTZ abstracts) and, if an NMDA-receptor arm is included, one **falling-profile** agent (ifenprodil or Ro 25-6981: ifenprodil effective in mouse at P7 and P10 and not P14 or P21, in rat up to P18 on one assay and proconvulsant on afterdischarges at P18 and P25; Ro 25-6981 effective at P12 and failing at P25 in rat). **All agent names are examples from the rodent literature only, not proposed for any use; the laboratory chooses** (abstract level, one origin for many rat rows: `adv054_age_window_sci_20261118/`). Diazepam is age-dependent in rat pups (status epilepticus not stopped at P9, stopped at P15 and P21; abstract level). **The one mouse datum, febrile induction worsened at P11 and not at P14, does not show a direction change:** its source (Kasahara 2019, full text read, groups of 4 to 5) is confounded (see 2.2: clonic incidence at P11 was not raised, 5 of 5 in saline; the worsening is in latency, threshold temperature and LFP events; bumetanide did not block the latency effect and alone lowered incidence; 5 of 5 deaths on high-dose diazepam at both ages; near-normothermia in the co-treatment arms), and the P11-versus-P14 direction claim of 9.5 of the original rests on it; 9.7 (2026-11-18) weakened the chloride-window reading drawn from the same study (that this also removes the 9.5 claim is my inference from 9.7, not a statement of the original). A direction change between P11 and P14 is a rat-level possibility, not a mouse finding, and the age series is the more needed for that reason; the panel names no benzodiazepine.
5. **A stimulus that gives wild-type pups a measurable non-floor response**, fixed in a pilot; it is the laboratory's own published standard assay; no number is proposed.
6. **Outcomes per animal:** (a) S = area under the stage against time over a **60-minute window (as in Cheng 2020; any other window stated before data)**, stage per 1-minute bin as the maximum in the bin; **a pup that dies keeps stage 6 for the rest of the window, and a pup rescued under the stopping rule keeps its last stage carried forward; both flagged, results reported with and without them** (primary); (b) **seizure type**: incidence and latency of minimal seizures (stages 1 to 2), **intermediate (stage 3, unilateral forelimb clonus with myoclonic jerk: its own category, included in S)**, generalised seizures (**stages 4 to 5 with a tonic phase, as the hashed priors file defines "generalised" for P8; stage 4 without a tonic phase (bilateral clonus) on its own line; stage 5 (loss of righting, tonic-clonic) separately inside the generalised count**) and **status, three or more tonic-clonic seizures within the window** (Cheng 2020). Reason: in rat pups several agents suppress generalised seizures while **raising minimal seizures** (MK-801 at P12, memantine, phenobarbital at P7 and P12, carbamazepine at P7 and P12), and wild-type pups sit at minimal-seizure level, so a mean stage can cancel two opposite effects.
7. **Analysis fixed before data:** R(genotype) = mean S treated over mean S same-genotype same-session vehicle, on the log scale; **interaction ratio = R(-/-) over R(+/+)**; animal the unit, litter the cluster (bootstrap over litters); an effect = R at most 0.7 with an interval excluding 1; a null = interval inside 0.85 to 1.15; otherwise unresolved (an interval containing 1 and excluding 0.7 is reported as "an effect of 0.7 or larger is excluded"). **Interaction thresholds** (reciprocal pair): genotype specificity = at most 0.7 or at least 1.43; **no genotype specificity = 0.7 to 1.43** (as in the priors file, E1, E3 and P5). **If vehicle S in a genotype is zero or at the floor, R is not computed and the arm is not interpretable.** With about 1.75 knockouts per litter the bootstrap over litters is the primary interval; with fewer than 10 litters in a cell a permutation test or a mixed model with a litter random effect, stated before data. The absolute-scale interaction is secondary. **Shared vehicle and multiplicity:** where five test arms share one vehicle group per genotype it is about twice the size of a test arm (square-root rule); the five comparisons are reported at 95% and 99% (Bonferroni for five); ethosuximide is the pre-specified primary, judged at 95%, every other "effect" confirmed only if it holds at 99%. A bounded scale makes a proportional effect look smaller in absolute stage where the baseline is low, hence the ratio scale and the floor rule. **Order of hypotheses:** (i) panel validity at each age; (ii) ethosuximide effect ratios in knockout and wild type at the assay age and older ages, descriptive with intervals; (iii) the interaction ratio with its interval, **exploratory unless the pilot SD makes it sized**.
8. **A motor and ataxia pre-test for every arm, reported per animal as a covariate; mandatory for ketamine-class and other NMDA-receptor and sedating agents** (ketamine causes ataxia at the lowest exposure in rat pups, more in younger ones, abstract): the laboratory's own standard; a pup is excluded only if it fails the laboratory's pre-specified criterion before the stimulus; exclusions listed.
9. **Regimen and dose checks (fixed before data).** **One regimen is the main lithium contrast: the Cheng 2020 repeated pretreatment (three injections within one hour before a low-dose PTZ challenge), the replication condition**; the laboratory states any other regimen before data. No regimen in the sources shows a clear sign flip against PTZ (the one acute pro-convulsant abstract is from one laboratory family, interval and method unreadable, full text closed; ADV-051 rows L05 to L09 hold acute pro-convulsant and chronic threshold-raising in one mouse PTZ paper family), so a **second regimen is an optional comparator arm** (a single injection, PTZ about 30 minutes later), not a design variable. **A lithium-alone arm is added, with a sedation, righting-reflex and weight readout at the PTZ time and the recorded interval from the last injection to PTZ** (Cheng's panel has none; a low stage cannot tell protection from reduced behavioural output). Because potency shifts with age and **no pup exposure measurement was found for any agent**, "an effect of the drug" is defined only with a **dose check per age: two exposures per agent (or a published pup exposure) at each age, or a measured exposure if available (not required)**; a null at an unchecked exposure is a **dose null, reported as unresolved**.
10. **Humane endpoints and approvals are the laboratory's:** about half of PTZ-injected knockouts reached status in Cheng 2020; a status stopping rule (rescue by the laboratory's approved procedure; the pup stays in the analysis with its last stage carried forward, flagged), written before the first animal, is a requirement.
11. **For any chloride-transport arm added** (none is proposed): a wild-type-on-agent group (a bumetanide effect on latency was the same in wild type and mutant in one model); assignment by animal within litter or analysis by litter (litter-level randomisation analysed per animal was a confound in the one genetic-DEE pup dataset); benzodiazepine tests timed from the onset of status; a sedation measure; developmental endpoints kept separate from timing endpoints.

**Sizing arithmetic (ASSUMED values, not a forecast).** Cheng 2020 used 6 to 7 knockout and 12 to 20 wild-type or heterozygous pups per group; a litter yields about 1.75 knockouts. A simple effect of ratio 0.7 (ln = -0.357) needs about 11, 31 or 79 animals per arm for a within-genotype SD of 0.3, 0.5 or 0.8 (two-sided 0.05, power 0.80). A genotype-by-treatment interaction contrast of the same size needs about four times as many per cell. For a binary outcome such as generalised seizure (assume 50% in vehicle knockouts), a fall to 10% needs about 20 per arm and a fall to 20% about 38. **Three knockout arms at 11 per arm would need about 19 litters; at 31 per arm about 53 (simple effect only).** For the **interaction** about 44 per cell at SD 0.3, about 132 knockouts in three arms, **about 75 litters**, out of reach at SD 0.5 or more; so the knockout interaction is realistic only if the pilot SD is about 0.3 or less, and even then needs about 75 litters across the three knockout arms (the original arithmetic; the ADV-061 paragraph below corrects the per-cell number to twice, not four times, the simple-effect number, and gives 22 per cell, 88 animals and 25 litters for one drug against vehicle at the optimistic SD of 0.3), and otherwise stage 2 answers through the order of hypotheses in 2.7, and the brief says so rather than promising an interaction test. Wild-type and heterozygous animals are not limiting. **A stage that runs in another session carries its own vehicle group per genotype.**
**Total for the required stages with the dose check (for planning, not a promise):** about 27 cells (wild-type age series: 3 ages times vehicle, ethosuximide at two exposures, one flat-profile and one rising-profile panel agent at two exposures each = 21 cells; knockout and heterozygote at the assay age: vehicle and ethosuximide at two exposures = 6 cells), about 300 animals at 11 per cell or about 1,350 at 50 per cell, of which 33 to 150 knockouts (19 to 86 litters). **The pilot decides the dose check first**: if the lower exposure is effective in the panel agent at an age, one exposure per agent at that age suffices and the totals fall.
**ADV-061 addendum (2026-11-18; record `fig7_quant_ethosuximide_lithium_sci_20261115/README.md`, section "ADV-061 addendum"; hypothesis level).** From the means and upward SEM bars of Cheng 2020 Fig. 7b and 7d (per-minute SD = SEM times the square root of N; S = mean stage over 60 minutes; independent to fully correlated minutes; N assumed constant): (1) **the knockout PTZ-alone groups give a CV of S of 0.58 to 1.19 at a within-animal correlation of 0.6 to 1.0 (log SD about 0.54 to 0.94; rigorous upper bound 0.67 for 7b and 0.94 for 7d), about 36 to 109 animals per arm for a ratio of 0.7**; "log SD 0.6 to 0.8" is inside that bracket and the optimistic SD of 0.3 is not credible (it needs a within-animal correlation of 0.05 to 0.15). (2) **Wild-type and heterozygous pups at that stimulus are at the floor** (mean S 0.27 to 0.41, CV above 1.4): a ratio of 0.7 would need about 140 to 210 per arm, so a non-floor wild-type stimulus is a requirement. (3) **Correction of the arithmetic above:** an interaction contrast needs **twice** (not four times) the simple-effect number per cell (4 (z sigma / delta)^2): about **180 to 320 animals per cell**, four cells, about 700 to 1,300 animals and 350 to 630 knockouts (200 to 360 litters) for one drug against vehicle (22 per cell, 88 animals, 25 litters at the optimistic SD of 0.3). **IT's independent read of the same curves (ADV-063) agrees within about 20 percent (SD of the summed stage 9.5 to 62 and 10.9 to 69 against 11.4 to 71.5 and 9.2 to 56.9 here) and concludes that the sizing flag stands.** **Limit:** whether dead animals keep stage 6 or leave the mean is not stated; the order of magnitude stands. **The genotype-by-treatment contrast is therefore not attemptable with the full-hour area summary**, only if the pilot finds a far less variable stimulus or summary (for example an incidence of generalised seizures). The litter and animal totals above are the optimistic scale: three knockout arms at 36 to 109 are 108 to 327 knockouts, about 62 to 187 litters.
**If resources allow only one step:** the pilot (stage 1), then the ethosuximide arm with the age series and panel (stage 2). **Required:** stages 1 and 2 with the panel and the age series. **Optional:** the T-type arm (needs an engagement control), stage 3, stage 4, and the add-ons of section 5. The animal counts are illustrative until the pilot gives the SD.

## 5. What this experiment cannot show, and the optional add-ons
**Cannot show:** spontaneous-seizure burden (no video-EEG in the basic design); acute rescue after seizure onset; any mechanism readout; development or survival effects; any statement about people. A provoked-seizure result in a pup says what a drug does to a PTZ seizure at that age, not what it would do to the null phenotype.
**Cheapest add-ons (all optional; none changes an arm, size or tier):**
(a) video-EEG on a subset of knockout pups (the only route to spontaneous-seizure burden). (b) **p-Ser9 and total GSK-3beta in tissue from the same lithium-treated and vehicle animals** (Cheng 2020 measured p-Ser9 only in untreated animals; its Methods and legend disagree on the age, day 14 against day 20). (c) A **Cav3 transcript or protein readout** from the same tissue (none in Wwox-null tissue was found by two routes, HE's ADV-052).
(d) **Deletion completeness (ADV-076):** **record it per animal and per region at the protein level** (immunostaining or a per-slice check) and report each effect against it; **residual bulk transcript is not deletion completeness** (control cortex about five times the hippocampal Wwox TPM; non-neuronal Wwox in bulk tissue; one of three knockout cortices inside the control range at P17; stage and deletion depth not separable at n = 3). The ADV-076 note states this for the slice pilot and the pup brief in the words "a Synapsin-Cre deletion"; how it applies to a germline null is the laboratory's.
(e) **A glucose series tied to seizure timing (ADV-074): optional, not a diet or glucose arm.** The held originals do not show whether hypoglycaemia drives seizures and death or only accompanies them (no held original manipulates glucose; the rescues normalise glucose, growth, seizures and survival together). Gaps: no glucose before P10 (null about 95 against 130 mg/dl at P10 to P11, about 58 at P14, about 57 at P19, read from plots); none at the time of any seizure; none ever reported for the neuron-targeted or progenitor-targeted seizure-and-death lines. Measurements: **blood glucose in the seizure-and-death lines (P7 to P14), glucose at seizure time (same-animal pairs with video-EEG or ECoG, the sampling burden stated), ketone and lactate by age, milk intake or stomach filling.** **The ketogenic-diet comparison is unchanged** (its rationale neither gains nor loses decisively; no change of tier).
(f) **A heterozygote carrier readout (candidate, one source).** In the germline-null report (3-week-old mice; chloral hydrate; legend SEM; one laboratory) transcranial motor-evoked-potential **onset latency was 1.39 +/- 0.13 ms in wild type (n = 10), 2.13 +/- 0.22 ms in heterozygotes (n = 5; P < 0.05 against wild type) and 2.44 +/- 0.37 ms in nulls (n = 4; P < 0.01), amplitude 59.2 +/- 9.0, 59.6 +/- 17.2 and 11.8 +/- 5.4 microvolts**: a **latency prolongation in heterozygotes against wild type, with amplitude not different from wild type (59.6 against 59.2 microvolts, n.s.; n = 5, large spread)**; the authors write "a partial Tc-MEP deteriorative change"; **Fig 2c prints a bracket between heterozygotes and nulls on latency marked n.s., and Fig 2b one on amplitude marked * (heterozygote against null), although the Methods name only a one-way ANOVA with no post hoc test, so the test behind the brackets is unnamed and heterozygotes are not shown to differ from nulls on latency**. The Results call the measure "onset latency" while the Methods define latency as shock-artefact onset to the major positive peak, and the knockout line used for the recordings is not stated. This brief carries only the heterozygote bursting incidence.
(g) **Myelin or conduction endpoints.** The endpoints with demonstrated neuronal-rescue evidence are structural (electron microscopy myelinated-axon counts per animal, g-ratio, MBP and CC1). **Callosal conduction latency was measured once**: n = 8 slices from 5 animals (controls) and 8 from 6 (knockout), the control n identical to another experiment's S-HT n (possibly copied), effects read from graphs (first-peak latency about 2.5 against 3.2 ms), neuron-targeted line only, acute slices P13 to P17, no rescue, no velocity: **a candidate needing re-measurement with a rescue arm and animal-level statistics, not yet an endpoint.** The held latency is a single-distance first-peak latency (no velocity), the unit is the slice, recorded in neocortical layer V after stimulation of the corpus callosum; the N2 (second-peak) latency, whose identity is not defined in Repudi 2021 (the unmyelinated or partially myelinated label is Palma's) and which is not significantly different (P = 0.5737; wide spread, 8 slices per group), is a candidate within-slice control that the brief does not yet carry; hypothesis; no compound, dose or schedule is chosen):** four cells in the same litters (wild type and knockout, vehicle and drug), because in healthy developing wild-type mice the pro-myelinating drug clemastine (one compound, Palma 2022; ## 7. Optional, conditional: a development-endpoint cohort for lithium on the AAV9 comparator's own endpoints (ADV-058 unit 2; text by IT; hypothesis level; no dose)
* **Not an arm of this design nor of the slice brief** (the provoking injection confounds survival, GFAP and myelination; about half of PTZ-injected knockouts reached status in Cheng 2020). A **separate cohort**, litter-blocked, with its own vehicle-only knockout group; the treatment start age is the laboratory's.
* **Only lithium qualifies** (with a second GSK-3 inhibitor of another class if run); the sign of GSK-3 inhibition for myelination is divided across studies. **Ethosuximide, the NMDA-receptor pore blockers and d-APV, and vigabatrin do not qualify**; valproate belongs to the provoked-assay comparator list only.
* **Endpoints (the comparator's own, age fixed beforehand):** survival to a fixed end age beyond day 17 with a stated censoring day (primary; untreated knockouts die by about day 17 in the comparator's panel); body-weight series from birth; one composite motor score at a fixed age; GFAP-positive cells; myelination; brain size and weight at about day 20. No power calculation (no variance is held for a drug arm).
* **A comparison with AAV9 would not allow:** prevention against rescue; equivalence of mechanism; durability and dose; a safety comparison (a drug needs its own harm endpoints, for lithium renal and thyroid readouts as the label lists); pup pharmacokinetics are unknown.
* **Condition for running it:** only after the provoked assay shows a lithium effect that is **selective, with the genotype-by-treatment term** (P5 of the priors: interaction outside 0.7 to 1.43, the second GSK-3 inhibitor reproducing it and the non-GSK-3 comparator not). **Fallback if the interaction cannot be sized (it usually cannot):** the interaction is exploratory and does not by itself decide; the condition becomes GSK-3 pharmacology in the knockout (lithium effect with R at most 0.7 reproduced by the second GSK-3 inhibitor and not by the comparator). **"General" is defined once, for the condition, fallback and drop rule: the lithium effect is also reproduced by the non-GSK-3 comparator**; then the arm has no rationale beyond a general anticonvulsant and is dropped. The prior is thin and an early floor is likely.
