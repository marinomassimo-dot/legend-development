## The loop's judgement for a laboratory with one slot (added 2026-11-19 from the second materials comparison; ACCEPTED by ADV on 2026-11-19 as the loop's judgement at hypothesis level: from one comparison with unequal originals; not validated; the reasoning, not the comparison, carries it; no laboratory is named; no dose; not medical advice)
**If a laboratory can take only ONE of the optional measurements (rows 6 to 11), take row 7, glucose tied to seizure timing, first, and row 6, PV count and signal per cell with a late-maturing and a myelin marker, next.** The attempt that produced this order ranked the other four EEG or ECoG before P14, callosal conduction, structural myelin, heterozygote carrier latency; the blind verifier judged only the first choice and the comparison, not that tail, so the tail is not part of this line.

**Why row 7 first.** Checked in the held texts in this window: the neuron-targeted deletion paper (Repudi et al., Brain 2021) reports seizures beginning at P9 and its text (the supplement was not searched) contains no glucose, insulin, lactate or ketone measurement (its glucose mentions are recording-solution compositions); the held AAV9 gene-therapy paper (Repudi et al., EMBO Mol Med 2021) reports that neuronal re-expression restored blood glucose together with seizures, growth and survival, and that null mice were hypoglycaemic from the second week. That pattern shows a central origin of the hypoglycaemia; it cannot say whether low glucose drives the seizures or only accompanies them, because no held original manipulates glucose: **glucose is here a readout that discriminates timing, not a lever.** Per the ADV-074 note and not re-checked here: no glucose is reported before P10, there is no same-animal glucose and seizure pair in any held original, and the glucose-by-age values in the held figures are read from plots (approximate). A same-animal glucose and seizure-timing measurement is the one of the six that separates "metabolic driver" from "co-rescued accompaniment", and it changes how the other readouts are read, although it changes no arm, size or tier.
**Next: row 6.** Per the ADV-068 note and not re-checked here: the P17 bulk deposits (3 knockout against 4 control animals) lean to developmental delay or an illness state with a PV component, one gene decides that verdict, and they cannot give the cell-level answer; the tissue work needs no new animals when slice-animal tissue exists.
**What it needs.** Seizure-and-death lines (neuron-targeted or neural-progenitor-targeted), pups P7 to P14, blood glucose at seizure time, ketone, lactate and milk intake by age; the blood-sampling burden in small pups is for the laboratory to state. Row 11 (EEG or ECoG before P14) is the prerequisite for same-animal pairs and gains most when paired with row 7.
**What would change it.** No seizure-and-death line, or no way to sample pups at P7 to P14: row 6 first. A laboratory whose main experiments centre on a myelin-promoting arm: rows 9 and 8 move up.
**Provenance and limits (one case, no general claim).** Two delegates (sonnet, medium) answered the same question under a hashed pre-registration (`generation_it59_20261119/PREREG_SECOND_COMPARISON_IT59.md`): one lean with held originals only, one with the loop's notes added. They chose different first measurements (the lean one: callosal conduction with a rescue arm; the notes one: glucose). The blind verifier judged the notes arm better by informativeness, defensibility, separation of alternatives and a retrieved question, and found a borderline harm in the lean one (a rescue arm credited with separating a myelin deficit from a seizure effect, which a neuronal rescue cannot do, because it restores seizures, glucose and myelination together). **Limit:** the list of originals given to both delegates omitted held papers that carry the decisive glucose facts (the AAV9 gene-therapy paper among them), which the notes arm received through the ADV-074 note; so the result does not show that the notes beat a lean arm given the same complete originals. Full record: `materials_test2_sci_20261119/MATERIALS_TEST2_RESULT_SCI_20261119.md` (under `advisor_pilot_20261113/brainstorm_20261116/`).

## DATED REFINEMENT OF ROW 6 by SCI, 2026-11-19 (from ADV-085 B, ACCEPTED by ADV 2026-11-19; hypothesis level; not validated; no dose; reference genotype class only; not medical advice; the order of rows is unchanged)
**What the held originals measure about PV cells (source: `advisor_pilot_20261113/brainstorm_20261116/pv_population_b_adv085_sci_20261119/PV_POPULATION_FIRST_PASS_ADV085_SCI_20261119.md`, section 0).** One PV cell count exists in them: Hussain 2019, germline knockout, hippocampus, 2 weeks, PV-immunoreactive cell bodies by stereology, 7 knockout against 7 control animals, group level (whole hippocampus -44 percent), per-animal counts in figures that are not held. No held original gives a per-animal or per-cell PV value or identifies PV cells in a recording; the Breton interneurons are not PV-identified; the human organoid papers show GABAergic markers up with no PVALB, SST or VIP row. The count fits loss and PV-negative but present cells alike: it has no denominator.
**The separating measurement for row 6 (SCI's reasoning from B section 4; not a result).** In the same neocortical slices as the recordings, at P14, P17 and P21, against age-matched littermate controls: (1) a fate-mapped (lineage) reporter for the interneuron population, independent of PV expression (an Nkx2-1, Lhx6 or Dlx lineage, not a Pvalb-driven reporter, which would depend on the onset it is meant to test); (2) per animal, reporter-positive cells per unit area and the PV-immunoreactive fraction of them, with per-animal values published; (3) then reporter-cell firing and evoked IPSC at physiological chloride. Reading: reporter number falls = loss; reporter number normal with a low PV fraction that rises by P21 = delayed marker onset; reporter cells present, PV normal, firing or IPSC reduced = state or circuit change. It needs no drug and no therapeutic target, and shares animals with row 6 as written.
**Limits.** The window ends near week 3 because the knockout animals are reported to die between 3 and 4 postnatal weeks (Breton, per B). That PV protein appears around the second postnatal week in rodents is general knowledge, not checked in the held files. Four supply items are not held (Hussain 2019 Figures 1 to 6 and supplementary tables, the Breton supplement, Steinberg 2026 Supplementary Fig. 8B, a clean Repudi 2021 Brain PDF); the row would be re-read against them when they arrive (decision page item 14).

**Update 2026-11-19 (SCI, ADV-088; the sentence above on PV onset is replaced, the text above is not edited):** from public whole-brain mouse RNA-seq (Cardoso-Moreira 2019, E-MTAB-6798, four samples per age, transcript level, no P7, P17 or P21) Pvalb is at about 0.02 of its plateau at P0 and P3, 0.47 at P14 (replicates 0.36 to 0.60) and 0.89 at P28: **P14, P17 and P21 all lie on its rising phase**, so the P14 slice is a window in which delayed marker onset could show (the pre-registered rejecting observation, 0.8 or more at P14, did not occur). Chloride-transporter and most myelin transcripts are near plateau by P14, so at P17 the PV component of the stage confound is the live one. Controls must be sampled on the same day (about 0.03 of plateau per day). Source: `advisor_pilot_20261113/brainstorm_20261116/expression_timecourse_adv088_sci_20261119/EXPRESSION_TIMECOURSE_RESULT_ADV088_SCI_20261119.md`.

## DATED LINES 2026-11-19 (SCI, ADV-090): assay ages of rows 4 and 6 and of the pup brief, from the time course (hypothesis level; no arm, tier, size or stop rule changed; any new age is for the laboratory that takes the measurement to decide; not medical advice)
**Basis, stated once.** One public dataset: whole-brain (as labelled) mouse bulk RNA-seq, CD-1, four samples per age at P0, P3, P14, P28, P63, transcript level (`expression_timecourse_adv088_sci_20261119/`). **No P7, P10, P17 or P21; no layers; no protein; not the knockout.** Every line below that uses a number rests on it alone; lines marked "design" hold without it.
* **Row 6 (PV fraction of the fate-mapped population).** (1) *Design:* a control animal from the same litter, **sampled on the same day**, at each age, and the exact age in days and body weight recorded per animal; the age of the animal, not the litter, is the unit. (2) *Dataset:* P14, P17 and P21 all lie on the rising phase of Pvalb (0.47 of plateau at P14, 0.89 at P28; P17 about 0.56 and P21 about 0.68 only by straight-line interpolation, an assumption): about 0.03 of plateau per day, so a two-day lag moves Pvalb at P17 by about 11 percent. (3) *What each age separates:* P14, the lowest PV fraction in controls (largest room for a delayed onset to show); P17, the steepest part (most sensitive to a lag, least tolerant of age mismatch); P21, the nearest to plateau the knockout can reach (animals are reported to die between 3 and 4 postnatal weeks): fraction converging towards control by P21 favours delay, staying low favours loss or a fixed state. (4) *Cannot be concluded:* if PV protein or the perineuronal net lags the transcript (unknown), controls are still rising at P21 and "not converged" is then weak; the reporter-cell number (loss) is the reading that does not depend on onset. (5) An earlier age (P10) would sit where bulk Pvalb is low in controls (0.02 at P3) and adds little for loss against delay; it is for the laboratory only if it wants to relate PV state to seizure onset.
* **Row 4 (E_GABA, perforated patch).** *Dataset:* the Slc12a5 / Slc12a2 transcript ratio is 3.12 at P3 and 5.65 at P14 (5.28 at P28): the rise of KCC2 relative to NKCC1 lies **between P3 and P14** in unaffected brain; the slice package's primary window (P13 to P17) sits after it. So, **if a chloride switch exists in this model, a delayed or accelerated switch can only be seen at an earlier age; at P13 to P17 a null-against-control difference would read as a persistent difference.** *What each age separates:* P13 to P17 (primary, the age of the bursting): whether E_GABA of the null differs from age-matched control once the control switch is complete (depolarised: persistent shift, primary or secondary to bursts); an age near the first behavioural seizures (about P12; neuron-targeted line from P9 by text): whether the difference exists as the seizures begin; **P9 to P10 (the package's separate arm, burst status unknown): the only age that can show a switch in progress before seizures, hence primary against secondary.** *Suggestion for the laboratory, not a change:* treat one early age (P9 to P10) as a defined point of the E_GABA arm, not only an exploratory add-on, with a control at each age (the control's own E_GABA shift between these ages is the yardstick). *Design caveats that hold without the dataset:* deletion completeness per animal at each age (row 1), because the Synapsin-Cre deletion may be incomplete at the earliest age and a null result there would then not be a negative; seizure history and burst status of the animal recorded before slicing. *Would change if:* layer II/III function differs from whole-brain transcript, or protein and E_GABA follow KCC2 transcript with a lag (then the window moves later and P13 to P17 is not past the switch).
* **Pup brief, ethosuximide age series (section 2, item 3).** *Design (holds without the dataset):* the wild-type age series is the **stage yardstick**: if the wild-type response changes between neighbouring ages, a developmental lag of a few days in knockouts could mimic genotype specificity in the interaction ratio R(-/-) over R(+/+), and only the series shows how large that could be. *Dataset:* PV-associated genes (Pvalb 0.47 to 0.89, Kcnc1 0.61 to 0.95) and the GABA-A alpha 1 / alpha 2 ratio (2.17 to 2.83) change between P14 and P28, so the series should have **close spacing inside that interval: the assay age, P17, P21, with P28 optional**, rather than "two older ages to about P28". The T-type channel transcripts (the ethosuximide target family) were not in the pre-registered set; a dated post hoc look in the same file is possible on request.
* **Pup brief, glucose series tied to seizure timing (add-on e).** The time course says nothing about glucose, ketone or lactate. What transfers is design only: same-day littermate controls at each age; exact age in days per animal. It adds one timing point: seizure onset (about P9 to P12) comes **before** bulk Pvalb has risen in unaffected brain (0.02 at P3, 0.47 at P14; P9 to P12 not measured), so an early onset is unlikely to be explained by the loss of a mature PV system (reasoning, not a result; protein and function unmeasured). This is for row 11 (EEG before P14) and the glucose pairs to read against.
* **POST HOC look, 2026-11-19 (SCI; genes chosen by ADV after the result; same file, unit and plateau):** the T-type transcripts (Cacna1g, Cacna1h, Cacna1i) are on a **flat phase between P14 and P28** (the composition changes between P3 and P14: Cacna1h falls to less than half, Cacna1i rises about 2.6 times), so a change in ethosuximide response across a P14 to P28 age series is not explained by T-type transcript level in bulk, and ages below P14 are a different target mix; Kcnq2 rises by about half between P14 and P28 (Kv7-arm series on a rising phase); GluN2B is flat and GluN2A mostly in place by P14. Whole brain, bulk transcript, one dataset. Source: `expression_timecourse_adv088_sci_20261119/POSTHOC_TARGET_FAMILIES_ADDENDUM_SCI_20261119.md`.
* **Rests on the one dataset (would change at layer or protein level):** the placement of P14 to P21 on the PV rising phase; the 11 percent figure; the KCC2 switch lying before P14; the close spacing of the WT series; the P9-to-P12 timing point. **Holds without it:** same-day controls; age in days per animal; deletion completeness per animal and age; seizure history recorded; the WT age series as a yardstick.
