## 2. One-page summary (what the laboratory reads)
**The question.** In neuron-specific Wwox-knockout neocortical slices (P13 to P17; the preparation of Breton 2021, PMID 34634460), d-APV 50 uM eliminated spontaneous bursts (5 slices, 3 animals), and the published text says bursts returned on washout. d-APV is a competitive glutamate-site antagonist. No use-dependent pore blocker has been tested in this preparation. A pore blocker may also act on other targets in these neurons, among them the sag current (HCN1), which Breton 2021 reports as increased in knockout neurons. Does a use-dependent pore blocker, matched to d-APV for fractional block of the evoked delayed NMDAR response (layer V stimulus, layer II/III field; Breton 2021 Supplementary Fig. S3C), change the spontaneous burst rate in the same way, and what is the burst rate at about 5, 15, 30, 60 and 120 minutes after washout, against time-matched vehicle slices? The evoked response is read at the same times, because a burst-rate fall after washout can come from block that has not fully washed out. The sag voltage (in mV, the definition of Breton 2021), input resistance and resting potential are read in whole-cell recordings of the **same cells before and after** the agent (paired), with n given in cells and animals and the within-cell SD reported.

**Why this is the cheapest decisive step.** One preparation, one laboratory, one extra drug against a positive control already published by that laboratory. A tie between five antiseizure mechanism classes in the repository's ranking can be broken only by measurement, and all contributing threads come from one network of laboratories (Breton 2021 and related work). No second laboratory has reported neuron-level slice recordings from a Wwox-deficient neuron (a literature check by two routes, 2026-11-17; supplements and figures not examined; the check cannot see an unindexed report).

**Design in brief** (the laboratory may change anything marked as its own). Three arms in matched slices: pore blocker (class; the **laboratory chooses the agent within the class and states it before data**), d-APV, time-matched vehicle; knockout and wild-type littermates. **Suggested agents of the depth series (hypothesis level; the laboratory chooses):** d-APV, one partly trapped pore blocker (memantine-like) and one more trapped pore blocker with a documented Ih action (ketamine-like), at the same evoked depths; the first contrast isolates use-dependent pore block, the second what is specific to the agent; an optional fourth arm is a GluN2B-selective negative allosteric modulator. Block depth is matched in pilot slices by the evoked delayed response, **for every agent at the same depths**: a series of about one tenth, one fifth and three tenths of the response suppressed, plus one deep depth (at least one half). A model of the burst network (a hypothesis generator, not evidence) says the agents can be told apart at the intermediate depths, where the burst rate is steep, and not at the deep depth alone; the comparison that matters is the pore blocker against d-APV at the same depth: a pore blocker below d-APV at equal depth is an Ih effect or use-dependent block (the paired sag and the evoked response to a stimulus train against a single pulse are the discriminants), above it a disinhibition route, equal an NMDAR-dependent effect alone. Washout at least 120 minutes. Readouts: burst rate, duration, amplitude, interval at about 5, 15, 30, 60 and 120 minutes after washout (the early reads catch a short rebound; the model's date of its end moves with its assumptions: the 5-minute read lies inside it, the 15-minute read inside or at its end, **30 minutes is the end of the rebound zone, not clear of it**, **the 60-minute read is the clean one**, and one optional read at about 45 minutes is welcome; the classification is robust to the model's seed and time constant and the date of its end is not: it fell at 25 and 30 minutes in the original run and at 10, 15 and 20 minutes in a second seed, and with the longest time constant a residual of about 1.1 to 1.3 times baseline remained for about 50 to 95 minutes, of the order of the baseline's own bin noise (0.89 to 1.16). This is a MODEL, a hypothesis generator and not evidence; the rerun is IT's, relayed by ADV (ADV-063), and its report was not re-read here); the evoked response before, during and after, with its precision reported. **Rule for the post-washout reads, fixed before data:** a fall of the burst rate to 0.7 of its pre-exposure value or lower is called a lasting change only if the evoked response is back within 10% of its baseline; otherwise it may be block that has not washed out. **A failure to suppress bursts at deep block is not by itself a rejection of the NMDAR route**: a loss of inhibition large enough could also keep bursting going, so the intermediate depths decide. Unit of analysis is the animal, with slices nested. Animals: the repository's ranges are about 5 to 29 per genotype per arm for one primary comparison (technical brief section 5); the variance is not held, so a pilot of four animals per genotype fixes it first, and the laboratory's pilot should say how many depths are feasible (the design is larger than a one-deep-point version; if it must choose, the intermediate depths carry the discrimination). Stop rules: if knockout slices do not burst under the laboratory's conditions, or d-APV does not abolish them, nothing further is run. If the evoked delayed response is not stable in both genotypes, the arm is dropped, not run unmatched.

**Added 2026-11-26 (hypothesis level; blind-checked against Breton 2021).** Deep depth: tied to the block that d-APV gives in the laboratory's own hands, which the laboratory measures first. The published knockout slice gave a delayed field response lowered to about 0.4 of baseline at 100 uA in the d-APV used for the burst experiment (graph read of Breton 2021 Supplementary Fig. S3C, 6 slices from 3 animals; 50 uM is the paper's one stated d-APV concentration and is not restated for S3C; slice identity with the 5 burst slices is not shown), that is about 0.6 of the measured amplitude blocked, and about 0.7 to 0.8 of the part above an amplitude floor if that floor is additive and equals a wildtype reading (0.17 to 0.25 of the knockout baseline at 100 uA; with the wildtype reading at 100 uA, the upper end). 'At least one half' is therefore close to, but below, the block d-APV reached in that figure at 100 uA (about 0.5 to 0.55 at 10 to 50 uA against the same-strength baseline). The laboratory defines its measure (peak amplitude at the delayed latency and its floor) before data. *Note on the post-washout reading rule above (a note, not a replacement; a proposed two-sided wording):* "Post-washout reading rule, fixed before data: the evoked delayed response is read at each post-washout time in the exposed slices and in the time-matched vehicle slices, at a stated probe interval; call its departure from the pre-exposure value a washout deviation when it lies outside plus or minus 10 percent AND outside the vehicle's own value at the same time. A fall of the burst rate to 0.7 or lower is called a lasting change only if the evoked response shows no washout deviation; with a low deviation (below 0.9) the fall may be block that has not washed out; with a high deviation (above 1.1) neither residual block nor a lasting change can be told from a raised response, and the fall or rebound is reported as occurring with a raised evoked response. A rate inside 0.7 to 1.3 or a rebound at 1.3 or more is read together with the evoked class in the same way. In the one held case, d-APV in the knockout slice, the evoked response after washout read about 1.3 to 1.5 times same-strength baseline at 40 to 100 uA (graph read, Breton 2021 Supplementary Fig. S3C, 6 slices, 3 animals, no time-matched control)."

As a bracketing reference only, a calibration series of about 0.4 to 1, 2 to 4 and 8 to 10 uM in the bath, and 20 uM as a labelled probe, would bracket plasma values reported for **one** agent in the few reports the repository has read (mostly abstracts); it is not a range of the class, not an exposure limit, and a bath concentration is not a plasma concentration. **Priors, written and hashed before this page was sent** (sha256 `3c567acddf10244602c4536d3153f134cab4eba0690321290edd23772b7d8be0`): P1 at deep block the burst rate falls by at least half, 0.70. P2 at shallow block no change within 20% 0.55, a rise of at least 20% 0.20, a fall of at least 20% 0.25. P3 in the 30 to 60 minute post-washout window the rate is within 0.7 to 1.3 times its pre-exposure value 0.55, a rebound of at least 1.3 0.30, a lasting fall to 0.7 or below 0.15. P4 the pore blocker lowers the knockout sag by at least 20%, 0.75 (not a headline prediction: the unpaired arithmetic needs about 420 cells per arm, so it is read only from paired data and only once a paired SD exists; P1 to P3 and P5 are the headline predictions). P5 d-APV at the same depth falls in the same post-washout class, 0.50. These are subjective weights fixed before data so that a later score is literal, not validated probabilities; none is a result. The 120 minute read, the early reads, the intermediate depths and the lasting rule have no prior and are exploratory. The depth series and early reads come from a model, not from a source; the priors file has no prior for them and is untouched.

**What each outcome changes** (all at the level of mechanism; none says anything about the use of any agent). Both agents return to baseline: a transient NMDAR-dependent effect with no memory. Both overshoot and then decay: a slow variable, and the published washout overshoot of about 1.8 times baseline (read from the published figure by image, with an error bar that includes 1.0 and no test in the paper; per-slice values are not recoverable from the held supplement, which is raster) becomes a measured effect. The pore blocker differs from d-APV, or the paired sag falls under the pore blocker alone: a class-specific action (HCN1 or trapping), so that any later effect of the class in these neurons cannot be attributed to NMDAR dependence alone. Both stay low: a lasting change, with nothing in the repository to explain it. A pore blocker above d-APV at the same intermediate depth would be the first sign of a disinhibition route; below it, a class-specific action (the sag and a stimulus-train test of use dependence would then separate HCN1 from trapping). A failure to suppress bursts at deep block would call for the intermediate depths before any conclusion about the NMDAR route. **Branching order if the laboratory answers (planning order, not an assignment):** washout reads first; if the pore blocker differs from d-APV, an Ih-blocker arm (class label) at a matched sag change; if both overshoot, vary the exposure duration; if a shallow-block rise appears (P2), a low-occupancy depth series.

## 3. Optional measurements the laboratory may add (each is optional, declinable, and changes no arm, size or tier above)
**3.1 Chloride gradient: E_GABA and a factorial layout.** The perforated-patch E_GABA readout in the null against wild type at matched age comes first in the slice pilot; an NKCC1-blocker arm is conditional and second, only if the null is depolarising. The chloride arm is a **factorial layout**: a GABA-A modulator (a synaptic-selective and an extrasynaptic class kept apart) by a transport lever (an NKCC1 blocker or a KCC2 enhancer), each with an engagement readout, read with E_GABA in the same cells; a single bumetanide arm cannot separate the gradient from its rivals. E_GABA points: bath **without** bumetanide as the primary condition; ages P13 to P17 with P9 to P12 as a separate arm whose burst status is unknown; sex recorded; the fraction of depolarised cells reported with the mean; bursting against non-bursting slices from the same animal; a wild-type-on-agent group for any lever arm. An organoid arm at week 7 (knockout against rescue lines) is an alternative first route. The Kasahara 2019 full text shows the P11 modulator signal is confounded (5 of 5 deaths with high-dose diazepam at both ages, groups of 4 to 5, latency effect not blocked by bumetanide) and does not by itself support a P11 to P14 chloride window. No Wwox system has a measured E_GABA.

**3.2 Deletion completeness (ADV-076).** Do not assume a Synapsin-Cre deletion is uniform across regions and animals: **record deletion completeness per animal and per region at the protein level** (immunostaining or a per-slice check) and report each effect against it. Residual bulk transcript is not deletion completeness (control cortex about five times the hippocampal Wwox TPM; non-neuronal Wwox in bulk tissue; controls spread about three-fold); in the public P17 cortex contrast one of three knockout animals sat inside the control range, and stage and deletion depth cannot be separated at n = 3.

**3.3 PV, a late-maturing marker and a myelin marker at two ages (ADV-068).** In the tissue of the slice animals, with age-matched controls at two ages (the pilot's window and one earlier): PV cell count and signal per cell, one late-maturing neuronal marker, one myelin marker. Why: in public per-animal bulk RNA at P17 the lower Pvalb transcript is **PV-weighted, not PV-specific at transcript level** (the late-maturing neuronal set and the myelin genes fall with it). The ADV-068 rule has two readings (the "same sign and at least half the PV shift" parenthesis as a threshold or as an extra condition), the cortical verdict is fragile, and the primary animal set includes a knockout indistinguishable from controls by Wwox; the decision stands: **no PV-directed drug arm** on this evidence. Leanings: fewer PV cells with normal late markers favours a PV-specific loss; lower PV signal per cell with a normal count favours a state or maturation effect; late markers lower at the later age but not the earlier one favours a stage-of-illness effect. The transcript evidence is one laboratory's cohort family, bulk tissue, not a cell-level result.

**3.4 Heterozygote group, carrier readout and timing.** Heterozygote slices serve the passive, tonic and rebound readouts, not a burst-rate endpoint (4 of 23 slices burst, against 36 of 42 knockout and 0 of 11 wild type; the heterozygote and knockout counts are the text values of Repudi et al., Brain 2021, PMID 33914858, whose Fig. 2 legend gives 4 slices from 3 heterozygote animals (20 percent) and 36 slices from 20 knockout animals (84 percent), so text and legend disagree; the wild-type count is as printed in the technical brief 13.1, not re-checked here). A candidate carrier readout from one source (Cheng 2020, germline null, 3-week-old mice, one laboratory): transcranial motor-evoked-potential latency **1.39 +/- 0.13 ms in wild type (n = 10), 2.13 +/- 0.22 in heterozygotes (n = 5; P < 0.05 against wild type) and 2.44 +/- 0.37 in nulls (n = 4; P < 0.01), with amplitude 59.2 +/- 9.0, 59.6 +/- 17.2 and 11.8 +/- 5.4 microvolts**: a **latency prolongation in heterozygotes against wild type, with amplitude not different from wild type (n.s.; n = 5, large spread)**; the authors write "a partial Tc-MEP deteriorative change"; **Fig 2c prints a bracket between heterozygotes and nulls on latency marked n.s., and Fig 2b one on amplitude marked * (heterozygote against null), although the Methods name only a one-way ANOVA with no post hoc test, so the test behind the brackets is unnamed and heterozygotes are not shown to differ from nulls on latency**. **Timing limit:** of the structural findings in that report only the **E16.5 cortical thinning precedes seizure onset** (seizures "commonly observed after postnatal day 12"); the doublecortin increase is at P14, brain-size reduction at P20, and the apoptosis ratio and vermis fusion at about P19 to P20 (per IT's reading): concurrent or later, no causal sequence stated, and "brain-wide" is not the paper's word. The rule stands: state the age and the structural state of the slices beside each effect.

**3.5 Myelin and conduction endpoints.** Structural myelin endpoints (electron microscopy myelinated-axon counts per animal, g-ratio, MBP and CC1 immunostaining) have neuronal-rescue evidence in the held reports (neonatal neuronal AAV9 re-expression; counts per field, three animals per group: animal-level units and a blinding statement are needed). **Callosal conduction latency was measured once**: n = 8 slices from 5 animals (controls) and 8 from 6 (knockout), the control n identical to the S-HT n of another experiment (possibly a copied n; the originals do not decide it), first-peak latency about 2.5 against 3.2 ms (graph-read; Wilcoxon P = 0.0104), neuron-targeted line only, acute slices P13 to P17, no velocity, no rescue, never in vivo. It is a candidate needing re-measurement with a rescue arm and animal-level statistics, not yet an endpoint. The held latency is a single-distance first-peak latency (no velocity), the unit is the slice, recorded in neocortical layer V after stimulation of the corpus callosum; the N2 (second-peak) latency, whose identity is not defined in Repudi 2021 (the unmyelinated or partially myelinated label is Palma's) and which is not significantly different (P = 0.5737; wide spread, 8 slices per group), is a candidate within-slice control that the brief does not yet carry; no held source gives a conduction readout with a drug arm in a Wwox-deficient model, and no WWOX patient report with an evoked-potential or conduction result was found by a title-and-abstract search on two routes (2026-11-25). **Candidate arm set for a drug test of conduction (ADV-288; hypothesis; no compound, dose or schedule is chosen):** four cells in the same litters (wild type and knockout, vehicle and drug), because in healthy developing wild-type mice the pro-myelinating drug clemastine (one compound, Palma 2022; daily from P5, slices at P21; one mouse as the unit, n = 10 to 12) slowed callosal N1 conduction velocity (ratio about 0.84 read from Fig 2B; one asterisk, P < 0.05 by its legend, while the text states P < 0.01 and the Methods name a paired t-test for two independent groups), so a wild-type drug-only arm is needed before a knockout effect can be read as rescue; callosal N1 velocity from the distance-latency slope over at least three electrode distances with N2 as the control, one value per animal, the same animals' g-ratio, seizure status recorded, recording at P17 to P21; **24 animals per arm planned** (from the held spread, by graph reads, a slowing of the size seen by Palma needs 9 to 24 animals per arm, and at 12 per arm only a ratio of 1.29 or more, or 0.78 or less, is detectable at the upper spread) with a pilot of at least 8 vehicle animals per genotype to replace the graph-read spread; the comparison stops for a compound if wild-type N1 velocity falls by at least 10 percent with the interval excluding zero. The Olig2-against-Mbp contrast in the P47T report is cross-region (Olig2 in corpus callosum, Mbp in parietal cortex; the Mbp panels are Supplementary Figure S6 of the same paper, **held on disk** (`files/supplement/PMID36828035/`; caption read, panels not opened): parietal cortex above the corpus callosum, 80 and 250 days, n = 3 per genotype, unpaired t-test not significant) and is a composition caveat only, not "oligodendrocytes lost without hypomyelination".

**3.6 Sex, litter and cage.** The public per-animal deposits record no sex, litter, cage, dissection batch or animal identifier, so every per-animal result drawn from them is uncontrolled for sex and litter. In the laboratory's own design record sex and litter and say so.
