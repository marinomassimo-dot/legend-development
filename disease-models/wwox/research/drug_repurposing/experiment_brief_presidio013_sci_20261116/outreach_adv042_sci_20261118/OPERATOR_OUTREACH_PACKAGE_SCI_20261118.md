## 2. One-page summary (what the laboratory reads)
**The question.** In neuron-specific Wwox-knockout neocortical slices (P13 to P17; the preparation of Breton 2021, PMID 34634460), d-APV 50 uM eliminated spontaneous bursts (5 slices, 3 animals), and the published text says bursts returned on washout. d-APV is a competitive glutamate-site antagonist. No use-dependent pore blocker has been tested in this preparation. A pore blocker may also act on other targets in these neurons, among them the sag current (HCN1), which Breton 2021 reports as increased in knockout neurons. Does a use-dependent pore blocker, matched to d-APV for fractional block of the evoked delayed NMDAR response (layer V stimulus, layer II/III field; Breton 2021 Supplementary Fig. S3C), change the spontaneous burst rate in the same way, and what is the burst rate 30, 60 and 120 minutes after washout, against time-matched vehicle slices? The sag voltage (in mV, the definition of Breton 2021), input resistance and resting potential are read in whole-cell recordings of the **same cells before and after** the agent (paired), with n given in cells and animals and the within-cell SD reported.

**Why this is the cheapest decisive step.** One preparation, one laboratory, one extra drug against a positive control already published by that laboratory. A tie between five antiseizure mechanism classes in the repository's ranking can be broken only by measurement, and all contributing threads come from one network of laboratories (Breton 2021 and related work). No second laboratory has reported neuron-level slice recordings from a Wwox-deficient neuron (a literature check by two routes, 2026-11-17; supplements and figures not examined; the check cannot see an unindexed report).

**Design in brief** (the laboratory may change anything marked as its own). Three arms in matched slices: pore blocker (class; the **laboratory chooses the agent within the class and states it before data**), d-APV, time-matched vehicle; knockout and wild-type littermates. Block depth is matched in pilot slices by the evoked delayed response: deep (at least one half of the response suppressed) and, for the pore blocker only, one shallow sub-arm (under one fifth). Washout at least 120 minutes. Readouts: burst rate, duration, amplitude, interval at 30, 60, 120 minutes after washout; the evoked response before, during, after. Unit of analysis is the animal, with slices nested. A pilot of four animals per genotype fixes variance first. Stop rules: if knockout slices do not burst under the laboratory's conditions, or d-APV does not abolish them, nothing further is run. If the evoked delayed response is not stable in both genotypes, the arm is dropped, not run unmatched.

**Concentration reference (optional; see part 5, point 1).** **[OPERATOR DECIDES: keep, shorten or delete this paragraph.]** Matching is by the evoked response, not by a concentration. As a bracketing reference only, a calibration series of about 0.4 to 1, 2 to 4 and 8 to 10 uM in the bath, and 20 uM only as a labelled supra-range probe, would cover the published human blood-level range of agents of this class; each figure is a published value with its source in the repository's concentration note, not a dose, a schedule or a recommendation.

**Priors, written and hashed before this page was sent** (sha256 `3c567acddf10244602c4536d3153f134cab4eba0690321290edd23772b7d8be0`): P1 at deep block the burst rate falls by at least half, 0.70. P2 at shallow block no change within 20% 0.55, a rise of at least 20% 0.20, a fall of at least 20% 0.25. P3 in the 30 to 60 minute post-washout window the rate is within 0.7 to 1.3 times its pre-exposure value 0.55, a rebound of at least 1.3 0.30, a lasting fall to 0.7 or below 0.15. P4 the pore blocker lowers the knockout sag by at least 20%, 0.75 (not a headline prediction: the unpaired arithmetic needs about 420 cells per arm, so it is read only from paired data and only once a paired SD exists; P1 to P3 and P5 are the headline predictions). P5 d-APV at the same depth falls in the same post-washout class, 0.50. These are labelled hypotheses written before any data of this kind; none is a result. The 120 minute read has no prior and is exploratory.

**What each outcome changes** (all at the level of mechanism; none says anything about the use of any agent). Both agents return to baseline: a transient NMDAR-dependent effect with no memory. Both overshoot and then decay: a slow variable, and the published washout overshoot of about 1.8 times baseline (read from the published figure, with an error bar that includes 1.0 and no test in the paper) becomes a measured effect. The pore blocker differs from d-APV, or the paired sag falls under the pore blocker alone: a class-specific action (HCN1 or trapping), so that any later effect of the class in these neurons cannot be attributed to NMDAR dependence alone. Both stay low: a lasting change, with nothing in the repository to explain it. A rise at shallow block and a fall at deep block would be the first sign of a disinhibition route.

**What the laboratory needs, and what we do not ask.** Needs: the knockout and wild-type slice preparation, the evoked-response assay, a field-recording rig, a whole-cell rig for the paired sag readout (same cells, before and after), about three hours of slice survival, and the choice of agent, concentration series and burst-detection rule, fixed before data. **We do not ask for**: animals or tissue to be sent; any information about any person or patient; clinical advice; the nomination of any compound as a use; or any commitment of the laboratory's time beyond what it chooses.
