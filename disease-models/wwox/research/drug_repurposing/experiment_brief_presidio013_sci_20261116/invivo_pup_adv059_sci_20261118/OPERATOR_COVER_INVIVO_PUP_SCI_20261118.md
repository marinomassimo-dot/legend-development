# One-page cover for the operator: the in vivo pup experiment (SCI, 2026-11-18)

Written for: the operator, who decides whether and to whom to send anything. Plain words. **Not medical advice.** Nothing here concerns any person; the repository holds no individual-level record and none was used. No agent contacts anyone, and no agent can promise anything on the operator's behalf. Tags: **[OPERATOR DECIDES]** marks what only the operator can decide.

**What this is.** A design brief (`INVIVO_PUP_EXPERIMENT_BRIEF_V1_SCI_20261118.md`) for a laboratory that already works with Wwox-knockout mice. It sets out one experiment: young knockout, heterozygous and wild-type mouse pups, given a standard seizure-provoking test, with and without two drugs that a published study (Cheng 2020) reported to reduce those seizures in knockout pups (ethosuximide and lithium), plus a few extra control drugs and a wild-type age series.

**The question it asks.** Are those drug effects specific to the knockout, or are they what any such drug would do in a pup of that age? The published study cannot tell, for three reasons: it did not test whether the drug effect differs between genotypes, it did not state the pups' age, and wild-type pups barely seized at all, so "no effect" in them may only mean there was nothing to reduce. The brief is built to settle exactly that, and to say plainly when a result cannot be read.

**What a laboratory would be asked.** To read the brief and say whether it can run something like it: the pups at one age, all three genotypes in the same session, a seizure test of its own published design, per-animal scoring by someone who does not know the genotype or drug, a control group in every genotype, and a small set of reference drugs in wild-type pups of several ages so that a "no effect" is read only where the test can show an effect. **The laboratory chooses its own doses, timing and test and states them before any data.** The brief proposes no dose.

**What it cannot show.** It cannot show whether any drug would help the spontaneous seizures of these mice (that needs recordings the basic design does not include), whether a drug works after seizures start, how a drug works, or anything about people. A good result would not be a reason to use any drug in any person.

**What may not be ready.** Whether any laboratory has the knockout line and capacity is unknown to the repository (nothing was looked up or recorded about any person or laboratory). The number of animals needed depends on how variable the seizure scores are; the brief says so, and the first step is a pilot to measure that. Knockout pups are scarce (about one in four from a heterozygote cross) and fragile, so a full test of "different between genotypes" may need many litters; the brief says what it would do if that is too many.

**What the operator decides [OPERATOR DECIDES].** Whether to approach any laboratory at all; which one and in what order (the repository names none for this experiment); what, if anything, to offer or promise (nothing is promised in the brief); and whether to attach the full brief or only this page.

**Do not confuse it with the other ask.** There is already a **separate** package for a different experiment in brain slices (a matched-block comparison of a pore-blocking drug class against d-APV in the Breton knockout slice): `outreach_adv042_sci_20261118/OPERATOR_OUTREACH_PACKAGE_SCI_20261118.md`. That is a slice recording experiment with its own readiness notes; this one is whole-animal and provoked-seizure. They share no animals, no priors file, and no dependency: the slice experiment comes first or in parallel and does not wait for this one, and nothing in either needs the other's result.
