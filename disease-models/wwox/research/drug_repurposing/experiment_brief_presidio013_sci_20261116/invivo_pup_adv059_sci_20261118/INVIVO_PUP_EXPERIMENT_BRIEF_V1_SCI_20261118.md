## 1. The question, in one sentence
In Wwox-null pups, are the seizure-suppressing effects reported for ethosuximide and for lithium specific to the knockout, or are they the same proportional effect any anticonvulsant of that kind would have in a pup of that age, and does lithium act through GSK-3?

**Why this question.** The only in vivo drug data in a Wwox-null animal that the repository holds are one experiment (Cheng 2020, PMID 32000863, global knockout, "preweaning" pups, pentylenetetrazol-provoked seizures scored on a 0 to 6 scale, one laboratory): lithium lowered the stage in all three genotypes; ethosuximide lowered it in the knockout and was not significant in wild-type and heterozygous pups. **No genotype-by-treatment test was reported or can be computed from the figures** (group sizes, mean ± SEM curves, one-way ANOVA; no per-animal values; the age of the test is not stated; the knockout PTZ-alone curves of the two drug panels differ in height (an eye read of the figure), so the drugs appear to have been tested against different cohorts; wild-type stage is about 1 on 0 to 6, close to the floor). A claim of knockout selectivity therefore rests on a difference in significance.
**Readings to separate for ethosuximide:** (E1) knockout-selective; (E2) age-limited, with a wild-type floor (rat pups lose the PTZ effect of ethosuximide below about 25 days in Prague abstracts; the mouse ontogeny was not found by two routes; one mouse pup datum exists for a different seizure type, Cortez 2004, PMID 15582027, abstract only); (E3) a general anticonvulsant effect, proportional in every genotype. **For lithium:** (L1) general, against (L2) specific to GSK-3 in the knockout (p-Ser9 is lower in untreated knockout brain in Cheng 2020 Fig. 7c, by one representative blot per region, and lithium engagement was not measured; lithium has other targets).

**Sizing flag, read this first.** IT's eye read of Cheng 2020 Fig. 7b (knockout PTZ-alone curve, N = 6; SEM bars of about a third to a half of the mean) suggests a standard deviation of S of the order of 0.7 to 1 times the mean, i.e. about 0.6 to 0.8 on the log scale, which puts a **simple** effect of ratio 0.7 at roughly 44 to 79 animals per arm (log SD 0.6 to 0.8, the same arithmetic as section 3); the SD of 0.3 used in section 3 is the **optimistic** case, not the expected one, and at the pessimistic scale the **interaction is out of reach**. The interaction contrast needs **four equal cells** (the arithmetic of this flag is corrected by the dated addendum in section 3) (treated and vehicle, in knockout and wild type): at 44 per cell that is about 176 animals for one drug against vehicle, of which 88 are knockouts; **75 litters is about 525 pups** (plus the panel and the age series) and about 132 fragile knockouts. The brief therefore plans a pilot first and promises no interaction test (sections 2.7 and 3).

## 2. Design frame (fixed before the first animal)
1. **All three genotypes (+/+, +/-, -/-) in the same session, at the same postnatal age** (**a session = one assay day at one assay age; cells are filled across litters, with litter as a block for the vehicle groups where numbers allow; a litter yields only about 7 pups, so all nine genotype-by-arm cells cannot come from the same litters**) (heterozygote crosses; expected 1:2:1; knockout pups die by about 4 weeks and are fragile). Vehicle per genotype in every session (same-day control); **within a session, the ethosuximide, T-type, lithium, second GSK-3 and comparator arms share one vehicle group per genotype** (cheaper, and the interaction contrast is cleaner); the panel shares the vehicle of its age as in item 4. **Allocation:** block randomisation of arm within genotype and litter, scarcest genotype first (knockouts are allocated across arms in rotation before the others), unequal cell sizes accepted and reported; n per cell is reported in cells, animals and litters. **Blinding:** drug syringes coded by a person who does not score; **genotype is read before the test by a person who does not score** (needed for the allocation above), and the scorer stays blind to genotype and arm; **every trial is video recorded and scored from the coded video by a scorer who was not present**. Complete blinding to genotype may be impossible where knockout pups are visibly smaller; the brief then asks for blinding to arm and the limit is stated in the report. Litter is recorded as a cluster.
2. **Assay age defined by the laboratory** (Cheng 2020 does not state it) as the age at which knockout vehicle pups reliably show provoked seizures and can still be tested; ADV's working window for the knockout is spontaneous seizures after about P12 and death by about 4 weeks (not re-verified here; to be confirmed by the laboratory from its own colony).
3. **A wild-type age series** (no null animal needed): wild-type pups, same stimulus and scale, at the assay age and at **at least two older ages inside the window to about P28**, spaced by the laboratory, vehicle against each test agent; knockouts are tested at the assay age only (they cannot be matched at older ages). Purpose: to see whether a wild-type non-response is age.
4. **An assay-sensitivity panel in separate wild-type pups at every age of the series, sharing that age's vehicle group with the test agents** (so that a null is read only at an age where the assay can show a drug effect; the valproate effect in mouse pups is unknown, so the panel's validity is established empirically at each age and a failed panel is reported as an assay failure, not as a negative): a **flat-profile** agent (the rat literature has valproate protecting against PTZ at every age tested; mouse unknown, so it is itself a check), one **rising-profile** agent (carbamazepine, or cannabidiol, whose efficacy rises with age in rat PTZ abstracts) and, if an NMDA-receptor arm is included, one **falling-profile** agent (ifenprodil or Ro 25-6981: ifenprodil effective in mouse at P7 and P10 and not at P14 or P21, in rat up to P18 on one assay and proconvulsant on afterdischarges at P18 and P25; Ro 25-6981 effective at P12 and failing at P25 in rat). **All agent names in this brief are examples from the rodent literature only, not proposed for any use; the laboratory chooses** (abstract level, one origin for many rat rows: `adv054_age_window_sci_20261118/`).
5. **A stimulus that gives wild-type pups a measurable non-floor response**, fixed in a pilot (otherwise a wild-type null cannot be told from a floor). The stimulus is the laboratory's own published standard assay; no number is proposed here.
6. **Outcomes per animal, not only a mean stage:** (a) the summary S = area under the stage against time over a **60-minute observation window (as in Cheng 2020; the laboratory states any other window before data)**, the stage assigned per 1-minute bin as the maximum stage in that bin; **a pup that dies keeps stage 6 for the rest of the window, and a pup rescued under the status stopping rule keeps its last stage carried forward to the end of the window; both are flagged and the results are reported with and without them** (primary); (b) **seizure type**: incidence and latency of minimal seizures (stages 1 to 2), **intermediate (stage 3, unilateral forelimb clonus with myoclonic jerk: its own category, reported and included in S)**, generalised seizures (**stages 4 to 5 with a tonic phase, as the hashed priors file defines "generalised" for P8; stage 4 without a tonic phase (bilateral clonus) is reported on its own line and stage 5 (loss of righting, tonic-clonic) is reported separately inside the generalised count**) and **status, fixed as three or more tonic-clonic seizures within the observation window** (the Cheng 2020 definition). Reason: in rat pups several agents suppress generalised seizures while **raising minimal seizures** (MK-801 at P12, memantine, phenobarbital at P7 and P12, carbamazepine at P7 and P12), and wild-type pups sit at minimal-seizure level, so a mean stage can cancel two opposite effects.
7. **Analysis fixed before data:** effect ratio R(genotype) = mean S treated over mean S same-genotype same-session vehicle, on the log scale; **interaction ratio = R(-/-) over R(+/+)**; animal as the unit with litter as cluster (bootstrap over litters); an effect = R at most 0.7 with an interval excluding 1; a null = interval inside 0.85 to 1.15; otherwise unresolved (an interval that contains 1 and excludes 0.7 is reported as "an effect of 0.7 or larger is excluded"). **For the interaction ratio** the thresholds are the reciprocal pair: genotype specificity = at most 0.7 or at least 1.43; **no genotype specificity = 0.7 to 1.43** (as in the priors file, E1, E3 and P5). **If vehicle S in a genotype is zero or at the floor, R is not computed and the arm is not interpretable.** With about 1.75 knockouts per litter the bootstrap over litters is the primary interval; with fewer than 10 litters in a cell the laboratory uses a permutation test or a mixed model with a litter random effect and says so before data. The absolute-scale interaction is a secondary report. **Shared vehicle and multiplicity:** where five test arms share one vehicle group per genotype, the vehicle group is made about twice the size of a test arm (the square-root rule for five arms), and the intervals of the five comparisons are reported at 95% and at 99% (the Bonferroni level for five); the ethosuximide comparison is the pre-specified primary and is judged at 95%, every other "effect" counts as confirmed only if it holds at 99%. A bounded scale (0 to 6, floor at 0) makes a proportional effect look smaller in absolute stage where the baseline is low; that is why the ratio scale is primary and the floor rule exists. **Order of hypotheses (fixed before data):** (i) panel validity at each age; (ii) the ethosuximide effect ratios in the knockout and in the wild type at the assay age and at the older ages, as descriptive ratios with their intervals; (iii) the interaction ratio with its interval, **exploratory unless the pilot SD makes it sized** (section 3).
8. **A motor and ataxia control, applied to every arm as a pre-test before the provoked seizure and reported per animal as a covariate; mandatory for ketamine-class and other NMDA-receptor and sedating agents:** in rat pups ketamine causes ataxia at the lowest exposure, more in younger animals (abstract), which would confound a stage score; the laboratory uses its own standard (righting, gait or equivalent); a pup is excluded only if it fails the laboratory's own pre-specified criterion before the stimulus (stated before data), and exclusions are listed.
9. **Regimen and dose checks (fixed before data).** The lithium regimen (a single acute pretreatment against repeated pretreatment) is a **design variable**, because the rodent literature the repository holds has acute lithium pro-convulsive and chronic lithium threshold-raising in one mouse PTZ paper family (IT's ADV-051 rows L05 to L09); **Cheng 2020's repeated pretreatment within an hour before PTZ is the replication condition** and the laboratory states any other regimen before data. Because potency shifts with age and **no pup exposure measurement was found for any agent**, "an effect of the drug" is defined only together with a **dose check per age: two exposures per agent (or a published pup exposure) at each age of the series, or a measured exposure if the laboratory has the means (not required)**; a null at an exposure that was not checked at that age is a **dose null, reported as unresolved**, not as no effect.
10. **Humane endpoints and approvals are the laboratory's:** about half of PTZ-injected knockouts reached status in Cheng 2020; a stopping rule for status (when to rescue, by the laboratory's own approved procedure; the pup then stays in the analysis with its last stage carried forward, flagged), written before the first animal, is a design requirement, not an option.

**Sizing arithmetic (ASSUMED values, not a forecast).** Cheng 2020 used 6 to 7 knockout and 12 to 20 wild-type or heterozygous pups per group. With a heterozygote cross and about 7 pups per litter, a litter yields about 1.75 knockouts. On the log scale a simple effect of ratio 0.7 (ln = -0.357) needs about 11, 31 or 79 animals per arm for a within-genotype SD of 0.3, 0.5 or 0.8 (two-sided 0.05, power 0.80). A genotype-by-treatment interaction contrast of the same size needs about four times as many per cell (the contrast is a difference of two differences). For a binary outcome such as generalised seizure (assume 50% in vehicle knockouts), a fall to 10% needs about 20 per arm and a fall to 20% about 38. **Three knockout arms at 11 per arm would need about 19 litters; at 31 per arm about 53 (simple effect only).** For the **interaction** the same arithmetic gives about 44 per cell at SD 0.3, i.e. about 132 knockouts in three arms, **about 75 litters**, and it is out of reach at SD 0.5 or more. The knockout interaction is therefore realistic only if the pilot SD is about 0.3 or less, and even then needs about 75 litters across the three knockout arms; otherwise stage 2 answers the question through the order of hypotheses in section 2.7 (panel validity, the effect ratios by genotype and age, the interaction as an exploratory interval), and the brief says so rather than promising an interaction test. Wild-type and heterozygous animals are not limiting.
**A stage that runs in a different session carries its own vehicle group per genotype** (the shared vehicle of item 2.1 holds only within a session).
**Total for the required stages with the dose check (two exposures per agent per age, panel included; for planning, not a promise):** about 27 cells (the wild-type age series: 3 ages times vehicle, ethosuximide at two exposures, one flat-profile and one rising-profile panel agent at two exposures each = 21 cells; knockout and heterozygote at the assay age: vehicle and ethosuximide at two exposures = 6 cells), i.e. about 300 animals at 11 per cell or about 1,350 at 50 per cell, of which 33 to 150 knockouts (19 to 86 litters). The dose check multiplies the animals, so **the pilot decides the dose check first**: if the lower exposure is effective in the panel agent at an age, one exposure per agent at that age is enough and the totals fall accordingly.
**Dated addendum to the sizing arithmetic (2026-11-18, ADV-061; priors file untouched; record `fig7_quant_ethosuximide_lithium_sci_20261115/README.md`, section "ADV-061 addendum"; hypothesis level).** From the means and upward SEM bars of Cheng 2020 Fig. 7b and 7d (per-minute SD = SEM times the square root of N; S = mean stage over 60 minutes; bounds from independent to fully correlated minutes; N assumed constant): (1) **the knockout PTZ-alone groups give a CV of S of 0.58 to 1.19 at a within-animal correlation of 0.6 to 1.0 (log SD about 0.54 to 0.94; the rigorous upper bound is 0.67 for 7b and 0.94 for 7d), i.e. about 36 to 109 animals per arm for a ratio of 0.7**; the "log SD 0.6 to 0.8" of this brief is inside that bracket and the optimistic SD of 0.3 is not credible (it needs a within-animal correlation of 0.05 to 0.15). (2) **Wild-type and heterozygous pups at that stimulus are at the floor** (mean S 0.27 to 0.41, CV above 1.4): a ratio of 0.7 would need about 140 to 210 per arm, so a stimulus with a non-floor wild-type response is a requirement, not an option. (3) **Correction of this brief's arithmetic:** an interaction contrast needs **twice** (not four times) the simple-effect number per cell (4 (z sigma / delta)^2); with the bracket above it is about **180 to 320 animals per cell**, four cells, about 700 to 1,300 animals and 350 to 630 knockouts (200 to 360 litters) for one drug against vehicle (22 per cell, 88 animals, 25 litters at the optimistic equal SD of 0.3). **IT's independent read of the same curves (ADV-063, relayed by ADV, written before IT opened this bracket) agrees within about 20 percent (per-minute means within 0.05 and 0.15 stage; SD of the summed stage 9.5 to 62 and 10.9 to 69 against 11.4 to 71.5 and 9.2 to 56.9 here) and concludes that the sizing flag stands.** **Limit on direction (SCI, 2026-11-18):** the bracket assumes the legend N over the whole hour; whether animals that die keep stage 6 or leave the mean is not stated, and the two possibilities bias the SD in opposite directions (fewer contributing animals than the legend N makes the bracket overstate the per-minute SD; survivor-only curves make it understate the full-cohort SD); the net direction needs the authors' per-animal data and does not change the order of magnitude of the sizing. **The genotype-by-treatment contrast is therefore not attemptable with the full-hour area summary at the variability these curves show**; it is attemptable only if the pilot finds a stimulus or a summary (for example an incidence of generalised seizures) with far lower variability. The order of hypotheses in item 2.7 stands: panel validity and the effect ratios by genotype and age come first, the interaction is an exploratory interval. The litter and animal totals of the sections above (19 and 53 litters; 75 litters; the dose-check totals) are the optimistic scale and should be read with the 36 to 109 per arm knockout bracket: three knockout arms at 36 to 109 are 108 to 327 knockouts, about 62 to 187 litters.
**If resources allow only one step:** the pilot (stage 1), then the ethosuximide arm with the age series and panel (stage 2). **Required:** stages 1 and 2 with the panel and the age series. **Optional:** the T-type arm (needs an engagement control to be readable), stage 3, stage 4, and the add-ons of section 5. ## 5. What this experiment cannot show, and the cheapest optional add-ons
**Cannot show:** spontaneous-seizure burden (no video-EEG in the basic design); acute rescue after seizure onset (all arms are pretreatment before a provoked seizure); any mechanism readout; development or survival effects; any statement about people. A provoked-seizure result in a pup says what a drug does to a PTZ seizure at that age, not what it would do to the null phenotype.
**Cheapest add-ons (optional):** (a) video-EEG on a subset of knockout pups (the only route to spontaneous-seizure burden); (b) **p-Ser9 and total GSK-3beta in tissue from the same lithium-treated and vehicle animals** (Cheng 2020 measured p-Ser9 only in untreated animals, and its Methods and legend disagree on the age, day 14 against day 20); (c) a **Cav3 transcript or protein readout** from the same animals' tissue (no Cav3 measurement in Wwox-null tissue was found by two routes, HE's ADV-052).

## 9. Dated addenda
**9.1 (2026-11-18, ADV-058 unit 2; text by IT, `it_adv058_20261118/DEVELOPMENT_ENDPOINT_ARM_TEXT_FOR_SCI_IT_20261118.md`; hypothesis level; no dose; OPTIONAL, conditional): a development-endpoint cohort for lithium, on the AAV9 comparator's own endpoints.**
* **Not an arm of this provoked-seizure design, and not an arm of the slice brief 13.11** (a slice cannot read development endpoints; and in this design the provoking injection confounds survival, GFAP and myelination, since about half of PTZ-injected knockouts reached status epilepticus in Cheng 2020). It would be a **separate in vivo cohort**, litter-blocked, with its own vehicle-only knockout group, treatment start age is the laboratory's.
* **Only lithium qualifies** (with a second GSK-3 inhibitor of another class beside it if it is run): it has the only in vivo Wwox-null drug effect (general-property reading); the sign of GSK-3 inhibition for myelination is divided across studies, so the arm decides nothing in advance. **Ethosuximide, the NMDA-receptor pore blockers and d-APV, and vigabatrin do not qualify** (no development rationale, or a harm experiment first; reasons in IT's file); valproate belongs to the provoked-assay comparator list only.
* **Endpoints (the comparator's own, age fixed beforehand):** survival to a fixed end age beyond day 17 with a stated censoring day (the primary comparable; untreated knockouts die by about day 17 in the comparator's panel); body-weight series from birth; one composite motor score per animal at a fixed age (the comparator's panel does not state its age: ask the source laboratory or fix our own); GFAP-positive cells; myelination (electron microscopy is the heavy item; a staining readout can precede it); brain size and weight at about day 20. Per-animal summaries and the genotype-by-treatment term are fixed before the first animal; no power calculation is offered (no variance is held for a drug arm).
* **What a comparison with AAV9 would not allow:** prevention against rescue; equivalence of mechanism; durability and dose; a safety comparison (a drug needs its own harm endpoints, for lithium renal and thyroid readouts as the label lists); pup pharmacokinetics are unknown for every agent here.
