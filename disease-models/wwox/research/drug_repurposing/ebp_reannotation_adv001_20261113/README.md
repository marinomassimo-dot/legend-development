## Answer first

**The substrate is small and stated: 31 curated signed rows (9 compound rows, 22 genetic) and a 20-compound comparison (15 favourable, 5 adverse).** The 216-row layer that IT29's README quotes is **not a committed artefact**: no number is reproduced on it here (the "106 of 137 consistent rows are glucocorticoid-receptor agonists from one study" remains IT29's statement).
1. **How many favourable compounds have documented EBP activity: 5 of 15 by S0's definition, 4 under a strict reading** (benztropine, clemastine, tamoxifen, quetiapine; ifenprodil is binding-only). Evidence is uneven: benztropine, clemastine and tamoxifen are named by Hubler 2018 as zymostenol-accumulating and direct EBP inhibitors (tamoxifen also has independent database rows: human EBP binding Ki 5.0 nM, cell sterol rows); **quetiapine** rests on one main-text sentence of Sax 2022 ("inhibited EBP in our assays": a cell sterol readout, abstract/text depth, figures and supplement unreached); **ifenprodil** has six emopamil-displacement binding rows at the sterol delta8-delta7 isomerase site (Ki 2.7–19.8 nM, guinea-pig liver) and one human EBP row, but **Hubler describes it as having "affinity for TM7SF2"** and gives no functional EBP datum for it, so the two statements conflict in attribution.
2. **The four-way split of the 15 favourable compounds (the fairest summary):** 4 (+1 binding-only) EBP-documented; 3 **indirect sterol-profile negatives** (clobetasol: Sax, "the only compound that did not inhibit cholesterol synthesis"; liothyronine and bexarotene: Hubler, "minimal effects on sterol levels in OPCs"; indirect, not enzyme assays); 3 acting on **another sterol enzyme with EBP unmeasured** (amorolfine, TM7SF2/sterol 14-reductase, whose zymostenol accumulation is also the EBP-substrate readout; miconazole and ketoconazole, CYP51); 4 with **EBP unmeasured** (atropine, ipratropium, oxybutynin, sobetirome). Atropine, ipratropium and oxybutynin are named neither in Hubler's open full text nor in Sax, and have no row in 334 ChEMBL EBP-orthologue rows plus a scan of all activity rows of 23 parent ids: **unmeasured, not inactive**. No azole or mammalian amorolfine EBP datum was found (a fungal delta8-delta7 isomerase inhibition by phenylmorpholines exists at abstract level; fenpropimorph, a close analogue, has Ki 30 nM at human EBP: an amorolfine EBP effect is plausible and unmeasured, IPOTESI).
3. **At the evidence-row level, 11 or 12 of the 22 unique favourable rows are tied to EBP-documented compounds** (11 by the comparison file's map; 12 by row text, because R08 names benztropine). **This is not an independent share:** the rows come from 7 papers, 5 of which carry a documented compound; R14, R36 and R08 are shared; 7 of the 11 rows are compound-only (no target control) and 4 target-controlled. Neither "5 of 15" nor "11 of 22" is a stand-alone summary.
4. **In the 31-row layer no favourable compound row is newly confounded.** The 9 compound rows: S1-06 and S1-07 (EBP) are EBP by design; S1-05 (CYP51A1) and S1-08 (TM7SF2) are other sterol enzymes by design; S1-09 (NR3C1), S1-10 and S1-11 (RXRA, RXRG) are receptor agonists whose compounds (clobetasol, bexarotene) have indirect sterol-profile negatives; S1-02 and S1-04 (CHRM1, CHRM3 compounds) are **already** `context_dependent` with the contested attribution written in the curation note. The genetic muscarinic rows S1-01 (CHRM1 loss) and S1-03 (CHRM3 loss) are favourable, with the ceiling caveat in S1-01 (Chrm1-null OPCs already differentiate more). **Competing explanation:** the layer is not EBP-dominated: three receptor routes (GR, THR, RXR) enhance differentiation with sterol-profile negatives, and by IT29's own count a single-study GR cluster dominates the row count.
5. **Does a consumer sentence have to change? Yes, in several places, though the layer itself does not.** The verifier found sentences that attribute the clemastine/benztropine/quetiapine effect to their stated class without an EBP qualification (`consumer_delta.md`): the card F02 `identity` ("muscarinic-antagonist class such as clemastine and benztropine, thyroid-hormone and antipsychotic routes", card and packet: no match for EBP, sterol or zymostenol), the INV-06 `evidence_available` sentence ("signs several intrinsic precursor-differentiation routes (muscarinic-receptor, ...)", whose `next_test` in the same row already carries the EBP qualification), `needs/needs.json` line 4480 (the CHRM1 role) and the IT14 glia README rows C1 and C2. **The INV-06 sentence is not stronger than the layer taken whole** (the genetic muscarinic rows are favourable); it needs the qualification that the **drug-class** support is EBP-confounded for the amine compounds. The qualification must **not** claim that EBP explains the muscarinic route, that atropine, ipratropium or oxybutynin act through EBP (unmeasured), or that M1 is irrelevant (two routes are documented for benztropine and clemastine and their attribution is unsettled).
6. **The decision.** The marker-level signed layer may be used as evidence for a target-specific pro-myelinating hypothesis **except** for the amine CNS compounds with a non-sterol stated target (benztropine, clemastine, tamoxifen, ifenprodil, quetiapine), where it must carry an EBP-confound limit; **a sterol-profile control arm per compound is needed before any target attribution for such compounds** (zymostenol/zymosterol readout; already in the X3 clause for the reagent pair), and is **not** needed for the receptor-agonist routes whose compounds have indirect sterol-profile negatives. The compounds with EBP unmeasured (atropine, ipratropium, oxybutynin, sobetirome) need the sterol-profile arm before their attribution is read.
