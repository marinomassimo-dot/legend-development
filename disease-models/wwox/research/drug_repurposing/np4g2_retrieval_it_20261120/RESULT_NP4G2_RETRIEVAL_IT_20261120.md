## What the case answered about NP4G2 itself
By two routes (PubMed, Europe PMC with preprints), spellings stated in `run_arms.py`, at abstract level, no independent report was found that a Wwox-deficient neuron or OPC alone shifts OPC differentiation: every R item is either from the laboratory of Brain 2021 (LAB-A) or a held in-vivo knockout report from another laboratory. Three records outside K were not held (PMID 15026124, 2004 expression in the developing mouse nervous system, A; PMID 32051108 and PMID 42721537, single case and a seven-patient series, A): none moves RP-017.

## Defect found in the engine path (the one real gain): the reopen query had lost the gene
The scout's own functions, applied to the row text, produce the Europe PMC query `TITLE_ABS:opc AND TITLE_ABS:drg` (`B_query.txt`). The row never states the disease (the folder does), `wwox` is excluded from the bridge terms by design, and the two named objects (OPC, DRG) are abbreviations common outside WWOX, so the one hit is an oligodendrocyte electroporation methods paper. Reproduction caveat: I could not run the scout's real discovery path for this row (`--discover-only --theme` returned no themes for the row id, and a hand-built item was skipped by `build_questions`); B is the formula applied by hand with the scout's `entities` and `key_terms`. Minimal correction for HE, if HE agrees after reproducing: when a reopen row sits in a disease folder and its text names no gene symbol, prepend the folder's gene as the first AND term, behind one flag, removable. Not tested here as a gain; a test needs its own criteria (the known positives above give a recall set; the cost is one more query per such row).
