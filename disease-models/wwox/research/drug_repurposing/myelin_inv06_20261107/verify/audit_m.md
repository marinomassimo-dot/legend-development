## 3. Experiment items

X0 (public S-KO nuclei dataset). Judgement: sound as a cheap first step, but two statements need repair.
- The Wwox paper shows the snRNA-seq in Fig 3F (hippocampus, n = 1 vs 1, P17; matured oligodendrocytes 15%, committed progenitors 68%, OPCs 150% as stated) and says "data not shown" only for the marker-based cluster identification. So the instruction "confirm it is the dataset the paper reports as data not shown" mis-describes it; what is held back is the cluster annotation, not the dataset. Composition (low committed/premyelinating, high OPC) is already reported by the paper.
- Direction: the activity-linked branch says "AMPAR genes shifted as in the activity-reduced pattern". In the neonatal study Gria2 (whole lineage) and Gria3/Gria4 (OPCs) went up when activity was reduced (Gi-DREADD, P8). The S-KO is hyperexcitable (bursts, seizures from P9), so the hyperactivity-linked prediction is the opposite direction (lower AMPAR genes with fewer premyelinating cells and more OPCs, which the S-KO composition already shows). As written, the activity branch has the sign inverted for this genotype and the neonatal pattern was never measured under increased activity. Both readings predict low premyelinating share, so the AMPAR direction is the only discriminator and it must be stated with the correct sign. Also different region (hippocampus vs cortex/corpus callosum), age (P17 vs P8), method (nuclei vs cells), n = 1 per arm.
- No step infers myelin damage or benefit from burst reduction. Level 1 only, as stated.

X1 (contact vs soluble vs neuronal state). Judgement: partly separates; arms need controls.
- Arm (b) (null-DRG medium on wild-type OPCs on wild-type DRG) can reveal only a dominant soluble inhibitor; a missing soluble promoter would be masked by the wild-type neurons present. Arm (c) (null-DRG medium on OPCs alone) has no wild-type-medium comparator listed, so a deficit has no baseline. Arm (d) (fixed null DRG) needs a fixed wild-type DRG comparator to say substrate/surface (the neuregulin paper notes that the default programme can ensheath fixed axons, so a fixed-wild-type positive arm is feasible).
- "Needs neuronal state" is only addressed by live vs fixed; activity or firing is not controlled until X2.
- Add axon density/health normalisation (NF-H area), because the published KO panel shows NF-H clumps and a substrate-quantity difference would read as "contact".
- Medium contains forskolin and N-acetyl-cysteine in the published format (E05): keep identical across arms and note it for any soluble-factor or CREB reading.
- Levels 1-2 only; correct. Does not infer function from dish data.

X2 (activity and bursts). Judgement: design is right in structure but the null arm is uninformative in the default medium.
- In the closest published coculture (rat, no neuregulin) neither TTX, MK-801 nor NBQX changed myelination (E30-E31). So "deficit unchanged by every activity arm" in a medium without a mode-switch factor cannot be read as "not activity-linked"; it may only mean the activity-independent default mode is operating. The factor arms are what make the test informative; they must be mandatory, not optional.
- Receptor target: the coculture paper implicates NMDA receptors (kainate currents unchanged), the neonatal slice paper AMPA receptors; including both blocks is justified. Bath block acts on every cell type (E29, E31, E34).
- Recording firing in the dish is the right way to verify any "reduced bursts" claim; peripheral DRG neurons are not cortical burst generators (stated). No arm infers myelin damage or benefit from burst reduction.

X3 (enhancer: counts vs functional myelin). Judgement: logic sound, one wording defect and one missing arm.
- Wording: "counts up and EM/latency up: acts below the missing signal" uses "latency up" for the good outcome; higher latency is slower conduction. It should read latency shorter.
- The slice step lists S-KO animals only; to reproduce or exclude the neonatal wild-type clemastine discordance (E39: differentiation up, myelinated-fibre conduction down at 50 mg/kg i.p. P5-P21), a treated S-Control arm and a vehicle S-KO arm are needed at the slice level, not only in the dish.
- The two clemastine sources disagree in context (wild-type neonate: conduction down; Pdk1 icKO adult-onset hypomyelination: marker, puncta and behaviour rescue, no EM or conduction after treatment), so the enhancer must not be treated as a universal positive control; the item does not do this explicitly, and the wild-type adverse-effect arm is the correct guard.
- S-KO die at 3-4 weeks: the treatment window is necessarily narrower than either published regimen (stated).

X4 (which GSK3 arm is live). Judgement: sound; the key guard is present.
- Requires a genetic GSK3 control and two chemotypes before attributing a compound effect to GSK3: correct, given that the Foxg1 paper used one compound, measured total GSK-3 beta only and described its effect as inhibition of "expression" (E16). The evidence on direction conflicts across the sources (abstract-only report: inhibition raises differentiation; Foxg1 paper: inhibitor lowers the mature share against knockdown; the Foxg1 Introduction cites GSK3 activation promoting differentiation), so the design correctly does not presuppose a direction.
- Needs: pair any phospho-Ser9 readout with a substrate-phosphorylation readout (the item has "a substrate readout"); consider both GSK3 paralogs in the genetic control; forskolin in the co-culture medium raises cAMP and can confound the CREB arm (E05). The Wwox paper itself names Wnt/beta-catenin as a WWOX-regulated pathway in its Discussion, a point the item may use.
- Gated on X1 showing a level 1 defect: appropriate.
