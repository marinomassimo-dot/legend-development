## The question and why this one

**Question.** Is a PDK inhibitor (dichloroacetate, DCA) a defensible partial-benefit lever for a Wwox-deficient brain? Three sub-questions, in the order that decides it: (1) is there a WWOX-linked pyruvate dehydrogenase (PDH) restraint for the inhibitor to release; (2) when the same lever was tried on a seizure endpoint in a model where PDH restraint is documented, did the seizure endpoint move; (3) which way does the lever move blood glucose in a setting that resembles the Wwox-null phenotype (lethal hypoglycaemia with raised glycolytic uptake)?

**The datum that decides (two lines).** The new systems comparison removes the middle link of the chain (PDK1 is not higher in either human neural transcript system: lower in sign only and inside its single-gene null, p about 0.30 and 0.49, so the datum is the absence of support, not a demonstrated decrease; PDH activity is unchanged in the one human line), while the only primary seizure test found of a PDK inhibitor in a PDH-restrained epilepsy model (Durie 2018, PMID 29913407) showed no anticonvulsant effect and a slight pro-convulsant signal. And the same lever lowers glucose in most of the datasets found (diabetic adults, cirrhotic and healthy adults, endotoxaemic minipigs) and the gluconeogenic precursors lactate and alanine in adults, in a disease whose null mouse dies hypoglycaemic: a safety axis that the repository's earlier framing (peripheral nerve only) did not carry.

## Findings that are new in this cycle

1. **PDK transcript sign is cell-type dependent across the WWOX-deficient systems held.** Up in Wwox-null mouse fibroblasts (Pdk1) and in human knockout fibroblasts (qPCR sentence, isoform unspecified in the quoted sentence but PDK1 in the Methods list, normoxia and normoglycaemia, p below 0.05; the system is a polyclonal lentiviral CRISPR/Cas9 knockout with WWOX mRNA about 3-fold and protein about 8.5-fold lower, against scrambled-sgRNA control); lower in sign only in the two human neural transcript systems (organoid p about 0.30, progenitors p about 0.49 against the single-gene null; neurons +0.44 inside a ±0.45 interval at n = 1), so no decrease is demonstrated. Each contrast carries its own confound. It does not say what WWOX does; it says that the repository's "PDK1 up" prediction should not be carried into a neural argument.
2. **Wording flag in the human fibroblast paper.** One Results sentence lists "pyruvate dehydrogenase kinase (PDK)" among the enzymes whose activity was unchanged, whereas Methods 4.10 describes only a PDH activity kit and Figure 5b is labelled PDH. The repository reads it as the PDH assay (manifest entries 7 and 8); the paper does not resolve it.
3. **PDH restraint is documented after chemoconvulsant status epilepticus in two non-WWOX models** (seizure, status-epilepticus injury, neuron loss and drug exposure are not separable in either; the rat paper measures protein and phospho-protein level at one week, not activity): rat after pilocarpine seizure (PDK2 up 83 percent, PDH down 62 percent, Ser293 phosphorylation up 62 percent; PMID 36432491, body read) and mouse in the chronic stage (PDH activity down 65 percent, protein down 37 percent, Ser300 phosphorylation up 52 percent; PMID 29913407, abstract). A restraint found in a seizing WWOX-DEE brain would therefore not be shown to be WWOX-dependent by itself; this is a competing explanation that the module's metabolic argument had not carried.
4. **The seizure endpoint**: in that chronic mouse model DCA partially restored PDH activity and did not restore the second-hit seizure latency, did not lengthen latency in the acute test and was slightly pro-convulsant in the 6 Hz test (abstract). The rat result that is quoted in favour (DCA plus pyruvate) is a histology endpoint (surviving neurons); the inactivity of the single agents is, in the text read, a Discussion statement citing earlier work (the design had single-agent arms; the figure was not read). Sample sizes differ between the rat paper's Methods and legends.
5. **Efficacy record in children.** Randomized double-blind trial in 43 children with congenital lactic acidosis, six months: post-meal lactate rise reduced; global assessment, linear growth and intercurrent illness not different (PMID 16651305). Long-term follow-up (PMID 18411236): cerebrospinal fluid lactate lower (the compound reaches the CNS compartment), conduction slowing, standardized growth below the first percentile (not separable from disease). MELAS trial (PMID 16476929): 15 of 15 on DCA stopped medication against 4 of 15 on placebo, 17 of 19 discontinuations for neuropathy, trial terminated early.
6. **Glucose: the weight of the datasets points to lowering; the sign in a hypoglycaemic young animal is unmeasured.** Adults with raised glucose: fasting glucose down 24 percent, lactate down 73 percent, alanine down 82 percent, ketone bodies up 71 percent (PMID 625308). Endotoxaemic minipigs: hypoglycaemia deeper than untreated through lower hepatic glucose output (3521930). Cirrhotic and healthy adults, acute infusion: glucose production down 7 to 9 percent and plasma glucose down 9 to 14 percent in BOTH groups; only the group difference is null (10546922; corrected after the blind audit, the first version recorded this as a null). Septic mice: hypoglycaemia reversed (35730570), but plasma lactate and pyruvate were lower in septic mice, a fasting confound is acknowledged and anorexia was reversed too, so this is not a comparable lactic-acidosis state and the reversal may follow feeding. Severe malaria with lactic acidosis: the infusion study prevented hypoglycaemia by giving glucose (8052155). Three of four datasets therefore point to lowering. The Wwox-null mouse is the model whose lethal phenotype is the low glucose, and its hypoglycaemia is rescued by a neuronal promoter (strong ubiquitous promoters rescued it early only), so it is not purely peripheral. Prediction 2 in the card now has three categories: a fall beyond wild type, a modest fall in both genotypes, no fall or a rise (read with food intake).
7. **Genotype-dependent response is the design precedent**: in PDC-deficient patient fibroblasts DCA lowered glycolysis by 29 percent with no significant effect on control cells (PMID 16765624). A WWOX experiment therefore asks for a genotype-by-inhibitor interaction, not an effect in the deficient cells alone.

## Counter-evidence kept (for and against, in the card)

For: higher hexokinase and LDH activity and lactate in a human knockout fibroblast line, and LDHA with a hand-picked HIF-type gene set higher in the two human neural transcript systems (the broader 19-gene panel is inside the null in the progenitor line, and the organoid mean is driven by five genes), with Pdk1 up in mouse cells; cerebrospinal fluid lactate fell in children on DCA (drug level in CSF not measured; CNS exposure stays UNKNOWN) and there is a paediatric safety record; DCA plus pyruvate protected hippocampal neurons after seizure in rats; second-hand report of protection against hypoglycaemia-induced neuron death in rats; a genotype-dependent response is demonstrable in cells. Against: see findings 1 to 6; plus a thiamine-linked oxalate observation in rats, a G6PD-deficient neonate case (methaemoglobinaemia), and the 22 percent rise in urinary oxalate in the long-term cohort; the large inter-individual variability in DCA exposure through autoinhibition of its clearing enzyme.

## Transfer limit

Everything about the lever comes from mitochondrial disease (children, adolescents, adults), adult epilepsy mice and rats, diabetic or cirrhotic adults and large-animal or mouse sepsis. None of it is a WWOX-deficient system, a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy, or an infant-stage brain with a documented hypoglycaemic phenotype. A PDH restraint released by the lever in a PDH-deficient cell does not show that a WWOX-deficient cell has one; the seizure null is an adult mouse; the glucose evidence is of mixed sign. The lever is a metabolic bypass: partial benefit at best, and only if the bottleneck exists.

4. Priority: the measurement that decides several cards at once is a PDH phosphorylation/activity and glucose-tracer experiment in a WWOX-deficient human neural system; it ranks above further literature work on this strand. No favourable compound is offered. Status of the card: controversial.
