# Addendum B2 to checkpoint HE-B (HE successor, 2026-11-20 night): retirement of a released predecessor is ordinary maintenance

Reference-genotype class only; nothing here is medical advice; no personal data. Checkpoint B and its addendum B1 stay unedited (their digests were given to launchers); this file supersedes the wording below wherever they differ.

## What B section 4 and section 5 said, and the correction
- B section 4 ("Left for the operator ... idle old windows ... `claude bg-spare` holders") and B section 5 ("no `claude stop`") described the stopping of released **background** predecessors as an operator-only duty, after one automatic refusal of a compound shell command on 2026-11-20. One refusal of one specific command is not a standing rule for all analogous maintenance.
- Current rule (cross-session transport, "Retire the released predecessor at a session handoff"; design record `governance/design_records/session_retirement_20261120.md`): ordinary retirement of an exact, released, idle **background** predecessor needs no new operator instruction. Use `mandate_watch.py handoff --task <ID> --old <full id> --new <full id> --retire-old`, or the hourly `check --all --deliver resume` catch-up. Only the supported `claude stop <runtime id>` is issued, as a separate argument vector; no `kill`, no `claude rm`, no `--force`, no deletion of conversations, worktrees or checkpoints; the outcome is verified by the process actually being gone.
- Still the operator's or still protected: interactive windows (for example the old interactive HE terminal), a session with live descendants or a live task job, an ambiguous or absent successor, an observation that could not be made, and a runtime refusal of a specific action (resolve that action; do not retry it through another wrapper or permission mode, and do not turn it into a ban on all retirement).
- Old worktrees and `claude bg-spare` holders of old worktrees are a different matter from stopping a released host: `git worktree remove` (no `--force`) is already authorised for closed, integrated, clean worktrees (LEGEND_CORE 21e item 3) once nothing holds them.

## The defect found and fixed in the catch-up (2026-11-20 night)
The hourly catch-up reached `retire_released` only for the states `RUNNING` and `STOPPED_RESUMABLE`, so a released predecessor of a successor whose mandate was `BLOCKED` or complete-pending (`COMPLETE`, the state of the standing mandates) stayed live. `framework/scripts/mandate_watch.py` now reaches it for `RUNNING`, `STOPPED_RESUMABLE`, `BLOCKED` and `COMPLETE`; not for `PAUSED` (which includes `CANCELLED`), `UNKNOWN` or `DUPLICATE_HOST`; all protections inside `retirement_state` are unchanged. Regressions: `test_mandate_watch.py` (states that retire and states that do not; the real classifier's states for ALL_BLOCKED, COMPLETE_PENDING and PAUSED/CANCELLED tied to the set).
