## 2 · The one decisive question, narrowed before searching
*Which pharmacological perturbation could improve a functional component of myelination while the neuronal defect persists, and which comparison would tell a limit set by the neuronal signal or axon from an intrinsic block of the oligodendrocyte?*
**Narrowed to:** a seizure-free neuron–OPC co-culture (mouse, wild-type or Wwox-deficient neurons with wild-type OPCs, then the reverse pairing), **sheath formation and structure** (MBP-positive internodes aligned with axons: number per axon length and internode length, with a marker level read beside it) as the endpoint, treatment started **after** the defect is established (recovery) and, separately, from plating (prevention). Levels kept apart: proliferation, survival and maturation; differentiation markers; sheath formation and structure; conduction and network function (not measurable in the dish without a recording arm; named as the next level, not claimed). **A result at one level does not settle the others.**
**The discriminating logic.** A reagent whose pro-maturation action is shown **independent of neurons** (it works in OPC monoculture) is an *intrinsic* lever. If it raises sheath formation equally on wild-type and on Wwox-deficient neurons, it does not close the null-specific gap: the limit sits with the neuron or axon (signal, contact, substrate). If it closes the gap, the block is OPC-intrinsic or permissive. A reagent that acts **through** a neuronal signal (activity, a neuregulin- or BDNF-type cue) tests the signal side: it should act only where the neuron can answer. The pair, with a genotype factor, is what separates the explanations; each alone does not.

## 4 · Priors, fixed before the search (recollection or inference; unverified; scored afterwards, may fail)
* **P1.** The pro-differentiation action of clemastine and benztropine is retained in neuron-free OPC culture, and several of these compounds converge on the sterol enzyme EBP (accumulation of 8,9-unsaturated sterols) in addition to or instead of M1 antagonism; so "two chemotypes, one target" is **not** to be assumed (the muscarinic and the sterol readings predict different dishes).
* **P2.** No source tests an OPC-intrinsic enhancer on a **neuron-driven** hypomyelination at the level of sheath structure **and** conduction; the nearest (Pdk1 icKO, clemastine) is marker, synapse and behaviour.
* **P3.** Where an intrinsic enhancer was tested in a model with a persistent neuronal or axonal defect, markers rose more than sheath structure or conduction (a ceiling), or the effect depended on the neuronal context.
* **P4.** Evidence on the neuronal-signal side (activity, neuregulin, BDNF) is context-dependent: sheath formation can be activity-independent in one culture format and NMDA-receptor-dependent with added factors (held: Lundgaard 2013).
* **P5.** At least one source shows a lipid or substrate supply manipulation that rescues or fails to rescue hypomyelination with a defect outside the oligodendrocyte; its transfer to a neuron-driven model is not established.
* **P6.** A conduction readout after a pro-myelinating treatment can move opposite to the marker readout (held: neonatal clemastine raised differentiation and slowed callosal conduction).
