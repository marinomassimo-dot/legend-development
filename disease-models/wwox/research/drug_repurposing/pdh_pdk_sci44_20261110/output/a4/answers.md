## Q4a — LDH inhibition and neuronal excitability
**Answer (DATO, small base).** One open-access primary source with an LDH inhibitor: oxamate shortened status epilepticus in adult mice (1 g/kg i.p.; 5 vs 8 animals; extracellular lactate fell, 3 animals) and, at 3 mM in CA1 slices, lowered network calcium activity (20 neurons) and sEPSC frequency but not amplitude (8 cells) (A4D01, A4D02; 37632130). No resting potential, input resistance, KATP test or in-slice engagement was reported; oxamate was not controlled for other actions. The stiripentol report that says LDH inhibition hyperpolarised neurons and that pyruvate reversed it is abstract-only (A4D03). **Harmful or neutral side:** in adult mouse dmPFC, deleting Ldha in astrocytes lowered lactate and firing (13 vs 12 neurons) with resting potential and input resistance unchanged, and worsened depressive-like behaviour; neuron-targeted Ldha knockdown changed nothing; lactate uptake through neuronal MCT2 was required (A4D04, A4D05; 36759610). So the sign of an LDH change depends on the compartment, and a lactate shortage can lower excitability, which is not the same as benefit. Stiripentol's other documented actions (GABA-A potentiation EC50 24.6-35.5 uM in recombinant cells, A4D06; cytochrome P450 inhibition and channel actions via a review, A4D07) were **not** controlled for in any primary neuron source reached.

## Q4b — metabolic state and resting K+ conductance
**Answer (DATO; ABSENCE within the perimeter).** The measured metabolic-to-K+ link in these sources is KATP, in young (P12-19) dentate granule cells, and it needs ATP not to be dialysed: 2 mM BHB raised single-channel KATP open probability about threefold (peak 3.14 vs 1.09 percent; 8 vs 35 patches), basal 1.71 vs 0.46 percent (A4D08); Bad knockout granule cells fired about half as much, restored by 200 nM glibenclamide, and the authors state KATP is almost completely inactive in intact wild-type cells (A4D10). Lactate and astrocytic Ldha loss changed neither Vm nor Rin in adult dmPFC pyramidal neurons; lactate acted through BK channels on the fast AHP (A4D11). No K2P effect was tested in any source read. **Size:** the only conductance figures (+30 percent for activity-driven KATP, about +100 percent with BHB) are the authors' calculation for young high-resistance granule cells (A4D09), not measurements of mV or percent Rin; they are of the order of, but a different sign and cell type from, the roughly 28 percent LOSS of conductance in the Wwox neuron. A fuel or redox change opens channels (adds conductance); it could only offset a loss if the loss were itself a KATP-type deficit, which nothing read supports (WT KATP is mostly closed). No source with a pyramidal-neuron Vm/Rin/K+-current measurement for ketone, pyruvate, lactate, glucose or LDH manipulation was found (A4D12; perimeter in `queries.tsv` Q03, Q05, Q06, Q23). A connection to the SCI43 potassium branch is therefore **not established**; it remains an open measurement (INFERENZA).

## Q4c — other enzyme-side levers
**Answer.** By name, nothing was found for a PDP activator, thiamine/lipoate/CoA or acetate in neural cells (strings Q16, Q22, Q26, Q27; listing only, `levers.tsv` L11-L13). Two items were read: the pyruvate-carrier inhibitor UK5099 (5-10 uM) cut maximal pyruvate oxidation by more than 50 percent in rat cortical neuron cultures without cell death, raised glutamate oxidation and was protective in excitotoxicity — a lever that moves opposite to PDH release (A4D13); and triheptanoin, in chronic pilocarpine epileptic mice, was associated with maximal hippocampal PDH activity of 1.0 vs 0.4 (untreated epileptic) and 1.3 (non-epileptic) nmol/min/mg (7-9 mice), activity not flux or phosphorylation (A4D14). ## Q4d — comparison at matched suppression (stiripentol)
Stiripentol's documented neuron-level actions at concentrations in the tens of micromolar are GABA-A potentiation (recombinant cells) and channel actions, while LDH inhibition is described at about 500 uM, so any antiseizure or firing effect of stiripentol cannot be assigned to the metabolic route from the sources reached, and the one primary neuron report was unreachable. A comparison at matched suppression of firing or seizures between an LDH route (oxamate-type or Ldha-selective) and a receptor route (GABA-A potentiation, ideally with GABA-A blocked or with pyruvate added back) would resolve whether the metabolic step is necessary or only coincident, and whether it changes resting properties or only evoked firing; no clinical preference or substitution is implied.
