## Item 1. The metabolic-to-K+ link (A4D08-A4D11, A4D12)

* **A4D08 SUPPORTED.** Tanner 2011 (PMC3133530): 2 mM R-BHB, mean peak popen 3.14 +/- 0.90 percent (n=8 patches) vs 1.09 +/- 0.32 (n=35, ACSF); basal 1.71 +/- 0.64 vs 0.46 +/- 0.17; P12-16 male C57BL/6 dentate granule neurons; cell-attached with a K+/TEA/4-AP/Cs+ pipette (ATP undisturbed); no Vm or Rin with BHB; BHB patches not re-tested with glibenclamide. The ACSF controls are unpaired and exclude the paired-glibenclamide controls (Methods). Correction: `k_coupling.tsv` K01 and K03 say recording temperature not stated; Methods: "All recordings were obtained at room temperature."
* **A4D09 SUPPORTED.** The +30 percent (activity-driven) and about +100 percent (BHB) are the authors' calculation: NN414 whole-cell conductance about 0.8 nS (n=2) times the popen ratio (peak 1.1 percent is about one fifth of the NN414 4.7 percent, about 160 pS) against a 400-500 pS resting conductance of young cells (input resistance 2.5 GOhm, 0.4 nS). INFERENZA I add: the same 160 pS against the roughly 4 nS the authors give for older cells is about 4 percent. The figure does not transfer to mature neurons.
* **A4D10 PARTLY.** Bad-/- vs WT (Fig 2B viewed): n=7 per group, spikes about 4.5/6.3/8.8 vs 9.6/13.2/14.9 at 100/150/200 pA; amphotericin perforated patch, room temperature, P13-19; glibenclamide 200 nM restores Bad-/-. "Almost completely inactive in wild-type cells" is in the Results as the authors' inference. "Without changing wild type" is n.s. only: the WT glibenclamide means in the figure are higher (about 10.9/16.3/20.9 vs 9.6/13.2/14.9, raster read) with large error bars.
* **A4D11 PARTLY.** (1) Attribution: the Vm/Rin null with 13 vs 12 neurons (3 vs 3 mice; RMP t(23)=0.83, Rin t(23)=0.94) is the GFAP-Cre conditional knockout; the paper says this Cre "is present in neural progenitor cells, which give rise to neurons and astrocytes". It is not an adult astrocyte-specific deletion. The astrocyte-specific adult arm (AAV-GFAP-Cre, Fig 4e, 12 vs 13 neurons, 3+3 mice) reports lower firing and lactate, no Vm/Rin. Same error in `decisive.tsv` A4D04, `k_coupling.tsv` K05 and `levers.tsv` L06 ("astrocyte-targeted"). (2) The fAHP: Source Data (Fig 7f) gives 8.30 vs 6.02 mV, a 2.27 mV difference, not "about 2.6 (raster)". The lactate Vm/Rin null (15 neurons, 5 mice, mean Vm -66.0 vs -66.6 mV, Rin 0.161 vs 0.156 GOhm) is right; 10 mM sodium lactate for 2 h; adult male; slices held at 24 C, recording temperature not stated; 4 mM Mg-ATP and 10 mM phosphocreatine pipette; no KATP or K2P test: correct.
* **A4D12 NOT SUPPORTED** as an absence claim. It reads "No primary report was found in which a ketone, lactate, pyruvate-supply, glucose or LDH manipulation changed Vm, input resistance or a K+ current of neocortical or CA1 pyramidal neurons with ATP and temperature stated". Counter-examples from my strings V01-V12 and the corrected strings:
  * Karagiannis 2021 (34766906, PMC text read): juvenile (13-24 day) rat and mouse barrel cortex, layers I-IV, regular-spiking and bursting glutamatergic cells among 277 neurons; KATP modulators change membrane potential, membrane resistance and firing (diazoxide roughly doubles whole-cell conductance; whole-cell at 25.3 C); in gramicidin perforated patch (ATP undisturbed) 15 mM lactate with 2.5 mM glucose roughly doubles firing, reduced by diazoxide, absent in Kcnj11-/-; pyruvate acts like lactate; the pooled analysis does not separate pyramidal cells, so the pyramidal share is INFERENZA.
  * Epilepsy Res 2026 (42263507, abstract): 3 mM BHB hyperpolarises resting Vm of CA1 pyramidal neurons and enhances an A-type current in immature KA-treated mice; abstract only.
  * Epilepsia 2026 (42574076, abstract): BHB hyperpolarises dentate granule cells through GIRK channels; inhibiting KATP had no detectable effect (4-week mice); abstract only.
  * eLife 2022 (35188099, abstract): MPC1 loss in adult glutamatergic neurons lowers M-type K+ channel activity and causes hyperexcitability; ketone bodies restore it.
  * J Neurochem 2009 (19943846, abstract): ketone bodies, pyruvate or lactate added to glucose hyperpolarise neonatal neocortical neurons (Em and E-GABA).
  The statement is true only of the producer's read set. `answers.md` Q4b ("No source with a pyramidal-neuron Vm/Rin/K+-current measurement ... was found") must be re-worded as "not found in the sources read here".
* **Decision statement "the link to the Wwox potassium branch is not established, opposite sign": PARTLY.** "Not established" for a Wwox preparation is justified: nothing was measured in a Wwox neuron. "Opposite sign" is not a general property of metabolic manipulations: lactate and pyruvate close KATP and raise firing (Karagiannis); BHB opens KATP in juvenile dentate granule cells (Tanner) but later work finds GIRK (dentate), A-type current (CA1 pyramidal) or KCNQ2/3 (recombinant, EC50 0.7 uM, 31757819, title and abstract only) as the BHB route. The sign relative to the Wwox loss was not given to me and I did not check it.
* **Item 9a: the Q4b sentence "a fuel or redox change opens channels ... could only offset a loss if the loss were itself a KATP-type deficit, which nothing read supports (WT KATP is mostly closed)" NOT SUPPORTED.** (a) Fuels do not generally open channels (lactate closes KATP). (b) A conductance added through any K+ channel changes resting Rin and Vm; the constraint "only if KATP-type" does not hold (a channel-specific function would not be restored, a resting-conductance deficit could be offset). (c) "WT KATP mostly closed" is the authors' inference for juvenile dentate granule cells; in neocortical neurons KATP activation doubles the whole-cell conductance. Rewrite as an open question labelled INFERENZA.

## What changes in the decision lines

* **Metabolic-to-K+ link (Q4b).** Keep "not established for a Wwox preparation". Replace "opposite sign" and "no source with a pyramidal-neuron measurement was found" by: a metabolic-to-K+ coupling is measured in neocortical, CA1 and dentate neurons, through different channels and in both directions (lactate/pyruvate close KATP and raise firing; BHB opens KATP in P12-16 dentate granule cells, or acts through GIRK, A-type or M currents in other preparations), with ages 2-4 weeks or immature; none in a Wwox neuron; the sign against the Wwox K+ loss cannot be assigned from these sources. Drop the "only if the loss were KATP-type" constraint. The dmPFC null for Vm/Rin is the GFAP-Cre knockout (neurons and astrocytes), not an astrocyte-specific result.
* **Lactate side as lever or probe (Q4a).** As a lever for benefit: not supported (no benefit measurement; oxamate effects uncontrolled; astrocytic/GFAP-Cre Ldha loss lowered excitability and worsened a behavioural readout). As a probe: usable, with two documented routes to excitability (KATP closure in juvenile neocortex; BK-mediated fAHP in dmPFC) and a ready design (diazoxide, tolbutamide, Kcnj11 knockout, IbTx), but oxamate's resting properties were never measured and its off-target actions are uncontrolled.
* **Antiepileptic comparison (Q4d).** First sentence stands; second sentence should be labelled IPOTESI and list the arms (pyruvate add-back, KATP block or opener, GABA-A block) and the resting-property readouts.
* **Two A4 and A2 absence statements** (acetate/PDH-neural tests; isoform-selective loss in neurons) must be withdrawn; 35188099, 30787166, 37188779, 36551928 and the two titles 33725483 and 35444068 should be read before the lever question (A1 decision 3) is closed.
