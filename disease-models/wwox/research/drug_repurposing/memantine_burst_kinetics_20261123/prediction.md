# Does temporal clustering alone confer selective memantine block?

The Wwox neuronal-deletion mouse study reports a median cortical field burst duration 0.66 s (0.53–1.06) and interburst interval 26.2 s. A supplementary median frequency of 0.84/min implies an alternative interval near 71.4 s. These are field measurements and cannot identify the glutamate concentration, number of synaptic releases, membrane voltage, or intracellular calcium at an individual receptor.

Before simulating, take the published 22-state GluN1/GluN2A memantine model with final Table 2 rates (not initial NMODL defaults), fixed voltage −65 mV, zero extracellular Mg and imposed intracellular calcium. Compare five hypothetical 1 mM, 2-ms glutamate pulses clustered over 0.66 s against the same five pulses spread evenly through 26.2 s. These inputs match total pulse number and cumulative duration, conditional on exactly five releases at one receptor. Biological release concentration and pulse count are unobserved. Compute fractional integrated conducting charge versus the no-drug trajectory of the same pattern, at periodic equilibrium and on the first cycle starting with drug-free receptors. The primary model contrast is cluster-minus-even percentage-point block at 0.36 µM memantine and 0.1 µM intracellular calcium. A ≥5-point excess is the predeclared screen for a material timing-only preference in this idealized model. Test 1 and 10 µM, zero and 10 µM calcium, the two duration bounds, a longer period, and 15 or 33 pulses. Also include sustained saturating agonist as a steady-state calibration against the original data. No outcome will estimate clinical efficacy, Mg-aware block, or inhibition of a WWOX burst in vivo.
