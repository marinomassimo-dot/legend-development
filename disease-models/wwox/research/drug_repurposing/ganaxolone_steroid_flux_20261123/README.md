# Ganaxolone and the possibility of a missing endogenous neurosteroid

**Decision, 23 November 2026.** A possible androsterone-to-androstanediol defect merits a paired metabolite measurement if suitable WWOX-deficient neural material becomes available. Current evidence gives no sign for a change in endogenous GABA-A modulation and provides no disease-specific biochemical rationale for assigning ganaxolone higher priority. The most informative historical experiment uses mouse protein in human kidney cells. Its figure follows disappearance of the precursor, and the disease brain retains expression of a second mouse enzyme capable of the same reaction. [Locked prediction](prediction.md) · [Earlier receptor-level comparison of ganaxolone with allopregnanolone](../ganaxolone_developmental_history_20261123/README.md)

## Two different chemical reactions

In a 2006 dissertation, Brigitte D. Keller reported that 293T cells expressing mouse Wwox converted the 17-ketosteroids androstenedione and androsterone to testosterone and androstanediol, respectively. The second product is a GABA-A modulator. The substrate androsterone also modulates this receptor. Redistributing steroid between the two may change the magnitude of endogenous inhibition, but substrate and product activities, local free concentrations and their other metabolic routes determine the sign. Conversion of 5α-dihydroprogesterone to allopregnanolone would instead require reduction at C3 of a different substrate. The thesis provides no direct evidence for that reaction. The C3 methyl group of ganaxolone further distinguishes it from allopregnanolone, while human studies document other routes of ganaxolone metabolism including reduction at C20. A drug might substitute for a receptor input even if it did not repair the missing conversion. [Keller, §§2.6.1–2.6.2 and 2.6.10](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/603773/603773.pdf#page=81) · [Androstanediol electrophysiology](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20551294/) · [Androsterone primary study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1181535/) · [Human ganaxolone metabolism](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36863866/)

## What changes after checking the actual curves?

We recalculated differences from the vector markers in Keller's figure 2.58. At 48 h the mouse-Wwox minus mock contrast was about +22.5 percentage points for testosterone appearance and +21.5 points for androsterone disappearance. At 96 h it was +55 and +10 points, respectively. By this last time, mock cells had already consumed about 86% of the androsterone. The right-hand axis and the text identify *remaining precursor subtracted from initial precursor* as the plotted outcome. The baseline chromatograms also indicate a hydroxylated product, and the androstanediol reference produced two peaks. A difference in substrate disappearance cannot be assigned quantitatively to the active 3α,17β product. The overexpression experiment measures intact-cell metabolism; ancillary enzymes, uptake and secondary metabolism can contribute. [Source figure and Table 2.10, printed p. 69](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/603773/603773.pdf#page=81) · [Recalculated contrasts](results/figure_contrasts.tsv)

Using the printed apparent kinetic parameters and equal substrate concentrations in separate assays gives an androsterone-to-androstenedione velocity ratio of 4.33 at 0.1 µM, 6.62 at 1 µM and 10.92 at 10 µM. These are conditional calculations, including extrapolation. The androstenedione fit was explicitly forced, enzyme concentration was unknown and velocities were derived from living cells. A preference inferred from these numbers does not override the time-dependent background or identify the product. The human construct yielded no demonstrable conversion, with overexpression unconfirmed. A separate human WWOX fusion study used crude bacterial extracts and did not identify products. The corpus already has a verified full-text analysis of that study. ## Does the developing mouse brain retain a competing route?

Mouse Hsd17b12 converted both of the same androgen substrates in an independent transfected-cell study. In that study the human and monkey homologues had different substrate specificity. We therefore looked for expression of this competitor in the actual mouse disease material and separately reported human expression with no assignment of equivalent androgen activity. [Mouse and primate substrate comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17641292/)

In each mouse series Hsd17b12 decreased relative to control and remained above the prespecified 1 TPM threshold in every mutant. Pooled hippocampal nuclei assigned to excitatory neurons contained detectable Hsd17b12 in 329/1,596 control and 286/1,262 mutant nuclei. In inhibitory neurons the counts were 73/246 and 64/293. In the control library, 123 excitatory and 46 inhibitory nuclei contained both Wwox and Hsd17b12 RNA. This places a potential alternative in the relevant cells without establishing compensation, steroid turnover or protein abundance. Each genotype contributed a single pooled library, and expression in control nuclei can be underestimated by dropout. Residual WWOX transcripts, including higher total WWOX gene-level counts in the human KO libraries, do not report intact or functional protein. ## What observation would promote this route?

The relevant outcome is isotope-resolved appearance of 3α,17β-androstanediol from androsterone, alongside residual labeled precursor and competing products. A lower flux in WWOX-deficient neural preparations that returns toward control after restoration of WWOX would support dependence on the gene. Establishing direct human catalysis requires an expression-verified assay capable of separating product stereoisomers. To connect that flux to ganaxolone, reconstitute the **measured free steroid mixture** from each genotype in the same receptor context. A deficit in current that follows the mixture, is corrected by restoring the missing product, and responds differentially to ganaxolone would support functional substitution. If mixtures elicit similar currents at native concentrations, the case for substitution weakens even if labeled turnover differs. A receptor response to ganaxolone under both conditions would still be compatible with ordinary antiseizure pharmacology.
