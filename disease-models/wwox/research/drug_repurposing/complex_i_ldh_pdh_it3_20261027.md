**What the quantity is.** The 25 to 150 uM series is NADH-driven oxygen consumption of heart and liver mitochondrial preparations (flux of the organelle chain entered at complex I), not an isolated-enzyme activity; the same paper's INT and duroquinone assays are dye-reduction activity measures; LDH inhibition in the other sources is lysate or recombinant-enzyme activity at 250 to 500 uM or cellular lactate (Guyon). No source measures LDH and complex I in one assay and no LDH IC50 exists: NO potency comparison; only "effective concentrations in separate systems are ordered" (INFERENZA) stands. Stiripentol lowers respiration while genetic LDHA/B knockout raises respiratory capacity (Guyon): no phenocopy.

**Decisions.** (i) Stage 0 of HYP-20261026-11: the complex I arm was already added on main (batch 23); the PARS2 reading adds only a readout rule (ISR and LDH abundance read beside LDH activity and lactate in every complex I arm), because an Ldh abundance rise accompanies a complex I-reduced, ISR-active fly neuron model. That makes two questions, not results: a raised Ldh in the Wwox-reduced fly may reflect mitochondrial or ISR state; complex I inhibition might recruit an Ldh rise that opposes LDH lowering. Acute drug exposure is not chronic genetic loss of mitochondrial translation. (ii) E02: wording of DR-E02-006 made explicit (flux readout of preparations); PARS2 observation row and counter-evidence/not_known sentences added. (iii) Counter-evidence: the HYP-11 CONTRA entry already carries the complex I point; nothing further. No separate counter-evidence registry record was found for stiripentol beyond HYP-11 CONTRA, E02 `evidence_against`, DL-REPO-004.
