## What the two runs tell, on the same 25 passages

1. The glossary removed one error class: terms of art whose programme sense a translator gets wrong (engagement, translation probe, glia line). That is real and was measured: 11 of 12 earlier CHANGED rows cleared.
2. It created another: where the programme sense is not the ordinary sense of the English word ('lead', 'restated', 'need'), a rendering that follows the programme is graded CHANGED or MINOR by a reader who does not have the programme sense, and a rendering that follows the ordinary sense is wrong for the programme. A translation cannot carry a sense that the English text does not carry. HE's decision already says what has to change for that: the English for new text ('governing position', 'the reference intervention with a WWOX-system result', 'considered, not decisive') is plainer. Until the source says it, a blind check can only be passed by a gloss that a reader can see, and a visible gloss adds words to every row.
3. The residual error rate has a floor made of the translator's own slips (two rows here: washout, 'corte'). They were caught by the blind check, not by the parity check; the parity check saw 0 of the 8.
4. Variance: the two runs used different delegates and one different set of renderings; 12 against 8 rows is not a measured improvement, because the delegates' thresholds differ (ADV-132's delegate graded 'lead' FAITHFUL or did not see it as an issue; this one graded it CHANGED). Rows are comparable by cause, not by count.

## The choice (one paragraph)

Keep the notice as the default; do not extend the translated scope and do not go section-wise with the glossary now. The glossary is worth keeping as a reference for any later attempt, because it cleared the three term classes that made ADV-132 fail; but the stop rule fired again, on a class the glossary cannot fix by itself ('lead', 'restated', 'need': programme senses the English does not carry), and the two repairs that are the translator's alone (washout, 'corte') would need a third blind pass that this window has no reason to spend before the English changes. The next experiment that could change the choice is narrow: wait until HE lands the plainer English for 'lead', 'restated' and 'need' in the programme text (HE's decision says new text may use it at once), then repeat the 8 rows and the 9 'restated' rows only, with a different delegate and the same stop rule. 'Stop at the covered sections' is the same as the default plus no extension, and is what this result supports until then.
