## What the new rows change in the one-page answer

The earlier table was almost all non-replications. The new rows add **one confirmed repurposed drug, one missense precedent family, one external comparator that held up and one measurement of untreated drift**. They change the answer in four places, each tied to the row that changes it:

1. **A confirmed effect exists, and it is a seizure-count effect on a short, tightly controlled design (35499850, 42389070, 42334048).** The one repurposed drug here whose effect was later confirmed was confirmed on a **count** (drop or convulsive seizures) over a **4-week prospective baseline and 14 weeks of treatment, with concomitant medication frozen**, in randomised placebo-controlled trials with a global-impression scale as the non-seizure secondary. Development was not an endpoint of the trial; the later non-seizure claims are post hoc, caregiver-rated and only weakly tied to seizure change. So the earlier statement "no precedent shows disease modification" stands and is sharpened: the only *confirmed* repurposing effect here is symptomatic, and an evaluation in WOREE that wants the same credibility needs the same ingredients (a prospective baseline, frozen background treatment, a count whose placebo response is known) before it adds a development endpoint.
2. **The global-impression threshold decides whether placebo is separable (35499850; 36538782; 42334048).** In the confirmed trial a "much or very much improved" threshold separated drug from placebo (5 to 6 percent against 26 to 34 percent) while "any improvement" did not (34 to 37 percent on placebo; P 0.06 by the investigator). In the soticlestat pain trial any-improvement was 44 to 47 percent in both arms. In the real-world registry "improved" included "minimally improved" for 34 of the 59 points on cognition, in under half of patients. An evaluation card must name the threshold before data.
3. **Untreated drift in a monogenic DEE can be measured and was small in the cohort mean, which sharpens, not removes, the need for a per-patient baseline (39439575 against the SCN2A antisense child of the earlier note).** In 65 SCN2A children over about a year without intervention the growth scale value changed by minus 1.5 (interval minus 4.32 to 1.33, receptive) and plus 0.3 (minus 1.11 to 1.72, expressive), with two-week test-retest intraclass correlations above 0.90, while the standardized score fell by 2.8 points a year. The single treated child in the antisense study moved 15 to 28 growth-scale points between two assessments before any dose. So the cohort mean is stable and a single child is not: **what a card needs is the within-patient scatter over its own baseline, which the cohort mean does not give.** The earlier note's "untreated scatter in WOREE" remains **not established here** (no WOREE longitudinal measure was found; this is not an absence claim).
4. **An external natural-history comparator held up when the decline was steep, ordinal and near-monotone (40162009).** Real-world treated children matched 1:1 on age and exact baseline score against registry controls reproduced the pivotal-trial effect (difference in decline rate 1.42 against 1.75 points per 48 weeks; hazard ratios 0.08 and 0.07 against 0.14 and 0.02). The controls fell by a median 1.66 points per 48 weeks with the control curve falling to zero by month 30, so the effect was large against both the scatter and the follow-up. This replaces "no external-control framework is mature" with a condition: an external comparator is credible when the untreated course is fast, hard-endpointed and measured on the same scale; none of those conditions is shown for WOREE (one timepoint per patient). The treated and control groups still differed on onset age and sex, controls were followed for less time, and the authors list residual confounding.

What is **not** changed: no pharmacodynamic anchor exists for WWOX; soticlestat now has a third indication (pain) with a 70 percent fall in the marker and a primary whose interval includes zero (36538782), which adds to, and does not alter, the earlier warning that engagement is necessary and nowhere near sufficient.

### 35499850 - Knupp 2022, fenfluramine phase 3 in Lennox-Gastaut syndrome - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-35499850-01` (partial: the protocol and eMaterial PDFs were not read; text, Tables 1-2 and Figures 1-3 read) - manifest PASS, 16 locators.

- **Design (DATO).** Double-blind, placebo-controlled, 65 sites, 263 randomised 1:1:1 (two active groups and placebo) after a **4-week prospective baseline**; 14 weeks of titration plus maintenance; antiseizure drugs, diet and stimulator settings were **not allowed to change**; caregiver electronic diaries; diagnosis confirmed by an independent consortium panel; serial gatekeeping for multiplicity.
- **Endpoint type (DATO).** Primary: percentage change in expert-confirmed **drop-seizure count** (rank analysis of covariance, Hodges-Lehmann median). Key secondaries: 50-percent responder rate and a clinician-rated global-improvement scale (clinician and caregiver raters). No development, adaptive or quality-of-life instrument.
- **Result (DATO, Figure 2, Table text).** Median reduction 26.5 points (higher dose), 14.2 (lower dose) and 7.6 on placebo; placebo-adjusted minus 19.9 points (interval minus 31.0 to minus 8.7, P 0.001); lower dose minus 10.5 (interval minus 25.0 to 4.0, P 0.09), **an interval that includes zero and is not a null**. Responders 22 of 87 (25 percent) against 9 of 87 (10 percent); 25-percent responders 33 percent on placebo. Global impression: clinically meaningful 26 against 6 percent (investigators), 34 against 5 percent (caregivers); any improvement 49 against 34 percent (P 0.06) and 61 against 37 percent.
- **Where it needs care (DATO).** Baseline median drop seizures 83 and 85 in the drug groups against 53 on placebo; the generalised tonic-clonic subgroup (46 percent of patients) fell 45.7 percent against a 3.7 percent rise on placebo, a nominal subgroup result; in atonic seizures the placebo reduction (about 28 percent) exceeded the lower-dose one (Figure 3). The authors' only limitation is short duration. Table 1 prints a median body-mass range that cannot contain its own median (a source typo; not used).
- **Replication (INFERENZA from the paper's own introduction and later papers).** The drug was repurposed (an old anorectic), approved first for Dravet syndrome on the strength of randomised trials the paper cites as reducing convulsive seizures by 54 to 65 percent, then confirmed here in a second syndrome with a smaller effect; the paper also cites an earlier 13-patient open pilot in LGS (53 percent median reduction in convulsive seizures), which is a different endpoint from the randomised drop-seizure count and cannot be compared with it. The primary Dravet trials are paywalled and were not read.
- **Card consequence.** The template for a confirmed repurposing effect: a frozen background, a prospective baseline, a count, an expert-adjudicated seizure definition, and a preset hierarchy. It also shows the effect size of a *successful* symptomatic repurposing in a refractory DEE: about 20 points over a placebo that itself moves 8 points.

### 42389070 - Janszky 2026, narrative review of fenfluramine in Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut syndromes - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-42389070-01` (complete; no figures or supplement exist) - manifest PASS, 16 locators. A secondary source; the primary trials were not available to this reader, so every number it carries is a rounded citation.

- It frames the drug as obtained through **repositioning**, with two randomised Dravet trials (effect early in titration, a dose-response pattern), one LGS trial of 263, open-label extensions and real-world cohorts as confirmation of durability.
- **The non-seizure claim and its basis (DATO).** Executive-function improvements come from **post hoc** analyses of a **caregiver-rated** inventory; the review says they are only partly explained by seizure reduction, that in LGS the correlation between fall-seizure change and score change is "negligible to weak", and that confirmation needs prospective, performance-based endpoints. Confounders it names: sedation, appetite and weight change, sleep, background polytherapy. **This is the nearest thing in the group to a symptomatic-versus-modifying separation, and it rests on a rating that has not been validated against performance.**
- **Internal tension (DATO).** The introduction says valvulopathy and pulmonary hypertension "have not been reported in the antiepileptic dose range"; the safety section says post-marketing experience has identified such cases. The review also contains an algorithm and dose ceilings; **none is carried here**. The authors declare language editing with a generative tool.
- The CDKL5 phase 3 is reported only at company-result level, which the review says must be read cautiously until peer review.

### 42334048 - Villanueva 2026, fenfluramine in 166 patients with developmental and epileptic encephalopathies (Spanish registry) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE as a paper; one registry row names a WWOX variant

Receipt `FTR-20261021-42334048-01` (complete; Figures 1-2, Tables 1-4 and the supplement including Figures S1-S2 read) - manifest PASS, 16 locators.

- **Design (DATO).** Retrospective, 18 hospitals, no control group, treatment begun after approval; Dravet and LGS plus "other DEEs" treated under compassionate use at physician discretion; 3-, 6- and 12-month chart visits; echocardiography every 6 months.
- **Result and what it carries (DATO).** Median total-seizure reduction 68.8 percent at 12 months; 12-month responders (at least 50 percent) 101 of 165 (61 percent) overall, 32 of 42 (76) in Dravet, 21 of 40 (53) in other DEEs (Table 3); retention 77 percent (Dravet about 0.93, LGS 0.74, other DEEs 0.68 on the supplementary curves). **Figure 1A prints 16 percent of all patients as worsening at 12 months** beside a 68.8 percent median reduction, which a median hides; the 50-percent bar is cumulative (it contains the 15 percent seizure-free). The text does not say how the 38 patients who stopped before 12 months enter the responder denominators.
- **Non-seizure outcomes (DATO, Figure 2).** Clinician-recorded 7-point global impressions by domain: cognition improved in 51 of 86 patients (59 percent), but **fewer than half of the 166 have a rating in any domain** (86, 88, 74, 67), and the improved segment includes about 34 points of "minimally improved"; in "other DEEs" about 15 percent had much worse behaviour. The authors accept that CGI is not formal testing.
- **The WWOX row (DATO, Table 1 footnote and Supplementary Table S7).** One patient with a WWOX variant (the variant is not stated) sits in the "other DEE" group (reading of the all-zero column corrected 2026-10-22: UNDETERMINED, see the 20261022c note); the supplement lists no seizure freedom, not a 50-percent responder, no worsening and no adverse event at 3, 6 or 12 months for that one patient. **n = 1, uncontrolled, variant and concomitant therapy unstated, and the authors state no conclusion can be drawn by gene.** This is a row in a table, not a finding. It is recorded for completeness of the WWOX-adjacent record and for a blind check that the all-zero column means "not a responder" and not "not evaluable".
- **Card consequence.** Real-world replication of a confirmed seizure effect cannot say anything about development; the mix of syndromes, the unselected "other DEE" group (22.5 percent stopped for adverse events, against 2.4 percent in Dravet) and the CGI denominators show how a pooled registry hides the genotype that matters to a card.

### 37045569 - van Ginkel 2023, aggregated N-of-1 trial nested in a parallel RCT (a bladder-pain syndrome protocol) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-37045569-01` (complete as to the protocol; no results exist) - manifest PASS, 16 locators. Read for design only.

- **Design (DATO).** Group A (40) placebo then drug then drug; groups B.1 and B.2 (20 each) drug, placebo, drug and drug, drug, placebo; 6-week blinded periods with 4-week wash-outs; the first period is the "standard RCT". **The same patients serve both analyses; the designs are nested, not independent.**
- **What it says about when an N-of-1 can be used (DATO).** "Limited to chronic non-curable diseases/symptoms" whose treatment must continue to suppress the symptom: **by the authors' own statement the design cannot test a persistent or disease-modifying effect**. Stated benefit: half the patients or fewer (28 against 58 for 80 percent power; 38 against 80 with drop-out), from an effect size taken from an unblinded comparison against another instillation and a placebo effect from a different trial.
- **Endpoint choices that weaken it (DATO).** Efficacy is declared if **any one of three** criteria is met (a 2-point pain change, a 2-point change in the most dominant symptom, or at least 5 on a 7-point global scale), and the 2-point threshold was fixed by a patient-panel interview, not an anchor method; the analysis and the success definition do not state multiplicity handling.
- **Card consequence.** An aggregated N-of-1 is a symptom-suppression design with a wash-out assumption; for WOREE it applies only to a symptomatic endpoint and cannot support a disease-modification statement. Its "any of three" success rule is the form of endpoint hierarchy an evaluation should refuse.

### 36538782 - Ratcliffe 2023, soticlestat phase 2a in complex regional pain syndrome - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-36538782-01` (complete; Figures 1-3 and supplementary Tables S1-S2 read) - manifest PASS, 18 locators.

- **Design (DATO).** Three UK sites, 24 adults randomised 2:1 (15 drug, 9 placebo), 15-week double-blind part then an optional 14-week open-label part; the primary is the change in the mean of a three-times-daily diary score, with a **7-day pre-randomisation baseline** (diary compliance above 90 percent); powered for at least 65 percent at an alpha of 0.10 to detect 2 points.
- **Result (DATO).** Change minus 0.75 on drug and minus 0.41 on placebo, difference minus 0.34 (interval minus 1.55 to 0.88, P 0.57): **the interval includes zero and effects up to about 1.5 points of benefit, and its lower end does not reach the 2-point difference the study was powered for; an underpowered result, not a demonstration of no effect.** Responders 4 of 15 and 2 of 9. Global impression (much or minimally improved) 7 of 15 (46.7) against 4 of 9 (44.4); 12 of 18 (66.7) at week 29 after everyone had received the drug, which the authors say cannot be separated from the placebo effect of being in a study. Baseline imbalance despite randomisation (11.3 against 6.7 years since onset; opioids 73 against 33 percent).
- **The marker (DATO, Figure 3).** Plasma 24-hydroxycholesterol fell by about 70 percent within 2 weeks (about 51 to 16 ng/mL on drug, placebo unchanged); the authors read it as adherence and as support for the marker, while the clinical endpoint was flat. **A third disease, the same pattern as the two soticlestat phase 3 DEE trials (about 80 percent).**
- **Smaller observations.** The drug and placebo lines in Figure 2 separate only after about week 17, when placebo participants have moved to open-label drug; every error bar spans zero. Supplementary Table S2 shows the placebo completers' physical-function mean rising more (35.83 to 38.51) than the drug completers' (34.65 to 36.38) while main Table 2 prints changes of 2.4 and 1.5 for drug and placebo; whether this is completers against all randomised is not stated, and the discrepancy is not resolved here. One participant had depression and suicidal ideation judged related, in the open-label part.
- **Card consequence.** A pharmacodynamic fall, a pre-dose baseline and an underpowered null interval do not make a trial informative; they show how a 24-participant proof of concept can be read both ways. Declare the power and what interval would count before the trial.

### 40162009 - Schulz 2025, cerliponase alfa in clinical practice against natural-history controls (a lysosomal enzyme deficiency with epilepsy) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE, and **not a repurposed drug** (an enzyme replacement therapy)

Receipt `FTR-20261021-40162009-01` (partial: supplementary Tables S1-S4 read, three supplementary figures not opened) - manifest PASS, 15 locators.

- **Design (DATO).** Registry analysis; treated patients (prospective) against untreated controls (some retrospective), matched 1:1 on baseline age within 12 months and **exact baseline motor-language score**; controls followed only for as long as their matched treated patient; a sensitivity analysis also matched on genotype. Outcome: a four-step ordinal motor-language scale scored by the treating physician every 6 months.
- **Result (DATO, Figure 1, Table 2).** Hazard ratio for an unreversed 2-point decline or score of 0: 0.08 (interval 0.02 to 0.28); for an unreversed score of 0: 0.07 (0.01 to 0.40); rate of decline 0.46 against 1.88 points per 48 weeks (difference 1.42, interval 0.74 to 2.10); 6 of 21 matched controls (29 percent) died in the follow-up and none of the treated. **The treated curve in Figure 1A is flat from month 25 on 6 to 1 patients at risk; the flat tail is censoring, not a measured step.**
- **Why the comparator held up (INFERENZA).** The untreated course is fast and hard-endpointed (the control curve for an unreversed score of 0 reaches zero probability of no decline by month 30, Figure 1B), the scale is disease-specific and the same in treated and untreated, the controls were available as a registry cohort, and the effect (1.42) is large against the control scatter (SD 1.45). It also agrees with the pivotal-trial effect (1.75; hazard ratios 0.14 and 0.02), which is the reason the real-world comparison is credible.
- **Where it can still mislead (DATO).** The treated group had later onset (3.4 against 2.9 years, P 0.02 in the full cohort) and fewer motor-first presentations; sex differed in the matched groups; controls had shorter follow-up; five children with scores of 2 or less were matched and two showed no benefit; the authors list residual confounding and no contemporaneous control as limitations.

### 41250984 - Bidwell 2026, KCNQ2 developmental and epileptic encephalopathy: variant function against non-seizure severity - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-41250984-01` (partial: supporting information not read; Figures 1-2 and Table 1 read) - manifest PASS, 14 locators.

- **Design (DATO).** Parent-reported natural-history survey (cross-sectional, 48 individuals, 38 variants of which 36 missense) joined to **blind** automated voltage-clamp classification of every variant as severe loss, loss, wild-type-like or gain of function.
- **Result (DATO).** No single non-seizure feature (mobility, communication, eating, hand use, vision, scoliosis) was associated with functional class; a composite severity score was modestly higher in severe loss than in loss (P 0.02, rho 0.32); the lower spasm prevalence in dominant-negative variants (2 of 19 against 7 of 18, P 0.04) is called a possible chance finding given the number of tests. Severe mobility and communication impairment occurred in 56 and 64 percent regardless of class. Retigabine response was reported in 3 of 4 and the responders' variants responded in vitro; carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine and phenytoin were reported to give significant or complete seizure reduction in 7 of 13, 13 of 17 and 10 of 13 treated (parent-reported, no control, timing unknown).
- **Why it matters.** The same missense channelopathy where in-vitro function sorts variants cannot predict the non-seizure phenotype and the authors cite a **dissociation between ongoing seizures and non-seizure severity**; the timing question (does early seizure control change development) is explicitly unanswerable here. A heterologous-cell class can miss trafficking defects. **For a missense WWOX lesion this argues against assuming that a drug's effect on a measured molecular class will carry to development.** Internal count check: the abstract gives 3 gain-of-function participants (47 of 48 in total); Table 1 and the Results give 4.

### 42286026 - Xiong 2026, gabapentin in four patients with KCNQ2 developmental and epileptic encephalopathy - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-42286026-01` (partial: the supplementary document was not read; Figures 1-3 opened as embedded rasters) - manifest PASS, 14 locators. No dose or exposure number is carried.

- **Design (DATO).** Four patients with four variants (three in the same segment), open-label add-on, no control; seizure freedom by video-EEG before and after; cognition by **parent report without an instrument**; patch-clamp of each variant in cells.
- **What the data show (DATO).** Three patients became seizure-free (after 1 year 5 months in one, within 3 months in another) and one did not; **three of the four had earlier seizure-free periods on a sodium-channel blocker that ended in relapse after about 6 to 7 months**, so a remission of that length can occur without the studied drug. Parent-reported cognitive gains were described in all four, **including the non-responder**; motor improvement was absent in the three seizure-free children; the authors say the cognitive improvements were similar across patients.
- **Why the genotype-response story is weak (DATO and INFERENZA).** The authors did not test the drug on the mutant channels, call their account of the one non-responder speculative and note other drug targets. In Figure 2b the S4 variant that the text calls mixed loss-and-gain keeps large currents only against the severe S6 variants: with its physiological partner subunit its curve lies **below** the wild-type curve at nearly every voltage (about 150 against 270 pA/pF at 0 mV). The single-epoch EEG panels (Figure 3) show persisting discharges after treatment in two patients and carry no counts.
- **Card consequence.** n = 4, one variant per class, uncontrolled, with relapsing-remitting baselines: it can generate a hypothesis and cannot support a class claim; the repurposed-drug-by-variant-class pairing needs the drug tested on the channel and a longer pre-drug baseline.

### 39093319 - Gras 2024, KCNT1-related epilepsy: antiseizure drugs, ketogenic diet, cannabidiol and quinidine (systematic review of case reports) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-39093319-01` (partial: two supplementary documents not read; Figures 1-2 opened) - manifest PASS, 15 locators.

- **Design (DATO).** 605 records to 43 studies and 197 patients, almost all case reports and series (two of the highest evidence level for quinidine: a systematic review and an N-of-1 trial; one randomised crossover at level 2). **No response threshold**: benefit was any reduction in frequency, duration or intensity, any quality-of-life gain, or "reported as beneficial" even without details.
- **Result (DATO).** Quinidine (a repurposed antiarrhythmic and channel pore blocker) was reported beneficial in 25 of 56 infants with migrating focal seizures (44.6 percent) and in none of 8 patients with sleep-related hypermotor epilepsy; QT prolongation occurred in 7 of 31 non-responders. **Persistence of benefit at 3 months was almost never documented and, where documented, very low** (authors). A regrouping of two protein domains gave 12 of 16 against 13 of 40 (P 0.007) after a first grouping gave 19 of 44 against 6 of 12 (P 0.75): a post hoc subgroup. In-vitro and clinical responses to quinidine disagreed for several of 14 tested variants.
- **The authors' own caution (DATO).** The natural history of a decline in seizures over time is not considered in most reports, so a drug started shortly before the decline is credited with it; expectation bias; cannabidiol and diet were always co-prescribed in the infants. They recommend N-of-1 designs and natural-history comparators.
- **Card consequence.** The missense gain-of-function precedent for a repurposed channel blocker: a response rate from unthresholded case reports that did not persist and did not follow the in-vitro prediction. A card should record the response definition, the persistence interval and whether a natural-history comparator existed.

### 39439575 - Berg 2024, SCN2A clinical trial readiness: communication instruments, floor effects, untreated change and test-retest - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-39439575-01` (partial: read from the PMC page text because the XML and bucket routes are restricted for this record; the five figures were not obtained and are captions only; eTable 1 not opened) - manifest PASS, 13 locators. The page was lawfully reachable; **no access control was circumvented**; the sibling gross-motor paper from the same cohort returned a bot challenge and was not read.

- **Design (DATO).** Foundation-recruited convenience sample of 65 (55 completed all or almost all assessments), parent-reported instruments and a trained-rater Vineland interview at baseline, 6 and 12 months; a second administration about 2 weeks later in a subset for test-retest.
- **Result (DATO).** Most communication standardized scores sat at the floor, and floor effects grow with age (expressive 25 percent at 12 to 23 months, 81 to 96 percent after 2 years). **Untreated change over about a year:** the standardized composite fell 2.8 points a year (P 0.002); growth scale values did not change substantially (receptive minus 1.5, interval minus 4.32 to 1.33; expressive plus 0.3, interval minus 1.11 to 1.72); all test-retest intraclass correlations exceeded 0.90; growth scale values correlated at 0.7 to 0.9 with the raw-score instruments and mostly below 0.5 with the standardized scores.
- **How far it carries (DATO and INFERENZA).** It measures **cohort-mean** untreated change and measurement reliability, not per-patient scatter; the authors state that sensitivity to within-patient change is inferred from cross-sectional discrimination, not demonstrated, and that growth scale values lack a normative reference and regulatory acceptance. The convenience sample may skew severe. **This is the first measurement in the group of untreated drift against which a within-patient change could be read; it is a different disease and a parent-reported cohort.**

## The five questions - what the new rows add

1. **Symptomatic benefit versus disease modification.** Still none of the interventional studies shows modification. The only confirmed repurposing effect (35499850) is a seizure count with no development endpoint; its non-seizure claims are post hoc and caregiver-rated (42389070); the KCNQ2 survey (41250984) shows seizures and development dissociating. The aggregated N-of-1 protocol (37045569) says in terms that the design cannot test a persistent effect.
2. **What the non-replications and the confirmation together say about scoring a candidate.** A confirmed effect looks like: prospective baseline, frozen background, an adjudicated count, an effect of about 20 points over a placebo that itself moves 8, and replication in a second syndrome with a smaller effect. A candidate backed by a marker move (24HC, three diseases), a global impression at any-improvement, or an unthresholded case-report response rate has none of these.
3. **External control and natural history.** One framework worked (40162009), under named conditions; for WOREE none of the conditions is shown. A cohort measurement of untreated drift exists for a sister disease (39439575) and is small in the mean.
4. **N-of-1 and aggregated N-of-1.** Adds the protocol that states its own limits (37045569) and a four-patient add-on series that illustrates what an uncontrolled N-of-1 cannot say (42286026).
5. **Instruments.** Adds the cohort evidence that growth scale values and raw scores are stable untreated and reliable on retest in a profoundly impaired monogenic DEE (39439575) against the standardized score that falls with age; responsiveness to treatment remains **not demonstrated**.
