## Self-evaluation

**What the process got right.** The FIND-X rule inherited from the previous batch was treated as binding
rather than advisory, and it is the only reason this batch has content: **four of the nineteen candidates
carried a negative about their own source, and all four were wrong or over-scoped** — three corrected at
source, one reversed outright. The rule works because of its mechanism, not its wording: the auditor is told
which surfaces exist and instructed to FIND the thing said to be absent, so the search ends at the
enumeration of surfaces rather than at the reader's first look. Two further habits paid in the same hour:
Step 0 ran **before** dispatch and recovered four artefacts by digest that a peer worktree held, and every
quantifier was asked which set it ranges over — which is what turned *«all terminated mice reviewed»* into
*«an unsized subset of roughly a quarter»* and *«no antibody assay anywhere»* into a located table.

🔴 **What went wrong, and the upgrade.** The nineteen candidates arrived in **three different authoring
formats** — a fenced JSON op array, `old`/`new` bullet pairs under an `### Op N` heading, and paired
unlabelled fences under a `### N.M` heading — and nothing in the repository parses all three. I wrote a
parser; it took three iterations, and the first two silently produced **zero ops** for two candidates and an
op with **no record id** for a third. A silent zero is the dangerous failure: a batch that reads
`0 ops` for a candidate propagates nothing and can still report success. The byte-level editor cannot
catch it, because an op that was never built is not an op that was refused.

**The upgrade, stated for the next batch and small enough to be real:** *an integrator must reconcile the op
count it extracted against the candidate's own declaration before propagating*. Every candidate in this wave
states its op count in prose (*«two `replace-within` ops»*, *«6 record-scoped ops»*), and three of them
disagreed with my first parse. The cheap form is a parser that prints, per candidate, `ops_parsed` beside the
number its text declares, and refuses to proceed on a mismatch — the same shape as `growth_anchors`'
cardinality check, applied to the extraction step instead of the registry. Until that exists as a script,
the discipline is the line this batch had to learn: **a candidate from which you extracted no ops is a
parse failure until you have proved it is a no-ops candidate** — exactly one candidate in this wave really
was, and it says so in its own title.

🔵 **A second finding, about who checks the integrator.** Two of this batch's defects were caught not by an
audit but by a **gate on the integrator's own output**: LINT refused an annotated `Status` value, and the
link-target suite refused a wikilink fragment. Both were my text, written after every audit had passed, and
neither could have been caught by any auditor — they are not claims about a source. The auditing discipline
covers what a reading says; the gates cover what the integrator writes. **Both halves were needed in this
batch, and the lesson is that an integrator's prose is not exempt from measurement just because it was
written to record somebody else's measurement.**
