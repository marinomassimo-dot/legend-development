# Self-evaluation — scientist-c, repair of group R3 after the blind audit, 2026-10-22

Verdict: the repair is complete at source for all 51 non-supported triples of the audit (46 overshoot, 4 undershoot, 1 unverifiable) over six manifests, each declared with `contradicts_locator` and the audit object; `locator_contradiction_audit.py --working-tree --fail-on-undeclared` reports no undeclared revision and all six manifests re-verify PASS. **The repairs are unaudited**: the author of a repair does not certify it, and the 22 substantive corrections especially need a second reader.

What was wrong, in numbers: 46 of 129 triples overshot (36 percent), 29 of them only because the quote was narrower than the proposition (span), 17 because the source says something different from the proposition. Four undershot (the source says more) and one could not be established.

**The common shape, and whether the rule was applied.** Two shapes, not one.
1. *Substantive (17 overshoot plus 4 undershoot plus 1 unverifiable): a proposition built from one surface while another surface of the same paper answers it differently* — the text against the supplementary table (PK proportionality, 40665325), the text against the figure panel (Myc lanes, 38147624; normal-cell curves; gliosis; Pdk1 mRNA ratio), the legend against the supplement (the 38147624 schedule, a conflict that did not exist), the Results sentence against the Fig 1A timeline (42310739 age range), the text against itself (Rictor survival, 35945201). This is dismissal-ledger row **D-22** ("two surfaces that name the same fact agree") in its within-paper form; no new row is minted. **It was not applied, and it was catchable**: the rule is written, the surfaces were on disk, and in most cases the contradicting surface was one the reader had already opened for another entry. What was missing is a step that makes the cross-read a reflex at the moment a proposition contains a quantity or an "only / none / not" word: ask which other surface carries that quantity.
2. *Span-only (29): the quote is one sentence, the proposition a paragraph.* D-22 does not cover this; nothing in the verifier does either, because `deepdive_manifest.py` checks that the quote exists in the source, never that the quote carries what the proposition asserts. Not catchable by the verifier; **catchable by a mechanical check**, measured here on the pre-repair manifests: a numeral in the proposition that is absent from the snippet flags 25 of 45 text-surface entries the audit faulted and 8 of 38 entries it passed (recall 56 percent, 24 percent false flags among flagged-good). A proposition containing a number the quote lacks is the cheapest tell.

Micro-upgrade (one, proportional, not built here): a check in the manifest verifier, warning (not blocking) when a text-surface proposition carries a numeral, a percentage or a p value absent from its quote; measured recall 56 percent at the rates above. Candidate for Harness Engineering. Until then the reader's rule: **write the proposition from the quote, not the quote for the proposition**, and where the proposition needs a neighbouring sentence, quote the contiguous passage.

Two consequences recorded rather than hidden: the earlier note's "sub-proportional" correction of the PK statement was itself an overreach from one comparison (21 versus 42 mg) and is withdrawn; and a flagged "schedule conflict" in 38147624 was the reader's arithmetic error, withdrawn.
