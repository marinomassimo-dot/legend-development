## What changed understanding

PMID 30370248 is the appropriate 2018 animal-model baseline for Steinberg 2021, but not a source of
new data. It establishes how strongly strain, cell of origin, tissue and cooperating lesions alter
the interpretation of “WWOX loss”. Steinberg's cell-targeted CNS deletions and neuronal restoration
are later additions, not content already present in this baseline.

The most consequential finding was a citation failure. Tanna 2018 says Mallaret et al. showed
spontaneous and audiogenic seizures in a Wwox-KO mouse, but its reference 53 is a human SCAR12
paper. The rat seizure experiment is reference 104. The visual summary obscures the distinction by
pooling mouse and rat into “Rodents”. This reproduces the mouse-epilepsy attribution already
rejected by the completed primary chain.

PMID 21318118 adds historical framing. In 2011 conditional Wwox models were still future-facing;
global-null, heterozygous challenge and hypomorphic models dominated. It explicitly says the small
global-null mice did not show abnormal behaviour or impaired motor skills. Its only figure concerns
FHIT cooperation and supplies no WWOX visual evidence.

## Pattern audit

I did not search either review for “the comparison that is needed was not drawn”. It did not jump
out in either paper. In 21318118, calling an explicitly proposed future compound-mouse experiment a
missing comparison would manufacture the pattern. In 30370248, what jumped out was instead the
mouse-seizure sentence because it collided with a known primary baseline; the numbered-reference
audit then exposed the human/rat misattribution. On this run the missing-comparison pattern was not
observed, while citation hardening was observed without a targeted search.

were unavailable. PyMuPDF rendered only after the second page DPI was changed from 344.8 to an

locator surface. The group fields were corrected to allowed evidence classes; reference-list
quotes remain anchored explicitly to numbered references in the XML but use the validator's
`body` surface vocabulary. The next strict run passed for both papers.
