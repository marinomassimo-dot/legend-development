## Self-evaluation

**What the process got right.** Step 0 ran **before** the audits and caught something no previous batch had
to face: the scope's artefacts measured **0 of 143 present**, because this worktree has no `files/` — which
is byte-identical in appearance to a real artefact loss. Measuring first, and then fixing the cause rather
than the symptom, is why thirteen audits ran with **zero** `UNVERIFIABLE_SURFACE` verdicts. And the choice
to audit **all thirteen** candidates rather than the two that owed it paid for itself seven times over:
three load-bearing sentences were **false** and four counts were **wrong**, and every one of them would
have passed every byte-level gate in this repository — because a record-scoped editor can prove that
nothing else moved and cannot know whether what moved is true.

🔴 **What went wrong, and the upgrade.** Three of the thirteen candidates asserted a **negative about their
own source** — *«published nowhere»*, *«quantified by no panel»*, *«no graded per-arm incidence»* — and all
three were false; a fourth asserted a **ranking** that inverts on the paper's own fuller panel. The pattern
is sharper than the previous batch's *«a self-check is a claim like any other»*: **a negative about a source
is the most expensive claim a reading can make and the cheapest one to write**, because the reader stops
looking the moment the absence seems established, and a later reader inherits a licence to stop looking
too. **The upgrade, applied here and stated for the next batch: a candidate sentence of the form «the source
does not print X» is audited by a sub-agent whose instruction is to FIND X — across every deposited surface,
not only the one the reading used — and the auditor is told which surfaces exist before being asked whether
X is there.** Two of the three falsifications in this batch came from exactly that instruction (*«does any
sheet in ANY supplementary table carry this value?»*, *«does any panel quantify abundance?»*); the third
came from asking an auditor to rank a quantity the candidate had ranked. A negative read only on the surface
where the reading already looked is not a measured absence — it is an unfinished search.

🔵 **A second finding, about scope words.** Four of the nine adverse verdicts were not wrong facts but wrong
**scope words**: *«only»* in one of two studies, *«animals»* for one animal, *«every dose»* for a pooled
endpoint, *«all groups»* for three of seven. Each was the candidate's own summarising word, not the
source's, and each would have survived any check that compares a quote to its anchor — because the quote
was right. **Where a candidate's sentence carries a universal or an exclusive (`only`, `every`, `all`,
`none`, `no`), the integrator's job is to ask which set it quantifies over and whether the source names
that set.**
