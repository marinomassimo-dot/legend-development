# Drug-repurposing batch: study selection for up to four Scientists (WOREE / WWOX-DEE)

Date: 2026-10-21. Author: read-only study selector. No repository file was edited, staged or committed; the only file written is this one. Nothing here is medical advice; this is a reading list, not evidence. Class level only (WWOX-DEE genotype class).

## Method

1. Repository context read first: `disease-models/wwox/research/drug_repurposing/README.md` and `reconciliation_20261020.md` (headings, P5 and P3 status), `repurposing_known_roads_20261020_scientist-a.md` (sections 0 and 2.4), `repurposing_early_benefit_20261020_scientist-b.md` (section 0 and headings), the three recovered Q230P dossiers under `vps_recovery_20260925/residue_q230p_20260922/` (premise sections), `discovery_ledger_current.md` DL-MECH-170, queue entries FT-113, FT-115 and FT-123, and the Method and Headline sections of `metabolic_batch_selection_20261019.md` as template.
2. Discovery: Europe PMC REST (title/abstract field queries per group, 2018 onward by default, open-access restricted for the candidate pass and `OPEN_ACCESS:n` for the BLOCKED pass) plus PubMed E-utilities. About 50 query formulations were run across the four groups; several hundred records were screened by title and 86 distinct PMIDs were carried to verification. The PubMed MCP search was tried once and discarded: its OR-translation was too loose.
3. Identity (test 0): every PMID in every table below was re-fetched from NCBI esummary on 2026-10-21 and the title column is the PubMed title; first author and year come from the same record. No mismatch was found against the Europe PMC record for the same PMID.
4. Test 1 (substantive abstract): the abstract (its results and conclusion part, at least the last 400 to 700 characters) was read for every selected record. Records whose abstract was only a protocol or a review are labelled so in the question column.
5. Test 2 (lawful body): for each selected record Europe PMC `core` was queried (PMCID, isOpenAccess, licence) and `fullTextXML` was fetched for the PMCID. "OA XML" = 200 and a full article body. A record with a PMCID whose XML is refused is labelled "PMC page/PDF only" and is not treated as a bot-challenged item. A record with no PMCID and isOpenAccess N goes to BLOCKED. Three DOI landings (Elsevier) were tried and returned publisher redirects, not bodies; they are BLOCKED with the route stated as unverified. Bodies were not opened or read by the selector.
6. Test 3: the question each paper could change is in one line in the table.
7. Dedup (mandatory): for every candidate `framework/scripts/registry_records.py get --pmid <PMID> --hops 0` (never the two large registries whole), `framework/scripts/fulltext_receipts.py status --pmid`, a scan of `disease-models/wwox/research/receipt_staging/*/` and `framework/scripts/owed_sections.py --pmid`. A further grep of each PMID across `analysis/`, `research/` and `hypotheses/` (excluding the queue, surface census and earlier selection files) caught mentions the registry call does not. Outcome: of the 86 PMIDs verified, only five of the selected or considered candidates had any trace (see "Dedup findings"); nothing selected has a complete receipt.
8. Tier: 1 = decisive primary or directly tests a stated gap; 2 = strong comparator or mechanism; 3 = methods or review carrying one testable lesson. Registry status vocabulary: "new" = no registry record, no receipt, nothing staged; "queued" = named in the full-text queue without a receipt; "HELD-partial" = a partial receipt exists and the owed sections are named.
9. Every non-WWOX paper below is **NOT WWOX EVIDENCE**. It is a precedent, a mechanism or a method that could change a repurposing decision. Absence from the registry is a statement about this repository, not about the literature, and no novelty is claimed.

## Headline findings for the Orchestrator

- The WWOX-direct literature with a lawful body is exhausted, as briefed: the dated queries in group R2 return the same single record as before (the Kilic cohort, complete receipt). The four groups below are therefore transferable literature. Group sizes: R1 16, R2 16, R3 16, R4 16 selected; 2 HELD-partial rows in R1, one informational row in R4.
- **R1 carries two items the queue already flagged and never paid.** PMID 38378758 (Parkin variant atlas) carries a published negative that the TX-003 prior may not use until it has a receipt, and the queue says it is already retrievable and "should not wait"; PMID 29785012 (the abundance method) is listed in the same entry as "not read". Both are in the R1 table.
- **R1 also contains a failed-replication paper and a limiting-assay paper**, not only supportive precedents: a zebrafish study that argues against repurposing 4-phenylbutyrate for STXBP1 disorders (42268630) and an inter-assay discrepancy study of the chaperone "amenability" call (32023956). Selecting only supportive precedents would bias the card.
- **R2 contains two negatives that constrain DL-MECH-170**: a new Cav3 inhibitor that did not reduce Dravet seizures and increased spike-wave discharges in an absence model (37082241), and the standing challenge to the burst-firing mechanism (30955423). It also contains a paper linking T-type block to AKT and GSK3B phosphorylation in neural progenitors (29463073), which touches a WWOX partner already in the repository.
- **R3: the compound behind the organoid intervention is already identified in the repository as BTX-A51 (inference, not fact)** and its class (CK1 alpha plus CDK7/9) has dedicated analysis files; the human phase-1 paper (40665325) is queued at abstract level only. The group therefore avoids repeating that deconvolution and instead selects what the repository lacks: attribution in a Csnk1a1 system (38147624), a CSNK1A1 infantile-spasms paper that makes CK1 alpha loss itself a seizure signal (40156289), Cdk7 loss in cortical neurons (42154236), and Wnt activation as anti-epileptogenic (42401046).
- **R4**: the strongest design papers are the EPISTOP pair (33180985, 39625912), the regulatory vocabulary paper (34270884), and two phase-3 non-replications of soticlestat (41784472, 41891667). The one ganaxolone paper selected (38959712) is the only one in the group measuring non-seizure outcomes in a randomised DEE trial.
- Searches for primary clinical trials of fenfluramine, ganaxolone and cannabidiol in DEE returned mostly paywalled records; these are in R4 BLOCKED. The selected R4 set is mostly design, endpoints and trial-readiness, which is what the brief says tier 1 should be.
- A group of 2026 phenylbutyrate-in-SLC6A1 clinical reports (parent-reported benefit, a cohort, a repurposing protocol) is paywalled and sits in R1 BLOCKED. They are exactly the clinical side of the R1 mechanism papers; an operator PDF request for them would be worth more than most items in the table.

### R1 five questions

1. Can a Q230P-class lesion be separated into "made normally then degraded" (inhibitor-sensitive recovery, 29785012 / 38740822 / 33914734) versus "never made" (polysomal or turnover read-outs, 31402619 / 29449567) with one experiment, and which of the selected papers has the cleanest negative control?
2. When a chaperone or proteostasis regulator raises protein, how many of the selected studies also measure function (current, uptake, seizures, white matter) and how many stop at quantity? Is there a precedent where the two came apart (32023956, 34649609, 38378758)?
3. Is 4-phenylbutyrate a pharmacochaperone, an HDAC inhibitor or neither (42157447 versus 42268630), and does the answer depend on the target protein being a transporter, receptor or enzyme? What does that imply for an enzyme-class WWOX lesion?
4. What fraction of pathogenic missense variants are stability lesions, and does stabilisation overshoot into dysfunction (38396641, 33914734)? Is a Q230P-class stability lesion the default expectation or a hypothesis to test first?
5. Which degradation-pathway modulators have been shown to change a mutant protein level in a mouse with a seizure or pathology endpoint (38731820, 41178115, 31402619), and what exposure window did they use?

### R2 five questions

1. Is a genotype-restricted ethosuximide effect plausible, given that ethosuximide suppressed an HCN1 DEE model but a different Cav3 inhibitor failed in Dravet and aggravated spike-wave discharges (36300716, 37082241)? What does a within-subject design with a wild-type arm look like (42274296)?
2. Does ethosuximide act through GIRK channels or the reticular thalamic nucleus in addition to channel block (37275776, 40827489, 30955423), and which of these could be tested in a Wwox-null cheaply?
3. Does a non-channel neurodevelopmental gene knockout raise T-type current (40834072), and is there any developmental-stage dependence that would make the Wwox-null infant brain a different target from the adult (40068874, 29463073)?
4. If T-type block lowers AKT and GSK3B phosphorylation in neural progenitors (29463073), is there a safety or developmental-toxicity signal that a repurposing card must carry for an infant disease?
5. What does a T-type effect on seizure not tell us: do selected papers show suppression of seizures with no effect on comorbidity (42536722, 41137852), i.e. a symptomatic result that cannot be counted as disease modification?

### R3 five questions

1. What is known about the organ toxicities, exposure and pharmacodynamic markers of the compound class behind the only intervention applied to a WWOX-deficient human neural model (40665325), and does Csnk1a1 haploinsufficiency or CDK7 loss reveal a neurodevelopmental or seizure cost (40156289, 42154236, 38147624)?
2. Does the direction of Wnt modulation matter for hyperexcitability: Wnt activation reduced pathological granule-cell remodelling (42401046) while the compound suppresses Wnt; is there a measured window in which suppression is safe?
3. Which approved or class-adjacent drugs have measured myelin effects in a genetic neurodevelopmental model (35945201, 41980417, 42310739, 40911604), and which of those measured hyperexcitability at the same time?
4. Which classes have a documented harm in developing myelin or microglia (35372341, 37296418, 39762658) that a repurposing card must record as a counter-indication?
5. What is the infant and paediatric safety record of the mTOR, GSK3 and CDK-adjacent classes (36615165, 42217144, 40871085), and what monitoring does it imply?

### R4 five questions

1. Which of the selected trials separated a symptomatic effect from a change in disease course, and with what design (pre-symptomatic start in 33180985 and 39625912; non-seizure outcomes in 38959712; neuropsychological outcome in 39762914)?
2. What did the two soticlestat non-replications (41784472, 41891667, with 35841234) change about how a repurposed or mechanism-matched candidate should be scored before a phase 3?
3. Which external-control or natural-history frameworks (38015929, 42446932, 42481851) are mature enough to serve as comparator for a rare WWOX-DEE genotype, and what minimum follow-up do they imply?
4. How were n-of-1 and aggregated n-of-1 designs built for repurposed drugs (41999155, 38144875, 37537625) and which of them published a pre-specified primary outcome?
5. Which clinical-outcome assessments for cognition and behaviour in DEE are responsive (42246937), and how many of the trials above actually used them?
