## 4 · The one datum with reach beyond oncology — flagged, not promoted

**An exon 6-8-skipped WWOX transcript is detectable in 9 of 13 non-malignant ovarian samples** (L35), by a late-cycle assay the authors themselves call unquantifiable (L23). Read together with the other two papers of this lot — a clean Δ6-8 in a benign oral lesion (`CC-20260914-18061530-01`) and Δ6-8-bearing tumours with full-strength protein (`CC-20260914-16152610-01`) — this says that **aberrant exon-skipped WWOX transcripts occur in non-malignant adult human tissue at appreciable frequency**.

🔴 **Why this is a flag and not a delta.** It is adjacent to `CLAIM 018` (the exon 6 splice-site allele `c.517-2A>G` causes exon 6 skipping, `consolidated baseline`) and to `CLAIM 033`'s operational corollary (RT-qPCR on the exon 8→9 junction in variant-carrying cells as candidate ACMG PS3 evidence). **It weakens neither**, and this candidate proposes no change to either:

- `CLAIM 018` rests on a canonical acceptor-site variant with human data; **exon 6 skipping caused by a splice-site allele is not the same object** as a low-abundance Δ6-8 transcript in wild-type tissue.
- `CLAIM 033`'s corollary is a **quantitative** junction assay, not qualitative detection of an exon-skipped species.
- What the observation does bear on is **how a qualitative RT-PCR finding would be interpreted**: detecting an aberrant WWOX transcript in a patient sample is not by itself evidence of an allele effect unless the normal-tissue background rate is known. These three papers give only a crude background (69% of 13 ovaries, one benign oral lesion), by non-quantitative nested or late-cycle assays. `INFERENZA`, reader's, routed to the orchestrator.
