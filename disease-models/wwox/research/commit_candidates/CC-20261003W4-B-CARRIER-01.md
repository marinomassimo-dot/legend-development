## Why — the finding, and why it is new

The wave-4 selection record states that the group's carrier arm *"is built from human heterozygote data
instead"* because the murine heterozygote literature is receipt-complete. **That premise is wrong in a
way that matters**: the single most important myelin paper in this corpus, PMID 33914858, already
contains a murine heterozygote arm, and no LEGEND record mentions it.

In acute neocortical slices at P13–P17, spontaneous bursting occurs in **0 % of control slices (0/11,
seven animals), 17 % of Syn1-Cre heterozygote slices (4/23, fourteen animals) and 86 % of homozygous
conditional slices (36/42, twenty-four animals)**. One *Wwox* allele is therefore not electrophysiologically
silent in this model, on this measure, at this age.

The bound is in the same figure, and it is strict: **no statistical test of the heterozygote against the
control is reported on any measure**; the significant spectral contrast is always the homozygote; the
figure legend states the same experiment with different denominators (~20 % / ~84 %, counting animals in
which bursting occurred rather than animals assayed); and **no myelin, imaging or behavioural endpoint is
reported for the heterozygote at all**.

The human side of the same question, read in this wave, supplies a matching **negative**: in 204 people
selected for low HDL-C, the four WWOX "occurrences" resolve to **three distinct heterozygous missense
alleles**, one of them ClinVar-**Benign** and common (gnomAD 7.5 × 10⁻³); WWOX does not reach
significance in the burden test; no lipid value is reported for any WWOX carrier; and there is no
normal-HDL-C comparison cohort at all. That record must never be cited as four carriers with a WWOX-attributable
biochemical abnormality.
