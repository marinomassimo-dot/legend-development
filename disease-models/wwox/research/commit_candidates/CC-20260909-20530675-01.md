## 3 · CORRECTION 2 — a defect with no lawful repair, referred upward

`FTR-20260814-20530675-01` carries `study_id.doi = 10.1038/onc.2010.222`. **This is not repairable
under `fulltext_read_receipt.md` as written**, and appending the new receipt with the *correct* DOI was
**refused by the ledger writer**:

```
ERROR: line 133: conflicting identifiers for the same study
```

`validate_ledger_sequence` flags a contradiction when two receipts on one PMID both declare a DOI and
the DOIs differ. **The ledger therefore refuses the correct identifier because an incorrect one was
persisted first.** Three routes, all closed:

1. **Match the wrong DOI** — refused. Writing a known falsehood into new canonical state to satisfy a
   consistency check is the failure the check exists to prevent, inverted.
2. **`reread_reason: receipt_correction`** — impossible by construction. The protocol requires a
   correction to *preserve study identity*; study identity is the field that is wrong.
3. **`record_kind: receipt_invalidation`** — inapplicable. That route is for a receipt whose *reading
   evidence belongs to a different study*. This reading genuinely is of this paper, and the prior
   partial reading was legitimate work; invalidating it would destroy a real reading to fix a metadata
   field.

**Taken:** `FTR-20260909-20530675-01` omits `doi` from `study_id` (the checker requires both sides
present), and records the correct value in `evidence_basis`. **Referred to the operator / harness as an
open protocol gap:** there is no lawful route to correct a wrong DOI on a persisted receipt whose
reading is otherwise sound. A candidate fix — a `record_kind: identity_correction` that may change
`study_id` **only**, leaves every reading field frozen, and links through `prior_receipt` — is named
here and **not implemented**, because the receipt schema is not this actor's to change.

**Proposed addition to that note:**

> `PMID 20530675` (Kurek 2010, `FTR-20260909-20530675-01`) is the source of the strongest
> pro-osteosarcoma number in this literature — *"100% of Wwox-deficient mice had developed OS by 18
> days-of-age"* — and **the paper's own Figure S1 legend reports 19 of 22 knockout mice with tumours
> (86%)**, with 13/19 bilateral against Supplemental Table 1's 15+7=22. The percentages nearly coincide
> (68.2% of 19 versus 68% of 22), which is why the discrepancy survives a reader checking percentages

> mice"* — supplies the arithmetic: 22 − 19 = 3, split 2 bilateral + 1 unilateral, so **two animals were
> scored bilateral from a single imaged femur**. The screening criterion (*irregular protrusions on the
> endosteal or periosteal sides of the cortex*) is applied to a knockout cortex that Figure S1 itself
> shows is thinner and more porous than wild type, and **no blinding is stated anywhere**. This
> **narrows** the conflict rather than widening it: the pro-OS extreme is 86%, not 100%.
