## 3 · Two corrections this reading makes to its own prior locators

Both are corrections of `scientist-b`'s 2026-08-11 work by `scientist-b`, made **after** measurement at original
resolution, never before it.

**3.1 · `entries[4]`, Figure 2 — the premise "the two traces are PARALLEL" is false.** Measured against the four
calibrated y-axis ticks, the GG−TX separations are **0.186, 0.107, 0.147, 0.123** — an ~80% range. GG falls ≈0.070
over 21 years while TX is flat (≈−0.007), so the traces **converge**. Independently corroborated: those four values
reproduce Table 2's published effect sizes **−0.18, −0.10, −0.15, −0.12** to two decimal places, which is what
validates the pixel calibration rather than my eye. **The conclusion the prior locator drew survives and is
strengthened** — the separation is largest at the first timepoint and never grows, so the longitudinal result is
persistence and not progression. What was wrong was the geometry, and "parallel" is the stronger claim a reader
would have carried forward.

**3.2 · `entries[6]`, Figure 1 — the clause "no sentence in the paper describes" is false.** The Methods describe
exactly the design that produces the isolated peak: LDSelect tag SNPs at an **r² threshold of 0.85**, so the plotted
markers are by construction mutually non-redundant and no dense LD shoulder can appear around **any** peak. The
pixel observations in `entries[6]` stand unchanged; the inference drawn from them was too strong.
