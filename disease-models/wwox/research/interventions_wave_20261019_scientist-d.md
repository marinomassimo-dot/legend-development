## 3. What each paper measures, supports and does not support

### Tier 1

**Yuskaitis 2022 (36044864).** Neuron-specific Depdc5 knockout mice (6-9 weeks), rat cortical neurons with shRNA knockdown, and a 24 h fast with a PTZ 65 mg/kg seizure assay. **Measures:** in controls a 24 h fast cut the proportion with generalised seizures from about 56% to about 22% (legend n = 25 fed, 27 fasted, log-rank p = 0.0126) without changing latency or duration; cortical pS6 and pAKT fell. In the knockout, fasting did not protect (about 79% fed, about 75% fasted), although blood glucose fell from about 190 to about 100 mg/dL in wild-type (Supplementary Figure S1D) and ketones rose more than in controls (plasma beta-hydroxybutyrate about 1.4 mM controls, about 2 mM knockout). Only knockout animals died after PTZ (about 26% fed, about 40% fasted). Neurons sense leucine, arginine and glutamine through DEPDC5. **Does not support:** transfer to WWOX. It shows that fasting protection in this model is **genotype-dependent**: a lesion in nutrient sensing abolished it while glucose fell and ketones rose. One acute 24 h fast, one chemoconvulsant, young adult mice, water ad libitum; no calorie-graded, dextrose or fluid-volume arm exists. **Changes in the model:** a premise for any fasting rationale, not a claim: protection is not a property of fasting per se.

**Phillips-Farfan 2015 (25814935).** Twenty weanling male Wistar rats (five per cell, pooled to ten per diet for molecular data), 15% caloric restriction for 55-64 days without vitamin or mineral supplementation, amygdala kindling. **Measures:** a higher after-discharge threshold (about 160% of ad libitum) and shorter after-discharge duration on days 14-16 only; kindling rate unchanged; fasting-state (4 h) glucose, beta-hydroxybutyrate and insulin unchanged between diets; pPKB and pS6 lower in cortex and hippocampus, pAMPK higher in hippocampus; mTOR read after the kindling series. Weight gain was reduced even at 15%, which the authors say may limit use in children. **Does not support:** an anti-epileptogenic effect (the authors say anticonvulsant only), an mTOR mechanism established as causal, or any statement on children. **Contrast with Yuskaitis:** a 24 h fast lowered glucose, a 15% restriction did not; both lowered cortical pS6.

**Salvati 2022 (35134125).** Two absence-epilepsy rodent models (141 WAG/Rij rats, 44 DBA/2J mice), video-ECoG. **Measures:** an 18 h fast (glucose about 114 to 75 mg/dL, beta-hydroxybutyrate about 1.1 to 3.3 mM) raised spike-wave count (about 22 to 35 per 4 h) without lengthening seizures; insulin 3 IU/kg (glucose about 112 to 46 mg/dL, ketones unchanged) raised it; intrathalamic 2-deoxyglucose doubled it; AMPK activation potentiated GABA-B currents in slices and raised seizure count in vivo; metformin 200 mg/kg gave convulsive status epilepticus and five of six WAG/Rij rats died (non-epileptic Wistar rats had no seizures). **Fasting therefore worsened absence seizures here and protected in the two papers above: direction is model-dependent.** **Defect found:** the main text gives P = 0.0078 for the WAG/Rij count and calls it nearly two-fold, while Table S1 gives 21.9 versus 34.9 (about 1.6-fold) with P = 0.0044 for WAG/Rij and 0.0078 for the DBA row. Declared as `text_contradicted_by_panel`; a blind auditor confirmed the mismatch. **Does not support:** an in vivo AMPK measurement (none; slice p-AMPK varied), a GABA-B-dependence of the in vivo fasting effect (no antagonist rescue), or relevance to a DEE without absence seizures. The discussion's note that GLUT1 variants account for about 10% of early-onset childhood absence epilepsies is a citation.

### Tier 2

**Hahn 2026 (42360174).** Prospective single-centre two-period crossover pilot in adults with drug-resistant epilepsy (36 enrolled, 22 analysed, mostly focal), 16:8 time-restricted eating with or without a 360 kcal MCT shake. **No calorie target:** intake in the eating window was unrestricted. **Measures:** no difference in seizure frequency (Friedman p = .183); responder analysis post hoc; Kaplan-Meier curves are defined by starting sequence (IF-MCT first versus IF first), not by condition, so sequence and treatment are mixed. **Defects:** the Discussion says seizure freedom occurred "only during IF-MCT", while Table 2 gives participant 3 as 0, 0, 0 in all three IF blocks; beta-hydroxybutyrate medians were about 75-90 micromol/L (a ketone level far below the 0.5-3.0 mmol/L the authors cite for a ketogenic state); dropout 39%; funder is the shake's manufacturer, with honoraria declared. **Does not support:** efficacy, a ketosis mechanism, or any child or genetic-epilepsy statement.

**Sun 2025 (40589985).** Rat pups given insulin 40 U/kg on postnatal days 2, 4, 6 (glucose at or below about 0.6-0.8 mmol/L at 4 h, ended with a 2 mL/kg intraperitoneal 50% glucose bolus), visual stimulation, MRI on a subset, PTZ score at P28. **Measures:** higher PTZ seizure score after repeated hypoglycaemia (4.1 versus 2.4) and lower with visual stimulation (3.0). **Defects:** the tables say n = 25 per group while the reported t statistics fit about 5-9 animals (blind-audit arithmetic; my first estimate of about 10 was corrected); the text puts 3-4 h glucose below the meter threshold of 0.06 mmol/L while Table 1 gives 0.6-0.8; a synaptophysin value of 0.235 against 0.024 and 0.021 looks like a decimal slip; pups with spontaneous seizures or death were excluded; the MRI figure is one slice per group. The result also ran against the authors' own hypothesis. **Does not support:** a safe or unsafe glucose range, or any treatment statement.

**Garg 2025 (40119223).** Narrative review of neonatal hypoglycaemia thresholds. **Records at class level:** no consensus across ten guidelines (thresholds from 36 to 50 mg/dL by age); follow-up cohorts link severe or recurrent hypoglycaemia (below about 36 mg/dL) to executive and visual-motor deficits at 4.5 years and glucose below 30 mg/dL to lower IQ at 7-11 years, with no impairment found at 2 years; a rodent statement that glucose infusion worsened neuronal death after insulin hypoglycaemia through superoxide; lactate and ketones "do not entirely meet" neuronal demand; dextrose gel 40% at 200-400 mg/kg and an intravenous 200 mg/kg bolus with 6-8 mg/kg/min are the dosing the review prints. **Defects:** a correction for a dosage error on page 5 was published (the version read is the one served on the acquisition date); Figure 3 heads the escalation step "mg/kg/hr" while the text says 8-10 mg/kg/min; the text puts the day-4 plasma plateau at 89 mg/dL while Figure 2A plots 82-83; the Summary offers 47 and 60 mg/dL as safe in the same paragraph that advises staying above 36. **These dose numbers are printed here because the review prints them; they are not a recommendation and must not be reused as one.**

**Sobrinho 2026 (42638834).** Retrospective Brazilian cohort of 40 patients with SLC2A1 variants, ketogenic therapy in 85%. **Measures:** a severity score the authors made and call not externally validated; diet-only patients had lower scores than patients on drugs (median 7 versus 13), a confounded comparison (reader's inference). **Defects:** 11 (31.4%) with seizure control exclusively on the diet in one paragraph and 21 (56.7%) relying exclusively on it in the next; a language score of 6 where the scale stops at 5; a genotype split summing to 110%. **Use here:** a disease-class comparator for brain glucose transport failure treated by glucose-sparing diet. **It does not make WOREE a glucose-supply disorder:** the repository's own WWOX/HIF1A readings show increased glucose uptake and GLUT1 in WWOX-deficient fibroblasts and a sign that changes with condition (see section 4, question 3).

**Dienel 2023 (36117414).** Review and hypothesis paper, not data. **Records:** in rats starved 24 h before bicuculline seizures glucose stayed low, glycogen was nearly depleted and did not recover; interictal brain glucose is normal; acute hypoglycaemia raised glycogen in healthy adults and repeated hypoglycaemia blunted that response; brain glycogen synthase-deficient mice were seizure-prone; auxiliary fuels (ketones, MCT, pyruvate) are proposed as an expectation. The text never mentions GSK3 or WWOX (string count 0), so the selection file's link to the GSK3B arm is not carried by this paper.

**Tao 2024 (38681648).** One 64-year-old woman with diabetes, confusion days after intravenous glucose for insulin-overdose hypoglycaemia about two months earlier, MRI changes read as Wernicke encephalopathy, recovery on intravenous thiamine 500 mg every 8 h. **Does not support:** the causal claim in the conclusion (no thiamine level, no transketolase, no follow-up imaging, the hypoglycaemic episode itself an unexamined alternative); the authors' own figure of 30-50 mg thiamine reserves lasting 4-6 weeks sits awkwardly beside "rapid" depletion. Adult, one case.

### Tier 3

**Funayama 2026 (42087252).** Retrospective single-centre cohort, 208 admissions of 98 adults with anorexia nervosa (mean BMI 12.2). **Measures:** parenteral dextrose calories (parenteral nutrition about 81% carbohydrate) were associated with lower nadir magnesium and larger percent falls in magnesium and calcium after Bonferroni adjustment; **phosphorus was not significant** (p 0.0702 nadir, 0.0212 percent fall, unadjusted). **Defects:** a ten-fold mismatch between Table 4 (-0.119) and Table 7 (-0.0119) for the same coefficient; only nine admissions in the magnesium parenteral subgroup; "parenteral dextrose" mixes intravenous fluid, peripheral and total parenteral nutrition; confounding by indication is admitted; the offered mechanism (hepatic first-pass) was not measured; the text never mentions thiamine (count 0).

**Abbas 2025 (39871710).** Retrospective ancillary cohort of 77 children (47 organic disease, 30 anorexia nervosa) under one continuous enteral refeeding protocol starting at 10 kcal/kg/day, with oral phosphorus and 3 days of oral thiamine in the protocol (Supplement S1). **Measures:** refeeding syndrome 10.4% (8 of 77) by the ASPEN definition, hypophosphataemia below 0.8 mmol/L 13.7% (2.5% symptomatic), peripheral oedema 6.7% between days 2 and 21, one hallucination episode with phosphate 0.42 mmol/L, hypoglycaemia at inclusion in 22% (38% of the anorexia group). **Defects:** the same paragraph gives six children with two abnormalities and none with three, which cannot yield eight with refeeding syndrome; "premature stop" is an exclusion criterion while 14 early interruptions are analysed. No comparator; effectiveness cannot be judged.

**Trico 2026 (42384016).** A 13C-BHB spectroscopy tracer study (5 with type 1 diabetes, 6 controls) and an open-label 4-week MCT-versus-isocaloric pilot (8 per arm) under insulin clamps held at 2.8 mmol/L with a variable 20% dextrose infusion. **Defects found by reading the panels:** the sentence "(2.8 mmol/L)" beside plasma BHB is the glucose target (BHB is about 0.65 mmol/L, Figure 1A); total TCA flux and absolute ketone oxidation were **lower** in type 1 diabetes (Figure 1J, K), so the higher relative contribution (17.3% versus 13.7%) reflects a falling denominator; memory accuracy shows a ceiling (six of eight MCT participants at 100%, one outlier at about 72%); the FFA-accuracy correlation is non-significant (r 0.37, p 0.16) although the text says both fuels correlate strongly; the brain-activity correlations (Figure 5) pool all three groups although the Discussion says they are in the MCT group. Plasma BHB stayed below 0.5 mmol/L throughout the diet arm. Epinephrine was modestly and non-significantly lower on MCT.

**Burst 2026 (41069084).** Retrospective single-centre cohort, 852 adults with plasma sodium at or below 125 mmol/L. **Measures:** confirmed hyponatraemic cerebral oedema in 4 (0.5%; 1.3% of those with severe symptoms) and osmotic demyelination in 11 (1.3%); in the 475-patient set with a better 24 h sodium estimate, overly rapid correction was a risk factor (odds ratio 4.2, p 0.004) but not in the full set; about 38% of severely symptomatic patients had other brain pathology on imaging. A 28-year-old with high-volume fluid ingestion and MDMA appears in both the oedema table and, by description, the demyelination table (not cross-referenced). **Defect:** the text says 1101 patients had sodium at or below 125 mmol/L; the flow diagram's 101,602 minus 100,549 gives 1,053. **Use here:** class-level documentation that water excess, low-solute diet and malnutrition appear in the oedema and demyelination case lists; adults, common etiologies.

**Ziaka 2025 (40944047).** Non-systematic review of resuscitation fluids in adult TBI (AI-assisted language editing disclosed). **Records:** acute fall in osmolality drives water into the brain; the claim that blood-brain-barrier disruption impairs the brain's protective water shift is stated as "one might argue"; positive daily fluid balance associated with higher ICU mortality in two large cohorts; relative hypotonicity of balanced crystalloids is raised with mortality signals in TBI subgroups, called "theoretical" in the conclusion. The text never mentions dextrose.

**Tesser 2025 (39861362).** Qualitative review of 22 studies (12 randomised) of parenteral macronutrients in very preterm infants. **Defects:** the text says 2.8% language delay in the enhanced-nutrition group while Table 1 gives 3.8% (2 of 52); "Tan 2008" in Table 1 and "Tan 2015" in the risk-of-bias figure; the Conclusions say adequate early nutrition is mandatory and that aggressive nutrition may not help in the same paragraph. Prints guideline glucose rates for preterm infants (4-8 mg/kg/min on day 1, 8-10 target). Its Table 1 sat outside the body sections and was missed by the first text rendering; it was read in a second extraction.

**Morris 2022 (36235546).** Pilot randomised trial (90 enrolled, 87 analysed) of enhanced versus standard parenteral nutrition in the first week (glucose infusion about 5.5 versus 4 mg/kg/min, both advanced to 12-14), neurodevelopment secondary, follow-up 33, 46 and 29 infants. **Measures:** parenteral energy 81.6 versus 65.2 kcal/kg/day with parenteral protein nearly equal (3.6 versus 3.5), so the contrast is glucose and lipid; P100 latency longer in the enhanced arm (145 versus 177 ms, p 0.01) and no longer different after adjusting for enteral calories (p 0.098); Bayley scores no different; the enhanced arm had lower enteral intake (17.5 versus 31.4 kcal/kg/day in the VEP group). **Defects:** the text's "10.13 ms per 10 kcal/kg/day" against an estimate of -1.13 per kcal/kg/day; the same p value (0.475) for antibiotic days as for Apgar score.

## 4. The five questions the selection posed

1. **Is the anti-seizure effect of fasting or restriction independent of mTOR and AMPK, and does Wwox loss alter either node?** Not answerable for WWOX. Non-WWOX: in one model the fasting effect needed intact DEPDC5-mTORC1 amino-acid sensing; in another, restriction lowered pS6 and pAKT and raised the after-discharge threshold with unchanged glucose and ketones; in a third, fasting raised absence seizures through a proposed AMPK-GABA-B route. No paper measures mTORC1, AMPK or pS6 in a WWOX-deficient neuron; the WWOX/mTOR autophagy paper (36621327) has no lawful body (blocked).
2. **Is hypoglycaemia in the null an energy failure or counter-regulatory failure, and what range is documented as safe for the developing brain?** Not answerable for the null mouse. The class-level literature has no consensus threshold; thresholds in newborn guidelines range from 36 to 50 mg/dL, outcome data are cohort-level and concentration-dependent. MCT did not change the epinephrine response in eight adults with type 1 diabetes; nothing here tests counter-regulation in a WWOX model.
3. **Is Glut1 deficiency a useful analogy for WOREE?** Weakly. Glut1-DS is transport failure treated by glucose-sparing diet; the repository's WWOX/HIF1A readings show higher glucose uptake and lactate in WWOX-deficient fibroblasts and a direction that changes with condition. The analogy rests on the shared diet response, not on a shared defect.
4. **Does a high fluid-volume, low-calorie regimen carry documented osmotic or oedema risk in encephalopathic children?** The documents are adult or non-encephalopathic: hyponatraemic oedema and demyelination with water excess and low-solute diets (Burst), water-into-brain physiology and fluid-balance associations after TBI (Ziaka), oedema in 6.7% during enteral refeeding of children (Abbas). Whether a WWOX-null brain is more or less exposed is unknown; the zebrafish embryo oedema phenotype is a knockdown finding (held row, not re-read).
5. **Is thiamine and refeeding risk a documented class-level concern with dextrose in undernourished or ketogenic-adapted children?** Documented as a case report (Tao), as electrolyte falls with parenteral dextrose in underweight adults (Funayama, which never mentions thiamine), and as a protocol that gave thiamine and phosphorus to undernourished children with refeeding syndrome in 10.4% (Abbas). None concerns ketogenic-adapted children.

## 6. WWOX-direct held rows reopened with a stated question

**Su 2026 (41677633), independent pass; scientist-c's landed re-read (`FTR-20261019-41677633-03`, complete) is the record and this section is a second reader's observations, to be compared with theirs, not a receipt.** The question was what exactly the nutrient stress is, in what genotype and with what readout. **The stress is removal of serum from the culture medium** (serum-free RPMI-1640 for MEFs; DMEM or RPMI for SCC-15 and HeLa); the paper does not manipulate glucose and describes no medium glucose. **Measures:** WWOX protein rose 2.2-3.5-fold in SCC-15 and HeLa under serum starvation and under 100 micromolar hydrogen peroxide; in SCC-15 the qPCR mRNA rose only to about 1.6-fold at 12 h and was below baseline (about 0.8) at 48 h while protein kept rising (the text concludes transcriptional activation). In primary E14.5 mouse fibroblasts, **Wwox-null cells were more resistant to starvation-induced death**: viability against serum-fed cells at 72 h about 21% (wild-type) versus about 42% (null), sub-G0/G1 about 21% versus about 11% (Figure 4B shows single bars with no error bars although n = 3 is stated), mitochondrial membrane potential falling to about 0.35 in wild-type and staying near 0.8 in null; reactive oxygen species rose in both genotypes and more in wild-type (about 5.3-fold at 6 h to about 10-fold at 48 h versus about 3-fold to about 4.6-fold), with no genotype difference in freshly isolated cells; N-acetylcysteine blunted the WWOX rise. Chloroquine blocked the Mcl-1 loss in wild-type cells (no inhibitor experiments in null cells). **Defects:** the text gives 48 h for the sub-G0/G1 comparison and the legend 72 h; supplementary densitometry panels are single quantifications without error bars; WWOX induction ranges from about 2.2-fold (Figure 3A) to about 6.3-fold (Figure S2A) between blots. **Does not support:** any statement on fasting, calorie restriction or dextrose. It shows that in culture, **loss** of Wwox protected fibroblasts from serum-withdrawal death, which is the opposite polarity from a "loss is detrimental" expectation and sits beside, not against, the null mouse's postnatal hypoglycaemic lethality (cell versus organism). Cancer-cell and fibroblast systems, extrapolated by the authors to patients in the final sentence.

**Carvalho 2022 (35984507), an extract, not a read; no receipt (scientist-c's resume landed first).** Re-opened from the stored PMC page copy and figures 2-3 to answer "which glucose concentrations were used". Differentiated SH-SY5Y neuroblastoma cells were exposed to 10 or 25 mM glucose **added to** DMEM-F12 for 24 or 48 h; the control is DMEM-F12 "without glucose supplementation", and **the base medium's own glucose is not stated for the incubation**. Phosphorylated WWOX (Tyr33) rose about 1.8-fold in all four glucose conditions (p < 0.05 each); viability fell to about 83% of control with 25 mM at 48 h; the Zfra1-31 peptide at 20 micromolar, added 3 h or 24 h after glucose, restored viability to about 94-98% and lowered pWWOX, p53 and caspase 3 activity. In Goto-Kakizaki type 2 diabetic rats (blood glucose about 85% higher) pWWOX rose about 2.2-fold in cortex and about 3-fold in hippocampus at 6 months and not at 12 months. In this system WWOX **activation** is the damaging arm and its inhibition protective; the peptide's specificity and a genetic arm are not shown in what I opened. It does not bear on WWOX loss of function except as a reminder that glucose excess is itself a WWOX-pathway stressor in neuron-like cells. scientist-c's landed resume of this paper (figures and ESM, receipt `FTR-20261019-35984507-03`) supersedes this extract; none was written by me.

## 7. Candidate leads (all `IPOTESI`; none is a claim; each with its falsifier)

- **L1. A fasting seizure-threshold experiment in a Wwox-deficient mouse, prediction fixed before data.** Prediction: a 24 h fast lowers PTZ seizure incidence in Wwox+/+ and Wwox+/- animals as in controls of Yuskaitis 2022 (about 56% to about 22%), because mTORC1 sensing is not shown to be altered by WWOX loss; the alternative is loss of protection as in the Depdc5 knockout. Falsifier of "fasting protection is intact": incidence unchanged in the deficient genotype with glucose lowered and ketones raised. This is a plan for an experiment, not an indication for any fasting.
- **L2. Resolve the cell-versus-organism polarity.** Su 2026 shows Wwox-null fibroblasts resisting serum-withdrawal death; the null mouse dies hypoglycaemic. A glucose-withdrawal (not serum) experiment in Wwox-null neurons with ATP, ROS and viability readouts would say which direction applies to neurons. Falsifier of "loss protects neurons from nutrient stress": null neurons die faster than wild-type under glucose withdrawal.
- **L3. pWWOX and mitochondrial readouts in a WWOX-deficient neuron exposed to 10 and 25 mM glucose** against the Carvalho conditions, with a genetic control for the peptide. Falsifier of "WWOX activation mediates high-glucose damage": the damage is unchanged in knockdown cells.
- **L4 (method).** The blind audit of this wave's 101 load-bearing triples found 23 propositions that outran or undershot their quote (section 9). A reading-time proposition-versus-quote check, which last wave named as a gap, would have caught most of them before commit.
