## What the paper did
Retrospective laboratory-record review, 2015–2018, 105 children aged 1 month–19 years with unexplained NDD; array-CGH first (CNV hit rate 16 %), WES for 79 CNV-negative patients (P/LP SNV hit rate 24/79, 30 %). Variants classified by ACMG.

## WWOX content (class level)
- **Table 2 (SNV variants), two WWOX rows:**
  - `WWOX (NM_016373.4):c.606-1G > A` — **Homozygous — Pathogenic** — a canonical splice-acceptor (−1) allele.
  - `WWOX(NM_016373.4):c.33del (p.Asp11GlufsTer69)` — **Homozygous — Likely pathogenic** — a frameshift allele.
  - Both rows carry the OMIM phenotype string "Developmental and epileptic encephalopathy-28, esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, somatic, spinocerebellar ataxia, autosomal recessive-12" — a gene-level OMIM label, **not** the patient's phenotype.
- **Results / Discussion:** WWOX is named among "genes and SNVs … involved in development delays, such as VPS13B, RNASEH2A, and WWOX".
- **Supplement Table 4 (Datasheet1):** c.606-1G>A at 16:78458766 (GRCh37), rs730882215, GERP 5.3499, CADD 34; c.33del at 16:78133708, GERP 5.11 — in-silico scores only.
- **How many WWOX patients:** two variant rows, each homozygous. INFERENZA: two different homozygous WWOX alleles in one person is improbable, so two patients; the table is a variant list, not a patient list, and the paper never says "two patients".
- **Allele consequence: predicted, not measured.** No RNA, protein or minigene assay for either allele; the paper does not even print a predicted consequence for c.606-1G>A. INFERENZA (my derivation from RefSeq NM_016373.4, exon map c.517–605 = exon 6, c.606–791 = exon 7): c.606-1 is the **intron 6 acceptor of exon 7**; skipping exon 7 (186 nt) would be **in frame** (62 codons), so this canonical-acceptor allele cannot be assumed to be a null without an RNA measurement — the consequence is unknown, not "loss of function by default". c.33del is in exon 1.

## What the paper does NOT state
Per-patient phenotype (the only per-patient datum is the row itself), age, sex, epilepsy, MRI, course, outcome, consanguinity, segregation, parental testing, or whether the two WWOX patients are related. Table 3 (CNVs) carries no 16q event. The paper reports epilepsy as the main NDD in only 5 % of the cohort and developmental delay in 60 %.

## Authors' caveats
Retrospective; WES misses large indels/rearrangements/regulatory variants; karyotypes not available for all.
