## 1 · What the paper is

A primary mouse study (FVB, male, intrahippocampal kainic acid) of a three-drug adrenergic BLOCKADE (propranolol, prazosin, atipamezole; "PPA"). It reports delayed and altered status epilepticus, lower mortality and fewer spontaneous seizures weeks later, restored tracer influx and efflux in cortex and hypothalamus, less gliosis, and a worse course in aquaporin-4 knockout mice. It is the experimental mirror of the agonist papers: here adrenergic signalling is switched OFF.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Acute (5 minutes after kainic acid): onset of the first seizure later (0.9 to 3.9 h), each seizure longer (115 to 204 s), interval longer (8 to 20 min), total seizures not different, maximum Racine score lower (5.5 to 5.1); phase 2 shorter (22.5 to 8.6 h); total duration of status not different (26.1 against 19.9 h, P 0.265).
2. Three daily doses: death in the first ten days lower (12 mice per group); at 3 to 4 weeks fewer spontaneous convulsive seizures, shorter duration, lower maximum Racine, normalised open-field and rotarod (6 against 7 mice for EEG).
3. Starting at 30 h: fewer seizures at 3 to 4 weeks and 2 months, no effect on duration, weight or mortality; maximal score and seizure-free days not different at 2 months.
4. A single dose at 3 weeks: transient reduction over 6 h, not 12 h.
5. Tracer influx raised 47.7 percent in healthy mice; restored in the kainic acid mice in cortex and hypothalamus, not hippocampus; efflux restored; AQP4 polarisation partly restored; gliosis and one cytokine lower.
6. AQP4 knockout (a different strain): worse acute and chronic seizures.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- No registry record holds an adrenergic term, so nothing is confirmed or contradicted; this is unmodelled territory.
- Within group H3 the three receptor papers do not share a sign: beta2 agonist in piriform cortex lowered seizures; alpha2 agonists lengthened spike-wave; the combined beta, alpha1 and alpha2 BLOCKADE here lengthened each acute seizure and delayed their onset yet reduced mortality and later seizures. Propranolol blocks the same beta2 receptor whose agonist was anticonvulsant in the first paper; atipamezole blocks the alpha2 receptors whose agonists were pro-absence in the second. A single "adrenergic" direction does not exist at class level.
- The published peer review file records reviewer 4 writing that most effects are on status epilepticus and not on epileptogenesis, reviewers 1 and 2 that causality via glymphatic flow is undemonstrated, and reviewer 2 that the cocktail is non-specific. This is context, not evidence; the first-pass negatives above were written before it was read and agree with it.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Any WWOX statement; the model is acquired epilepsy after a chemoconvulsant, not a genetic DEE.
- That glymphatic enhancement mediates the benefit: the cocktail acts on neurons directly, hippocampal glymphatic flow did not improve, and the knockout is a different strain with developmental adaptation.
- That the effect is anti-epileptogenic as distinct from altered initial injury: most effects are on status epilepticus; survivors differ in number; late dosing has a weaker, shorter effect.
- Any statement about adrenaline, epinephrine or noradrenaline: the paper blocks receptors, it does not give or measure the hormone.
- Safety: no heart rate, blood pressure or sedation data under a beta, alpha1 and alpha2 blockade.

## 5 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype (WWOX-DEE)

ESPANSIONE, thin. System gap: adult male FVB mice, kainic acid, acquired epilepsy, three-drug blockade. What transfers is the design lesson that a seizure outcome measured weeks later depends on survival and initial injury, and that blockade and stimulation of the same receptor family can each appear beneficial in different models.
