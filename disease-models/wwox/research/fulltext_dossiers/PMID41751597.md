## What the paper did

C57BL/6J mice of both sexes received one injection of a self-complementary AAV9 vector with a strong constitutive promoter driving GFP (CBh-EGFP-BGH polyA) or vehicle, at postnatal day 1, 5, 10 or 28, by one of four routes: lumbar intrathecal (IT), intracerebroventricular (ICV), IT plus ICV four hours apart with the dose split (IT and ICV), or intravenous (IV). Fixed total doses of 2.5 and 5 x 10^11 vector genomes per animal, **not scaled to age or body weight**; volumes differed by route and age (Table 1). Tissues collected at day 56: GFP vector DNA by qPCR (vg per diploid genome, normalised to GAPDH; Wilcoxon with Benjamini-Hochberg), GFP immunohistochemistry on a mean-representative subset of two to three mice per group (no statistics), H&E on euthanised animals. A separate cohort gave organ weights by age (Table A1). Not every age was crossed with every route or dose (the design is incomplete: Table 1).

## What it measured and states

1. **Mortality (Figure 1, Table A2).** After day-1 injection: IT 47 percent (mean age at termination 10 days), IT and ICV 39 percent (18 days), ICV and IV none. After day-5 injection: IT at the higher dose 70 percent and at the lower dose 17 percent, IT and ICV at the higher dose 40 percent and none at the lower dose, ICV at the lower dose 17 percent, IV none at either dose. No survival difference at day 10 or day 28. Panel check: Figure 1a and 1b group sizes (day 1: IT 15, IT and ICV 13; day 5: IT 12 and 10, ICV 12) reproduce the stated percentages and the Table 1 survivor counts (8 and 8; 10 and 3; 10), so Table 1 lists analysed survivors, with a footnote that the day-5 higher-dose IT group was "not fully enrolled due to a high mortality rate".
2. **Lesion.** Every euthanised animal reviewed had spinal cord abnormality (vacuolation and degeneration of white-matter tracts, diffuse myelomalacia, scattered degenerating neurons, endothelial hypertrophy); in the day-5 IT and ICV animals with brain findings these were focal (thalamic mineralisation, perivascular infiltrate, olfactory bulb). **No abnormality in the dorsal root ganglion in any euthanised animal reviewed** (Table A2 lists eight animals; cervical DRG "normal" even beside diffuse cervical spinal cord degeneration). One of the eight had mild multifocal myocarditis. H&E was done on euthanised animals; DRG histology of survivors is not reported.
3. **Authors' attribution.** The deaths were "most likely due to localized GFP overexpression toxicity": timing about two weeks after injection (peak of self-complementary expression), IT alone worse than the half-dose IT within IT and ICV, higher dose worse, GFP said to be toxic and potentially immunogenic, and other transgenes at similar or higher doses tolerated at these ages in other studies. **No null-cassette, promoterless or empty-capsid arm, no expression measurement in the animals that died, no immune read-out** was done; the attribution is an inference by the authors.
4. **DRG exposure (Figure A6, Figure 3).** GFP IHC area in cervical and lumbar DRG about 60 to 90 percent at day 1 and day 5 for every route including IV (read from the panel; n of 1 to 5 per bar; vehicle about 9 percent at day 28), lower after day 28 injection for IV (about 30 to 35 percent) and some CSF arms; the authors say it is unclear whether route or age changes DRG expression. So DRG was heavily transduced and, in euthanised mice, not injured.
5. **Route.** CSF routes gave higher brain and spinal cord vector genomes than IV at equal total dose and did not de-target peripheral organs (Table A3). Whole-cerebrum qPCR, lower dose: day 1 IT 0.102, ICV 0.294, IT and ICV 0.246, IV 0.00276; day 5 IT 0.0253, ICV 0.195, IT and ICV 0.957, IV 0.00186 (vg per diploid genome; Table A3, cell-wise from the JATS table). Spinal cord GFP area by IHC about 80 to 90 percent for CSF routes at day 1 and day 5 and about 20 percent for IV (panel read, n 2 to 5).
6. **Age.** The authors state expression is higher after injection at younger ages "regardless of route" and that day 1 injection overestimates the transduction a postnatal human would reach. Eye transduction peaked at day 5; liver vector genomes rose with age (IV 0.068 at day 1 to 2.01 at day 28, at different doses).
7. **Dose.** No significant dose effect on biodistribution between 2.5 and 5 x 10^11 at day 5; the authors say the study may be underpowered rather than saturated. The lower dose is stated to approximate a 1 x 10^15 vg human dose (citing another paper).

## Panel and table checks that change a sentence

- **"Younger = higher regardless of route" is qualified by the paper's own numbers.** Whole-cerebrum qPCR for ICV at the same lower dose is 0.294 at day 1 and 0.396 at day 28 (n 10 each; large SEM at day 28), IV 0.00276 at day 1 against 0.00464 at day 28 (different doses), and IT and ICV is highest at day 5, not day 1. Figure 2b IHC (read by eye, +/- about 3 points, n 2 to 5) shows a fall with age for the CSF routes (caudal brain about 67 to 75 percent at day 1 versus about 23 to 38 percent at day 28) but not for IV (about 10 percent at day 1, 16 at day 5, 14 at day 28). The age trend is therefore a CSF-route IHC result; by whole-cerebrum qPCR it is not monotonic, and the IV arm shows none.
- **Dose per body weight was not constant, and the age confound is the design.** From Table A1 (mean of the two sexes; my arithmetic): body weight about 1.4 g at day 1, 2.7 at day 5, 6.0 at day 10, 13.7 at day 28; brain 0.087, 0.18, 0.34 and 0.44 g. A fixed 2.5 x 10^11 vg is then about 1.8 x 10^11 vg per g body weight at day 1 against 1.8 x 10^10 at day 28 (tenfold), and about 2.9 x 10^12 vg per g brain at day 1 against 5.6 x 10^11 at day 28 (fivefold). The groups injected at day 28 mostly received the higher dose, so per-brain-gram the day-28 IT arm (1.1 x 10^12) is nearer the day-5 lower-dose arms (1.4 x 10^12). Age and dose per unit tissue move together in every comparison except where the same total was given at two ages; no arm matches dose per tissue mass across ages.

## What the paper does NOT say

- No toxicity read-out other than survival, weight and H&E in euthanised animals; no blood chemistry, no neurofilament, no behaviour, no DRG histology of survivors, no nerve-conduction or sciatic nerve data despite collecting sciatic nerve.
- No expression data (protein or mRNA) of a disease-relevant transgene; GFP is a reporter and a possible toxicant. No disease model.
- No WWOX, no myelin cell-autonomy analysis (the white-matter vacuolation is a descriptive histology finding at the level of a tract), no statement about human infants beyond a loose age analogy (day 1 about a preterm infant, days 5 to 10 about a full-term infant or toddler, day 28 about a pre-pubescent child, citing a source).
- No immune characterisation, no tolerisation or immunosuppression arm.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

Doses were not normalised to size; volumes differed by route and were not tested as a variable; no significant dose effects may reflect low power; IHC numbers too low for statistics; IV values at day 28 were "markedly reduced" relative to prior reports at similar doses, which the authors suggest may reflect subclinical GFP toxicity or immunogenicity in the liver; the ICV day-28 arm received half the total of the other day-28 arms.

## Reader's caveats (INFERENZA)

- The three toxicity explanations (dose, route, age) are not separable in this design: IT puts the vector next to the spinal cord, young animals receive more vector per gram, and the cassette is strong, constitutive and self-complementary with a non-neutral transgene. The authors' own synthesis (local expression is the main driver) fits the ordering IT, then IT and ICV, then ICV, then IV, but it is not tested.
- Whether the spinal cord and DRG outcomes would differ for a non-reporter, disease-relevant transgene cannot be read from this paper; the authors note other transgenes at similar doses were tolerated elsewhere (citations not opened).

## Transfer limit to the WWOX-DEE genotype class

Mouse, wild type, one reporter vector, one capsid, total vg per animal. It gives a measured example that in neonatal mice the same capsid and the same total dose produced lethal spinal-cord injury by one CSF route (lumbar intrathecal) and none by another or by IV, and that the DRG was heavily transduced without DRG injury in the euthanised animals. It does not bound any WWOX cassette, dose, route or age, and a constitutive null models neither human missense allele; absolute vg do not transfer across species.

## Verdict

**INGEST (measured, with the dose-per-weight and route-by-age confounds stated).** Compared with what is held (see the intake note): it adds a mouse in-vivo route-by-age by dose design in which mortality tracks local spinal exposure and dose, DRG is transduced but spared, and the age effect on expression is route-dependent.
