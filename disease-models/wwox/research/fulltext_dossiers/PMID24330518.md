## 3 · What is measured — and at what level

1. **WWOX knockdown raises four SMAD3 target transcripts in MCF10, and re-expression lowers them** (L9, L10, L14, L15) — moderate strength, replicated at mRNA and secreted-protein level. Weaker and not uniform in 184B5 and MCF7, each with a single shRNA.
2. **WWOX re-expression flattens the TGF-β reporter (about 91 → 38) and the SMAD3 ChIP signal** (L16–L19). No test for the reporter; on ANGPTL4 the SMAD3 signal with WWOX sits at or below the IgG background of one condition.
3. **WWOX and SMAD3 co-immunoprecipitate endogenously in MCF10**; a GST-WW1+2 pull-down retrieves Flag-SMAD3 strongly with wild type and **weakly but visibly** with W44F/P47A (L21, L22).
4. **In one displayed cell**, ectopic Myc-WWOX coincides with loss of bright nuclear SMAD3 (L23, L24).
5. **In public data, a WWOX-low/ANGPTL4-high cluster is enriched for TNBC and basal-like tumours** (L28, L29), with no correlation statistic reported.

## 6 · Strength, and transferability to the reference genotype

- **Strength:** the **binding** is the solid part — an endogenous co-IP plus a domain-mapped pull-down, in one cell line, with the mutant retaining residual binding. The **transcriptional consequence** is supported by concordant knockdown/re-expression on four targets. The **mechanism** (sequestration) and the **tumour correlation** are the weakest claims and carry no statistics.
- **Transferability: T3 for the breast-cancer content; the domain-level datum is the transferable part.**
  - **WWOX WW1 binds SMAD3, which carries a PPGY motif** (L20, L21, L22). DATO for the interaction; this is a WW1-dependent partner, so it bears on any allele that alters WW1 (the W44F/P47A mutant is the reagent that shows the dependence). Transfer to neurons or to a developmental phenotype is **ESPANSIONE** — nothing here is neural, and the cells overexpress the partner.
  - **A partner-competition frame:** WWOX is placed alongside YAP, PIN1, NEDD4L and SMURF1/2 as WW-domain regulators of SMADs (Discussion), which is context for interpreting WW1 variants, not evidence about them.
