## 1 · What the paper measures

One adult man (40 years old), homozygous WWOX c.35C>T p.Thr12Met by whole-exome sequencing with Sanger confirmation; non-consanguineous parents; retrospective narrative of seizures, development, regression and respiratory course; one long-term video-EEG session (2019, age 36, Figures 2 and 3); head CT at 35, no MRI; seven antiseizure medications. Nothing else is measured: no protein or functional assay of the variant, no metabolic laboratory value, no cardiorespiratory or autonomic recording.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Onset at 4 months with prolonged febrile tonic-clonic seizures; then epileptic spasms in clusters, autonomic seizures with impaired awareness and myoclonic seizures; spasms and autonomic seizures stopped by 1.5 years; tonic drop attacks 1 to 12 years; myoclonic seizures, eyelid-myoclonia absences and behaviour-arrest seizures lifelong; 10 to 30 paroxysms daily.
2. Seven antiseizure medications (phenobarbital, valproate, carbamazepine, topiramate, levetiracetam, ethosuximide, perampanel) gave no sustained effect.
3. Development: sitting after 1 year, crawling from 1.5, supported walking from 3; language lost after 1 year; regression of motor skills after 12 with scoliosis, kyphosis and contractures.
4. Respiratory: recurrent infections from infancy, four pneumonias a year from 36, bulbar palsy with tube feeding, respiratory failure worsening after COVID-19 at 38 or later, ventilatory support since.
5. EEG at 36: preserved 7 to 8 Hz posterior rhythm and sleep architecture; sleep-activated epileptiform activity (70 to 80 percent of some epochs, below 20 to 25 percent overall); slow spike-wave at 1.5 to 2 Hz (Figure 3); ictal recording captured myoclonic seizures only.
6. Variant: not in HGMD or ClinVar at the time; another substitution at the same residue (Thr12Arg) is in ClinVar as uncertain significance and as pathogenic; gnomAD one heterozygous allele; predictors mixed; ACMG likely pathogenic on PM2, PM5, PP3, PP4.
7. Authors classify the case as Lennox-Gastaut despite no generalised paroxysmal fast activity, and say early diagnosis would not have changed therapy then.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- CLAIM 020 ("adult survival with persistent severe disability and possible late motor regression ... supports long-horizon respiratory and functional surveillance") is supported as a single-case observation. The 2026-08 reading and this one agree on the content; the two readings also agree on the caption versus calibration-bar discrepancy and on the ECG and EMG channels that only the images show.
- The record says "40 years follow-up". The article does not report serial observation: the course is a retrospective narrative and the only instrumented data are one EEG day and a head CT. "We were able to track the dynamics of changes in seizure types over 40 years" (Discussion) is a recall-based timeline.
- PAPER 015 note "genotype different from the reference genotype; does not change strategy": unchanged.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Pathogenicity at the level of mechanism. No functional test of p.Thr12Met; PM5 rests on a Thr12Arg entry that ClinVar itself lists as both uncertain and pathogenic; MutPred benign-moderate, FATHMM and PROVEAN uncertain.
- A true homozygote. Parents are non-consanguineous; Sanger "in the proband and unaffected family members" is named in Methods but no parental genotype, segregation or deletion-exclusion is reported, and the Manta CNV/SV analysis has no stated outcome. Hemizygosity over a deletion (or a similar event) is not excluded by the text. Conservative reading: a homozygous call, not a confirmed biallelic homozygous state.
- Any autonomic or metabolic claim. "Autonomic seizures" are a recalled early-childhood semiology (stopped by 1.5 years), with no ictal autonomic recording; the ECG channel in the figures is a polygraphy trace with no analysis; no glucose, lactate, ketone, lipid or endocrine value exists. The only mention of hypoglycemia is the mouse study the Discussion restates.
- A cause for the respiratory decline. Recurrent infection, bulbar palsy, scoliosis and a COVID-19 event co-occur; the authors say respiratory illness "contributed to significant neurological regression", which is a causal direction with no data; the reverse (neurological disability driving aspiration and restrictive disease) is not excluded.
- A genotype-severity rule. The Discussion cites a rule (biallelic missense leads mostly to an ataxia phenotype; one missense allele raises 5-year survival) taken from the 2023 cohort and does not note that this biallelic-missense patient has a DEE phenotype with Lennox-Gastaut features and survived to 40; that is an exception to the cited rule, unremarked by the authors. n = 1 does not overturn a cohort rule.
- Treatment negatives are complete. The seven medications listed contain no ketogenic diet, steroid or ACTH course, vigabatrin, benzodiazepine or neurostimulation; absence of mention is not absence of use.

## 5 · Design and limits

Single case, retrospective, unblinded; first-author group is an adult epileptology and genetic-diagnostics pairing; one author declares employment at a genetic-testing company. One EEG day; no MRI; no tables, no supplement, no limitations section.

## 7 · Horizontal impact and transfer

Metabolic theme: nothing measured. Autonomic: nothing measured. Epilepsy treatment: a refractory phenotype survived four decades on seven classical and recent ASMs without a ketogenic or hormonal trial being reported; informative as a negative base only for those seven drugs. Transfer to the reference genotype (a destabilising SDR missense allele and a splice-acceptor allele): an N-terminal missense homozygote with a Lennox-Gastaut phenotype, long survival and no functional characterisation; phenotypic transfer T1 at most.
