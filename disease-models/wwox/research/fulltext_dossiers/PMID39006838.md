## What it is

An eight-patient series of COL4A2-variant infantile epileptic spasm syndrome (CSF cytokines before and after treatment, seizure outcomes) and cell experiments in a rat astrocyte line and primary rat astrocytes stimulated with LPS, comparing adenoviral overexpression of wild-type and a novel variant (p.G613V), with the JAK inhibitor WP1066. WWOX: zero occurrences; no WWOX content.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** The paper is a genetic-DEE-class comparator for neuroinflammation and for basement-membrane/blood-brain-barrier biology.

## What it measures (DATO)

Six patients had CSF IL-1beta and IL-6 above reference before seizure freedom; after treatment four patients' IL-1beta and IL-6 fell; LPS-stimulated astrocytes overexpressing the variant show higher GFAP, IL-1beta, IL-6, TNF-alpha, p-JAK2 and p-STAT3 than those overexpressing wild type, reversed by WP1066.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **The functional variant is of uncertain significance and inherited from an unaffected-for-epilepsy mother.** Seven of the eight patients carry de novo likely pathogenic variants, but the one modelled in cells (patient 7) is classed uncertain and maternal; "incomplete penetrance" is one reading, a non-causal variant is another. The "type VII DEE gene" conclusion from this is not supported.
2. **No loss-of-function or disease-relevant test.** The cell experiment compares overexpression of mutant against overexpression of wild type in LPS-stimulated astrocytes; no knockdown, null, patient cell, collagen-IV deposition, or blood-brain-barrier readout; the cell types that produce type IV collagen in vivo are not tested (outside-knowledge note: endothelial and mural cells are the main source). LPS drives all groups; the finding is a modulation of an LPS response by overexpression.
3. **Table 4 duplicates Table 3 values.** For the four patients listed in Table 4 (3, 4, 5, 7), ten of the twelve cytokines have the same value to one decimal as in the before-treatment row of Table 3 (only IL-1beta and IL-6 differ); the table title says six patients but four are listed. I cannot say why (reuse of values, rounding or an editing error); it is a data-integrity query for the authors, not an accusation.
4. **Text and tables disagree on the CSF story.** Patient 3's IL-1beta (10.5) and patient 4's IL-6 (18.7) are within reference; the text says four patients' cytokines fell "whose seizures were controlled" while Table 4 patients 5 and 7 are uncontrolled in Table 1. Whether cytokine fall tracks seizure control cannot be judged from two controlled and two partly-reduced patients.
5. **A cross-panel concern.** The IL-6 ELISA bars in the CTX-TNA cells (Figure 1E) and in primary astrocytes (Figure 3D) read about 25, 63, 64, 52, 81 and 25, 63, 65, 52, 82 pg/mL with the same significance marks and tiny error bars, while IL-1beta and TNF-alpha panels of the two systems differ. Compared after cropping and enlargement; the match is at the limit of the reading precision. INFERENZA only; the original ELISA data would resolve it.
6. **Western blots do not show what the text says for GFAP.** The primary-astrocyte GFAP blot (Figure 3F) has near-equal bands across groups though the text reports an increase (IF only). One lane per group and no replicate quantification of any western.
7. **Statistics.** 50 cells per group from three experiments plotted and tested as units with one-way ANOVA; three ELISA replicates; shared control groups across Figures 1 and 2.
8. **Not about plasma products** and not about serum immunoglobulin; the BBB link is a stated hypothesis (collagen IV and astrocytes) not measured.

## Design and limits

Small retrospective series, mixed heterozygous missense variants of differing classification, rat cells, LPS, overexpression.
