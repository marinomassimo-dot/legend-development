## 1 · What the paper is

A narrative review (PubMed, Web of Science, Embase to January 2024; no appraisal, no quantitative synthesis) of ketogenic diet (KD) in paediatric developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (DEE): diet types, putative mechanisms, syndrome sections (infantile spasms, Dravet, Ohtahara, Lennox-Gastaut, EMAS), a gene table, safety, outlook. It is a secondary source. WWOX does not occur in it (term count 0).

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Syndrome-level responder figures: infantile spasms 23/43 spasm-free; Dravet 63, 60, 47 percent with at least 50 percent seizure reduction at 3, 6, 12 months (attrition stated); Lennox-Gastaut 47 percent of 189; EMAS over 50 percent with at least 50 percent reduction and 18 percent seizure-free.
2. Gene-level (one study, its ref 9, behind four genes): SCN1A n 18 at 77.8 percent, KCNQ2 n 6 at 83.3 percent, STXBP1 n 4 and SCN2A n 3 at 100 percent responders (counts are not printed; they follow from n and rate); CDKL5 0/10 in that study, while a 104-patient registry reports 69 with any change and 61 of those improved, with poor long-term efficacy. Responder rate therefore runs from 0 to 100 percent by gene in cohorts of 3 to 18.
3. Mechanism section lists ketone-body glutamate/GABA effects, mitochondrial ATP, adenosine/KATP, HDAC inhibition, cytokines, microbiota, PUFAs, and says the mechanisms are not established. Figure 1 is a schematic with no data.
4. Safety: hypoglycaemia and dehydration are the common early adverse effects (fasting, reduced intake); in infants up to 12 months two children had significant hypoglycaemia at initiation. Contraindications include mitochondrial respiratory chain disorders and fatty-acid oxidation defects; KD is first-line for GLUT1 deficiency and PDH deficiency.
5. The Outlook says KD reduces or controls seizures in "most" DEE cases, stronger than the review's own 47 to 63 percent figures; it also says KD is typically used after multiple drugs and even surgery have failed (a pre-selected population).

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- CLAIM 042 (in observation): three of five WOREE patients reported to improve on KD, denominator of exposed patients not stated, no Q230P carrier among the responders. PAPER 133 records a WOREE patient on whom KD was ineffective. Neither has an aetiology-wide prior to be read against. This review supplies one: across DEE genes and syndromes the responder fraction at the 50 percent threshold spans roughly 47 to 100 percent (excluding the CDKL5 zero), from cohorts that are small and pre-selected as refractory. A 3-of-5 with an unstated denominator sits inside that range and cannot be distinguished from it, nor from the null that DEE patients on KD simply respond often.
- The review answers question 3 of the selection (the prior) and leaves the second half (energy repletion versus Warburg reversal) to the Joshi 2025 paper: here no mechanism is singled out.
- Initiation-phase hypoglycaemia is a named adverse effect. The Wwox-null mouse is hypoglycaemic (registry). That does not make WOREE infants hypoglycaemic, but a KD start in a WWOX-DEE child is an exposure where the one measured Wwox-null physiological phenotype and the diet's own early risk coincide (INFERENZA, not shown anywhere).
