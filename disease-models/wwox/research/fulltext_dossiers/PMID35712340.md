## What the paper did

One boy of a consanguineous family; clinical description at 21 months; metabolic panel (Tables 1-2); commercial exome;
parental carrier status inferred from "prenatal WES data"; a literature comparison table (Table 3).

## Genotype class and what was measured

- **Allele class × zygosity:** missense `c.406A>G` × homozygous; Table 3 gives `p.Ile136Val` and `Chr16:78149048`.
  The variant lies four nucleotides upstream of the exon 4 donor (`c.409`), and the paper reports an *in silico*
  prediction of altered splicing. 15 of 21 *in silico* tools called it pathogenic.
- **Measured:** DNA only. Neither the splice prediction nor any protein effect was tested. The affected sibling
  ("known to have seizures") and the cousin with cerebral palsy were **not genotyped**.

## Phenotype axes

- **Seizures:** none — "no early seizure disorder"; "abnormal gaze around three times" on examination only.
- **Development:** delayed but progressing — sat, crawled and pulled to stand by 15 months; at 21 months walks and runs with falls, ten words.
- **MRI:** normal. No microcephaly, no optic atrophy.
- **Other:** mild hepatosplenomegaly, raised CK, cholestatic liver profile, low lipids, low cystine, slightly raised alpha-aminobutyric acid.
- **Survival:** alive at 21 months.

## What the paper states and what it does NOT

- States, in its abstract and introduction, that WWOX causes DEE28 and frames the child within it; the Discussion
  concedes the phenotype is only "partially supportive of DEE28".
- Does **not** demonstrate that the variant causes the phenotype: no segregation in the affected relative, no RNA,
  no protein, a conservative substitution, and a phenotype without the defining features of WWOX-DEE.
- Does **not** support its Conclusion that "loss of function of WWOX could be responsible" for the CK, cholestasis and lipid findings — that is asserted, not tested.

## Source-internal and review-table defects

1. Table 3 assigns `c.160G>T p.Arg54` to **both** Abdel-Salam and Ben-Salem; Ben-Salem's own title (reference 10) is "A novel whole exon deletion".
2. Table 3 gives a genomic interval `chr16:25,457,305-79,894,309` in a variant column.
3. The Discussion says "The present case report study reported the first case of a WWOX-related phenotype", which is false as written.
4. "DEE28; OMIM:6162111" carries an extra digit.
Table 3 is therefore not a count source.

## Caveats the authors give

"the number of cases with WWOX mutations is low ... studies on more patients are needed".
