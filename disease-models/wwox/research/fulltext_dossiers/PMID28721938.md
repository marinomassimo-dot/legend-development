## What the paper did
A clinical commentary on one proband, with a short literature discussion and "practical clues" for diagnosis.

## What it measured
- Genotype: compound heterozygous `c.173-1G>T` (intron 2 canonical acceptor; splice effect **predicted** by Alamut only) and `c.918del p.(Glu306Aspfs*21)` (exon 8 frameshift); in trans by parental testing. Non-consanguineous family. Allele class: predicted null / null.
- Course: reduced fetal movements; hypokinetic from birth; focal-onset seizures at four weeks followed by spasms; hypsarrhythmia; transient control with vigabatrin and then ACTH; later drug-resistant multifocal seizures and spasms; sleep-fragmented hypsarrhythmia persisting; no developmental acquisitions; severe cerebral visual impairment.
- Head circumference **normal throughout** (P50 at birth, P85 at 2.6 years).
- MRI at five weeks: thin corpus callosum, **normal myelination for age**. MRI at 2.6 years: cortico-subcortical atrophy, very thin corpus callosum, brainstem flattening; the figure legend adds delayed myelination.
- Extensive metabolic work-up negative.
- Death from aspiration pneumonia at almost three years.

## What it states
- Microcephaly is not mandatory for diagnosis, even with progressive atrophy; persistent spasms and sleep-fragmented hypsarrhythmia are proposed as clues.
- "Almost 20 patients (including our patient)" reported to date.
- With Patient 5 of Mignot et al., the only severe-phenotype patients with normal head circumference.
- WWOX was not on epileptic-encephalopathy gene panels at the time.
