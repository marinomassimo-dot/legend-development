## What the paper did

A retrospective aggregation of liver-related safety data from **six cynomolgus macaque studies,
146 animals** (41 vehicle control, 105 dosed with scAAV9; 73 of those dosed intrathecally (IT) at
1–3 × 10^13 vg/animal, 32 dosed intravenously (IV) at 1.4 × 10^14 – 1.1 × 10^15 vg/animal), study
endpoints 8 days to 1 year. All vectors were self-complementary AAV9 with a CMV-enhancer/CBA
hybrid promoter; transgenes were human SMN1, human ASPA, or EGFP. Four animals received empty
capsids and four a promoterless genome. Ages 13 months to 6.7 years. Animals were pre-selected
for the **lowest** anti-AAV9 titres.

## What it measured

Clinical observations; clinical pathology (ALT, AST, ALP, GGT, bilirubin, platelets, monocytes,
CRP, albumin, coagulation, WBC); plasma cytokines and complement fractions; anti-AAV9 and
anti-EGFP antibody titres; liver histopathology graded 1–5 by board-certified pathologists;
molecular localisation in liver by IHC (VP1/2/3, CD3, CD68, CD163, ADAR1, cleaved caspase-3,
gasdermin D) and RNAscope ISH (vector sense/antisense, DDX58, DHX58, IFIH1, IL-1A, IL-1B, IL-6,
TNF, IFN-B1); vector-genome biodistribution by ddPCR normalised to CFTR (vg/dg).

## What it states

1. **Route sets liver exposure and the timing of injury.** Median liver load was **721 vg/dg**
   after IV at 1–2 × 10^14 vg/kg and **19 vg/dg** after a single IT injection at
   1–6 × 10^13 vg/NHP. Transaminase elevations appeared 3–4 days after IV dosing and about
   2 weeks after IT dosing.
2. **The injury requires a transducing, expressing genome.** "No transaminase elevation or
   microscopic changes were observed with intrathecal administration of empty capsids or a
   'promoterless genome' vector" — despite a rapid, robust anti-AAV9 antibody response in those
   same animals. The authors conclude liver injury after CSF dosing "is driven by viral
   transduction and transgene expression".
3. **Transgene identity modulates magnitude.** IT ALT rose ~2- to 77-fold overall, and
   ~4- to 77-fold when the transgene was EGFP versus SMN1 — i.e. a foreign transgene produced the
   larger elevation at a similar or lower dose.
4. **Immunosuppression did not prevent the injury.** Prednisolone (1 mg/kg) with IV or IT dosing,
   and rituximab (20 mg/kg) plus everolimus (0.5 mg/kg b.d.) with IT dosing, "did not prevent"
   the ALT/platelet/monocyte changes or the microscopic findings. Prednisolone did **marginally
   mitigate** early CRP, albumin, platelet and ALT changes, **prevented** periportal CD3+ T-cell
   infiltration at 6 weeks, and decreased hepatocyte ADAR1; all of those differences had
   disappeared by 26 weeks.
5. **Humoral response is not the lever either.** Rituximab + everolimus depleted peripheral blood
   B cells and reduced splenic cellularity yet produced "no pronounced difference" in anti-AAV9
   titre.
6. **There is a steep dose step to lethality.** scAAV9-CBA-ASPA IV at ~2 × 10^14 vg/kg
   (~4.2–8.8 × 10^14 vg/animal) was **not tolerated**: moribundity/mortality in all treated
   animals by day 7–8, with early type-1 interferon and chemokine rises within 24–48 h,
   alternative and terminal complement activation, consumptive coagulopathy, ALT up to ~153-fold
   and AST up to ~173-fold, and coalescing hepatocellular necrosis. The two animals given the
   lowest total doses were least affected.
7. **The correlate is vector load, not peak transgene.** "the affected hepatocytes were not
   necessarily the cells expressing the transgene at the highest expression concentrations";
   signal was dominated by vector genomes and capsid, with RIG-I/LGP2 (DDX58/DHX58) and ADAR1
   upregulation and IL-1A/IL-1B/IL-6 induction.

## What it does NOT state

- It says **nothing about WWOX** and nothing about any DEE.
- It reports **no DRG finding**; dorsal root ganglia appear only as a biodistribution tissue in
  the Figure 1 legend. This paper is a liver paper.
- It does **not** test empty capsid, promoterless vector, or dual immunosuppression with **IV**
  dosing — the authors say so explicitly.
- It does not test combined multi-agent immunosuppression ("a hypothesis we did not explore").
- It does not model a recipient who is **naive to the transgene product**: SMN1 and ASPA are
  >96% identical between human and cynomolgus, so the transgene was effectively self. EGFP is
  the only foreign-transgene arm, and it is the arm with the largest ALT rise.
- It makes no claim about late (>1 month) injury: the authors state the NHP model's "limitation
  in mimicking later injury more than 1 month post dose".

## Caveats the authors themselves give

Retrospective collation across studies with differing objectives and limited dose escalation;
species-specific susceptibility differences; the ASPA cassette differs from SMN1 in codon
optimisation and CpG/GC content (GC 54.5% vs 45%) and unmethylated CpG motifs were **not**
experimentally mapped; clinical attribution of liver events is confounded by disease and
co-medication.

## Transfer limit for a WWOX-DEE programme

Cynomolgus macaque, juvenile to adult, liver endpoint, self or reporter transgene, scAAV9 with a
strong ubiquitous promoter. A predicted-null WWOX recipient is **transgene-naive** to WWOX
protein, which is the one immunological variable this dataset does not contain; the EGFP arm is
the nearest proxy and it is the worst-behaved arm. Nothing here is medical advice.
