## What the paper did

The companion of PMID 30073179: same route (image-guided suboccipital injection), species (rhesus), capsid (AAV9) and promoter and polyA cassette (CMV enhancer/chicken beta-actin hybrid, rabbit globin polyA) but the cargo is human iduronate-2-sulfatase (hIDS), at higher doses: 5e13 GC (high) and 1.7e13 GC (low). Thirteen animals, all male, necropsy day 90: vehicle 1, high 3, low 3 (no immunosuppression), plus an immunosuppressed second experiment with high 2 and low 3 (mycophenolate mofetil to day 60, rapamycin to day 90; one high-dose animal withdrawn before dosing for regimen side effects).

## What it measured

- Well tolerated; no transaminase, coagulation or blood-count change in non-immunosuppressed animals. CSF mononuclear pleocytosis 5-20 cells/uL in 2/3 high, 1/3 low and 1/3 low-plus-immunosuppression animals, peaking days 21-45.
- Anti-IDS antibodies low and transient: serum 3/3 high, 2/3 low, 0/5 immunosuppressed. Transgene T-cell responses in one low-dose animal only. AAV9 neutralising antibodies dose dependent, lower with immunosuppression at high dose.
- DRG neuronal degeneration with mononuclear infiltrate (mostly CD3 T, fewer CD20 B cells, around IDS-positive neurons) and dorsal-column axonopathy in all vector animals bar one low-dose animal; grade 2 worst; sciatic axonopathy in a few; no peripheral-organ lesion; brain and grey matter normal.
- Figure 3F (inspected): DRG cumulative score about 3.4 high, 1.1 low, 3.0 high plus immunosuppression (two animals), 4.7 low plus immunosuppression; axonopathy about 4.0, 2.0, 2.0, 3.3. Groups of two to three, wide error bars.
- One animal with a pre-existing low serum AAV9 neutralising titre (1:40) had liver and heart transduction abolished with CNS and DRG transduction unchanged; its serum enzyme activity never rose (n = 1).
- Serum IDS activity fell below baseline in non-immunosuppressed animals that made antibodies (authors: assay cross-reactivity with macaque enzyme), stayed up with immunosuppression.

## What the authors state

- Subclinical sensory ganglionitis similar to that after IV AAV9-SMN1 in piglets and macaques and after the companion IDUA vector; incidence and severity lower than with IDUA at "similar doses" (their IDUA high dose 1e13 sits just below the lowest IDS dose 1.7e13), with lower transgene immunogenicity (cited as unpublished data and a mouse paper).
- "Immune responses are probably not the only explanation, as IS did not prevent neuronal degeneration." Hypothesis: overexpression stress in DRG neurons gives low-grade injury that an adaptive response then worsens, compared with anti-Hu paraneoplastic ganglionitis.
- CSF pleocytosis "did not strictly correlate with histological findings".

## What the paper does NOT say

- No claim that immunosuppression reduces DRG injury; in this study the low-dose immunosuppressed animals scored highest on DRG (panel) and the high-dose immunosuppressed animals did not score lower on DRG.
- No dose-matched cargo comparison: the IDUA and IDS studies differ in dose (up to five times), date, group size, sex mix (IDUA mixed, IDS all male), vehicle n and necropsy-time design.
- No intracellular-protein cargo, no sensory function endpoint, no mechanism test.

## Reader's observations (INFERENZA where marked)

- Taken with PMID 30073179 the pair shows the DRG lesion at both cargoes with the same capsid, promoter, route and species, so the lesion is not specific to one transgene product. Whether severity is cargo-dependent is a between-study contrast with confounds above (INFERENZA: it favours a transgene-dependent component but cannot isolate it).
- The immunosuppressed groups are the smallest (two and three animals) and the regimen itself made animals ill; the DRG score with immunosuppression is not lower than without in the panel.
