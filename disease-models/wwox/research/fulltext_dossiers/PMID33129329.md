Read as an **assay specification**, not as oncology: the question put to this paper is what it
measures about WWOX, in what matrix, with what reagent, against what controls, with what dynamic
range and what reliability — and whether any of that could be run on a sample taken from a living
patient. The disease question (WWOX-DEE) is nowhere in this paper and no claim about it is drawn
here. **Nothing in this file is medical advice.**

---

## 1 · What the paper did

A pilot characterisation of WWOX in canine cutaneous mast cell tumours, in two canine mastocytoma
cell lines (BR, C2), in two murine mast cell lines (C57, P815) and in normal canine bone-marrow
cultured mast cells, followed by a gain- and loss-of-function arm in the two canine lines.

Four measurement surfaces, four different technical bases:

| Surface | Matrix | Reagent | Read-out | Normaliser |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Transcript | fresh-frozen tumour, cultured cells | SYBR primer pair, canine-specific | relative, comparative-Ct | 18S ribosomal RNA |
| Protein (blot) | fresh-frozen tumour, cultured cells | catalogue polyclonal rabbit anti-WWOX | densitometry of a ~47 kDa band | β-actin at 43 kDa |
| Protein (tissue) | formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded, tissue microarray | rabbit anti-WWOX **antiserum**, gift, epitope = residues 12–94 | two ordinal scores read by eye | none |
| Function | two canine cell lines | lentiviral WWOX cDNA; two shRNAs | proliferation/viability; clonogenic survival after irradiation | vector / scramble controls |

Cohort: 26 primary tumours with 19 paired normal skin biopsies on the microarray; 7 excluded for
inadequate tumour, leaving 19 scored (14 low grade, 5 high grade). Five fresh-frozen tumours on
the blot. The authors state the design was a pilot and that **no power calculation was made**.

## 2 · What it measured, and what the numbers are

**Transcript.** Normal mast-cell preparations carry more WWOX transcript than primary tumours as a
group. Read from the panel rather than the sentence, the separation is group-level only: the two
normal preparations plot at roughly 0.0018 and 0.0007 on the 2^−ΔΔCt axis, and **three tumour
samples equal or exceed the higher of them** (about 0.0026, about 0.0028, about 0.0011) while the
remaining ~17 sit near 0.0002. No significance mark is drawn on the panel. The second transcript
panel is plotted on a **broken axis**, which makes a roughly seventeen-fold difference between the
normal preparation and the higher-expressing tumour line look categorical.

**Protein by blot.** Three of five fresh-frozen tumours gave low or non-detectable WWOX. The
uncropped blots in the supplement show the limits of this surface directly: the target band sits
~47 kDa and the loading control 43 kDa on the **same stripped-and-reprobed membrane**; the tumour
lysate membrane carries **multiple immunoreactive species**, with the reported band selected by an
arrow (one lane carries two bands, another carries two bands both below the arrow, a third carries a
band above the 50 kDa marker that no arrow indicates); and the membrane is hand-annotated with a
**three-hour exposure**, i.e. the signal is being pulled off the detection floor.

**Protein in tissue.** Staining intensity and percent positivity both fall with histological grade.
Read from the panel, the two metrics behave differently: intensity **overlaps** between grades at
score 1, while percent-positivity **separates completely** in this cohort (low grade at 3–4, high
grade at 1–2.5). The authors measured the reliability of both and report it as *fair*: weighted
kappa **0.264** for intensity and **0.273** for percent. The metric that separates is the metric
with the weaker agreement.

**Function.** An earned negative and one thin positive. Restoring WWOX by lentivirus or removing it
by shRNA changed **neither proliferation nor viability** in either line. Knockdown increased
clonogenic survival after irradiation at **6 Gy (p = 0.0416)** only; the adjacent 4 Gy point did not
reach significance (p = 0.1200), and the overexpression arm could not be assessed by colony
formation at all because that line formed no colonies in the assay medium.

## 3 · What the paper does NOT state

- **It states no analytical performance figure of any kind.** No limit of detection, no limit of
  quantification, no linear range, no standard curve, no recombinant calibrator, no spike-recovery,
  no absolute concentration anywhere. Every WWOX number in the paper is a ratio to something else
  measured in the same run.
- **It states no clinical performance figure.** No sensitivity, no specificity, no ROC, no cut-off,
  no classification of a single sample. The sensitivity/specificity figures quoted in the Results
  (78–94% / 88–93%) belong to a **cited copy-number study by other authors**, not to any WWOX
  measurement made here.
- **It does not validate either antibody against a genetic negative.** The immunohistochemistry
  negative control is an isotype-matched irrelevant antibody — a reagent control, not a target
  control. No knockout tissue, no knockdown tissue, no peptide competition. The knockdown cells
  existed in the same study and were not used this way.
- **It does not establish that the two antibodies agree.** Tissue and lysate are read with
  different reagents and the paper never compares them on the same material.
- **It says nothing about any body fluid.** Every matrix is tumour tissue or cultured cells. No
  blood, no plasma, no serum, no CSF, no urine, no buccal or skin-biopsy-from-a-living-patient
  surface is attempted or discussed.
- **It says nothing about brain, neurons, glia or any nervous-system tissue**, nothing about any
  WWOX variant or allele, nothing about epilepsy or neurodevelopment, and nothing about enzymatic
  activity or any WWOX substrate. WWOX appears 257 times in the body of this paper and **not once in
  a neurological context**.
- **It does not demonstrate restoration at protein level convincingly.** The uncropped blot behind
  the overexpression validation shows the empty-vector lane and the WWOX lane at comparable
  intensity, with two further unlabelled lanes on the same membrane comparable to both.

## 4 · Caveats the authors themselves give

- The study is a pilot and was not powered.
- "The limited number of primary MCTs evaluated in the current study precludes us from drawing
  conclusions about the expression of WWOX in high-risk MCTs."
- No outcome data accompanied the archival samples, so no prognostic value could be assessed.
- Blot variability between tumours is attributed by the authors to stromal, inflammatory and normal
  skin content in the lysate — i.e. **the matrix is a cell mixture and the assay does not resolve
  it**. The immunohistochemistry arm exists precisely because the blot cannot do this.
- The authors note WWOX's phenotypic consequence is cell-context dependent and use that to explain
  why their functional result disagrees with the human carcinoma literature. That sentence is
  simultaneously the transfer limit on everything in the paper.

## 5 · Classification under LEGEND_CORE §13

§13: a disease biomarker must measure the functional state of the target gene; Tier 1 is a direct
gene read-out (protein, mRNA, splice, enzymatic activity, immediate substrates); Tier 3 is a distal
clinical or neurophysiological endpoint and is not a gene biomarker at all.

**The modalities are Tier 1 by construction; the paper is not a biomarker study and is not an
endpoint study.** The distinction that matters here is *modality* versus *validated assay*:

- WWOX mRNA by comparative-Ct, WWOX protein by immunoblot and WWOX protein by immunohistochemistry
  are all **direct gene read-outs**, so each sits in the Tier 1 *class*.
- §13's second sentence is decisive: no candidate biomarker may be called validated without direct
  sensitivity/specificity evidence **in the disease population**. This paper supplies no
  sensitivity, no specificity, and no disease population — the population is canine cutaneous
  neoplasia. Therefore nothing in it is a validated biomarker for anything, and nothing in it may be
  carried as one.
- It is **not an endpoint** in any sense: no clinical, functional or neurophysiological outcome is
  measured, and the only outcome-shaped measurement (clonogenic survival after irradiation) is a
  cell-culture property of a tumour line.

Verdict: **Tier 1 modality demonstration; not a validated biomarker; not an endpoint.** The paper's
contribution to a restoration-spec is *negative and specific*: it shows what each of the three
direct read-outs costs and where each fails, in a setting where the investigators had unlimited
tissue and still could not get past ordinal scoring with fair agreement.

## 6 · Could it work in a living patient sample?

Taking the question literally and answering only from what this paper prints:

| Read-out | Could it run on a sample from a living patient? | What this paper shows about that |
|---|---|---|
| mRNA, comparative-Ct | Technically yes, on any nucleated cell | The panel shows case/control overlap at the single-sample level; a relative assay with no calibrator cannot report a patient's value, only a batch comparison |
| Protein, immunoblot | Yes, on a biopsy or cell pellet | Requires a three-hour exposure, resolves multiple species, normalises to a protein 4 kDa away on a reprobed membrane, and is destroyed by cell-mixture matrices |
| Protein, immunohistochemistry | Yes, on fixed biopsy tissue | Ordinal, by-eye, two readers, kappa 0.26–0.27 — "fair". This is the reliability ceiling the paper measured, in experienced hands |
| Enzymatic activity | **Not attempted** | WWOX's oxidoreductase activity is never measured anywhere in this paper |

The paper therefore **bounds** the restoration-spec question rather than advancing it: it shows that
the three obvious direct WWOX read-outs are all runnable, all relative, none calibrated, and the
most clinically deployable of them (tissue immunohistochemistry) carries only fair inter-rater
agreement when scored by two board-certified pathologists. A pharmacodynamic read-out that must
detect a *change* in WWOX after an intervention needs a precision this paper does not demonstrate
for any of them.

## 7 · Transfer limits — stated exactly

1. **Species.** Canine throughout, with two murine lines. The immunohistochemistry antiserum was
   raised against human/murine residues 12–94 and applied to dog on a stated homology argument
   (94.4% protein-level similarity overall, 96% across the epitope). That argument is about the
   *epitope*; it is not a validation, and it says nothing about the human CNS performance of either
   reagent.
2. **Lineage.** Mast cells and skin. Not neurons, not glia, not any neural tissue. The authors'
   own explanation for their negative functional result is that WWOX's effect depends on which
   partners a cell type expresses — which forbids transferring either the positive or the negative
   across lineage.
3. **Genetics.** Somatic reduction of a wild-type gene in tumour cells. Not a germline variant, not
   a missense allele, not a splice allele, not a zygosity statement. Nothing here transfers to
   P47T, Q230P, G372R, A141T or P252A, and nothing here is a statement about haploinsufficiency.
4. **"Function" means proliferation, viability and clonogenic survival.** It does not mean
   enzymatic activity, substrate turnover, protein interaction, or any neuronal property.
5. **The functional result is a null plus one dose point.** The one significant result is a single
   radiation dose in one cell line after knockdown. It may not be carried as a WWOX dose-response.
