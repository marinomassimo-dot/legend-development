## 1 · What the paper is

A narrative review of adult cardiac-arrest epinephrine: efficacy, dose, timing, route and comparison with vasopressin, organised in five tables of trials and cohorts. It is the safety-literature anchor of group H3: it says what the best-studied systemic epinephrine use does and does not buy.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Against placebo, epinephrine raises return of spontaneous circulation (odds ratio about 3.4) and admission; discharge survival differences are small (3.2 against 2.4 percent at 30 days in the large British trial) and neurological outcome shows no significant difference; the number needed to treat for one 30-day survivor is quoted as 112.
2. Observational cohorts show lower discharge survival and fewer good neurological outcomes with epinephrine.
3. Higher doses raise return of circulation but not discharge or neurological outcome; the guideline dose is 1 mg.
4. Early administration associates with better outcomes in cohorts; the placebo-trial secondary analysis shows the time effect in the placebo arm too.
5. Intraosseous and intravenous routes are similar; one before-and-after study of intramuscular dosing is positive; vasopressin is no better for neurological outcome.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- No registry record touches epinephrine; this review is unmodelled context.
- The review models a general pattern the laboratory should keep: a short-term physiological end-point (return of circulation) can move strongly while the outcome that matters (neurological function) does not. That is an analogy for any adrenergic claim in WWOX (seizure counts or glucose against development); it is not a finding about WWOX.
- Methodological: the stated inclusion rules do not match the tabulated designs, and one observational study is called placebo-controlled; the tables should be read with the underlying design in mind, not the review's wording.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Any WWOX, neonatal, paediatric-genetic or seizure statement: adult cardiac arrest only; the paediatric route (endotracheal in infants) appears in one paragraph.
- Epinephrine causation of poor neurological outcome: confounding by indication and resuscitation duration is not addressed.
- A dose, timing or route recommendation for anyone; the review reports trials, not guidance.
