## 1 · What the paper establishes

**Design.** Two-stage targeted association across a 12.4 Mb linkage region on 16q23-q24. Stage 1: 1,318 tag SNPs in
50 Finnish dyslipidemic families. Stage 2: 25 SNPs in Dutch FCHL and French-Canadian families plus 475
European-descended low-HDL-C cases and controls. Then Mexican FCHL families, the METSIM cohort (4,463 males) and
the prospective Young Finns cohort (1,561 with complete four-timepoint data). **9,798 subjects in total.**

| Result | Value | Type |
|---|---|---|
| rs2548861 (WWOX intron 8), overall combined | p = 6.9 × 10⁻⁷, dominant model | `DATO` |
| Case-control effect | OR 2.0 (1.4–3.0); one T allele ≈ **+17%** probability of low HDL-C | `DATO` |
| Young Finns variance explained | **1.5%** | `DATO` |
| Longitudinal, four measurements over 21 years | RANOVA p = 0.003 | `DATO` |
| Population effect sizes | **−0.08 to −0.18 SD** | `DATO` |
| Functional | 691 bp region acts as a cis-regulatory element in HepG2; rs2548861 has an allele-specific effect (p = 0.0004, 5.3 × 10⁻⁶) | `DATO`, in vitro |
| EMSA | Nuclear-factor complex on both alleles; **preferential for the nonrisk G allele** | `DATO`, semi-quantitative |

**The multiple-testing handling is sound.** Bonferroni over 1,318 stage-1 SNPs plus 25 SNPs × 2 models = 1,368
tests, which is the number they actually use. 50,000 permutations and 50,000 gene-dropping replicates on top.

**The haplotype confound is properly controlled.** The reporter constructs are two-SNP haplotypes of
rs2738572–rs2548861. H2 (A-T) and H3 (C-T) share the risk allele and differ at the neighbour, and do not differ in
activity (p = 0.3) — which licenses attributing the effect to rs2548861 rather than to rs2738572.

## 2 · What it does **not** support — the product, not the residue

**2.1 · It does not support "WWOX controls HDL-C."** The authors say so themselves: *"this SNP rs2548861 alone is not
a major HDL-C determinant but rather that it is a modifier variant."* 1.5% of variance.

**2.2 · It does not identify a causal variant.** rs2548861 is a **tag** capturing rs2738577 (r² = 0.93) and
rs2550606 (r² = 0.96), neither separately genotyped. The functional work was performed on rs2548861 by choice of
tag, not by genetic resolution.

**2.3 · The subset-linkage claim is circular by construction.** "rs2548861 explains much of the linkage" rests on
splitting 62 families into associated/unassociated **by whether they contribute positively to the association
statistic**, then showing the associated subset carries the linkage. It is a published method and cited as such, so
this is a bounded interpretive limit, not an error — but the defensible reading of Figure S1 is *"linkage and
association are carried by overlapping families"*, not *"the SNP explains the linkage."* The authors themselves
declare a residual shoulder at 105–110 cM.

**2.4 · One arm of the central functional experiment is undisclosed.** Methods: constructs tested *"in the forward
and reverse orientation."* Results: reduction against empty vector reported only *"in the reverse orientation."*
What the forward constructs did is never stated, shown or discussed.
`PREMISE: DATO` (both sentences quoted). `REVIVAL_TRIGGER`: any later publication or dataset from this group
reporting the forward-orientation values.

**2.5 · The p = 0.008 cis-regulatory anchor rests on a violated test assumption.** Methods declare a two-tailed
t test *"under an assumption of equal variance"* (n = 6, SD bars); Figure 3A's empty-vector bar carries ≈ ±4.2
against ≈ ±0.3 on the constructs. Visible only across two surfaces, which is why it is modelled as a coupled
panel→text contradiction. **The allelic comparisons are unaffected** — this narrows one claim and leaves the
paper's headline result standing.

**2.6 · It establishes nothing about brain, development, or a biallelic genotype.** The entire functional arm is
HepG2. The genetic arm is adult and adolescent lipid phenotype.

## 4 · The surface, and why the PDF could not carry a quote

No XML exists (`efetch`: *"The publisher of this article does not allow downloading of the full text in XML form"*;
Europe PMC OA endpoints refuse it as not open access). The Elsevier PDF text layer is corrupt in the exact rule-5d
pattern, and the corruption map was decoded **quantitatively** against the clean HTML:

| PDF layer | Page / HTML | Count match |
|---|---|---|
| `U+0002` | `−` (minus) | **74 ↔ 74** |
| `U+0003` | `°` | **5 ↔ 5** |
| `¼` | `=` | 65 occurrences |
| `3` | `×` | 12 in `× 10` constructions |
| `b` / `0` | `β` / `′` | — |

So the page's `p = 6.9 × 10⁻⁷` extracts as `p ¼ 6.9 3 10⁻⁷`. **Four of the six substitutions are printable**, so no
control-character screen sees them. The HTML is the declared text surface: zero controls, correct entities.

## 5 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype

> **`ESPANSIONE`, and deliberately not more.** This is a **common regulatory modifier allele** producing a **small,
> dominant, quantitative** shift in a plasma lipid trait in adults and adolescents. The reference genotype is a
> **biallelic loss-of-function neurodevelopmental** genotype. These are different objects, and no dosage, direction
> or mechanism transfers between them.

What legitimately crosses is narrower and worth having:

1. **`INFERENZA` — WWOX dosage has measurable consequences in humans outside the nervous system, at population
   scale.** 9,798 subjects, replicated across ascertainments. This is the corpus's **only** human quantitative WWOX
   phenotype outside cancer and outside neurodevelopment.
2. **`ESPANSIONE` — WWOX has a regulatory architecture, and this corpus has never opened it.** Measured: **1 of 68**
   deep-dive manifests mentions cis-regulatory function, an enhancer or intron 8 — and it is this paper's own.
   Every mechanism read so far is protein-level. Two functional elements and two independent association signals sit
   in intron 8.
3. **`DATO` (corroborative only) — WWOX transcript is present in human brain**, on a 2008 endpoint RT-PCR gel that
   modern atlases supersede. Value is that the paper's own text omits it.
4. **A negative worth keeping:** the single common nonsynonymous WWOX variant found by resequencing (rs12918952,
   Ala179Val, MAF 0.44) shows **no** HDL-C association (p = 0.63), and none of the 21 sequenced variants is in LD
   with rs2548861. Coding variation does not explain this locus.

**What must not be carried:** that the T risk allele "lowers WWOX". The constructs *reduce* reporter output relative
to empty vector, so in this assay the element is repressive and the risk allele more so — but whether that
corresponds to more or less WWOX protein in vivo is **not established**. Any such statement is `IPOTESI`.
