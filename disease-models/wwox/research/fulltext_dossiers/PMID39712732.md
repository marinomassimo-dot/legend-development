## What it is

A small hospital-based case-control study (50 first-febrile-seizure children, 100 febrile controls, 6-60 months) comparing admission blood counts and iron indices. WWOX: zero occurrences. No figures, no supplement.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** Febrile seizures in neurologically normal children; developmental delay was an exclusion criterion, so a DEE child would not have been eligible.

## What it measures (DATO)

Cases had lower haemoglobin (8.62 versus 9.49 g/dL), lower mean corpuscular volume, higher red cell distribution width and total iron-binding capacity, lower ferritin (49.84 versus 80.64 ng/mL) and transferrin saturation, and 62 versus 16 percent labelled iron deficiency anaemia.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No causal or "correctable risk factor" claim.** A single admission blood draw in a case-control design with convenience sampling cannot show that anaemia precedes or lowers the seizure threshold, or that iron treatment prevents seizures; no intervention arm exists.
2. **The comparison group is itself anaemic.** Mean haemoglobin was below 11 g/dL in both groups and 85 percent of febrile controls were anaemic; the contrast is a more-versus-less anaemic comparison, not anaemic versus healthy.
3. **The headline odds is fragile.** Anaemic versus not among 150 children gives OR 4.24 with a 95 percent interval of 0.93-19.3 (Woolf method, computed here from Table 3; two non-anaemic cases), while the paper states "four times higher" without an interval and with a chi-square p of 0.045. INFERENZA about the interval, DATO about the counts.
4. **Confounding unaddressed.** Cases were older and heavier, controls had the higher temperature (contrary to the usual febrile-seizure pattern), no multivariable adjustment; ferritin is an acute-phase reactant measured during fever with no inflammatory marker, and the iron-deficiency anaemia label is not defined (mean case ferritin was 49.84 ng/mL).
5. **Internal inconsistencies:** control anaemia 85 percent (Results, Table 3) versus 86 percent (Discussion); duplicated variable names in Methods; a Table 1 row label repeated; a keyword ("oligodendrocyte") for a topic not measured; the Disclosures give ethics approval as "NA".
6. **Not about iron supplementation or transfusion** in any child.
