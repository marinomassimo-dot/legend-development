## What it is

A PROSPERO-registered meta-analysis of seven studies (four RCTs, three observational) of cerebral tissue oxygenation (crSO2) and cerebral fractional oxygen extraction (cFTOE) before and after red-cell transfusion in preterm infants, split into restrictive and liberal haemoglobin-threshold subgroups. WWOX: zero occurrences.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** Preterm infants with anaemia of prematurity.

## What it measures (DATO)

crSO2 rose 6.40 points (restrictive, 357 transfusion events) and 2.75 (liberal, 220); cFTOE fell 0.08 and 0.03; subgroup tests p = 0.04 and p below 0.001; heterogeneity I-squared 0.93 for crSO2. GRADE: very low certainty throughout.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **The headline subgroup contrast is not a randomised comparison.** The only study that contrasts thresholds inside one randomised design (the TOP arms) shows 4.48 versus 4.84 points, no difference. The pooled gap comes from between-study differences: the restrictive subgroup contains small-variance single-centre observational studies with large rises (Smith 8.5 and 10.7, Van Hoften 11.7), and the liberal subgroup is two studies, one with a baseline crSO2 of 77-78 percent (little room to rise) and rows of 3.55 and 0.67. Baseline-ceiling confounding is plausible (INFERENZA); the authors acknowledge cross-study heterogeneity but the conclusion line reads "enhanced physiological benefit".
2. **Subgroup assignment is contestable (cross-document reading).** Aktas is classed restrictive with thresholds 12, 11 and 9 g/dL by week, which equal or exceed the TOP high-threshold values for infants without respiratory support in weeks 1 and 2 that I read in the TOP NIRS supplement (12.0 and 11.0), and it shows the smallest rise (1.32). INFERENZA from two documents; the review's own definition ranges overlap (10-13 versus 7-11).
3. **Unit of analysis is the transfusion event, not the infant;** Pellegrino's 22 events come from 8 infants; sub-arms are entered as separate rows, so seven studies appear as eleven rows; sensitivity analyses by infant are said to agree.
4. **Certainty is stated inconsistently:** Results say low, the Limitations and supplement GRADE say very low.
5. **No clinical benefit shown.** The authors cite a trial in which NIRS-guided treatment reduced cerebral hypoxia by 58 percent without improving death or brain injury, and cited data that the rise may be gone by 24 hours. Neither crSO2 nor cFTOE change is shown to predict outcome here.
6. **Nothing about a brain with abnormal oxygen utilisation or about any genetic disease;** the readout (crSO2/cFTOE) answers delivery-versus-consumption balance and is device-dependent (four monitor types, five sensor settings).

## Design and limits

Meta-analysis with random effects in SPSS, no publication-bias test (n = 7), observational studies with serious confounding bias, two RoB-2 studies with concerns or high risk.
