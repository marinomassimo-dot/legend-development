## What the paper did

Docking, vesicle sedimentation and SPR of the DVL2 PDZ domain with different sterols; sterol-synthesis inhibitors (AY9944, statins, U18666A) and cholesterol depletion in HEK293T, including an endogenously tagged DVL2-mEGFP line; FoxK2 co-IP and a PDZ inhibitor; TurboID-DVL2 proximity proteomics; human iPSC-derived neural stem cells (control and Smith-Lemli-Opitz) and cortex from a Dhcr7 mutant mouse.

## WWOX content (all of it)

- One discussion sentence: silencing of Wwox increases nuclear DVL2 in head and neck cancer, citing ref 79 (Celebi 2020, *J Cancer*, PMID 32368285). WWOX is not measured or manipulated anywhere.
- The TurboID dataset (Table S3, about 3,100 protein groups including background) contains no WWOX protein group. This is an absence of detection in HEK293T, not evidence that DVL2 and WWOX do not interact.

## The finding that bears on LEGEND's Wnt reasoning

- Sterol inhibition moved DVL2 from the membrane to the **nucleus** (HEK293T, NSCs, mouse cortex), and **in the same conditions a TCF/LEF reporter showed canonical Wnt signalling inhibited**, not increased (Figure S6E: Wnt-conditioned-medium induction about 125-fold in standard medium against about 12–20-fold under AY9944, simvastatin or U18666A; three biological replicates; no statistics printed on the panel). The authors read this as nuclear DVL acting independently of β-catenin.
- So in this system nuclear DVL2 accumulation and canonical Wnt hyperactivation come apart. That does not test WWOX, but it weakens any inference that runs "DVL2 in the nucleus, therefore Wnt/β-catenin is hyperactive".

## What the paper does NOT state

- Anything about WWOX beyond the one cited sentence.
- That the DVL2–p53 relation is co-localisation: it is a STRING-integrated network (Figure 7C) and a single representative co-IP (Figure 7D) whose input lane is itself stronger in the treated condition.
