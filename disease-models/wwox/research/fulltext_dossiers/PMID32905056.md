## 1 · What the paper is

One 20-month-old boy with HMGCS2 deficiency (a ketone-synthesis disorder) who presented with encephalopathy, hepatomegaly and severe high anion-gap acidosis after a short febrile illness, and was NOT hypoglycaemic (4.6 mmol/L) before dextrose. The paper adds urine organic-acid markers (4HMP, 3-hydroxyglutarate, others) and raised triglycerides, and argues hypoglycaemia is not a defining feature of the crisis.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Glucose 4.6 mmol/L at presentation after two days of vomiting; serum ketone bodies and lactate normal, marked ketonuria; pH 6.8; mild hyperammonaemia; triglycerides 34 mmol/L.
2. Dextrose 10 percent started after first sampling; acidosis resolved at 13 hours; full neurological recovery by day 4; hepatomegaly resolved by day 28.
3. Urine: dicarboxylic aciduria, 4HMP, glutarate, 3-hydroxyglutarate; 4HMP persisted at one year.
4. The mechanism of normoglycaemia is called unclear; the child also had fever, EBV, adenovirus and rhinovirus, colloidal silver, and later features suggestive of haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- The registry holds no adrenergic or counter-regulatory record. The paper does not touch a claim; it informs a question.
- What the paper does is separate two things the null-mouse hypoglycaemia story tends to merge: a block in fuel supply (here, ketogenesis) can coexist with normal glucose in a sick, stressed child. It therefore cannot be used as a counter-regulation datum, because counter-regulation was not measured: no insulin, cortisol, catecholamine, growth hormone or free fatty acid result is given.
- The one-patient observation that glucose was normal during a severe febrile crisis is compatible with a stress-hormone-driven rise in glucose (IPOTESI, unmeasured) and equally with intact hepatic glucose output.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Any WWOX statement, or any statement about the null mouse (human enzymopathy of a different pathway).
- A mechanism for normoglycaemia (the authors say it is unclear).
- A role for adrenaline or any counter-regulatory hormone (not measured, not discussed).
- A population rate: the claim "third case without hypoglycaemia" rests on three reports of 29, one of which had glucose sampled after intravenous fluids.
- Cerebroprotection by ketonuria (hypothesis only; the patient also received maximal dextrose).

## 5 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype (WWOX-DEE)

ESPANSIONE, thin. System gap: human toddler, hepatic ketogenesis defect, acute crisis. The transferable lesson is methodological: in a lethal-hypoglycaemia model the useful panel is glucose with insulin, cortisol, catecholamines, free fatty acids and ketones at the same time point; a glucose value alone is silent on counter-regulation.
