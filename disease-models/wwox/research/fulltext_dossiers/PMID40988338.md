## What the paper did

A codon-optimised human *SYNGAP1-Aα1* cDNA with a C-terminal 3×FLAG tag, driven by the pan-neuronal
hSyn1 promoter with WPRE3 and a shortened poly(A), **5.136 kb ITR-to-ITR** — above the nominal
~4.7 kb AAV limit — was packaged in AAV-PHP.eB. In vitro: GAP activity against H-Ras G12V, PSD-95
phase separation, dendritic localisation. In vivo: bilateral ICV at P2 (1 × 10^11 vg) or retro-orbital
at P21 (1 × 10^11 / 3.16 × 10^11 / 1 × 10^12 vg) in CMV-Cre;*Syngap1*^+/fl mice, followed by open
field and elevated zero maze in adults and then EEG/EMG.

## What the paper states

- The oversized genome packages: a prominent gel band, TEM full/partial 94.60% versus 5.40% empty
  (Table S1, six images), and a two-fold lower yield than a 4.617 kb control virus produced the same
  day (2.78 versus 4.25 relative yield, Table S2).
- The model expresses ~55% of wild-type SynGAP in hippocampus — a **heterozygote, not a null**.
- P21 retro-orbital mid and high doses restored total SynGAP to wild-type levels (1.114 and 1.087 of
  non-injected +/+) and reduced parietal interictal spikes from 112 ± 13 to 8 ± 4 and 8 ± 2 per hour;
  they corrected hyperactivity and nose-poking dose-dependently and normalised FFT band power.
- P2 ICV at 1 × 10^11 vg raised hippocampal SynGAP only to ~0.69 of wild type (not significant),
  reduced spikes to 36 ± 9 per hour, and **failed on every behavioural measure**.
- A ceiling: FLAG signal rose ~2.5-fold from mid to high dose with no further increase in total
  SynGAP, "suggesting a ceiling effect that limits overexpression in vivo".
- Weight-normalised, the doses are 3 × 10^13–1 × 10^14 vg/kg (P21) and ~5 × 10^13–1 × 10^14 vg/kg (P2),
  which the authors say is "within the range used clinically" for Zolgensma.
- Generalised tonic-clonic seizures were not abolished at any dose or age; the GTC analysis was
  underpowered.

## What the paper does NOT state

- It does not separate **age** from **dose and distribution**. The neonatal arm was given one dose
  only, the lowest, by a route that produced forebrain-biased expression; the juvenile arm that
  worked used 3–10× more vector and achieved brain-wide spread. The authors name both confounds but
  run no experiment that isolates either. The paper therefore does **not** show that a later window
  is better, and does not show that an early window is sufficient.
- It does not test a null or homozygous model, and does not report what expression level a null would
  need. All rescue is relative to a 55%-of-wild-type baseline.
- It reports no overexpression-toxicity endpoint: no histopathology, no survival, no peripheral-organ
  assessment. The authors state that ectopic peripheral expression "warrants investigation in future
  preclinical studies" and that "more detailed and long-range studies are required".
- It reports no immune endpoint at all — no anti-capsid antibody, no T-cell assay, no neuro-
  inflammation measure — although the transgene carries a 3×FLAG tag and the protein is human in a
  mouse.
- It does not report vector genome copy number per cell in any tissue.
- The behavioural battery is two tests (open field, elevated zero maze): no learning, memory, social
  or motor-coordination endpoint, so "cognitive" benefit is inferred from spike reduction and EEG
  band power, not measured.
- Data are available only "from the corresponding author upon request"; no deposited dataset.
- It says nothing about *WWOX*.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

Direct-adapter ONT sequencing may overrepresent short species; supraphysiological expression
"remains an important consideration" and prior work shows overexpression alters synaptic structure;
only one isoform was delivered, which may limit neonatal efficacy; a single-isoform, pan-neuronal
design may be inferior to excitatory-neuron targeting (they cite the Dravet precedent where
restricting *SCN1A* supplementation to inhibitory neurons improved safety and efficacy); the GTC
analysis was underpowered. The work was funded by a sponsored research agreement with a company,
four authors are founders of a gene-therapy company, the authors hold a provisional patent on these
vectors, and generative AI was used to draft manuscript sections.

## Two caveats found by reading the supplement, not stated in the body

1. **Dose-associated surgical attrition, never reported as an adverse outcome.** Methods say "half of
   the mice that received the high-dose … also showed poor recovery after the headmount implantation
   surgery". Table S3 quantifies it: high dose 5 of 10 implanted had poor surgery outcomes and only 5
   were recorded, against 0 of 8 (low dose), 0 of 4 (mid dose), 2 of 11 (P2 ICV) and 1 of 11
   (non-injected +/fl). The high-dose EEG and FFT data therefore come from the surviving half of that
   group, and the Discussion's safety paragraph does not mention this at all.
2. **The packaging claim is a size bin, and a sixth of the DNA is oversized.** The body reports "a
   prominent band at ∼5.1 kb … approximately 75.81% of total DNA signal". Figure S1C shows that
   75.81% is the **4–6 kb bin**, with 8.96% at 3 kb and **15.22% above 6 kb** (99.99% in all). The
   body text mentions truncated and snapback species but never the >6 kb fraction.

Group sizes for the rescue claim are small: mid dose n = 4 recorded, high dose n = 5 recorded.
