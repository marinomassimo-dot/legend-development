## What it is

A descriptive single-centre cohort of 22 children (median 104 months) who received therapeutic plasma exchange for neuroimmunological disease in one paediatric intensive care unit over five years: 6 Guillain-Barre syndrome, 6 autoimmune encephalitis, 4 infection-triggered encephalopathy (acute necrotising encephalopathy and similar), 3 MOGAD, 2 ADEM, 1 MERS. Median 7 sessions, started a median of 9 days from onset. WWOX: zero occurrences; no genetic or developmental encephalopathy cohort.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** Plasma exchange here is an immune-clearance treatment for antibody- or cytokine-mediated disease; the cohort contains no genetic DEE.

## What it reports (DATO)

- 14 of 22 (63.6 percent) favourable outcome at discharge (PCPC below 3), 8 unfavourable including 3 deaths (13.6 percent); 9 children ventilated (3 to 77 days); 4 needed vasoactive drugs; "no major TPE-related complications".
- Replacement fluid was fresh frozen plasma and/or 5 percent albumin, plasma predominant; the per-patient fluid is not given.
- Supplementary Table S3: lower admission GCS and more ventilation in the unfavourable group (p 0.016, 0.014).

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Nothing about WWOX or a genetic DEE;** no genetic encephalopathy was studied, and the paper cannot inform plasma exchange in a WWOX-deficient child (H2 questions 1, 2, 5: not testable here).
2. **No efficacy or safety inference.** The authors say so: no control group, every child also received steroids and/or IVIG (some rituximab), the decision to exchange was not protocolised, outcome is discharge-only. Their own wording: not "a measure of TPE efficacy".
3. **The 63.6 percent favourable figure is a case-mix number.** By Table 2: the six GBS children were all favourable; of the 16 others 8 were favourable (50 percent); of the four infection-triggered encephalopathy children none was (two deaths, two with sequelae). INFERENZA from DATO rows.
4. **Text versus table.** The Discussion says most autoimmune-encephalitis patients had unfavourable discharge outcomes; Table 2 shows 3 of 6 (patients 3, 4, 8), that is half. "Mortality confined to severe encephalopathic syndromes": of the three deaths, two are infection-triggered encephalopathy and one is anti-NMDAR autoimmune encephalitis.
5. **Table 3 arithmetic.** The percentages 5.9, 11.8 and 17.6 correspond to a denominator of 17 (1/17, 2/17, 3/17), while the text and the row label give 16 tested; the CSF microbiological row shows 2 (17.6) although 2 of 16 is 12.5 and 2 of 17 is 11.8. The counts themselves agree with Supplementary Table S1.
6. **Replacement fluid is not analysed.** Plasma versus albumin cannot be compared: fluid per patient is not tabulated, plasma was predominant, so every exchange is also a donor-plasma exposure; the paper reports no coagulation, allergic, infectious or catheter events, and no complication definition.
7. **The mechanism in the Introduction (removal of autoantibodies, complement and cytokines) is a premise.** No antibody titre, complement or cytokine is measured before or after exchange in this cohort.
8. **Selection and exploratory testing.** Seven unadjusted exploratory comparisons (S3), two nominally below 0.05, ventilation is itself part of illness severity.
9. **Retrospective single-centre cohort with data on request only;** ethics approval is dated after the study period (fine for a retrospective review but noted).
