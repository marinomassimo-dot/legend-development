## What it is

A perspective review from the originating laboratory. **It performs no experiment.** Its seven figures are
schematics and republished panels of the group's own earlier work.

## What it states about the hierarchy

Two statements, two strengths, both in the same article:

- Section 11: *"We determined that Zfra overrides WWOX in suppressing cancer growth and the progression of
  neurodegeneration."*
- Section 11.2: *"Zfra may override WWOX deficiency in restoring normal physiological functions."*

The three "routes" the review then lists are mechanisms, not a comparison: (i) Zfra4-10 / WWOX7-21 / HAson8
activate Hyal-2+ CD3− CD19− "Z cells"; (ii) the peptides drive intracellular WWOX–target complexes;
(iii) Zfra conjugates targets covalently for ubiquitin-independent degradation ("zfration"). **No experiment
in this article, and none it cites, compares a WWOX-restoration arm against a Zfra arm in the same model**,
which is what "overrides" would need. The one head-to-head datum it does carry is the opposite kind: Zfra4-10
and WWOX7-21 *given together* lose the antitumour effect each has alone, which the authors read as mutual
antagonism by covalent binding.

## What it adds on the organelle axis

- pY33-WWOX is placed on the outer mitochondrial membrane in normal physiology and as the agent of
  mitochondrial apoptosis under stress.
- *"WWOX becomes Y33-phosphorylated in response to high glucose, which leads to mitochondrial apoptosis"*,
  cited to an **independent** laboratory in a **neuronal** system (PMID 35984507, Carvalho et al. 2022, in
  which inhibiting WWOX with Zfra1-31 restores mitochondrial homeostasis and viability). This is the same
  direction as PMID 41677633 — removing or blocking WWOX function protects the mitochondrion under a
  metabolic stress — reached by a different group in a different system.
- Reference 31 is *"WWOX promotes apoptosis and inhibits autophagy in paclitaxel-treated ovarian carcinoma
  cells"* (PMID 33300063) — a second laboratory on the same side of the autophagy contradiction as
  PMID 24008736.
- `pT12-WWOX` aggregates are reported in the cortex of **heterozygous** *Wwox* mice but not wild-type — cited,
  not shown here, and the only dose-sensitive observation in the article.
- `pS14-WWOX` is said to support disease progression; `pT12` is speculated to nullify `pS14`.

## What it does NOT state

- No measurement, no n, no statistic of its own.
- No endpoint on lysosome, autophagic flux, ROS or ΔΨm measured here.
- No human WWOX genotype class; no missense, no splice allele.
- It does not say how the "override" would behave where WWOX protein is absent rather than functionally
  misdirected — the case the WWOX-DEE genotype class actually presents. The closest sentence is conditional
  ("may override WWOX deficiency").

## Caveats the authors themselves give

"The turnover rate of WWOX-regulated protein complexes is unknown"; "How pT12-WWOX becomes aggregated is
unknown"; "Whether overly polymerized Zfra blocks neurodegeneration is unknown"; "The activation markers for
Z cells are being determined".
