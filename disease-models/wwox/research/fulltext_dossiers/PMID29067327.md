## Is this a WWOX model at all? — No.

The treated animals are **3×Tg-AD** (`B6;129-Psen1tm1Mpm Tg[APPSwe,tauP301L]1Lfa/Mmjax`, Jackson), i.e. `Psen1` M146V + APPswe + tau P301L, with **wild-type `Wwox`**. There is no WWOX allele anywhere in the intervention cohort. WWOX enters only as (a) a phospho-epitope read-out, pS14-WWOX, in a *melanoma* neurodegeneration model in nude mice, and (b) one supplementary bar chart.

## The one WWOX-genotype datum, now inspected (FT-104 discharge)

Supplementary Figure 1 was extracted from `mmc1.docx` (`word/media/image1.tiff`) and rendered. **Its numbers do not reproduce its own legend.**

- Legend: "Both short-term and long-term memories for the *Wwox+/−* mice have dropped greater than 55 % at ages 10–12 (n = 5), whereas 3xTg mice exhibited decline for less than 50 %."
- Figure: two panels, x-axis **"3, >10, 8, 10 Month"**, y-axis "% Exploration time", a red 50 % line and a red vertical divider. **No genotype label appears on either axis.** There is **no age 12** anywhere. No n per bar, no error convention stated, no statistical test.
- Reading the bars: short-term ≈ 78 → 45 and ≈ 75 → 65; long-term ≈ 87 → 42 and ≈ 75 → 50. Whichever pair is assigned to which genotype, the drops are ≈ 43 % / 13 % and ≈ 52 % / 33 %. **No arrangement yields "greater than 55 %".**

So the claim that `Wwox+/−` mice decline faster than 3×Tg — which B3, B6 and the wider corpus all cite to this paper — rests on an unlabelled, unquantified supplementary panel that does not support the number its own legend states.

## What the paper does state, and what it does not

States: Zfra4–10 improves 24-h novel-object recognition (p = .008) and probe-test target-quadrant time (p < .001); reduces Aβ-positive cells, thioflavin-S plaques, pS412-tau, pT181-tau tangles, pS35-TPC6AΔ and SH3GLB2 aggregates; does **not** change swim speed or escape latency; does **not** induce neurogenesis (an earned negative the authors report against themselves).

Does not state: any mechanism of CNS delivery — "Z cells did not appear to migrate to the brain", and the authors write that they "do not exclude the possibility" that a little peptide crosses the blood–brain barrier. Efficacy also fails when treatment starts at 12 months, which is the authors' own ceiling.

## Transfer limit

A peptide that reduces aggregate load in a tau-P301L/APPswe mouse at 10 months, by an unexplained route, with unblinded readouts and field-level statistics, is **not** evidence for a congenital biallelic WWOX loss in a human infant. The two models differ in the gene, in the lesion and in the developmental window; and the paper's own 12-month failure shows the effect is window-dependent in the direction that makes the transfer harder, not easier.
