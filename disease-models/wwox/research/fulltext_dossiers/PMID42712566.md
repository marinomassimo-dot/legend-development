## What it is

**A protocol, not a review.** The selection file lists it as "a systematic review"; the PubMed title ends "a systematic review protocol" and the body states it. It describes a planned review (PROSPERO registration cited) of iron supplementation in pregnancy or in children aged 0-3 years against myelination and neurodevelopmental outcomes. There are no included studies, no results, no figure and no limitations section.

## WWOX content

None (zero occurrences in the XML). There is **no WWOX-deficient evidence** here, and there is no iron evidence of any kind in the paper: the Introduction's statements about iron and myelination are cited premises (references 3-17), not findings.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **It does not answer question 2 of H1.** Whether hypomyelination in a WWOX-null model is modifiable by iron status is not addressed by a plan to search for studies. Not WWOX evidence; not evidence of any effect of iron on myelination.
2. **A DEE population would be excluded.** The eligibility text excludes participants with significant congenital neurological abnormalities unless outcomes are reported separately. If the review is carried out as written, it will probably say nothing about a genetic encephalopathy cohort; any transfer to WOREE/SCAR12 would be an extrapolation from iron-deficient, otherwise typically developing infants.
3. **The outcome frame is population nutrition**, doses and formulations of oral iron, not intravenous iron, transfusion, or iron handling in a mitochondrially stressed brain.
4. **Nothing is said about ferroptosis, iron overload mechanisms or neural cell death**; "iron overload or toxicity" appears only as an adverse-event category.

## Design and limits

Protocol of a systematic review (PRISMA 2020, Cochrane; ROB 2 and ROBINS-I; GRADE). Seven databases plus registries and grey literature. Observational and quasi-experimental designs are admitted "because of the anticipated limited availability of high-quality randomized evidence" (Methods). No timeline for completion is given.
