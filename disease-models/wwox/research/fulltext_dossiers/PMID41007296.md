## 1 · What the paper measures

TCGA level-3 bulk RNA-seq and clinical data for breast cancer (basal, HER2, luminal A, luminal B), hepatocellular carcinoma (371), glioblastoma (159) and low-grade glioma (515); patients split by an optimised cutpoint of the WWOX/HIF1A mRNA ratio; Kaplan-Meier, differential expression between the two groups (FDR below 0.05), KEGG and GO mapping, and, per cohort, one multivariable Cox model (ratio, age, stage, a gene list). No WWOX manipulation, no protein, no metabolite, no normal tissue, no neural non-tumour tissue.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Direction by tumour type: higher ratio is good prognosis in basal, HER2, HCC, GBM and LGG; higher ratio is bad in luminal A and B (stated as paradoxical).
2. In the GBM favourable group both WWOX is higher and HIF1A lower (log2 fold change 0.66 and -0.79), so the ratio does not separate WWOX from HIF1A.
3. The multivariable models: ratio not significant in basal (95 patients, 11 events; WWOX itself is, p 0.034), HER2 (57, 10), luminal B (119, 14; HR 7.92, CI 0.37 to 168.6), HCC (370, 89; HR 0.58, p 0.26), GBM (158, 106; HR 56.1, CI 0.03 to 99,080), LGG (515, 92; HR 0.54, CI 0.03 to 8.51); significant only in luminal A (216, 18; HR 2.76, p 0.047) with the opposite sign. Several printed HRs are numerically degenerate (1.18e-13, 3.72e-12).
4. The Discussion concedes the independent prognostic value did not reach significance in most cohorts; the Abstract and Conclusions call the ratio a robust prognostic marker.
5. The Conclusions state WWOX loss triggers HIF1 alpha stabilisation and metabolic rewiring in these tumours; the analysis is transcript-only.
6. Supplementary Table S1: cutpoint groups are very unequal (GBM 63 and 96; LGG 40 and 475; HER2 47 and 11), and the GBM disease-status field is extremely skewed between them (below the cutpoint 53 with tumour and 4 without; above 9 and 77).

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- The paper extends the group's ratio idea (2021 leukocytes, 2022 fibroblasts, 2022 review) into TCGA. It is the same laboratory, so it adds a fourth non-independent member to an arc the audit placed at hypothesis strength. In the 2021 reading the ratio's correlations were arithmetic coupling to HIF1A; here the GBM result repeats the pattern (WWOX up and HIF1A down in the favourable group).
- For glioma, the nearest neural setting in the arc: favourable survival goes with a high ratio, but the ratio is not independently prognostic, the groups are very unequal, and the GBM tumour-status skew raises a purity or normal-tissue explanation (INFERENZA; not tested in the paper). It says nothing about WWOX loss in normal developing brain.
- Selection question 1 (is the glycolytic shift measurable in a neural cell): this paper does not measure any glycolytic quantity; pathway language is inferred from mRNA lists. The answer remains no.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- The ratio as an independent, robust prognostic marker (its own models).
- HIF1 alpha stabilisation, glycolysis or oxidative phosphorylation changes (no protein or metabolite).
- Any transfer to WWOX loss of function in non-malignant tissue or development.
- Causal direction: ratio groups are post hoc cutpoint strata of public data.

## 5 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype (WWOX-DEE)

ESPANSIONE, with a negative: in a neural-lineage tumour, the ratio's apparent prognostic signal is not independent of age and does not distinguish WWOX from HIF1A. The paper adds nothing to the tissue-gap listed in the audit.
