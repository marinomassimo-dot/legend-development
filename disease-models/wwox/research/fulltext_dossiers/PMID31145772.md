## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Case-control: WWOX rs17797882 risk allele (T) frequency 0.245 in cases and 0.230 in controls, adjusted OR 1.054 (0.891-1.247), p 0.541 (Table 3). Only KCNK16 rs1535500 and GLIS3 rs7041847 reach p below 0.05; the other six, WWOX included, do not. The GRS of 8 SNPs: adjusted OR 1.065 (1.021-1.111), p 0.004.
2. Quantitative traits (Tables 5 and 6, controls only, adjusted for age, sex, log BMI): WWOX rs17797882 T allele associates with lower HOMA-beta (coefficient -29.231, p 0.014), higher log triglycerides (0.119, p 0.014) and higher HDL-cholesterol (0.037, p 0.042); fasting glucose, HbA1c, HOMA-IR, total and LDL cholesterol are null (p 0.309 to 0.918).
3. Internal inconsistencies: the text prints the HOMA-beta standard error as 11.961 while Table 5 prints 11.877 (11.961 is the CMIP row's value); the Table 5 footnote says values were log-transformed, yet the HOMA-beta coefficients are on a raw scale (-29.231, 8.521, 16.485) against fasting-glucose coefficients of 0.023 or -0.045. The HOMA-beta effect size is not interpretable as printed.
4. The Discussion says the loci "increased insulin resistance in the Chinese population"; Table 5 shows no SNP and not the GRS associated with HOMA-IR (smallest p 0.347).
5. Cases are older (47 versus 42 years) and heavier (BMI 26.7 versus 23.1) than controls; adjusted, not matched. Figure 1, labelled "Prevalence of T2DM (%)" by GRS, cannot be a prevalence in a 1000/1000 sample, is non-monotonic at low GRS (GRS 4 below GRS 3) and shows no per-bar n.
6. No multiple-testing rule is stated (eight SNPs by eight traits, plus GRS); the quantitative p values of 0.014 and 0.042 would not survive a correction for about 64 tests.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- The selection file said WWOX rs17797882 "associates with lower HOMA-beta and higher TG/HDL". The first half is as printed (controls only, p 0.014); "TG/HDL" is wrong in kind: TG and HDL-C are two separate traits, both higher. There is no ratio in the paper.
- The index SNP's original association (Cho 2012) was with T2D risk in East Asians; this independent Han Chinese sample did not replicate it (OR 1.05, p 0.54). That is a replication failure for the SNP, stated by the authors only as "the same direction of effect".
- Nothing here bears on WWOX loss of function: a common intronic-region index SNP of unknown function, with no expression or eQTL data in the paper.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- That WWOX variation affects type 2 diabetes risk (null in this sample).
- That WWOX variation lowers insulin secretion: a single nominal p of 0.014 in controls with an uninterpretable effect scale, no correction, no replication.
- Any statement about WWOX gene dosage, haploinsufficiency or biallelic loss: the allele is a common GWAS tag.
- The insulin-resistance sentence in its Discussion (contradicted by its Table 5).

## 5 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype (WWOX-DEE)

ESPANSIONE, effectively null. System gap: adult blood DNA from a metabolic case-control, common-variant tag, no function. It could matter only as one of several weak human-genetics pointers toward beta-cell or lipid traits; on this paper alone it does not even clear its own multiple-testing bar.
