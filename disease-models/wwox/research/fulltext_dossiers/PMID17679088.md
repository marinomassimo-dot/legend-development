## 2 · Where this paper stops saying anything about WWOX — stated first, because it is the finding

**"WWOX" occurs 9 times in 189,143 bytes.** Every occurrence either names the gene that FRA16D
spans, or cites it as a tumour suppressor lost in cancers, or labels the exon track in Figure 2B.

**The paper measures no WWOX protein, no WWOX transcript, no WWOX function, and contains no WWOX
allele.** It is a paper about the breakage behaviour of a DNA sequence. That is not a criticism —
it is the correct reading of its evidence class, and it is what bounds every use of it.

## 3 · What the paper did

A yeast artificial chromosome breakage assay in *S. cerevisiae*: a YAC carrying `URA3` plus a
`C₄A₄` telomere seed; breakage is rescued by *de novo* telomere addition, converting cells to
5-FOA-resistant, and a fluctuation analysis gives a rate. Two long YACs (801B6, containing the
270 kb most-fragile FRA16D region; 972D3, distal control) and short YACs carrying four cloned
FRA16D subregions — **Flex1** (~500 bp, 65–75% AT, with a perfect AT dinucleotide repeat cloned at
5, 14, 23 or 34 copies), **Flex4**, **Flex5-proximal**, and a non-peak control. Plus pulsed-field
gel time courses in synchronised cells, two-dimensional gel analysis of replication intermediates,
Mfold structure prediction, and a `rad50Δ` viability assay with hydroxyurea.

## 4 · What the paper states

> *"We have developed an assay to study human common fragile site FRA16D in yeast and used it to
> test whether regions of high flexibility increase chromosome breakage in a quantitative manner.
> We report that a short ~500 bp AT rich region that spans the peak of highest flexibility and
> contains a polymorphic perfect AT repeat increases chromosome fragility, whereas three other
> tested sequences within FRA16D do not."*

> *"we have demonstrated that the Flex1 region stalls replication fork progression in a manner
> dependent on the length of a variable perfect AT repeat predicted to form a cruciform structure"*

**Numbers, with their conditions.** FRA16D-containing YAC 4.6-fold over control (18.7% vs 4.1%
FOA-resistant), ≈20 × 10⁻⁶ vs 4.4 × 10⁻⁶ breaks per kb. Flex1(AT)23 **2.1-fold** over control in
wild-type cells without drug. Fork stalling on 2-D gels: strong for (AT)34, faint for (AT)23,
**undetectable** for (AT)14 and (AT)5. Flex4 and Flex5-p: **no increase in fragility** — the
specificity that makes this a result rather than "AT-rich DNA breaks".

**The authors' own caveat on their headline number:** the (AT)34 allele's breakage rate could not
be measured, because the structure appears to block telomere addition and therefore the recovery of
broken YACs; they state the (AT)23 rate *"may also be an underestimate"*.

## 5 · The one genuinely new lead for the disease model

> *"highly polymorphic in the population (97% heterozygosity) with alleles varying between 11–88 AT
> repeats, and alleles of 34 repeats or greater are quite common."*

> *"The fact that longer Flex1 AT alleles caused a significantly stronger replication barrier
> predicts that human alleles with longer AT length at the Flex1 locus could predispose to
> cancer-causing rearrangements in the FRA16D region."*

The fragility determinant is a **common human germline polymorphism, genotypable from blood DNA**.
That makes Flex1 AT-repeat length a candidate **genomic covariate** — a modifier of the probability
that a FRA16D rearrangement occurs at all.

🔴 **§13: this is neither a biomarker nor an endpoint.** It does not measure WWOX function; it is a
constitutional sequence feature. It is a *risk-of-rearrangement* covariate and must be written as
one. Recorded as a lead in `intake_wave_20261002_C.md`, not promoted.

And the authors themselves deflate it:
> *"Although a relationship between AT length and fragility could not be documented in the Finnis
> et al. study, a comparison of fragility rates of the different alleles in our assay suggests that
> the effect may be subtle."*

## 6 · The transfer limit — the whole of the assignment's question

The brief offered this paper as a *"candidate mechanistic basis for why exon-scale **deletions** are
so prominent in the WWOX-DEE allele classes."* Reading it, that transfer is **not supported**, and
the gap is not the species.

| | This paper | WWOX-DEE deletion alleles |
|---|---|---|
| Event class | **somatic**, in mitotically cycling cells | **germline**, inherited or *de novo* in the parental germ line |
| Cell state | log-phase yeast under hydroxyurea, or `rad52Δ`/`rad50Δ` | meiosis or early embryonic replication |
| Endpoint the authors predict | *"cancer-causing rearrangements"* | neurodevelopmental disease |
| Scale | YAC breakage and healing at one ~500 bp element | exon-scale deletions with defined breakpoints |
| WWOX function | not measured | the entire point |

What transfers is a **mechanistic precedent**: a specific, common, structure-forming sequence inside
FRA16D stalls replication forks and raises breakage, which is a non-trivial reason this locus is
rearrangement-prone *at all*. What does not transfer is any statement that this is why a particular
child's exons are deleted. The honest form is: *FRA16D carries a characterised replication-barrier
element, demonstrated in yeast for somatic breakage; whether it contributes to germline WWOX
deletions is untested.*

## 7 · What the paper does **not** state — including one thing the assignment needs

🔴 **It never says which WWOX intron or exon Flex1 lies in.** Figure 2B plots WWOX/FOR exons against
the FlexStab trace, but the panel bytes were not inspected and no sentence in the body gives the
position. Any statement placing Flex1 against a named WWOX exon would have to come from **Finnis et
al. 2005**, which is not read. Until it is, nothing may be asserted about whether Flex1 falls inside
or near the intervals deleted in WWOX-DEE allele classes — and that, not the yeast system, is what
would make or break the hypothesis.

Also absent: any human cell, any mouse, any neuron, any meiosis, any developmental phenotype, and
any mention of WWOX-related epileptic encephalopathy (the disease was not described until 2014).
