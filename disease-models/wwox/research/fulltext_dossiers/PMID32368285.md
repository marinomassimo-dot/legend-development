## 1 · What the paper did

Two questions in one paper, on two different materials:

1. **Cell lines.** mRNA (qRT-PCR, G6PD-normalised) and subcellular protein distribution (nuclear/cytoplasmic fractionation kit, immunoblot, ImageJ densitometry) of WWOX and DVL-1/2/3 in SCC-15 (human tongue squamous carcinoma) and HET-1A (human oesophageal squamous **epithelial**, non-cancer). WWOX was then silenced with siRNA — **in HET-1A**, the high-WWOX line — and the same readouts repeated against a non-targeting control siRNA.
2. **Tissues.** The same mRNA measurement in 98 HPV-negative HNSCC tumour/adjacent-normal pairs, and the fractionation/immunoblot in a 50-pair subgroup.

## 2 · What it measured, exactly

- The localisation quantity throughout is a **cytoplasm/nucleus ratio of band intensities**, not an absolute nuclear amount and not imaging. "Increased nuclear localisation" means that ratio fell.
- **After WWOX silencing (Table 4, HET-1A):** Wwox 1.04 → 1.41; Dvl-1 2.53 → 2.06; Dvl-2 2.23 → 1.14; Dvl-3 1.01 → 0.89. Knockdown efficiency 2^−ΔΔCt = 0.07 (Table 3).
- **Tissues (Table 6, 50 pairs):** Wwox tumour 2.8 ± 2.9 vs normal 2.7 ± 3.0, **p = 0.822**; Dvl-1 2.7 vs 4.9, p = 0.002; Dvl-2 1.91 vs 1.13, p = 0.001; Dvl-3 1.62 vs 1.07, p = 0.002.
- **mRNA in tissues (Table 5 / Figure 2):** decreased in WWOX 73.5 %, DVL-1 51 %, DVL-2 56.1 %, DVL-3 50 % of the 98 pairs; Figure 2 prints WWOX p<0.001, DVL-1 p<0.001, DVL-2 p=0.003, DVL-3 p=0.020 — **all four genes lower in tumour**.
- **Cell lines (Figure 1 panels):** DVL-1 p<0.001, WWOX p=0.007, DVL-2 p=0.084, **DVL-3 p=0.227**.

## 3 · What the paper states

- Silencing WWOX in HET-1A increased the nuclear localisation of all three Dvl proteins, by "10 %, 90 % and 10 %" for Dvl-1, Dvl-2 and Dvl-3.
- In tumour tissue only Dvl-1 shows the relatively-more-nuclear pattern.
- Conclusion: WWOX exerts its tumour-suppressor function through the Dvl proteins of the Wnt/β-catenin pathway, supporting Bouteille et al. 2009.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT state

- **It measures no Wnt output at all.** β-catenin, TCF/LEF reporter activity, AXIN2 or any Wnt target gene is absent from the whole paper. The inferential step "nuclear Dvl ⇒ canonical Wnt activation" is asserted from the literature, never measured here.
- It makes **no neural, neuronal, glial or developmental measurement**, and no measurement in any non-epithelial cell. The words neuron, astrocyte, oligodendrocyte, brain and epilepsy do not appear.
- It does not report a WWOX protein-level knockdown efficiency, a replicate count for any immunoblot, dispersion or a p value for Table 4.
- It does not silence WWOX in a head-and-neck cancer line. The title's "in head and neck cancer" describes the disease context, not the system the silencing was done in.
- It gives no allele: siRNA knockdown is not a null allele, and nothing here speaks to any missense variant.

## 5 · Caveats the authors give themselves

- "To clarify the effect of WWOX on the expression and cellular localization of Dvl in HNSCC further in vitro and in vivo studies need to be conducted."
- They note that cell-line and tissue expression profiles disagreed and attribute it to in vitro versus in vivo regulation.

## 6 · What I found that the paper's own text does not say

1. **The DVL-3 cell-line difference the Results call significant is p = 0.227 in the panel.** The sentence "the SCC-15 cell line expresses significantly higher levels of DVL-1 and DVL-3 mRNA" is contradicted by Figure 1B, which plots DVL-3 alone and prints p = 0.227 over it. Figure 1A's DVL-1 bracket is p<0.001, so half the sentence holds.
2. **The silencing immunoblot is not shown.** Figure 4, the paper's only blot image, has four lane pairs — HET-1A, SCC-15, Tumor Tissue, Normal Tissue — and **no control-siRNA or WWOX-siRNA lane**. The numbers in Table 4 that carry the paper's title come from a blot the reader never sees.
3. **No nuclear-fraction purity control.** β-actin (43 kDa), a cytoplasmic protein, is the only loading control shown for both fractions; no lamin, histone or other nuclear marker is blotted. A cytoplasm/nucleus ratio without a fractionation-purity control cannot separate a real import from carry-over.
4. **The percentages do not all reconcile with Table 4.** Reading the reported percentage as the change in the inverse (nucleus/cytoplasm) ratio reproduces Dvl-2 (+96 %, reported 90 %) and Dvl-3 (+13.5 %, reported 10 %), but gives +22.8 % for Dvl-1, reported as 10 %. No formula tried (N/C, N/(N+C), or the fall in C/N) yields 10 % for Dvl-1.
5. **Figure 4's caption names only cell lines** ("in HET-1A and SCC-15 cell lines") while the Results cite it for the 50 tumour/normal pairs, and the figure itself shows tissue lanes.
6. **The tissue data run against the oncogenic reading.** All three DVL mRNAs are significantly *lower* in tumours, and WWOX's own cytoplasm/nucleus ratio does not differ between tumour and normal (p = 0.822) — so in the tissue arm the WWOX localisation change that the cell-line arm is built on is absent.

## 7 · Transfer limit for the WWOX-DEE model

Human epithelial cells, transient siRNA knockdown, a ratio readout, no Wnt transcriptional output and no neural system. The strongest statement this paper supports is: *in a non-cancer oesophageal epithelial line, knocking WWOX down shifts the Dvl cytoplasm/nucleus band-intensity ratio, most clearly for Dvl-2.* It supports nothing about a neuron, an astrocyte, an oligodendrocyte, a biallelic null or a missense allele, and it measures no consequence of the shift.
