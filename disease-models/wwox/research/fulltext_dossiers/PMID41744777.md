## 1 · What the paper did

Combined five capsid point mutations from two previously published liver-de-targeted AAV9 variants —
positions 501, 505 and 706 from AAV9-16, and 527 and 533 from AAV9-HR — into one capsid, **AAV9-DM**.

Single experiment: single-stranded AAV genomes encoding firefly luciferase under a CBA promoter,
packaged into AAV9, AAV9-16, AAV9-HR and AAV9-DM, **1 × 10^11 vg per animal by tail vein into
C57BL/6 mice**. Whole-animal bioluminescence at 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9 weeks (n = 5), then necropsy at
9 weeks with qPCR for vector genomes and RT-qPCR for luciferase transcript in liver, heart,
gastrocnemius, brain and spinal cord (n = 3–5).

## 2 · What it measured

**Manufacturability — the cost the title does not carry.** From the same number of producer cells:
AAV9 **3.58 × 10^13** vg, AAV9-16 and AAV9-HR ~**2.0 × 10^13** vg, **AAV9-DM 4.5 × 10^12 vg** — an
**eight-fold drop in packaging yield** against the parent capsid.

**Liver.** AAV9-DM gave a **~127-fold reduction in vector genome copies** against AAV9, the largest
of any variant, and the lowest hepatic transcript ratio. This is the paper's strongest result.

**Whole-animal output.** Peak average radiance fell from 7.3 × 10^7 (AAV9) to 5.8 × 10^5 (AAV9-DM) —
about two orders of magnitude less total transgene signal per animal at the same vector dose.

**CNS — where the summary and the data diverge.** The abstract-level claim is that AAV9-DM has
"minimal liver tropism" while "minimally affecting CNS tropism". The Discussion states the measured
position plainly: *"In the brain, all mutated capsids resulted in significantly lower vector genome
copies as compared to AAV9, though relative expression levels were consistent amongst all capsids
investigated."* So in brain, **vector genomes fell significantly and the per-tissue transcript ratio
did not**. In spinal cord, vector genomes were equivalent to AAV9.

**Heart.** AAV9-DM reduced both vector genomes and transcript — a second de-targeting, not advertised
in the title.

**Durability.** Expression was detectable across all nine weeks, but AAV9-DM's declined significantly
at weeks 6 and 9 against its own week-3 value.

**Genome copies and expression disagreed in three of five tissues.** The authors propose that the
capsid itself modulates transgene transcription, citing recent work showing AAV9 capsid–promoter
interaction, and call the mechanism undetermined.

## 3 · What the paper does NOT state

- **No toxicity, histopathology, liver-enzyme, complement, cytokine or immunology measurement of any
  kind** — despite a Discussion built almost entirely on clinical deaths from high-dose AAV.
- **No pre-existing-immunity or neutralising-antibody data for the new capsid**, which is the obvious
  question for a novel capsid surface.
- **No dose-response.** One dose, 1 × 10^11 vg per animal.
- **No large animal, no primate, no human.** The authors do not claim otherwise.
- **No CSF route.** Systemic tail vein only, so nothing here speaks to intrathecal or
  intracerebroventricular delivery.
- **No therapeutic transgene.** Firefly luciferase under a ubiquitous CBA promoter.
- **No cell-type resolution in brain** — homogenate qPCR and RT-qPCR only.
- **No direct demonstration that the dose ceiling moves.** The dose-ceiling argument is entirely
  inferential: nothing in the paper measures a toxicity threshold before or after the modification.
- **Nothing about WWOX.**

## 5 · Transfer limits — stated exactly

1. **Species and strain:** mouse, C57BL/6. The paper's own framing notes that "minimal" is relative
   to AAV9 in this one setting.
2. **Route:** intravenous. A capsid's liver de-targeting advantage is largest where the liver is
   most exposed; by a CSF route the comparison is different and is not made here.
3. **Cargo and promoter:** luciferase under CBA, single-stranded. The paper's own finding that
   capsid may modulate transcription means a different promoter could rank the capsids differently.
4. **Duration:** 9 weeks, with a measured decline in the new capsid's own expression inside that
   window.
5. **The brain result must be stated as measured**, not as summarised: significantly fewer vector
   genomes than AAV9, with an unchanged per-tissue transcript ratio and a two-order-of-magnitude
   lower whole-animal output.

## 6 · Bearing on the Group B question

This is the only engineering answer in Group B to the immune/dose dilemma, and read carefully it
**bounds** that answer more than it advances it:

- **The liver de-targeting is real and large** (~127-fold fewer vector genomes), and liver is the
  organ implicated in the fatalities the authors catalogue. For a CNS cassette delivered
  systemically, that is a meaningful change in where the vector goes.
- **It is not free.** Eight-fold lower packaging yield means that producing a given clinical dose
  takes roughly eight times the manufacturing input — which works against, not with, an argument
  about affordable dose ceilings.
- **CNS equivalence is the claim most likely to shrink, and it has already shrunk inside this paper.**
  Brain vector genomes were significantly reduced. The expression equivalence that rescues the claim
  is a normalised ratio within each tissue, measured once, in mouse, at one dose, with a reporter.
- **The dose-ceiling claim is not measured at all.** A capsid that de-targets the liver plausibly
  raises the tolerated dose; this paper contains no toxicity endpoint and therefore no evidence of
  that. It should be carried as a mechanism hypothesis, not as a safety result.
