## 2 · What the paper did

A single-laboratory enzymology study. No human subjects, no animals, no cells of human origin.

**Protein source.** Human *WWOX* cDNA (from A. K. Bednarek's own collection, i.e. the group's
2000 clone) subcloned `BamHI`/`EcoRI` from pcDNA 3.1.A into **pET 44a(+)**, giving an
**N-terminal NusA fusion** (NUS-WWOX), expressed in *E. coli* Origami B (DE3) pLysS; and a second,
**GST fusion** (GST-WWOX) in a pGEX 2TK vector. Induction 0.2 mM IPTG (NUS system) / 0.7 mM IPTG
(GST system), overnight at 16–18 °C. Lysis with CelLytic B II + lysozyme + RNase/DNase + sarkosyl,
then a stabilising buffer with Tween 20 and Nonidet P-40.

**Critical limitation, stated by the authors themselves.** Purification on glutathione-sepharose
(GST) or TALON Co²⁺ resin (NUS) **destroyed the activity in every attempt**, so *all* enzymology
was performed on the **soluble fraction of an unfractionated bacterial crude extract**
(400 µg total protein per 1 ml reaction). No purified WWOX enzyme was ever assayed.

**Assay.** Spectrophotometric, A₃₄₀, 25 °C, ε = 6270 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ for the nicotinamide cofactor,
read at 5-min intervals over 30 min. Reaction: 100 mM potassium phosphate pH 7.4;
0.75 µmol NAD⁺ or NADP⁺ (or 0.15 µmol NADH/NADPH for the reduction direction);
0.24 µmol steroid; 400 µg crude extract; 1 ml total.
Dehydrogenase assays were quenched with methanol/chloroform (1:1) and the aqueous phase separated.

**Substrates (7).** 5α-androstane-3,17-dione (5α-A), 4-androstene-3,17-dione (4-A),
17β-estradiol (17β-E), estrone (E), 5α-dihydroprogesterone-*allo* (5α-DHP-allo),
progesterone (P), testosterone (T). A preliminary screen used the generic dehydrogenase
substrate **S-indan-1-ol** with NAD⁺ (Fig. 2) — its concentration is never stated.

**Controls.** The matched empty-vector extract (NUS alone / GST alone) with the same substrate,
and both extracts without substrate. WWOX "activity" is defined as the **difference** in A₃₄₀
between the NUS-WWOX extract and the plain NUS extract.

**Kinetics.** "Steady-state kinetics" with Lineweaver–Burk double-reciprocal fitting, substrate
varied in 0.025 or 0.05 µmol/ml steps over the ranges in Table I.

**Statistics.** Mann–Whitney, significance at p < 0.05. Replication: "the kinetics values were the
means of at least two or three demonstrations"; Fig. 2's caption says the curves shown are
"from one of two experiments, each done with a separate isolation of the soluble fractions".

## 4 · Recomputation from the printed numbers

All of this is arithmetic on the paper's own figures; the script is
`scratchpad/x_recompute.py` / `x_mw.py` (session scratch, not committed).

**(a) Reaction extent.** With ε = 6270 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ and 0.24 µmol steroid in 1 ml (2.40·10⁻⁴ M):

| NAD⁺ curve | A₃₄₀ at 30 min | NAD(P)H formed | as % of steroid supplied |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4-A, NUS-WWOX | 1.33 | 2.12·10⁻⁴ M | 88 % |
| T, NUS-WWOX | 1.34 | 2.14·10⁻⁴ M | 89 % |
| E, NUS-WWOX | 1.24 | 1.98·10⁻⁴ M | 82 % |
| 5α-DHP-allo, NUS-WWOX | 1.18 | 1.88·10⁻⁴ M | 78 % |

So the 30-min NAD⁺ progress curves run to **78–89 % consumption of the substrate**. The text's
statement that at 0.24 µmol/ml the substrate "was sufficient or even excessive" is not what the
panels show: these are near-exhaustion end-point curves, not initial-velocity measurements.

**(b) Substrate-independent signal.** The NUS-WWOX extract **without any steroid** still reduces
≈ 3.7·10⁻⁵ M NAD⁺ (A₃₄₀ ≈ 0.23) and ≈ 4.6·10⁻⁵ M NADP⁺ (A₃₄₀ ≈ 0.29). With NADP⁺ this
substrate-free signal (0.29) is **larger than the entire empty-vector-plus-substrate control** in
all seven panels (0.065–0.155). The extract therefore contains an endogenous NAD(P)⁺-reducing
activity that tracks the WWOX construct and does not need the added steroid.

**(c) The text's own fold-ratios do not match its panels.** Results states the host activity was
"3- to 6-fold lower compared to NUS-WWOX extracts" for 5α-DHP-allo, E and T, and "at the level
corresponding to the blank" for P, 17β-E, 5α-A and 4-A. Measured from the NAD⁺ panels:

| Substrate | NUS-WWOX / NUS | text's group |
|---|---|---|
| 5α-DHP-allo | 2.46 × | "3–6-fold" |
| Estrone | **1.55 ×** | "3–6-fold" |
| Testosterone | 1.94 × | "3–6-fold" |
| Progesterone | 3.96 × | "blank level" |
| 17β-Estradiol | 4.68 × | "blank level" |
| 5α-A | 2.67 × | "blank level" |
| 4-A | 3.41 × | "blank level" |

**None of the three substrates called "3- to 6-fold" reaches 3-fold**, and the two groups are
close to rank-inverted: the substrates the text calls low-background are the ones with the
*largest* host signal. This matters most for **estrone**, which carries the paper's central
biological inference: estrone has the **highest empty-vector background of all seven** (0.80,
i.e. 65 % of the NUS-WWOX trace) and the **smallest WWOX-specific increment** (0.44).

**(d) The Km values were extrapolated from above saturation.** Comparing Table II with Table I,
**13 of the 14 reported Km values lie below the lowest substrate concentration actually used**;
the single exception is testosterone with NADP⁺. The lowest [S] / Km ratio runs from 1.06 to 4.13
(estrone with NAD⁺: every point is at ≥ 3.3 × Km). A Lineweaver–Burk x-intercept obtained entirely
from the saturated arm is a long extrapolation, and the printed confidence intervals show it:
13 of 14 are asymmetric about their own Km, and two span 3–3.5 × the Km itself
(17β-E with NADP⁺, 1.96–13.79; T with NADP⁺, 8.40–54.47).

**(e) The p values are unattainable with the stated replication.** Methods state the kinetics
values were "the means of at least two or three demonstrations" and Fig. 2's caption says
"one of two experiments". For an exact Mann–Whitney U test the smallest attainable two-sided
p is 0.333 with n = 2 per group and 0.100 with n = 3; p = 0.0001 needs n ≥ 9 per group.
**Every one of the 14 p values in Table II (0.0000 to 0.0091) is below the floor permitted by the
stated n**, and `p = 0.0000` is not an attainable exact value at all. Either the true replication
is far larger than stated, or the test was applied to something other than independent
experimental replicates. The paper does not say which, so the p column cannot be used.

## 5 · What the paper states

1. The SDR domain of WWOX has **dehydrogenase (oxidative) activity** on all seven steroids tested,
   with both NAD⁺ and NADP⁺; velocities higher with NAD⁺.
2. **No reduction activity** with NADH or NADPH on the same seven steroids ("results not shown").
3. Apparent Km in the 1.5–14.6 ·10⁻⁵ M range; **lowest for estrone**, highest for 5α-A (NAD⁺) and
   testosterone (NADP⁺).
4. Hyperbolic progress curves are interpreted as "only one site for the steroid-to-substrate bond".
5. Activity is lost on purification, which the authors read as WWOX requiring partner proteins,
   possibly supplied by *E. coli* ("WWOX partners are present amongst bacterial proteins").
6. Interpretation: WWOX participates in **estrone metabolism in vivo**, supporting a role in
   sex-steroid metabolism; and tumour transcripts lacking the substrate-binding part of the SDR
   domain make loss of this activity "very likely" important in carcinogenesis.

## 6 · What the paper does NOT state

- **No Vmax, no kcat, no specific activity, no turnover number.** Methods promise Vmax; nothing is
  reported. Catalytic efficiency (kcat/Km) therefore cannot be derived for any substrate.
- **No purified enzyme.** Every number comes from an unfractionated bacterial crude extract.
  Activity did not survive purification in either system.
- **No identification of the reaction product.** No TLC, no HPLC, no GC-MS, no NMR, no radiolabel.
  The only readout is A₃₄₀, i.e. cofactor reduction. **Which position is oxidised, and to what
  product, is never determined** for any of the seven steroids. The paper's own framing
  (17β-HSD3-like cluster membership) is a sequence-clustering citation, not a measured regiochemistry.
- **No confirmation that the active band is WWOX.** No Western blot, no antibody, no MS, no
  molecular-weight ladder in Fig. 1. No catalytically-dead point mutant (e.g. of the YNRSK motif
  at 293–297 or of the GANSGIG cofactor site at 131–137) was made, so no construct ties the
  activity to the SDR active site rather than to a co-purifying *E. coli* dehydrogenase induced
  alongside the fusion.
- **No domain dissection.** No SDR-only construct, no WW-domain-deleted construct, no Y293F/K297
  variant. The claim "the SDR domain has dehydrogenase activity" is attributed to the domain by
  inference from full-length protein, not by measurement on the domain.
- **No human cell, no mammalian cell, no tissue, no mouse.** Protein source is *E. coli* only.
- **No variant of any kind.** The paper tests **wild-type human WWOX only**. No missense, no
  splice allele, no truncation, no patient-derived material appears anywhere.
- **No neurological content.** Epilepsy, encephalopathy, brain, neuron, myelin, cerebellum, seizure
  do not appear. The paper is entirely cancer/steroid-framed.
- **No units of activity**, no protein-normalised rate, no amount of WWOX in the 400 µg extract.
- **No error bars, no n per point, no raw data table** behind Figs. 2 and 3.
- **No supplementary material** exists for this article.

## 7 · Caveats the authors themselves give

- Purification abolished activity in both expression systems; the crude extract was a forced choice.
- The GST-WWOX fusion's "stability and the enzymatic activity varied over time".
- The host extract has its own oxidoreductase activity for several substrates.
- They call for "further investigations concerning WWOX and its enzymatic activity".

## 8 · Transfer to the reference genotype class — the limit, stated exactly

This paper measured **wild-type human WWOX** expressed in *E. coli*. It contains **no allele**,
no patient material, and no neurological readout. Therefore:

- It establishes a **wild-type reference property only**: that a crude extract containing a
  WWOX fusion oxidises seven steroids with NAD(P)⁺, with the limits in §4 and §6.
- **Nothing here transfers to any WWOX-DEE allele.** P47T ≠ Q230P ≠ G372R ≠ A141T ≠ P252A;
  each is a distinct allele with a distinct predicted structural context, and **none of them was
  tested here, nor was any variant protein made**. A missense allele's effect on this activity is
  **unmeasured**, in this paper and — on the evidence of this paper alone — anywhere.
- A **splice allele** of the reference genotype class is even further out of reach: the paper has
  no RNA work at all, so read fraction, frame, NMD and residual protein are all untouched.
- The residues the paper names as functional (**GANSGIG 131–137** cofactor site, **YNRSK 293–297**
  substrate site, plus the serine 12 residues upstream of the YNRSK tyrosine) are the one
  position-level fact that could, in principle, be used to reason about where in the protein an
  allele sits. **That is a sequence annotation taken from the group's own 2000 paper and from
  Duax & Ghosh 1997, not a measurement made here**, and proximity of a residue to a motif is not
  a measured functional consequence. Any statement of the form "allele X disrupts the WWOX SDR
  catalytic site" is INFERENZA and must be labelled so; this paper supplies no allele-level datum
  to raise it.
- The paper is also silent on whether the activity is **physiologically relevant in brain**:
  nothing was measured in any mammalian tissue, and the whole argument for relevance is the
  expression pattern (testis, prostate, ovary) cited from the group's 2000 paper.

## 9 · Could this be an endpoint or a pharmacodynamic assay? (LEGEND_CORE §13, read)

§13 defines Tier 1 as a **direct gene readout — protein, mRNA, splice, enzymatic activity,
immediate substrates**; Tier 2 proximal pathway; Tier 3 distal clinical endpoints, which are not
gene biomarkers. §13 also forbids calling any candidate "validated" without direct
sensitivity/specificity evidence in the disease population.

**Verdict: the *concept* is Tier 1; this paper's *assay* is not usable as one, and nothing here
is validated.** Specifically:

- A WWOX steroid-dehydrogenase activity is, by §13's own wording, a Tier-1 class of readout
  ("enzymatic activity"), so an allele-resolved WWOX enzymatic assay would be a legitimate
  Tier-1 biomarker *target*.
- **This assay cannot serve as that readout as published.** It has no purified enzyme, no product
  identification, no activity units, a substrate-independent background of 25–50 % of signal, a
  host background up to 65 % of signal for the very substrate the conclusion rests on, and no
  catalytically-dead control. Its dynamic range is an absorbance difference between two crude
  lysates, which is not transferable to human material.
- **As a pharmacodynamic assay it fails additionally on matrix**: there is no demonstration that
  the activity is detectable in any mammalian cell, let alone in an accessible human matrix, and
  no evidence it changes with any intervention.
- It is **not an endpoint** under §13 in any case: an endpoint is a clinical outcome, and this is
  at most a target-engagement-class readout. A biomarker is not an endpoint.
- What would be needed to make it one is concrete and listed in §11 below.

## 10 · Independence of the laboratory

**Not independent of the discovery lab, and the paper says so on its face.** The corresponding
author, A. K. Bednarek, is the first author of Bednarek *et al.* 2000 (the paper that identified
WWOX) and of Bednarek *et al.* 2001. The *WWOX* cDNA used here "came from A. K. Bednarek's
collection". Both WWOX-specific sequence annotations the paper relies on — the GANSGIG cofactor
site and the YNRSK substrate site — are cited to Bednarek *et al.* 2000. The group (Medical
University of Lodz) is distinct from the Aldaz/Croce/Huebner lineage, but Bednarek is himself a
co-author of the Aldaz 2000 discovery paper, so this is a **follow-up by one of the discovery
authors in his own laboratory**, not external replication. No orthogonal laboratory, and no
replication of this enzymology by anyone else, is cited in the paper.
