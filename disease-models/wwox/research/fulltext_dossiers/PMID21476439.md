## 2 · What the paper did

A single-laboratory enzymology study. No human subjects, no animals, no cells of human origin.

**Protein source.** Human *WWOX* cDNA (from A. K. Bednarek's own collection, i.e. the group's
2000 clone) subcloned `BamHI`/`EcoRI` from pcDNA 3.1.A into **pET 44a(+)**, giving an
**N-terminal NusA fusion** (NUS-WWOX), expressed in *E. coli* Origami B (DE3) pLysS; and a second,
**GST fusion** (GST-WWOX) in a pGEX 2TK vector. Induction 0.2 mM IPTG (NUS system) / 0.7 mM IPTG
(GST system), overnight at 16–18 °C. Lysis with CelLytic B II + lysozyme + RNase/DNase + sarkosyl,
then a stabilising buffer with Tween 20 and Nonidet P-40.

**Critical limitation, stated by the authors themselves.** Purification on glutathione-sepharose
(GST) or TALON Co²⁺ resin (NUS) **destroyed the activity in every attempt**, so *all* enzymology
was performed on the **soluble fraction of an unfractionated bacterial crude extract**
(400 µg total protein per 1 ml reaction). No purified WWOX enzyme was ever assayed.

**Assay.** Spectrophotometric, A₃₄₀, 25 °C, ε = 6270 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ for the nicotinamide cofactor,
read at 5-min intervals over 30 min. Reaction: 100 mM potassium phosphate pH 7.4;
0.75 µmol NAD⁺ or NADP⁺ (or 0.15 µmol NADH/NADPH for the reduction direction);
0.24 µmol steroid; 400 µg crude extract; 1 ml total.
Dehydrogenase assays were quenched with methanol/chloroform (1:1) and the aqueous phase separated.

**Substrates (7).** 5α-androstane-3,17-dione (5α-A), 4-androstene-3,17-dione (4-A),
17β-estradiol (17β-E), estrone (E), 5α-dihydroprogesterone-*allo* (5α-DHP-allo),
progesterone (P), testosterone (T). A preliminary screen used the generic dehydrogenase
substrate **S-indan-1-ol** with NAD⁺ (Fig. 2) — its concentration is never stated.

**Controls.** The matched empty-vector extract (NUS alone / GST alone) with the same substrate,
and both extracts without substrate. WWOX "activity" is defined as the **difference** in A₃₄₀
between the NUS-WWOX extract and the plain NUS extract.

**Kinetics.** "Steady-state kinetics" with Lineweaver–Burk double-reciprocal fitting, substrate
varied in 0.025 or 0.05 µmol/ml steps over the ranges in Table I.

**Statistics.** Mann–Whitney, significance at p < 0.05. Replication: "the kinetics values were the
means of at least two or three demonstrations"; Fig. 2's caption says the curves shown are
"from one of two experiments, each done with a separate isolation of the soluble fractions".

**There is no Vmax column, and no Vmax value appears anywhere in the paper.** The word `Vmax`
occurs exactly once in the whole article, in Methods ("Lineweaver-Burk analysis was applied in

the table's own footnote it is a **difference of two Km values**, which is not a kinetic constant
of any resolved enzyme.

## 4 · Recomputation from the printed numbers

All of this is arithmetic on the paper's own figures; the script is
`scratchpad/x_recompute.py` / `x_mw.py` (session scratch, not committed).

**(a) Reaction extent.** With ε = 6270 M⁻¹ cm⁻¹ and 0.24 µmol steroid in 1 ml (2.40·10⁻⁴ M):

| NAD⁺ curve | A₃₄₀ at 30 min | NAD(P)H formed | as % of steroid supplied |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4-A, NUS-WWOX | 1.33 | 2.12·10⁻⁴ M | 88 % |
| T, NUS-WWOX | 1.34 | 2.14·10⁻⁴ M | 89 % |
| E, NUS-WWOX | 1.24 | 1.98·10⁻⁴ M | 82 % |
| 5α-DHP-allo, NUS-WWOX | 1.18 | 1.88·10⁻⁴ M | 78 % |

So the 30-min NAD⁺ progress curves run to **78–89 % consumption of the substrate**. The text's
statement that at 0.24 µmol/ml the substrate "was sufficient or even excessive" is not what the
panels show: these are near-exhaustion end-point curves, not initial-velocity measurements.

**(b) Substrate-independent signal.** The NUS-WWOX extract **without any steroid** still reduces
≈ 3.7·10⁻⁵ M NAD⁺ (A₃₄₀ ≈ 0.23) and ≈ 4.6·10⁻⁵ M NADP⁺ (A₃₄₀ ≈ 0.29). With NADP⁺ this
substrate-free signal (0.29) is **larger than the entire empty-vector-plus-substrate control** in
all seven panels (0.065–0.155). The extract therefore contains an endogenous NAD(P)⁺-reducing
activity that tracks the WWOX construct and does not need the added steroid.

**(c) The text's own fold-ratios do not match its panels.** Results states the host activity was
"3- to 6-fold lower compared to NUS-WWOX extracts" for 5α-DHP-allo, E and T, and "at the level
corresponding to the blank" for P, 17β-E, 5α-A and 4-A. Measured from the NAD⁺ panels:

| Substrate | NUS-WWOX / NUS | text's group |
|---|---|---|
| 5α-DHP-allo | 2.46 × | "3–6-fold" |
| Estrone | **1.55 ×** | "3–6-fold" |
| Testosterone | 1.94 × | "3–6-fold" |
| Progesterone | 3.96 × | "blank level" |
| 17β-Estradiol | 4.68 × | "blank level" |
| 5α-A | 2.67 × | "blank level" |
| 4-A | 3.41 × | "blank level" |

**None of the three substrates called "3- to 6-fold" reaches 3-fold**, and the two groups are
close to rank-inverted: the substrates the text calls low-background are the ones with the
*largest* host signal. This matters most for **estrone**, which carries the paper's central
biological inference: estrone has the **highest empty-vector background of all seven** (0.80,
i.e. 65 % of the NUS-WWOX trace) and the **smallest WWOX-specific increment** (0.44).

**(d) The Km values were extrapolated from above saturation.** Comparing Table II with Table I,
**13 of the 14 reported Km values lie below the lowest substrate concentration actually used**;
the single exception is testosterone with NADP⁺. The lowest [S] / Km ratio runs from 1.06 to 4.13
(estrone with NAD⁺: every point is at ≥ 3.3 × Km). A Lineweaver–Burk x-intercept obtained entirely
from the saturated arm is a long extrapolation, and the printed confidence intervals show it:
13 of 14 are asymmetric about their own Km, and two span 3–3.5 × the Km itself
(17β-E with NADP⁺, 1.96–13.79; T with NADP⁺, 8.40–54.47).

**(e) The p values are unattainable with the stated replication.** Methods state the kinetics
values were "the means of at least two or three demonstrations" and Fig. 2's caption says
"one of two experiments". For an exact Mann–Whitney U test the smallest attainable two-sided
p is 0.333 with n = 2 per group and 0.100 with n = 3; p = 0.0001 needs n ≥ 9 per group.
**Every one of the 14 p values in Table II (0.0000 to 0.0091) is below the floor permitted by the
stated n**, and `p = 0.0000` is not an attainable exact value at all. Either the true replication
is far larger than stated, or the test was applied to something other than independent
experimental replicates. The paper does not say which, so the p column cannot be used.
