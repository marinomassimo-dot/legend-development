## 2 · What the paper establishes

**A. The human prevalence datum — the strongest thing here.** WWOX immunoreactivity absent or reduced
in **58% (48/83)** osteosarcoma specimens from 51 patients against **0/12** normal bone and cartilage
(`P<0.0001`, Fisher, Table 1A). Every Table 1A interval reproduces exactly under the paper's stated
exact-binomial method. `DATO`.

**B. The longitudinal within-patient direction.** Two opposite movements of the same marker in the
same people: WWOX **increases** in 7/16 post-chemotherapy resections (5 initially negative), and
**decreases** in 4/9 metastases relative to the primary. Visible in Figure 1B/1C as pixels, not only
tabulated. This is a stronger argument than any prevalence figure because each patient is their own
control. `DATO` for the direction; the mechanism is untested.

**C. WWOX is low in most human OS lines, at protein level.** 4 of 5 human OS lines carry low WWOX
mRNA and barely detectable protein (Fig 2A/2C). `DATO`.

**D. Re-expressing WWOX suppresses tumour phenotypes in WWOX-low lines.** Reduced proliferation
(Fig 3B), increased sub-G1 (Fig S3: KHOS ~6-fold, HOS ~3-fold — both reproduce from the panel),
reduced xenograft volume (Fig 3C, `P=0.014` and `P=0.007`, n=5), reduced colony formation, soft agar,
migration and Matrigel invasion (Fig 4, Fig S4, Fig S5). `DATO` for the effects **as measured against
the empty-vector control**; see §3 for how much that qualifier carries.

## 3 · What the paper does **not** support — the product of this reading

### 3.1 🔴 The title of Figure 5 is refuted by the paper's own supplement, and the direction is reversed

Figure 5 is titled *"Inverse association of WWOX and RUNX2 expression in osteosarcoma"* — a claim
about the disease. **Supplemental Table 8 contains the paired human data and it does not show an
inverse association anywhere.** Computed from its six cells (all of which reconcile exactly with
Table 1B: 9+11=20 biopsies, 12+13=25 resections, 4+7=11 metastases):

| Stratum | WWOX-strong RUNX2⁺ | WWOX-low RUNX2⁺ | Odds ratio | Fisher 2-tailed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Biopsies | 7/9 = **78%** | 5/11 = **45%** | **4.20 — same direction** | p = 0.197 |
| Resections | 2/12 = 17% | 2/13 = 15% | 1.10 | p = 1.00 |
| Metastases | 3/4 = 75% | 5/7 = 71% | 1.20 | p = 1.00 |
| Pooled (56) | 12/25 = 48% | 12/31 = 39% | 1.46 | p = 0.59 |

The honest verdict on the human tissue is **NULL for any WWOX–RUNX2 association**, and the only
non-trivial trend — in biopsies — runs **opposite** to the figure title, though it does not reach
significance. The authors state the negative plainly in the Results (*"this was not evident when
paired comparisons were performed on the 56 available cases"*) and in the supplement
(*"a correlation was not found when cases were examined in detail"*). **The modality is intact; what
travels beyond the evidence is the SCOPE of a title.**

**And the panel behind that title does not show what its caption says.** Read from pixels, Figure 5A's
RUNX2 row: MC3T3 and K7M2 — the two lines the caption places on the RUNX2-low side — both carry
distinct bands comparable to HOS, which the same caption places on the RUNX2-high side. The only
blank lane is MCF7. **The darkest RUNX2 band in the row belongs to ROS, a lane named nowhere in the
caption or the Results.** *(Independently reproduced from the same bytes the prior reading used.)*

**What this does NOT overturn:** the WWOX-low human lines HOS, SAOS-2 and KHOS do carry high RUNX2,
MCF7 vs MDA-MB231 does run inverse, and WWOX re-expression in HOS does reduce RUNX2 mRNA and protein
plus MMP-9/COL1A1/OC (Fig 5B/5C). The **cell-line** axis survives. The **human-tissue** axis does not,
and the title generalises to *"in osteosarcoma"*.

### 3.2 🔴 The headline mouse number is contradicted by the paper's own figure legend

| Source | Tumour-bearing KO | Bilateral | Unilateral |
|---|---|---|---|
| Body text (P13) | **100%** | 68% | — |
| Supplemental Table 1 | 15 + 7 = **22/22 (100%)** | 15 (68%) | 7\* (32%) |
| **Figure S1 legend** | **19/22 = 86%** | 13/19 (68.2%) | 6/19 (31.8%) |

The percentages nearly coincide (68.2% of 19 vs 68% of 22) — **which is exactly why the discrepancy
survives any reader who checks percentages and not counts.** Supplemental Table 1's own footnote
supplies the arithmetic: *"only one femur was analyzed by microCT in 3 mice"*, and 22 − 19 = 3, the
difference being 2 bilateral + 1 unilateral. **Two mice were therefore scored bilateral on a single
imaged femur**, which is not obtainable from one femur.

This matters beyond the paper: *"100% of Wwox-deficient mice had developed OS by 18 days-of-age"* is
the sentence that revises this lab's own earlier ~30% (ref 7, PMID 17360458) and is offered as the
justification for the entire human study. **The paper's own supplement supports 86%, not 100%**, and
the screening criterion — *irregular protrusions on the cortex* — is applied to a KO cortex that
Figure S1 shows is itself thinner and more porous than WT, with no blinding stated anywhere.

### 3.3 🔴 The in vivo rescue of the failed correlation is weaker than the word used for it

Having conceded the human data, the Discussion says *"although we did observe an inverse correlation
in vivo in Wwox-deficient mice"*. Read from the pixels, **Figure S6 is a two-genotype immunostain in
epiphyseal growth-plate cartilage** — columnar and hypertrophic chondrocytes in a blue cartilage
matrix, **not osteoblasts and not tumour** — with **a single unlabelled IgG panel assigned to neither
genotype**, no n, no scoring, no densitometry. Direction is as claimed (KO browner than WT).

Two things follow. **A two-genotype comparison of one marker is not a correlation** — that is a
category upgrade, not a measurement. And Runx2 is a *required* regulator of chondrocyte hypertrophy,
so its elevation in growth-plate cartilage of a mouse with a documented bone-metabolic phenotype has
an explanation that never touches the tumour axis. `PREMISE: DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK` on any reading
that treats "Runx2 up in KO femur" as evidence for the WWOX–RUNX2 tumour mechanism.
**`REVIVAL_TRIGGER`:** quantified Runx2 in osteoblasts or in the tumour tissue itself, with per-genotype
IgG controls and an n.

Reagent gap in the same place: Figure S6 names **polyclonal anti-Runx2 (M-70, Santa Cruz)**, while
both the main and supplementary Methods describe only a **monoclonal** primary, and the only Runx2
secondary described is **anti-mouse** — which is not compatible with a rabbit polyclonal, and would in
any case raise the mouse-on-mouse background problem on mouse tissue. **The Methods do not describe
the experiment in Figure S6.**

### 3.4 🔴 The selectivity inference rests on an underpowered negative that points the same way

The Discussion concludes WWOX over-expression *"may not affect the cellular phenotype in
WWOX-sufficient cells"*, from K7M2. In Figure 3C, **K7M2 moves in the same direction as the responsive
lines** — WWOX ~0.13 vs GFP ~0.265, roughly half — with `P=0.56` at **n=5** and a MOCK error bar
reaching ~0.47, beyond twice its own mean. In Figure S3 the same line's *baseline* sub-G1 (~5.6–6.5%)
already **exceeds** the AdWWOX-treated HOS value (~6.7%) that is counted as a response.

**`ns` is not equivalence** — a lesson this repository has already recorded twice. The therapeutic
reading *"restore WWOX only where it is lost"* would be an `INFERENZA` resting on this negative, and
the negative does not carry it.

### 3.5 🔴 Two of four confidence intervals in Table 1B use the wrong denominator

The paper states its method (*"95% C.I. are based on exact binomail confidance interval"*). Clopper–Pearson
reproduces the two correct rows **to the second decimal**:

| Table 1B row | Printed 95% CI | CI for the stated fraction | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biopsies 12/20 | 36.05 – 80.88 | 36.05 – 80.88 | ✅ exact |
| Metastasis 8/11 | 39.03 – 93.98 | 39.03 – 93.98 | ✅ exact |
| Resections 4/25 | 5.45 – 41.91 | 4.54 – 36.08 | ❌ printed value is the CI for **4/21** |
| **Total OS 24/56** | **56.60 – 88.54** | 29.71 – 56.78 | ❌ printed value is the CI for **24/32** |

In both defective rows **the denominator used is the NEGATIVE count, not the total**. The two correct
rows are precisely the two where positives exceed negatives, making that ratio impossible — a single
consistent mechanism, not two coincidences. **The Total OS interval lies entirely above its own
printed point estimate of 43%**, which is visible on the face of the table. Every interval in the
**supplement** (7/16, 4/9, 8/12) reproduces exactly, so this is a main-text defect, not a lab-wide
method error. Point estimates are unaffected; any downstream use of *"43% (95% CI 56.6–88.5)"* is not.

### 3.6 A main-text percentage its own supplement contradicts

Body: *"WWOX decreased in post-chemotherapy specimens in **22%** (2/16) of paired samples."*
2/16 = **12.5%**, and Supplemental Table 3 prints **12**. With 22 the paragraph's three categories sum
to **110%**; with 12 they sum to 100.

### 3.7 The functional rescue depends on which clone and which control — visible only in Figure S4

Colony number (Fig S4B): parental HOS ~102, **HOS-EV ~60**, HOS-WWOX **CL11 ~8**, HOS-WWOX **CL8 ~48**.

- The **empty-vector control alone removes ~41%** of colony formation before any WWOX is introduced.
- **The two clones of the same construct differ ~6-fold from each other.**
- Main Figure 4B displays **only CL11**, the extreme clone (*"CL11 is shown as an example"*).
- The KHOS arm used a transduced **pool**, not clones (Fig S5A legend), while the main text refers to
  KHOS-WWOX stable *"clones"*.
- Figure 4D and Figure S5F report invasion as **single percentages beside representative fields**,
  with no dispersion and no n.

The effect is real in direction and is not one number.

### 3.8 The significance operator is missing from the source, and must not be supplied

Figure 4B's caption reads **`(P 0.001)`** — no relational operator — on **two independent surfaces**
(the `efetch` JATS deposit and the PMC rendered HTML page), while the same deposit correctly encodes
`P&lt; 0.0001` three times, `P =0.003` for Figure 4D, and `P &lt; 0.05` in Methods. The supplement
repeats it in the Figure 5S legend — `(P 0.04)`, `(P 0.002)`, `(P 0.001)` — while preserving `<` in
`"<5"` and `"< 90% necrosis"`. **The absence is a property of the source, not of the extraction**, and
per rule 5d it is neither hand-corrected nor read as `P<0.001`.

## 7 · 🔴 An open protocol gap this reading hit head-on

Appending this receipt with the **correct** DOI was **refused**:
`line 133: conflicting identifiers for the same study`. `validate_ledger_sequence` flags a
contradiction when two receipts on one PMID both declare a DOI and the DOIs differ — and the prior
receipt declares a DOI belonging to a different journal. **The ledger therefore refuses the correct
identifier because an incorrect one was persisted first.**

Three routes, all closed:

1. **Write the wrong DOI to match** — refused. That launders a known falsehood into new canonical state.
2. **`reread_reason: receipt_correction`** — impossible. The protocol requires a correction to
   *preserve study identity*, which is the very field that is wrong.
3. **`record_kind: receipt_invalidation`** — inapplicable. That route exists for a receipt whose
   *reading evidence belongs to a different study*; this reading genuinely is of this paper, so
   invalidating would destroy a legitimate partial reading to repair a metadata field.

**Taken:** the DOI is omitted from `study_id` (the checker requires both sides present), and the
correct value is recorded in `evidence_basis` and here. **Reported, not worked around:**
`fulltext_read_receipt.md` has no lawful route to correct a wrong DOI on a persisted receipt whose
reading is otherwise sound.

## 8 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype

**`ESPANSIONE`, and deliberately not more.** This is somatic tumour biology in a WWOX-null mouse and
in human osteosarcoma. There is no WWOX-DEE allele anywhere in it, no neural endpoint, no CNS
measurement. Transferability **T3**.

What transfers is **method, not phenotype**:

- **`IPOTESI` — a post-transcriptional lead, and the cause remains unresolved.** SAOS2 holds ~45% of
  normal WWOX mRNA with barely detectable protein, while KHOS at 0.09 mRNA looks the same on the blot.
  What that pattern licenses is that **the loss is post-transcriptional**; it does **not** identify
  which post-transcriptional step. 🔴 **At least four accounts remain open and this paper excludes
  none of them:** accelerated **turnover**; **impaired translation**; **insolubility**, where the
  protein is made but partitions out of the extracted fraction; and a purely technical floor —
  epitope accessibility, antibody sensitivity or handling — since the blot is semi-quantitative with
  no loading-normalised densitometry. **Collapsing this to "degradation" is precisely the
  over-transfer this repository has already corrected once** (P252A: normal mRNA, accelerated
  turnover, MG-132-insensitive, lysosomal — a route established by assay in *that* paper and not
  transferable here, and whose undue carry to Q230P was itself reversed on audit).
  The Discussion gestures at *"enhanced protein degradation"* from its ref 5 and **never connects it
  to its own panel**. → discovery ledger. **`REVIVAL_TRIGGER`:** a cycloheximide chase in SAOS2 with
  proteasomal versus lysosomal/CMA inhibitors, plus a soluble/insoluble fractionation and a
  polysome or metabolic-labelling readout — the three together are what would separate turnover from
  translation from insolubility. Until then the mechanism is **unresolved** and only its *location*
  (post-transcriptional) is supported.
- **A method warning worth carrying:** a figure **title** can generalise beyond its data while every
  hedge in the running text stays intact and correct. Modality-compression screens do not catch this;
  **scope generalisation in a title is a distinct failure mode** and needs its own check.
- **A second method warning:** where a paper's supplement is retrievable, the paired data that
  qualify its headline are disproportionately likely to live there. Here the supplement supplied the
  reversal (§3.1), the penetrance contradiction (§3.2), the clone/control dependence (§3.7) and the
  antibody gap (§3.3) — **four findings, none of them visible from the main text alone.**

**What this paper is legitimately good for:** it is a well-powered human prevalence and longitudinal
IHC study whose clinical arm (§2A, §2B) is solid and is *unaffected* by every defect above.
