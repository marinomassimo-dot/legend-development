|---|---|

**Systems used, complete list:** mouse only for new data — `Vk∗MYC:Wwox` WT/HET/KO, sorted CD138⁺ and CD19⁺ BM populations, and extramedullary tumours. The single human component is a re-analysis of existing microarray series. **No WWOX disease variant anywhere**; the alleles are wild-type, heterozygous or B-cell-deleted.

## 3 · What is measured, with what controls

1. **A heterozygous arm exists and is carried through the genomics** — HET-BM appears in the WES, signature, CNV and Apobec panels. This is the one dose-sensitivity handle in either paper of this lot, and it is *not* analysed for survival or tumour incidence.
2. **The WT-BM arm is a genuine negative control for driver mutations:** none of the five WT-BM samples carries a cancer-driver SNV (L23, L27).
3. **Tumour incidence is the primary genotype effect**, and the classification is anchored in a per-animal supplemental table (Table S1, L28) listing all 17 KO and 4 WT tumours with CD138/CD19 grades.
4. **The kataegis and signature claims are stated per sample**, so they can be checked: SBS30 in 2 of 3 PBPs, SBS26 in specified BM samples (L13, L14, L32).
5. **Aicda/Apobec2 overexpression is reported with its failed test** — P = .2646 and .2680, n = 4 tumours (L15), and `Apobec1`/`Apobec3` are shown flat (S8, L33).

**Controls that are absent or weak:** **no WT-PBT transcriptome or exome** — the 4 WT tumours in Table S1 were not sequenced, so every "PBT vs BM" contrast is within-KO and cannot separate *Wwox* loss from being a tumour; the χ² incidence test is **not significant by Fisher's exact test** (p = 0.052, recomputed); RNA-seq n = 3/group for the 87-gene comparison; no protein-level confirmation of any inflammatory regulator (TNF, NF-κB, IL-6, CCL2 are **IPA inferences**, and `Tnf`, `Il6`, `Rel` and `Nfkb1` are **not themselves in either DGE list**, L34).

## 4 · What is asserted but not measured

| Assertion | Status |
|---|---|
| *Wwox* **deletion** in the tumour lineage | **Reduction, not absence.** S1b's qRT-PCR shows KO ≈ 0.3 vs WT ≈ 1.5 (L30), and in the RNA-seq the `Wwox` transcript is down **only 1.61 log₂ (≈3-fold), padj 0.021**, in sorted CD138⁺ cells (L34). Consistent with Cd19-Cre efficiency and contaminating non-B cells; it means "KO" here labels a hypomorphic population, not a null one. |
| Wwox loss **"promotes"** plasmablastic tumours | **Supported as incidence, by a marginal test.** χ² P = .036 as printed (L25); Fisher exact two-tailed **p = 0.052**. The paper's own Table S1 shows WT `Vk∗MYC` mice develop the **same tumour kinds** (3 PBPs and 1 PBL of 4) — so the genotype changes frequency, not phenotype class (L28). |
| **Proinflammatory signature** driven by Wwox loss | **INFERENZA from IPA over 87 genes**, of which **31 are immunoglobulin V-region genes** and **19 of the top 20 by effect size are Ig genes**, up *and* down (`Igkv5-45` −7.6, `Ighv14-4` −7.4) — a pattern expected from different clonal populations between animals. The named protumorigenic genes are modest and mid-list (Foxq1 1.64, Vegfa 1.54, Jun 1.44, Tgfb2 1.05; ranks 28/30/34/46 of 87) (L8, L34). No cytokine was measured. |
| **Monoclonal gammopathy** as a Wwox-dependent phenotype | **Explicitly not:** 100% of both genotypes, same age of onset (L4, L5, L31). The Abstract's framing does not claim it; a reader skimming might. |
| Hypermutation reflects **Aid/Apobec** activity | **Hypothesis, imported from ref 43 and not significant here** (L15). Sharpened by the panel: **SBS85, the "indirect effects of Aid" signature, appears only in WT1-BM and KO4-BM — in none of the four PBTs**, including the hypermutated PBL (L32). |
| Wwox loss **impairs DNA repair** (Discussion, L21) | **Imported from refs 3, 26, 72–74**, not measured here. What is measured is a mutational-signature association, and **SBS26 (defective mismatch repair) is present in a wild-type sample too** (L32) — the text says "all BM groups", which is accurate, and which also means the signature is not Wwox-specific in this data. |
| "Extensive" CNVs **across all PBT samples** (Figure 7B caption) | **Overstated for one of four.** Supplemental Table 2 holds **KO2-PBT 59, KO3-PBT 44, KO1-PBT 17 and KO4-PBT only 7** segments, and KO4's own panel is essentially flat (L35). |
| chr11 amplicon involving **Rel, Xpo1, Bcl11a** | **Direction is sample-specific and the text gives only one direction.** Gain in KO1-PBT (cn 4) and KO4-PBT (cn 3) — but the **same interval is a loss (cn 1) in KO2-PBT** (L35). |

## 5 · Discrepancies found only in the images or the supplements

1. **Figure 6C's caption inverts two samples.** It states the green point is *"KO1-PBT (PBP)"* and the red *"KO4-PBT (PBL)"*, while **Figure 6A's column headers and Figure 6B's panel titles both label KO1-PBT as the PBL and KO4-PBT as a PBP** (L32, L36). Read at 400 dpi from the vector artwork, not from the deposited JPEG. Two figures of one paper disagree about which tumour is which histology — and the Aicda/Apobec2 claim is attached to those two points.
2. **Sample column order differs between Figure 5 and Figure 6A** — Figure 5 runs PBP, PBP, PBP, PBL (its caption says so) while Figure 6A runs PBL, PBP, PBP, PBP. Combining the two panels by position silently mismatches samples.
3. **Figure 7B's caption claims uniformity the table denies** (L35, item above).
4. **The 87-gene list is dominated by immunoglobulin genes** — visible only by parsing `mmc3.xlsx` (L34).
5. **`Wwox` is itself in the DGE list at only ≈3-fold down** (L34) — the deletion's own efficiency, readable only from the supplement.
6. **Thiopurine/azathioprine signatures (SBS32, SBS87) in untreated mice**, including wild-types (L32). The Discussion addresses this as an MMR association rather than drug exposure, which is the honest reading, but the panel shows how broadly they are distributed.
7. **Text says the oncoplot's mutated BM samples are "2 HET and 1 KO"** — confirmed at 400 dpi: HET columns 2 and 3 carry mutations, and the single KO-BM hit is **Kmt2c in the last KO column** (L27). My first pass on the 642-px JPEG suspected the panel showed *no* mutated KO-BM sample; re-rendering the vector at 400 dpi before writing showed the text is right. Recorded because the low-resolution surface, not the paper, produced the error.

## 6 · Strength, and transferability to WWOX-DEE

- **Incidence claim:** moderate at best — one cohort, χ² P = .036 that does not survive an exact test, and no difference in tumour class from controls.
- **Genomic-instability claim:** moderate. Real and per-sample (drivers absent in 5/5 WT-BM, present in 4/4 PBTs; CNV burden elevated), but confounded by the absence of any sequenced WT tumour.
- **Inflammation claim:** weak-to-moderate. Pathway-level inference over a 3-vs-3 comparison whose dominant signal is immunoglobulin clonality, with no protein measurement.
- **Human relevance:** association only, from re-analysed public arrays with a median split (L19, L20, L29).
- **Transferability to the reference genotype: T3 / indirect.** Murine B and plasma-cell lineage, no neural material, no disease allele, and a *MYC*-driven oncogenic context absent from WWOX-DEE. What is potentially portable is **not the tumour biology but two general properties of partial WWOX loss**: that a ~3-fold transcript reduction suffices for the phenotypes reported, and that **heterozygous animals already carry driver mutations and MMR signatures while wild-types carry none** — a gene-dose observation in primary cells. Both are `INFERENZA` here and neither was tested for dose-response by the authors.

## 8 · Unresolved ambiguities, carried rather than resolved

1. **Which tumour is the PBL — KO1-PBT or KO4-PBT** (§5.1). Both readings are printed in this paper; nothing in the artefact adjudicates.
2. Whether the **inflammatory signature survives** controlling for immunoglobulin clonality; not answerable from the deposited tables.
3. Why **no WT tumour** was sequenced, given that four existed (Table S1).
4. Whether the **HET arm** shows a survival or incidence phenotype — collected, genotyped, sequenced, and never analysed for either.
5. What fraction of the residual `Wwox` signal in "KO" CD138⁺ cells is escaped deletion versus contaminating non-B cells.
