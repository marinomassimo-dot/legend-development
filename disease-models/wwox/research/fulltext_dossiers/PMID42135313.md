## What the paper did

7,745 people with Parkinson's disease from 24 longitudinal cohorts (biomarker, population-based, clinical-trial designs). Five candidate progression loci chosen from the literature: GBA1 variants, APOE ε4, RIMS2, TMEM108 and one WWOX SNP (rs8050111). Outcome: time to dementia (Cox, random-effects meta-analysis of cohort estimates; pooled frailty models) and yearly MMSE change (linear mixed models).

## WWOX content (all of it)

- **Exposure:** one common SNP, rs8050111, "in the WWOX locus"; position and function not described. Coded as carrier vs non-carrier of the minor allele; a weighted score uses 0/1/2 dosage. No result for WWOX homozygotes is reported. 662 carriers (8.5%).
- **Alone (Table 3):** random-effects HR 1.56 (1.17–2.07) overall; **1.16 (0.81–1.66) in the biomarker cohorts** (the largest group, 65% of patients), 2.30 in population-based and 1.85 (0.69–4.98) in clinical-trial cohorts. Frailty model 1.47; biomarker cohorts 1.10.
- **Heterogeneity:** the WWOX forest plot in the supplement shows a significant test for subgroup differences by design (random effects p = 0.02; common effect p < 0.01). The main text describes WWOX heterogeneity as small and cites "Supplementary Fig. S3", but in the supplement S3 is TMEM108 and the WWOX plot is Supplementary Figure 4.
- **Cognitive slope:** WWOX carriers' extra yearly MMSE decline is null in the pooled model (−0.06, −0.24 to 0.12).
- **Burden ("dose"):** the dose is the **number of loci carried** (0, 1, 2, ≥3), not WWOX allele dose. Figure 1a classifies 5,184 patients (2,784 / 1,907 / 449 / 44), fewer than the 7,745 analysed; the text gives 25% and 5.8% as fractions of 7,745. The difference is not explained in what I read. Only 28 patients carry APOE ε4 + GBA1 + one of RIMS2/TMEM108/WWOX.
- **Independence:** WWOX was nominated by a single earlier study (the authors' 2021 genome-wide survival study, 15 cohorts, 3,821 patients), and 15 of the present 24 cohorts were harmonised in that earlier work. The WWOX estimate is therefore not an independent replication.
- **Mechanism:** one sentence, "WWOX, an Alzheimer's disease GWAS locus, modulates tau phosphorylation", citing a review (PMID 30158849).

## What the paper does NOT state

- Anything about WWOX expression, protein or function in these patients.
- Any effect of loss-of-function WWOX alleles.

## Caveats the authors give

Incomplete GBA1 screening; MMSE insensitive to executive decline; mostly European ancestry; dementia in the first year of PD excluded; more unbiased survival studies needed.

## Transfer limit

A common SNP in adult Parkinson's disease, association with dementia timing only. No transfer to biallelic loss of function, to WWOX-DEE, or to any WWOX dosage threshold. It is the only "dose-response" design in this wave, and the dose it measures is a count of loci.
