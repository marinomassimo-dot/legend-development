## 1 · What the paper did

A **retrospective pooling** of five separately conducted nonhuman-primate studies: 71 cynomolgus
macaques, 47 dosed with one of four rAAV9 clinical candidates, **28 analysed for biodistribution**
plus 20 vehicle controls. The read-out is **vector genome copies in tissue** by qPCR, expressed as
vg per µg genomic DNA and as vg per diploid genome (vg/DG). Six brain regions (prefrontal, frontal,
parietal, occipital, temporal, cerebellum) and three spinal-cord levels, or in one study five
rostro-caudal brain slabs with three samples each.

| Vector | Route | Dose (vg/animal) | Necropsy | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TSHA-101 | lumbar IT | 5.8 × 10^13 | Day 30 | 4 |
| TSHA-102 | lumbar IT | 5.8 × 10^13 | Day 90, 180 | 6 |
| TSHA-105 | lumbar IT | 2.3 × 10^14 | Day 90, 180 | 7 |
| TSHA-120 | lumbar IT | 3 × 10^12 and 1.2 × 10^13 | 52 weeks | 8 |
| TSHA-102 | ICM | 2.3 × 10^14 | Day 90 | 3 |

Three of the four vectors are self-complementary; one is single-stranded.

## 2 · What it measured

- **Brain, at doses ≥ 5.8 × 10^13 vg/animal: 1–4 vg per diploid genome.** Distribution was broadly
  uniform across brain regions and across the rostro-caudal axis.
- **Spinal cord receives roughly an order of magnitude more than brain** — about 5 vg/DG against
  0.16–0.8 vg/DG in brain at Day 30 for the lowest-studied vector at that dose.
- **Cerebellum is the lowest brain region**, about five-fold below prefrontal cortex in the Day 30
  dataset (~0.16 vs ~0.8 vg/DG).
- **Dose scaling is sublinear**: a 4-fold dose increase gave a ~2.5-fold increase in brain vector
  genomes, at both time points.
- **At the low end there is no dose-response at all** across a 3.9-fold range (3 × 10^12 to
  1.2 × 10^13 vg/animal); the authors cannot explain it and suggest assay sensitivity.
- **Brain vector genome levels rose between Day 30 and Day 180**, apparently linearly, at a fixed
  dose.
- **Intracisternal (ICM) at 2.3 × 10^14 gave ~3.5 vg/DG in brain and ~5.5 vg/DG in spinal cord**,
  the single direct route comparison for one vector.

## 3 · What the paper does NOT state — and this is most of it

- **No expression of any kind.** Not mRNA, not protein, not activity. The authors state why: the
  four vectors carry different promoters and regulatory elements, so no expression comparison was
  possible. **Everything here is DNA in tissue.**
- **The authors forbid the inference a reader most wants:** "mean vg/DG must not be understood as an
  estimate of the proportion of transduced cells", and vector DNA detection "offers no assurance
  that the vector genome is fully intact or that it has been internalized into the cell and cell
  nucleus."
- **No function, no behaviour, no histopathology, no dorsal-root-ganglion toxicity arm, no
  immunology, no neutralising-antibody data.** This is biodistribution alone.
- **No cell-type resolution.** Tissue homogenate only; neurons, glia and non-neural cells are not
  distinguished, and the authors note cell-type differences in uptake efficiency as a reason the
  vg/DG figure cannot be converted.
- **No ICV arm in primates.** Only IT and ICM.
- **No controlled comparison.** Studies ran separately under different protocols, which the authors
  say "limits the precision of any quantitative claims".
- **Control contamination is disclosed**: in the TSHA-120 study some negative-control samples showed
  low-level vector genome contamination — the same study that showed no dose-response.
- **Nothing about WWOX.**

## 4 · The safety datum this paper carries from elsewhere

Inside the limitations section the authors report a canine study in which, at similar biodistribution,
**ICV-dosed dogs developed strong and in one case fatal encephalitis driven by T cells specific to
the transgene product, while intracisternally dosed dogs did not.** They add that the relevance to
primates and humans is uncertain. For a restoration spec this is the most consequential sentence in
the paper: two CSF routes that deliver comparable vector genomes differed in whether they triggered a
fatal transgene-directed immune response. Route is not only a distribution parameter.

## 5 · Declared interest

Eight authors are employees or contractors of the sponsor whose four clinical candidates are
analysed; one declares consulting income and inventor royalties from it. The paper closes by citing
early results from that sponsor's own first-in-human trials. The animal measurements are weighted as
real measurements; the framing is read with the interest in view, which is why the authors' own
limitations are given primacy above.

## 6 · Classification under LEGEND_CORE §13

**Neither biomarker nor endpoint.** Vector genome copy number is a pharmacokinetic measure of the
delivery vehicle, not a read-out of the target gene's functional state, and not a clinical outcome.
A **delivery-parameter record**.

## 7 · Transfer limits — stated exactly

1. **Species:** cynomolgus macaque. Closer to human than mouse, still not human.
2. **Quantity:** vector genomes per diploid genome in homogenate — explicitly not a transduced-cell
   fraction, explicitly not evidence the genome is intact or nuclear, explicitly not expression.
3. **Vectors:** four different payloads and regulatory designs, three self-complementary and one
   single-stranded. The authors note from prior work that self-complementary AAV9 gives at least a
   ten-fold transduction advantage over single-stranded — an uncontrolled variable across this
   pooled dataset, and one that bears directly on a cassette whose ORF is too large to be
   self-complementary.
4. **Posture, volume and injection technique** are not reported in a form that makes the numbers
   protocol-portable, and the pooled design precludes it.
5. **No dose-response below 5.8 × 10^13 vg/animal**, so the lower part of the dose range is
   uninformative here.

## 8 · Bearing on the Group B question

- **The route reaches primate cortex, at about 1–4 vector genomes per cell, and only at high dose.**
  That is a real and useful floor for a restoration spec, and it is the first primate number in this
  wave's Group B.
- **It reaches spinal cord about ten times better than brain, and cerebellum worst of the brain
  regions.** For any phenotype with a cerebellar component, lumbar IT is the least favourable
  CSF route among those measured here.
- **It says nothing about whether enough protein is made.** The step from vector genomes to a
  functional WWOX read-out is exactly the step this paper declines to take, and it is the step
  Group B's measurement question (PMID 33129329) shows is itself poorly instrumented.
- **The dose ceiling cannot be lowered by route alone.** Sublinear dose scaling means a four-fold
  dose increase buys about 2.5-fold more vector in brain.
