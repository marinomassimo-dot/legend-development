## What it says (DATO about the review's text; all cited, none new)

- Anaemia and epilepsy meet in three ways: sickle cell disease (more epilepsy, stroke-linked), iron-deficiency anaemia and febrile seizures (contradictory), and anaemia caused by antiseizure drugs.
- Antiseizure-drug anaemia: direct marrow toxicity (felbamate, carbamazepine), immune and oxidative haemolysis (oxcarbazepine and carbamazepine case reports), folate and B12 depletion (carbamazepine, oxcarbazepine, valproate, pregabalin, topiramate, primidone). Management: stop the offending drug, check counts, consider transfusion where haemolysis is severe.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No disease-versus-drug resolution.** The selection file hoped it would separate disease from treatment effects. It does so only qualitatively by listing mechanisms; no prevalence in a DEE or genetic cohort, no denominator and no comparison of untreated against treated anaemia.
2. **No WWOX or DEE cohort; no data on whether anaemia occurs in WOREE/SCAR12.** The only repository item that could record haemoglobin in a WWOX cohort is the held Gao 2025 clinical spectrum paper (partial receipt, supplement unavailable); this review does not touch it.
3. **The seizure-threshold statements contradict each other.** The review says anaemia lowers the seizure threshold and, earlier in the Introduction, that iron deficiency acts as protective by raising it. The Conclusions concede that studies are contradictory. No direction can be taken from it.
4. **Transfusion appears once**, as a single case-report-sourced management step in drug-induced haemolysis. It is not evidence that transfusion changes seizures or development.
5. **Quality limits:** secondary and old primary sources (several from the 1960s-1990s), one non-sequitur in the abstract (daily antiseizure drugs as a strategy "to avoid future seizures" after a discussion of anaemia), a lifestyle figure unrelated to the topic.
