## Core scientific verdict

This paper is historical context, not a new WWOX datum. It captures the field in 2011, when the
global knockout, heterozygous chemical-challenge models and a hypomorph were available, and when
conditional Wwox models were described primarily as future tools. Its most useful lesson is
context dependence: tumour-suppressor phenotypes often require a background, genotoxin or second
lesion. The review therefore proposes compound fragile-site models rather than treating a single
gene knockout as a universal cancer phenotype.

## WWOX synthesis and boundaries

- Wwox heterozygotes are summarized as having higher two-year tumour incidence while sometimes
  retaining the second allele, which the authors interpret as haploinsufficiency.
- ENU exposure is summarized as 80% tumour incidence in heterozygotes versus 40% in wild type;
  NMBA as almost 100% versus 29% forestomach tumours. These are imported primary results.
- Global nulls are summarized as hypoglycaemic, hypocalcaemic, growth delayed and dead by 3–4
  weeks, while explicitly lacking abnormal behaviour or impaired motor skills.
- The femoral lesions and Runx2 mechanism are presented with cautious language: the authors say
  they “speculated” about the transactivation chain and call for further investigation.
- The conclusion proposes an unbuilt `Fhit−/− Wwox+/−` chemical-challenge experiment. This is a
  research programme, not evidence that the interaction exists.

## Relation to later baselines

Compared with Tanna 2018, this review is narrower and earlier: it lacks the mature tissue-specific
WWOX map and still treats conditional Wwox work as prospective. Compared with Steinberg 2021, it
contains no cell-targeted CNS deletion, organoid or neuronal-restoration evidence. Its statement
that global-null mice did not show abnormal behaviour or impaired motor skills is nevertheless an
important historical counterweight to Tanna 2018's later mouse-seizure assertion. Because both are
reviews, the primary sources remain decisive.
