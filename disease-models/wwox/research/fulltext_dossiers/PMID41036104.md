**WWOX occurs zero times in this paper.** That is an earned null for the gene: the paper makes no
statement about WWOX and none is drawn from it. It is read only for the transferable question —
which promoter a CNS restoration cassette should carry — and the transfer limit is stated on every
datum. **Nothing in this file is medical advice.**

## 1 · What the paper did

Ten ssAAV9-EGFP vectors differing only in promoter, each given as a **single intracerebroventricular
injection of 5 µL to C57Bl/6 neonates at postnatal day 0–1**, necropsied at 4 weeks (two promoters
also at 8 weeks). Ten animals per construct for assays plus three for whole-organ imaging; some
groups fewer (p546 and MAG N = 9; rMBP and control N = 8).

Promoters: CAG (1,700 bp, ubiquitous), tCAG (581 bp), EFS (240 bp), **p546** (truncated MeCP2,
neuron-directed), GFAP (full length), gfaABC(1)D (truncated astrocyte), **gfa1405** (1,405 bp, new,
astrocyte), CNP and MAG and rMBP (oligodendrocyte-directed).

Read-outs: EGFP mRNA by RT-qPCR against Actin in brain regions, three spinal-cord levels, sciatic
nerve, heart, liver, quadriceps; whole-organ fluorescence (**declared qualitative only** by the
authors); quantitative western blot; and immunofluorescence colocalisation with NeuN, GFAP, S100B
and Olig2.

## 2 · What it measured

**Reach, as percent of brain area with EGFP** — CAG 42%, p546 41%, CNP 37%, gfa1405 35%, GFAP ~24%,
gfaABC(1)D 9%, EFS 5–9% (up to 16%), tCAG 5.8%, MAG 5.5%, rMBP 2–4%.

**Mean EGFP intensity (RFU)** — CAG 474.8, gfa1405 412.1, GFAP 387.7, p546 154.0, gfaABC(1)D 91.54,
CNP 45.51, MAG 9.548, tCAG 8.443, rMBP 3.258, EFS 2.678.

**Cell-type specificity, as percent of EGFP-positive cells colocalising** —

| Promoter | astrocyte (S100B) | neuron (NeuN) |
|---|---:|---:|
| CAG | 36% | 27% |
| p546 | 1.9% | 72% |
| GFAP | 44% | 13% |
| gfaABC(1)D | 58% | 19% |
| gfa1405 | 37.7% | 11.9% |

CNP: up to 16% Olig2, 21–29% NeuN, **~50% of transduced cells never identified**. MAG: up to 65%
Olig2, 13.5% NeuN.

**Headroom.** The new gfa1405 saves 803 bp against full-length GFAP and the authors state it
"maximize[s] the accommodating transgene length to **2.3 kb**", inside a declared 4.8 kb ssAAV
packaging capacity.

**Durability.** gfa1405 and p546 held their distribution, intensity and cell specificity between 4
and 8 weeks.

## 3 · The finding that bounds the cell-specific-promoter argument

The selection premise for this paper was that it would decide whether a WWOX cassette should be
neuron-restricted or broader. It supplies a different and more useful answer: **a cell-restricted
promoter did not keep the transgene out of the liver.** The astrocyte-restricted gfa1405 gave robust
hepatic EGFP mRNA and, by quantitative western blot, **the most hepatic EGFP protein of any promoter
tested** — more than the ubiquitous CAG. Whole-organ fluorescence showed almost nothing there, so
the fast read-out missed it entirely.

The Discussion then reassures the reader about liver burden — but the paragraph is about **CAG**,
whose hepatic transcript was modest and whose hepatic protein was minimal. The one promoter with
confirmed significant hepatic protein is the cell-restricted one, and the Discussion does not return
to it. This is not an error in the data; it is an asymmetry in what the prose emphasises, and it
matters because the entire safety rationale offered for cell-restricted promoters in this same
Discussion is hepatic transgene expression in the clinical AAV fatalities it cites.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT state

- **No dose-response.** One dose per vector, and the doses are **not matched across arms**:
  7.0–8.5 × 10^10 vg per animal, a spread of about 21% in a head-to-head promoter comparison that
  the paper does not adjust for.
- **No adult animal.** Neonatal P0–P1 only; the authors acknowledge this and defer the adult
  characterisation to future work.
- **No primate, no large animal, no human tissue.**
- **No therapeutic transgene.** EGFP throughout, with an SV40 intron and polyA. The paper says
  nothing about how a 414-codon oxidoreductase would behave in the same cassette, nor about protein
  folding, turnover, dose-sensitivity or toxicity of an overexpressed product.
- **No immunology, no toxicity, no histopathology, no functional outcome.**
- **No absolute quantification.** Every mRNA value is a fold change against Actin; every protein
  value is a relative blot ratio or a fluorescence intensity; the authors say fluorescence intensity
  "does not necessarily reflect absolute expression levels".
- **Nothing about WWOX, any WWOX variant, or any WWOX-related phenotype.**

## 5 · Caveats the authors themselves give

- Whole-organ imaging is "used in our study solely as a qualitative readout".
- Sole testing in neonatal animals "may not provide a complete picture of promoter activity".
- The qPCR, western-blot and immunofluorescence sections of brain were cut at different rostro-caudal
  positions, so the hindbrain region in particular is "vulnerable to variability between the given
  assays" — the authors' own explanation for mRNA/protein disagreements.
- The oligodendrocyte promoter CNP "has more widespread distribution but is also leaky and less
  specific to oligodendrocytes than initially expected".
- p546's western-blot signal could not be made to corroborate its imaging data; the authors attribute
  this to dilution of a soma- and neurite-restricted signal in bulk tissue.

## 6 · Classification under LEGEND_CORE §13

**Neither biomarker nor endpoint, and not about the target gene at all.** EGFP is a reporter of

This is a **vector-design parameter record**.

## 7 · Transfer limits — stated exactly

1. **Species and age:** mouse, neonatal P0–P1 at dosing, 4 weeks old at necropsy. Promoter behaviour
   in a developing mouse brain does not transfer to an older brain or to a primate one; the authors
   themselves show that gfaABC(1)D behaved differently here than in transgenic mice.
2. **Route:** intracerebroventricular. Every number is specific to direct CSF delivery into a
   neonatal ventricle, not to intrathecal, intracisternal or systemic delivery.
3. **Cargo:** EGFP. A promoter ranking obtained with a small, well-tolerated, highly stable reporter
   does not transfer to a cassette carrying a different protein.
4. **Vector:** single-stranded AAV9. The paper itself notes that episomal single-stranded vectors
   are "more susceptible to local cellular environments".
5. **Statistic:** percentages of EGFP-positive cells colocalising with a marker are not transduction
   efficiencies of that cell type; they say what fraction of transduced cells are of that type.
6. **The dose spread across arms is unadjusted**, so small differences between promoters within
   about 20% should not be read as promoter effects.

## 8 · Bearing on the Group B question

For specifying a CNS restoration cassette in advance, this record supports three statements, each
with the limits above:

- **Reach and cell restriction trade off, but less than expected.** A neuron-restricted promoter
  (p546, 72% NeuN, 1.9% astrocyte) achieved essentially the same brain-area coverage as the
  ubiquitous CAG (41% vs 42%) at about a third of the intensity. Restriction cost intensity, not
  reach.
- **"Cell-specific" is not "peripherally silent."** The astrocyte-restricted promoter put the most
  protein in the liver. Any cassette whose safety case rests on promoter restriction must measure
  hepatic protein, not hepatic fluorescence, and not hepatic transcript alone.
- **Headroom is a promoter choice.** 1,405 bp of promoter left 2.3 kb for the transgene in a
  single-stranded AAV9. Taking the 414-codon figure for the WWOX ORF from the assignment rather than
  from any source read here, ~1.25 kb of coding sequence would sit inside that budget with room for
  a polyA and a short regulatory element — but this paper measures no such cassette, and the
  arithmetic is arithmetic, not evidence.
