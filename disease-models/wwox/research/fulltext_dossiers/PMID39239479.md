## What it is

A behavioural characterisation of a constitutive Irp2-null mouse (backcrossed to C57Bl/6J), the iron-regulatory-protein-2 knockout whose human counterpart is biallelic IREB2 loss with dystonic cerebral palsy, epilepsy, microcytic hypochromic anaemia and frontal atrophy (human findings cited from earlier case reports, not measured here). Tests: rotarod, hang test, hot/cold plate, open field, elevated zero maze, Y-maze, Barnes maze, touchscreen extinction and reversal learning, hindlimb clasping.

## WWOX content

None (zero occurrences). **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** The paper is a comparator, not a test of anything in a WWOX system.

## What it measures (DATO)

- Null mice: about 40 percent shorter rotarod latency; shorter hang time after weight adjustment (nulls were lighter); reduced thermal nociception on hot and cold plates; mild hyperactivity; no anxiety difference; intact spatial working memory (Y-maze); Barnes-maze search-strategy and reversal-probe deficits (n = 6 per group); touchscreen reversal learning impaired (about threefold more learning errors, 9 vs 13 mice).
- Not measured here: haemoglobin, red-cell indices, iron content of brain or blood, seizures or EEG. The human anaemia and epilepsy are cited context; the mouse paper has no readout for either.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No data about anaemia, transfusion or any iron intervention.** No haematology and no treatment arm. It cannot speak to whether correcting anaemia or iron status would modify a neurological phenotype.
2. **The mouse is much milder than the human disease, by the authors' own statement.** A mouse null phenotype of modest behavioural effects stands against a severe human disorder; the converse is the situation in this repository, where the Wwox-null mouse is severe and short-lived. The paper therefore illustrates that severity does not transfer between species or between null and hypomorph states in either direction; it is not evidence about WWOX. INFERENZA.
3. **Small, partly under-powered subgroups.** Barnes maze n = 6 per genotype; Figure 1C splits 54 animals into four age-sex groups of about five to twelve; the hang-test effect rests on a weight covariate. Text gives p below 0.05 for the rotarod statistics where Figure 1A is marked with three asterisks, and the figure legend's significance key is truncated (a text/figure grain mismatch, no consequence for the direction).
4. **The mechanisms offered are speculation by the authors**: impulsive or novelty-seeking behaviour for the motor result, and monoamine synthesis ("double hit" with mitochondrial dysfunction) for the cognitive one. Neither is tested here.
5. **Mitochondrial dysfunction is cited, not measured** (earlier work in this mouse). No bearing on the WWOX respiratory-chain findings can be drawn from this paper.

## Design and limits

Single laboratory, constitutive null, mixed age cohorts across tests, sexes pooled with sex tested in the rotarod only, blinded scoring stated for some assays. No statistical correction across the battery.
