## Verdict

The engineered deletion of exons 2–4 eliminates detectable Wwox protein and causes complete pre-weaning mortality in homozygotes. In adult heterozygotes, spontaneous tumor-bearing animals were 10/58 versus 2/60 WT; after ENU, tumor incidence was 37/46 versus 20/42, with more lung tumors and lymphomas. These are direct genotype-by-context tumor-susceptibility data.

The celebrated juvenile osteosarcoma result is much less settled. Four of 13 null mice had focal periosteal lesions interpreted morphologically as osteosarcoma/chondroid osteosarcoma, some at day 3 or 5. No molecular tumor markers, blinded pathology, clonality or longitudinal behavior are reported. A later independent conditional-null study (PMID 19936220) found zero neoplasia in nine nulls by four modalities and explicitly left the discrepancy unresolved. Therefore “Wwox-null mice develop osteosarcoma” remains `CONFLICTING EVIDENCE`.

The paper's “haploinsufficiency” conclusion is also interpretive: tumors retain Wwox immunoreactivity, but protein positivity does not demonstrate an intact second allele or unchanged dosage.

## Internal source errors

The ENU lymphoma paragraph twice labels heterozygote results as WT. It says 59% of “ENU-treated WT mice” had enlarged spleens versus 31% of “ENU-treated WT mice”, and later says aggressiveness was higher in WT than WT. Table 1 and the surrounding argument establish the intended order as HET 27/46 (59%) versus WT 13/42 (31%), with higher aggressiveness in HET. The dossier preserves this as an author-text inconsistency; it does not silently rewrite the source.
