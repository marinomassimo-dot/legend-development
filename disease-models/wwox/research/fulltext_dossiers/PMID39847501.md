## What the paper did

AAV9 carrying mouse Navβ1 cDNA with C-terminal myc/FLAG tags under a **truncated mouse Gad1
promoter**, plus WPRE and a rabbit β-globin polyA; empty-vector control under CMV. Bilateral ICV in
Scn1b-null, heterozygous and WT littermates on C57BL/6J. Survival (Kaplan-Meier), continuous infrared
video seizure scoring by three blinded investigators on a modified Racine scale, hyperthermia-induced
seizure challenge at P16, RT-qPCR of somatosensory cortex, Western blot with PNGase F deglycosylation,
quantitative double-label IHC for cell-type specificity and coverage, and whole-cell current-clamp
recordings from cortical PV⁺ interneurons and pyramidal neurons.

## What it measured

- **Dose and route.** 1 µL per hemisphere at P2 → 1.1–1.7 × 10¹¹ vg/mouse; 2 µL per hemisphere at P10
  → 2.2–3.4 × 10¹¹ vg/mouse (the Results text says 3.4 × 10¹¹, "twice the dose"; the Methods give the
  range — a small internal inconsistency). Vector titres 5.3–8.5 × 10¹³ gc/mL.
- **Age effect.** P2: lifespan extended well past P100 (p < 0.0001), hyperthermia seizures abolished
  (0/6 treated null vs 5/5 untreated null having grade 5–6 seizures ending in SUDEP between 37.2 °C and
  37.7 °C), Scn1a mRNA restored to WT level, PV⁺ and pyramidal depolarisation block reversed.
  P10: all 8 treated null pups died in the usual P16–P25 window; survival indistinguishable from
  untreated.
- **Cell-type reach.** Anti-myc signal was 87.4 % (WT) / 83.8 % (null) restricted to NeuN⁺ neurons;
  34.4 % / 26.4 % of NeuN⁺ neurons transduced; GABAergic specificity ~10.9 %, coverage 22.4 % / 14.8 %;
  PV coverage 45.9 % / 36.0 %; SST coverage 63.8 % / 50.6 %; VIP coverage 7.75 % / 17.72 %. No signal
  in microglia (Iba1) or astrocytes (GFAP).
- **Seizure counts (Table 1, read from the table image).** Untreated null, 3 mice × 3 days:
  average 16.4 events/day < 10 s and 8.7 events/day > 10 s. Treated null, 3 mice × 3 days: average
  8 events/day < 10 s and **0** events/day > 10 s. **n = 3 per arm, no statistical test in the table.**

## 🔴 The selection's premise, tested and corrected

The wave-3 selection called this "the transferable **window** lesson". The paper itself does **not**
claim an irreversible developmental window. It attributes the P10 failure to **expression level and
biodistribution**: "We attribute this effect to the relatively low level and distribution of the
Navβ1 transgene in comparison with P2-injected mice", and supports that with its own IHC showing
"Brain expression of Navβ1-myc protein in P10-injected animals was substantially restricted in
comparison with P2-injected animals", with no expression in frontal cortex or cerebellum and animals
in which the signal was near zero. It also notes single-stranded AAV needs 7–14 days to reach
therapeutic levels, so expression would have arrived after seizure onset at P10–13. So the question
the selection wanted settled is settled, and the answer is **dose/biodistribution, not window** —
by the authors' own reading and their own negative control. For a human programme that is the more
actionable finding: it is a delivery problem, not a biological deadline.

## What it does **not** say

- "AAV-Navβ1 administration to WT mice resulted in β1 overexpression in brain but no obvious adverse
  effects" is an absence-of-evidence statement at the organism level, and the paper's own
  electrophysiology contradicts its innocence at the cellular level: treated **WT** PV⁺ interneurons
  showed "a leftward, hyperpolarizing, shift in the I-O curve … with increased AP firing at lower
  current injections", which the authors ascribe to extraordinarily high β1. Overexpression in a
  competent brain changed excitability; it merely did not produce seizures or death in this cohort.
- There is no EEG. The null pups were too small and fragile to implant, so every seizure datum is
  video-scored behaviour, and the authors say so: "Without electrographic evidence, we cannot be
  confident that these events were indeed seizures."
- Patients are not null. Except for one splice-acceptor variant, SCN1B DEE alleles make protein; the
  authors state that overexpressing WT β1 may not be appropriate, or safe, for all of them.
- Body weight was never rescued: treated nulls stayed under 10 g to P80 against ~20 g for treated WT.
  Lifespan and seizures moved; growth did not.

## Transfer limit to WWOX-DEE (stated exactly)

Transferable: the **experimental design** — a single neonatal bilateral ICV dose with a late-dosing
arm as its own control, and an IHC readout that explains a negative result instead of leaving it to
interpretation. Also transferable as a caution: an endpoint that moves (seizures, lifespan) can sit
beside an endpoint that does not (growth), and in a DEE the developmental endpoint is the one that
matters to a family.
**Not transferable:** the age mapping (mouse P2 vs P10 has no validated human equivalent, and the
authors explicitly say it "remains to be established whether this phenomenon occurs in human
infants"); the dose; and the genetics — Scn1b-null is a constitutive null that models neither of the
reference-genotype alleles, and β1 is a channel subunit whose replacement restores a measurable
current, whereas WWOX has no comparable fast functional readout.
