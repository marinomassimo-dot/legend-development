## 1. What the paper did

*Drosophila melanogaster* genetics, three systems:

1. **Egr/TNFα modifier screen in the eye.** Low-level ectopic Eiger/TNFα under GMR-GAL4 gives a
   small, rough eye. WWOX dosage was varied against it: two independent RNAi lines
   (`UAS-WWOX-IR#1` v22536, `#2` v108350), two heterozygous loss-of-function alleles
   (`WWOX¹`, `WWOX²`), and two overexpression constructs (`UAS-WWOX-ORF`, `UAS-WWOX-cDNA`).
   Readout: adult eye area in pixels (ImageJ, ten randomly selected eyes per genotype).
2. **Wing-disc apoptosis.** hh-GAL4 drives Egr/TNFα plus GFP in the posterior compartment;
   cleaved-caspase-3 immunostaining area as a proportion of total disc area; Ci marks the anterior
   compartment; CellRox for ROS.
3. **Cell competition.** MARCM clones mutant for *scribbled* (`Scrib¹`) in the eye disc, with
   WWOX knocked down inside the clone (`Scrib¹; WWOX-IR#2`), or with reduced/absent WWOX in the
   whole animal (`WWOX¹/+`, `WWOX²/+`, `WWOX¹/WWOX²`). Readouts: GFP⁺ clone area as a percentage
   of disc area, total disc area, adult eye area, size-binned necrotic patches, and adult
   viability.

## 3. What the paper states

* WWOX modulates Egr/TNFα-mediated cell death in vivo: reducing it suppresses the phenotype,
  increasing it enhances it, and the modulation is specific — altered WWOX does **not** modify the
  eye phenotypes of ectopic p53 or Hid (S2 Fig).
* WWOX acts through cleaved caspase-3, and the authors propose ROS as the link, on the strength of
  their own earlier work; SOD1 or SOD2 overexpression suppresses the Egr phenotype (Fig 2).
* Ectopically expressed WWOX stays **cytoplasmic** in eye and wing discs, with and without Egr
  (Fig 3, S3 Fig) — explicitly contrary to the mammalian reports of nuclear translocation for
  pro-apoptotic WWOX that they cite.
* WWOX is required cell-autonomously for the elimination of *Scrib* mutant clones.

## 4. What the paper does NOT state

* **It measures no ROS in any WWOX-manipulated animal.** The only CellRox data (Fig 2A–2C) compare
  Egr-expressing to non-expressing compartments of the same disc, with WWOX unaltered. The
  WWOX→ROS link is carried entirely from reference 30 and is labelled a hypothesis
  (*"We now hypothesise that…"*).
* It does not examine any neuron, any CNS tissue, or any excitability or behavioural endpoint.
* It does not report a *Drosophila* WWOX phenotype in the absence of stress: *"Ectopic expression
  of WWOX alone does not result in any obvious cell death-induced phenotype"*, and the
  trans-heterozygous animals that lack WWOX entirely are **viable** and were scored as adults.
* It reports no n, no error bars and no test for the Fig 6L necrotic-patch bins.
* It gives no dose series: "↓WWOX" is RNAi or one copy, "↑WWOX" is a GAL4-driven transgene; the
  intermediate levels are not sampled.

## 5. Caveats the authors themselves give

* Endogenous WWOX *"are too low to be detected in D. melanogaster"*, so localisation is studied on
  ectopic protein only, and the S1 Fig H blot shows the empty-vector lane blank.
* The ORF overexpression results *"were more variable despite comparable levels of expression"*.
* Others report a mild WWOX effect on ectopic *hid* that this paper does not see.
* The ROS mechanism is *"context dependent"*, given opposing reports.

## 6. 🔴 The quantification that contradicts the paper's own claim

The Results say: *"Decreased WWOX activity together with ectopic expression of Egr/TNFα … resulted
in a decrease in the relative area of Caspase 3 staining (Fig 5C, 5D and 5G). Conversely, increased
WWOX expression increased the relative area of Caspase 3 staining (Fig 5E–5G)."* The legend agrees
(*"(D) Decreased WWOX expression results in a decreased region of Caspase 3 staining… (F) Increased
WWOX expression results in a increased region of Caspase 3 staining"*), and panels C–F are labelled
accordingly (C, D = ↓WWOX; E, F = ↑WWOX).

**Panel 5G is labelled the other way round.** Its x-axis reads `↑TNFα control` (≈21.5 %),
`↑TNFα ↑WWOX` (≈18 %, `**`), `↑TNFα ↓WWOX` (≈24 %, `*`) — i.e. the quantification as drawn shows
*more* WWOX giving *less* caspase-3 and *less* WWOX giving *more*, the opposite of the text, the
legend and the panel labels above it.

Panel 5G is the **only quantification in the paper supporting the central caspase-3 claim.** Either
its axis labels are transposed or the result is inverted; from the distributed article this cannot
be decided. Either way the effect is small (±≈15 % of a 21.5 % baseline) with substantial scatter
across ≈22–25 discs per genotype.

Separately, Methods declare that the most severely affected discs were **excluded** from the clone
quantification: *"Significant disruption to eye disc morphology was observed in 13/52 pairs of the
Scrib¹ clones and 31/50 pairs Scrib¹;WWOX-IR clones and these were not included in these
analyses."* The exclusion removes 26 % of control and **62 %** of knockdown discs, i.e. it removes
preferentially from the arm with the stronger phenotype, biasing the clone-area comparison toward
the null.

## 7. Answer to the assigned question, for this source

Nothing in this paper is a rescue readout for the WWOX-DEE genotype class, and the paper's value
lies elsewhere.

* **Adult eye area under an Egr/TNFα challenge is the one well-powered, quantified,
  dose-sensitive endpoint**: a single heterozygous loss-of-function allele shifts it significantly
  (S1 Fig F), two independent RNAi lines shift it `****`, and the direction reverses on
  overexpression. It is a genuine in-vivo **gene-dosage** assay. It is also an insect eye under
  ectopic human-TNFα-orthologue expression: as a readout for a human neurodevelopmental disorder
  it is a modifier screen, not an endpoint.
* **Direction is the transferable fact, and it is a caution.** Less WWOX → less stress-induced
  death; more WWOX → more. Under
  [`LEGEND_CORE.md` §13](../../../../framework/instruction/LEGEND_CORE.md#13-biomarker-scope-hard-rule)
  caspase-3 activity is a Tier-3 pathway readout, not a WWOX biomarker, and it cannot be sampled
  in a living patient's neurons — so it is **recorded as a rejected candidate readout**, and
  carried only as a direction-of-effect observation.
* The *Drosophila* complete null (`WWOX¹/WWOX²`) is **viable to adulthood**. That is a hard model
  limit: the fly does not reproduce the lethality or the neurological severity of biallelic human
  WWOX loss, so a negative result in this system has little power against a human phenotype.

**Transfer limit.** *Drosophila* WWOX shares 49 % amino-acid identity with the human protein; the
assay is epithelial, developmental and stress-induced; the "gain" arm is a GAL4 transgene on a
background where endogenous Wwox is below the blot's detection limit, so "overexpression" here is
closer to "expression".
