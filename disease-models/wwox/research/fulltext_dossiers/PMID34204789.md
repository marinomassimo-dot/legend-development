## 1 · The decisive question, and its answer in one line

**How was "WWOX-dependent" established?**
**By splitting bulk tumour RNA-seq on a WWOX expression cut-point and correlating transcript
abundances. WWOX was never perturbed.** There is no knockdown, no overexpression, no CRISPR, no
rescue and no protein measurement anywhere in this study.

> *"Optimal WWOX expression cut-point was determined to separate high- and low-expressing groups of
> patients. The obtained cut-off value, 222.6, had significantly separated groups"*

> *"was used to correlate the top genes with WWOX using Spearman's rank correlation coefficient."*

---

## 2 · What the paper did

672 TCGA GBM + LGG + paired-normal samples (FireBrowse RNA-seqV2, RSEM). Pipeline: Evaluate
Cutpoints → GSEA (1,898 genes, FDR < 0.25) → Monocle3/UMAP module discovery → edgeR differential
expression per clinical comparison → multiple SVM-RFE feature ranking → ROC → replication on an
Agilent 244K microarray cohort from UCSC Xena → GEPIA2 Spearman correlation with WWOX → Kaplan-Meier
and Cox with an ESTIMATE-score covariate.

> *"A total of 672 samples were included in the study. Determining the suitable WWOX expression
> cut-point to stratify the population into two groups was achieved with the help of the R-based
> Evaluate Cutpoints tool designed in our Department"*

⚠️ The cut-point tool is the authors' own — a disclosed methodological self-dependency.

## 3 · The three readouts, with their actual numbers

| Gene | Nature | Spearman R with WWOX | Direction in GBM | Best discrimination reported |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **PLEK2** | transcript (bulk tumour) | −0.44 | up | AUC 0.9663 for G4 vs G2 |
| **RRM2** | transcript (bulk tumour) | −0.42 | up | AUC 0.9884 for untreated GBM vs normal |
| **GCSH** | transcript (bulk tumour) | +0.43 | down | AUC 0.8775 for G4 vs G3 |

Every AUC is for discriminating a **tumour class** (grade, tumour vs normal), never for
discriminating WWOX state.

## 4 · §13 classification — the answer to the assignment

| Criterion (§13) | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Does it measure the functional state of WWOX? | **No.** It measures a second transcript whose abundance co-varies with WWOX transcript abundance across tumours |
| Tier 1 (direct gene readout)? | No — not WWOX protein, mRNA, splice or activity |
| Tier 2 (proximal pathway with strong causal linkage)? | **No — the causal linkage was never tested.** Correlation of |R| ≈ 0.42–0.44 across bulk tumours is the whole of the "dependence" |
| Tier 3 (distal clinical/neurophysiological endpoint)? | No — it is not a clinical endpoint either |
| Samplable in a living patient (CSF, blood, imaging)? | **No.** Bulk tumour tissue only. None of the three has a reported CSF, blood or imaging assay |
| Validated per §13 (sensitivity/specificity in the disease population)? | **No**, and the authors say so |

> *"usefulness of PLEK2 , RRM2 , and GCSH as diagnostic or predictive biomarkers is yet to be
> confirmed"*

🔴 **VERDICT: REJECTED CANDIDATE — neither biomarker nor endpoint.** It fails §13 at the first
clause. The reason is not that the work is weak within its own frame; it is that the frame is
glioma classification, and a glioma-classification transcript is not a measure of WWOX function in
any tissue, least of all neural tissue in a living child.

## 5 · What the paper does **not** state

- It never claims these measure WWOX. "WWOX-dependent" is used throughout to mean
  "differentially expressed between WWOX-high and WWOX-low tumours".
- No protein-level measurement of anything, including WWOX.
- No CSF, blood, imaging or any body-fluid assay.
- No neural tissue outside tumour; no non-tumour brain except TCGA paired normals.
- Nothing about WWOX-DEE, any WWOX allele, or gene therapy.
- GCSH is explicitly ruled out even as a *therapeutic* target by the authors, because its
  expression falls rather than rises with grade.
