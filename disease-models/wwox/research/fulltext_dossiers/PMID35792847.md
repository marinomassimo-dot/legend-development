## What the paper did

A retrospective case series from one paediatric neurology service: nine children with homozygous WWOX variants from six families, with clinical, MRI, EEG and genetic description (Tables 1-2), plus a literature search (performed once, by one author) that adds 61 published patients from 19 studies and tabulates the epilepsy of all 70 (Table 3). Genetic testing was exome, a 597-gene exome-based panel with CNV analysis, or familial targeted testing. No functional work.

## What the paper states

- Genotype-phenotype correlations "in our cases and previously reported cases" are claimed in the **abstract**; the body contains **no genotype column in Table 3 and no correlation analysis**. The claim is unsupported by anything the paper prints.
- Seizure-type frequencies over 70 patients (spasms 50 %, focal 40 %, GTC 26 %, myoclonic 23 %); 78.6 % onset in the first three months; vigabatrin effectiveness "not well established".
- WWOX "might play a role" in Ohtahara syndrome (Discussion) — a suggestion, no data.

## Overlap and counting (checked after the first pass against held sources)

1. **Family 2 is probably the R54* sibship of Ehaideb 2018 (`PAPER 119`, PMID 30746283), re-described without cross-citation.** The two reports agree on allele pair (homozygous R54*), sibship structure (older sister, younger brother), sex, onset at two months with spasms, the sister's hypertelorism / large ears / high arched palate, and an MRI description that is word-for-word the same ("generalized asymmetrical brain tissue volume loss" with "asymmetrical ventricular dilatation" and callosal thinning). The ages printed are 18 months (sister) and 3 months (brother) in 2018, and 5 y and 3 y 9 mo here; the sister "was first seen in our hospital at the age of 18 months, with her younger brother". One discrepancy: Ehaideb gives burst suppression for the brother's EEG, this paper hypsarrhythmia (different recordings are possible). `PREMISE: INFERENZA` — strong, not author-confirmed. This paper also lists Ehaideb's three children as separate literature rows in Table 3, so its 70 counts the sibship twice.
2. **Tarta-Arsene 2017 and Piard 2019 patient 8** (one patient, `CC-20261003W4-A-PATIENT-OVERLAP-01`) are two rows of Table 3.

## What the paper does NOT say

- It does not test genotype-phenotype correlation, despite the abstract.
- It does not cross-reference any of its own patients to earlier publications.
- It does not report the second-allele search in the Q230P-heterozygous affected brother, nor any RNA study of c.606-1G>A.
- It gives no seizure-outcome denominator per drug.

## Reader's observations (INFERENZA, labelled as such)

- The three P47T homozygotes are the mildest in the series (onset 6 mo, no microcephaly or optic atrophy, seizures controlled in two of three, one ambulant with ataxia and tremor): an intermediate between the SCAR12 description of the same allele and WOREE. The authors label all nine WOREE. `PREMISE: INFERENZA`, n = 3 from two families.
- The Q230P homozygote (onset 21 d, suppression-burst, optic atrophy, band heterotopia) sits at the severe end; seizure freedom on vigabatrin plus diet is one patient's course, not a treatment effect. Genotype caution: Q230P homozygous is not the reference genotype (compound heterozygous); the datum transfers only as "Q230P in a homozygote can present as severe DEE".
- A Q230P-heterozygous affected sib is a heterozygote **with a phenotype**; it is not a demonstrated haploinsufficiency case — an undetected second allele (deep intronic or structural, which exome and panel testing can miss) is the simpler explanation. `PREMISE: INFERENZA`.
