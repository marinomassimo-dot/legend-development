## What it is

A narrative review from a Drosophila lipid-neurobiology group. It covers VLCFA biology (myelin, membranes, neuronal development), a proposed route from VLCFA accumulation to inflammation (VLCFA to sphingosine 1-phosphate in glia, then NF-kB in vertebrates or the IMD pathway in flies), fly models (ACOX1 loss and gain of function, ACSL mutants, PEX, ELOVL), therapeutic classes (ELOVL1 and ACC inhibitors, bezafibrate, fingolimod, gene therapy, diet) and gaps. The central mechanistic source is the senior author's own fly study. WWOX: zero occurrences. It reports no new measurement.

## What it says (DATO about the review's text)

- VLCFAs are low in abundance but enriched in myelin and rafts; mouse CerS2 knockout is cited for short-chain-ceramide myelin and progressive myelin loss.
- Glial VLCFA accumulation drives S1P synthesis and secretion to neurons, with NF-kB or IMD activation and macrophage recruitment (fly work, with a mouse extension cited).
- The authors themselves state that direct causative evidence is lacking, that the trigger and the microglial sensor are unknown, that some X-ALD patients with raised VLCFA stay asymptomatic, and that a severe fly and human but mild mouse ACOX1 phenotype is unexplained.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Nothing about WWOX.** No VLCFA, S1P or sphingolipid measurement in any WWOX model, and the review does not discuss WWOX.
2. **The VLCFA to S1P to inflammation route is one group's fly mechanism**, extended to mouse by citation; it is not shown in a WWOX or P47T tissue, and the review labels the causal link unproven.
3. **The review cannot say whether VLCFA are raised or lowered in WWOX loss.** The CerS2 and ELOVL1 literature read in this lot goes the other way (loss of the very-long species with a shift to C16-C18), so the direction of any WWOX lipid change is not given by this review.
4. **Therapeutic classes are listed, not tested for WWOX.** Nothing here is a medication recommendation.
