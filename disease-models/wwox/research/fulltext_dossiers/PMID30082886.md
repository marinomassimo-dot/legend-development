## Figure and supplementary audit

All four main figures were inspected at native pixels. PPTX effective-PPI was measured before
rendering: slide 1 was vector/text, and raster ceilings for slides 2–4 were 59.8, 119.3 and 190.4
ppi. The whole deck was therefore rendered at 59.8 ppi. Supplementary Figure S3 visually supports
the on-target WWOX rescue. Both DOCX supplements were read in full. The XLSB was converted to XLSX
and its OOXML inventoried: one `Wwox-cKO` sheet, range A1:IV47731, 47,731 rows.

Figure 1 shows 14/17 Wwox-deleted mice developing tumours and no tumours in the WT controls, but the
WT denominator is not printed in the survival panel. Figure 2 supports broad Trp53-locus loss in
three of four Wwox tumours; qPCR validation in panel D shows only two Wwox tumours. Figure 4A prints
the complete deletion table: 2/5 TP53-homozygously-deleted tumours also delete WWOX, versus 6/2047
without TP53 deletion. The Fisher result is therefore statistically striking but rests on extremely
small altered groups.

Figure 4B reports an overall four-group chi-square P value but no pairwise TP53-only versus double-
perturbed test. The body says WWOX perturbation was not associated with poorer survival within
TP53-perturbed patients, while the caption says poorer survival is observed in the double group.
That is a text/caption interpretive inconsistency, not a defensible `text_contradicted_by_panel`
case: the plotted curves and omnibus test do not establish the required pairwise claim.

## Scientific verdict

Mammary-epithelial Wwox deletion on a tumour-susceptible C3H/HeJ background is associated with
spontaneous grade-III, ER/PR-negative, frequently CK14-positive mammary tumours. The result is not a
clean test that Wwox loss alone is sufficient: the Wwox line was backcrossed seven generations to
C3H/HeJ, while the comparator Trp53 model remained B6/129. Genetic background is therefore fully
confounded with genotype in the cross-model comparison, and the authors explicitly propose a
C3H-facilitated second hit.

Tumour profiling, locus analysis and MCF7 CRISPR experiments support association between WWOX loss,
TP53 instability or repression, impaired ATM/KAP1/p53 signalling and increased clonogenic survival.
The human METABRIC co-deletion signal is based on only eight WWOX-homozygously-deleted tumours and
does not justify the Discussion phrase “commonly seen.” The paper supports cooperation and a
second-hit model more strongly than autonomous WWOX sufficiency.

## Tensions, transfer and hidden gold

- Wwox and Trp53 mouse cohorts have different strain backgrounds; transcriptomic similarity cannot
  remove that causal confounding.
- Tumour penetrance is 14/17 for Wwox deletion, but the normal WT denominator is not visible in the
  main survival panel and no randomisation or blinded outcome assessment is established.
- METABRIC sample counts differ between the prose (2509 samples) and the plotted deletion analysis
  (N=2052), consistent with analysis-specific availability but insufficiently explained.
- **Disease transfer:** low to WOREE. **Genotype transfer:** low; somatic deletion and tumour-selected
  second hits differ from germline reference genotypes. **Intervention transfer:** low; rescue is a
  mechanistic control, not a treatment study.

## Process report

- **Ambiguity:** “somatic loss” in the title can sound sufficient; the body explicitly qualifies it
  with a background-dependent second hit.
- **Tool refusals:** the presentation renderer lacked `pdf2image`; direct Node rendering failed
  because `node` was absent; Poppler's `pdftoppm`, `pyxlsb` and `openpyxl` were also unavailable.
  LibreOffice conversion, PyMuPDF rendering and direct OOXML inspection supplied bounded fallbacks.
- **Deduced rather than read:** causal confounding by strain, fragility of the rare-event Fisher
  result and absence of the needed pairwise survival contrast are design/pixel inferences.
- **Could not do:** reproduce GSE117387 processing, separate strain from Wwox effects, or infer a
  survival penalty for double perturbation from the published omnibus plot.
