## What it is

A narrative review that develops the hypothesis that, in paediatric developmental and epileptic encephalopathies, seizure activity drives classical complement (C1q, C3) deposition on healthy synapses and microglial CR3-mediated phagocytosis, so that preserving synaptic density rather than preventing necrosis defines neuroprotection; it surveys rodent status epilepticus data, human surgical tissue, CSF and serum biomarkers, candidate inhibitors (the anti-C1q antibody ANX005, the C3 inhibitor pegcetacoplan), safety, the therapeutic window and limitations. WWOX: zero occurrences.

## What it says (DATO about the review; all cited)

- Complement C1q and C3 tag synapses in development; in rodent chemoconvulsant status epilepticus at P14-P21 they rise and microglia engulf spines; C3-null mice show less neurodegeneration (cited).
- Human tissue evidence (C1q, C3, iC3b up in focal cortical dysplasia and TLE) is largely adult or mixed-age; Rasmussen encephalitis CSF has complement fragments.
- Neither ANX005 nor pegcetacoplan has shown efficacy in epilepsy; chronic C1q or C3 inhibition in children could disturb normal pruning and raise infection risk.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No WWOX or genetic DEE complement datum.** Evidence is from acquired status epilepticus models and surgical tissue; the review concedes it cannot show whether complement drives pathology or follows seizures.
2. **Complement inhibition is not shown to help any child with epilepsy.** The authors say so for both named agents.
3. **Text-versus-panel gap.** "As shown in Figure 2" the text reports reduced microglial phagocytosis with C3 inhibition in young mice; Figure 2 is a schematic with no data (panel qualifies text).
4. **Internal contradiction on timing.** The text says seizures give "a distinct time frame of approximately 48 h" to intervene; Table 1 states the acute timeline is unknown and the sequence hypothesised, and the review says the therapeutic window is a critical gap.
5. **Direction of effect is not secure.** C3 null protects in the cited status epilepticus model, yet the same review warns inhibition could disturb normal pruning; the selection file lists a C4-deficient mouse seizure-mortality paper (37234916) as a counter-lesson, which had not been read at the time of writing this dossier (it is read later in this wave). The review's own warning is enough to say that lowering a complement factor is not assumed protective.
6. **Not about plasma products.** Complement inhibitors are drugs, not plasma exchange, FFP or immunoglobulin. The review's relevance to H2 is the circulating-factor biology only.
