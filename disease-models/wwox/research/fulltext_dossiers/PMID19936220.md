## Verdict

**The imported premise is half true, and the half that is false is the half that matters.**

- **Early death is first-hand and quantified.** 43% of knockouts dead by 72 h, 77% by day
  17, none surviving past weaning. This is measured here, in this cohort, with n = 35.
- 🔴 **Epileptogenesis is not measured here in any form.** There is no EEG, no seizure
  observation, no behavioural assay, no brain histology and no neurological examination
  anywhere in the paper. The only brain measurement in the entire study is *brain weight*
  in Table 2. The words *seizure* and *epilepsy* occur in the body exactly once each,
  inside a single Discussion sentence citing a **rat** model — reference 26, Suzuki 2009
  (PMID 19500159) — whose allele is a 13-bp exon-9 frameshift, not a null, and whose
  seizures are *audiogenic*.

So the citation chain PMID 30290271 → PMID 19936220 → *epileptogenesis* does not terminate
in a mouse measurement. It passes through this paper and lands in the rat `lde` literature,
which the queue already tracks as `FT-042` (PMID 19500159) and `FT-041` (PMID 17803050).
**Naming this paper as the source of an epilepsy phenotype is a misattribution across two
species and two allele classes.**

Second, and independently valuable: this paper documents, in the same P14–P18 window in
which hippocampal phenotypes are reported elsewhere, a **measured systemic metabolic
crisis** — hypoglycaemia, metabolic acidosis, uraemia, hypocalcaemia, leukopenia and
anaemia. That converts "systemic confounding remains plausible" from a speculation into a
quantified alternative explanation with a citable source.

## Experimental design

- **Reagent generated:** a conditional `Wwox` allele, exon 1 flanked by loxP, made by
  positive–negative selection (pgk-neo positive, DT-A negative) in 129/svJ-derived
  sequence. Two correctly targeted lines from 240 ES clones screened. Chimeras onto
  C57BL/6.
- **The neo cassette is retained** in the 5′ regulatory region and was explicitly tested:
  `flox/flox` crosses gave normal Mendelian ratios, litter size and postnatal development,
  so the floxed allele is not a silent hypomorph. This is the validation that makes
  `Wwox^flox` usable.
- 🔴 **Figure-only method detail:** the Figure 1A key defines **FRT sites**, and two of them
  flank the neo cassette in both the targeting vector and the conditional allele — so the
  cassette is FLP-excisable. FRT is never mentioned in the body text. (The published
  caption also describes panels A–C while the image carries four panels, A–D.)
- **Knockout route:** `EIIA-Cre` (Jackson stock 003724), acting at the zygote stage in all
  cells including germ cells → germline `Wwox^ΔCre`. Intercross gave 39/85/30 of 154 at
  3 days, χ² p = 0.257.
- 🔴 **Different Cre driver from the importing study.** PMID 30290271 uses **BK5-Cre**. Both
  studies use *this* floxed allele, so this paper is the primary for the **allele** and only
  partially for the **model**. "Same knockout" is not supported.
- **Cohort sizes are small throughout:** organ weights n = 3/3/3; blood chemistry
  n = 3/3/4; bone histomorphometry KO n = 3 against WT+HET pooled n = 5; WBC n = 2 per
  genotype; necropsy series n = 9 KO.

## What the study directly shows

### Ablation of Wwox protein — and where it was checked

Immunoblot shows Wwox absent in knockout and roughly halved in heterozygote. 🔴 The tissues
are **kidney, lung and spleen only** (Figure 2C, visible in the image; the Results say only
"a variety of tissues"). Figure 6 adds kidney immunohistochemistry, one animal per genotype,
with the knockout section serving as the antibody-specificity control.

**No brain lysate appears anywhere.** EIIA-Cre at the zygote stage makes global deletion the
expected outcome, but expectation is not measurement: *this paper does not establish Wwox
absence in brain.* `PREMISE: DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK` — "a zygotic Cre deletes everywhere" is
exactly the kind of load-bearing background assumption this system requires to be named.

### Growth and mortality — first-hand

- 4.2 g at P14 versus 6.82 (HET) and 7.07 g (WT).
- Figure 3B, read as an image: the knockout curve is already low at day 3 (~1.65 vs
  ~2.35 g) and is **flat from day 10 to day 17** at ~4 g while WT and HET rise linearly.
  This is growth **arrest**, not slowed growth. WT and HET curves are superimposed.
- Survivor bias worth naming: the n = 8 knockouts plotted at day 17 are the survivors of a
  cohort 77% of which was dead by that day.
- The χ² for Mendelian ratios was run at 3 days — inside the window in which 43% of
  knockout mortality occurs. Observed KO 30 against 38.5 expected leans the same way as
  early loss. A passing χ² here does not exclude perinatal loss.

Plus splenic atrophy (9.3 mg, 0.21% of body weight, against 0.53%/0.46%; p = 0.0015) with
reduced red-pulp cellularity, a thinned thymic cortex, and 3% nucleated red blood cells in
one of two knockouts examined.

**Why this matters beyond this paper:** hypoglycaemia and metabolic acidosis independently
alter interneuron marker expression, glial reactivity and seizure threshold. Any hippocampal
marker phenotype measured in a *systemic* Wwox null at two to three weeks is measured in an
animal that is simultaneously hypoglycaemic, acidotic, uraemic and anaemic. This does not
refute such phenotypes; it means a systemic-null design cannot separate cell-autonomous
neuronal Wwox loss from secondary metabolic injury — which is precisely the gap the
conditional allele generated here was built to close, and which nobody has used it to close.

### Brain weight — read carefully

Table 2: absolute brain weight 0.390 → 0.356 g (−8.7%); relative brain weight 5.0% → **8.5%**
of body weight, p = 0.0003. The significance attaches to the *relative* figure and is driven
by the collapsed denominator. This is **brain sparing during wasting**, not brain overgrowth.
A reader importing "increased brain weight in KO mice" without the ratio will invert the
biology.

### Bone

Reduced bone volume (BV/TV 13.35% → 5.8%), reduced mineralised volume (Md.V/TV 13.3% →
5.7%), fewer and thinner trabeculae; osteoblast and osteoclast numbers unchanged. Conclusion:
a **mineralisation** defect, not a resorption defect.

Three cautions recovered by reading rather than stated by the authors:
1. 🔴 **A p = 0.07 trend becomes an observation.** Results: knockout femurs "**tended** to
   have more osteoid … p = 0.07". Discussion: "we **observed** … concomitant increase in
   osteoid". Same failure class this system tracks in its own outputs.
2. 🔴 **Number-order hazard.** The paper writes "13.35% vs. 5.8%" while the sentence subject
   is "KO mice" — but 13.35 is the WT/HET value. Anyone importing these pairs can invert
   them. The same inversion recurs three times.
3. 🔴 **An unreported direction.** "a significant difference in the number of active
   osteoblasts over bone surface (N. Ob/BS) … (p = 0.02)" — direction never stated, no
   figure or table, and it directly follows a sentence saying there were *no* significant
   osteoblast differences.

Figure 5C, read as an image: each knockout bar carries a single asterisk defined as
`*p<0.05`, while the Results text reports `p<0.01` for BV/TV and Md.V/TV. Knockout error
bars on BV/TV and Tb.N span roughly half the bar height at n = 3.

### Neoplasia — an explicit, unresolved conflict

Nine 18-day knockouts by complete necropsy, X-ray, multi-organ histopathology and microCT:
**zero** lesions compatible with neoplastic growth, and specifically zero osteosarcoma.
Aqeilan 2007 (PMID 17360458) reported 4 of 13 (31%) with periosteal osteosarcoma-compatible
lesions, one as early as day 3. The authors state the discrepancy and leave it open: *"The
reason(s) for the discrepancies between studies remain to be determined."* A second conflict
sits alongside it: Aqeilan reported higher osteoclast activity **without quantitation**;
this paper quantified it and found none.

`CONFLICTING EVIDENCE`, not settled biology. "Wwox-null mice develop osteosarcoma" is a
contested property.

### Heterozygotes

Normal on every measured axis — growth, survival, blood chemistry, bone. *"only one Wwox
allele is sufficient for full function."* Relevant to how carrier genotypes are reasoned
about; no mouse evidence of haploinsufficiency here.

## The authors' own hypothesis, and what happened to it

The paper proposes that death is caused by **renal tubular acidosis**, on the strength of
elevated BUN, reduced bicarbonate, and Wwox's strong expression in distal convoluted tubules
(Figure 6). It is explicitly flagged as speculation: *"we hypothesize"*, *"we speculate"*.

Field density, measured 2026-08-06: the PubMed query `WWOX AND ("metabolic acidosis" OR
"renal tubular acidosis")` returns **exactly one record — this paper.** `WWOX AND
hematopoiesis` returns 3 (one is this paper); `WWOX AND "bone mineralization"` returns 1
(this paper). `WWOX AND osteosarcoma` returns 28.

In seventeen years, nobody — including these authors — has tested the mechanism this paper
proposes for why Wwox-null animals die. The field kept the oncological reading of the mouse
and discarded the systemic-metabolic one, even though the systemic phenotype is what
actually kills the animals and is the phenotype most likely to confound brain measurements
made in the same window.

## Transfer to the reference genotype

`ESPANSIONE` throughout — mouse systemic null, oncology-motivated phenotyping, no human
allele modelled. What transfers is **methodological**, not phenotypic:

1. A systemic Wwox-null rodent at 2–3 weeks is a metabolically decompensated animal.
   Brain phenotypes from such models carry a measured, citable confounder.
2. The conditional allele exists and is validated. The nervous-system-restricted deletion
   that would separate cell-autonomous from systemic effects is *available* and, per the
   density counts, largely unused.
3. Allele class matters: the rat `lde` seizure phenotype comes from a C-terminal frameshift,
   not a null. Transferring it to null models requires an explicit argument.
