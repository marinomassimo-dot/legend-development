## What it measures

Case-control association of six common SNPs (EHBP1 x2, TUBB x2, WWOX x2: rs2222896 and rs2278075) with coronary artery disease (622), ischemic stroke (593) and controls (638), Guangxi Han, hospital-based; serum lipids in controls by genotype; haplotype, gene-gene and gene-environment models (GMDR and logistic regression). WWOX genotype: two common intronic or nearby SNPs (MAF 15 to 40 percent). No coding, loss-of-function, expression or protein measurement; no WWOX-related disease subject.

## What it shows about WWOX (DATO)

- rs2278075 T allele frequency: controls 14.6 percent (186 of 1276), CAD 21.2, stroke 22.9 (Table 2); dominant-model adjusted OR 1.63 (CAD) and 1.81 (stroke), the only WWOX result passing the paper's Bonferroni line of P below 0.00021 (Table 3). rs2222896 does not pass it.
- In controls, rs2278075 AT carries lower HDL-C (beta -0.312, Table 4); rs2278075 TT has too few controls (12).
- The paper says all six SNPs alter TC, TG, HDL-C, LDL-C and ApoA1/ApoB in controls (Figure 1; P below 0.0004).

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No dosage or loss-of-function information.** Common-SNP associations do not say WWOX is lower or higher in carriers; nothing here bears on heterozygous loss, null or hypomorph (selection question 2 is not answered).
2. **The control group is the outlier for the one WWOX hit.** The paper quotes 1000 Genomes CHB at T 22.0 percent and says the Guangxi Han minor alleles are similar. Its controls are at 14.6 percent; both case groups (21.2 and 22.9) sit at the reference value. Treating the reference as exact, the controls are 6.4 standard errors below it (reference sampling error not available from the paper, so this overstates). The "association" can be read as controls low as easily as cases high; the paper does not discuss it. INFERENZA (arithmetic on printed numbers).
3. **The lipid-by-genotype results are not physically plausible as common-SNP effects.** All six SNPs, in three different genes, are reported as altering nearly every lipid trait in 638 controls at P below 0.0004. Table 4 prints t statistics of 7.9 to 15.2 for single genotype terms on TG; a t of 15.2 with about 630 residual degrees of freedom implies a partial R-squared near 0.27 for one genotype term (arithmetic: t^2 / (t^2 + df); df approximated). GWAS-scale effects of common lipid SNPs are of the order of a few percent of an SD. Several printed betas lie outside their own 95 percent intervals (for example rs2222896 AA to AG on TG: beta -0.647, interval -1.053 to -0.741), consistent with a standardised beta printed beside an unstandardised interval; the table does not say. The heterozygote effect exceeds the homozygote effect for rs2222896 on TG (-0.647 versus -0.381), a non-monotone pattern. INFERENZA: these numbers are internally unusual and are not a basis for a WWOX lipid effect size.
4. **The deposited genotype file cannot support the joint analyses.** `Table1.XLSX` reproduces Table 2 exactly (all counts, all six SNPs, all groups). But in the CAD and stroke groups each genotype column sits in a handful of long contiguous blocks in file order (rs2278075 in CAD: five runs, A/T x30, T/T x14, A/A x386, T/T x14, A/T x178; rs3130685 in stroke: four runs), whereas the control rows are in a mixed order. A patient-level table in arbitrary order would show hundreds of runs per column; the pattern is that of columns ordered independently of one another. Within-row joint genotypes in those two groups therefore cannot be relied on, and haplotype, six-SNP and gene-gene results that need them cannot be re-derived from the deposit. The row-wise dosage R-squared of the two EHBP1 SNPs from the deposit (0.811, 0.941, 0.714 for controls, CAD, stroke) tracks the R-squared the paper prints for the same pair and groups (0.821, 0.938, 0.690), so the printed LD values are at least consistent with having been computed from the same row structure. OBSERVATION about the deposit; it does not show what the authors' analysis software received, and the deposit has no covariates, so the adjusted ORs cannot be checked either way.
5. **Citations do not match their sentences.** The Introduction attributes a WWOX rs2222896 genotype and LDL-C finding in an offspring cohort to "Johannsen et al., 2018"; reference 16 is a case report of a WWOX SDR-domain missense variant with complete protein loss in an epileptic encephalopathy. The rs2278075 genome-wide result "(p = 5.0 x 10^-9)" is attributed to "Joseph et al., 2017", reference 17, an epidemiology review. The statement that moderate drinking protects arteries cites "Saez et al., 2010", reference 29, which is the WWOX HDL association paper. The same pattern runs through the Introduction and Discussion (TUBB statements, the coronary-calcification GWAS, the Japanese dyslipidemia GWAS). The reference list appears to be offset from the in-text author-year labels; the paper's WWOX statements (WWOX regulates lipoprotein metabolism via ABCA1/ApoA1; an rs2278075 GWAS hit) therefore cannot be traced to a source from this article.
6. **Design limits the authors name.** Medications that alter lipids were not recorded; lipid-genotype associations could not be examined in cases; diet was not collected; controls are health examinees while cases are inpatients, with different alcohol and smoking profiles (Table 1; controls drink more).
