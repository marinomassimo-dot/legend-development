## 1 · What the paper did

A first-in-human, open-label, **single-patient** (n = 1), single-administration study of
intra-cisterna-magna (ICM) AAV9 gene transfer (AAV9.CB7.hIDUA) in severe mucopolysaccharidosis
type I, reported with more than five years of follow-up. Delivery was CT-guided ICM puncture under
sedation after a lumbar puncture for contrast; the recipient was in the **second year of life** at
screening. The subject remained on weekly intravenous enzyme replacement throughout and never
received a haematopoietic stem-cell transplant.

**The Orchestrator's selection card described this as "a small, open-label first-in-human cohort".
It is not a cohort. It is one patient.** This is the first premise correction of the group.

## 2 · What it measured

| Axis | Measured? | How |
|---|---|---|
| Route tolerability | yes | clinical course, discharge next day |
| Adverse events | yes | 23 AEs catalogued to date, attribution recorded per event |
| Target-gene function | yes | CSF IDUA enzyme activity (serial, to week ~290) |
| Substrate | yes | CSF heparan sulfate; urine total GAG / HS / DS |
| Anti-transgene immunity | yes | plasma and CSF anti-IDUA titres |
| CNS structural safety | yes | annual brain MRI |
| **DRG toxicity** | **no** | no nerve conduction studies were performed |
| Biodistribution | no | no tissue sampling; the subject is alive |

## 3 · What it states

- The ICM procedure was safe and well tolerated; treatment-emergent adverse events were mild and
  self-limiting; serial brain imaging has been normal; no neoplasms over >5.5 years.
- Of 23 recorded adverse events, 17 occurred in the first year and **16 of those 17 were assessed as
  possibly related to immunosuppression**, not to the vector. The attributable burden of this
  procedure, as the authors themselves score it, sits with the immunosuppression regimen.
- Immunosuppression was heavy and prolonged: methylprednisolone once pre-dose, then prednisone
  tapered over 8 weeks, tacrolimus to 32 weeks, sirolimus to 48 weeks — **48 weeks of triple
  immunosuppression** around a single CSF-route dose.
- CSF IDUA rose by week 8, peaked at 35 weeks, and **declined after immunosuppression weaning
  began**; at last measurement it is 6.8% of mean control. Plasma anti-IDUA returned to baseline at
  week 59 and peaked at 1:102,400 at week 85, after immunosuppression withdrawal.
- CSF and plasma antibody compartments are **not equilibrated**: the plasma:CSF anti-IDUA titre
  ratio at matched timepoints ranged from 320:1 to 1,280:1.
- Despite CSF IDUA falling to 6.8% of control, CSF heparan sulfate did not rise appreciably.

### The dose literal in this paper is printed with the wrong sign — do not carry it as printed

> "Following successful computed tomography (CT)-guided access to the cisterna magna with a 25-gauge
> neonatal spinal needle, 1E−10 vector genome copies/g brain mass (1.265E−13 genome copies) of
> RGX-111 in 5 mL of Elliotts B artificial CSF was injected over 5 min without adverse effects."
> — Results, "Clinical outcomes"

The abstract prints the same `1E−10 vector genomes/g brain mass`. Both are typographically
impossible: a negative exponent would mean a fraction of a single genome copy. The intended values
are recoverable by the paper's own internal arithmetic — `1.265E13 ÷ 1E10 = 1265`, a brain mass in
grams that is plausible for the recipient's age band, whereas no pairing of the printed negative
exponents yields a mass at all. The published rendering has lost the superscripts in **both** the
abstract and the body. Any number carried from this paper's dose statement must be carried with
this defect declared.

## 4 · What it does NOT state

- **It does not measure dorsal-root-ganglion toxicity.** The authors are explicit: *"Although no
  formal nerve conduction studies were performed, due to the protocol being a treatment
  investigational new drug (IND), the subject did not complain of paresthesias and did not
  demonstrate abnormal deep tendon reflexes, suggesting there was no clinically significant dorsal
  root ganglion (DRG) toxicity."* A child dosed in the second year of life cannot reliably report
  paraesthesia, and deep tendon reflexes are not a DRG sensory assay. This is an absence of
  clinical complaint in one child, not a measurement of DRG integrity.
- It does not establish a dose–response: one subject, one dose.
- It does not report biodistribution, DRG vector load, or any necropsy endpoint.
- It does not report an equivalent for a gene without a secreted, cross-correcting product.

## 5 · Caveats the authors themselves give

- "While these data are from a single patient…" — the authors foreground n = 1 in the discussion.
- They report, as a cited concern and not as their own finding, **an intraventricular CNS tumour
  demonstrating vector genome integration in a different severe MPS I child** who had received ICM
  RGX-111 in the separate phase 1/2 study at approximately age one year, and present the absence of
  neoplasm in their own subject over >5.5 years against it. This is the single most consequential
  safety item reachable from this paper, and it is **cited, not measured here**: it is a reading
  debt against that other report, not evidence this paper supplies.
- The orthopaedic and ophthalmologic course continued to progress, which the authors attribute to
  somatic disease beyond the reach of a CNS-directed dose.

## 8 · Verdict

**INGEST** — as transferable human delivery-safety evidence only. No WWOX content. The paper's
value to the model is: (a) the only human intracisternal AAV9 datum in the second year of life in
this group; (b) an immunosuppression precedent; (c) a worked example of a published dose literal
that cannot be carried as printed; (d) an explicit, author-acknowledged **absence** of DRG
measurement where a reader would otherwise assume one.
