## 3 · What the paper establishes

1. **`DATO` — WWOX's first WW domain binds a PPPY peptide, and this is a direct binding
   experiment, not a screen inference.** Against four proline-rich ligands and eight WW domains,
   GST–WWOX-1 and the tandem construct bound **only** the PPPY peptide, with GST alone blank and
   every control lighting its own ligand (FBP11/PPLP, FBP21+FBP30/PPR, PIN1/PLT\*P, YAP/PPPY)
   (Fig. 1b, inspected at 600 ppi).
2. **`DATO` — the domain is WW1, and it is required as well as sufficient for one endogenous
   partner.** WW1 alone pulls endogenous SIMPLE from MCF-7 extract, WW2 alone pulls nothing, the
   WW1-inactivating mutant loses the band and the WW2-inactivating mutant keeps it (Fig. 3b).
   The authors' own hedge is carried verbatim: *"The interaction with the individual WW domains and
   with the mutated WW domains both showed reduced binding to the first WW domain"* — so WW2 is
   offered as a stabiliser, not a non-participant.
3. **`DATO` — PPxY dependence demonstrated by mutagenesis in one partner.** In SIMPLE, mutating
   the first PPSY reduces binding, mutating the second does not, and mutating both abolishes it —
   against a total-protein lane for the double mutant that is **the heaviest of the eight**, so the
   loss is not a loading artefact (Fig. 3a).
4. **`DATO` — five array candidates, four confirmed, one never identified.** COTE1, NF-κB
   activating protein, WBP-1 and SIMPLE/PIG7 were re-tested by far-Western on bacterial protein;
   the fifth clone *"has not been identified as a cellular encoded protein"* and appears in **no**
   confirmation lane.
5. **`DATO` — the motif is a family.** Table 1 gives PPAY, PPSY and PPPY across the candidates and
   the compound run **PPPYYPPEY** in COTE1. Only **PPPY** (as a peptide) and **PPSY** (in SIMPLE)
   were ever tested directly.
6. **`DATO` — endogenous WWOX is perinuclear/Golgi in MCF-7, and not mitochondrial**, with partial
   SIMPLE overlap (Fig. 4a–i). The authors name the conflict with the mitochondrial reports
   themselves.

## 4 · What it does NOT support — the negatives, which are the product

- **Not binding in neural tissue, and not *in vivo* in any ordinary sense.** There is no animal, no
  neuron and no brain tissue in this paper. Its "*in vivo*" means *in a cell line* — MCF-7, a breast
  cancer line. **[EXPECTATION]** I came to this text expecting a neural mechanism because the claim
  it underwrites is used in a neurodevelopmental model; the paper licenses nothing of the kind.
- **Not "abolished" as an absolute, and not a motif class.** The specificity is **relative to four
  tested ligands**, and the paper's own absolute negative fails on its own panel: on the CDC25
  (PLT\*P) row there are **faint bands in the WWOX-1 and WWOX-2 lanes**, while the text says
  *"GST–WWOX-2 did not interact with any of the ligands tested"*.
- **Not five validated partners.** Four were confirmed by far-Western; one was never identified.
  And the array shows **at least two prominent uncircled dark spots** plus several at the top-right
  corner, with **no selection criterion stated anywhere** — so "five candidates" is a selection from
  the autoradiograph, not its content.
- **Not a quantified interaction.** No affinity, no stoichiometry, no densitometry, no statistics.
  *"Significantly reduced"* (Fig. 3a, SIMPLE mPY1) is an eye judgement in a paper with no tests.
- **Not proof that WBP-1's PPPY is the target in full-length protein.** For WBP-1 the pulled-down
  band sits at **the same height as the asterisked nonspecific GST band** that runs across all eight
  lanes, so that conclusion rests on intensity at a position the paper itself marks nonspecific.
- **Not a general account of WWOX interactions, and the authors say so:** *"the p53 proline-rich
  region does not contain any PPXY motifs"*. WWOX's most-cited partner does not carry the motif.
- **Not a function.** Nothing here measures what the interactions do. The functional language
  (p53, JNK1, TNFα, NF-κB) is all citation and speculation.

## 6 · Group assessment — primary for the gene, not for the disease

The **originating laboratory**: this group cloned *WWOX* and named FRA16D, and Mark Bedford (same
department) is a WW-domain methodologist. Measured 2026-09-13: `Aldaz CM[Author]` **138** records,

**Weigh the two halves separately.** The biochemistry is method-expert work with its own positive
controls — strong observation. The interpretation is wholly cancer-framed (FRA16D, LOH, xenograft
suppression, p53/JNK1/TNFα), and in 2004 the germline neurological phenotype did not exist in the
literature. **The risk this creates is ours, not theirs:** nothing here over-reads the nervous
system, but a later reader can carry an in-vitro cancer-cell result into a neurodevelopmental claim.

## 7 · Does `CLAIM 007`'s premise survive contact with its primary?

**Yes — and it is stronger than a restatement, with three boundaries the restatement had lost.**

- **Survives:** WWOX's **WW1** is a Group I WW domain that binds **PPxY**-type ligands, demonstrated
  by direct binding against four ligands and eight domains, with the domain requirement confirmed by
  mutagenesis on an endogenous partner. A missense lesion **inside WW1** is therefore mechanistically
  interpretable as a binding lesion — which is exactly what the P47T logic needs from this paper.
- **Boundary 1 — nothing neural, nothing in vivo.** Peptides, bacteria and one breast cancer line.
- **Boundary 2 — "specific" is relative to four ligands**, and the panel shows faint WWOX-1/WWOX-2
  PLT\*P signal that the text denies.
- **Boundary 3 — "PPxY motifs" is a family-level phrase resting on two tested members**, PPPY as a
  peptide and PPSY in SIMPLE.
