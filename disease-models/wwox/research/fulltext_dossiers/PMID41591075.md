## What it is

A large narrative review of BBB damage in epilepsy: permeability changes after insults, cellular mechanisms (endothelium, pericytes, astrocytes, microglia), the plasminogen-activation system, albumin extravasation and astrocytic TGF-beta signalling, immune-cell infiltration, comorbid depression and anxiety, drug resistance, and therapeutic targets (losartan, miRNA antagonists, imatinib, levetiracetam, valproate). WWOX: zero occurrences.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** The review is built on acquired models (kainic acid, pilocarpine status epilepticus, TBI, ischaemia) and surgical tissue; there is no developmental or genetic encephalopathy section.

## What it says that bears on H2 question 4 (DATO about the review; all cited)

- Albumin that leaks into the brain, in rodent experiments by intracerebral injection, lowers seizure threshold by acting on astrocytic TGF-beta receptor II (Kir4.1 loss, potassium and glutamate accumulation, perineuronal-net loss, IL-6); blocking the receptor (for example with losartan) prevents albumin-induced epileptiform activity.
- Human evidence for the arm: extravasated albumin and activated astrocytes in resected and post-mortem tissue; raised CSF TGF-beta1 and upregulated TGF-beta pathway proteins in temporal lobe tissue.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Nothing about WWOX, a genetic DEE, or the Wwox-null brain.** Whether a WWOX-null brain leaks albumin, or has astrocytic TGF-beta signalling changes, is unmeasured in this repository (H2 question 4: not testable from this paper).
2. **Albumin here is a leaked brain-exposure, not a therapy.** The experiments inject albumin into brain tissue or study leakage after insults; intravenous albumin infusion, plasma exchange or plasma products are not studied. The review cannot be read as evidence for or against giving albumin. INFERENZA about relevance; DATO about content.
3. **Human evidence is associative and mixed.** Endothelial claudin-5 is reported lower in one tissue study and higher in another; BBB leakage, TGF-beta and MMP-9 elevations come mostly from drug-resistant surgical cohorts, so cause versus consequence of chronic seizures and drugs is not separated.
4. **Direction is context-dependent by the review's own account:** tPA-null mice are less seizure-prone while neuroserpin-null mice are more so; infiltrating leukocytes can be damaging or protective; a damaged barrier increases permeability yet is said to reduce drug penetration; TGF-beta is pleiotropic and also falls in depression. The therapeutic conclusion ("restoring the BBB ... represents a viable strategy") is stronger than the evidence the review itself presents ("none of the studied candidates ... fully prevent epileptogenesis").
5. **Therapeutic suggestions rest on rodents** (losartan, antagomir timed to barrier opening, imatinib with valproate); the one human item is an observational hypertension cohort.
6. **No stated search method or appraisal** for a 297-reference review; selective sourcing cannot be excluded.
