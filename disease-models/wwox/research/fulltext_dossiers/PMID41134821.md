## What the paper did
- A 7 T MRI protocol (3D RARE, 78 x 78 x 292 um voxels, about 1 h per scan, isoflurane) measuring the L4 DRG cross-sectional area (CSA)
  from a maximum-intensity projection; CSA is the paper's stand-in for DRG volume.
- Test-retest in five wild-type male mice (ten L4 DRGs): ICC 0.9; limits of agreement -0.060 to 0.066 mm2; relative difference
  per DRG ranged about -15 % to +19 % (Table 2, extracted cell-wise); the Bland-Altman plot shows variability rising with CSA.
- Fabry (Gb3-synthase transgenic, alpha-galactosidase A knockout, male) mice: untreated, AAV9-null, AAV9-GLA, all IV at
  6.25e12 vg/kg to 8-week-old animals; MRI at 8, 12, 16, 20, 24 weeks. Wild type n = 5 (male C57BL/6).
- Group sizes: Table 1 gives Fabry 3, AAVnull 5, AAVGLA 5 (13); Methods say thirteen Fabry mice; Results says fifteen. Two AAVnull mice died before week 24.
- Ex vivo at 24 weeks: H&E of L4 DRG (representative images), LAMP1 IHC, MPZ in paw nerves (n 11/12/13 fields or animals per group as legended), hot plate on a separate cohort (n 11/13/13).

## What it states
- DRG CSA at 24 weeks about 25 % larger in Fabry than wild type (0.35 vs 0.28 mm2); enlargement visible from the 8-week baseline, significant from 16 weeks
  after pooling untreated and AAVnull groups (they did not differ; n 3 and 5).
- AAV9-GLA group: no significant difference from wild type at any time point; smaller than the pooled Fabry groups from 16 weeks to 24 weeks.
- H&E: hypertrophied vacuolated DRG neurons in Fabry and AAVnull; LAMP1 reduced after AAV9-GLA; MPZ-positive paw fibres higher after AAV9-GLA;
  hot-plate latency normalised after AAV9-GLA, up to 5-fold prolonged with AAVnull.
- "Response to AAV treatment" in the title is a therapeutic response of a storage lesion (DRG shrinks toward wild type when the enzyme is supplied).
  It is not a measure of vector toxicity.

## What it does NOT state / measure
No wild-type AAV-injected group, so no DRG effect of the vector itself in a healthy animal; the AAVnull comparator is in diseased animals and
carries no injury signal that the design could have detected over the pathology. No DRG neuronal loss count, no axonopathy, no
vector-genome distribution or transgene expression in DRG, no dose-response (one dose), no immune endpoint, no liver data, no intrathecal or CSF
route, no female mice, no human data. The DRG-MRI sensitivity to a vector-injury lesion (atrophy, loss) is untested: the only direction demonstrated is enlargement
from storage and its reversal.

## Authors' own caveats
CSA from MIP is a surrogate for volume; thick slices and partial volume; no contrast between DRG and cord, so the region is delineated from adjacent images;
the high-intensity region may include nerve roots, vessels, CSF; segmentation is manual.

## Transfer limits for the reference genotype class
A storage-disease mouse with an enlarged DRG is a different lesion from vector-associated DRG degeneration (the AAV-DRG toxicity pattern is neuronal
loss and axonal degeneration; this paper measured neither and does not show the method would detect it). Mouse 7 T L4 DRG CSA does not transfer
numerically to human MRI. The method shows repeatability in wild-type mice only; its agreement band (about +/-0.06 mm2 on a 0.26 mm2 mean) is about a quarter of the mean, comparable in size to the 0.07 mm2 disease effect
at 24 weeks, so a per-animal endpoint at n = 3 to 5 looks under-powered against the paper's own repeatability (INFERENZA, no power calculation is given).
