## 1 · What the paper measures

Wild-type and Slc2a5 (GLUT5) knockout and microglia-conditional knockout mice; neonatal fructose by intragastric injection or maternal 15 kcal percent fructose diet; prefrontal-cortex microglia number, morphology and phagocytosis (TUNEL puncta, PSD-95 engulfment) at P7; primary mouse and hPSC-derived microglia at 3.5 percent oxygen in 1 or 5 mM fructose with 5 mM glucose; 13C-fructose tracing (NMR in vivo, LC-MS in vitro); HK2 mitochondrial localisation; adolescent behaviour (novel object, Barnes maze, fear extinction). Group sizes N 4 to 10 mice. WWOX does not occur.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. High fructose lowers microglia number and phagocytosis in P7 prefrontal cortex; a high-glucose diet does not.
2. GLUT5 deletion (global or myeloid) reverses the phagocytosis defect; myeloid deletion does not restore microglia number.
3. Neonatal brain carries fructose carbon into lactate; the whole-brain total lactate pool is about 11 umol/g in HF wild-type pups against about 4 in controls and in GLUT5-null HF pups (Figure 3b, N 4 to 5).
4. In vitro, 5 mM fructose raises GLUT5, F6P, fructose carbon entry to the TCA cycle, lowers ATP and raises mitochondrial HK2; the HK2 inhibitor 3-BP restores phagocytosis.
5. Behaviour: novel-object preference lost (about 50 versus 75 percent) and fear extinction impaired; Barnes maze unaffected, with modestly better performance in some metrics; both NOR losses and fear extinction rescued by GLUT5 deletion.
6. The authors state fructose "itself likely does not cause" mood or anxiety disorders and may be a first hit.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- Not in the registries. The selection question was whether fructose and microglia matter for a Wwox-null brain with activated microglia. Nothing here is WWOX; the paper makes the fructose arm a diet-exposure question with a developmental window (gestation plus lactation, P1 to P7) and a single cell-type route (GLUT5 on microglia).
- It also contributes a caution for any lactate readout in Wwox-null brain: a diet exposure alone raises neonatal brain lactate nearly three-fold through a microglial transporter, so higher lactate in a knockout need not signal a HIF1A-driven glycolytic shift.
- It names HK2 mitochondrial binding as a lever on microglia phagocytosis. HK2 is a HIF1A target in the Wwox-HIF1A arc (CLAIM 009 neighbourhood); no link to WWOX is shown.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Any WWOX-related statement; any effect of fructose in genetic epilepsy or hypomyelination.
- Human neurodevelopmental causation (epidemiology is cited, not tested; the authors call it a first hit).
- That GLUT5 is the only route (myeloid knockout does not restore number).

## 5 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype (WWOX-DEE)

ESPANSIONE. System gap: wild-type mouse pups under a fructose diet. Value: a dietary-exposure covariate and a lactate-confounder warning; the fructose arm is not a priority for WWOX-DEE unless Wwox-null microglia are shown to be GLUT5-high or phagocytosis-impaired.
