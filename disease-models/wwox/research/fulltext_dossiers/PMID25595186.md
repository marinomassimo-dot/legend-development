## What it is

A narrative review by the group that cloned FRA16D and ran the Drosophila WWOX work. It measures nothing new. Its content: fragile-site biology; WWOX domain structure and conservation (two sequence figures); WWOX as a "non-classical" tumour suppressor; rodent and human mutants; Drosophila genetic interaction with SOD1 and IDH; WWOX expression responding to glycolysis versus oxidative phosphorylation; a proposal that WWOX metabolic function is enzymatic and that the substrate is unknown.

## What it says relevant to the lipid question (DATO about the review's text)

- "The in vivo substrate and reaction product for the SDR enzyme activity of WWOX are unknown"; in vitro targets exist (ref 11); mouse ablation impairs steroidogenesis (ref 12); steroids are "likely suspects"; Drosophila mutants lack the steroidogenesis phenotype.
- The one lipid statement is a family-level description: SDR enzymes "catalyze diverse small molecule reactions including those involving lipids, amino acids, carbohydrate, and steroids". It is not a WWOX finding.
- A WWOX allele lacking exons 6-8 (SDR domain absent, WW domains intact) is described in a family with a 46,XY disorder of sex development; the review notes the allele acts dominantly and offers haploinsufficiency or a dominant-negative truncated protein as alternatives.
- The putative C-terminal "substrate-binding domain" is called unique to WWOX orthologs (sequence homology); Figure 4 prints the region with a question mark.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No HDL, cholesterol or lipoprotein content.** The selection note lists "HDL links"; a full read finds none (the term "lipid" occurs once, in the generic SDR sentence). The review cites nothing from the WWOX HDL literature. It is not a route to the lipid arm.
2. **No evidence that the SDR domain acts on lipids.** The family-level sentence is background; the only WWOX-specific substrate claims are steroids (mouse ablation, ref 12) and unspecified in vitro targets (ref 11, a paper the repository's queue records as unacquirable). Elongase or very-long-chain function is not mentioned.
3. **The Warburg framing is a candidacy statement, not a finding.** "Perturbation of WWOX in cancer cells is therefore a clear candidate for contributing to the altered metabolism" is the authors' inference.
4. **Secondary description of mutants.** The review's statements on rodent, rat and human WWOX mutants are relayed; with 2015 knowledge only two recessive epilepsy reports are cited (the later WOREE and SCAR12 literature is absent).
5. **The substrate-binding region is a sequence guess.** The question mark is in the figure legend of the source itself.
