## What it is

A retrospective cohort from one Chinese neonatal unit, analysed as an emulated trial: 748 preterm infants (24 to 34 weeks) with a first APTT of 70 s or more; 434 received plasma within 24 hours, 314 did not; outcome was any-grade IVH within 28 days. Weighting with boosted propensity scores, doubly robust and covariate-balancing variants, an interaction with gestational age, decision-curve analysis, an E-value, a birth-weight negative control, and a causal forest. WWOX: zero occurrences.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** The paper concerns prematurity and IVH; no genetic condition.

## What it reports (DATO)

- Crude IVH 95 of 434 (21.9 percent) with plasma and 51 of 314 (16.2 percent) without; weighted OR 1.18 (0.79-1.78), doubly robust 1.03, CBPS 1.26; landmark OR 1.17.
- A significant gestational-age interaction (p 0.02, after adjusting for weight and sex) and, below 32.4 weeks, OR 2.16 (1.11-4.23); above, OR 0.67 (0.38-1.18).
- Causal forest ATE 0.98 percent (p 0.73) with exploratory subgroup trees.
- Patient-level data are shared (786-row landmark CSV, 748-row workbook, R code).

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Nothing about WWOX or a genetic DEE.** It cannot inform plasma use in a WWOX-deficient infant or child (H2 questions 1 and 5: not testable here).
2. **No evidence of harm below 28 weeks.** In the primary cohort reproduced from the shared file (748; 434 and 314; 95 and 51 haemorrhages, exactly as published), there are 10 infants below 196 days, all given plasma and 9 with haemorrhage; there is no control infant below 28 weeks. The "marked increase below 28 weeks" and the left end of the Figure 2D curve (about 0.62 against 0.19) are model extrapolation, not a comparison. DATO from the shared file; INFERENZA about the claim.
3. **The low-GA harm signal depends on the model.** In the 748, the low-GA group (below 226.8 days) has 65 of 214 (30 percent) haemorrhages with plasma and 16 of 106 (15 percent) without; a conventional covariate-adjusted logistic regression run in this review (not the authors' method) gives OR 1.49 (0.76-2.94) for low GA and 0.73 (0.39-1.38) for high GA, intervals that cross 1. The authors' weighted result (2.16, 1.11-4.23) and the interaction p 0.02 appear only in the model adjusted for weight and sex, with the cut at the sample mean after the interaction was seen. INFERENZA; this is a re-analysis note, not a correction.
4. **Confounding by indication is conceded and not removed.** Infants given plasma were more premature, sicker and more ventilated; gestational age stays unbalanced after weighting (0.14); only measured covariates are adjusted.
5. **The E-value is read backwards.** It is computed for an estimate close to 1 (OR 1.03), so the confounding needed to move it is tiny; the authors treat 1.22 exceeding the gestational-age effect as proof of robustness, whereas for a null a small E-value says a modest unmeasured confounder could hide a real benefit or harm. The text puts the gestational-age risk ratio at about 1.18; the panel bar is about 1.04. INFERENZA from DATO.
6. **The negative control fails by its own rule.** The Methods say a significant result in either model would challenge adequate balance; the unadjusted birth-weight difference is -116 g (p 0.005) and is explained away. Birth weight is also one of the 17 covariates in the propensity model, so it is a balance check, not an independent negative-control outcome. The shared file contains an infant weighing 11,480 g at 207 days and another 150 g at 236 days (likely entry errors) that remained in the data.
7. **Selection at both ends.** Exclusions (death, discharge or transfer within 72 hours; pre-existing haemorrhage; flowchart 12,290 to 1,265 to 748 without itemised counts; "about 1,000" excluded for haemorrhage in the Limitations; active haemorrhage in the flowchart but not in the Methods list) shape the cohort. In the shared landmark file the 38 infants who ended observation within 72 hours (the text says 37) included 13 with haemorrhage (34 percent, against 19.5 percent in the analysed cohort), 9 of them in the plasma group; their removal from the primary cohort is outcome-related, which the landmark sensitivity partly addresses.
8. **Timing and the "trial" label.** Controls are defined by not receiving plasma later in follow-up, a condition on the future; registration (20 May 2026) post-dates the 2025 data, so "strictly predefined" cannot be verified; the landmark analysis is post hoc. In the shared file all plasma times fall within 24 hours and none follows its infant's haemorrhage, which supports the design on that point.
9. **Text-versus-panel and printing errors.** The ATE interval is printed as -0.45 to 0.07 while the plotted whisker runs from about -0.046 to 0.064 (a decimal slip for -0.045); the tree root effect is 0.0035 against the stated ATE 0.0098; the cut-off is 32.3 weeks in the Abstract, 32.4 in the Results; the shared CSV holds 786 rows and 450 treated against 785 and 449 in the text (38 early endings against 37).
10. **Reference-list mismatches.** Several claims cite works whose printed titles concern other subjects: a sodium bicarbonate study for the E-value, a serum-urate study for the statement that prophylactic plasma gives no mortality benefit, an endoscopic histoacryl study for plasma-induced haemolysis and bleeding, a firth-penalised-regression paper for decision-curve analysis, a mean-centering paper for the "Kuperman" multicentre observational study, and a review of moderate-to-late preterm outcomes for causal forests. The "Egyptian guidelines" are cited to a decision-curve paper and an APTT reference-interval paper that appears twice (2014a and 2014b with the same title). The citation record does not support the sentences it is attached to. (Titles taken from the reference list as printed; the cited works were not opened.)
11. **Decision-curve analysis is in-sample** (the predicted risks come from the same data, with no cross-validation) and compares prediction strategies, not treatment effects; the "10 to 35 percent" benefit range follows from that.
12. **"First study worldwide" is contradicted by its own citations** (earlier cohort and review work on plasma and IVH are cited).
13. **The conclusion is stronger than the evidence.** The primary estimates are null with wide intervals (0.79-1.78); the recommendation of risk-stratified strategies rests on exploratory subgroup structure the legend itself calls hypothesis-generating.

## Design and limits

Retrospective single centre; any-grade IVH including mild grades; routine ultrasound ascertainment without a stated blinding; a battery of analyses (overall, interaction, subgroup, forest, tree, DCA) without multiplicity control; small leaves.
