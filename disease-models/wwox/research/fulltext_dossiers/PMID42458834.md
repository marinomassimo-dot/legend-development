## What the paper did

A deliberate overexpression-toxicity experiment, not a therapy study. A single-stranded AAV9 carrying
codon-optimised human *DDX3X* under the strong ubiquitous chicken β-actin (CBA) promoter was given to
wild-type C57BL/6J mice of both sexes by bilateral intracerebroventricular injection on postnatal day
1 at **1.3 × 10¹¹ or 2.6 × 10¹¹ vg**, or by lumbar intrathecal injection at 2.6 × 10¹¹ vg on P21.
Control vector: AAV9/CBh-GLDC (also single-stranded, also a strong promoter, different transgene).
Necropsy at P5 or P6 (P1 cohorts) and at 7 weeks (P21 cohort). Serum chemistry at IDEXX; H&E and oil
red O read by a veterinary pathologist; RT-qPCR for stress, lipid-handling and interferon genes;
analysis blind to group.

## What it measured

| Observation | Measured value |
|---|---|
| Mortality, high dose P1 i.c.v. | **All males and females dead by P8**, one week post injection (p = 0.014 M, p = 0.005 F vs vehicle) |
| Mortality, low dose P1 i.c.v. | By P21, **80 % of males and 20 % of females** dead; significant for males (p = 0.026), not females (p = 0.317); male-vs-female trend p = 0.08 |
| Mortality, high dose P21 i.t. | **All survived** to endpoint four weeks later |
| Transgene level, P6 | Heart ≈30-fold increase (both IHC and RNA); liver ≈6-fold (IHC) / ≈4.6-fold (RNA) |
| Cause of death | Focal/multifocal **myocardial degeneration** in all AAV-treated mice, absent in vehicle; pathologist's assessment that the mice died of heart failure |
| Stress / apoptosis | *Ddit3*/CHOP ≈14-fold up in heart, ≈5-fold in liver at P6 (11- and 3-fold at P5); cleaved caspase-3 IHC increased in heart; *Bnip3* unchanged |
| Liver | Hepatocyte ballooning and vacuoles in all treated mice; serum triglycerides ×2.4, AST ×4.2, ALT ×2.3, creatine kinase unchanged; diffuse micro- and macro-vesicular steatosis by oil red O; *Plin2* ≈4-fold up, *Pparα* 2.5-fold down in liver |

## What it states

That unregulated, strong, ubiquitous expression of a dose-sensitive neurodevelopmental-disease gene,
delivered **into the CSF**, can kill from a peripheral organ within days, with a transgene-driven
(not capsid-driven) type-I interferon response; and that the organ at risk from a CNS vector may not
be the CNS. The authors explicitly "raise caution to the use of strong promoters like CBA and
regulatory elements such as the woodchuck hepatitis virus posttranscriptional regulatory element to
boost transgene expression, especially for transgenes that are dose sensitive".

The single most transfer-relevant sentence in the paper is in Methods: "The highest dose in the mouse
studies (2.6 × 10¹¹ vg) would roughly equate to a human dose of 1 × 10¹⁵ vg, if extrapolated by
neonatal brain mass (375 g human vs. 0.087 g mouse). Several intra-CSF AAV9 clinical trials utilize
a dose of 1 × 10¹⁵ vg per patient." The lethal dose, so scaled, is a dose in clinical use.

## What it does NOT state

- Nothing about WWOX. DDX3X is an X-linked RNA helicase with direct roles in RIG-I/MAVS→IRF3
  signalling, stress-granule biology and *Ddit3* transcription; **the toxicity mechanism here is
  specific to those functions** and does not transfer to an arbitrary transgene. WWOX dose
  sensitivity is not addressed and must not be inferred.
- "The brain was overall unaffected" is a **histopathology and lipid-staining** statement. The
  paper's own data show *Cxcl10* mRNA up ≈23-fold in the brain, which the authors note and cannot
  explain ("no known direct link between DDX3X and CXCL10 has been published"). The abstract's
  "overall unaffected" is therefore true of morphology, not of the brain transcriptome.
  🔴 **Qualified on panel inspection of Figure 3:** the brain *Cxcl10* effect is a **single
  timepoint at the margin** — one significance bracket, p = 0.044, on the P5 bar, with three animal
  points — while the heart and liver brackets on the same axis read p = 0.002 and p < 0.001 on bars
  one to two orders of magnitude higher. In Figure 3B (*Ifnb1*) and 3C (*Ddx58*) the brain facet
  carries no significance bracket at all. The brain signal is real enough to forbid the word
  "unaffected" without qualification, and too thin to be called an effect.
- **n per group is absent from the running text and from the captions as deposited.** Survival is
  given only as percentages and p values and the toxicology cohorts are "another cohort" without
  counts. 🔴 **Corrected on panel inspection:** the group sizes *are* reported — inside the plotted
  legends of Figure 2, which the JATS caption does not carry. Figure 2A: vehicle ♀ n = 6, ♂ n = 6;
  AAV9/CBA-DDX3X 2.6 × 10¹¹ vg ♀ n = 6, ♂ n = 6. Figure 2D/2E and Figure 3B–3D: n = 3 per group,
  with female wild-type vehicle at n = 6. A reading that used only the body text would have
  reported this paper as giving no n at all; that is what the first pass of this dossier did, and
  opening the panel is what corrected it.
- **No graded histopathology severity scale** in the main text: Figures 1D and 2C are summary tables
  of pathological findings; the pathologist's report with per-animal findings is Table S1, not read.
- Only two doses, one route per age, and a single time point for most molecular endpoints — enough
  to show that a ceiling exists, not enough to locate it.
- Cardiac function was never measured (no ECG, no echocardiography); heart failure is a
  morphological inference.
- No DRG, trigeminal-ganglion or peripheral-nerve assessment at all.
- Weight data are cited as "data not shown".

## Caveats the authors themselves give

- The mechanism is "not mechanistically addressed in this paper"; the IFN-I → CHOP → apoptosis chain
  is offered as "a reasonable mechanism", and "there are likely many other targets".
- RNA biodistribution values "represent average expression across bulk tissue and may underestimate
  the magnitude of DDX3X expression in individual transduced cells."
- The P21 survival is attributed, by the authors' own preference, to dose per body weight rather
  than to an age effect: "it is more likely a factor of a higher dose per body weight at the
  younger age."
- The authors note the differences from the cited muscle-disease transgene study: route, age, and
  gene class.
