## Verdict

This is a carcinogen-susceptibility experiment, not evidence that heterozygotes spontaneously phenocopy WWOX developmental disease. After six NMBA doses, 25/26 heterozygotes versus 10/34 wild types developed forestomach tumors; 7/26 heterozygotes and 0/34 controls had invasive SCC. Tumor multiplicity and ≥1-mm tumors were also sharply higher. Histology, PCNA and KRT14 consistently support greater progression in the heterozygous group.

The paper calls this Wwox haploinsufficiency. The experiment establishes a one-allele-loss susceptibility state under a strong carcinogenic challenge. It does **not** establish that the remaining allele remains genetically intact: only Wwox immunostaining was measured, and expression becomes very weak in SCC. Loss, epigenetic silencing or selection affecting the second allele were not tested. `DATO` for susceptibility; `INFERENZA` for strict haploinsufficiency.

## Design and limits

- 34 WT and 26 HET mice, 10–12 weeks old, mixed background backcrossed four generations.
- All animals received NMBA; there is no untreated genotype control in this experiment.
- Tumors ≥0.5 mm were scored at 15 weeks; Fisher exact tests and one-way ANOVA were used.
- Sex distribution, randomization, blinding and pathology adjudication are not reported.
- The mouse forestomach is an exposure model for distal esophageal squamous biology, not a homologous human organ.

## Horizontal impact

Qualifies `CLAIM 032`: heterozygotes can be normal under unchallenged physiological assays yet highly susceptible under carcinogen exposure. This is not a contradiction; it is a context boundary. The experiment says nothing about seizures, neurodevelopment or untreated carrier health. Transfer to the reference genotype is `ESPANSIONE` and limited to the principle that partial WWOX dosage can interact strongly with an external stressor.
