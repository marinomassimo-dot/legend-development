## 1 · What the paper did

A proximity-labelling interactome of the intermediate-conductance calcium-activated potassium
channel KCa3.1 (gene KCNN4). Murine KCNN4 coding sequence fused at its C-terminus to FLAG-TurboID,
expressed from a doxycycline-inducible lentiviral construct in **KPCY murine pancreatic ductal
adenocarcinoma cells** derived from the KPC genetically engineered mouse model. Biotin for 3 h,
streptavidin pull-down, TMT11-plex labelling, Orbitrap Fusion Lumos, IsobarQuant/Mascot against the
**mouse** proteome, three independent biological replicates. Hit criterion: at least two unique
peptides, FDR < 5%, fold change ≥ 1.5. Result: **283 KCa3.1-specific interactor proteins**. Control
condition: cells expressing **free FLAG-TurboID**, plus no-biotin conditions for both.

Downstream: GO/KEGG enrichment, a STRING network on the 138 membrane proteins among the hits, public
expression/survival/immune-infiltration analyses of KCNN4, and biochemical validation of a small
number of candidates.

## 2 · The WWOX content, exactly

**WWOX occurs five times in the article body and once in the data.**

- **In the data:** one row of the supplementary protein list — `Wwox` / `WW domain-containing
  oxidoreductase` / `Tumor suppressor; apoptosis and steroid metabolism`. Read cell-wise from the
  workbook XML, that sheet is **A1:D284**: a header row plus 283 protein rows across four columns —
  Protein, Protein Name, Subcellular Localization, Function / Notes. **There is no numeric cell
  anywhere in the sheet.** No fold change, no p value, no FDR, no peptide count is attached to
  WWOX or to any other protein in the list.
- **In the body:** one clause listing WWOX among "altered tumor suppressor proteins" alongside APC,
  SCRIB, PTPN13 and PTPN14, and one sentence citing someone else's finding that WWOX loss
  accelerates pancreatic cancer through TGFβ/BMP2. Both are in the Discussion. The bibliography
  entry behind the second is the fifth occurrence.

**The gene symbol is `Wwox`, mouse.** The search database is the *Mus musculus* proteome. This is a
murine protein identified in murine cells; the human protein is never measured.

## 3 · What the paper states about WWOX

Only this: that WWOX was among the proteins identified in the KCa3.1 proxisome, and that the
presence of altered tumour suppressors in that proxisome "underscores how changes in KCa3.1
expression may trigger the loss of key regulatory mechanisms". The causal verb belongs to the
channel, and the sentence is an interpretive gloss, not a result.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT state

- **It does not validate the WWOX association.** Two candidates were taken to co-immunoprecipitation
  and confirmed: STIM1 and ITGB4. WWOX was not tested.
- **It measures its own false-positive rate and the number is poor.** Five further candidates
  (SUCLA2, IARS2, SFXN3, STOM, FKBP8) were taken to co-IP and **could not be confirmed**, and a
  sixth with strong prior expectation (Orai1) also failed. So of the candidates this paper itself
  put to an orthogonal test, **two of eight reproduced**. Membership in this 283-protein list is
  therefore a weak prior, measured as weak inside the paper.
- **It reports no per-protein statistic for WWOX.** The FDR < 5% / 1.5-fold criterion is stated for
  the dataset; where WWOX sits within it is not recoverable from any served artefact.
- **No electrophysiology involving WWOX.** No current, no conductance, no membrane potential, no
  calcium measurement is made with WWOX manipulated in any way.
- **No neuron, no glia, no brain, no excitable cell of any kind.** KPCY cells are epithelial tumour
  cells. KCa3.1 in this paper is studied as an oncology target, and partly as a mitochondrial
  protein.
- **No WWOX variant, allele, zygosity, expression level or functional state** is measured or
  mentioned. No epilepsy, no neurodevelopment, no WWOX enzymatic activity.
- **It is not a restoration, delivery, promoter, capsid or route record** in any respect.

## 5 · Caveats the authors themselves give, plus two structural ones

- The authors chose an inducible system explicitly "to avoid possible artefacts in the interactome
  determination due to a constantly present high expression of the channel" — i.e. they name
  overexpression artefact as the risk. The channel is still overexpressed, from a transgene, with a
  C-terminal fusion.
- The control is **free FLAG-TurboID**, a soluble ligase. A membrane-targeted control is not used.
  A soluble control cannot report the background labelling of the membrane compartment, so a
  membrane-proximal protein can be enriched over this control for being at the membrane rather than
  near this channel. 138 of the 283 hits are membrane proteins.
- A generative language model was used for language editing of the manuscript (declared by the
  authors). This matters only because the whole WWOX content of the body is prose.
- **A supplement-labelling defect:** the text directs the reader to "Supporting Information S2:
  Table 1" for the complete protein list and to "Supporting Information S1: Figure S1" for the
  figures, but in the archive served by Europe PMC the file named `s001` is the table and the file
  named `s002` is the figure set. A reader following the citation to the matching filename gets the
  wrong file.

## 6 · Classification under LEGEND_CORE §13

**Neither biomarker nor endpoint.** §13 requires a biomarker to measure the functional state of the
target gene; nothing here measures WWOX's state — WWOX appears as an identified protein in a list,
with no quantity attached. It is equally not an endpoint: no clinical, functional or
neurophysiological outcome involving WWOX is measured. The appropriate classification is a
**discovery-tier association of unknown reliability**, below Tier 2, and explicitly below the
threshold §13's second sentence sets for the word "validated".

## 7 · Transfer limits — stated exactly

1. **Species:** murine Wwox, murine Kcnn4, murine cells, mouse proteome database. Nothing here is a
   measurement of the human protein or of a human channel.
2. **Cell type:** pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma epithelium, non-excitable. Not a neuron, not a
   glial cell, not any CNS cell.
3. **Construct:** an overexpressed, doxycycline-induced, C-terminally tagged channel. Not endogenous
   stoichiometry.
4. **Evidence class:** proximity labelling is a proximity statement, not a binding statement and not
   a functional coupling. The paper demonstrates in its own hands that six of eight candidates
   tested orthogonally did not reproduce as complexes.
5. **No direction of effect exists to transfer.** No experiment in this paper changes WWOX and
   measures the channel, or changes the channel and measures WWOX.

## 8 · Bearing on the wave-8 Group B question

The selection note proposed this paper as "the first mechanistic bridge between WWOX and
excitability that is not transcriptional". **That premise is not supported by the source.** The
paper contains no excitability measurement connected to WWOX, no excitable cell, and no validated
WWOX–channel interaction; WWOX's entire presence is one unquantified row in a 283-protein list from
a mouse pancreatic tumour line, and the paper's own validation arm shows that such rows reproduce
poorly. The honest standing of the datum is: **a hypothesis-generating co-occurrence, in the wrong
species and the wrong cell type, with no number attached.** It neither adds to nor bounds a
restoration-spec read-out; it does not supply a pharmacodynamic assay, and it should not be carried
as a WWOX–ion-channel link.
