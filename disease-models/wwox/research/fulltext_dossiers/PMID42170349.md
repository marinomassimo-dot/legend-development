## What the piece is

A commentary on a liver expression-profiling primary (RNA sequencing of liver after high-dose systemic AAV9-SMN1 in cynomolgus macaques and in rats; PubMed record of the primary exists, not held in this repository). The piece measures nothing; the primary's results are relayed.

## What it states

1. **Relayed result.** Across species and doses, the magnitude of the PERK-branch unfolded-protein response was strongly associated with transgene expression level; mitochondrial-apoptosis genes were induced; XBP1s and processed ATF6 were repressed; immune pathways were activated and "liver identity" genes were repressed in both species and both sexes. Rats expressed the transgene less and lacked the unfolded-protein response; the commentator reads this as UPR tracking transgene product synthesis.
2. **The commentator's framework.** Three separable dose-scaled stimuli: (a) vector DNA presenting hundreds of double-strand-break-like ends per nucleus and engaging DNA-damage repair (apoptosis noted in cells lacking p53); (b) innate sensing of capsid and DNA through TLR2 and TLR9 in membrane and endosomal compartments; (c) supra-physiologic transgene synthesis and the UPR. Figure 1 is a schematic of these.
3. **DRG link.** "This form of dose-related toxicity has been observed in the dorsal root ganglia and is less significant when the transcriptional activity is lower" (two earlier macaque papers cited).
4. **Stated limits.** No clinical toxicity syndrome was predicted before it was seen in humans; natural infection says little because the multiplicities used are never met in nature; the way forward is "backward translation" from clinical events to animal models.

## What it does NOT state

- No dose threshold in any unit, no n, no organ-specific numbers.
- Nothing about the central nervous system, neonatal recipients, the intrathecal or intra-CSF route, or any WWOX-relevant tissue. The primary is a systemic, liver, high-dose study.
- It does not separate which of the three stimuli causes the clinical syndromes; it presents them as co-present.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

The mechanisms of the clinical syndromes (hepatotoxicity, thrombotic microangiopathy, thrombocytopenia, myocarditis, respiratory distress, haemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis) are "not well understood".

## Reader's caveats (INFERENZA)

- The selection note called this "the dose-side counterpart" to the DRG commentary. In the source it is a liver, systemic-route piece whose DRG content is one sentence; the DRG statement is a citation, not a finding.
- A single sentence in a commentary that a UPR magnitude "tracks" expression does not show that the UPR causes the toxicity; the commentator does not claim that it does.
