## 1 · What the paper measures

Seven GABRA1 DEE variants in HEK293T (in-silico stability, total and surface alpha1, patch clamp), a 7-day PBA treatment (100 mg/kg/day, i.p.) of Gabra1 +/A322D mice with brain western blots, and one child with R214C given glycerol phenylbutyrate.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. PBA increases alpha1 expression of both wild-type and variant subunits (not specific to the mutant); predicted stability change is a weak predictor of expression loss.
2. Mice: alpha1 up 14.0 percent (cortex) and 72.8 percent (thalamus), none in hippocampus or cerebellum; gamma2 up 42 percent in thalamus; 3 to 4 animals per group; no seizure, behaviour or function outcome.
3. Human: one child, seizure-free 245 days after adding glycerol phenylbutyrate to several drugs, with better emotional regulation; the trial (NCT04937062) is ongoing; no control or blinding; Table 2 is a narrative summary.
4. The Discussion calls the preclinical data "predictive of efficacy in human patients" on that basis.
5. WWOX does not occur.

## 3 · Transfer (system gap, stated)

GABA-A receptor subunit assembly and surface trafficking is the lesion class; PBA acts here as a general proteostasis and expression enhancer (it raises wild-type protein too). For WWOX that would mean raising residual or wild-type WWOX, which matters only if residual protein exists; for a variant with no detectable protein the paper gives no route. The regional heterogeneity in vivo (thalamus yes, hippocampus no) warns that a brain-wide effect cannot be assumed. The statement of predictive efficacy rests on one uncontrolled child.
