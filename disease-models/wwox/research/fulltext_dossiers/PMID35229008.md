## What the paper did

Cynomolgus monkeys, single cisterna magna dose, four weeks, two per sex per group (Table 1): vehicle; AAV9-CB7-hCLN2 (human TPP1, a secreted lysosomal enzyme) at 3.1e13 GC made by three purification routes (74, 86 and 96 percent full capsids); the same vector at 1.1e14 GC (the only group imaged by MRI); and an AAV9 "Null" vector at 2.8e13 GC whose genome carries a polyA, a CpG-depleted intron and an RPE65 cDNA with the start codon mutated and the frame disrupted, described as lacking a functional promoter and producing no mRNA or protein. Endpoints: histopathology, biodistribution, TPP1 and antibodies, sensory and motor nerve conduction, intraepidermal nerve fibre density (IENFD), MRI of the lumbar and sacral cord and nerves.

## What it measured

- Lesion in all expressing-vector animals regardless of purification: DRG neuronal degeneration with increased cellularity, spinal-nerve-root and sciatic degeneration, dorsal-tract degeneration in the cord, worst in lumbosacral and lumbar levels, extending to cervical level at 1.1e14. Neuronal degeneration: shrunken hypereosinophilic neurons with mononuclear cells; "increased cellularity" a mixture of satellite glia, lymphocytes and macrophages (Figures 1 and 2, inspected).
- Null vector: vector DNA in brain, cord and DRG similar to or higher than with the expressing vector, and no DRG neuronal degeneration (Figure 2 panel: zero in every region). Text-level qualifier: lumbosacral DRG "increased cellularity" in 2/4 Null animals vs 1/4 vehicle, and minimal-to-mild sciatic degeneration, in different animals (Results, Histopathology).
- Function: no abnormality in sensory or motor nerve conduction, F-wave or H-reflex in any of 16 expressing-vector animals; no clinical signs.
- IENFD: no group difference; some animals at the low doses fell below the control range (two animals in one purification group with marked lumbosacral root and sciatic degeneration), not at the highest dose (Figure 4, inspected).
- MRI (1.1e14 group and controls only): raised signal on T2 and STIR in spinal nerves and dorsal cord, lower fractional anisotropy (two animals with the most pathology had at least 24 percent reduction in two or more nerve pairs), gluteal T2 signal up without STIR change (read by the authors as fatty infiltration).
- Brain, expressing vector only: perivascular mononuclear cells in the meninges, cerebellar neuronal necrosis (Fluoro-Jade B), gliosis; antibodies to the human transgene product in all expressing animals with TPP1 falling by day 30. Authors: possibly immune to human TPP1.

## What the authors state

- "Primarily mediated by transgene overexpression" is the hypothesis supported "alongside other published data"; the Null control argues against capsid/DNA presence alone and the three purifications argue against empty capsid fraction as a determinant.
- The Null differs from an earlier parenchymal-injection control (CAG promoter, no protein) that did produce cord degeneration, which they attribute to high mRNA without protein.
- Lesions are sub-clinical; long-term NHP studies "suggest they resolve over time"; the duration of this study was four weeks and "it is unclear" whether longer would show more.
- They write that in earlier AAV9 NHP studies "immune suppression ablated T cell responses, but not DRG pathology" (citing the two companion ICM papers).

## What the paper does NOT say

- No immunosuppression arm, no immunohistochemical identification of the infiltrate type, no direct dose-response across the two dose levels for the lumbosacral lesion (similar scores at both doses, Figure 2).
- No test that the expression itself, rather than a difference in the Null genome's other features, is the driver; the Null is one construct, one dose, four animals.
- No proof of resolution: the resolution claim rests on a cited 4-year juvenile study.
- Nothing about WWOX or an intracellular cargo; TPP1 is a secreted enzyme.

## Reader's observations (INFERENZA where marked)

- The companion-paper citation is stronger than the cited papers: PMID 30073179 reports one of three immunosuppressed animals with a transgene T-cell response (not ablated) and a lower axonopathy score only at three animals, and PMID 30073178 reports no DRG reduction with immunosuppression. "Ablated T cell responses" holds for antibodies in the IDUA paper and is not what either paper shows for T cells in every animal.
- Persistence of lesion with an expression-null control is the cleanest result in the group, within n = 4 and a four-week window. A transgene-product-dependent lesion is a mechanism-level inference (INFERENZA): the data show lesion requires something that happens only with the expressing genome (transcription, protein, or the specific cDNA), not which.
- The lesion is not dose-ordered between 3.1e13 and 1.1e14 in the lumbosacral DRG panel; the high dose recruits additional levels.

## Transfer limit

Cynomolgus, cisterna magna, AAV9, CB7 promoter, secreted lysosomal-enzyme cargo, four weeks, n = 4 per group, no immune manipulation, no WWOX. The functional negatives (nerve conduction, IENFD) are at four weeks only.
