## 1. What the paper did

Two arms.

**Human.** A GWAS of *self-aware sleep duration* in 8,840 participants of the Korean Genome and
Epidemiology Study, split as discovery (Ansan, n = 4,635) and replication (Ansung, n = 4,205).
Affymetrix SNP 5.0, imputed to 1000G Phase I against hg19; 6.42 million SNVs after QC. Linear
regression under an additive model, adjusted for age, sex, occupation and PC1–PC10. Phenotype is a
single questionnaire item about the past month; time in bed (TIB) is derived from reported bedtime
and waking time, and habitual sleep efficiency = duration/TIB.

**Fly.** *Wwox^f04545* (Bloomington BL18783), a loss-of-function insertion allele, backcrossed six
times into the *w^1118* control background, homozygous, **male flies only**, 2–5 days old.
Trikinetics DAM, 12 h : 12 h LD at 25 °C, entrained three days and measured on day 4; sleep bout
defined as ≥5 min of inactivity. Free-running period from 7 days of constant darkness, χ²
periodogram. n = 32 per genotype (30 for the rhythm analysis of the mutant).

## 3. What the paper states

* Two intronic *WWOX* SNVs associate with self-aware sleep duration in two Korean cohorts, and
  deleting *Wwox* in *Drosophila* reduces daytime sleep duration and quality while increasing
  night-time sleep, without touching the circadian clock.
* Mechanistic candidates offered in the Discussion — metabolic dysregulation, ROS, Tau — are all
  imported from other papers; none is measured here.
* Limitations, in the authors' own words: *"the WWOX gene did not reach the conventional
  genome-wide significance level"* and *"the number of subjects in each cohort was not sufficient
  for GWAS"*.

## 4. What the paper does NOT state

* **It never measures WWOX expression in humans.** The Results sentence *"The associations between
  WWOX expression and sleep parameters are presented in Table 2"* is wrong on its face: Table 2
  reports **genotype** association, not expression. The same conflation recurs in the Abstract
  (*"WWOX expression is associated with sleep duration in both humans and Drosophila"*) and the
  Discussion. No human eQTL, RNA or protein measurement appears anywhere.
* **It does not reach genome-wide significance**, and the correction it does pass is not
  genome-wide either: Supplementary Table 1 gives pBONF = 0.0197 and 0.0364 for the two lead SNVs.
  Bonferroni over 6.42 million imputed SNVs would put the threshold near 5 × 10⁻⁸; the reported
  pBONF implies correction over roughly 1.8 × 10⁵ tests. The Manhattan plot's drawn threshold is
  **P = 1.0 × 10⁻⁵**, which the supplement labels "the preset threshold".
* **The fly allele is not characterised as a null.** *Wwox^f04545* reduces mRNA by ≈92 %; it is a
  strong hypomorph by the paper's own measurement. The abstract and title call it "deletion" and
  "loss-of-function".
* **No rescue.** There is no genetic rescue, no second allele, no tissue-specific knockdown, and
  one control genotype. Female flies were not tested.
* It does not measure sleep architecture, EEG, arousal threshold, or any physiological sleep
  variable in humans — only a questionnaire item.

## 5. Caveats the authors themselves give

Both quoted above (no genome-wide significance; cohorts underpowered for GWAS), plus the call for
*"external validation at other race/ethnicity groups"*.

## 6. Reading the panels

* The LocusZoom plot (Fig 1) places the signal in a single tight LD block near chr16:78.95 Mb, at
  the **distal** end of the >1 Mb *WWOX* gene body. One LD block, not independent signals: the
  nine SNVs in Table 2 all share r² colouring with the index SNV and their betas are all between
  −0.104 and −0.127.
* The human effect **tracks time in bed**, not sleep quality: duration and TIB move together and
  by similar magnitudes, while habitual sleep efficiency and the Epworth score are flat. That
  pattern is what an *opportunity* phenotype looks like, not a sleep-physiology phenotype — and
  self-reported duration and self-reported TIB are two items from the same questionnaire.
* Fly night-time sleep reaches ≈650 of 720 available dark minutes (≈90 %), close to the ceiling of
  the assay.

## 7. Answer to the assigned question, for this source

**The fly daytime-sleep endpoint is the only measurable, large-effect readout here** (≈−40 % of
daytime sleep, `****`, n = 32, with a fragmentation correlate in bout length). It is cheap,
automated, continuous and non-terminal — all the properties a rescue readout wants. What it lacks
is a rescue arm: nothing in this paper shows the phenotype is reversible by restoring *Wwox*, so
its specificity to *Wwox* rests on one insertion allele in one background.

**The human arm supplies no readout at all.** A 12–18 minute shift in a self-reported duration,
at suggestive significance, in a phenotype that moves with time in bed, is not an endpoint — and
under [`LEGEND_CORE.md` §13](../../../../framework/instruction/LEGEND_CORE.md#13-biomarker-scope-hard-rule)
self-reported sleep duration is a Tier-3 distal clinical endpoint and not a WWOX biomarker in any
tier. **Rejected as a candidate readout**, with the reason recorded rather than carried.

**Transfer limit.** A *Drosophila* hypomorph of a gene sharing 49 % amino-acid identity with human
WWOX, measured in adult males; the human arm is a common intronic variant in a general adult
population, not an allele of the WWOX-DEE genotype class. The link from "common intronic variant
shifts sleep by minutes" to "biallelic loss causes a DEE sleep phenotype" is a hypothesis, and
this paper does not test it. Sleep disturbance is a reported feature of the WWOX-DEE phenotype
band; this paper does not measure it in anyone who has the disorder.
