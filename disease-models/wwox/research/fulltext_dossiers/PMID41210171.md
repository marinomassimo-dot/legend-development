## 1 · What the paper did

A non-GLP study in **15 female cynomolgus macaques** (Mauritius origin), 12 dosed with a tool
vector (scAAV9-CBA-mCherry) and 3 with vehicle, **approximately 41 months of age** and 2.8–5.4 kg at
dosing. Animals were pre-selected into bands by **pre-existing** anti-AAV9 total antibody (TAb)
titre: vehicle (group 1), low (group 2), medium (group 3), high (group 4), n = 3–4 per group.

Route: lumbar intrathecal injection at the L5–L6 intervertebral space, slow bolus over ≥1 min, up
to 1 mL CSF withdrawn first, Trendelenburg-like positioning for ≥10 min. **No immunosuppression was
given.** Study duration **4 weeks**; biodistribution and histopathology are terminal, one timepoint.

**The brief's framing — "the one variable that decides whether an *infant* is an easier or harder
recipient than an adult" — is not what this study can answer.** There is no infant arm, no juvenile
arm, no male animal, and no dose arm.

## 2 · The dose is a single level, so this study cannot speak to dose at all

Figure 6C, read at panel level, prints the dose row: **1.2 × 10¹³ vg/animal for groups 2, 3 and 4
alike**, and 0 for group 1. Only pre-existing immunity varies. Anything this paper shows is a
property of immunity at one fixed dose.

## 3 · What it states

- High serum anti-AAV9 TAb (up to 1:327,680) **did not** reduce vector-genome biodistribution to the
  CNS, including spinal cord.
- Peripheral biodistribution **was** reduced in high-titre animals — specifically **to the DRG**,
  liver and heart — while spleen vector-genome copies **increased**.
- High-titre animals showed a rapid, transient systemic interferon response, faster
  treatment-emergent antibody rise in CSF, and stronger AAV9-specific splenic CD4/CD8 T-cell
  responses — **without** enhanced complement activation.
- Histopathology across treated groups included mononuclear cell infiltrate, neuronal degeneration,
  axonal degeneration and gliosis in brain, injection site, spinal cord, cauda equina / nerve roots
  **and/or DRG**, "mostly minimal to slight in severity with high individual variabilities".
- Clinical chemistry: minimally-to-mildly raised ALT and GLDH in one to two animals per treated
  group (1.5–4× baseline), **without impact of pre-existing immunity**.
- CSF IL-12p40 rose in AAV9-treated animals versus controls by day 30.
- The authors' policy conclusion: using serum AAV titres as an eligibility criterion for
  **intrathecal** gene therapy "could exclude patients who might benefit from treatment".

## 4 · What the figure panel shows that the running text does not

Figure 6C (read from the rendered panel, not the caption) tabulates brain findings by group:

| Finding | Vehicle (n=3) | Low (n=4) | Medium (n=4) | High (n=4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Meningeal mononuclear infiltrate, total affected | **2** | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| — minimal / slight / moderate | 2 / 0 / 0 | 3 / 1 / 0 | 3 / 1 / 0 | 2 / 1 / **1** |
| Neuropil perivascular infiltrate, total affected | 0 | **0** | **2** | **1** |

Three things follow, and each weakens the authors' running text:

1. **Two of three vehicle animals already had meningeal mononuclear infiltrate.** The text classes
   this lesion among "test-article–related microscopic findings"; the panel shows it occurring
   without test article. At minimum the lesion is partly procedural (lumbar puncture, catheter-free
   bolus, anaesthesia), not vector-attributable.
2. The reported "trend toward increased severity … in animals with high pre-dose anti-AAV9 TAbs"
   rests on **one animal of four** being graded moderate. No animal in any group reached marked or
   severe.
3. Perivascular neuropil infiltrate is **non-monotonic** in titre: 2 of 4 at medium, 1 of 4 at high,
   0 of 4 at low, 0 of 3 at vehicle. A dose-of-immunity gradient is not what the panel shows.

Panel D (CSF IL-12p40) carries a single bracket annotated **p = 0.06** within the group-2 time
course and has a broken y-axis; the scatter is dominated by one group-3 animal near the top of the
scale. The text's "confirming test-article–induced local changes in inflammatory status" is
stronger than the panel supports. The authors do concede elsewhere that the titre-related IL-12p40
trend "was not conclusive because of high inter-animal variability".

## 5 · The DRG finding here is unquantified — exhaustively so

The paper's DRG claim rests on **one representative photomicrograph** (Figure 6A top, a sacral DRG
from a group-3 animal) and one prose list. I searched for an incidence or severity count for DRG:

- the article contains **zero** `<table-wrap>` elements;
- Figure 6C, the only histopathology summary, tabulates **brain only**;
- Supplementary Document S1 was fetched and converted to text; its tables are **S1 (pre-dose serum
  and CSF titres), S2 (serum TAb by group), S3 (CSF TAb by group)** — none reports pathology.

**So the DRG lesion in this study has no incidence, no severity distribution, no per-group counts and
no concurrent-control comparison anywhere in the paper or its supplement.** Methods confirm DRG at
four levels (cervical, thoracic, lumbar, sacral) were sectioned and H&E-stained, so the data were
generated; they are simply not reported.

## 6 · What it does NOT state

- Nothing about infants, juveniles, or males.
- Nothing about dose dependence (one dose).
- Nothing beyond 4 weeks; no recovery cohort, so reversibility is unknown.
- No functional nerve measure: no nerve conduction, no sensory testing, no NfL.
- No immunosuppressed comparator, so the modifiability of the immune component is untested here.
- It does not claim pre-existing immunity causes DRG injury — the figure caption says the pathology
  occurred "without impact of pre-existing AAV9-Ab titers".

## 7 · The inference this paper actually licenses for an infant — and its status

Pre-existing anti-AAV9 immunity, at a fixed intrathecal dose, left CNS exposure intact while
**reducing DRG vector load**. An infant is typically seronegative (maternal antibody aside). It
follows, as a **HYPOTHESIS and not a measured claim**, that a seronegative infant would receive a
*higher* DRG vector exposure than the seropositive adults in this study at the same absolute dose —
i.e. for the CNS target immunity is irrelevant, but for the DRG the seronegative recipient is the
*more* exposed one. This inverts the brief's framing of the question. It is untested: no infant was
dosed, DRG injury was never quantified here, and vector load is not injury.

| Datum | Transfers? | Limit |

## 10 · Verdict

**INGEST** — the strongest single source in group C for separating what is immune-driven from what
