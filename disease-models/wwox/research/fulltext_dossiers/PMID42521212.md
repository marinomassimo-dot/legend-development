## What the paper did

Two separate things. (1) Identified four conserved upstream AUGs in the SCN1A 5' leader (exon 5, at
−40, −29, −17 and −8 relative to the primary AUG, the three most proximal terminating past it) and
tested them in a **plasmid reporter**: the 5' leader plus the start of the Nav1.1 coding sequence fused
in frame to nanoluciferase, transfected into HEK-293 with firefly luciferase as normaliser, uAUGs
mutated to AGU combinatorially. (2) Knocked an 11-amino-acid HiBiT tag into the endogenous SCN1A locus
before the stop codon in a control human iPSC line by CRISPR/Cas9 homology-directed repair, validated
the clones by PCR, SNP array, Sanger, Illumina and Oxford Nanopore sequencing, differentiated them
into NGN2-induced excitatory neurons, and measured Nav1.1-HiBiT luminescence after ASO treatment.

## What it measured

- Reporter: mutating all four uAUGs raised nanoluciferase 6.9-fold; uAUG2+3+4 raised it ~8-fold;
  uAUG4 alone 1.6-fold; uAUG3+4 over 4-fold; the two distal uAUGs alone did nothing. N = 12–27 per
  condition, Kruskal-Wallis.
- Line: gRNA-C clone 8 carries HiBiT in frame on one allele and an 8-base deletion
  (p.(T1990Nfs*6)) on the other; clone 12 carries HiBiT plus a single-base insertion
  (p.(I1993Nfs*6)). Both are therefore **heterozygous-null models of DS haploinsufficiency by
  construction**. No off-target editing at the ten top predicted loci; no pathogenic CNV, **but**
  clone 8 and its NGN2 derivatives carry a 985-kb 20q11.21 duplication including BCL2L1.
- Assay performance: a gapmer ASO against the SCN1A 3' UTR gave a dose-dependent fall in signal from
  10 nM to 10 µM; a scrambled control did nothing; a proprietary splice-switching ASO gave a 4- to
  7-fold rise at days 17, 24 and 31.

## What it does **not** say

- **The uORF result is reporter-only.** It is a transfected plasmid in HEK-293, not the endogenous
  locus, and the paper's own attempt to act on it failed: "our preliminary experiments with ASOs
  targeting uAUG2, uAUG3, or uAUG4 failed to increase luciferase activity in transfected cells or
  in vitro translation systems (data not shown)". The abstract's "demonstrating the therapeutic
  viability of increasing Nav1.1 from a wild-type allele" rests on that reporter.
- **The dynamic range is not quantified and is admitted to be poor**: "The low baseline luciferase
  signal in day 17–31 iNeurons likely reflects the developmental timing of Nav1.1 expression …
  Future studies will optimize conditions to improve dynamic range."
- **Reproducibility across platings was not tested**: "each HiBiT assay was conducted from a single
  plating of cells, with the inclusion of biological and technical replicates and a variety of
  concentrations or timepoints of collection, due to limitations in the ASO reagents."
- The splice-switching ASO's sequence and design are proprietary, so the upregulation arm is not
  independently reproducible.
- Nothing is shown about whether the HiBiT tag perturbs channel function; the argument is structural
  (the C-terminus is not involved in voltage sensing or conduction) and by reference.

## Transfer limit to WWOX-DEE (stated exactly)

This is the one genuinely portable asset in the group, and what transfers is the **method, not the
data**: endogenous knock-in of an 11-residue HiBiT tag plus LgBiT complementation gives a plate-format,
live-cell, antibody-free quantification of a native low-abundance protein, in a line that can be
differentiated into whatever cell type the disease needs, with the untagged allele carrying a
loss-of-function variant so the line models the patient genotype rather than a wild-type control.
That is the shape of the pharmacodynamic assay a WWOX programme does not have — and PMID 42181696 in
this same group of six is a worked example of what its absence costs: a phenotypic rescue whose
molecular intermediate could not be measured.
**Limits on the transfer, stated:** (i) nothing here is WWOX-specific — WWOX is a ~46 kDa
SDR-family protein with a WW-domain-dependent interactome, and whether a C-terminal tag is tolerated
must be tested, not assumed; (ii) the whole point of the destabilising-SDR-missense hypothesis is that
the protein is **degraded**, so a HiBiT readout on a missense allele reports abundance, which is
exactly the right endpoint there, but it reports nothing about oxidoreductase activity; (iii) the
line's own caveats — single plating, undocumented dynamic range, a BCL2L1-containing 20q duplication —
travel with the method and should be designed out, not inherited; (iv) the uORF lever is **not**
transferable as evidence: it is a reporter result whose own ASO test failed, and whether WWOX has
conserved uORFs at all is unexamined here.
