## What the paper did

A retrospective pooled analysis (the authors call it a meta-analysis) of **260 cynomolgus macaques**
across **nine toxicity studies** (four GLP, five non-GLP) conducted at two Labcorp sites: 193 dosed
with AAV9 vectors — of which 4 received empty capsid and 4 a promoter-less construct, leaving 185
with a full vector — and 67 concurrent vehicle controls. Ages 12–50 months. Routes: intrathecal
lumbar puncture or intra-cisterna magna (224 animals, 4.8 × 10¹² to 9.2 × 10¹³ vg per animal) and
intravenous (36 animals, ≈1.0 × 10¹⁴ vg/kg). Genomes self-complementary in eight studies,
single-stranded in one; promoters mostly CMV enhancer / chicken-β-actin hybrid, with one study each
using a *MECP2* promoter fragment, the human synapsin promoter, and proprietary promoters.
Transgenes: human cDNAs or enhanced GFP. Two studies used Omnipaque 180 contrast.

NfL was measured on the Quanterix Simoa NF-light assay in serum, plasma and CSF. Histopathology was
read by an ACVP board-certified pathologist with contemporaneous peer review and consensus grading on
a 5-point scale (1 minimal to 5 severe), with **18–21 DRG sampled per animal** (5–6 each cervical,
thoracic, lumbar; 2–3 sacral). A per-animal **composite endpoint** took the maximum severity across
degeneration/necrosis, satellite-cell aggregate/neuronal loss and glial increase/neuronal loss in
DRG and trigeminal ganglion. Final composite distribution: 132 unremarkable, 67 minimal, 41 slight,
20 moderate.

## What it states

That blood NfL tracks asymptomatic AAV9-induced DRG injury well enough to be used as a longitudinal
surrogate in **nonclinical** toxicology, that serum or plasma sampling is as informative as CSF
sampling, and that the lesion is non-progressive with a trend to resolution by 52 weeks. It states
that "NfL could be a useful biomarker in nonclinical toxicity testing" and that "caution should be
applied for any translation to humans."

## What it does NOT state

- Nothing about WWOX. NfL measures neuro-axonal injury of any cause; it is **not** a readout of any
  gene's functional state.
- It does **not** validate NfL for clinical use. "To date, NfL measurements have not been
  incorporated into clinical trials for AAV therapies. The correlation with respect to clinical
  manifestations, histopathologic DRG toxicity, and serum NfL measurements has yet to be verified in
  humans." The human parallel offered is one cited report of transient blood-NfL rise after
  intravenous onasemnogene.
- It does not give a **concentration** threshold, only fold-change-from-pre-dose cut-offs — which
  presupposes a pre-dose sample and a stable individual baseline.
- Strata are not resolved in the paper's tables as published here: the 78 % and 42 % figures are
  pooled across capsid-identical but promoter-, transgene-, dose- and route-heterogeneous studies,
  and the per-stratum breakdowns are in Tables S1–S3, which I did not fetch.
- No mechanism: no immunophenotyping, no transcriptomics, no cytokines, and no test of any
  mitigation. This paper cannot speak to whether the lesion is immune-preventable.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

- The vehicle-control elevation means NfL is confounded by the study procedures: an IT lumbar
  puncture alone can keep CSF neurofilament elevated "for up to 3 weeks", and femoral blood
  collection, co-housing and handling are named as contributors.
- "Because of the relative heterogeneity of the lesion and because not all DRGs from all animals
  administered AAV were examined microscopically, it is possible that an increase in NfL (which is
  representative of all DRGs) may not have been associated with microscopic changes."
- NfL "is constitutively released at lesser concentrations during normal physiologic processes such
  as neuronal growth, repair, and aging" and raised NfL "is not disease specific, which could make
  clinical application… in the background of neurodegenerative diseases and other comorbidities
  challenging compared with assessment in healthy NHP nonclinical development studies."
- The symptomatic animal's mixed sensory-motor picture "was specific to the payload used in this
  particular study and, therefore, is not expected to be generalized to other transgenes."

## Internal inconsistencies found on reading

1. **Dose range.** Results and Table 3 both state the IT range as 4.8 × 10¹² to **9.2 × 10¹³**
   vg/animal; the Discussion states "one-time IT (4.8 × 10¹² to **9.2 × 10¹⁴** vg/animal)" — a
   ten-fold disagreement with the paper's own methods table.
2. **Denominator for the clinical-sign rate.** Results: "only 1 of 193 AAV9-dosed cynomolgus
   macaques (0.5 %)". Discussion: "1/185 animals treated with full AAV9 vector". Figure 1's
   denominator is 185 treated against 67 control. The rate 0.5 % matches 1/193; 1/185 is 0.54 %.
   The difference is the 8 empty-capsid and promoter-less animals.

## Relationship to PMID 35331006 — these two are not independent

Same sponsor, overlapping authors (Tukov, Meseck, Penraat, Chand appear on both), and the pooled
dataset is drawn from the sponsor's own study warehouse. Three of this paper's descriptors match the
other paper's studies specifically: a small group of **Mauritius-origin** macaques in one non-GLP
study (PMID 35331006's 13-week mechanistic study used Mauritius-origin animals and was non-GLP);
**two studies using Omnipaque 180 contrast** (PMID 35331006's two intrathecal studies both did);
and an intravenous arm at ≈1.0 × 10¹⁴ vg/kg (PMID 35331006's intravenous study used
1.1 × 10¹⁴ vg/kg). Neither paper cross-lists its study identifiers, so the overlap cannot be proved
from these two artefacts alone — but it is likely, and these two sources must not be counted as
independent corroboration of each other.
