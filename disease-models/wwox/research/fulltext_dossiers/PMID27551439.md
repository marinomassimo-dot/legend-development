## What the paper did

Time-lapse FRET microscopy, co-immunoprecipitation, protein-aggregation assays, cell-cycle/apoptosis flow cytometry, and immunostaining of one mouse genotype. Systems used: COS7 (monkey kidney fibroblast), HEK293, SCC-9, BCC, B16F10, SK-N-SH (neuroblastoma), Jurkat, THP-1, U937, L929, primary rat glia, `Wwox−/−` and `Wwox+/+` MEF, and `Wwox−/−` mouse brain sections.

## What it does **not** state

- It does not measure the cascade in a neuron. Every apoptosis and aggregation readout is a transfected line; the one neuronal line (SK-N-SH) is used only for over-expression toxicity and for a TIAF1→TPC6A expression effect.
- It does not show a wild-type brain comparator. In Figure 7 the control for panels a–c is the **same knockout tissue with the antibodies peptide-blocked**, and panel d's control is `p-TPC6A (peptide blocking)` on knockout tissue. A peptide block tests antibody specificity, not genotype dependence.
- It does not establish the *order* independently of the companion paper: the sequencing sentence in the Discussion cites that paper and siRNA experiments reported there.
- It reports no quantification of the in-vivo claim beyond FRETc values with n = 6.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

- "the functional properties of these exosome-like particles remain to be established"; "The underlying mechanism of this regard is unknown"; "The role of TPC6A in the nuclei is unknown".
- Two load-bearing Discussion steps — the WWOX intramolecular WW–SDR fold, and TGF-β/Hyal-2/WWOX/Smad4 signalling in traumatic brain death — are cited as **unpublished data of the same laboratory**.

## Internal inconsistencies recorded verbatim (all three persisted as locators)

1. Results: "the S35G mutant failed to undergo aggregation, and the Y112F mutant had a reduced aggregation" — Discussion: "alteration of Tyr112 to Phe does not reduce TPC6AΔ aggregation". **Figure 5a adjudicates**: the Y112F bar carries p = 0.040 and the S35G bar p = 0.002 for medium vs TGF-β1, i.e. the mutant the Results call non-aggregating has the most significant TGF-β1 response of the three.
2. Results name **Tyr112** for the cytoplasmic relocation step; the Discussion names **Tyr216** for the same step. The protein is 173 aa (wild type) / 159 aa (Δ) by the companion paper's own alignment, so residue 216 does not exist in it.
3. Results: the S35G mutant "failed to undergo aggregation" and, two sentences later, "TGF-β1 significantly increased the aggregation of the S35G mutant".

## What I did not read

The fifteen supplementary movies (`.mp4`) were **not decoded** — no video decoder (`ffmpeg`, `cv2`, `av`) is installed in this environment. Several load-bearing time-lapse assertions (Videos 1–15, including the S35G apoptosis claim and the exosome-release observation) rest on those movies and are therefore **unverified by this reading**. The supplementary `.doc` was opened: its text and both embedded figure images (Supplementary Figures S1 and S2) were extracted from the OLE `Data` stream and inspected.

**Verdict: INGEST.** The paper is the fixed point of the cascade-ordering claim, and the reading narrows it: the order is inferred from shuttling kinetics and siRNA in cell lines, the apoptosis link is over-expression with no isoform specificity, and the single in-vivo figure has no wild-type comparator.
