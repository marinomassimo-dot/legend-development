## 3 · Citation fidelity — what did drift

### 3.1 🔴 The mouse penetrance: the higher of two numbers its own primary contains

> *"Using this protocol, osteosarcomas are detected in 100% of the post-natal mice prior to their death."*

Read in full **earlier today**, ref 23 (PMID 20530675) carries **two** numbers: 100% in the body, and
**19/22 = 86% in its own Figure S1 legend**, with a footnote showing **3 of 22 mice had only one femur
imaged**. The review propagates the higher one, drops the 86% entirely, and **broadens the window**:
the primary says *"by 18 days-of-age"* over animals examined at 14–18 days; *"prior to their death"*
implies eventual penetrance across the whole ~4-week lifespan.

*(Sentence spans a column break; verified against the rendered page before quoting.)*

### 3.2 🔴 Two independent negatives disposed of without a test

> *"Analyses by others of other Wwox null rodent models failed to detect osteosarcomas [36, 47], most
> likely due to the lack of a sensitive detection method such as μCT to find these tiny tumors."*

Ref 36 is **`PAPER 057`** (PMID 19936220, **0/9** by multimodal examination); ref 47 is the **lde/lde
rat** (`PAPER 058/059`). **Both are read in full in this repository.** The attribution to detection
insensitivity is offered as the likely cause with **no test, no re-examination of those animals and no
method comparison** — an `IPOTESI` written in the grammar of a fact.

**This is the sentence in the literature where the conflict `CLAIM 036` records as unresolved gets
resolved by assertion.** `PREMISE: DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK` — *a negative result is explained by method
insensitivity*. **`REVIVAL_TRIGGER`:** μCT re-imaging of archived limbs from the Ludes-Meyers or lde
cohorts, or any blinded side-by-side of histology versus μCT on the same animals.

### 3.3 🔴 The Conclusions conflate two different assays

| Where | What it says | Correct? |
|---|---|---|
| Abstract | *"the WWOX gene is deleted in 30% of cases and WWOX protein is absent or reduced in ~60% of tumors"* | ✅ separate and right |
| Body | *"deletion of WWOX in 3 of 10 (30%) … by CGH"*; separately *"loss of WWOX protein … in up to 60%"* | ✅ separate and right |
| **Conclusions** | *"WWOX is consistently **deleted** in the **majority** of human osteosarcomas"* | ❌ **conflated** |

The mechanism word **deleted** inherits the prevalence of the **other** assay, turning a **3/10 finding
in ten samples** into a majority. **The defect sits in the sentence most likely to be cited onward.**

The **only new human figure** in the review is a 3×3 grid of **one patient**: primary biopsy,
post-chemotherapy resection, metastasis, each stained H&E / WWOX / RUNX2. Read from the pixels,

That is **precisely the relationship the same review states was not evident in the paired cohort**

against it. The reader's visual takeaway is the opposite of the cohort result.

The caption's own inference — *"suggesting it originated from a chemotherapy-resistant clone"* — is
drawn from one patient's staining with **no clonality analysis anywhere in the review**. `IPOTESI`.
