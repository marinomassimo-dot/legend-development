## Premise test
The selection note called 16q23.1 "a recurrent pleiotropic signal at the WWOX locus" and warned it might be assigned to a neighbouring gene or not name WWOX. Result: WWOX is named zero times, in the text layer and in the Figure 3 gene labels. The paper itself assigns its 16q23.1 signal to CTRB1/CTRB2 (and BCAR1 appears on the same Figure 3 branch). Whether those genes lie near or far from WWOX within the cytoband is not stated in the paper; I did not measure it here and make no claim about it.

## What the paper did
Meta-analysis of acute-pancreatitis GWAS summary statistics (three biobanks; 18,045 cases, 1,285,319 controls per the abstract) crossed with four MRI-derived fat traits (intrapancreatic fat, liver fat, visceral and abdominal subcutaneous adipose tissue). Methods named: LDSC and HDL genetic correlation, CPASSOC cross-phenotype association, FUMA, Bayesian colocalization (plus-minus 250 kb windows), MAGMA gene-level analysis on GRCh37/hg19 annotation with plus-minus 10 kb gene windows, enrichment analyses, two-sample and multivariable Mendelian randomization. Mostly European-ancestry summary data.

## What it measured about the locus (class level only)
- 50 pleiotropic risk loci in 26 independent regions; 16q23.1 recurred across all four pairs.
- Three loci reached strong colocalization (PP.H4 at least 0.80); the text does not name them. In the rendered Figure 3, an orange (colocalized) node sits at 16q23.1 on the intrapancreatic-fat branch.
- At 16q23.1 the paper names CTRB1, CTRB2 (discussion: trypsinogen/trypsin degradation, prior links to alcoholic and non-alcoholic pancreatitis) and BCAR1 (figure label). CTRB1 is among eight genes found in all four pairs.

## What it does NOT state
- Nothing about WWOX, a WWOX allele, WWOX expression or any WWOX-DEE genotype.
- No fine-mapping to a gene at 16q23.1, no eQTL or functional validation (the authors list these as outstanding).
- Supplementary files 1 and 2 (Tables S1-S9, including the colocalization and loci tables) are cited but I found no link to them on the article page: not read, unavailable.

## Authors' own caveats
European-ancestry data; acute pancreatitis not stratified by aetiology or severity; fat traits measured in older populations (survivor bias); summary-level data only; window mapping can assign variants to several neighbouring genes, introducing redundancy; findings are "shared genetic architecture rather than definitive causality".

## Transfer limit (exact)
A common-variant, European-ancestry pleiotropy signal at a cytoband that contains WWOX, attributed by the authors to other genes. It says nothing about WWOX function and does not touch any rare WWOX allele or the reference genotype class. Earned null for the gene. Not medical advice.
