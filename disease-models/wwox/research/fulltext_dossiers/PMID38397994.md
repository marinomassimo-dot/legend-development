## 1 · What the paper is

A mini-review of hypoglycaemia unawareness in people with diabetes: definition, symptoms, risk factors, pathophysiology (autonomic failure, brain glucose sensing, cerebral adaptation, hormonal regulation, sleep), consequences and prevention. The section that matters for group H3 is the counter-regulatory hormone account in which epinephrine is one of three blunted hormones.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Antecedent hypoglycaemia blunts the sympathoadrenal response (epinephrine) and the glucagon and growth-hormone responses; a vicious cycle follows. Avoiding hypoglycaemia for two to three weeks restores awareness.
2. Brain glucose sensing and cerebral adaptation: threshold reset to lower glucose; ventromedial hypothalamic GABA tone proposed.
3. Sleep blunts epinephrine and awakening responses; a single study found counter-regulation preserved after sleep loss.
4. The review says brain cells cannot store glycogen.
5. Everything is about adult diabetes; there is no neonatal, genetic or encephalopathy content.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- The registry has the null-mouse hypoglycaemia as a documented feature but nothing about epinephrine or other counter-regulatory hormones. This review is the textbook for what a counter-regulatory panel is (epinephrine, glucagon, growth hormone, cortisol) and what failing it looks like in adults; it is a blueprint, not a datum.
- Counter-regulatory failure in diabetic adults is acquired (recurrent antecedent hypoglycaemia) and insulin-driven; a null mouse with an inherent metabolic or endocrine defect is a different mechanism class.
- Cross-document: the brain-glycogen sentence here disagrees with the glycogen review read earlier in this campaign; neither measures glycogen in a hypoglycaemic genetic model.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Any WWOX statement, any neonatal statement, any genetic-hypoglycaemia statement.
- That epinephrine is deficient in any genetic encephalopathy: the review describes an acquired adaptation in diabetes.
- Treatment of hypoglycaemia with epinephrine: the review mentions no adrenaline therapy; its therapeutic section is about insulin analogues, glucose monitoring and education.
- Mechanistic specifics (VMH GABA, sleep memory) beyond single cited studies.
