## 1 · What the paper did

Drug-target Mendelian randomisation of 6 888 druggable genes: whole-blood cis-eQTL (eQTLGen, 31 684 Europeans)
and plasma cis-pQTL (deCODE; UKB-PPP replication) against FinnGen R12 mature T/NK-cell lymphoma (440 cases);
Bayesian colocalisation; SMR/HEIDI with GoDMC blood mQTL; GEO microarrays (GSE19067, GSE90597) for expression,
CIBERSORT and GSEA; phenome-wide MR over 853 UK Biobank phenotypes; CTD chemical–gene look-up; AutoDock Vina.

## 3 · The direction inversion

The Results state: "The compounds potentially increasing WWOX expression include Vorinostat, Valproic Acid,
Sunitinib, Jinfukang, and Arsenic Trioxide", and present vorinostat, valproic acid, JQ1, ifosfamide, GSK1210151A
and calcitriol as reducing THBS2. **The paper's own Table S7, the CTD export the sentence rests on, lists every
WWOX row as "Decreases expression" and every THBS2 row as "Increases expression"** (read cell-wise). The
Discussion builds an HDAC-inhibitor strategy ("can simultaneously upregulate WWOX expression and downregulate
THBS2 expression") on the inverted reading.

For LEGEND this matters because valproic acid is an anti-seizure medicine. Table S7 attributes "valproic acid
decreases WWOX expression (Homo sapiens)" to five CTD reference PMIDs (23179753, 24935251, 26272509, 27188386,
28001369). **None is held and none was read**, so the direction, the cell type, the dose and whether the change is
transcript or protein are unknown here. It is a curated-database assertion, not a measurement in this paper, and
it says nothing about patients. Recorded as a lead with its falsifier in `CC-20261004W8-A-VPA-DIRECTION-01`.

The phenome-wide paragraph has the same kind of slip: having proposed raising WWOX, it reads a null scan as "WWOX
inhibition may not lead to widespread adverse effects".

Docking: −5.7 (vorinostat), −4.2 (valproate), −6.5 (sunitinib) kcal/mol against "WWOX protein", contacts at
residues numbered 7–18; the PDB entry is not named. Figure 7A (panel read) shows a small domain-sized fragment, not
a full-length protein. A docking score is not binding.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT state

- No experiment on WWOX, in any cell or tissue.
- Nothing about brain, neurons, epilepsy, or any WWOX allele.
- No dose–response: an eQTL-instrumented OR is a population association of small common-variant expression
  differences with risk, not a dose curve, and not an estimate of what restoring WWOX in a null background would do.

## 5 · Caveats the authors give themselves

European-ancestry data only; few cases and no independent validation cohort; residual pleiotropy and confounding
possible; future in-vitro and in-vivo validation needed; the WWOX protein association did not survive FDR.

## 6 · Transfer limit

None to WWOX-DEE. No allele, no neural tissue, no measurement. The one item with a possible bearing on the
disease model is a database direction (valproate → lower WWOX expression) that this paper misreports and does not
test; it is carried as a reading debt, not as evidence.
