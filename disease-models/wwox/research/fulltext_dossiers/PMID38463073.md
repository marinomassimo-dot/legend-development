## What it is

A narrative review of neonatal transfusion: neonatal physiology, RBC, platelet and granulocyte transfusion, trial and guideline tables (Tables 1-4), complications (hyperkalaemia, hypothermia, glucose homeostasis, 2,3-diphosphoglycerate), and a conclusion in favour of individualised strategies with a lean toward restrictive thresholds. WWOX: zero occurrences. No figures, no methods, no supplementary document.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** Neonates and preterm infants in general.

## What it says (DATO about the review; all cited)

- RBC transfusion raises haemoglobin and tissue oxygenation; 90 percent of extremely low birth weight and 58 percent of infants under 32 weeks are expected to be transfused (cited).
- The largest trial row: higher thresholds mean more transfusions and little evidence of benefit; one older small trial reports more brain injury with restriction.
- Complications of transfusion in neonates: hyperkalaemia, hypothermia, glucose fluctuation (both directions), 2,3-DPG depletion in stored red cells (leftward shift, reduced oxygen release for hours).

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No WWOX, no DEE, no genetic or metabolic encephalopathy.** The review concerns preterm and neonatal anaemia and surgery; it does not address a child with chronic neurological disease.
2. **Its direction of recommendation is internally unstable.** The abstract says trial outcomes are comparable and then that restriction "can reduce" them; the Highlights say higher thresholds are linked to more mortality and morbidity; the Advantages section says liberal thresholds bring more morbidity and mortality (no reference) and also better respiratory outcomes; the Conclusion says no clear support. A reader cannot take a direction from it.
3. **Table 4 ("RBC trials") is mostly observational.** Of its rows, the randomised trials are a few; the rest are cohorts relating transfusion to BPD, ROP or RDW. Association with transfusion in cohorts is confounded by illness severity, which the review itself concedes for glucose.
4. **No methods.** No search, inclusion rule or appraisal; 102 references.
5. **Oxygen carrying capacity is more than haemoglobin.** The 2,3-DPG paragraph is the only item here that separates carrying capacity from delivery (stored cells release less oxygen for 3-8 hours in infants), and it is a single cited statement; nothing about brain oxygen use.
