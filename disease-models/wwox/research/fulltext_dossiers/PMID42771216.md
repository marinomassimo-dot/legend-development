## 1 · What the study did

Two human ex vivo substrates from the same tumour specimens: patient-derived neurospheres and
patient-derived tissue slice cultures (seven specimens matched). **Self-complementary** GFP vectors,
AAV2 or AAV6, with and without EGF in the medium. Readouts: live confocal fluorescence intensity,
GFP-positive area fraction, vector-genome qPCR normalised to a single-copy human gene, flow
cytometry, immunofluorescence. Slices treated after 24 h and followed 5 days; spheres up to 14 days
in two experiments.

## 3 · What the study does NOT establish

That one substrate predicts the other (no dose-matched comparison); that genome abundance implies
expression (the authors separate entry, trafficking, persistence, processing from transcription and
translation); any immune, vascular or long-term behaviour — slices are ex vivo; anything about a
non-dividing, developing neuronal target, since the material is tumour and tumour-adjacent.

## 4 · Transfer limit for the restoration spec

What transfers is **method discipline, not a number**: (a) a slice-culture read-out without a
titration is not a potency comparison; (b) self-complementary cassettes make early expression a
property of the construct, so an early endpoint flatters any vector; (c) a 5-day endpoint can invert
by day 14; (d) specimen-to-specimen variance can be total, so a single donor's slice tells you
nothing about the next. Serotype rankings here (AAV6 over AAV2, EGFR-dependent) are **tumour-biology
specific** and do not transfer to a CNS neuronal target at all.
