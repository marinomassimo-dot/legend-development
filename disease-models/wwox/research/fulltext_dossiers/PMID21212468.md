## What it does NOT state

- No neuronal data of any kind; the neurodegeneration section is explicitly headed "Perspective" and its
  only WWOX-and-brain content is the cited hippocampal downregulation and tau result.
- No endogenous-level experiment: the article says so itself — "Whether the endogenous Zfra blocks the
  apoptotic function of p53 and WOX1 remains to be determined."
- No quantitative value anywhere: no n, no statistic, no effect size. The one number given is a cited
  ">85 %" suppression of Bcl-2/Bcl-xL by the ectopic SDR domain.
- No reconciliation of the mechanism it proposes (Zfra blocks cytochrome c release, possibly via VDAC) with
  the membrane-potential dissipation it also reports; the authors call the combination "very unusual and
  intriguing" and offer binding to VDAC or to cytochrome c as speculation.

## How it bears on the group's question

It supplies the **mitochondrial arm at endpoint level** — Bcl-2-family protein level, cytochrome c release
and ΔΨm are named as separable readouts — and it states a relation between Zfra and WWOX that is an
inhibition of a **gain-of-function** effect. Against PMID 41677633, where WWOX induction lowers
Bcl-X<sub>L</sub>/Mcl-1 and collapses ΔΨm, the two are not in conflict: both make activated/overexpressed
WWOX a pro-death agent at the mitochondrion, and Zfra an antagonist of it.
