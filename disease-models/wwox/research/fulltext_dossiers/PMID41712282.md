## What the paper did

A **self-complementary** AAV9 vector was built around a minimal synthetic JeT-plus-intron promoter
(UsP) driving a codon-optimised 1.7 kb human *SLC13A5* coding sequence with a synthetic poly(A),
flanked by an AAV2 wild-type ITR and a mutated ΔITR to force self-complementary packaging. Function
was shown in HEK293T cells (membrane localisation; ~70% reduction of extracellular citrate by
GC-MS). In *Slc13a5*-knockout mice:

- **P10 arm:** intrathecal lumbar puncture, 2 × 10^11 vg (low) or 8 × 10^11 vg (high), followed ~4
  months (a second cohort to 6–7 months for CSF citrate).
- **Adult arm:** 3 months of age, 8 × 10^11 vg by intracisterna magna versus intrathecal lumbar
  puncture, followed ~6 months.

Endpoints: blood and CSF citrate, NaCT immunohistochemistry, EEG spike trains, sleep staging and
power spectral density, pentylenetetrazol kindling (eight 30 mg/kg injections, modified Racine
scale), survival, and droplet-digital-PCR biodistribution.

## What the paper states

- **Cargo:** the short promoter is what makes self-complementary packaging of a 1.7 kb coding
  sequence possible, and self-complementary AAV "is predicted to stably transduce at least 10-fold
  more cells than single-stranded AAV". The choice of scAAV is justified by speed and stability of
  onset, not by necessity.
- **The metabolic endpoint is dose-graded and can overshoot.** Plasma citrate fell to 87 ± 7.7% of
  wild type at the low dose and **65 ± 8.0%** at the high dose; CSF citrate to 81 ± 3.9%. The
  knockout baseline is ~20% above wild type in blood and ~50% above in CSF.
- **The seizure endpoint saturates.** "Surprisingly in our PTZ kindling paradigm, low-dose
  AAV9/SLC13A5 administered at P10 provided similar benefit as high dose." Spike trains, brain waves
  and sleep, by contrast, improved more at the high dose.
- **The two phenotypes therefore do not share a dose.** The authors raise the matching risk: a
  transgenic model overexpressing *SLC13A5* in CAMKIIa+ neurons from embryonic development showed
  autistic-like and jumping behaviours and altered white-matter integrity and synaptic plasticity,
  and rodent *Slc13a5* expression is low at birth and rises afterwards — so high NaCT from a vector
  in the prenatal or immediate post-neonatal period "may impact proper brain development".
- **Window:** benefit was obtained at both P10 and 3 months, but the age effect on delivery is large.
  At the same dose and route, cerebellar vector was 15.7 × 10^3 ± 3.1 × 10^3 vg per mouse genome when
  dosed at P10 against 1.2 × 10^3 ± 7.4 × 10^2 when dosed at 3 months. Switching the adult route to
  ICM recovered part of it (7.5 × 10^3 ± 5.9 × 10^3) — more than IT in the adult, still below the
  neonatal figure — while liver vector was comparable by either adult route (~1.4–1.5 × 10^4).
- Route mattered for outcome, not just distribution: ICM gave the significant spike-train reduction
  and the better seizure-severity improvement; IT in the adult was weaker on both.
- Cell type: transduction covered neurons and glia, with NaCT-positive cells more often neuronal than
  astrocytic at 6–7 months (e.g. 16% of MAP2+ versus 6% of S100B+ in brain stem).
- Tolerability: no adverse effects by body weight, survival, general activity; blood biochemistry
  within normal range; no gross necropsy abnormality including kidneys; urinary citrate 27% lower at
  the low dose but not significant (P = 0.6983).
- The authors propose blood or CSF citrate as a biochemical marker that functional NaCT has been
  restored — explicitly a marker of target engagement, and they note elevated extracellular citrate
  "may not directly correlate with disease outcome".

## What the paper does NOT state

- It reports **no immune endpoint**: no anti-capsid antibody, no T-cell assay, no CNS inflammatory
  histology, no DRG assessment. The human transgene is a foreign protein in a knockout mouse — the
  closest thing in this group to a cross-reactive-immunological-material-negative animal — and that
  is precisely the measurement not made.
- It states that long-term NaCT overexpression was not assessed: "adverse effects related to
  prolonged or excessive NaCT expression were not evaluated in the present study". The multi-species
  toxicology and biodistribution studies are said to be complete but "will be reported separately",
  so this paper carries no toxicology package.
- No seizure-free or spontaneous-seizure endpoint: spontaneous seizures were not seen in young adult
  knockouts at all, and the efficacy claim rests on a chemoconvulsant challenge that the authors
  concede "does not model the primary metabolic defect".
- No motor or cognitive endpoint — the authors name the inability to test them as a limitation.
- No human data, no proposed human dose, and no dose-scaling calculation of any kind.
- No measurement of NaCT protein level relative to wild type: the antibody does not recognise mouse
  NaCT, so wild-type staining is background and there is **no physiological reference** for how much
  transgene protein is "normal". Expression is reported as presence and pattern, never as a fraction
  of wild type.
- It says nothing about *WWOX*.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

The knockout phenotype is mild and lacks the motor and cognitive deficits that dominate the human
disorder; no animal model captures the whole disorder; species differences in NaCT kinetics may
matter; most patient variants are **missense** producing partially functional or misfolded protein,
so a complete knockout is not the patient's genotype and a knock-in "could yield phenotypes distinct
from complete KO"; mutant protein might compete with vector-derived protein, which they argue is
unlikely but do not test; the weaker adult sleep and theta/alpha rescue "may be due to group sizes
and being underpowered" or may be a developmental feature not rescuable later; PTZ severity worsens
with age in mice, which their own wild-type controls show (mean Racine 1.4 at 4 months versus 2.9 at
9 months); the relative contribution of neuronal versus glial NaCT loss is unknown. The senior
author is an inventor on the vector design and has received income from it; funding came from a
patient foundation and from a gene-therapy company.

## Caveats found by reading, not stated as limitations

- **The control arm is shared with an earlier publication.** The data-availability statement says the
  wild-type-plus-vehicle and knockout-plus-vehicle sleep architecture and power spectra of the P10
  cohort were already reported in the group's prior paper; only the treated arms are new. Anyone
  tallying independent cohorts across the two papers would count the same control animals twice.
- **The vehicle control itself dies in the adult challenge.** By the eighth pentylenetetrazol
  injection at 9 months of age, 2 of 8 wild-type vehicle mice (25%) had died alongside 6 of 9
  knockout vehicle mice (67%); the treated groups were 1 of 6 each. The mortality endpoint therefore
  sits on a background in which the healthy comparator also dies, and the treated group sizes are six.
- Three vector lots were used across the studies and the certificates of analysis are in a supplement
  that was not fetched, so lot-to-lot comparability (titre method, empty-capsid fraction) is not
  verifiable from the body text.
- The P10 arm used one route only (intrathecal), so age and route are not fully crossed: the
  neonatal-versus-adult comparison at matched route and dose exists, but there is no neonatal ICM arm.

### What the age contrast is and is not (the window question)

1. **Same total dose is not the same dose per kilogram.** Both arms received 8 x 10^11 vg in absolute terms. From the paper's own day-0 body weights in Supplemental Figure 2 (P10 high-dose animals, n = 22, mean 5.8 g) and Supplemental Figure 5 (3-month IT animals, n = 7, mean 21.1 g), that total is about 1.4 x 10^14 vg/kg at P10 and about 3.8 x 10^13 vg/kg at 3 months, a factor of about 3.6 (arithmetic done here from the workbook; the authors state no per-kilogram figure). So a part of the delivery difference is a per-mass dose difference.
2. **Cross-cohort, untested, unequal.** Figure 8A and 8B are separate cohorts; the legend gives one unpaired t test per panel and no test between ages. Group sizes are 14 against 4 (3 female and 1 male in the adult IT group; Supplemental Table 1), and follow-up differs (P10 cohort followed to about 4 months, adult cohort to about 6 months). Three of the 14 P10 high-dose animals (cerebellum 1,057, 1,271 and 2,501) are below the largest adult IT value (3,362), so the distributions overlap at the low end. The P10 low dose (2 x 10^11 vg) reaches a cerebellar mean of 7,223, equal to the adult ICM mean at four times the dose.
3. **No age-stratified efficacy panel exists.** Efficacy panels (Figures 3 and 6) are separate cohorts tested at about 4 and about 9 months; the wild-type vehicle Racine score is about 1.4 at 4 months and about 3 at 9 months (Figure 3C and 6C), so the challenge itself moves with age and effect sizes are not comparable across cohorts. There is no age-by-treatment test. The adult IT arm, with about 13 times less cerebellar vector than P10 high dose, still shows fewer PTZ deaths than adult vehicle (1 of 6 against 6 of 9 in the text), so efficacy in this challenge did not track delivery across ages either; that is an observation about a chemoconvulsant paradigm, not a window result.
4. **Answer.** No panel or table in the supplement re-opens the window question. The standing negative (the window cannot be bounded) is unchanged and is strengthened by one confound that DIS-033 does not name: the same absolute dose is about 3.6-fold higher per kilogram at P10.

### Other findings against the carried text

- **Tolerability evidence is narrower than the headline.** The Results say blood biochemistry stayed in range "across all groups" (Supplemental Figure 3). That figure's legend shows wild-type vehicle, knockout vehicle and the **low dose only** (n = 6 to 7 in the low-dose group); no high-dose animal and no adult animal has urine or chemistry (Supplemental Table 1 has no such cohort for them). For the high dose (the one with 65 percent citrate and individuals near 30 percent) tolerability rests on weight, survival and activity (Supplemental Figures 2, 5, 7). The abstract says pups and adults were safe and well tolerated.
- **Two of three lot certificates.** The Methods say three lots were used and Supplemental Figure 8 holds the certificate for each; the figure shows two (LAV55-conc, 1.55E+14 vg/mL, 90 percent full by electron microscopy; LAV131, 1.38E+14 vg/mL, 92 percent full). The lot used for each cohort is not stated.
- **Undisclosed exclusions in the data sheets.** Figure 8A values marked with an asterisk (for example a high-dose brain-stem value of 2,180,689 and a liver value of 271,433) and a row marked "value excluded" appear in the workbook; the plotted high-dose brain-stem mean (about 2.8 x 10^4) is reproduced only without the starred values. No Methods or legend sentence states an outlier rule. The cerebellum values the registry carries have no flagged cell, so they are unaffected.
- **Unit label.** Axes and the 8B sentence say vg per mouse genome; the sentence introducing Figure 8A says vg per microgram host genomic DNA. Values of 10^3 to 10^4 are not a plausible per-diploid-genome copy number; the unit is as printed and is probably a normalisation, so carry it with the printed label and do not convert.
- **Adult mortality denominators.** Results: 2 of 8 vehicle wild-type mice (25 percent) died; Supplemental Table 1 and the Methods exclusion list give 7 analysed (11 minus 4 that died on the first injection and were excluded as hypersensitive); the survival curve ends near 71 percent, which fits 2 of 7. The first dossier carried "2 of 8" from the text. Four of 11 adult wild-type vehicle mice (36 percent) died on the first PTZ injection and are outside the analysis.
- **Immune endpoint: confirmed absent** from the whole supplement (RL-GT-002 unaffected). WWOX occurs zero times in the supplement text layers.
