## What it is

A single-centre, uncontrolled pre/post study in 23 adults with low-risk, transfusion-dependent MDS and ferritin above 1000: peripheral-blood flow cytometry before and after oral deferasirox (median nine months in Methods, six in the abstract). Readouts: ferritin, endothelial and adhesion markers, circulating endothelial progenitor cells, leukocyte ROS, glutathione and Rhodamine-123 fluorescence (a membrane-potential surrogate), intracellular cytokines. WWOX: zero occurrences. Patients were enrolled 2009-2011; the paper is 2025.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** Adult myeloid-malignancy leukocytes, not neural tissue, not a genetic encephalopathy, not a child.

## What it measures (DATO)

Ferritin fell about 55 percent (2090.87 to 946.39 ng/mL); leukocyte H2O2 and superoxide fell, glutathione and Rhodamine-123 signal rose, adhesion markers and monocyte-platelet aggregates fell, IL-1, IL-3, IL-6 and TNF-alpha fell and IFN-gamma rose. Haemoglobin and the other classical haematological parameters did not change, and no correlation with the biological endpoints was found.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No causal statement that transfusional iron damages mitochondria.** The design has no comparator (the authors' own limitation) and transfusion burden was not recorded. Deferasirox is named by the authors as acting "beyond iron removal" (pleiotropic effects), so a change after the drug is not a change after iron removal. The H1 question 4 premise (transfusional iron overload damages mitochondria in a way that overlaps WWOX respiratory-complex findings) is **stated in the paper as a model (Figure 5), not shown**.
2. **No brain, no neuron, no child, no genetic disease.** Mitochondrial readouts are leukocyte dye fluorescence; nothing about CNS or about a mitochondrially stressed brain. Not WWOX evidence.
3. **The statistics do not carry the paper's weight.** Exploratory, no multiplicity correction in the main analysis, a p threshold of 0.001 with nearly every p below 0.0001, post-treatment standard errors an order of magnitude smaller than baseline for ferritin in the same 23 patients (14.51 vs 148.38), and healthy controls shown for one panel only.
4. **Figure/text discrepancies (panel-vs-text), both read from the image at its supplied 649 x 397 px.** (a) The text says MDS patients had reduced EPC and EC levels at baseline; in Figure 2 the healthy-control EPC box (about 0.06) sits below the pre-treatment box. (b) The text gives healthy EC as 0.30 plus or minus 0.078; the yellow EC box is at about 0.1-0.15. The "MFI" labelling on cell-frequency axes (Figures 1B, 2) and the text description of Figure 1B as T cell-monocyte interaction against its axis label (platelet-monocyte) are further looseness.
5. **Supplementary Table S1's multiplicity adjustment does not reproduce.** I recomputed in this session the Benjamini-Hochberg and Holm adjustments for 17 outcomes at p = 0.0001 and one at 0.0013 (m = 18), the table's own stated inputs: BH q = 0.000106 for all seventeen tied outcomes and Holm = 0.0018 for all; the table prints a ladder (BH 0.000556, 0.001111 ... 0.01; Holm 0.0018, 0.0017 ... 0.0001). The ladder resembles BH critical values (rank over 18 times 0.01), not adjusted p-values (INFERENZA). The conclusion that the headline effects survive adjustment is not threatened by the correct numbers; the table is not a valid source of adjusted values.
6. **Internal inconsistencies:** treatment duration (abstract about six months median; Methods nine); a Results sentence that says pre-treatment ROS "were markedly reduced" while the numbers fall after treatment; IPSS-R category "Intermediate I" for 16 patients where inclusion was low-risk (the IPSS-R scheme has an unqualified intermediate category, an outside-knowledge note); the ethics statement describes a retrospective study and written informed consent.

## Design and limits

Single centre, n = 23, uncontrolled, historical enrolment, flow cytometry on MFI for all readouts (cell counts reported as MFI), cytokines intracellular only, no clinical outcome.
