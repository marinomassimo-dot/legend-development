## What it measured

**The sequence, which is the paper's real contribution.** Ultrastructural change in DRG sensory
neurons (ER dilation/vesiculation, mitochondrial swelling) from **day 5**; immune infiltrates as
distinct foci (NK cells, monocytes, B cells, CD4⁺ T cells) from **day 5**, with pDCs and CD8⁺ T cells
at days 15–29; CSF CXCL10 and MIP1α significantly raised at day 5 and 9 respectively; DRG innate-immune
and type-I-IFN transcriptional modules up at day 5 — all **before** the first histopathological lesion,
which appears at **day 15** in one of two animals and is widespread by day 29. Cleaved caspase-3 only
at day 29. Serum NF-H not significantly different from vehicle at days 5, 9 and 15 (means 1 496,
3 581, 32 936 pg/mL vs vehicle 2 382) and significantly raised only at day 29 (42.97-fold, p = 0.036).

**Dissociation of day-5 from transgene level.** Day-5 module changes "do not appear to correlate with
transgene expression levels", whereas days 15 and 29 changes do — the authors read this as two
distinct mechanisms.

**The immunosuppression effect, as incidence over DRGs examined.**
- IMS #1 (hSMN1): 2/3 untreated animals with minimal DRG neuronal degeneration vs 1/3 with IMS #1
  (a single DRG, two degenerate neurons). Motor-neuron degeneration 2/3 without IMS, **0/3** with.
  IMS #1 did **not** reduce trigeminal mononuclear infiltrates or sciatic nerve-fibre degeneration.
- IMS #2 (hGBA1): **10 of 26 DRGs** affected in 3/3 animals without IMS vs **1 of 29 DRGs** with IMS.
- IMS #3 (mir-SOD1, non-protein cargo): **11 of 36 DRGs** in 3/3 animals without IMS vs **1 of 36**
  with IMS; spinal-cord nerve-fibre degeneration eliminated; sciatic findings eliminated; serum NF-H
  27 699 ± 14 885 pg/mL without IMS vs 1 224 ± 601 with IMS, the latter equal to vehicle.
- Transgene expression in DRG was **not** reduced by any regimen (p = 0.45, 0.997, 0.33), so the
  benefit is not explained by less expression.

**The pooled argument.** Across the four studies from day 15 onward, mononuclear infiltrates in 79 of
249 DRGs, neuronal degeneration in 35, and **34 of those 35 (97 %) were among the infiltrated**.
11/12 (91 %) unsuppressed ICM/IT animals developed DRG pathology.

## What it states

A causal role for the immune response in AAV-mediated DRG and nerve-fibre toxicity, demonstrated by a
pharmacological block that works across three cargos including one that expresses no protein — and
therefore that the effect is not an anti-transgene response but most plausibly capsid-peptide
cross-presentation to CD8⁺ T cells. Also that CSF CXCL10 and MIP1α are candidate biomarkers alongside
serum neurofilament.

## What it does NOT state

- Nothing about WWOX, and nothing about any CNS disease the vectors were not already aimed at.
- The causal claim rests on a **pharmacological block**, which the authors concede could act through
  several arms; no arm was depleted genetically or with a single-target antibody. They state plainly:
  "we cannot conclude the 'optimal' regimen".
- **No capsid- or transgene-specific T cells were detected** in PBMCs by ELISpot (Figure S11). The
  CD8⁺ cross-presentation mechanism is explicitly a postulate: "it is reasonable to postulate…".
  Infiltrating DRG lymphocytes could not be isolated for antigen-specificity testing.
- **The regimen is not shown to be safe or sufficient in an infant, or over the needed duration.**
  The authors predict "the immunosuppression regimen would need to be maintained for at least
  6 months", which no study here tested; the longest arm is 43 days.
- **n = 3 per group** in every immunosuppression study, with no power statement. Study-to-study and
  animal-to-animal variability is acknowledged: one unsuppressed animal (22501) had no pathology
  despite equivalent transgene and vector levels; one suppressed animal (24001) had pathology, which
  the authors speculate reflects pre-existing memory capsid T cells that they did not measure.
- No dose-response: each study uses one dose.
- Cargo and route are confounded with regimen across the three studies, and IMS #1 differs from #2
  and #3 in start day and in carrying an extra intrathecal dexamethasone dose.
- No efficacy endpoint: these are healthy macaques, so the regimen's effect on therapeutic benefit
  is unmeasured (only that transgene expression is preserved).

## Timing correction to the received description

The abstract and discussion say "prophylactic treatment". That is accurate for IMS #2 (day −2) and
IMS #3 (day −3), but **IMS #1 started on day 2 — after dosing on day 1** (Methods, "AAVhu68-hSMN1
immunosuppression study #1"). Any statement that the mitigation must begin before vector
administration is not supported by study #1, and study #1 is the one with the weakest effect.

## How this paper itself characterises the disagreement with PMID 35331006

The source does the work: its reference 14 **is** PMID 35331006 (Tukov et al., *Hum Gene Ther*
2022;33:740–756, DOI 10.1089/hum.2021.255). It writes: "Other groups have tested the efficacy of a
glucocorticoid steroid prednisolone,14 MMF, and mTOR inhibitor rapamycin,12,13 or B-cell-depleting
antibody rituximab and mTOR inhibitor everolimus14 without apparent reductions in AAV-mediated DRG
toxicity." Its own explanation is drug class, not biology: "Since prednisolone alone was unable to
reduce DRG lesions, we reason that the inhibition of T cells via the addition of Tacrolimus to an
anti-inflammatory glucocorticoid steroid is responsible for observed effect." It adds that it is
"unclear why rituximab-containing regimens have been unsuccessful… more studies are required".

## Species caveat the authors themselves raise

In mice "neuronal degeneration… can occur in the absence of immune infiltrates, and thus, immune
infiltration into the DRG in mice is considered a secondary response to neuronal degeneration",
and mice "elicit poor or dysfunctional T cell responses to AAV vectors". The immune mechanism is
therefore a primate finding and does not transfer to a mouse study of the same question.
