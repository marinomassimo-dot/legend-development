## What it is

The only WWOX-direct paper in this lot. WWOX immunostaining in human psoriatic skin and imiquimod-treated mouse skin, public microarray corroboration (Supplement S2), and a loss-of-function experiment in SV40-immortalised human epidermal keratinocytes (adenovirus-delivered microRNA against WWOX) stimulated with poly(I:C): p-p65, five cytokines and PKC activity, with a calcium chelator and a PKC inhibitor as pathway tools and a partial rescue by overexpression (Supplement S4).

## WWOX content

Direct, but non-neural, non-developmental, partial knockdown (not a null, a hypomorph or a patient variant) in an immortalised human keratinocyte line. **There is no WWOX-deficient evidence on plasma, FFP, plasma exchange, albumin or IVIG in this paper.** It is the nearest WWOX-direct anchor for H2 only through NF-kB and cytokines.

## What it measures (DATO)

WWOX immunoreactivity is higher in psoriatic than normal epidermis (3 normal against 6 patients; about 380 percent of control) and in imiquimod skin (time course peaking at day 5); lesional skin has higher WWOX probe signal than non-lesional in four paired biopsies (public data). Knockdown to about half protein lowers poly(I:C)-induced p-p65 (about 9300 to 5100) and partly lowers IL-6, IL-8, TNF-alpha, IL-1beta and CCL20 mRNA; PKC activity after poly(I:C) is lower in knockdown cells.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Direction: it shows WWOX knockdown reducing inflammatory output, the opposite of other WWOX-loss inflammation reports in this repository.** Registry titles for the already read papers: partial Wwox loss increases murine sepsis and neuroinflammation severity (39868255), endothelial WWOX knockdown increases inflammation in ventilator-induced lung injury (38563965), B-cell Wwox deletion promotes a proinflammatory signature (41090157). Compared at title level only (I did not re-read those papers here). This is a further instance for the context-dependent-output principle in CLAIM 028; it does not reconcile the signs.
2. **No neural, plasma or complement link.** The "plasma" relevance is through NF-kB/cytokine biology in keratinocytes. Nothing about circulating factors, complement, albumin or immunoglobulin.
3. **The knockdown is partial and the rescue is partial.** Protein about 45 percent of control (Figure 2A) while mRNA is nearly absent (Figure 3A); the rescue restores p-p65 to about 3400 against 4800 in control; the authors state they could not fully restore it. Overexpression is far above endogenous.
4. **The mechanism chain is predicted, not shown.** WWOX-JPT2 comes from Ingenuity software (Supplement S5), no experiment on JPT2; calcium and PKC are tested with BAPTA-AM and GF109203X, which act regardless of WWOX; neither agent was tested in WWOX knockdown cells, so no epistasis is shown. BAPTA-AM also lowers untreated baselines and no viability assay is shown.
5. **Text-versus-panel discrepancies (read from the images):** Figure 3C IL-1beta secretion is marked 'ns' while the text says secretion was significantly decreased; Figure 4B untreated knockdown PKC activity is lower (about 72 against 107) while the text says no change; Figure 5C IL-8 mRNA does not fall with GF109203X while the text says it "decreased but was not statistically significant"; Figure 4A (1.2-fold poly(I:C) effect at 1 h) and Figure 4B (about 2-fold) disagree on the same stimulation; Figure 1B's histogram compares days to day 1, not to control.
6. **Normalisation and controls.** p-p65 against actin only (no total p65), Student's t-tests on multi-group experiments, n = 3, no antibody-specificity control for IHC, WWOX also appears as multiple higher bands on the uncropped blot (S3). A catalogue number is repeated for two reagents.
7. **Human tissue n is small** (3 versus 6) and the supplement's S1D mouse n (5) differs from the histology n (2).

## Design and limits

In vitro single poly(I:C) stimulus, one line, one microRNA, no in vivo WWOX manipulation; association data in tissue are expression-only.
