## 1 · What the paper measures

A single-centre cohort of 53 patients with Glut1 deficiency syndrome (Glut1DS), 44 with epilepsy, treated with ketogenic dietary therapy (KDT) and followed 2000 to 2023. Readouts: seizure response (more than 90 percent reduction on KDT monotherapy), EEG discharges and background slowing before and on KDT, SLC2A1 variant class, add-on antiseizure medication. Ketosis by parent-logged capillary 3-hydroxybutyrate; EEG awake only, hand-rated by the treating authors. WWOX does not occur.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. 27 of 44 (61 percent) responded on KDT monotherapy; 17 did not; 10 of those 17 responded to KDT plus an ASM and 7 stayed uncontrolled.
2. No association of response with SLC2A1 variant location or class (missense versus truncating), age, sex, KDT type, seizure type or onset age; the authors could not say why KDT fails.
3. Figure 3: of the 17, six (panel A) and four (panel C) had an initial KDT-monotherapy period described as responsive, then relapsed; seven (panels B, D) never responded. At least ten non-responders are therefore secondary failures, which the 61 percent headline hides.
4. Arithmetic slip: ASM co-medication is said to work in "23 percent of non-responders"; 10 of 17 is 59 percent and 23 percent is 10 of all 44.
5. Figure 5: discharges 15 to 5 (responders) and 14 to 9 (non-responders); background slowing 4 to 0 and 4 to 4. The Abstract's "improved in 5" and "improved in 9" are the persisting counts; Results text gives resolved counts 10 and 5.
6. Background-slowing reversal (4 of 4 against 0 of 4) is proposed as a response marker.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- Selection question 3 asked how far ketones fail when the defect is known. Here, in the disease with the cleanest fuel-supply lesion, 39 percent of epileptic patients are not controlled by KDT alone and covariates do not explain it. A KD response in any other aetiology, including WOREE, is therefore weak evidence about mechanism in either direction: a responder does not prove an energy lesion, a non-responder does not rule one out.
- This resets how the three-of-five WOREE report (CLAIM 042, denominator unstated) should be read: even a clean response rate would not identify a mechanism, and the Hu 2025 review supplies the base rate (47 to 100 percent by syndrome and gene, from small cohorts).
- The cohort also shows secondary loss of response, a case type the WOREE series (timing and duration of diet not stated) cannot exclude.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Any claim about WWOX or non-Glut1 aetiologies.
- That response is predictable from genotype (no correlation found), or that EEG slowing reversal is a validated marker (n 4 and 4, hand-rated, awake only).
- The 23 percent figure as stated.

## 5 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype (WWOX-DEE)

ESPANSIONE as a calibration. System gap: a defect of glucose entry across the blood-brain barrier, in a childhood clinic cohort. Use: an upper-bound benchmark; if KDT fails in a third of a pure fuel-supply epilepsy, response frequencies in other DEE are not diagnostic of an energy mechanism.

## 6 · Epistemic tags and reopening

- DATO: 27/44 and the secondary-failure pattern of Figure 3. INFERENZA: at least ten secondary failures among the non-responders. PREMISE: DATO (Figure 3 legend and bars).
- Rejected: reading KD response as evidence for an energy or Warburg-type lesion in WWOX-DEE. PREMISE: INFERENZA. REVIVAL_TRIGGER: brain energy measurement (FDG-PET, MR spectroscopy lactate) paired with KD response in WWOX-DEE patients.
