## What it is

A narrative review by authors employed by (and stockholders in) the developer of the drug. It assembles preclinical evidence for a brain-specific cholesterol-catabolism enzyme inhibitor and proposes three mechanisms: lowering 24-hydroxycholesterol (an NMDA-receptor positive modulator), preserving membrane-raft cholesterol and astrocytic glutamate uptake, and reducing neuroinflammation. WWOX: zero occurrences. It reports no new measurement and no clinical efficacy figures.

## What it says (DATO about the review's text)

- Total brain cholesterol is held constant; deleting the enzyme in mice leaves it unchanged because synthesis falls. More than 95 percent of brain cholesterol is made in the brain.
- In mice the drug protects in two Dravet models, the intra-amygdala kainate model, kindling and audiogenic models; it fails in maximal electroshock, subcutaneous pentylenetetrazol and 6-Hz assays (eight animals per arm in Table 2). The review notes electroshock deaths in the vehicle arm and none in the drug arm.
- The knockout mouse has a mild phenotype with near-complete loss of the metabolite; in slices only the NMDA/AMPA ratio changes.
- All three mechanisms are labelled hypotheses; the review says the second and third need future study.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Nothing about WWOX.** There is no WWOX model, genotype, cholesterol or oxysterol measurement in the review, and none in this repository's registry from this lot. The route is not shown to be reachable in WWOX loss of function; it is not shown to be unrelated either. The question is open for lack of a measurement.
2. **The efficacy claim is model-dependent**, and the mechanism is undetermined by the authors' own statement. The review gives no human efficacy numbers; phase III results are described only as completed.
3. **The drug works in models with a sodium-channel lesion, kainate-induced or kindled network hyperexcitability**; none is a hypomyelinating or WWOX model. The WWOX epilepsy mechanism recorded in the working model is not tested.
4. **Sponsor authorship** is a weighting fact, not a finding.
5. **The neuroinflammation arm is a correlation** in a tauopathy mouse the authors say may reflect damage rather than cause.
