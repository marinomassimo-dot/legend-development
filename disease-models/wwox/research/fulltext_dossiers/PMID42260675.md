## 1 · What the paper is

A trial protocol (no results) for an open-label stepped-wedge cluster quasi-randomised trial of an initial intramuscular epinephrine dose given before standard intravenous or intraosseous epinephrine in children (1 day to under 18 years) with out-of-hospital arrest. It is the only paediatric epinephrine item in group H3 and the only one that includes neonates.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Primary outcome: time to return of circulation, in patients who achieve it; secondary: discharge survival, survival and neurological status at discharge, 6 and 12 months, quality of life, and adverse events.
2. Dose: IM at up to 10 times the IV dose (0.3 to 3 mg by weight band) followed immediately by IV or IO epinephrine; possible double dosing is acknowledged.
3. Safety: reasoned from anaphylaxis practice and an adult before-and-after study; no stopping rules; an independent safety board (two clinicians and a biostatistician) with interim looks at 50 and 75 percent.
4. Controls include 111 retrospective patients; 3 intervention patients to the end of 2024.
5. Unblinded outcome assessment by attending paramedics.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- No registry record touches epinephrine in children; this is an unmodelled safety-and-method frame, with no WWOX data.
- The protocol's primary outcome is the surrogate the adult review shows dissociating from neurological outcome; the protocol says it is "strongly associated with survival" on the strength of one cited source.
- The design carries known biases the protocol does not discuss: historical controls over two years in a changing practice, outcome measured by unblinded clinicians, and an analysis conditioned on achieving the outcome.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Any WWOX, seizure or neonatal-metabolic statement.
- Efficacy or safety of IM epinephrine in children: it is a plan, not a result.
- That the dose is safe by analogy to anaphylaxis: the anaphylaxis dose and the trial dose differ and the authors themselves flag a double-dose possibility and a prior ten-fold-dose trend towards harm.
- That the stepped-wedge design is equivalent to a randomised comparison given the retrospective control period.

## 5 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype (WWOX-DEE)

ESPANSIONE, minimal. System gap: paediatric arrest, not a genetic encephalopathy, not a drug for chronic use. What transfers is that even the best-organised paediatric epinephrine protocol is built around a short-term resuscitation surrogate with no predefined stopping rules, and that neonates are an included class without a stratified plan.
