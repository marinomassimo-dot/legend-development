## 2 · What the paper is

Exome sequencing of mouse tissues and cell lines from two common-fragile-site knockouts —
`Fhit` (FRA3B) and `Wwox` (FRA16D) — with COSMIC single-base-substitution signature decomposition,
asking whether fragile-gene loss is the endogenous process behind the "clock-like" signatures SBS5
and SBS40.

**The group.** Measured `2026-09-09`: **Huebner K — 408 publications, 131 with FHIT, 32 with
WWOX.** This is a FHIT laboratory of thirty years' standing for which WWOX is the adjacent second
fragile site. Aqeilan RI (137 publications, 65 on WWOX, 14 on epilepsy/encephalopathy) contributed
one tissue set; Aldaz CM (38 on WWOX) contributed the P47T kidneys. **Primary for the gene is not
primary for the disease, and this group is primary for neither WWOX nor WWOX-DEE** — no human
subject, no patient, no neurological phenotype, no reference-genotype variant anywhere in the
study. Weighting applied throughout: first-hand weight to the Fhit system, which is theirs;
interpretation weight to every step that carries a Fhit-cell observation across to a WWOX
statement.

**Field density**, measured today: WWOX **707** · WWOX ∧ mutational signature **18** ·
WWOX ∧ "fragile site" **123** · FHIT ∧ mutational signature **11** · SBS40 **18**. A thin corner,
which is why one paper's conclusion carries disproportionate weight in it.

## 3 · What the paper establishes

**`DATO`.** In **Fhit**-knockout mouse kidney and lymphoma tissue, SigProfilerExtractor recovers a
mutation load an order of magnitude above wild-type controls, decomposed into SBS1, SBS5, SBS23,
SBS46 and SBS54 (Figure 2). Measured from the pixels of the extracted image stream, and

**`DATO`.** Under SigProfilerAssignment (Figure 3), **SBS40c is detected in Fhit-knockout

## 4 · What the paper does **not** support — the product of this reading

### 4.1 🔴 SBS40c is never observed in a *Wwox* sample. Not once.

The paper's headline WWOX claim is its closing Results sentence:

> *"We conclude from the mutational signature profiles in cells and tissues that Fhit/Fra3B loss or
> deletion underlies SBS5 and **Wwox/Fra16D loss likely underlies SBS40c** mutation accumulation in
> these mouse knockout tissues and cells."*

**Measured mechanically on Figure 3's pixels:** pixels matching the SBS40c legend swatch
`RGB(43,160,72)` occur in **exactly 11 bar columns spanning x = 1165–1560, entirely inside the
"Fhit samples" axis bracket (x = 1170–1633). Zero such pixels occur anywhere within the "Wwox
samples" bracket (x = 1645–2049).** The tallest bar under the Fhit bracket reaches ≈**831**
mutations; the tallest under the Wwox bracket reaches ≈**42** — a ~20× gap.

The authors say so themselves, twice, and both statements are in the paper:

> *"The Wwox ko cells show very few SBSs, far too few to detect the clock-like SBSs."*
> *"We did not observe SBS40 or SBS40 a,b,c in these Wwox ko or wt samples."*

**The attribution is therefore carried entirely by an argument from co-deletion**, and the authors'
own connective is the hedge: *"suggesting that SBS40c might be due to Wwox loss in the Fhit ko
cells"*. The Discussion is weaker still — *"could have been contributed by Wwox loss, since Fhit
inactivation regularly is associated with Wwox inactivation."*

**The seam is between the Discussion and the Results.** `AUTHOR_INTERPRETATION` — a hedged
attribution resting on co-deletion. What the Results sentence and the abstract's framing then carry
is stronger than what Figure 3 shows. The abstract, to its credit, is the most honest of the three:
*"similar analyses performed with **Fhit ko samples** where Wwox is also frequently lost identified
SBS40c."*

`PREMISE: INFERENZA` — that WWOX is co-lost in these Fhit-knockout cells at a level sufficient to
generate a signature. **It is never measured in this paper**: no WWOX protein blot, no WWOX
transcript, no FRA16D copy number in the Fhit-knockout material. The two empirical sources for the
co-loss premise are refs [10] and [13] (Guler, breast carcinoma), and **both are unread in this
corpus** — queued as `FT-086`.

**The panel shows no separation of mutation burden by *Wwox* genotype.** No sentence in the paper
states this, and the figure legend does not mention it.

### 4.3 🔴 29.4% of the *Wwox* signal is in signatures the authors themselves call artefacts — cited to a placeholder

The legend of that same supplementary figure reads, verbatim:

> *"SBS46 and 54 are known artefactual SBSs **(ref,LBA 2020)**."*

`(ref,LBA 2020)` is an **unresolved citation placeholder** — a note-to-self published in the
deposit. No "LBA 2020" appears in the 36-item reference list. And it is load-bearing: across the 13
Wwox samples, **2877 of 9798 plotted mutations (29.4%) sit in SBS46+SBS54**, and in three samples
the artefactual share exceeds **60%** (KO3 62.1%, KO4 61.2%, KO5 60.8%). In Figure 2 the same two
signatures carry **22.5% + 17.3% = 39.8%** of the total by the panel's own printed percentages.

So the sole support for discounting two-fifths of Figure 2 and three-tenths of Figure S1 is a
citation that was never filled in.

### 4.4 The measurement is algorithm-dependent, and the paper says so without drawing the consequence

SBS40c appears **only** under SigProfilerAssignment (Figure 3). Under SigProfilerExtractor on the
same material (Figure S1) the recovered signatures are SBS1/5/23/46/54 and *"We did not observe
SBS40 or SBS40 a,b,c."* Two algorithms on one dataset return different signature sets, and the
paper's WWOX conclusion exists in exactly one of them. `INFERENZA`: an attribution that does not
survive a change of decomposition method is a property of the method until shown otherwise.

### 4.5 A reference-version inconsistency sits on top of that

Methods: *"using the COSMIC mutational signatures data files **v3.3** for genome mm10"*.
Deposited Table S1: *"Cosine similarities of Cosmic SBSs version **3.4** mm10."*

`DATO` — the two declarations disagree, established from the deposit alone.
🔴 `PREMISE: DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK` — my background knowledge that the SBS40 → SBS40a/b/c split was
introduced *at* COSMIC v3.4, which would make v3.3 incapable of producing the paper's central
entity. **That is a research target, not a foundation**; the internal inconsistency stands
regardless of it, and the version actually used for Figure 3 is not recoverable from the paper.

### 4.6 The wild-type comparison is partly circular

Methods: *"Additionally, variants that were present in any of our WT mice were removed from all
samples prior to mutational signature analysis."*

The wild-type group is depleted **by construction** before being used as the contrast against which
the knockout groups are read. This does not touch the *within-Wwox* ordering of §4.2 — all those
samples went through the same filter — but it does qualify the KO-versus-WT height difference in
Figure 2, which is the study's headline visual.

### 4.7 The abstract's per-sample counts are not in the figure

The abstract states *"~1000 SBS1 and ~4000 SBS5 mutations per sample"*. Measured from Figure 2 by
two independent routes, the per-sample means are **≈491–505 SBS1** and **≈1297–1315 SBS5**; the
maxima across all 13 knockout samples are **636** and **1736**. The figure's *total* per-sample
burden across all five signatures — **≈3534, max 4666** — is what approaches "~4000".

## 7 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype

**`ESPANSIONE` · T3 · clinical relevance LOW.**

The reference genotype is a WWOX-DEE genotype class. This study contains no human subject, no
patient, no seizure, no developmental endpoint, no reference-genotype variant, no therapeutic agent
and no biomarker proposal. Its only neural material — Wwox-null mouse cortex at P18 — is the one
place the question could have been asked, and it is precisely where the study is silent: the
cortex knockouts contribute ≤42 mutations per sample, carry no clock-like signature, and are never
compared against the four wild-type cortex samples the same table declares.

**What may legitimately be carried forward is a negative, and it is worth carrying.**
`INFERENZA`: on the evidence in this paper, WWOX loss in mouse cortex at P18 does **not** produce a
detectable clock-like mutational-signature phenotype. That is useful precisely because it closes a
door — a somatic-hypermutation account of WWOX-DEE neuropathology gains no support here, and the
corpus previously had **zero** readings on this axis (0 of 43 dossiers, 0 of 70 manifests mention a
mutational signature).

**What may not be carried forward** is the paper's own title-level claim. *"WWOX loss underlies
SBS40c"* is `IPOTESI` — an attribution made in Fhit-knockout cells on an unmeasured co-deletion
premise, absent from every Wwox sample, present under one decomposition algorithm and not the
other, and contradicted in direction by the only panel that compares Wwox genotypes.

**`REVIVAL_TRIGGER`.** Any of the following reopens this: (a) SBS40c detected in a *Wwox*-null
sample with adequate mutation depth, under either algorithm; (b) WWOX protein or FRA16D copy number
actually measured in the Fhit-knockout cells, establishing the co-deletion premise as `DATO`;
(c) deeper sequencing of Wwox-null cortex — whole-genome rather than exome — reaching the burden
needed to test the signature question in neural tissue; (d) a resolved citation for the SBS46/SBS54
artefact claim, which would change how much of the plotted signal is real.
