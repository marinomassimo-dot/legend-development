## What the pass added

1. The supplement quantifies the only human protein-level test: TUBA1A immunofluorescence in cortex of one 21-week affected fetus against one age-matched control, 3 regions of interest each, about 450 against about 740 (arbitrary units), p = 0.5714 (Mann-Whitney). The regions are subsamples of one specimen per side, so n is 1 versus 1; the result is no significant difference, which the main text compresses to a half-sentence (earlier locator 13) and which leaves the paper's central molecular claim (reduced tubulin and transport genes) untested at protein level in the human.
2. Supplementary Figure S1 legend: vermis hypoplasia in panel A, "no macroscopic abnormalities or malformations in the brain" in panel B; the wording does not say whether vermis hypoplasia counts.
3. Figure 1G places different substitutions at one residue (S304P here; Ser304Phe in the 2023 cohort supplement; Ser304Tyr in a 2024 case report) without the text referencing the label.
4. Figure 3 (read on the bucket JPEG) agrees with the earlier panel readings: no change in cortical wall thickness, Satb2 and Tbr1 densities mostly unchanged, BrdU birth-dating shifted across eight of ten zones in n = 3 rats per genotype.

## Does not support

A migration mechanism established in human WWOX-DEE brain; any metabolic or autonomic inference. No metabolic parameter is measured. No commit candidate. Revival triggers: more than one fetal specimen; protein-level tubulin quantification at animal or specimen level.
