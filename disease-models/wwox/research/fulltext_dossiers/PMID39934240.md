## What it is

A pilot case-control study of serum from 46 adults with drug-resistant epilepsy (pre-operative surgical candidates, long-standing disease) and 45 healthy outpatient controls: 13 complement analytes, a 27-plex cytokine panel (15 or 16 analytes evaluable), correlations among them and with age and full-scale IQ. WWOX: zero occurrences.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** Adults, acquired or structural focal epilepsies (mesial temporal sclerosis, focal cortical dysplasia, idiopathic), no child, no genetic DEE.

## What it measures (DATO)

Lower serum C1q, C4, C4b, Factor B, Factor H (about 30 percent) and lower C3, C3b/iC3b and C5 in DRE; MBL, C2, C3a, C5a and C5b-9 unchanged; IL-8 lower, CCL2 and CCL5 slightly higher; bFGF detected only in patients; correlations among analytes differ between groups; some analytes correlate with FSIQ in subsets.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No cause or direction.** The authors state the lower complement could be cause, consequence or effect of antiseizure drugs; patients were on medication, healthy controls were not.
2. **Serum complement is lower in this adult cohort, while the complement literature on brain tissue (including the review and the mouse study read earlier in this wave) describes local up-regulation.** The two are not contradictory (blood versus tissue) but the paper shows peripheral decrease cannot be read as a surrogate of brain complement activity. INFERENZA.
3. **"Sex-specific" complement dysregulation is not supported by an interaction test.** Supplementary Table 2 gives condition-by-sex interaction p-values of 0.25 or more for every complement analyte (C3b/iC3b 0.498, C1q 0.340); the sex difference rests on one analyte being significant in males and not in females (difference between significant and non-significant). Only TNF-alpha has a significant interaction.
4. **Correlation "disruption" may be range restriction or small-n noise.** In DRE males the C3b/iC3b range is much narrower (about 250-600 against about 100-2300 in healthy males; Figure 3G, H) and groups are 18-24 per sex; a lost correlation is not evidence of pathway failure. The text also contradicts Figure 5P: both sexes are said to show a DRE C3b/iC3b-CCL2 correlation while the male panel is r = 0.45, p = 0.094.
5. **Multiplicity and units.** Welch t-tests across 29 analytes without family-wise correction for the main comparison; FSIQ correlations in 12 and 16 patients with p between 0.007 and 0.039 among dozens of tests; figure cross-references wrong in places (the text cites Figure 5H for DRE females, Figure 3 for cytokine panels that are in Figure 4); bFGF "elevated" is a detectability difference (below assay limit in healthy samples).
6. **No plasma-product relevance.** Nothing about infusing or removing circulating complement, immunoglobulin or albumin. The paper only says what circulating complement looks like in adult DRE.

## Design and limits

Pilot, single draw, adults on medication versus unmedicated outpatient controls, no timing relative to seizures, assay-sensitivity limits, no correction, no adult-versus-child analysis.
