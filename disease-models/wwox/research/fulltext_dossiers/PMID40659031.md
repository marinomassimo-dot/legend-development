## 1 · What the paper is

A computational study: an ODE model (159 reactions) of one excitatory tripartite synapse, extended with ketone chemistry, run for normal glucose (5 mM) and ketone (0.25 mM), low glucose (3.5 mM) and ketogenic ketone (2 mM), and five single-rate-constant defects (GLUT1, pyruvate dehydrogenase complex, MCT1, complex I). No animal, cell, patient or WWOX data; WWOX does not occur in the text.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. The model's pathological state (Regime E, short bursts) appears when presynaptic ATP generation falls below about 1.17 x 10^6 molecules per second; baseline is about 1.34. At normal ketone the switch occurs below 3.8 mM glucose, with 2 mM ketone below 3.1 mM; the glucose-ketone boundary is a fitted line (R squared 0.99) through a handful of points.
2. Ketone rescues low glucose, GLUT1 deficiency (more ketone for deeper defects) and PDH complex deficiency (needs 4 mM; 2 mM fails). It does not rescue MCT1 deficiency (worsens it) nor complex I at 55 percent (up to 10 mM).
3. The paper's own check against the clinic is mixed: GLUT1 and PDH agree; one MCT1 case report agrees; for complex I the clinical series it cites (eight patients, all improved) disagrees, and the explanation (biogenesis, expression changes over days to weeks) lies outside the model.
4. Competing mechanisms are discussed at length (HDAC, GABA/glutamate, inflammation, astrocytes, circadian, microbiota) and none is modelled. The conclusion is hedged ("may be", "do not exclude").
5. Stated limits: reduced-model parameters are not physiological, glucose and ketone are fixed boundary conditions, the astrocyte-neuron lactate shuttle is assumed.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- Selection question 3 asked whether energy repletion is a better rationale than Warburg reversal. The paper cannot answer it: a model containing only an energy mechanism can show that energy repletion is sufficient to remove the modelled defect, and cannot rank it against mechanisms it omits.
- The model does give a map of where an energy rationale would and would not apply: upstream of acetyl-CoA (glucose supply, GLUT1, PDH) yes; at transport of ketone itself (MCT1) or at complex I no. For WWOX loss the measured lesion in fibroblasts is a lactate rise with PDH and citrate synthase unchanged (audit), i.e. neither a PDH nor a complex I defect; the paper has nothing to say about a glycolytic-shift lesion except that supplying acetyl-CoA from ketone bypasses upstream limits.
- Hypoglycaemia is the model's cleanest trigger (glucose below 3.8 mM). The Wwox-null mouse is hypoglycaemic; this paper gives a mechanistic reading of how low glucose alone could produce burst firing in silico (IPOTESI, never tested for Wwox-null brain).

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- That energy repletion is the primary mechanism (not compared with any other).
- Any quantitative threshold for real tissue (authors' own caveat).
- Anything about WWOX, WOREE or any measured brain.
- Its complex I prediction (contradicted by clinical data it cites).
