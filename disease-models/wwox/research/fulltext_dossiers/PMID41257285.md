## 1 · What the paper did

A retrospective RNA-seq analysis across tissues from **40 female cynomolgus macaques**, aggregated
from two non-GLP toxicology studies, **12 to 50 months old at dosing**, pre-selected for the
**lowest** anti-AAV9 antibody titres. Route: intrathecal or intracisterna magna. Duration: 28 days.
Tissues sequenced: DRG, liver, heart, skeletal muscle, spleen.

The design is what makes this paper load-bearing — it contains the arms the other NHP sources lack:

| Study | Arms | Dose |
|---|---|---|
| A (n = 24) | vehicle; scAAV9-CB-GFP low; scAAV9-CB-GFP high | 1.0 × 10¹³ and 3.0 × 10¹³ vg/NHP |
| B (n = 16) | vehicle; **empty capsid**; **promoterless** scAAV9-CB-GFP (scrambled promoter); full scAAV9-CB-GFP | 2.73 × 10¹³ capsids/NHP, all four arms |

So this study varies **dose** (study A) *and* **what the capsid carries** (study B) — capsid alone,
capsid + genome without a working promoter, capsid + genome + expression.

## 2 · The central result: toxicity requires a transcriptionally productive genome, not capsid load

> "Hepatic and DRG toxicities were only detected after administration of full AAV9 viral particles,
> but not empty capsids or Promoterless test articles."
> — Results, "In-life summary and organ toxicities…"

Empty capsid at 2.73 × 10¹³ produced neither liver nor DRG toxicity. A full genome without a
working promoter produced neither. Only the expressing vector did. **Capsid burden alone, at a dose
comparable to the toxic one, is not sufficient for the organ toxicity that halted a human trial.**

The transcriptional data follow the same shape: differentially expressed genes were far fewer with
empty capsid or promoterless vector, though both remained distinguishable from vehicle.

## 3 · But the magnitude of expression does not predict which tissue is injured

The paper reports a dissociation it is careful about:

- vector genome biodistribution was **highest in liver and DRG** — the two injured tissues;
- transgene transcript was **highest in heart and skeletal muscle** — tissues with no consistent
  pathology;
- "the amount of transgene product itself is not directly predictive of the observed toxicities
  (even when expressing a foreign protein such as GFP)";
- the authors' own reading: "the amount of vector DNA entering the cells may be damaging and a key
  trigger of cellular stress rather than the transgene product itself, even if the expression of the
  transgene is also necessary to the observed changes".

**This matters because PMID 42134074 (C6) asserts the opposite emphasis** — that DRG toxicity is
"mostly related to supraphysiological gene expression levels with strong ubiquitous promoters". The
two sources agree that a promoter is *necessary*; they disagree on whether expression *level* is the
graded driver. At tissue level, this paper's data say it is not. That tension is unresolved and is
recorded as such, not harmonised.

## 4 · The patient-measurable item: NfL

> "Single-cell necrosis and neuroinflammatory changes were also observed in DRGs, which correlated
> with elevated levels of NfL that peaked on day 28."
> — Results, "In-life summary and organ toxicities…"

A **blood-measurable** marker tracking histological DRG injury that is otherwise only visible at
necropsy. This is the most directly useful item in the group for the "measurable in a patient"
clause of the question.

**Reading-debt flag:** this sentence, the histopathology, the bilirubin/liver-enzyme elevations, the
transient whole-body tremor at high dose, and the biodistribution are all cited to reference 10 —
the authors' own prior report. **They are not measured in this paper.** This paper measures
transcriptomes. The NfL–DRG correlation is a restatement, and the primary behind it has not been
read by LEGEND.

## 5 · What the transcriptomes show

- Most differentially expressed genes were **up**regulated; downregulated genes were few and shared
  almost nothing across tissues (no downregulated gene shared between DRG and liver at all).
- DRG and liver signatures were dominated by innate and adaptive immune programmes: type I and
  type II interferon (CXCL9, CXCL10, CXCL11, CXCR3, IRF family, OAS/OASL, APOBEC), antigen
  presentation (CD74, HLA class II, TAP1/TAP2), monocyte/macrophage recruitment (CCR2, CCL19),
  T- and B-cell activity (CD3, CD8, CCL5, CD40, CD80, CD86, TNFRSF13/13B).
- Spleen and skeletal muscle signatures showed a "clear lack of overlap" with DRG, liver and heart.
- The response was **still present at 4 weeks** — "a persistent low-grade immune response".
- Heart showed immune gene upregulation without consistent pathology.

### The mechanistic consequence the authors draw, which is the most actionable item here

The animals received **no immunosuppression**. The authors note that patients typically receive
glucocorticoids, which are NF-κB inhibitors, but that the pathway they actually identify —
type I/II interferon — signals through **JAK/STAT, not NF-κB**:

> "This could provide some explanation as to why glucocorticoids are not always as potent as
> expected in preventing AAV-driven toxicity."

If correct, the modifiable immune component of AAV9 CSF-route toxicity is **not fully addressable by
steroids**, and the implied target is JAK/STAT. This is the authors' hypothesis, stated as one
("we can hypothesize that IFN signaling is exacerbating the stress…"), and it is recorded here as a
hypothesis.

## 6 · Human numbers this paper carries second-hand

The discussion cites hepatotoxicity frequencies in SMA patients — 21.9% after intrathecal
onasemnogene abeparvovec at 1.2 × 10¹⁴ vg/patient versus 34% after intravenous dosing at
1.2 × 10¹⁴ vg/kg (refs 33, 36). **Cited, not measured here.** Note the units differ between the two
figures (per patient vs per kg), so the two percentages are not a like-for-like dose comparison and
must not be reported as one.

## 7 · What it does NOT state

- Nothing about infants: the youngest animals were 12 months, and age is not analysed as a variable.
- Nothing about males: all 40 animals were female.
- Nothing about seropositive recipients: animals were selected for the lowest titres (the exact
  complement of PMID 41210171's design, and a reason the two cannot simply be pooled).
- No immunosuppressed arm, so the modifiability claim is inference, not measurement.
- No functional neurological endpoint; no recovery timepoint beyond 28 days.
- The authors state transcriptional change "is not injury" only implicitly; the paper does not claim
  the DEGs cause the lesions.

## 8 · Caveats the authors themselves give

- IT delivers lower total vector than IV, so "the transcriptional changes reported in the present
  study are most certainly lower in magnitude than what could have been observed after i.v.
  delivery".
- Kinetics differ by route: liver enzyme elevation within the first week after IV but only ~2 weeks
  after IT; hepatotoxicity more pronounced after IV, while "more severe histopathological changes
  are commonly observed after IT dosing".
- Clinical cardiotoxicity cases they cite arose in a disease context (DMD) that healthy animals do
  not recapitulate.

## 9 · Transfer limits toward a WWOX-DEE programme

| Datum | Transfers? | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Empty capsid and promoterless vector cause neither liver nor DRG toxicity | **yes — the cleanest discrimination in the group** | one capsid, one species, 28 days, female only, seronegative only; from the authors' prior report for the pathology itself |
| A WWOX restoration cargo is a protein transgene under a promoter | — | so it falls on the **toxic** side of this study's dividing line; the promoterless arm is not a model of it |
| GFP is a foreign protein; WWOX protein is self | partially | a WWOX-null recipient may nonetheless be immunologically naive to WWOX protein, which is the CRIM-negative-analogous situation; this study cannot distinguish foreign-ness from expression |
| NfL as a blood marker of DRG injury | yes, as a candidate | cited not measured here; and see PMID 41948127 for its bidirectionality problem |
| IFN/JAK-STAT rather than NF-κB as the modifiable axis | as a hypothesis | no immunosuppressed arm was run; the inference is from pathway identity, not from an intervention |
| Dose 1 × 10¹³–3 × 10¹³ vg/NHP | as an exposure anchor only | not scaled to brain mass or CSF volume; see PMID 42205472 on why a per-animal total does not convert |
| Any of this as a WWOX biomarker | **no** | §13: interferon transcripts, NfL and liver enzymes measure vector–host interaction or generic axonal injury, not WWOX functional state |
