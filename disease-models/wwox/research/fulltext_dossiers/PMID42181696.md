## What the paper did

Designed 18 new AntagoNAT sequences against the Scn1a natural antisense transcript, embedded in
miR-155 scaffolds; screened them in differentiated N2a cells; compared a CMV (pol II) against a U6
(pol III) promoter; packaged the two best, H and K, into AAV9 with a CMV-GFP reporter; dosed newborn
Scn1a⁺/⁻ mice and WT littermates by bilateral ICV alone (dose A 1 × 10¹¹ vg, dose B 1 × 10¹⁰ vg) or by
combined ICV + IV (dose C, 3.5 × 10¹⁰ vg total); and separately dosed P14 mice (dose D 3.5 × 10¹⁰ vg,
dose E 1 × 10¹⁰ vg). Blinded and randomised. Readouts: survival to P100, weight, open field,
hyperthermia-induced seizures at P20, 15-day EEG (P30–45), cortical and cardiac Scn1a mRNA by qPCR,
vector copy number, NaV1.1 by capillary immunoassay, GFAP/CD68 immunohistochemistry.

## What it measured — and this is the paper that actually answers "how much protein"

**Efficacy, neonatal dose C (ICV + IV):** survival 50 % → 84.2 % (p = 0.04); hyperthermia-induced
seizures 11/15 (PBS) → 2/13 (treated), p < 0.001, with shorter duration and lower Racine score;
spontaneous seizures 1.12 → 0.03 per mouse per day (p = 0.025); seizure duration 25.14 s → 2.56 s.

**The molecular readout under that rescue (Figure 4, panels inspected):**
- Cortical Scn1a mRNA: WT ≈ 1.00, untreated HET ≈ 0.85, treated HET ≈ 0.93 (p = 0.032 vs untreated).
  The whole significant effect is a shift of roughly **8 % of the wild-type level**.
- Cortical NaV1.1 protein: WT ≈ 1.00, untreated HET ≈ 0.45, treated HET ≈ 0.49, **no significant
  difference and no bracket drawn**. Verbatim: "NaV1.1 expression in the cerebral cortex for
  AAV9-AntagoNAT-H Scn1a⁺/⁻ group (0.49 [0.16]) was not significant when compared to PBS control
  group (0.44 [0.08])".
- The same holds for the P14 cohort: Scn1a mRNA up, NaV1.1 unchanged.

So a large, blinded, multi-endpoint phenotypic rescue was obtained **without a measurable increase in
the protein the therapy exists to raise**. The authors give two readings and keep both: the NaV1.1
antibody is unreliable, and "normalization of NaV1.1 expression to wild-type levels may not be
entirely required to have a therapeutic benefit".

**Dose, and the sharpest safety datum in the group:** the dose–response is **non-monotonic and
inverted at the top**. Neonatal ICV-only at 1 × 10¹¹ vg gave 12.5 % survival for AntagoNAT-H
(PBS 50 %) with an open-field hyperactivity phenotype, while the lower combined dose rescued. At P14,
3.5 × 10¹⁰ vg gave 14 % survival against 60 % for PBS, with astrocyte, microglial and hepatic
macrophage activation; dropping to 1 × 10¹⁰ vg gave 87.5 %. The authors' own conclusion: "By
decreasing the total dose from 3.5 × 10¹⁰ vg to 1 × 10¹⁰ vg/mouse, we showed that this treatment has a
narrow therapeutic range", and they hypothesise that excessive antisense activity inverts the
regulatory balance and suppresses Scn1a.

## What it does **not** say

- It does not show a NaV1.1 protein increase anywhere, in brain or heart, at any dose or age.
  The conclusion sentence "modestly increasing NaV1.1 production" is **stronger than the Results**,
  which report no significant change; the Discussion states the null plainly. Quote the Results.
- It does not establish the heart arm: cardiac Scn1a did not reach significance and NaV1.1 was
  undetectable in heart.
- It did not reproduce febrile seizures at all in the P14 cohort (neither arm), so that experiment is
  uninformative; the authors attribute this to timing or genetic drift.
- Vector genomes fell markedly between P20 and P100; the GFP signal is confounded by CMV promoter
  silencing, and the authors propose removing the reporter entirely.
- Internal labelling inconsistency: the Figure 4 panel key reads "Dose B – ICV+IV" where the body and
  the caption call the combined arm dose C.

## Transfer limit to WWOX-DEE (stated exactly)

**The target class is the question.** The whole approach requires that a repressive natural antisense
transcript exists at the locus. The paper's evidence that this generalises is a single cited study
showing the SCN1A NAT is abundantly expressed in human frontal and temporal lobe. **Whether a
repressive NAT exists at WWOX is unestablished and is the first thing to check** before any of this is
imported; nothing in this paper bears on it.
**What transfers if, and only if, such a NAT exists:** the construction (AntagoNAT in a miR-155
scaffold, U6 pol III promoter outperforming CMV, AAV9, one-time dosing instead of repeated intrathecal
administration), and the dual ICV + IV route when a peripheral organ is also implicated.
**What transfers unconditionally, as a warning:** (i) a dosage-stabilised gene punishes
overshoot — the highest dose was the worst outcome in both age cohorts; (ii) mRNA and protein came
apart, so an mRNA endpoint is not a pharmacodynamic endpoint; (iii) a phenotypic rescue can be real
while its proposed molecular intermediate is undetectable, which is exactly the situation in which a
sensitive protein assay (see PMID 42521212) would have decided the question.
**What does not transfer:** haploinsufficiency arithmetic. Scn1a⁺/⁻ has one good allele to raise.
A WWOX-DEE biallelic null has none, and upregulating a transcript from an allele that makes no
functional protein raises nothing. This approach is relevant to WWOX only for a **leaky** allele with
residual correct product — e.g. a splice allele — and that limitation should be stated wherever the
lead is carried.
