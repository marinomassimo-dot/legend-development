## 2 · What this paper establishes

**DATO — Wwox binds mouse Brca1, and not where it binds human Brca1.** The `981PPLF984` motif that
human Wwox uses is *«conserved in primates but not in rodent species»* while the Chk2 site at S971
is conserved (Fig 1A). Myc-Wwox nonetheless co-precipitates mouse Brca1 in both phosphorylation
states (Fig 1B), and **exon 11 is dispensable** in mouse (Fig 1C) — the region that carries the human
site. This is a real species difference, established rather than assumed, and it is the paper's
reason to exist.

**DATO, and the best-controlled result here — the interaction requires the BRCT tandem domain, shown
with two independent mutants and a bridging control.** A cancer-associated BRCT point mutant
(M1717R, equivalent to human M1775R) abolishes co-IP (Fig 1D); a second, independent BRCT mutant
(S1655F) abolishes the in-situ interaction by PLA (Suppl Fig S1); and in **ABC triple-mutant** cells,
where none of Abraxas, Brip1 or CtIP can bind Brca1 at all, Wwox **still** binds the BRCT domain
(Fig 1E) — which excludes the obvious alternative that Wwox is bridged to Brca1 by one of those three.
Supplementary Figure S1 also carries the right negative control: PLA foci fall from ~12.3 per cell in
`Brca1+/+` to ~2.8 in the BRCT mutant and ~2.1 in `Wwox−/−` cells, with the mutant and the Wwox-null
**not different from each other** (`ns`) — i.e. the mutation reduces the signal to the floor set by
having no Wwox at all.

**DATO — a second, Brca1-independent layer: Wwox binds Abraxas, Brip1 and CtIP directly.** Abraxas
co-IPs with Wwox and irradiation does not change it (Fig 2A); Brip1 binds Wwox (Fig 2B); CtIP binds
Wwox (Fig 2C); and both persist when Brca1 is silenced (Fig 2D) or destabilised in `Bard1−/−;53BP1−/−`
cells (Fig 2E).

**DATO — the timing result, which is the paper's title claim.** After 10 γ IR, Wwox-KO MEFs show more
Abraxas foci at every timepoint to 4 h, more Brca1 foci at 0 and 1 h, and more CtIP foci at 0.5 h;
Abraxas–Brca1 co-localisation peaks at 0.5 h in Wwox-silenced cells against 1 h in controls (Fig 3).
The effect is a **shift in timing, not a monotone increase** — and the paper marks that honestly: the
Brca1 series is N.S. at 2 h and **reverses at 4 h**, with wild-type higher (`*`), and the CtIP
advantage is N.S. by 1 h.

**🔴 DATO — the direct resection measurement, which is exactly what paper 1 of this lot lacked.** A
SMART assay measures resected ssDNA on single DNA fibres: at 1 h after IR the distributions are
bimodal and fully separated, wild-type fibres peaking at 1–2 µm (~50% frequency) and both knockout
clones at 5–10 µm (~47%) (Fig 4A). The in-panel legend names **WT3, WT7, KO3, KO5**, matching Methods
§4.7. Corroborated by pRPA-S4/S8 foci at 0.5 h (`**`, Fig 4B) and pRPA recruitment to chromatin
(Fig 4C). *This converts the 2017 paper's inference into a measurement.*

**DATO — an abundance mechanism the 2017 model did not contain.** Wwox silencing raises the
**steady-state** levels of CtIP, Rad50, Nbs1 and Mre11, reproduced in three different mouse cell
types — immortalised MEFs, 31-03 mammary tumour and KPC-2 pancreatic tumour cells (Fig 4D). This is
not a timing mechanism at all: it says the resection machinery is pre-loaded before any damage. The
authors flag it themselves as *«Unexpectedly»*.

**DATO — mutagenesis, measured.** HPRT mutant frequency after IR is ~3.5-fold higher in KO MEFs
(`**`, Fig 4G). Paper 1 argued a higher mutational burden and measured none; this measures one.

**DATO — a selective therapeutic lever.** A Chk2 inhibitor (BML-277) or Chk2 silencing collapses the
survival advantage of Wwox-KO MEFs under IR and carboplatin, while leaving wild-type survival
unchanged (Fig 5) — synthetic lethality with the direction and the selectivity control both present.

## 3 · What this paper does NOT support — the negative is the product

**The binding site is not identified.** *«We did not find a known Wwox interaction motif in the
murine or human Brca1-BRCT domain»*. So the interaction is mapped to a **domain** by mutation, and
the mutants used (M1717R, S1655F) are known to disrupt the folded tBRCT structure — which the authors
concede: *«it does not seem surprising that binding of a protein such as Wwox (46 kD) to a region
within this tandem repeat domain might also interfere with the BRCT domain folded structure»*. Loss
of binding to a structurally disrupted domain does not localise a site, and no direct-binding
experiment (recombinant protein, ITC, peptide array, structure) appears anywhere. **`INFERENZA`, not
`DATO`, that Wwox binds the BRCT groove itself.**

**The causal step from binding to timing is never tested.** The model requires that Wwox *occupancy*
delays A/B/C complex formation. What exists is a correlation between Wwox absence and earlier foci.
The experiment that would close it is named by the authors as **not done**: *«we hypothesize that
generation of murine cells expressing mutations or deletions in the potential Wwox-binding motifs
would lead to the phenotypes observed in this study»*. There is no separation-of-function Wwox allele
here — every Wwox manipulation is loss of the whole protein (KO or siRNA), so nothing distinguishes
"Wwox blocks the BRCT domain" from any other consequence of losing a 46 kDa multifunctional protein.

**Two mechanisms are offered and never disentangled.** Wwox loss both (a) accelerates recruitment
timing and (b) raises the steady-state abundance of MRN/CtIP. Either could produce faster resection
on its own; the paper measures both and tests neither against the other. The Discussion states the
abundance route as the likelier one — *«The increased MRN complex level may preset the cells»* — while
the title and Figure 6 sell the timing route. **Recorded as an unresolved ambiguity, not as a
hierarchy.**

**Counter-evidence the paper itself raises and does not resolve.** *«Reczek et al. have shown that
ablation of Brca1-CtIP interaction did not affect pRPA foci formation after IR in murine cells»* —
i.e. an independent group found the Brca1–CtIP axis dispensable for exactly the readout this paper
uses. The authors answer it with a hypothesis (Brip1-mediated, Brca1-independent recruitment), not
with an experiment. To their credit, they print the objection.

**Figure 4F contradicts the text on basal Rad51.** The Results assert *«more Rad 51 foci in the KO
vs. WT cells»* without IR, and draw the inference *«suggesting that the Wwox absence affects basal
activity of the HR repair pathway»*. Figure 4F marks that 0 h comparison **N.S.** (~10% vs ~13% of
cells with >10 foci). The panel refuses the premise of the inference. Declared in the manifest as
`text_contradicted_by_panel` on that sentence.

**The reporter effect replicates in direction but not in size.** Figure 4E's two independent DR-GFP
clones give ~7% → ~20.5% (`***`) and ~8.5% → ~11% (`**`) — roughly 3-fold against 1.3-fold. A
clone-level magnitude spread of that width is not reconcilable with a single effect size, and the
text reports only the direction.

**The transient results are reported as if sustained, in two places.** Supplementary Figure S2's
CtIP-PLA advantage exists at 0.5 h (`*`), is N.S. at 0 h, and **reverses at 1 h** with wild-type
higher — under the title *«CtIP recruitment to DSBs occurs more dramatically in Wwox KO MEFs»*.
Supplementary Figure S3's Rad51 silencing effect is significant at 0.5 and 1 h and **N.S. again by
2 h**. Both are early-window differences.

**The therapeutic figure carries no statistics at all.** Figure 5A and 5B show four series each with
error bars and **not one asterisk, bracket or P value**, and the legend states none. This is not an
artefact of my reading: the vector-text extraction returns 0 significance marks for page 9 against 16
for the Figure 3 page. The paper's most translational claim — selective resensitisation, the basis of
*«an alternative therapy to selectively target the high percentage of Wwox negative, treatment
resistant tumors»* — rests on curve separation alone. The **selectivity** half (no effect in WT) is
the better-supported half, because it is a null and the curves are superimposed.

**Figure 4G's endpoint is narrower than the sentence.** The panel shows only the 0 and 0.5 γ arms
while Methods specify 0, 0.5 and 1 γ for the mutation assay, and the y-axis is *«Relative survival in
6-TG medium»* normalised to untreated cells — a surrogate for mutant frequency, not a frequency per
viable cell.

**Statistics, across the paper.** Unpaired two-tailed *t*-tests throughout (one-way ANOVA only in
Suppl Fig S1), S.E.M error bars, *«at least three replicates»*, foci counted **manually**, and **no
correction for multiplicity anywhere** despite four-timepoint comparisons repeated over six proteins.
Figure 3's own marks include seven `N.S.` out of sixteen, which is at least visible.

**Nothing here is a human experiment.** Every result is mouse. The human claims in the Introduction
and Discussion — resistance in human cells, patient survival in ovarian and lung cancer, earlier
resection in human cancer cells — are **all citations**, to refs 6, 7 and 10, and ref 10 is unread in
this corpus (§6).

## 4 · An expectation of mine that the artefact refuted

Having read the 2017 paper first, I expected its hedging to recur — that model figure prints a
question mark beside the load-bearing arrow. **Figure 6 here carries no interrogative mark on any
arrow.** Every step, including the mutagenicity endpoint, is drawn as an assertion: *«The pre-formed
MRN-CtIP complex is rapidly recruited to DSBs by Brip1 and initiates resection immediately, which
inhibits NHEJ and promotes HR/SSA repair. These repairs are mutagenic causing IR/Platinum
resistance»*. So the 2022 model is drawn **more** confidently than the 2017 one while resting on a
binding-site attribution that is weaker (a domain by disruptive mutation, versus a motif by deletion
mapping). Recorded because I went looking for the opposite.

## 6 · Materials, and what they mean for independence

**The MEF clones are the same material as PMID 27869163, and this paper says so.** Methods §4.1:
the WT and KO MEFs *«(WT3, WT4, WT7, KO3, and KO5) were isolated from individual mouse embryos as
described before [7]»*, and reference 7 **is** PMID 27869163 (verified from the artefact's own
`ext-link` PMID, not from the citation text). WT3 is the one clone that does not appear in the 2017
paper. Which clones appear where is itself uneven: Fig 4A/SMART uses WT3+WT7, the foci panels use
WT3+WT4, Figure 5B's inset uses WT4+WT7 — all against KO3+KO5.

**Everything else is externally sourced, and that strengthens the paper.** Every mutant line —
`Brca1Δex11`, `Brca1M1717R`, `Brca1S1598F`, `Brca1FHWT`, `Brip1FHWT`, the ABC triple phospho-site
mutant, `Bard1−/−;53BP1−/−`, the `PimDRGFP` reporter, the 31-03 mammary and KPC-2 pancreatic tumour
lines — was *«obtained from the laboratory of Dr. Thomas Ludwig»*, and the Brca1 and Abraxas antisera
come from that laboratory while the CtIP monoclonal comes from R. Baer's. The Wwox antibody is the
group's own. So the BRCT-domain genetics carries reagents from laboratories with no stake in WWOX,
which is the strongest structural feature of the evidence here. The reciprocal weakness: those
reagents' characterisation lives in seven papers this corpus has never read (`FT-176`).

## 7 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype

**Absent, and marginally weaker than paper 1's.**

Every system is a mouse embryonic fibroblast or a mouse tumour line; every endpoint is molecular or
clonogenic; the interventions are γ-irradiation, carboplatin, a Chk2 inhibitor and siRNA. There is no
neural cell, no developmental stage, no post-natal timepoint, no behaviour, and **no WWOX-DEE allele**.
Where paper 1 at least touched human tissue (a brain-cancer database, weakly), this paper is
exclusively mouse and exclusively cancer-directed in its framing — its stated motivation is
*«preclinical cancer treatment research»*.

Typed honestly:
- **`INFERENZA` (gene-level):** WWOX constitutively restrains BRCA1-BRCT complex assembly and the
  onset of end resection, and its loss pre-loads the MRN–CtIP machinery. `PREMISE: DATO` for the
  binding and the resection kinetics; `PREMISE: INFERENZA` for the occupancy mechanism.
- 🔴 **A species boundary that runs the wrong way for transfer, and it is this paper's own finding.**
  The human WWOX–BRCA1 interaction is reported to work through `981PPLF984` in exon 11; the mouse one
  does not use that motif at all and works through the BRCT domain. So the two species use **different
  binding surfaces for the same partner**. Any inference that carries a mouse WWOX-repair result into
  human genotype reasoning must now cross a demonstrated species difference in the interaction itself
  — which makes mouse WWOX-repair biology *less* transferable to a human genotype than it looked
  before this paper, not more. This is the sharpest cross-document consequence of the lot.
- **`ESPANSIONE`:** replication/repair stress as a contributor to a WWOX neurodevelopmental
  phenotype. Post-mitotic neurons do not perform appreciable HR, and every effect here is measured in
  dividing cells. To be tested, never assumed.
- **Explicitly NOT transferable:** Chk2 inhibition. It is synthetic lethality aimed at killing
  Wwox-deficient **tumour** cells; carrying a strategy whose mechanism of benefit is selective cell
  death toward a developmental encephalopathy would invert its purpose. `BLOCCO 1` is not engaged
  because no clinical proposition toward the reference genotype is made here.

**Revival triggers:** a WWOX-DEE patient allele tested for BRCA1-BRCT binding; any resection or
repair-pathway measurement in WWOX-deficient neurons, NPCs or organoids; or a separation-of-function
Wwox allele that uncouples the BRCT interaction from the rest of WWOX's functions — the experiment
this paper names as undone.
