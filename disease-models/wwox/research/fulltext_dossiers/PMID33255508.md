# Full-text dossier — PMID 33255508

**Study:** Aldaz CM, Hussain T. *WWOX Loss of Function in Neurodevelopmental and
Neurodegenerative Disorders*. Int J Mol Sci. 2020;21:8922. DOI
`10.3390/ijms21238922`; PMCID `PMC7727818`.

**Reading status:** complete full-text read on 2026-08-06. The PMC XML was read
sequentially; all nine figure images were inspected; both supplementary workbooks were
read sheet-by-sheet. The paper is a narrative review with re-analysis/visualisation of
public expression resources, not a new disease-mechanism experiment.

## Verdict

The review is a useful 2020 map of WWOX neurology, but it must not be used as primary
proof for its mechanistic conclusions. Its strongest contribution is the explicit assembly
of three observations: WWOX expression in oligodendrocyte progenitors, WWOX links to lipid
homeostasis, and WWOX interactions with endomembrane-trafficking proteins. The proposed
bridge from those observations to myelination is labelled by the authors themselves as a
suggestion: it remains an `INFERENZA`, not a demonstrated causal chain.

It adds no new canonical claim. Existing primary evidence in the repository is stronger for
GABAergic loss/glial activation, developmental hypomyelination and trafficking. The correct
landing is therefore a qualification in the discovery layer plus explicit reading debt for
the primary lipid and rat-myelin papers.

## What the source supports

- `DATO (public-dataset description)`: the review's BrainRNAseq analysis reports the highest
  oligodendrocyte-lineage Wwox expression in progenitors and lower expression in mature
  myelinating oligodendrocytes. This is expression, not necessity or cell autonomy.
- `DATO (reviewed primary finding)`: the review accurately attributes reduced hippocampal
  GABAergic interneurons and GAD65/67 to Hussain et al. 2019 (PMID 30290271). The primary
  paper, not this review, must carry the claim.
- `DATO (case aggregation as of 2020)`: p.Gln230Pro appears in eight WOREE cases across six
  families in the review's aggregation. The same paragraph states that mechanisms of most
  missense variants were unknown. Frequency does not establish molecular mechanism.
- `INFERENZA`: lipid composition plus trafficking biology make a WWOX–myelin link plausible.
  The review does not perturb lipid metabolism or trafficking in neural cells and does not
  show that either mediates WWOX-dependent myelination.
- `RESEARCH GAP`: the authors explicitly ask for conditional CNS models and human-variant
  knock-ins because short-lived constitutive knockouts cannot resolve cell type, timing or
  allele-specific mechanism.

## Figures and supplementary audit

Figures 1–6 are descriptive views of public bulk, regional and single-cell expression data;
the graphics do not show a new perturbation experiment and generally do not expose
inferential statistics on the face of the plots. Figure 7 is a variant/CNV map: it is useful
for topology and recurrence, not functional proof. Figures 8–9 map ASD-associated and
population CNVs across the fragile locus; their visual overlap cannot separate WWOX effects
from broad or multigene CNVs.

Supplementary Table 1 aggregates 69 SCAR12/WOREE cases available to the authors in 2020.
Supplementary Table 2 contains 49 ASD/ID CNV records; many are inherited, broad, multigene
or not independently validated. These tables are census aids, not penetrance estimates and
not causal assays. No individual-level data from them are promoted into the public disease
model.

## Horizontal comparison

- `CLAIM 003/004` and `DL-MECH-010/031` now have stronger primary genetic/rescue evidence
  for a predominantly neuron-driven, non-cell-autonomous myelin phenotype. The review's OPC
  expression pattern does not overturn that result.
- `CLAIM 005` is anchored to the primary GABA/glia study (PMID 30290271); this review is
  secondary corroboration only.
- `CLAIM 026` and `DL-MECH-018` hold the primary HEK293T interactome evidence. The additional
  step `trafficking disruption -> neural myelin defect` remains untested.
- `DL-MECH-012` has later and stronger primary neuroinflammation evidence. The review's LPS
  expression plot is descriptive and cannot establish that WWOX restrains inflammation.

## Transfer and limits

The Aldaz group is a primary WWOX gene laboratory, but this paper is a review and public-data
synthesis. Group expertise increases confidence in field coverage and gene-specific context;
it does not convert a cross-paper mechanistic proposal into an experiment. The 2020 cutoff
also matters: later conditional deletion, rescue and human-organoid work supersedes several
open questions in the review.

No therapeutic recommendation follows from this paper. Any clinical interpretation belongs
with the treating clinical team.
