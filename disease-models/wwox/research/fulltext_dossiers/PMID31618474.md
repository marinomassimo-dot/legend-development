## What the paper did

An international consortium cohort of 135 EIMFS patients (128 families) with exome, targeted or panel testing; per-gene phenotype summaries (Table 1), variant tables for nine "novel" genes (Table 2), per-patient phenotypes (Supplementary Table 1) and per-variant literature (Supplementary Table 2).

## What it measured about WWOX

- **One patient (P93)** among 135. Compound heterozygous: the recurrent missense **E17K** (`c.49G>A`) and, on the other allele, **two intronic deletions** found by clinical microarray (Supplementary Table 1: introns 3 and 4). Table 2 classifies **both alleles VUS**. The splicing consequence is "predicted"; **no RNA or protein test in this paper**.
- Phenotype (Table 1 row, Supplementary Table 1): female, non-consanguineous, focal onset at 2.5 months, later epileptic spasms, tonic, hemiclonic and other seizures and status epilepticus; normal MRI at 6 months; acquired microcephaly (head circumference falling across centiles); profound impairment; spasticity, scoliosis, hypotonia; alive at 6 y; seizures ongoing.
- Supplementary Table 1: "Not previously reported". Supplementary Table 2 links E17K to Piard 2018 patient 12 as a separate earlier carrier.
- Figure 1 (inspected): WWOX is one slice among the recessive genes, n = 1.

## What the paper states

- WWOX is one of nine "novel EIMFS genes" and this is "the first case of EIMFS" with biallelic WWOX variants; the patient "also developed epileptic spasms".
- Nothing about mechanism, drug response or survival specific to WWOX.

## Overlap and counting (after the first pass)

- **P93 is patient 6 of the 2023 WWOX-DEE cohort (`PAPER 018`, PMID 36779245).** That cohort states that its patients 6 and 7 were "briefly reported" and cites this paper (its reference 29); its patient 6 carries E17K with an intron-4 deletion (two intronic deletions, introns 3 and 4, in its text), is female, non-consanguineous, recruited in the same country, had EIMFS with clinically evident migration, and is listed as deceased at 11 y 7 mo. **One patient, counted once** — the identity is stated by the later authors, not inferred.
- The 2023 cohort also reports what this paper only predicts: RNA sequencing and RT-PCR suggested the intron-3 deletion is benign and the **intron-4 deletion causes exon 5 skipping**.
- **Not Piard patient 12**: that carrier's second allele is the missense S304F (`c.911C>T`, Piard Supplemental Table 1). The 2026-09-21 triage's suspicion that P93 overlapped the Piard series is not supported; the overlap is with the 2023 cohort.
- **Not the 16q deletion patient of PMID 31353122:** this cohort's 6.8 Mb deletion (patient 54) is a chromosome 2 deletion containing SCN1A and SCN2A, and no patient was recruited from the country of that case report.

## What the paper does NOT say

- It does not test the intronic deletions at RNA level, does not phase them by anything but parental testing, and does not classify either WWOX allele above VUS.
- It does not say how the gene was judged causal with two VUS alleles.
