## What it measures

Systemic Pex2 knockout mice (mixed Swiss Webster x 129, survive about 1-2 weeks) at P10, five CNS regions: catalase and cholesterol-enzyme activities, brain sterols, regional proteomics (n = 5), qPCR/northern for cholesterol, fatty-acid, sphingolipid and myelin genes, CNP activity, MBP immunostaining, AMPK/AKT/mTOR/ERK signalling, cytokine and microglial/astrocytic markers. WWOX: zero occurrences in the text.

## What it shows (DATO)

- Brain cholesterol is similar; desmosterol rises about 55 percent. Cholesterol-synthesis enzymes fall in spinal cord and brainstem and rise in cortex.
- Hypomyelination at P10: CNP activity down 50 to 60 percent in all regions; MBP, CNP and PLP1 protein and mRNA lower; myelin-proteome proteins mostly down.
- Sphingolipid and fatty-acid synthesis proteins fall in white-matter regions: CERS2, DEGS1, FA2H, UGT8 (protein and mRNA), ELOVL1/5/6/7 mRNA; ELOVL1 protein not detected.
- p-RPS6 lower in every region (mTORC1 activity read as lower); p-AMPK slightly lower; pro-inflammatory cytokines and Trem2 up; full disease-associated microglia not present at P10.
- Deposited proteomics: WWOX protein is detected in spinal cord, brainstem and cerebellum and does not change (log2 fold change +0.14, -0.06, -0.26; P 0.40, 0.73, 0.67).

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Nothing about WWOX loss; the single WWOX datum is a null.** WWOX protein is unchanged in Pex2-null CNS regions; this says WWOX levels are not affected by peroxisome loss at P10, not that WWOX lacks a lipid role.
2. **The hypomyelination may be partly nutritional and systemic.** The knockout has bile-acid-dependent fat malabsorption; early starvation causes irreversible myelin deficits in rats (cited); the paper cannot separate nutritional, metabolic and cell-intrinsic causes (conditional models in the literature give later, different phenotypes).
3. **Whole-region tissue, single age, small groups.** Controls pooled from two genotypes; knockouts n = 3 to 6; cannot assign changes to cell types (authors' stated limitation); no longitudinal data possible.
4. **Cholesterol itself does not change.** The sterol finding is desmosterol; the authors' proposal that desmosterol and impaired sterol transport contribute to hypomyelination is a hypothesis (LXR targets are down, not up, despite desmosterol).
5. **The mTORC1 story rests on one phospho-readout** (p-RPS6) with AKT and AMPK not activated.
6. **A peroxisomal lesion, not a chain-length lesion.** Chain-length shortening is not measured here (no sphingolipid lipidomics); sphingolipid synthesis is inferred from protein and mRNA of CERS2, DEGS1, UGT8, FA2H.
