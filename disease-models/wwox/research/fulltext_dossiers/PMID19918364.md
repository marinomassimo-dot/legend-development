## Design

Unilateral sciatic nerve transection in adult male Sprague-Dawley rats (2 mm of distal stump removed), sham = nerve exposed without transection; L4/L5 DRG and spinal cord sampled from 30 min to 2 months. Plus `p53` wild-type and knockout mice. Plus in-vitro promoter assays and FRET.

## The four things this reading establishes

**1. The size classification is purely morphometric, and the nociceptive reading is imported.** The paper defines "small" as `<20 µm in diameter`, medium 20–30, large >30 — and nothing else. The strings `nocicept`, `unmyelin`, `IB4`, `CGRP` and `substance P` occur **0 times** in the artefact. Any statement that this paper is about nociceptors is LEGEND's convention, not the authors'. *(This confirms the prior LEGEND audit's finding, now against the source.)*

**2. The headline chronic result is not lateralised.** The Results sentence places the month-2 co-activation "in the small neurons of **both non-injured and injured sides**". Supplementary Figure S3, rendered and inspected, gives the numbers: contralateral small neurons ≈ 72 % with nuclear p-WOX1 at month 2 against ≈ 71 % ipsilateral, with sham already at ≈ 37 % / 31 %. **Axotomy is therefore not the sufficient cause of the chronic phase the title names.**

**3. The "co-activation" percentages come from a categorical heat map.** Figure 4 is a grid of five ordinal bins — none 0 %, very low <20 %, low 20–40 %, moderate 40–65 %, high >65 % — with **no n, no error and no test anywhere on it**. The ">65 %" and "40–65 %" in the abstract are bin labels, not measured values with intervals.

**4. The directional claim is a cell-line reporter result.** "WOX1 inhibits the neuroprotective CREB … WOX1 enhances NF-κB promoter activation" comes from **transient over-expression of EGFP-WOX1 in HEK-293 fibroblasts** with Gal4-fusion luciferase reporters (Fig 5A–F). No panel of Figure 5 contains a neuron. The in-neuron work in this paper is *binding* — FRET in primary DRG cultures, immuno-EM in tissue — never transcriptional output.

## What the paper does state

Rapid (30 min) `Wwox` mRNA upregulation in injured DRG by in-situ hybridisation and qPCR; time-related nuclear accumulation of p-WOX1 (Tyr33); binding of WOX1 with CREB strongest in the nucleus, strengthened by hypoxic stress (deferoxamine); complex formation of p-WOX1 with p-CREB and p-c-Jun by immuno-EM; p53-independence of the nuclear relocation; Smad4 absent from neurons and confined to glia; CREB co-expression augmenting WOX1-induced apoptosis of SK-N-SH.

## What it does not state

- It does not measure neuronal death as a function of WWOX: **"Approximately less than 5% neuronal death was observed post axotomy for 2 months"**, and the larger figure the Discussion leans on (37 % by week 32) is another laboratory's.
- It does not show that CREB inhibition causes the death; it shows co-occurrence plus a reporter effect in a kidney line.
- It gives no WWOX allele: this is injury-induced endogenous activation in wild-type rats.
- Two Discussion steps — WOX1 binding IκBα, and the nuclear NF-κB consequence — are cited as **unpublished data of the same laboratory**.
- No blinding or randomisation is mentioned (0 occurrences).

## Why it matters to the model

This is the one record in group B whose direction is **pro-death for activated WWOX**. It is not reconciled anywhere in the corpus with the protective framing of the 2015–2022 papers, and the review (PMID 34359949) resolves the tension only by positing two phospho-states (pY33 protective, pS14 pathogenic) whose "underlying signaling pathways … remain to be established". For a loss-of-function genotype class the practical consequence is narrow but real: a therapeutic strategy that **raises** WWOX activity in stressed neurons has one in-vivo record pointing the wrong way, and that record has never been replicated outside this laboratory (`WWOX AND "dorsal root ganglion"` → exactly 1 PubMed record, this one).
