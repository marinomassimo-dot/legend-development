## What it is

A two-patient case report from adult intensive care. Case 1 is a 67-year-old with profound lactic acidosis after presentation with vomiting and diarrhoea; case 2 is a 50-year-old with cardiogenic shock and kidney injury who developed a persistent high anion gap acidosis in hospital. Each received three sessions of plasma exchange with 3 L of 5 percent albumin after continuous renal replacement therapy failed to clear the acidosis. WWOX: zero occurrences; adult critical care, no genetic or paediatric encephalopathy content.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** This is the weakest evidence class in the plasma group (n = 2, uncontrolled, adult, not neurological).

## What it reports (DATO)

- Both patients' acid-base values improved after the exchanges (case 1: pH below 7.0 at presentation, 7.38 after the third session; case 2: 7.22 to 7.36); lactate fell from 15.0 to 2.0 mmol/L (case 1) and from 4.8 to 1.8 (case 2).
- Case 1 had raised plasma acylcarnitines and amino acids; case 2 had no such workup described.
- The authors suggest protein-bound acids as the mechanism and say causality cannot be established.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Nothing about WWOX, a neurological disorder or a child.** It cannot inform plasma exchange or albumin in a WWOX-deficient child (H2 questions 1 and 5: not testable here).
2. **No causal inference from two uncontrolled patients.** Both were also on CRRT, with continuing management of the underlying illness; a falling lactate or recovering kidney function over the same days is the ordinary course of recovery. In case 2 the exchanges coincide with creatinine falling from 2.6 to 1.0 and withdrawal of shock support; in case 1 the lactate was 20 on CRRT and 12 before the first exchange.
3. **The mechanism is unmeasured.** No protein-bound acid, binding capacity or removal is measured; the "low albumin electrophoresis" finding is flagged by the authors as possibly methodological; the amino-acid and acylcarnitine findings are called non-specific and the candidate inherited deficiencies speculative.
4. **Internal table inconsistencies (recomputable DATO).** In case 1 the reported anion gaps (46, 44, 42, 40, 30, 12 in row order) exceed the value from the table's own sodium, chloride and bicarbonate by 18 mmol/L before the first exchange (138 - 99 - 17 = 22 against 40) and after CRRT (140 - 100 - 16 = 24 against 42), by 11 after the first exchange (139 - 100 - 20 = 19 against 30), and by 16 at presentation if the text's bicarbonate of 11 is used (138 - 97 - 11 = 30 against 46); the last row is close (15 against 12), so the gap closes as much by recomputation as by treatment. In case 2 the recomputed gap agrees within about 4. The case 2 text gives bicarbonate 10 mEq/L while Table 2 lists 15 to 16 before the exchanges. The case 1 text says hydroxocobalamin led to eventual resolution of the lactic acidosis, while Table 1 shows lactate 15 to 20 through CRRT and the first exchange. Both tables are captioned as before and after each session but omit session 2 rows.
5. **Albumin is the replacement fluid, not a tested agent.** The report does not compare albumin with plasma, nor exchange with CRRT alone; it cannot show that albumin replacement did anything.
6. **Publication selection.** A positive two-case report in a journal that publishes case reports; no negative cases.
