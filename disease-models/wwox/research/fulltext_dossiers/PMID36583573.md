## What it measures

CerS2 deleted in myelinating cells (CNP-Cre; exons 2-11 flanked; C57BL/6J). Readouts: untargeted brain lipidomics at 6 weeks (6 controls, 7 knockouts), targeted lipidomics of purified myelin (5 versus 6), luxol fast blue and western blots at 6, 10 and 16 weeks, EM of corpus callosum (3 mice per group), motor tests, gliosis staining, whole-brain spectral cytometry (3 mice per group), denatured-MBP staining, and microglial markers in cuprizone and EAE tissue. Heterozygous deletion (dO/+) is also studied. WWOX: zero occurrences.

## What it shows (DATO)

- Brain and myelin: very-long-chain (C22-C24) GalCer/HexCer, sulfatide, sphingomyelin and ceramide species collapse, C16-C18 species rise; totals of HexCer, sulfatide and ceramide are higher in myelin, total sphingomyelin is unchanged; myelin cholesterol is unchanged; dihydrosphingosine is about ten-fold higher. The deposited per-animal table reproduces Table 2 for four checked lipids (for example ST(d18:1_24:1) 49,891.33 versus 1,287.83).
- Timeline: normal-looking myelin at 6 weeks but fewer myelinated axons (about 79 versus 66 percent) and thinner myelin; oligodendrocyte density normal to 10 weeks; astro- and microgliosis from 6 weeks (cerebellum) and widespread by 10 weeks; APP spheroids at 10 weeks; myelin and oligodendrocyte loss and motor deficits at 16 weeks; sudden deaths from 14-16 weeks, 80 percent by 22 weeks.
- A distinct microglial subset (TMEM119 and P2RY12 low; CD68, CD11c, CD45, MHC-II high; "myelin-responsive microglia") is about four-fold more abundant at 6 weeks and eight-fold at 10 weeks (3 mice per group); denatured MBP staining is higher at 6 weeks. The same marker phenotype is seen in cuprizone and EAE tissue.
- Heterozygous oligodendrocyte deletion: no myelin loss; subtle balance-beam deficit.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No WWOX measurement, no cholesterol-composition change.** This is the important lipid-composition negative for question 1 of the batch: even in a lipid-synthesis null with severe hypomyelination, myelin cholesterol does not change and total sphingolipid amount does not fall; chain length does. If the WWOX-null hypomyelination is a lipid defect, "cholesterol supply" is not what this model predicts; chain-length readouts are.
2. **The mechanism of microglial activation is not identified.** TREM2 sensing and the denatured-MBP epitope are named as possibilities in the Discussion; neither is tested. The authors cannot say whether microglia respond to lipid, to protein conformation or to a stress signal from oligodendrocytes or neurons.
3. **Small groups throughout.** EM n = 3 per genotype; cytometry n = 3 per group (so the 4- and 8-fold MRM changes rest on three animals); lipidomics 6 versus 7; different sexes between analyses (untargeted males, myelin lipidomics females). The deaths were not investigated (cause unknown).
4. **The C16-C18 increase has no enzymatic explanation.** CerS1 and CerS5 antibodies could not be validated; CerS6 protein is not higher (it is lower in cortex, Supplementary Figure 2). The authors attribute it to substrate availability (dihydrosphingosine), not shown.
5. **Cell-of-origin.** CNP-Cre targets myelinating cells; here the oligodendrocyte-lineage lesion is sufficient to cause myelin loss, seizures and death, whereas the repository's CLAIM 003 records WWOX hypomyelination as non-cell-autonomous after neuronal deletion.
6. **Internal timing note.** Abstract: 90 percent died between 16 and 26 weeks; Results: deaths start at 14-16 weeks and reach 80 percent by 22 weeks. Minor.
7. Not a model of WWOX loss of function in any aspect: it is a lipid-synthesis null with a late, progressive degeneration; the Wwox-null mouse dies in weeks.

## Comparison with the laboratory's records

- **Sun 2026 (same wave).** Same enzyme, independent groups: both find C22-C24 loss with C16-C18 rise, early hypomyelination, late seizures and death, gliosis. They differ on the Cre driver (CNP versus nestin/olig), age of death (about 4-5 months in both), and in details: Teo reports no change in myelin cholesterol and in total sphingomyelin, and a distinct microglial subset; Sun reports GFAP up in the proteome and a microdomain step. The two papers' agreement on chain length and timing is a replication of the lipid phenotype, not of the mechanism.
- **PAPER 007 / CLAIM 005 (P47T and Wwox-KO neuroinflammation).** The Teo microglial markers (CD11c, CD68, MHC-II high; TMEM119, P2RY12 low) give a concrete flow panel to apply to Wwox models; CD68/CD11c immunostaining of white matter is the simplest first test. No Wwox measurement of these exists in the repository.
- **CLAIM 003.** Differs in cell-of-origin (see point 5); the CerS2 dO/+ heterozygote has no myelin loss, which is the lipid-null analogue of "heterozygous loss carries no myelin phenotype", not evidence for WWOX.
- **Selection question 1.** Not answerable here; this paper adds the cholesterol-unchanged negative and a panel (chain-length species, myelin-isolate lipidomics, MRM cytometry) to apply.
