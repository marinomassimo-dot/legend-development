## What it is

A prospective observational secondary study inside the NICHD TOP randomised trial: near-infrared spectroscopy (cerebral and mesenteric tissue saturation) around red-cell transfusions in extremely low birth weight preterm infants randomised to high or low haemoglobin transfusion thresholds, plus an exploratory classification-tree and regression analysis of death or neurodevelopmental impairment at 22-26 months. WWOX: zero occurrences.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** Extremely preterm infants with anaemia of prematurity; the study excludes infants with congenital conditions other than prematurity that affect neurodevelopment, so a genetic encephalopathy would have been excluded at enrolment.

## What it measures (DATO)

Transfusion raised cerebral saturation by about 2.7-4.8 points and mesenteric saturation by 5.6-6.7 points while peripheral saturation did not change, with no significant difference between threshold groups. Exploratory: transfusions at pre-transfusion cerebral saturation below 50 percent were associated with death or impairment (OR 2.41, 1.08-5.41, P = .03).

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No WWOX or genetic-DEE brain.** It answers H1 question 3 only for the readout: **cerebral NIRS saturation (and fractional extraction) moves after transfusion in preterm infants**. Whether it would move, or mean the same, in a brain with impaired oxygen utilisation is not tested; NIRS reports the balance of delivery and consumption, and the authors list cardiac output, vascular resistance, carbon dioxide and ductus status as confounders of the readout. INFERENZA: a mitochondrially stressed brain could show a different extraction at the same haemoglobin; nothing here measures it.
2. **Higher saturation after transfusion is not shown to be benefit.** The trial arm (high versus low threshold) did not change survival without impairment (the parent trial's result, cited), and the paper itself says haemoglobin threshold was not a significant outcome variable.
3. **The outcome association is exploratory and confounded.** Hypothesis-generating by the authors' label; the tree's low-saturation leaf has 10 infants; higher mean haemoglobin was linked to worse outcomes, which the authors attribute to sicker infants receiving extra transfusions; the regression adjusts only for gestational age, sex and centre. Low pre-transfusion saturation may mark illness, not a modifiable cause. Not a basis to target saturation or to transfuse.
4. **Attrition.** 140 transfused infants, 101 with valid pre/post data, 80 in the primary 28-day analysis; 99 events lacked sufficient NIRS data before or after.
5. **Unit slip:** the Discussion writes haemoglobin 12.5 mg/dL where the table and figure say g/dL (cosmetic).

## Design and limits

Secondary study; masked NIRS; 16 centres; INVOS adult/neonatal sensor caveat acknowledged; group sizes 65 versus 36 transfusing infants; no correction for the many CART candidate variables.
