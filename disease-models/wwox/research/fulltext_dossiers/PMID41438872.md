## What the paper did

Juvenile male Macaca nemestrina, unilateral intracerebroventricular injection of 4e13 vg/kg of AAV-PHP.eB (3 animals) or AAV9 (3 animals) carrying nuclear 3xFLAG-histone-2B under the human synapsin 1 promoter; one saline control. Six weeks. All animals pre-screened AAV9-neutralising-antibody negative. Vectors CsCl-enriched for full capsids, titres matched. Endpoints: FLAG and NeuN immunostaining in matched cortical regions and spinal segments (counted per region of interest), ddPCR vector genomes in brain regions and peripheral organs, necropsy.

## What it measured

- Cortex: median neuronal labelling 11.1 percent (PHP.eB) against 2.2 percent (AAV9) over 336 and 343 regions of interest (p < 0.001); PHP.eB flat along the anterior-posterior axis, AAV9 highest in visual cortex (Figure 4A-C, inspected).
- Spinal cord: about 1.5-fold higher with PHP.eB (16.4 against 10.3 percent, regions of interest per segment) (Figure 4F-G).
- Subcortical: putamen, thalamus and cerebellum barely transduced by either capsid; no capsid difference in putamen by ddPCR.
- ddPCR: motor cortex 5.7 against 0.73 copies per diploid genome (p = 0.009), visual cortex 2.84 against 1.28 (p = 0.041).
- Periphery: vector genomes in liver about 200 to 250 copies per diploid genome in both groups, about 100-fold more than heart, kidney or muscle (Figure 5B, inspected; panel agrees with text within reading error).
- No physiological or behavioural abnormality; necropsy and pathology "no vector-related macroscopic or microscopic findings"; no microglial or astrocytic activation near clusters.

## What the authors state

- PHP.eB gives a cortical advantage in a primate even though its rodent advantage is attributed to LY6A, absent in primates; mechanism "warranting further investigation".
- Peripheral leakage and limited subcortical reach are "considerable challenges".
- Limitations given: body-weight difference between capsid cohorts may bias against AAV9; no serum liver enzymes or other clinical chemistry; only neurons assessed.
- Epileptic encephalopathies and Rett syndrome are listed among disorders that could benefit from broad cortical coverage (no experiment in either).

## What the paper does NOT say

- No toxicity endpoint of any kind beyond necropsy histology; no DRG examined or mentioned; no liver enzymes; no immunology other than pre-screening.
- No dose-response, no dose below or above 4e13 vg/kg, no second route.
- Not the same question as the group's: the capsid comparison is about transduction efficiency, so it cannot say whether a more efficient capsid buys the same neuronal transduction at a lower dose (no dose-titration arm).
- The statistical unit is the region of interest, with three animals per capsid; animal-level inference is weaker than the p-values suggest (INFERENZA).

## Transfer limit

Juvenile male pigtail macaque, ICV (unilateral), one dose, one reporter and promoter, six weeks, n = 3 per capsid; the comparator animals were lighter (about 3.1 to 3.4 kg against 4.4 to 4.5 kg). Says nothing about the cargo-dependent DRG or liver lesions, nor WWOX. A neuron-restricted nuclear reporter does not model an untagged therapeutic protein at therapeutic expression levels.

## Verdict

INGEST. Efficiency comparison only. It does not shrink the dose-driven arm of the toxicity argument because no toxicity or dose comparison is made.
