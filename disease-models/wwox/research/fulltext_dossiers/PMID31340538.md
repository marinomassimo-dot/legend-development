# Full-text dossier — PMID 31340538

**Study:** Tochigi Y, Takamatsu Y, Nakane J, Nakai R, Katayama K, Suzuki H.
*Loss of Wwox Causes Defective Development of Cerebral Cortex with Hypomyelination in a
Rat Model of Lethal Dwarfism with Epilepsy*. Int J Mol Sci. 2019;20:3596. DOI
`10.3390/ijms20143596`; PMCID `PMC6678113`.

**Reading status:** complete full-text read on 2026-08-06. Abstract, Introduction, Results,
Discussion, Materials and Methods, all six figure captions, all six figure images, back
matter and 56 references were read. No tables or supplementary scientific files exist.

## Verdict

This is a useful primary developmental-pathology study, but the repository had overextended
it in three ways. It is by **Tochigi et al.**, not Kumada et al.; the title says **lethal
dwarfism with epilepsy**, not lissencephaly; and every new experiment is postnatal
(`PND5–21`), so the paper cannot itself be a prenatal-development anchor.

The strongest result is narrower and still important: systemic, near-null Wwox deficiency
in male rats preserves NeuN-positive cortical cell counts and gross cortical thickness while
strongly reducing neuritic markers, myelin markers and mature oligodendrocytes during early
postnatal maturation. It demonstrates a dissociation between neuronal cell number and
neurite/myelin maturation. It does not establish reversibility, OPC arrest, a direct
oligodendrocyte-autonomous mechanism or prenatal cortical misassembly.

## Experimental design

- Inbred `lde` rat carrying a recessive 13-bp exon-9 deletion, `c.1190_1202del`, predicted
  `p.Leu371Thrfs*53`; systemic phenotype includes dwarfism, epilepsy and early mortality.
- Male `+/+` and `lde/lde` rats examined at PND5, 10, 15 and 21. `+/lde` appears only in the
  Wwox-expression comparison, not in the developmental phenotyping.
- At least three affected and three normal males per experiment and age. Litters were reduced
  below five pups around PND5 to improve mutant survival.
- Immunohistochemistry and Western blotting for Wwox, NeuN, MAP2, FluoroPan neuronal marker,
  MBP, CNP, APC/CC1, GFAP and Iba1. Identical cortical regions were quantified with ImageJ.
- Pairwise Student t-tests at every age/outcome; no power calculation, multiplicity
  correction, randomisation or blinding is reported.
- No rescue, conditional deletion, lineage restriction, electron microscopy, g-ratio,
  electrophysiology, OPC marker (`NG2`, `PDGFRA`, `Olig2`) or functional conduction assay.

## What the study directly shows

### Wwox abundance and localisation

- `DATO`: Wwox is detected broadly in the CNS and increases from PND5 to PND21 in whole brain
  and cortex.
- `DATO`: the homozygote lacks the normal Wwox band but shows a very faint, slightly
  higher-molecular-weight band compatible with the predicted frameshift product. Calling it
  unstable or non-functional is the authors' interpretation; turnover and function were not
  measured.
- `DATO`: `+/lde` brain shows a normal-molecular-weight band described as about half the
  wild-type intensity and strong cortical/callosal immunostaining. The paper does **not**
  phenotype heterozygous brain development, so it does not demonstrate “half protein, normal
  brain”.
- `DATO`: at PND21, Wwox immunoreactivity colocalises with NeuN, APC and GFAP in representative
  images and is not detected in Iba1-positive microglia. There is no quantitative
  colocalisation analysis; the defensible wording is “not detected by this assay”, not
  universal absence of microglial expression.

### Neuronal maturation

- `DATO`: NeuN-positive counts, NeuN Western signal, cortical thickness and PND21 layer-wise
  NeuN counts do not differ detectably between `+/+` and `lde/lde`.
- `DATO`: MAP2 immunoreactivity and Western signal are markedly reduced; FluoroPan-positive
  vertical processes are visually reduced at PND21.
- `INFERENZA`: neuronal abundance is preserved while neuritic maturation is impaired.
  “Neurons intact” is too broad: morphology, connectivity, physiology and survival beyond
  PND21 were not established.
- `INFERENZA`: the Discussion invokes Wwox–GSK3β–Tau literature to explain MAP2/neurite loss,
  but this paper measures none of GSK3β abundance/activity, Tau phosphorylation or
  microtubule dynamics.

### Myelin and glia

- `DATO`: MBP and CNP staining/Western signals are strongly reduced during the PND5–21
  interval; Figure 4 most clearly supports MBP differences from PND10 onward and CNP
  differences across the measured interval.
- `DATO`: APC/CC1-positive mature oligodendrocytes are reduced at PND15 and PND21 in a
  selected cortex/white-matter region and corpus callosum.
- `UNRESOLVED`: no precursor marker was measured. The study cannot distinguish fewer OPCs
  from blocked differentiation, delayed maturation, systemic developmental delay or altered
  axon–glia signalling.
- `DATO`: cortical GFAP-positive and Iba1-positive populations/signals are reduced, with the
  exact significant ages differing by readout.
- `TENSION`: this cortical reduction has the opposite sign from hippocampal astro/microglial
  activation in the Wwox-knockout mouse cited by the authors. Region, species, age, marker
  interpretation and disease stage all remain possible explanations. A single universal
  “WWOX loss causes gliosis” direction is not licensed by these two studies alone.

## Figure audit

- **Figure 1:** broad Wwox distribution; three experiments; descriptive localisation.
- **Figure 2:** normal band absent in `lde/lde`, faint higher band visible; the heterozygote
  intensity is not quantified in a genotype graph. Age curves use only `+/+`.
- **Figure 3:** the cell-count/thickness panels are negative results with wide error bars;
  MAP2 gives the positive maturation signal. The figure supports preserved measured counts,
  not globally intact neurons.
- **Figure 4:** large reductions in MBP/CNP and APC-positive mature cells; no ultrastructure,
  precursor count or function.
- **Figure 5:** reduced cortical GFAP/Iba1, not activation. Western and cell-count timelines
  are not identical.
- **Figure 6:** representative colocalisation images only; no fraction of double-positive
  cells, specificity metric or cell-type expression quantification.

## Transfer to the reference genotype

- **Genotype:** `lde/lde` is a biallelic systemic frameshift/near-null model. Its exon-9
  lesion makes it a useful comparator for the reference splice-acceptor allele, but it is a
  deletion rather than a splicing defect and says nothing about ASO correctability. It does
  not model the compound null/missense state or Q230P residual function.
- **Model:** rat systemic illness can delay brain maturation indirectly. Later conditional
  deletion/rescue studies are required for cell autonomy and have stronger causal leverage.
- **Stage:** the study spans PND5–21. It supports early postnatal maturation and myelination,
  not a directly measured prenatal lesion or reversibility at later stages.

## Multi-hop

Thirty of 56 references are directly about WWOX, its rat model or WWOX disorders. Twenty-nine
resolve in the structured corpus. PMID 17803050—the original phenotypic characterisation of
the `lde` rat—is absent and is now `FT-041`. PMID 19500159—the genetic-mapping paper that

but has no full-text receipt and is now `FT-042`. These primaries must carry claims about
mutation causality, seizure penetrance and the earlier mRNA/protein observations.

No therapeutic recommendation follows from this study. Interpretation belongs with the
treating clinical team.
