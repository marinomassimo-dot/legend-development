## 1 · What the paper measures

A retrospective single-centre cohort: 3,727 children with refractory epilepsy reviewed, 520 meeting inclusion; 529 tests (413 exome, 116 TruSight One panel). Significant (pathogenic or likely pathogenic) variants in 261 tests (49.3 percent), 181 distinct variants, 82 novel. A gene list with counts follows; Table 1 lists variants of uncertain significance; Figure 1 plots counts by gene and test.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. WWOX appears with n = 4 among the significant findings, in a flat list after SCN1A (20), STXBP1 (9), CDKL5 (9), beside MECP2 (6), ADGRV1 (6), CACNA1H (6), KCNQ2 (5), RELN (5), TREX1 (4).

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

FT-115 says this record is the only PubMed hit for WWOX with a T-type calcium-channel gene, that it joins them only as co-occurrence in a roughly 45-gene list across different patients, and that the negative "no T-type calcium evidence in WWOX deficiency, so the ethosuximide efficacy in the Wwox-null mouse is a pharmacological fact without mechanism" would invert if the paper reported a CACNA1H finding in a WWOX patient. The read confirms the negative: there is no WWOX-CACNA1H finding of any kind, no T-type text, and no patient-level data. One refinement to FT-115's wording: "across different patients" is not something the paper states or lets one check; it gives gene counts only, so the paper cannot exclude a patient carrying both and cannot show one. The conclusion that the record is not evidence of a T-type link stands; the retirement of FT-115 should say "no patient-level data", not "different patients".

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

Any statement about WWOX variants (type, zygosity, phenotype, treatment) in this cohort; a link between WWOX and any calcium channel gene; a metabolic or autonomic conclusion; the claim that WES is significantly better than panel testing (its own P is 0.085); a ketogenic-diet claim for WWOX.
