## What the paper did

GWAS of four global brain-tissue volumes (intracranial, grey matter, white matter, CSF) from T1/T2 MRI at about
5 weeks of age in **561 infants** (295 singletons, 17 sibling pairs, 232 twins; 63% European ancestry, the rest
primarily African). Twin-aware ACE linear mixed models, 8.76 million SNPs (imputed, R² > 0.8, MAF ≥ 0.01). Study
genome-wide threshold 1.25e-8 (5e-8 divided by four phenotypes).

## What it measured about WWOX

- **rs10514437**, intronic in WWOX, with ICV-adjusted **white matter volume**: P = 1.56e-8, "fell just short of
  genome-wide significance" by the study's own threshold (it would pass the conventional 5e-8). Table 1:
  effect −6617 per A1 allele, A1 = G, MAF 0.03, likelihood ratio 31.97, variance explained 4.14% after
  winner's-curse correction, −3.76% WM volume per G allele. The SNP is **directly genotyped**, not imputed
  (Table 1 footnote; cluster plot Supplementary Figure 7 not inspected).
- **Direction, read from two tables:** Supplementary Table 8 gives the G allele a frequency of about 0.97 across
  ancestry groups, so G is the common allele and **A is the minor allele**. Each copy of the common allele is
  associated with less white matter; equivalently, carriers of the rare A allele have **more** white matter. This
  is my reading of Table 1 plus Supplementary Table 8; the paper never states the direction in words.
- **Robustness:** permutation empirical P 8e-8 (Supplementary Methods); consistent sign and size across European
  (P 2.1e-5), non-European (P 6.5e-4), >32 weeks' gestation, no-NICU, unrelated-only and reduced-covariate
  subsets (Supplementary Table 6). FST < 0.05.
- **Replication: none.** Supplementary Table 10 lists rs10514437 as **NA in both PNC and ENIGMA2** — it was not
  tested in either cohort. The authors say the comparisons "do not represent a conventional replication sample"
  and that "independent replication is critical".
- **Function: none.** No eQTL, chromatin or functional annotation is reported for the WWOX SNP; Supplementary
  Table 5 lists WWOX's OMIM, GWAS-catalog and mouse knockout annotations only. Supplementary Appendix 2 shows
  WWOX expression **rising from prenatal to postnatal life** across brain regions (Supplementary Table 9).
- External context (not from the paper, labelled): dbSNP places rs10514437 at chr16 78.459 Mb (GRCh38), inside
  WWOX intron 8 of NM_016373.4 and downstream of the short isoform NM_130791.5.

## What the authors state about WWOX

The Discussion connects the locus to WWOX disease phenotypes (SCAR12, epilepsy, microcephaly, hypomyelination,
callosal hypoplasia) and to adult WOX1 expression in axons and the corpus callosum (ref 54, Chiang 2013) as
"consistent with our observed association with WM". That is interpretation by citation.

## What the paper does NOT say

- No genome-wide significant WWOX result by its own threshold; no replication; no functional variant; no LD to
  a coding or regulatory element; no claim of reduced myelination — the phenotype is total white-matter **volume**
  on segmentation, not myelin content.

## Transfer limit to the WWOX-DEE genotype class

T3–T4. A common, non-coding variant in typically developing infants, of small effect and with its minor allele
associated with *more* white matter, says nothing about biallelic loss-of-function. It cannot support or bound the
hypomyelination limb as it applies to the reference genotype class; at most it shows that WWOX-locus variation is
not inert for infant white-matter volume, unreplicated.

## Verdict

**INGEST (bounded).** A real, unreplicated, sub-threshold locus with a direction most readers would assume the wrong
way round.
