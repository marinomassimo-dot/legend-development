## What the paper did

A single-stranded AAV9 vector carrying human *AP4B1* cDNA under a chicken β-actin hybrid (CBh)
promoter with an hGH poly(A) — plus a neuronal-specific synapsin (SYN) variant kept as a
contingency — was tested in *AP4B1*-knockout HeLa cells, patient fibroblasts, patient iPSC-derived
neurons (compound-heterozygous loss-of-function versus the clinically unaffected heterozygous
parent) and *AP4B1*-knockout SH-SY5Y cells, then in the *Ap4b1*^−/− mouse (CRISPR 76 bp exon-1
deletion, frameshift, truncated transcript):

- **Route comparison**, P2–3, 4 × 10^13 vg/kg, n = 3 per group: intracisterna magna versus
  intravenous.
- **Neonatal efficacy**, P2–3, 4 × 10^13 vg/kg ICM, both promoters, to 9 months.
- **Adult dose-response**, ~P60 ICM, three doses, stated in the Results as 5 × 10^12, 3 × 10^12 and
  2 × 10^12 vg/kg.
- **Safety 1**: wild-type C57BL/6 mice, dosed P1–3 at 4 × 10^13 vg/kg ICM, splenocyte IFN-γ ELISpot
  against an hAP4B1 peptide library at 3 months, n = 4.
- **Safety 2**: wild-type mice, P42, 1.5 × 10^14 vg/kg ICM, one year, histopathology at 28 and 365 days.
- **GLP NHP toxicology**: wild-type cynomolgus monkeys, single ICM dose, vehicle (n = 2),
  3.2 × 10^12 vg/kg (n = 3) and 1.7 × 10^13 vg/kg (n = 3), sacrificed at 29 and 113 days, design
  agreed with the FDA at a pre-IND meeting.

## What the paper states

- ICM beats IV for CNS delivery: higher vector genome copies and mRNA in cerebrum, cerebellum and
  spinal cord, and IV mRNA in the spinal cord and cerebrum was "insufficient to restore formation of
  the AP-4 complex". ICM restored AP4E1 by 25% (cerebrum), 37% (spinal cord) and 16% (cerebellum).
- **Partial restoration sufficed.** In HeLa cells AP4E1 rose from 18 ± 4% to 54 ± 12% of wild type and
  that was enough to correct ATG9A trafficking; in mice the AP4E1 rescue is described throughout as
  "partial" while motor, anatomical, biomarker and cargo endpoints were rescued.
- **The window is endpoint-specific, not global.** Neonatal treatment rescued corpus-callosum
  thinning and ventriculomegaly; adult (P60) treatment did not ("no significant improvement … likely
  to be due to the late stage of intervention"), while motor function (rotarod to near-wild-type,
  hind-limb clasping) was rescued at *both* ages. P60 in mice is said to translate to about 6–7 human
  years.
- Plasma NfL was elevated in 9-month knockouts (untreated 40.6 ± 2.1 ng/mL; control 36.5 ± 1.5) and
  fell below the assay's lower limit of detection after either vector; CSF NfL was not significantly
  elevated in the first place. The authors propose plasma NfL as a trial biomarker.
- A quantitative **in vitro potency assay** in *AP4B1*-knockout SH-SY5Y cells gives a dose-response
  for ATG9A translocation (68.36 ± 12.88% rescue at MOI 1.6 × 10^7) with a recommended working MOI of
  2 × 10^6 (33.66 ± 4.30% rescue) where cell count and TGN morphology are unchanged.
- **Two-sided dose risk is measured, not assumed.** Cell count fell at MOI ≥ 4 × 10^6 in SH-SY5Y and at
  MOI 20 in iPSC neurons; TGN morphology changed above MOI 4 × 10^6.
- NHP findings: dose- and time-dependent treatment-related microscopic changes — minimal neuronal
  cell-body degeneration and minimal-to-mild mononuclear infiltration in DRG at the high dose,
  minimal-to-mild axonal degeneration in spinal cord and sciatic nerve at both doses — with severity
  never above 2 of 5 and with normal nerve-conduction velocity.
- **Dose translation method:** FDA guidance was to scale human dosing by **CSF volume**. The NHP high
  dose corresponds to 4 × 10^14 genome copies in a 4-year-old child; the proposed range is
  5 × 10^12–6 × 10^12 vg per mL of CSF. Planned first trials in children aged 1–5.
- Route economics from the cited literature: CSF delivery achieves comparable CNS transduction at a
  30-fold lower dose than IV, and intracisternal is up to 100-fold more effective than lumbar
  intrathecal for brain gene transfer.
- The promoter is a trade-off the authors name: CBh is strong (so a lower dose suffices) but "combined
  with larger transgene lengths often exceeds the limits of AAV9 packaging capacity so this strategy
  cannot always be implemented", and strong promoters risk peripheral off-target expression.

## What the paper does NOT state

- **The immunogenicity package contains no null animal.** Safety study 1 used *wild-type* mice, and
  the NHPs were wild-type too. Every animal tested for an immune response to the human AP4B1 protein
  already expressed its own AP4B1 orthologue — i.e. every animal was the equivalent of
  cross-reactive-immunological-material **positive**. The mice were also dosed at P1–3, when
  tolerance to a neo-antigen is most readily induced. The population that a recessive-null patient
  actually models — an animal with no endogenous protein, dosed after the neonatal period — was never
  tested for immunogenicity. The paper does not note this.
- No antibody assay appears anywhere. The only immune assay is an IFN-γ ELISpot (T cells), yet the
  caption of Fig. EV5 is titled "generated no **B cell** response to the hAP4B1 peptides".
- It does not report the cassette's size in kb, the full/empty capsid ratio, or any vector-lot
  characterisation.
- It does not report any seizure or EEG endpoint, although early-onset epilepsy is named in the
  introduction as part of the SPG47 phenotype. The functional battery is rotarod, clasping and open
  field, and the open-field phenotype did not replicate the authors' own earlier characterisation.
- It gives no human efficacy or human safety data of any kind: the clinical dose is a projection from
  mouse and NHP, by CSF volume, with no patient dosed.
- It does not test redosing, and says so: single dosing was adopted by rationale, not tested.
- It does not establish a no-observed-adverse-effect level or a maximum tolerated dose in the NHP; two
  doses were run, both declared acceptable.
- It says nothing about *WWOX*.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

The AP4B1 antibody was unreliable, so AP4E1 is used as the proxy for AP-4 complex restoration
throughout; the mouse phenotype is mild, so "a higher dosage may be necessary in humans"; vector
genome copies differed between vectors for three candidate reasons they cannot separate (cassette
size, small-insert packaging, procedural variability); liver and heart transduction in the adult and
P42 studies is attributed to CSF leakage; the lentiviral toxicity at high MOI is attributed to the
vector rather than the transgene because the GFP control shows it too; anatomical rescue is unlikely
in post-mitotic neurons; treatment effect is expected to diminish with age; DRG findings are placed
in the context of a published meta-analysis in which severity depends on dose and age (juveniles
less affected than adults) and subsides by 6 months.

## Caveats found by measurement, not stated by the authors

1. **The adult doses are stated inconsistently.** Results: "(i) 5 × 10^12 vg/kg (n = 12),
   (ii) 3 × 10^12 vg/kg (n = 12), and (iii) 2 × 10^12 vg/kg (n = 12)". Discussion: "both mid-
   (4 × 10^12 vg/kg) and high-dose (5 × 10^12 vg/kg) treatment groups". The mid dose is 3 × 10^12 in one
   place and 4 × 10^12 in the other, so the dose at which the pivotal adult rescue occurred cannot be
   read off this paper unambiguously.
2. **The stated group size is not the analysed group size.** n = 12 per dose is declared in the
   Results; the Fig. 6 caption gives n = 6 (ATG9A, corpus callosum, ventricles), n = 3 (mRNA) and
   n = 9 (rotarod, clasping) per group. No endpoint is analysed at n = 12.
3. **The clasping percentages in the Discussion are not reproducible from the Results.** The
   Discussion claims "a 60.6% reduction in clasping severity at 6 MPT and 66.9% at 9 MPT"; the
   Results means give 64.0% and 64.0% for CBh and 66.7% and 56.5% for SYN. Command:
   `python3 -c "print(100*(1.5-0.54)/1.5, 100*(2.00-0.72)/2.00)"`.
4. **The safety headline overstates the body.** The abstract says the NHP study "revealed no
   significant adverse events associated with the therapeutic intervention" and Fig. 8's title says
   "no adverse events or histopathology were present"; the body reports dose- and time-dependent
   treatment-related DRG, spinal-cord and sciatic-nerve degeneration in the NHPs, a treatment-
   associated white-cell and lymphocyte elevation at 28 days in the ICM CBh cohort (p = 0.0014 and
   p = 0.0028, resolved by one year), three humane sacrifices for ulcerative dermatitis and one
   unexplained death at 80 days in a mouse that had received ICM AAV9/CBh-hAP4B1 and was sacrificed
   for lethargy. "No significant adverse events" is a grading judgement, not an absence of findings.
5. Two captions contradict the body on timing and direction: Fig. EV4's title says adult rescue was
   assessed "9 months following treatment" where the body says 4 months post treatment at 6 months of
   age; Fig. EV5C says hAP4B1 mRNA was "elevated … in the brain, liver and spinal cord … with lower
   expression within the liver".
