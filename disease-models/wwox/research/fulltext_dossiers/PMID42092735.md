## What the paper did

A single neonate, followed to 4 months: clinical description, video-EEG, two MRIs, metabolic work-up, exome, parental
segregation, ACMG classification. No functional or neurochemical measurement.

## Genotype class and what was measured

- **Allele class × zygosity:** missense `c.716T>G p.(Leu239Arg)` × homozygous; both parents heterozygous; parents
  not consanguineous by report but from the same village.
- **Measured:** DNA only. ACMG "likely pathogenic" (PM2, PM3, PP3_Moderate) beside a two-star ClinVar "pathogenic" entry.
- **Not measured:** CSF monoamine metabolites — "could not be performed due to lack of availability in our center" —
  so the "neurotransmitter disorder" was a clinical working diagnosis, never tested either way. No dopaminergic drug was tried.

## Phenotype axes

- **Perinatal confounders:** meconium-stained delivery, ~15 s of positive-pressure ventilation, Apgar 6/8, clavicle
  fracture; a diffusion-restricted focus in the left lateral thalamus on the first MRI, attributed by the authors to birth.
- **Movement:** axial hypotonia with peripheral hypertonia, hypokinesia, facial hypomimia, paroxysmal alternating tone,
  stimulus-triggered spinal rigidity; "persisted" at 4 months.
- **Seizures and drug response:** perioral twitching day 13; seizure-like events reduced on phenobarbital; flexor
  spasms 20-30/day about one month after discharge; **vigabatrin added to phenobarbital — spasms continued**;
  valproate reduced them; clobazam added — spasms ceased, EEG improved with a disorganised background; seizure-free
  for one month at 4 months.
- **MRI (second):** bilateral frontotemporal atrophy, corpus callosum hypoplasia, ventricular enlargement. No optic atrophy.
- **Survival:** alive at 4 months.

## What the paper states and what it does NOT

- States: first WWOX case presenting as neonatal parkinsonism; mechanism is a "developmental circuitopathy" with
  GSK-3β and glymphatic contributions. These mechanism sentences are drawn from cited rodent and organoid literature;
  **nothing about dopamine was measured in this child**.
- States that the same variant was reported before (its reference 14 = Serin et al. 2018, *Neurol Sci*, a different
  centre) "without prominent parkinsonian features". It does **not** cite the 2025 cohort from its own university
  (PMID 41153369), which reports two further homozygous p.L239R siblings.
- Does **not** separate a WWOX effect from the perinatal injury, the antiseizure drugs, or the spasms themselves.

## Possible overlap with PMID 41153369 (identity hypothesis, unresolved)

Same university, same allele, homozygous; the cohort's case #49 is a male infant examined at 3 months with West
syndrome and multifocal epileptiform EEG — compatible with this boy. Against identity: this report says there was no
consanguinity and no family history of neurological disease, while the cohort's case #49 has an affected sister
(#50) and "Consanguinity: Yes"; the cohort window is October 2017 to July 2020 and this report gives no dates.
**No shared author, no cross-citation.** Do not sum the three carriers without author confirmation.
