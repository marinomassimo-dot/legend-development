## 0 · A premise in the assignment that the source does not support

The selection note described this paper as "the size-constraint counterpart" that buys headroom
"in an **AAV9** genome". **Every in vivo experiment here uses the AAV-DJ capsid, not AAV9.** AAV9
appears in this paper only as a literature reference for how others have used hSYN. The promoter is
capsid-agnostic in principle, but this paper supplies no AAV9 measurement, and a headroom claim for
an AAV9 cassette cannot be sourced to it.

## 1 · What the paper did

Designed **CP040**, a **126 bp** synthetic mini-promoter assembled from 5′ UTR excerpts of the human
ERCC8 and ERCC6 genes plus three spacer sequences carrying additional transcription-factor sites,
trimmed in silico with the PROMO tool. It contains **no viral promoter or enhancer sequence**.

Three experiments:

1. **In vitro.** Dual luciferase in HEK293 and in human iPSC-derived neural progenitors, comparing
   the full-length 126 bp construct against four truncation variants, five isolated fragments and a
   chicken β-actin control. Full length was the only effective design.
2. **In vivo, neonatal.** AAVDJ-CP040-eGFP at 1 × 10^13 vg/kg by temporal-vein i.v. into wild-type
   FVB/NJ neonates, necropsy at 6 weeks, N = 10. Highest transcript in cortex and cerebellum, then
   spinal cord, liver and heart; little in spleen, quadriceps, kidney. Colocalisation:
   12.75% ± 7.70% GFP⁺/NeuN⁺, 12.10% ± 6.55% GFP⁺/GFAP⁺, 15.33% ± 6.26% GFP⁺/Olig2⁺ (N = 3).
3. **In vivo, adult, head-to-head.** CP040 vs CBA, EF1α and hSYN, all in AAV-DJ, all at
   1 × 10^12 vg/kg, into 6-week-old adult FVB/NJ mice by **ICV** and by **intrathecal (IT)**,
   necropsy 9 weeks later, N = 6 for molecular assays and N = 3 for imaging. **Vector genomes were
   equal across promoter arms** — the dose control the companion promoter paper lacks.

## 2 · The result that transfers

**The route changes the ranking of the promoters.** Same promoters, same capsid, same dose, same
strain, same readout:

- **By IT:** CP040 transcript was **significantly higher** than CBA, EF1α and hSYN in cortex and
  cerebellum; comparable in hypothalamus, hippocampus and spinal cord.
- **By ICV:** CP040 was **lower** than both ubiquitous promoters in hippocampus and hypothalamus,
  lower than CBA in cortex, and lower than EF1α and hSYN in cerebellum.

**Off-target:** in liver and kidney CP040 behaved like the neuron-restricted hSYN and significantly
below both ubiquitous promoters, by both routes.

**Protein-level transduction was low and similar for all four promoters.** By ICV, the fraction of
NeuN⁺ cells expressing eGFP was 9.52% ± 5.25% (CBA), 10.00% ± 5.06% (EF1α), 14.52% ± 4.72% (hSYN)
and 13.98% ± 4.41% (CP040); astrocyte and oligodendrocyte fractions were of the same order, as were
all twelve IT values. **No promoter in this paper transduced more than about one cell in six of any
CNS cell type.**

**Headroom.** The authors state CBA at ~1,600 bp leaves ~3,400 bp of vector space. CP040 at 126 bp
returns roughly 1.5 kb of that. Note an internal ambiguity: the same paper's methods table lists
"CB — chicken β-actin promoter — 583 bp". The two numbers denote different constructs (the 583 bp
core versus the ~1,600 bp CMV-enhancer-plus-intron assembly) and a headroom calculation must say
which it means.

## 3 · What the paper does NOT state

- **No AAV9 arm**, and no comparison across capsids.
- **No dose-response** — the authors say multiple doses are deferred to future studies.
- **No therapeutic transgene** — eGFP only, stated as a limitation.
- **No large animal, no primate, no human.**
- **Maximum follow-up 9 weeks**, which the authors contrast explicitly with a human requirement of
  years to a lifetime.
- **No immunology** — "potential immune responses to prolonged CP040-driven transgene expression
  remain to be investigated".
- **No cell-type restriction claim.** CP040 expressed in neurons, astrocytes and oligodendrocytes at
  similar fractions; it is a small ubiquitous promoter, not a targeted one. Its liver/kidney
  behaviour is a strength-level property, not a demonstrated restriction mechanism.
- **Nothing about WWOX.**

## 4 · Caveats the authors themselves give

The limitations paragraph is unusually complete and is quoted in the manifest: reporter-only, mouse
only, 9 weeks only, dose-response deferred, immune response uninvestigated, large-animal and human
validation outstanding. One further caveat they state on their own dose choice: neonatal mice dilute
episomal vector as they grow, which is why the neonatal dose was ten-fold higher.

## 5 · Classification under LEGEND_CORE §13

**Neither biomarker nor endpoint.** eGFP reports promoter activity, not WWOX's functional state.
A **vector-design parameter record**.

## 6 · Transfer limits — stated exactly

1. **Capsid:** AAV-DJ. Nothing here measures AAV9, and capsid–promoter interaction is not neutral —
   the companion capsid paper in this same group of six argues the AAV9 capsid itself can modulate
   transgene transcription.
2. **Species and strain:** mouse, FVB/NJ.
3. **Cargo:** eGFP.
4. **Duration:** 9 weeks maximum, so nothing here speaks to promoter silencing, which the authors
   themselves raise as the classic failure mode of viral promoters.
5. **The route-dependence is itself the transfer limit**: a promoter ranking measured by one CSF
   route does not predict the ranking by another, in the same animals' species, at the same dose.

## 7 · Bearing on the Group B question

- **Headroom is real and cheap.** 126 bp of all-human, non-viral promoter sequence performed
  comparably to promoters an order of magnitude larger, with lower liver and kidney expression.
  For any cassette where ORF plus regulatory elements is tight, that is roughly 1.5 kb recovered.
- **But "can the cassette be specified in advance" gets a partial no from this paper**: the same
  promoter was the best and the worst of four depending only on whether it was delivered by IT or
  ICV. Promoter and route must be specified **together**, and a promoter chosen on ICV data cannot
  be carried to an IT protocol.
- **The non-viral composition is a regulatory property worth carrying**, given the FDA guidance the
  authors cite recommending non-viral promoters to reduce genotoxicity potential.
