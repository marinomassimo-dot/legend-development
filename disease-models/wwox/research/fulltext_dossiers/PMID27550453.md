## Figure and supplementary audit

The six main figures were retrieved at 1050-pixel native width and inspected as images. The 36-page
supplement was measured before rendering: pages 1–8 and 16–36 were vector/text; page ceilings 9–15
were 137.9, 203.1, 82.3, 220, 40.3, 69.5 and 220 ppi. Each raster page was rendered at or below its
ceiling and inspected.

Figure 3 draws the decisive comparisons explicitly. In early Osx1-lineage cells, DKO reaches 23/23
tumours with median 260 days versus 7/11 and median 325 days for p53-only (P=.003). In mature
Oc-lineage cells, DKO is 8/10 with median 288.5 days versus 9/11 and 295 days for p53-only, with no
significant difference. This is direct stage-specific evidence, not a missing-comparison pattern.

Supplementary Table S6 conflicts internally with the main cytogenetic prose. The body says twenty
metaphases of each cell type and describes 6/20 versus 3/20. Table S6 totals 20 DKO spreads but 30
p53-only spreads, and distinguishes break-bearing spreads from total breaks (eight in DKO). The
phrase “6 breaks per cell out of 20 cells” is not a coherent representation of that table.

## Scientific verdict

Wwox loss alone impairs osteoblast differentiation and mineralisation but did not produce
osteosarcoma in 0/15 Osx1-lineage knockouts. Wwox and p53 double loss accelerates tumour formation
when imposed in early osteoprogenitors, but adds no detectable acceleration when imposed in mature
osteoblasts/osteocytes. The same genetic interaction therefore changes with lineage stage. This is
an unusually clean demonstration that the tissue in which a phenotype is measured is not enough:
the developmental cell population in which WWOX is required determines the tumour effect.

Tumour-derived DKO cells are less differentiated, more proliferative, chemoresistant and show
altered DNA-damage responses. Human osteosarcoma IHC finds low/absent WWOX more often in p53-positive
cases, but p53 mutation status is inferred from staining and the 57-sample association is not a
causal experiment.

## Tensions, transfer and hidden gold

- p53 deletion partially rescues the early Wwox differentiation defect even though the combined
  deletion accelerates transformation; differentiation and tumour suppression are not interchangeable.
- The human assay classifies p53-negative staining as WT and positive staining as mutant based on
  prior work, an imperfect surrogate that can misclassify null and non-stabilising variants.
- The cytogenetic text/table mismatch prevents a precise numerical claim about break frequency.
- **Disease transfer:** low to WOREE but high conceptual value for tissue/stage necessity.
  **Genotype transfer:** low; lineage-specific double deletion differs from germline biallelic loss.
  **Intervention transfer:** none; no therapeutic manipulation is tested.
