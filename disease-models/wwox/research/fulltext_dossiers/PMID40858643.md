## 1 · What the paper did

Short-read genome sequencing with five SV callers, targeted long-read sequencing, optical genome mapping and
LCL RNA-seq for selected cases. 55 of 260 families carry potentially disease-related variants (Table 2). The text
develops non-WWOX cases (RNU4-2, CHASERR, ATP6V0C, TBL1XR1, NR2F1, STXBP1, CHD3).

## 3 · What the paper does NOT state

- No RNA analysis for WWOX (RNA-seq was on LCLs of selected other cases), so neither exon-6 skipping nor
  cryptic-acceptor use is shown for `c.517-1G>A`.
- No frame statement for the exon-5 deletion and no protein measurement for either allele.
- No narrative phenotype, age, course or outcome for the WWOX case.
- No reverse-phenotyping step: "Unclassified (Epilepsy)" is the referral-category column, not a re-diagnosis.

## 4 · Caveats the authors give themselves

- Cohort referral bias toward epilepsy clinics and suspected known syndromes; most patients had no prior
  microarray; labour-intensive manual SV inspection may raise yield above routine practice.
- Novel candidates still need functional validation.

## 5 · Allele class and transfer limit

`c.517-1G>A` changes the same intron-5 acceptor dinucleotide as `c.517-2A>G`, the allele of the patient-derived
organoid line in PMID 34268881 (checked in that held artefact). A same-site allele is still a different allele;
nothing measured for one is carried to the other. Neither allele is in the reference genotype's class (the
reference splice allele named in LEGEND's ASO skill is an intron-8 acceptor; a donor allele is a different class
again). **No transfer.**
