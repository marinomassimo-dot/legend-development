## What the paper did
Cross-sectional referral cohort, 2010–2025, 107 patients (106 families) with radiologically confirmed CCA; karyotype in all, FISH in 24, array-CGH (Agilent 4×44K, coordinates hg18) in 54, MLPA in one, WES in six unresolved patients. P/LP CNVs in 10/54 arrays.

## WWOX content (class level)
- **Table 3, one row:** a male infant ("M 1 M" — INFERENZA: one month, by the table's own M = months convention), "Dysgenesis of CC", phenotype "Hydrocephaly / Microcephaly", karyotype 46,XY, array "Arr16q(74,718,513 87,891,103)x1" (the separator between the two coordinates is missing in the deposited XML; the Size cell reads "L" instead of a size), genes of interest "WWOX ANKRD11 ZNF778 CDH15 CDH13", **de novo**, ACMG **P**, "Consistent with 16q23q24 microdeletion syndrome, including corpus callosum dysgenesis".
- **What this is:** a ~13 Mb **heterozygous** (x1) contiguous-gene deletion of 16q that removes one whole copy of WWOX together with ANKRD11, CDH13 and many other genes. **It is not a WWOX-DEE allele-class observation:** one WWOX copy is deleted, and nothing about the patient's phenotype can be assigned to WWOX (ANKRD11 haploinsufficiency alone is a recognised syndrome; INFERENZA from general knowledge, not from this paper).
- **What was examined in the carrier, and what was not:** examined — karyotype, array-CGH, inheritance (de novo). **Not examined — the remaining WWOX allele.** WES was done in six patients; Table 1 lists the four with variants (P7, P16, P17, P18) and this patient is not among them; the paper reports no sequencing of the non-deleted WWOX copy, no parent-of-origin, no epilepsy, no EEG, no course.
- **Figure 7A (STRING network, read as image):** WWOX is drawn linked to CYFIP1 and AFG3L2 — database-derived edges; the authors call these analyses "exploratory" and "not independent evidence of pathogenicity".
- WWOX appears twice in the XML (Table 3 row; nowhere in the running text). The paper says nothing about WWOX beyond listing it in the deleted interval.

## What the paper does NOT state
Any relationship with other Tunisian or North-African WWOX reports; the deletion's breakpoints at base resolution; the second WWOX allele; follow-up.

## Authors' caveats
Hospital-based referral bias; testing not uniform (array in 54, WES in 6); 15-year technology drift; incomplete segregation; no functional validation.

## Limits that bind

A multigene contiguous deletion in a cohort with callosal abnormalities cannot isolate WWOX; the other allele
and RNA and protein are not examined; figures are cytogenetic, not phenotypic. Nothing here supports or
refutes "one copy preserves some endpoints".
