## What the paper did
Retrospective, 27-centre real-world study (May 2019 – March 2024). Inclusion: a P/LP (ACMG) monogenic cause, >99 % highly purified CBD (two products), ≥ 3 months of follow-up. Baseline = monthly seizure frequency over the 3 months before CBD. Seizure counts from diaries and clinical records; outcomes at 3, 6, 12 and 24 months, analysed at the **last follow-up visit** (truncated at 24 months). CGI-I assigned **retrospectively** from records. Mixed model with centre as random effect.

## What it measured, and the WWOX row
- 266 patients, 77 genes; median age at CBD 12 years (IQR 7–19); median 3 concurrent ASMs; median 7 prior ASMs. Mean seizure reduction 38.6 %, ≥ 50 % responders 47.5 %, seizure-free 7.4 %.
- **Table 2, row `WWOX (3 pts)`:** mean seizure reduction **41.7 % (SD 38.2)**; 50 % responder rate **2/3**; CGI improvement **2/3**. Cell-wise extraction confirmed the four cells (`WWOX (3 pts) | 41.7 (38.2) | 2/3 | 2/3`).
- Columns are **mean reduction, ≥ 50 % responder rate, CGI improvement — all at last follow-up.** They are *not* responder counts at two timepoints (the selection record's wording is wrong on this point).
- Footnote: percentages only for groups of more than five patients — rows of three are fractions by design.
- Appendix S1: the 15 seizure-worsening patients (by gene) and the 19 CGI-worsened patients (by gene) are listed; **neither list names WWOX**. Table S1 lists only single-patient genes; WWOX is not among them (it is a three-patient gene).

## What it does NOT state about the three WWOX patients
Allele pair or allele class, zygosity, sex, age at onset, age at CBD, epilepsy syndrome (IESS/other DEE), baseline frequency, CBD dose, concomitant ASMs, duration of follow-up, which centre, retention, adverse events, or whether the 2/3 responders are the same two as the 2/3 CGI-improved. No per-patient value at all. The worsening lists exclude WWOX by omission only — no WWOX patient is stated to have *not* worsened.

## Caveats the authors give
Retrospective design with recall bias and heterogeneous collection; no standardised side-effect questionnaire; and, verbatim, that in subgroups of two or three patients "the observed response rates may be influenced by chance or individual patient characteristics rather than the underlying genetic cause". Responder status and CGI-I agree only moderately (kappa .44).

## Reader's limits on the WWOX row (not the authors')
- n = 3, open-label, no comparator; CBD added to a median of three ASMs, so a reduction cannot be attributed to CBD alone.
- A mean of 41.7 % with SD 38.2 over three values is compatible with very different individual courses (e.g. one near-zero and two large reductions); the row gives no individual values. **INFERENZA:** with 2/3 ≥ 50 % responders, at least one patient had < 50 % reduction.
- Last-follow-up analysis with a 3-month minimum: follow-up length for these three is unknown.
- The cohort's median age at CBD is 12 years; the WWOX patients' ages are not given, and a WWOX-DEE patient surviving to CBD start is a survivor-selected subset of a disorder with early mortality (INFERENZA, not stated by the paper).
- **Do not extrapolate from three patients.** The row is a real-world effectiveness observation, not a genotype-specific efficacy result, and says nothing about the reference genotype class.

## Comparison with held sources (after the first pass) — can these three be held patients?
- No held WWOX source is cited by this paper; its only WWOX statement is the table row.
- Oliver 2023 (PMID 36779245, receipt-complete; **under PUBLICATION_INTEGRITY_HOLD**) reports 4 of 13 WWOX-DEE patients started cannabidiol and all four continued — **continuation, not response**. Its author centres (Melbourne, Rome Bambino Gesù, Rome Tor Vergata, Berlin, Paris, Lyon, Montreal, Seattle, Memphis, Sydney) do **not** coincide with the CBD study's listed centres by name. Overlap is therefore not supported by shared centre; it cannot be excluded either (no identifiers, ages or alleles to match). **INFERENZA:** overlap unlikely but undetermined.
- Riva 2022 (PMID 35573960, held via `analysis/woree_phenotypic_approach_audit_20260921.md`): one severe WOREE case with "CBD oil" listed among ineffective agents. Three of that paper's authors are also authors of the CBD study, and their institution is a contributing CBD centre. Whether that child later received >99 % purified CBD and entered this cohort cannot be determined from either text; the product named there is "CBD oil", not the purified product this study requires. **INFERENZA:** possible overlap of one patient; undetermined.
- Count rule applied: these three are counted **once, as an unlinked aggregate of three**, never added to held per-patient CBD observations (4/4 continuation; one "CBD oil" ineffective) as if independent.
