## What the paper did

AAV9 vectors encoding HA-tagged *S. aureus* Cas9 under either a neuron-selective human synapsin I
promoter (AAV-SynI-Cas9) or a cytomegalovirus promoter (AAV-CMV-Cas9) were injected into wild-type
C57BL/6J mice either intracerebroventricularly at postnatal day 2–3 or intracortically (and, in one
arm, intracerebroventricularly) in adults. Controls: empty-vector AAV, saline, untreated, and
AAV-EGFP in adults. Readouts at one month (and three months for the neonatal cohort): Cas9-HA
immunofluorescence and mRNA, NeuN density, Iba1, GFAP, MHC II, CD3, cytokine RT-qPCR, and serum
anti-Cas9 IgG by dot blot. A final arm primed at P2–3 and redosed at P50–65 in the other hemisphere.

## What the paper states

- **Adult CNS:** no detectable Cas9 protein by either route, with Cas9 mRNA present but very low;
  neuronal density down 30.6% versus empty vector, 32.3% versus saline and 34.8% versus untreated for
  the SynI vector (25.1% versus untreated for CMV); Iba1 cell counts up 139.6% versus empty vector;
  GFAP intensity up 334.5% versus empty vector for SynI; MHC II+ cells 21,677/mm³ versus 3,250
  (empty vector) and 1,422 (untreated); CD3+ T cells ~3,386 and ~3,358/mm³ versus 593, 73 and 68 in
  controls. The inference drawn is that Cas9-expressing neurons were eliminated by a cytotoxic
  response before collection.
- **Neonatal CNS:** robust Cas9 expression maintained at three months, no neuronal loss, a modest and
  transient microglial elevation (resolved by three months), MHC II "virtually absent", no detectable
  CD3 response, and no astrocyte activation.
- **The window buys cellular, not humoral, tolerance.** Cas9-reactive serum IgG was raised after
  *both* neonatal and adult injection: "neither treatment time point circumvented eliciting a humoral
  immune response", and at three months after neonatal dosing the antibody signal was significant for
  both promoters (p = 0.007 and p = 0.002 versus empty vector).
- **The window does not transfer to a later dose.** After a neonatal prime, an adult redose still cut
  neuronal density by 25.3% (p = 0.006) and raised microglial numbers by 70.2% (p = 0.004); Cas9 was
  detectable in the adult-injected hemisphere in only three of seven mice. Prior exposure "lowers,
  but does not entirely eliminate" the toxicity. The astrocytic response was the component that
  looked blunted (+196%, not significant).
- **"Foreign protein" is not one category.** AAV-EGFP in the adult brain gave strong expression with
  no neuronal loss and no glial activation versus empty vector. A jellyfish protein was tolerated in
  the same compartment, at the same age, where a bacterial protein was not.
- The AAV9 capsid and genome alone were weak immunogens here: of the two microglial metrics only
  Iba1+ cell counts rose in neonates versus untreated (+20.2%), and the authors suggest this may be
  an advantage of intra-CSF over intravenous delivery.
- Cytokine transcripts (TNFα, IL-1β, CCL2, IFNγ, IL-10, TGF-β) were **not** elevated in any group at
  either age, despite the histological activation.
- The HA tag is not the explanation: a tag-free SynI-SaCas9 construct also produced no detectable
  Cas9 in the adult cortex.
- A mechanistic warning about promoter choice: because tolerance is thought to need antigen-specific
  CD4+ regulatory T cells, which require MHC II presentation, and neurons do not express MHC II,
  "the use of the SynI promoter may have hampered the development of Cas9-reactive Tregs". Consistent
  with this, the CMV vector — which expressed mainly in non-neuronal cells — produced the *higher*
  anti-Cas9 antibody titre after neonatal dosing and the *lower* astrocyte response in adults.

## What the paper does NOT state

- **Age and dose are not matched.** Because the two-month-old brain is about four times the P2 brain,
  "the doses administered to adult mice were four times higher than to neonatal mice". The adult arm
  therefore received four times more antigen as well as being older, and the experiment does not
  separate antigen load from immune maturity. The paper reports the design openly but never names it
  as a limitation of the age comparison.
- **Age and route are also not matched** in the quantified immune arms: neonates were injected
  intracerebroventricularly, adults intracortically — a parenchymal needle track — with saline and
  empty-vector controls as the only control for that difference. The adult intracerebroventricular
  arm was run only for the Cas9-absence finding.
- It does not test a self protein, a human protein in a humanised or knockout background, or a
  protein absent in the host because of a null mutation. Every animal is a wild-type mouse, and the
  antigen is bacterial. There is no arm that models a patient who lacks a human protein.
- It does not test any immunosuppression regimen, so it cannot say whether the neonatal advantage is
  additive to, or redundant with, the drugs actually used in patients.
- It reports no functional or behavioural outcome: neuronal density and glial markers are the
  toxicity endpoints; no motor, cognitive or electrophysiological measure appears.
- It measures only the somatosensory and motor cortex near the injection, at essentially one or two
  time points, so the kinetics asserted (a T-cell peak before collection, a response "winding down")
  are inferences, not measurements. The authors say so.
- It gives no vector dose in vg/kg, no empty-capsid fraction and no human translation.
- It says nothing about *WWOX*.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

Mice were not germ-free, so pre-existing immunity to *S. aureus* Cas9 may have amplified the adult
response and may contribute to the measured antibody levels; previous Cas9 CNS studies reported
weaker responses than this one; the scarcity of T cells at collection is read as the tail of a
response whose peak was missed; the GFAP difference between promoters in adults is explained
post hoc by an earlier onset and resolution with CMV; the neuron-specific promoter may itself have
impeded tolerance induction. Group sizes are small (N = 3–6 per cohort for the three-month analysis;
n = 5–11 for the dot blots; seven mice in the redose arm), and the CD3 analysis reached significance
by one-way ANOVA but not in any pairwise comparison, "likely due to the high variability".
