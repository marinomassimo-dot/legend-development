## 1 · What the paper did

Whole-exome sequencing of **204 individuals** selected for HDL-C below the 10th percentile of a
single research cohort, adjusted for the hyperbolic HDL-C/triglyceride relationship. Two analyses:
a candidate-gene filter (594 lipid genes; a 104-gene HDL subset) with a ten-tool damaging
prediction at the 80th percentile, and a one-tailed binomial burden test against de-novo
mutability.

## 2 · The WWOX content, at class level

WWOX appears **three times in the body and three times in the variant tables**, and nowhere else.

- It is listed among the eleven HDL candidate genes with the highest occurrence of damaging
  variants, at **n = 4**.
- Supplemental Table S4 ("Rare heterozygous damaging variants among HDL candidate genes" — printed
  as "Supplemental Table S34") resolves that 4 into **three distinct heterozygous missense alleles
  in four participants**:

| Allele | gnomAD | rsID as printed | ClinVar | Damaging predictions | Participants |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p.Gly30Arg | 5.61 × 10⁻⁶ | rs126890262 (S3) / rs1268902626 (S4) | none | 10/10 | 1 |
| p.Arg120Trp | **7.50 × 10⁻³** | rs141361080 | **other \| Benign \| Benign** | 7.2/10 | 2 |
| p.Leu307Val | 5.28 × 10⁻⁵ | rs200320711 | none | 9.5/10 | 1 |

  No loss-of-function allele, no frameshift, no splice allele, no homozygote, no compound.
- WWOX is **not** among the genes reaching genome-wide significance (ABCA1, LDLR, HK3, CFTR).
- **No copy-number event was found in any of the 104 HDL candidate genes**, so no WWOX deletion or
  duplication — an earned null, inside the stated sensitivity limit of exome-based CNV calling.

## 3 · What it states about WWOX

Nothing causal. Beyond the table rows, one conditional sentence in the Discussion: *"A critical
structural or functional role for the WWOX gene in the CNS **would account for** the observation
that mutations at that locus are associated with neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative
disorders, including Alzheimer disease."* That is speculation about an external literature.

## 4 · What it does NOT state

- It does not report the HDL-C, LDL-C or triglyceride value of any WWOX carrier, nor compare WWOX
  carriers with non-carriers. Those comparisons are made for ABCA1, LDLR and ABCC1/2/3 only.
- It does not claim an association for WWOX; the authors' own significance test excludes it.
- It has **no normal-HDL-C comparison cohort at all**: *"we were not able to compare the prevalence
  of mutations among a cohort considered to have normal levels."* Without a denominator, four
  carriers in 204 is not an enrichment.
- "Probably damaging" is a prediction from ten in-silico tools, not an assay. The two-participant
  allele is annotated **Benign** in ClinVar and is two orders of magnitude commoner than the other
  two.

## 5 · Caveats the authors give

Discovery study, no power calculation; ACMG guidelines deliberately not followed; exome CNV calling
acknowledged as insensitive; **five kinships inside the cohort** (sibling pairs and parent–child pairs,
enumerated in the paper), so the 204 participants are not fully independent observations.

## 6 · What I did not read

Figure panels gr1–gr3 were not inspected as images (their legends were read). Supplemental Tables
S1, S2, S5 and S6 were read only for WWOX rows and gene-list membership, not row by row.

## 7 · Verdict

**INGEST — as a bounded negative.** Its value to the carrier question is that it is the sharpest
available *failure* to find a WWOX heterozygote signal where one was looked for, and it must never
be cited as four carriers with a biochemical abnormality attributable to WWOX.

## What the panels and supplement show

1. **Figure 1:** WWOX bar = 4, entirely missense; agrees with the text (n = 4) and with three distinct missense
   alleles in Tables S3-S4. The header says "144 occurrences in 101 patients" whereas the text says 110
   participants with at least one variant (31 with two, 10 with three, implying about 151 to 161 occurrences)
   and 120 variants (116 heterozygous, 4 homozygous): cohort totals are not reconciled in the paper; the WWOX
   count is unaffected.
2. **Figure 3:** the Q-Q gene tables show ABCA1 and LDLR (MAF <= 0.0001) and ABCA1, LDLR, HK3, CFTR (MAF <=
   0.001) as the significant rows; no WWOX row or statistic anywhere in the figure or text.
3. **Neurological phenotype:** none. The cohort is selected for low HDL-C, so the lipid phenotype is constant by
   design; no per-carrier lipid value, no neurological or developmental assessment. This strengthens `CLAIM
   045`'s "nobody looked" premise rather than resolving it.
4. **Supplement:** the p.Arg120Trp row (gnomAD 7.50e-3, 2 participants) is "other|Benign|Benign" and its ClinVar
   disease column lists WWOX-related condition names (an early infantile epileptic encephalopathy and an
   autosomal recessive ataxia) beside the Benign classification, i.e. a list of conditions, not a pathogenicity
   assertion. 7.5e-3 is 7.5 times the study's rarity threshold (1e-3) and 0.75 of its 1e-2 filter (recomputed),
   which agrees with the record.
5. **Arithmetic screen:** 80 + 23 + 77 + 24 = 204; printed group percentages 57.5, 34.8, 58.4, 45.8 recompute
   exactly from 46/80, 8/23, 45/77, 11/24.

## Limits that bind

A lipid discovery cohort; "probably damaging" is a ten-tool prediction; no comparison cohort; heterozygous
missense alleles only, none of them the reference genotype's alleles; no CNV event in the candidate genes
(exome-based calling).
