## What the paper did

A single case report: one boy, non-consanguineous parents, severe developmental delay from birth, congenital and acquired comorbidities, West syndrome (spasms noticed at about 10 months, diagnosed at 1 y 4 mo with mild hypsarrhythmia), later EEG features the authors read as evolving toward Lennox-Gastaut syndrome. Genetic work-up and a drug history.

## What was examined in the carrier — the point of this record

1. **The deletion.** Exon-targeted panel sequencing (TruSight One, 4,813 genes) with exon-level CNV calling found a heterozygous interstitial deletion; chromosomal microarray (CytoScan HD) fine-mapped it to **16q22.2-q23.1, 6.8 Mb, 57 RefSeq genes**, hg19 71,689,186-78,530,357. **Both breakpoints lie inside genes, the distal one inside WWOX**: one copy of the 5′ part of WWOX is deleted. The paper does not say which WWOX exons the deletion removes.
2. **The other WWOX allele** was examined by the same exon-targeted panel, with every sequenced position of the 12 panel genes inside the deletion inspected in IGV for no-call artefacts. **No pathogenic variant.** Not examined: introns (the panel captures exons), structural variants below microarray resolution on the other allele, RNA or protein. In this design the remaining WWOX copy is hemizygous across the deleted span, so its exons there were in effect read without a second copy to mask a variant.
3. **Parental DNA unavailable** — inheritance of the deletion unknown.
4. **Other genes:** of 37 OMIM genes in the interval only 12 are on the panel; FA2H (recessive) was examined and clean. The authors say genes not on the panel may explain the phenotype and suggest exome or genome sequencing.
5. **Databases:** a DECIPHER search found no case with epilepsy and a deletion largely overlapping this one; it found two cases with a smaller deletion partially containing WWOX coding exons, inherited from a parent, with the **E17K** missense on the other allele.

## What the paper states

- The relationship between WWOX (or neighbouring-gene) haploinsufficiency and West syndrome "remains unclear"; other genes "within or outside" the deletion may be responsible.
- Seizures and EEG abnormalities stopped after valproate plus lamotrigine, following inadequate responses to phenobarbital, valproate and zonisamide (zonisamide stopped for a side effect); seizure-free at 4 y 7 mo. One patient, uncontrolled.

## What the paper does NOT say

- It does not attribute the phenotype to WWOX and does not claim WWOX haploinsufficiency causes epilepsy.
- It does not report any WWOX expression measurement.

## Overlap and counting

Not counted as a WWOX-DEE case (biallelic loss not shown). Not in the 2019 EIMFS consortium cohort (that cohort's 6.8 Mb deletion is on chromosome 2 and it recruited no patient from this case's country). The two DECIPHER carriers of a WWOX-coding deletion plus E17K are database entries, not literature records; whether either is the E17K carrier of Piard 2019 (patient 12, whose second allele is a missense) or of the 2023 cohort cannot be settled from what is printed, and their identifiers are not carried in this public edition.

## Transfer limit

A single-copy deletion of part of WWOX inside a 6.8 Mb, 57-gene deletion is not a model of WWOX haploinsufficiency: any phenotype has 56 other candidate genes, and an absent phenotype would not show sufficiency either. The datum the record supports is narrow: **a heterozygous WWOX-disrupting CNV with an exon-level-clean second allele was found in a child with West syndrome, and the authors declined to call it WWOX disease.**
