## Verdict

Global `Wwox` loss is associated at juvenile age with small gonads, sparse/small Leydig cells, undetectable serum testosterone, smaller ovarian follicles, reduced theca-cell Ki67, reduced pituitary Fsh/Lh and broad shifts in gonadal steroidogenic transcripts. The direct observation is strong for a multi-level endocrine phenotype. The causal label “Wwox is essential for steroidogenesis” is less specific: every animal is also growth-retarded and near lethal systemic metabolic failure, and the design cannot separate gonadal cell autonomy, pituitary input, developmental delay and generalized illness.

The in-vitro MLTC-1 result adds a domain clue, not a complete mechanism. Wild-type Wwox raises several `Hsd` transcripts; Y33R, which disrupts WW1/PPxY binding, moves them oppositely, while Y61R does not. No physical partner is identified, no steroid output is measured in vitro, and Cyp11a1/Cyp17a1 do not respond to transfection.

## Design and limits

- Mixed C57BL/6J × 129/SvJ background and juvenile global knockout.
- Histology was assessed by two pathologists, but group sizes for most histological/qPCR comparisons and sex-specific ages are not reported.
- Microarray: ovary 3 HET vs 3 KO; testis 4 WT, 2 HET, 6 KO. WT and HET were pooled for fold differences; 90% confidence that FDR was below 10%.
- Table 1 is an expression screen, not enzyme activity or steroid flux.
- Early death precludes spermatogenesis, follicular maturation and fertility analysis.

## Horizontal impact

Supports a steroid/endocrine branch of `CLAIM 036`, but also supplies its own alternative explanation: pituitary Fsh/Lh reduction can mediate gonadal changes. It does not license transfer of juvenile global-null endocrine values to the reference genotype. Field density is sparse (`WWOX AND steroidogenesis`: 3 PubMed records on 2026-08-11), making the paper important but not independently replicated by a dense literature.
