## What it measures

System: 801 unrelated Spanish adults from one population-based survey (433 men, 368 women); fasting HDL-C and triglycerides, 175 Affymetrix SNPs passing QC across the 1.1 Mb gene, imputed to 1045 common SNPs (151 genotyped, 894 imputed, all MAF 0.10 or more); linear regression adjusted for sex, age, BMI, smoking, alcohol, activity; adaptive permutation P; haplotype tests in four regions. Genotype: common intronic and promoter-proximal variants. No coding variant, no loss-of-function allele, no carrier phenotype, no expression or functional assay.

Observations (DATO, from the source):
- Four clusters of nominal signals (regions 1-4). Region 1 spans promoter to intron 4 (TG best SNP P 0.0002, beta -13.81); regions 2-4 lie in intron 8. Best HDL SNP: P 0.003 (region 3, beta -2.25).
- Haplotype tests: region 1 significant for TG (0.003), HDL (0.018) and TG/HDL (0.006); no intron-8 region significant for HDL; TG significant at regions 2 and 4.
- The originally reported HDL SNP of the 2008 paper was not replicated (P 0.299 in Table S2, imputed).
- No multiple-comparison correction, by declared choice; the authors state the study is underpowered below an effect size of 0.14 and that chance cannot be excluded.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **It is not evidence for loss of function.** The variants are common and non-coding; the paper has no allele that reduces WWOX dosage and no expression measurement. The "heterozygous loss" reading of the HDL association is an assumption brought to the paper, not something it tests (the authors' own functional step is a stated speculation, Discussion para 2).
2. **The HDL signal is weak by the paper's own account.** No intron-8 region is associated with HDL in the haplotype analysis; the lead HDL SNPs carry opposite signs in different regions (beta positive for rs4888865/rs4145518 in region 2, negative for rs2047927 in region 3); the replication target failed.
3. **No correction for 1045 tests.** A Bonferroni line for 1045 SNPs is about 4.8e-5 (arithmetic); the smallest P in any of the three tables is 2.4e-4 (TG, rs11645006). The nominal-hit counts from Tables S2-S4, computed by script on the strings-derived text, are 39 (HDL), 46 (TG) and 46 (TG/HDL) SNPs with P below 0.05 out of 1045: about 3.7-4.4 percent, at or under the 5 percent that independent null SNPs would give. LD makes the SNPs non-independent, so this is not a significance test; it only shows there is no excess of nominal hits over chance expectation. INFERENZA.
4. **The top signals rest on imputation.** All of the paper's headline SNPs are imputed (Table S2/S3 STATUS column), rs11645006 with Rsq 0.8756 (Table S1). Figure 1 does not distinguish genotyped from imputed points.
5. **A text-to-table inconsistency.** Body text gives beta 0.96 and 1.03 for rs4888865 and rs4145518; Table S2 gives 2.67 and 2.81 for the same SNPs (rs2047927 matches, -2.25). The unit or scale is not stated; the table, not the text, carries the CI. Reported, not resolved. Does not change a conclusion because both are nominal.
6. **No neural, no developmental, no disease cohort.** Adults without a stated neurological phenotype.

Direction check against the mouse: the introduction states that the Wwox-null mouse is hypotriglyceridemic and hypocholesterolemic. In the human table the best TG SNPs carry negative betas (lower TG per allele), which on the paper's own framing would be "in the same direction" only if the allele lowers expression, which was not measured. Not an inference this paper can bear.

## Comparison with the laboratory's records

- **Lee 2008 (PAPER 084).** This paper is the replication attempt that failed on the lead SNP. It then reports different intron-8 SNPs, in partial LD with the original (D' 0.72, text), plus a new 5' region. The laboratory's record already calls the Lee result T3 and LOW relevance; this reading supports that and adds that the second Western-European cohort did not reproduce the lead variant.
- **Iatan 2014 (PAPER 062).** The null-mouse HDL effect is whole-body only; hepatocyte deletion does not lower HDL. Nothing in this paper contradicts that; the human data here cannot discriminate tissue.
- **Dong 2022 (PAPER 156) and Liu 2021 (CORPUS-STUB-109)** carry DO_NOT_CITE notes against dose-effect reading. This paper sits in the same class (common SNPs, one population) and should carry the same caution.
