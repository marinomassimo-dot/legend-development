# PMID 35333110 — Efficacy and safety of a Krabbe disease gene therapy (Hum Gene Ther 2022)

## Identity and dedup
- DOI 10.1089/hum.2021.245; PMC9142772; first author Hordeaux, senior author Wilson (University of Pennsylvania Gene Therapy Program). No correction or retraction notice. No registry record, no receipt. Not a duplicate.
- Full text: Europe PMC REST fullTextXML; Figure 5 image and supplementary files (Data S1, S2; Tables S3, S4) via the PMC instance bin.

## What the paper did
Three studies of one capsid (AAVhu68, CB7 promoter) expressing galactosylceramidase (GALC), a secreted lysosomal enzyme:
- Newborn Twitcher mice (lateral ventricle versus intravenous): highest ICV dose 1E11 GC, median survival 130 days versus 40.5 (vehicle); the same dose intravenously 49 days.
- Pre-symptomatic Krabbe dogs (2-3 weeks old; seven enrolled: one vehicle wild type, two vehicle Krabbe, four treated): single cisterna magna (ICM) dose 3.0E13 GC in 1 mL, **no immune suppression or anti-inflammatory regimen**; treated dogs stayed ambulatory with preserved peripheral-nerve conduction; two followed to humane endpoint, two euthanised at 6 months.
- GLP toxicology in juvenile rhesus macaques (15-19 months): one ICM dose, vehicle n=2, then 4.5E12, 1.5E13, 4.5E13 GC (5.0E10, 1.7E11, 5.0E11 GC/g brain), n=6 each, **no immune suppression**, half euthanised at 3 months and half at 6 months; clinical, neurological, sensory nerve conduction, CSF and blood pathology, neutralising antibodies, T cell responses, enzyme activity, histopathology with a 0-5 severity scale.

## DRG content (the question for this wave)
- NHP histopathology: minimal sporadic DRG neuron degeneration, absent in some treated animals, minimal when present, with secondary axonopathy of central and peripheral axons; axonopathy dose dependent (most in the high-dose group); similar incidence and severity at day 90 and day 180 (no progression); asymptomatic clinically and neurologically.
- Figure 5C (inspected at native resolution): DRG neuronal degeneration scores reach at most 1 (minimal) in every group (group means about 0.1, 0.35, 0.5 for low, mid, high; vehicle 0). Dorsal-column axonopathy reaches 2 at low and mid dose and **3 (moderate) at high dose**, with a significant difference from vehicle at high dose only. The text sentence "minimal to mild when present" understates the high-dose panel (a grade-3 point is plotted); each point is one segment of one animal.
- Electrophysiology: median-nerve sensory action potential fell from 23.2 to 6.6 uV in one high-dose animal (grade 3 dorsal axonopathy and grade 2 perineurial fibrosis); other animals within the normal band at 28, 60, 90 days (Figure 5D).
- Dog arm: DRG collected from cervical, thoracic and lumbar levels showed no test-article-related findings; vector genomes highest in CNS, then DRG (0.01-0.4 GC per diploid genome) then peripheral organs. The authors argue dog is less sensitive than NHP.
- CSF: transient mild leukocyte increase on day 14 in some high-dose animals. Neutralising antibodies to the capsid in all treated animals by day 28; responses to the human transgene product (T cells 14/18) and loss of measurable circulating enzyme activity; none linked to histological findings.
- The authors conclude DRG risk "seems low" and move the candidate to a clinical trial (a registry identifier is given in the abstract).

## What the paper does NOT say
Nothing about WWOX; no cell-autonomous need (GALC is secreted and cross-corrects, so transduction need is lower than for a non-secreted protein); no dose beyond 4.5E13 GC in primates; no follow-up beyond 6 months; no immune-suppression arm for contrast; no per-animal DRG table in the text (data in Figure 5C only).

## Caveats given or visible
Small groups (n=6 per dose, 3 per time point); juvenile animals; one capsid and promoter; a secreted enzyme transgene with a foreign-species (human) protein in macaques raises transgene immunity that confounds the safety read-out; the dog arm uses canine GALC.

## Transfer limit
A secreted-enzyme cargo under one capsid and promoter, ICM route, up to 5.0E11 GC per gram brain, up to 6 months, no immune suppression, in juvenile rhesus: a DRG dose-response baseline for ICM AAVhu68. It does not characterise a WWOX cassette, a non-secreted intracellular cargo, patients, or longer horizons.
