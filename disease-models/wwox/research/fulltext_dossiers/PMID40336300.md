## Earned null for WWOX — stated plainly

**WWOX occurs zero times in this source** (0 matches for `WWOX` or `Wwox` on the raw artefact bytes).
The paper does not mention WWOX, does not test it, and does not include it in any of its
hydroxysteroid-dehydrogenase censuses: the proteomic survey of testicular hydroxysteroid
dehydrogenases detects HSD3B1, HSD3B6, HSD17B4, HSD17B7, HSD17B8, HSD17B10 and HSDL2 — and WWOX
(which carries an SDR domain) is not among the detected proteins, nor is it discussed as a candidate.

Headline results in the authors' own framing: the humanising L234F substitution in mouse HSD17B12
reduces intratesticular testosterone by about 16% in an Hsd17b3-null background without abolishing
masculinisation or fertility; HSD17B7 protein rises about 20-fold in Hsd17b3 KO testis; and **mouse,
but not human, HSD17B7 converts androstenedione to testosterone in transfected cells**.

## Why this bears on the WWOX enzymology question (transfer limit stated exactly)

The wave-10 reading of PMID 21476439 found that the WWOX SDR-substrate claim rests on one
laboratory's crude-extract assay plus motif annotations carried from a paywalled 2000 paper, and that
the regiochemistry attributed to WWOX comes from clustering WWOX with 17β-HSD enzymes — HSD17B3 above
all. This paper is the first orthogonal, non-WWOX source in this corpus that tests how much such
clustering is worth. Three transferable findings, each about **17β-HSD enzymology in general**, never
about WWOX:

1. **A single residue decides whether a C19 steroid can enter the active site at all.** Human and
   primate HSD17B12 carry a phenylalanine at position 234 that blocks C19-steroid entry; the mouse
   leucine does not. Same enzyme, same family, same fold, orthologous sequence — opposite substrate
   acceptance. **A family assignment therefore does not deliver a substrate**, and the regiochemistry
   a sequence cluster implies is a prediction, not a measurement. Transfer limit: this is a statement
   about HSD17B12 and HSD17B7 orthologues, not about WWOX, whose active-site residues this paper
   never examines.
2. **A published negative enzymology result was overturned by changing assay conditions.** Two earlier
   studies concluded that mouse HSD17B7 cannot use androstenedione; this paper reports the conversion
   and attributes the earlier negatives to lower substrate concentration and shorter incubation.
   Transfer limit: it says nothing about any WWOX assay, but it establishes that in this enzyme family
   a negative substrate result is condition-dependent — which cuts both ways for the WWOX chain,
   weakening confidence in negatives and in positives obtained under a single untitrated condition.
3. **The background problem is real and was measured.** The authors record that untransfected and
   eGFP-control HEK-293T cells produce low levels of testosterone from androstenedione, "suggesting
   some endogenous hydroxysteroid dehydrogenase activity". An activity assay in a cell lysate or whole
   cell therefore has a non-zero floor from endogenous enzymes — exactly the objection that applies to
   a crude-extract WWOX assay without a catalytically dead control.

And one further transfer, the one the paper itself writes as a protocol: to attribute a conversion to
one family member, the authors say the specific residues responsible must be identified first, then
mutated — i.e. the dead-mutant control is their own stated prerequisite, not an external demand.

## What the paper does NOT state

- Nothing about WWOX, its SDR domain, its substrates, its cofactor, or any WWOX allele
  (P47T, Q230P, G372R, A141T, P252A are all absent, as is any mention of a neurological phenotype).
- It does not measure a Km, kcat or any kinetic constant; the enzymology is a product-formation
  endpoint in transfected cells at one substrate concentration (150 ng/mL androstenedione, 24 h).
- It does not purify any enzyme; all activity is in transfected HEK-293T cells.
- It does not identify the HSD17B7 residues responsible for the androstenedione conversion — it
  names doing so as future work.
- It does not demonstrate that HSD17B7 actually carries this flux in vivo: that remains a suggestion
  ("may contribute"), since the proposed Hsd17b7-mutant cross has not been made.
- It makes no claim about human testosterone synthesis beyond the inference that HSD17B7 is "unlikely
  to contribute" in human HSD17B3 deficiency.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

- The proteomic comparison rests on n = 3 per group, with HSD17B7 undetectable in one wild-type sample.
- The testosterone reduction is "small but significant" (~16%) and does not change fertility or
  histology.
- Whether raised circulating estradiol is a direct consequence of altered enzyme activity "remains to
  be established".
- Increased 17-OH pregnenolone points to "other roles for the L234 residue in steroid metabolism" —
  i.e. the mutation is not cleanly single-function.
- The species conclusion is framed as a hypothesis about plasticity and redundancy.
