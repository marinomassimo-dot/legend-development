## What the paper did

Four children with genetically confirmed CLN7 disease (biallelic *MFSD8*), aged 4–5 years at
dosing, received a single intrathecal dose of a self-complementary AAV9 vector carrying *MFSD8*
driven by the **minimal JeT promoter**: one participant at 5 × 10^14 vg, three at 1 × 10^15 vg after
DSMB clearance. Dosing was a flat total dose, not weight-normalised (weights 15.5–24 kg at dosing).
All participants received prophylactic immunosuppression, stratified by predicted **CRIM status**:
predicted residual protein (CRIM-positive) → prednisone + sirolimus; predicted null (CRIM-negative)
→ prednisone + sirolimus + tacrolimus. Safety was the primary endpoint; motor, neuropsychological,
EEG, MRI, CSF and nerve-conduction measures were secondary.

## What the paper states

- The 1 × 10^15 vg dose is "the highest intrathecal dose utilised in any gene therapy trial to
  date", and the lot was **42% genome-containing particles**, so the delivered capsid dose was
  2.38 × 10^15 particles.
- Both dose levels were "well tolerated"; 2 of 57 reported adverse events were judged possibly or
  probably treatment-related (vomiting; CSF pleocytosis). No grade 4 or 5 events.
- The single **CRIM-positive** participant, on two immunosuppressants only, developed clinically
  asymptomatic CSF pleocytosis at day 180 *after completing the steroid taper*; it resolved on
  re-institution of steroids. A second mild pleocytosis at day 720 coincided with para-influenza.
  The three **CRIM-negative** participants on triple therapy had no positive anti-capsid or
  anti-transgene ELISpot and normal CSF.
- Serial nerve-conduction studies identified no peripheral neuropathy or altered DRG function, and
  no new autonomic dysfunction. The authors attribute DRG toxicity in the literature to *transgene
  overexpression* and name the weak JeT promoter as a possible contributor to safety.
- Efficacy: declines on Mullen developmental quotients (to day 360, then plateau), Vineland-3, GMFM
  and MRI; EEGs abnormal throughout and unchanged. Preserved respiratory function and oral feeding
  at ages 6–8 years is offered against a natural history of G-tube dependence at 8–10 years.
  3 of 4 families reported subjective myoclonic-seizure improvement.
- The window argument is made explicitly: timing "is likely to have a very large impact on the
  treatment efficacy", and future studies should identify participants earlier, "ideally
  pre-symptomatically".
- Preclinical NOAEL "exceeds the selected human dose by 3 times".

## What the paper does NOT state

- It does not report a weight-normalised dose (vg/kg) for any participant, so the dose cannot be
  compared with a preclinical vg/kg figure without computing it from Table 1.
- It does not report vector genome copies, transgene expression, or MFSD8 protein in any tissue or
  CSF compartment: there is **no measurement that the transgene was expressed in a patient**. Every
  efficacy statement is clinical or psychometric.
- It does not compare the treated cohort with a matched natural-history cohort — the authors say so
  for neuroimaging ("we cannot comment on whether the rate of progression was altered").
- It does not report any dose-response: "no noticeable difference is appreciated" between the low-
  and high-dose participants on imaging, and no efficacy measure is analysed by dose.
- It performs **no statistical testing at all** ("no formal statistics were performed").
- It does not state a maximum tolerated dose, and it did not reach one: the trial stopped at the
  dose available, limited by "the availability of the study drug".
- It does not report anti-AAV9 antibody titres as numbers, nor DRG histopathology (not obtainable in
  living children) — the DRG statement rests on nerve conduction and examination only.
- It says nothing about *WWOX*, and nothing about any gene other than *MFSD8*.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

Small sample size; open-label and non-randomised; no imputation and "the extent of missing data was
not systematically tracked"; data recorded on paper first; cognitive measures at the floor of the
instrument by day 180–360, so the Mullen composite "does not provide sufficient information"; one
family declined the day-540 and day-720 visits; the CRIM-positive participant's steroid taper was
not adhered to as prescribed and the child had multiple viral infections in the same period; the
regional-transduction and regional-neurodegeneration explanations for partial benefit are offered as
alternatives that the study cannot separate.

## Caveat found by measurement, not stated by the authors

Table 2 does not reconcile with itself or with the text. The event counts sum to **58**, while the
text says "57 adverse events"; the percentage column is computed on a denominator of 58; and two
rows disagree with their own percentages — "Nervous system disorders 7 … 8.6%" (8.6% of 58 = 5) and
"General disorders … 18 … 34.4%" (34.4% of 58 = 20), the latter also carrying the relatedness
denominator "0/20" against a count of 18. Command:

```
python3 -c "
c=[10,9,7,2,6,1,5,18]; p=[17.2,15.5,8.6,3.4,10.3,1.7,8.6,34.4]
print(sum(c), [round(x*58/100,1) for x in p])"
```

The grade distribution is likewise reported as percentages of each dose group's events without the
denominators ("90% at the low dose and 72% at the high dose"), so the grade-2 and grade-3 counts
cannot be rebuilt per dose from the published table.
