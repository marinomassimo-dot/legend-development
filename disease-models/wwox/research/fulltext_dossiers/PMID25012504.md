# PMID 25012504 — Abu-Remaileh & Aqeilan 2014

## 1. Evidence depth

- **Title:** *Tumor suppressor WWOX regulates glucose metabolism via HIF1α modulation*
- **Study type:** primary mechanistic study.
- **Models:** primary Wwox-null mouse embryonic fibroblasts; Wwox-null mice; MCF7 and HEK293T cells; transformed MEF xenografts; human breast-cancer tissue microarray.
- **Species/stage:** mouse embryonic cells and young postnatal null mice; adult human tumour samples.
- **Alteration:** complete Wwox loss, WWOX knockdown, full-length rescue and artificial WW1 mutant W44F/P47A (`WFPA`).
- **Full text:** found and read in full from structured PMC HTML; 10-page PDF reconciled; all 33 main-figure and 18 supplementary panels inspected; all six supplementary legends read.
- **Evidence strength:** high for a Wwox-null/HIF1α metabolic relationship in MEFs; moderate for the precise degradation mechanism; low for neurological or reference-genotype translation.

## 2. Core message

WWOX loss in MEFs increases glucose uptake and lactate, lowers oxygen consumption and ATP, raises normoxic HIF1α and several HIF1α target transcripts, and increases tumour dependence on HIF1α. Full-length WWOX, but not W44F/P47A, binds HIF1α and rescues multiple readouts. The study therefore supports a WWOX–WW1/HIF1α–glycolysis axis in null cells.

## 3. Deep biological dive

The strongest chain is `WWOX loss → normoxic HIF1α accumulation/activity → GLUT1/LDHA/PDK1/HK2/PKM2 induction → glucose uptake and glycolytic routing`. PDK1 is especially relevant because it can inhibit PDH and restrict pyruvate entry into the TCA cycle. Direct flux evidence comes from reduced Seahorse OCR and lower ATP; metabolite profiling adds fumarate and malate reduction plus fructose-1,6-bisphosphate elevation. Succinate is only a trend (`P<0.09`), despite the plural “reduced TCA intermediates” in the prose.

The binding determinant is the first WW domain, not the SDR domain. GST constructs containing WW1 pull down HIF1α, whereas the W44F/P47A mutant does not. This says nothing directly about Q230P: Q230 lies in the SDR, but the reference-genotype protein may be absent or unstable, so an intact WW1 sequence does not guarantee retained function.

The proposed degradation route is less complete than the headline mechanism. Wwox-null cells show less hydroxylated HIF1α and more total HIF1α; interaction becomes easiest to observe after MG132. PHD2 activity, HIF1α ubiquitination and degradation kinetics were not directly manipulated or measured. The authors themselves leave whether WWOX affects PHD2 activity unresolved.

## 4. DATO

- Wwox-null MEFs show approximately 2.2-fold glucose uptake, rescued by WWOX re-expression.
- KO MEFs show lactate increase, basal/maximal OCR reduction, ATP and NADH reduction, NADPH increase and altered metabolites.
- Normoxic nuclear HIF1α is higher in KO MEFs; skeletal muscle KO lysates show stronger HIF1α.
- Multiple HIF1α target transcripts, including PDK1, rise in KO cells and tissues.
- Full-length WWOX, but not WFPA, reduces HIF1α-associated readouts and glucose uptake.
- HIF1α knockdown suppresses the KO glucose-uptake phenotype and strongly reduces KO xenograft growth.
- A single acute digoxin experiment (`n=3/group`, 40 minutes) raises KO blood glucose. The reported dose is `100 mg/kg` in both HTML and PDF.
- Breast-cancer TMA: GLUT1 positivity is 6/51 among WWOX-positive samples and 9/29 among WWOX-negative samples (`P<0.05`).

## 5. INFERENZA

- The HIF1α branch is a real downstream consequence of complete WWOX loss in proliferative cells, not merely a tumour correlation.
- PDK1 induction provides a plausible bottleneck at PDH, but PDH phosphorylation/activity and carbon flux through PDH were not measured.
- Q230P could preserve the WW1 binding surface only if enough correctly localized protein survives; this is a testable conditional inference, not a property of the allele.
- The acute digoxin result validates HIF1α as a manipulable node in this mouse phenotype; it does not validate digoxin as a neurological or chronic therapeutic option.

## 6. IPOTESI / espansione

- Stage-matched Seahorse and isotope tracing in donor-derived cells or WWOX-deficient neural models could distinguish direct metabolic control from altered developmental state.
- Co-IP and HRE reporter assays with Q230P at matched protein abundance could test whether the WW1/HIF1α function survives an SDR lesion.
- PDH phosphorylation, PDK activity and labelled pyruvate/ketone flux would directly test the proposed bypass logic behind ketogenic or PDK-directed hypotheses.

## 7. Context integration

The study supports `DL-MECH-020`, `DL-MECH-028` and the PDK1 premise of `HYP-20260709-01`, but it also requires corrections now recorded in `DL-MECH-095`: in vivo the paper measures acute blood glucose, not glucose uptake; digoxin-associated transcript changes are GLUT1 and PHD3, not PDK1; and the reported dose remains unresolved. It does not resolve `DL-MECH-094`, because its direct flux evidence is in MEFs rather than neural tissue. Human metabolic screens in the WWOX disease corpus remain largely normal.

## 8. Reference-genotype relevance

- **Genotype:** low-to-moderate; null model versus compound N/M reference class.
- **Model:** low for CNS; moderate as a mechanistic cell assay.
- **Developmental stage:** uncertain; embryonic fibroblasts and moribund null mice do not reproduce a living human neural developmental trajectory.
- **Pathway relevance:** moderate-high as a falsifiable functional readout.
- **Therapeutic relevance:** low for digoxin; moderate for target-validation and assay design.
- **Biomarker relevance:** low for peripheral lactate/glucose; candidate functional cell readouts are stronger.
- **Overall:** **MODERATE**, chiefly experimental rather than clinical.

## 9. Research expansion

1. Measure WWOX abundance, localization, HIF1α interaction, HRE activity, OCR/ECAR and PDH phosphorylation in an isogenic N/M series.
2. Separate differentiation stage from metabolism in organoids before interpreting glycolysis/OXPHOS signatures.
3. Treat `WWOX–HIF1α suppression` as a functional rescue endpoint for Q230P stabilization studies, not as a blood biomarker.

## 10. Strategy space

- **Support of rationale:** ketogenic/PDH-bypass concepts gain a measured PDK1 premise, but no intervention was tested here.
- **Candidate assay:** HRE-luciferase + OCR/ECAR + phospho-PDH after allele rescue.
- **Non-operational hypothesis:** safer HIF1α/PDK modulation could be screened experimentally.
- **Excluded clinical candidate:** digoxin. Acute `n=3` glycaemia, ambiguous published dose and a narrow therapeutic index make it a target-validation reagent only.

## 11. Limits and uncertainty

- No neurons, brain tissue, seizures, myelination or developmental neurological endpoints.
- MEFs are null, proliferative and metabolically unlike mature neurons.
- Several key panels have very small `n`; supplemental digoxin gene-expression panels use `n=2/group` and show no inferential statistics.
- Supplement S5 panel titles label B as WT and C as KO, while the legend and main text assign B to KO and C to WT.
- Figure 5 promises a HIF1α-OH densitometry graph that is absent from the distributed image.
- Succinate is not significant (`P<0.09`).
- The `100 mg/kg` digoxin dose is printed in both HTML and PDF and cannot be silently corrected to µg/kg.
- One background reference is marked retracted; it is not required for the paper's primary data.

- **Working-model impact:** no immediate canonical rewrite; emphasize functional-cell validation and keep peripheral biomarkers de-ranked.

## Final verdict

This is a strong foundational null-cell paper for the WW1/HIF1α metabolic axis, but a weak direct bridge to WWOX-DEE treatment. Its best use is as a mechanistic assay blueprint. Its digoxin result is proof of target manipulability, not a treatment lead.

Nothing here is medical advice; any clinical-strategic interpretation belongs with the treating clinical team.
