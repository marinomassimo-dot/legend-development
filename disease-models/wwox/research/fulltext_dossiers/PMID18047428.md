## 3 · What is measured — and at what level

1. **WWOX IHC is present in all FTC (11/11), nearly all FA (19/20) and a minority of PTC (8/22)** (L13). Reconstructed counts from the reported percentages and group sizes; the paper prints only percentages.
2. **The intensity distribution separates PTC from both follicular-lineage groups:** 3+ in 82% FTC and 20% FA and **no** PTC (L14); absent in 0% FTC, 5% FA, 64% PTC (L16). Figure 2's printed bars agree exactly with the text (L18).
3. **Recomputed here** from those counts: PTC (none/weak) 20/22 vs FTC+FA 5/31, two-sided Fisher p=4.5×10⁻⁸; absent-vs-any 14/22 vs 1/31, p=1.6×10⁻⁶. The direction is robust to how the table is cut — which is what `= 0.000` conceals rather than shows.
4. **Within-patient comparison (n=7 pairs):** PTC absent in 5/7 and weak in 2, while the paired FA is moderate in 5/7 and strong in 2 (L23). This is the paper's strongest design element, and it rests on seven pairs.
5. **Normal thyroid always stains**, strongly in cuboidal follicular cells (L9), and staining is **exclusively cytoplasmic** in every tissue (L26).
6. **Microcarcinomas:** 7/7 with no expression (L20).

## 5 · Discrepancies found only in the images, tables or arithmetic

1. 🔴 **§ 5.1 — the one conflict the figure cannot settle, and why.** The Results call panel 1J *"weak staining"* (L10); the legend calls the same panel *"the same expression as normal thyroid"* (L11). These are different claims about one image. **The deposited figure is grayscale on both available routes** (L12), so DAB cannot be separated from haematoxylin and neither reading can be confirmed or refused from the pixels. Recorded as unresolved, not adjudicated.
2. 🔴 **The microcarcinoma count contradicts itself.** The Results report *"no expression in seven out of seven (100%) microcarcinomas"* (L20); the Discussion describes one of the two moderately staining PTCs as *"a 0.6 cm microcarcinoma"* (L21) — by the paper's own ≤1 cm definition, a microcarcinoma with moderate expression. The same sentence places it *"in association with an FTC"*, while the Methods list only FA-with-PTC and PTC-with-FA co-occurrences (L2). Which statement is right is not decidable from the paper.
3. **The abstract drops two cases.** For the paired comparison it says *"the same result (i.e., no expression in PTCs)"*, where the body gives no expression in 5 of 7 and weak in the other 2 (L23).
4. **Extent of staining was scored and never reported** (L7). Half the recorded measurement is absent from the paper.
5. **One p-value, printed as `= 0.000`, with no contingency table** (L17). Recomputed from the reconstructed counts, the comparison is p≈4.5×10⁻⁸ (or 1.6×10⁻⁶ absent-vs-any) — so the finding is real and the reporting is uninformative about which table was tested.
6. **53 lesions from 46 patients are treated as independent** in that test (L3); seven patients contribute two lesions each, which is also the paper's own most interesting design element (L23).
7. **One pathologist, blinding not stated** (L6); no inter-observer statistic is possible.
8. **The negative control is primary-omission only** (L5) and no negative-control image is shown, so non-specific chromogen cannot be judged.
9. **Counts are nowhere printed** — every result is a percentage, and the per-group numbers had to be reconstructed (L13-L16, L18).

## 6 · Strength, and transferability to the reference genotype

- **What is solid:** WWOX immunostaining is markedly lower in papillary thyroid carcinoma than in follicular adenoma and follicular carcinoma, in 53 lesions scored on one slide set with internal normal thyroid as positive control. The direction is large, it is consistent across every cut of the table, and the within-patient pairs point the same way. Normal follicular epithelium stains, and staining is cytoplasmic.
- **What is weak:** a single-reader, unblinded-as-stated, extent-unreported semi-quantitative IHC score; no molecular assay; non-independent lesions; one uninformative p-value; and a figure that cannot adjudicate any grade because the chromogen is gone.
- **Transferability: T3, nil toward WWOX-DEE.** Adult somatic thyroid cancer, protein expression by IHC, no germline WWOX allele, no neural material of its own, no developmental or seizure readout, no child. The paper's only neural content is a citation (L30). Its usable contribution to a disease model is limited to **parity of sources** — WWOX protein is present in normal thyroid follicular epithelium and reduced in one tumour type — plus the observation that this group's antiserum gives a cytoplasmic pattern in yet another epithelium (L26).
