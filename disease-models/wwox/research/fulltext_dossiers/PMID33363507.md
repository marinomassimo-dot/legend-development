## 1 · What the paper is

A narrative review of brain energy metabolism (glycolysis, astrocyte-neuron lactate shuttle, glycogen, ketones) and epilepsy: imaging and biochemical changes, metabolic causes (GLUT1 deficiency, hypoglycaemia, creatine disorders, mitochondrial encephalomyopathies), lactate and ATP as signals, metabolic treatment (2-deoxyglucose, LDH inhibition, KD variants, decanoic acid), antiseizure drug targets. No data of its own. WWOX does not occur.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Direction A: inhibiting glycolysis (2-DG, KATP opening, BDNF/TrkB reduction, PPP and neurosteroid route) and LDH (oxamate, stiripentol) is antiseizure in models.
2. Direction B: hypometabolic states (hypoglycaemia, GLUT1 deficiency, mitochondrial disease, creatine deficiency) cause seizures and are treated by supplying energy (ketones, creatine, glucose).
3. Lactate: raised in epileptogenic hippocampus (6.8 against 4.6 mM) and CSF in status epilepticus; its direct signalling effect is called uncertain and regionally opposite; the astrocyte-neuron lactate shuttle is acknowledged as controversial.
4. Hypoglycaemia is named as a seizure cause, more so in neonates and children, with an unclear mechanism; astrocytic glycogen-derived lactate fuels the brain in hypoglycaemia.
5. The Conclusion calls metabolic modulation a "rising panacea"; targets "deserve more attention".

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- This review makes explicit the point the lot keeps meeting: "metabolic" intervention has no single direction.
