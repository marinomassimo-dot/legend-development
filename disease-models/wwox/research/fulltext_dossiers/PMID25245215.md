## 1 · 🔴 The promise test — it delivers the content, at a weaker epistemic grade than it was given

The chapter's own closing proposal is **near-verbatim the same sentence**, with one word different:

> *"…**we propose** that the gene product of FRA16D, WWOX, is a functionally essential protein that is
> required for cell homeostasis and that its deletion has important consequences that contribute to the
> neoplastic process."* — chapter 8, abstract

**`propose` → `conclude`.** And the chapter's Concluding remarks hedge three further times in the same
direction, which is where the *evidentiary* anchor sits (the abstract is never an evidentiary surface):

- *"**It can be argued** that this phenomenon is more complicated and that gene products encompassing
  these sites **might** have important roles in stress response and in the neoplastic process."*
- *"Thus, **it can be speculated** that they function as part of a highly conserved stress response
  network…"*

> **Verdict: the chapter delivers what was promised, and the promise over-graded it.** The content is
> there in full. What the introduction reported as a conclusion, the chapter states as a proposal and
> closes as a speculation.

🔴 **The upgrade was performed by the same person.** The introduction that summarises chapter 8 is
single-authored by chapter 8's own first author. So the restatement chain carries **no independent
editorial judgement** — the summariser and the summarised are one, and `propose → conclude` is exactly
the seam `epistemic_discipline.md` and `scientist_reading_modes.md` §6.2 exist to keep visible.

**This is the fifth checkable restatement across this actor's lot, and like the four on PMID 27551470,
it changed something.**
