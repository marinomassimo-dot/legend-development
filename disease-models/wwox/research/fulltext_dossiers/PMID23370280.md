# PMID 23370280 — Salah et al. 2013

## 1. Evidence depth

- **Title:** *Tumor suppressor WWOX binds to ΔNp63α and sensitizes cancer cells to chemotherapy*
- **Study type:** primary in-vitro mechanistic oncology study.
- **Models:** HEK293/HEK293T, SaOS2 ΔNp63α Tet-On, HeLa and HaCaT cells; all lack normal p53 function by mutation, loss of expression or loss of function.
- **Perturbations:** WWOX overexpression, WWOX-Y33R, ΔNp63α-AAxA, WWOX knockdown, ITCH overexpression, MG132, cycloheximide, doxycycline and cisplatin.
- **Full text:** structured PMC XML read sequentially in full; nine-page PDF reconciled; all six figures and 23 labelled panels inspected from native fingerprinted JPEGs; no tables or supplementary material are distributed; all 66 references enumerated.
- **Evidence strength:** moderate for a WWOX–ΔNp63α interaction/competition mechanism in transformed cells; low for disease-genotype, neural, developmental or therapeutic translation.

## 2. Core message

WWOX binds ΔNp63α through its first WW domain, competes with ITCH for ΔNp63α, reduces ITCH-mediated ubiquitination and proteasomal turnover of ΔNp63α, and thereby increases ΔNp63α abundance. At the same time WWOX retains more ΔNp63α in the cytoplasm and suppresses its transcriptional output. The study therefore demonstrates that greater protein abundance can coexist with lower functional activity.

## 3. The attribution correction

The paper does **not** show ITCH stabilizing WWOX. Its measured causal object is ΔNp63α:

`WWOX binding to ΔNp63α → less ITCH–ΔNp63α interaction → less ΔNp63α ubiquitination/degradation → longer ΔNp63α half-life`.

This directly corrects the inherited phrase in `CC-20260810-41984841-01`, which says to keep “direct ITCH/proteasomal stabilization” attributed to PMID 23370280. The phrase reverses the object and obscures that WWOX antagonizes the ITCH/proteasome edge. ITCH-dependent K63 stabilization of **WWOX** belongs to a different paper and mechanism (PMID 24550385), not to Salah 2013.

## 4. DATO

- Co-immunoprecipitation detects WWOX–ΔNp63α association; WWOX-Y33R loses it.
- ΔNp63α-AAxA still associates with WWOX, so the canonical ΔNp63α PPxY motif is not required in this assay.
- WWOX reduces ITCH-driven ΔNp63α ubiquitination and the ITCH–ΔNp63α interaction; Y33R does not reproduce that competition.
- WWOX lengthens ΔNp63α half-life in a cycloheximide chase; WWOX knockdown in HaCaT cells lowers ΔNp63α abundance.
- WWOX shifts ΔNp63α toward the cytoplasm and suppresses ΔNp63α reporter activity in a dose-dependent manner.
- In the cisplatin experiments, doxycycline alone increases cell death about 2.6-fold and enhances cisplatin toxicity in control cells, so it is an explicit experimental confound.

## 5. INFERENZA

- Protein stabilization is not equivalent to functional rescue: here the stabilized partner is less transcriptionally active because localization changes.
- Any WWOX rescue/stabilization programme should measure partner occupancy, localization and downstream function alongside abundance.
- A surviving WW1 domain in Q230P would matter only if adequate, correctly localized WWOX protein exists; the paper does not test Q230P or any disease allele.
- The WWOX–ITCH–ΔNp63α result constrains interaction logic, but does not validate ITCH inhibition as a way to raise WWOX.

## 6. IPOTESI / expansion

- Compare wild-type and Q230P WWOX at matched protein abundance for ΔNp63α binding, competition with ITCH, partner localization and transcriptional output.
- Add subcellular localization and functional reporters to abundance-based rescue screens, because an abundance-only assay could call a nonfunctional state “rescued”.
- Encode ubiquitin mechanisms as directed triples (`E3 → substrate`, linkage/effect, modulator of the edge) to prevent subject/object inversion across papers.

## 7. Reference-genotype relevance

- **Genotype:** low; no disease allele and no endogenous Q230P context.
- **Model:** low for WWOX-DEE; transformed, p53-deficient cancer-cell systems.
- **Pathway:** moderate; WW1-dependent partner competition and abundance/function decoupling are transferable assay concepts.
- **Therapeutic:** very low. Cisplatin is a cancer-cell cytotoxic reagent, not a WWOX-DEE lead; doxycycline is a confound here, not a candidate.
- **Overall:** **LOW–MODERATE**, useful chiefly as a mechanistic and assay-design constraint.

## 8. Limits and uncertainty

- The evidence is dominated by overexpression in transformed cell lines.
- Y33R is an engineered interaction-defective control, not a patient variant.
- No direct binding biophysics, endogenous allelic series or rescue of WWOX knockdown is shown.
- Most blots lack densitometry; many assays do not state biological replicate counts or inferential tests.
- The ΔNp63α-AAxA conclusion is qualitative and does not define the alternative binding interface.
- Cytoplasmic sequestration is partial, and the authors explicitly leave other regulators possible.
- Doxycycline independently induces death and potentiates cisplatin in the control arm, limiting attribution in Figure 6.
- No neurons, brain tissue, developmental phenotype, seizures, reference genotype or in-vivo treatment experiment.

## 9. Commit impact

- **Correct:** supersede the object-reversed sentence in `CC-20260810-41984841-01`.
- **Preserve:** PMID 24550385 as the source for ITCH/K63-associated stabilization of WWOX; do not merge that mechanism with Salah 2013.
- **Add:** abundance-versus-function decoupling as a reusable assay constraint.
- **Do not promote:** cisplatin, doxycycline or ITCH inhibition as therapeutic leads.

## Final verdict

Salah 2013 can be closed completely. Its strongest contribution is a clean directionality correction: WWOX protects **ΔNp63α** from an ITCH/proteasomal route while suppressing ΔNp63α function through localization. It is not evidence that ITCH stabilizes **WWOX**.

Nothing here is medical advice; clinical-strategic implications exist only to support discussion with the treating clinical team.
