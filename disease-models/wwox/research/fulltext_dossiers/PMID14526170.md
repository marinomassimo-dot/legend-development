## 1 · The answer to the terminus question, first, because it is the deliverable

**The characterisation IS here. The chain stops. And it stops at immunoblot grade.**

### 1.1 · The immunogen, as this paper states it

> *«The antiserum was raised using a GST fusion to WWOX amino acid residues 12–94 containing both of
> the WW domains.»* — manuscript page 6

Stated **once**, unambiguously, in running prose. There is no Methods section: this is a
review-shaped article and the reagent's description lives in the body.

🔴 **This dissolves the discrepancy the lot was told to judge rather than inherit.** `PMID 15982416`
described its immunogen twice and differently — *«raised against the WW domains»* in Results, and
*«the recombinant GST-WWOX fusion protein used to raise the antibody»* three paragraphs later.
**Both are true of one construct**: a GST fusion whose insert is residues 12–94, which contains both
WW domains. Neither 2005 description was wrong; they named the fusion partner and the insert. The
useful residue is narrower than the tension it replaces: **the epitope is N-terminal, in exons 1–3.**

Cross-checked against this paper's own Fig. 3 at native 4200×3703: WW box 1 begins at L17, box 2 ends
near P86 — both inside 12–94. Immunogen and sequence map agree.

### 1.4 · Molecular-weight ladder — the panel-level finding

🔴 **Figure 5A, the panel carrying the whole specificity claim, has no molecular-weight ladder at
all.** Inspected at native 4200×2587 and again at 900 dpi: it bears only the two lane labels, the
lane numbers 1 and 2, and a `WWOX` band annotation. The only marks in the paper are on **Fig. 5B, a
different blot**, and they read literally **`— 85 kd`** and **`— 39.5 kd`** (spelled `kd`). The strong
band sits below the midpoint, nearer 39.5, consistent with 46 kDa — but bracketed by two marks
**45.5 kd apart**. So the *~46 kDa* attribution is unverifiable on the panel that asserts it and only
coarsely constrained on the panel that carries marks.

### 1.5 · Immunohistochemistry validated here?

**None. There is no immunohistochemistry in this paper at all** — no section, no fixation, no
paraffin, no tissue. The IHC-level control that the tissue atlas presupposed, and that
`ALDAZ-FT-B-003` found missing from both 2005 papers, is **missing at the terminus too — because the
terminus never attempts it.**

At this date the tissue work was explicitly *future*: *«We are currently using this antiserum to
measure WWOX protein levels in cancer cell lines and in normal tissues.»*

### 1.6 · 🔴 Two limits the panels give and the text does not

1. **The null lane is not blank.** At 900 dpi a faint higher-MW band sits in **both** lanes of
   Fig. 5A, including the WWOX-deleted Peo/Vector, plus a fainter lower band in both; in Fig. 5B the
   same faint upper band runs across all four lanes, including the two the caption calls undetectable.
   What the null lane shows is that the **~46 kDa band is WWOX-dependent**; it does not show that the
   antiserum binds **only** WWOX, and no control in the paper characterises the residual signal.
2. **The epitope sits upstream of the deletion.** The lesion recorded for this line elsewhere in this
   corpus is a homozygous deletion of **exons 4–8**; the epitope is residues **12–94**, i.e. exons
   1–3. The paper's own claim is carefully about **full-length** protein. `IPOTESI` with a
   `REVIVAL_TRIGGER` — see the M4b note §4; the faint band is unidentified and non-specific serum
   background is an adequate competing explanation, which is exactly why the missing ladder matters.

### 1.7 · What the reagent cannot do, by the authors' own statement

> *«since the anti-WWOX antibody recognizes epitopes within the WW domains it will also detect
> proteins encoded by the aberrantly spliced mRNAs if expressed»* — manuscript page 9

The aberrant variants delete SDR-encoding exons and **retain** the WW domains. Any band this reagent
produces is an aggregate of full-length and isoform signal unless size resolves them — and size
resolution is precisely what Fig. 5A lacks. **This is the single most consequential sentence in the
paper for anyone using the antiserum to call "WWOX protein" abundance.**

## 2 · What the paper is in its own right — a census of claims and their sources

Read as a census, never as a source of experimental fact. Measured deposit: **8 figures, 0 tables,
~56 references, 10 published pages, an abstract.** PubMed declares `pubtype ['Journal Article']` with
**no Review tag**; the paper's own abstract says *«In this article we will review…»* **and** *«present
evidence»*. Both available labels mis-describe it in opposite directions. `genre_discriminator`
returned `INSUFFICIENT_DATA` (it screened the pmc_html surface, where page span and reference count
are unmeasurable), so these counts were measured by hand from the PDF.

### 2.1 · Its own new data

- **Fig. 5A** — the antiserum and its specificity blot. *«We have **recently generated** WWOX specific
  antiserum»*: first description, and the reason this lot existed.
- **Fig. 5B** — breast-line WWOX protein panel (MDA-MB-435, MDA-MB-231, T47D, MCF7), carrying the
  paper's only two MW marks.
- **Fig. 8** — Δ5-7 and Δ8 GFP-fusion deletion mutants in MCF10F, both losing Golgi localisation.
- **Fig. 4** — aphidicolin metaphase with YAC933H2 hybridisation, presented as the laboratory's own
  independent confirmation that WWOX maps to FRA16D.

### 2.2 · Restatement of named primaries

Figs. 1, 2 and 7 compile Bednarek 2000/2001, Chen 1996 and Ried 2000: the 23×23 allelotype grid with
per-locus heterozygosity scores; the physical map with four myeloma breakpoints and exons E1–E9; the
transcript-variant schematic. All sources named in the captions. Fig. 7's *«only in cancer cell lines
and cancer tissues»* restriction is asserted in its key, not measured in the figure, and its
accession list prints AF2119443 for variant 5 against AF211943 for variant 1 — an evident typo.

### 2.3 · 🔴 Unpublished assertions — and the genre's failure mode, reproduced

`unpublished` occurs once, `manuscript in preparation` once, `data not shown` once. **Each marks a
claim that the abstract or a caption states without the qualifier:**

| Claim | Body | Abstract / caption |
|---|---|---|
| WW1 binds PPXY, a Group I domain | *«In **preliminary studies**…»* + *«manuscript in preparation»*, upgraded one sentence later to *«These studies **confirm**…»* | *«We **have identified** the WWOX WW domain ligand as the PPXY motif **confirming** the biochemical activity»* |
| Variant 1 encodes the full-length protein (MALDI-TOF of immunoprecipitated WWOX) | *«unpublished observation»* — no spectra, coverage or peptide list | not mentioned |
| SDR alone suffices for perinuclear localisation | *«(J.H. Ludes-Meyers, **data not shown**)»* | *«we **will demonstrate** that Golgi localization requires an intact SDR»* |

This is the same shape as `24932569`, read earlier in this batch, whose census found *"or
carcinogenic"* attributed to a paper containing no such data. **The genre's failure mode was tested
here rather than assumed, and it is present.**

### 2.4 · A conclusion that outruns its own figure

The body concludes *«we conclude that the SDR domain is **necessary and sufficient** for Golgi
localization»*. Necessity is in Fig. 8 (both deletion mutants lose localisation). **Sufficiency is
the "data not shown" fusion.** Fig. 8's own caption title asserts *«The WWOX catalytic domain is
sufficient for Golgi localization»* — which its two panels cannot show: there is no
catalytic-domain-alone panel, no full-length comparator, no Golgi co-marker, no scale bar, and the
caption cross-references a *«Fig. 7a»* that does not exist. This is the manifest's single
`text_contradicted_by_panel` entry, pointed at `entries[10]` with its needle.

The paper also concedes, without adjudicating, a conflict with Chang et al. 2001 on mitochondrial
targeting: *«It is not clear why there is a discrepancy between these two observations.»* Every
localisation observation in this lineage is of a **GFP fusion, transiently overexpressed** — never of
endogenous protein, and therefore nothing the antiserum could confirm or refute.

### 2.5 · The sex-steroid frame — restated, never tested

*«we **postulated** that WWOX may be an enzyme involved in sex-steroid metabolism»*, supported by
tissue distribution and by the company WWOX keeps in an SDR cluster analysis (Kallberg 2002:
CGI-82 prostate expression, PAN2 sequence similarity). **No substrate, product, enzymatic assay or
steroid is measured anywhere.** Third paper of this thread to restate it and the third not to test
it. Field density measured 2026-09-13: `WWOX AND sex steroid metabolism` = **10** records.

### 2.6 · Design and statistical limits

- **Xenograft (Fig. 6):** 1×10⁶ cells, thoracic mammary fat pad, BALB/c athymic, 35 days, values
  ±SEM. **No n per arm, no statistical test, no p-value anywhere**, and no latency data despite
  latency being asserted. The MDA-MB-231/WWOX bar carries **no visible error bar** while the other
  three do. The body calls this *«the strongest reported evidence demonstrating that WWOX behaves as
  a tumor suppressor»*. Direction large and consistent; inference unquantified.
- **ER association:** four cell lines, no statistics, and the paper itself names the primary-tumour
  test as future work.
- **No table anywhere in the article.**

## 6 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype

**None. T-none, and saying so precisely is the deliverable.**

This is a 2003 cancer-genetics review from the laboratory that cloned the gene. It contains no allele,
no developmental axis, no neural tissue, no dosage model and no intervention relevant to a
WWOX-DEE genotype class. WWOX-DEE did not exist as a described entity in 2003.

Its value to this corpus is **entirely methodological and entirely upstream**: it is the origin point
of the reagent on which a substantial part of the WWOX *protein* literature — including this corpus's
tissue-distribution arguments — ultimately rests, and it defines the boundary of what that reagent
licenses. **No claim is proposed from this paper, and proposing none is the recommendation.**

## 7 · What this reading does NOT support

- It does **not** validate the antiserum for immunohistochemistry, for tissue, or for neural tissue.
- It does **not** show the antiserum is mono-specific — the null lane is not blank and nothing
  characterises the residual band.
- It does **not** establish the ~46 kDa identity on the panel that asserts it (no ladder on Fig. 5A).
- It does **not** let anyone distinguish full-length WWOX from SDR-deleted isoforms with this
  reagent — the authors state the opposite, by design.
- It does **not** establish a sex-steroid enzymatic function; nothing hormonal or enzymatic is
  measured.
- It does **not** establish that the SDR domain is *sufficient* for Golgi localisation ("data not
  shown").
- It does **not** establish the PPXY ligand result (preliminary, manuscript in preparation).
- It does **not** quantify the xenograft effect (no n, no test, no p-value).
- It **refutes nothing** downstream. Everything affected is **QUALIFIED**.
