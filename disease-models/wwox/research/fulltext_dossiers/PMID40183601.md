## 1 · What the study did

733 consecutively referred paediatric epilepsy families at one academic clinical-genomics centre,
2014–2022; clinical exome then genome sequencing (640 families genome, 143 exome; 2029 individuals
sequenced). Phenotypes from referrals and records. Molecular diagnosis in **278/733 (37.9 %)**;
302 disease-causing variants across 130 genes; autosomal recessive homozygous accounted for
**32/281 (11.4 %)** etiologies; cohort mortality at review **68/733 (9.3 %)**.

### 2.2 Four WWOX individuals, three genotypes, one syndrome band

From Supplementary Table S9 (parsed cell-wise), at class level only: four individuals, all
**homozygous** with both parents carriers, all with **infantile epileptic spasms syndrome**, all
with global developmental delay; genotypes are a nonsense allele, a missense allele shared by two
individuals of one family, and the deep intronic allele. The two sharing the missense genotype are
recorded as deceased; the deep intronic case was alive at review. WWOX is tied with CDKL5 at four
individuals among the 51 solved spasms cases, behind STXBP1 at six (Table 1).

### 2.3 Denominators this gives the model

- WWOX diagnoses: **4/733** of the whole cohort (≈ 0.55 %), **4/278** of solved cases (≈ 1.4 %),
  **4/51** of solved infantile-spasms cases (≈ 7.8 %). Three independent families.
- Cohort mortality **9.3 %** is the comparator any WWOX-specific mortality statement needs; within
  this cohort two of four WWOX individuals (one family) were deceased — far too small a count to
  support a rate, and stated here only as what the table prints.

## 3 · Allele-class notes

- `c.107+119C>G` is **absent from the held ClinVar snapshot**; the nonsense and missense alleles are
  present there (pathogenic/likely pathogenic, and **uncertain significance** respectively). A
  homozygous VUS missense in two affected individuals of one family is a reclassification signal,
  not a reclassification.
- Transfer limit: a deep-intronic cryptic-donor allele in intron 1 is not an acceptor allele, not a
  canonical ±1/±2 allele and not a missense; nothing transfers to P47T, Q230P, G372R, A141T, P252A.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT state

For the WWOX deep intronic case: no tissue for the cDNA, no quantification, no protein, no NMD test,
no figure. For the WWOX cases generally: no seizure-course detail beyond the table's HPO terms, no
treatment response, no imaging description, no age at death. The paper makes no claim about WWOX
prevalence; the denominators above are the reader's arithmetic on its printed counts, and its
ascertainment is referral-shaped (one centre, both internal and external referrals).

## 2.4 · Classification: diagnostic here, uncertain in ClinVar

The authors kept VUS carriers **unsolved** (Results, Candidate variants) and list VUS individuals
in Table S10, which has **no WWOX row**. The His322Arg family is in the **solved** table, so the
authors counted the allele as diagnostic. The held ClinVar snapshot and the live ClinVar record
(three submitters, no conflicts, last evaluated 2024-10-18) both give **uncertain significance**.
Measured vs predicted: **nothing about the allele is measured** — no RNA, protein, stability or
activity; the diagnostic call rests on homozygosity in two affected relatives and the clinical fit.
