## 1. What the paper did

Cell lines, two directions of manipulation:

* **Gain.** PEO1 epithelial ovarian cancer cells, which are **homozygously deleted for *WWOX*
  exons 4–8 and lack WWOX protein** — i.e. a human *WWOX*-null line — stably transfected with
  *WWOX* (clones WWOX-6/7/8) or empty vector (Vector-9/p1/p2).
* **Loss.** siRNA knockdown of endogenous WWOX in SKOV-3 and OVCAR-4, and of transfected WWOX in
  the WWOX-8 clone, with two independent oligos (1 and 15) and a non-targeting pool.

Challenges: paclitaxel (8/16 nM in PEO1, 50 nM SKOV-3, 20 nM OVCAR-4), cisplatin, monastrol (a
selective Eg5 antimitotic), tunicamycin and thapsigargin (ER-stress inducers), the IRE-1 inhibitor
**KIRA6**, the PERK inhibitor **GSK2656157**, and the JNK inhibitor SP600125.

Readouts: SRB survival, Caspase-Glo 3/7, Annexin V/PI flow cytometry, MPM2 mitotic index,
apoptosis/phospho protein arrays, immunoblots (GRP78, phospho-eIF2A, phospho/total IRE-1,
PERK, phospho/total JNK, cleaved caspase-2/-3/-7), co-immunoprecipitation, and survival analysis
of two public ovarian microarray cohorts (TCGA, Tothill GSE9891), RMA-normalised, Cox models and
log-rank tests.

## 3. What the paper states

* WWOX re-expression sensitises WWOX-null ovarian cancer cells to paclitaxel, and knockdown
  desensitises them; the effect is specific to taxane, absent for cisplatin, and **independent of
  the antimitotic action** (no change in mitotic index, no change in the monastrol response).
* Paclitaxel induces ER stress (GRP78 up, eIF2A phosphorylated, JNK and p38 activated), as does
  tunicamycin, and WWOX directs that UPR toward apoptosis.
* Both IRE-1 and PERK are activated by paclitaxel; a phospho-IRE-1 band appears at 110 kDa and
  PERK shifts upward; *"an intact IRE-1 arm of UPR is crucial for WWOX to function"*.
* High *WWOX* mRNA predicts longer OS and PFS in taxane-treated ovarian cancer, and the
  association depends on GRP78 (HSPA5) level.
* No WWOX–JNK1 and no WWOX–GRP78 interaction was detectable by co-immunoprecipitation
  (Supplementary Figures 5 and 9).

## 4. What the paper does NOT state

* **It says essentially nothing about neurons.** The single relevant sentence is in the
  Introduction — *"WWOX is highly expressed in secretory epithelia, in reproductive, exocrine and
  endocrine organs and also in neuronal bodies throughout the central nervous system. Mutations in
  WWOX have been reported in several neurological pathologies"* — and no neural cell, tissue or
  measurement appears anywhere else.
* It does not quantify a single immunoblot. There is **no densitometry anywhere**: every ER-stress
  conclusion rests on visual inspection of band intensity.
* It does not report significance for the inhibitor experiments. **Figures 5c, 5d and 5e carry no
  asterisks and no P-values**, although they are the panels that carry the paper's central claim
  about the IRE-1 and PERK arms.
* It does not test the direction of causality between WWOX and ER stress; both manipulations are
  of WWOX, and ER stress is only ever the readout or the imposed challenge.
* It does not show WWOX-dependence of the KIRA6 rescue (see §6).
* It does not assess any WWOX allele: the comparison is presence versus absence of protein.

## 5. Caveats the authors themselves give

* The survival analyses are observational, on two public microarray cohorts, with dichotomisation
  at the median *"for the purposes of illustrating effects"* while the statistics come from Cox
  models on continuous expression.
* GRP78 total expression range across the Tothill set is only 7.89-fold, with the median 2.42-fold
  above the minimum — i.e. the high/low split is a split of a narrow distribution.
* The interpretation of the GRP78 interaction is offered as speculation (*"it might be
  speculated…"*).
* The WWOX–GRP78 interaction they would need was not found.

## 6. 🔴 What the panels show that the prose does not

1. **The IRE-1 inhibitor rescue is not WWOX-selective.** The text says that without WWOX, cells
   *"were less sensitive to KIRA6 and GSK2656157 as shown by the blunted effects on cell
   viability"*. In Fig 5c the absolute increments are the same: WWOX-8 rises 0.05 → 0.24 with
   KIRA6 (Δ ≈ 0.19) and Vector-9 rises 0.13 → 0.31 (Δ ≈ 0.18). The apparent "blunting" is a
   fold-change artefact of the lower WWOX-expressing baseline. With no statistics on these panels,
   the claim that an intact IRE-1 arm is required *for WWOX to function* is not supported by the
   figure it cites.
2. **An inverse dose-response in the knockdown.** Fig 2a shows oligo 15 leaving a clearly visible
   residual WWOX band while oligo 1 removes more — yet oligo 15 produces the **larger** survival
   rescue (0.39, `***`) and oligo 1 the smaller (0.28, `*`). Knockdown depth and effect size run
   in opposite directions across the two oligos.
3. **The two "WWOX-transfected" clones express very differently.** In Fig 1c the WWOX band in
   WWOX-7 is far stronger than in WWOX-8, yet their paclitaxel survival is nearly identical
   (0.17 vs 0.19) and their Annexin V values differ almost two-fold (21 % vs 12 %) in the opposite
   direction. There is no monotonic relation between WWOX level and effect.
4. **Figure 7e is reported as positive and is not.** The text states that in Figures 7e and f,
   *"in patients treated with platinum and taxane (Figure 7e) but not in patients treated with
   platinum alone (Figure 7f), this dichotomisation suggests longer PFS in patients with WWOX
   expression detectable at a level comparable to normal tissue."* Panel 7e is printed with
   **P = 0.13**.
5. **The Figure 6 legend does not describe Figure 6.** The legend declares panels (a) through (h)
   and assigns (a)/(b) to whole cohorts; the published figure has **six panels**, with (a) = TCGA
   with taxane (n = 373), (b) = TCGA without taxane (n = 33). The running text matches the figure;
   the legend does not.
6. **The taxane-specificity rests on two tiny subgroups.** The "platinum alone" arms are n = 33
   (TCGA OS), n = 24 (TCGA PFS) and n = 48 (Tothill). A null in a group of that size is not
   evidence of absence, and the paper's central clinical claim is a contrast between a significant
   large group and a non-significant small one.
7. **Figure 8a magnitudes are small.** Subtype 1 does have the lowest median WWOX across all three
   probesets, but on a log₂ scale the medians differ by ≈0.2–0.3 (≈1.2-fold) with heavily
   overlapping boxes.
8. A minor provenance discrepancy: the main text names PERK antibody `ab65142`, the Supplementary
   Figure 6 legend names `ab65152`.

## 7. Answer to the assigned question, for this source

**No endpoint in this paper can serve as a rescue readout for the WWOX-DEE genotype class, and the
reason is not the tissue — it is the direction.**

Every quantified endpoint here measures WWOX as a **pro-death** factor under stress: restoring
WWOX into a null background roughly halves survival under paclitaxel and tunicamycin; removing it
roughly doubles survival. A "rescue" in this system means *restoring the cell's ability to die*.
For a neurodevelopmental disorder in which the therapeutic aim is to keep neurons alive and
functional, an endpoint whose rescued state is increased apoptotic sensitivity is the wrong sign.

The candidate readouts, and why each is rejected:

| Candidate | Rejected because |
|---|---|
| GRP78 induction | a generic ER-stress chaperone, Tier 3 under [§13](../../../../framework/instruction/LEGEND_CORE.md#13-biomarker-scope-hard-rule); not WWOX-specific, not quantified here, not samplable from a living patient's neurons |
| phospho-IRE-1 110 kDa band | same tier; detected only as a band appearing/disappearing, never quantified; the inhibitor experiment that would establish WWOX-dependence carries no statistics |
| PERK band shift | same, and the paper states explicitly that *"No changes in activation of PERK were observed as a result of WWOX status"* |
| Cleaved caspase-3 / Annexin V | a death readout; its rescued direction is the wrong one for a DEE, and it is terminal |
| *WWOX* mRNA as a response predictor | this is a **tumour** chemosensitivity association, not a measure of the functional state of WWOX in a patient with a germline WWOX disorder |

**What does transfer** is a direction-of-effect, and it converges with two independent systems read
in the same batch: PMID 26302329 (Drosophila epithelium — less WWOX, less stress-induced caspase-3;
more WWOX, more) and PMID 34268881 (human WWOX-null organoids — cleaved caspase-3 in the
ventricular zone falls from ≈24 % to ≈9 % and returns toward wild type on WWOX restoration). Three
systems, three organisms, one direction: **WWOX loss lowers the apoptotic threshold response, and
WWOX restoration raises apoptotic sensitivity under stress.** For a WWOX gene-replacement strategy
that is a dose-and-context question worth carrying, not a reason to stop.

**Transfer limit.** Epithelial ovarian carcinoma lines, one of them a *WWOX*-null by homozygous
exon 4–8 deletion; the stressor is a chemotherapeutic; the human data are observational tumour
cohorts. Nothing here touches a germline missense allele, a neuron, or a developing brain.
