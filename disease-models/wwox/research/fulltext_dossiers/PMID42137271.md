## What the piece is

A commentary on a separate primary (an intra-cisterna-magna time course in cynomolgus macaques with AAVhu68 carrying human SMN1, plus two further cargos). It measures nothing. Every number or result in it is the primary's or an earlier paper's, relayed, and the commentary is a counter-reading, not a replication. Locators are therefore labelled *relayed*.

## What it states (first pass, before any registry record was opened)

1. **Sequence in the primary, as relayed.** Ultrastructural cell stress (ER dilation, mitochondrial swelling) and transgene transcripts in transduced DRG cells at day 5; type I interferon signalling in CSF and immune-cell infiltration at day 5; T and B lymphocyte infiltration at days 15 and 29; neuronal degeneration at 2 to 4 weeks. Severity tracked transgene expression level; one animal with low expression had no DRG findings.
2. **Immunosuppression, as relayed.** Dexamethasone plus tacrolimus plus mycophenolate for the whole study "reduced but did not eliminate" the severity or incidence of DRG toxicity, in two protein-coding vectors; with a non-protein (microRNA) cargo two of the agents reduced toxicity and infiltration. Effect on infiltration varied by tissue.
3. **The commentators' objections.** (a) The primary found no direct T-cell killing of neurons. (b) Immunosuppression ran for the entire in-life period with no taper, so durability is unknown. (c) Their own earlier work (two rhesus ICM papers, refs 7 and 8) saw a rebound when a time-limited antimetabolite was withdrawn at day 60, giving a delayed DRG phenotype equal to or slightly below untreated animals. (d) The order immune-then-stress versus stress-then-immune is not settled; a promoterless payload with an immune response and then DRG toxicity would be the decisive experiment, and the primary does not supply it.
4. **Their model.** Over-transduction is the root cause common to both readings; stress in transduced neurons (transcriptional or RNAi-machinery overload, protein overexpression) primes innate immunity; satellite cells recruit lymphocytes; adaptive immunity amplifies. Physiological transduction levels are said not to produce meaningful findings.
5. **Mitigations they favour.** Lower dose, weaker or tissue-restricted promoter, DRG-specific regulatory elements (including microRNA-binding sites), or short targeted suppression of the first innate step, over broad prolonged immunosuppression. Clinical DRG toxicity is described as rarely observed (two citations), and routine broad immunosuppression is not supported by that experience.
6. **Figure 1** (schematic, inspected as an image): day 5 excessive transduction, cellular stress, satellite-cell activation and innate response; day 15 and later adaptive infiltrate and toxicity; three interception points drawn (limit transgene expression upstream; suppress innate; suppress adaptive).

## What it does NOT state

- No dose, no vector genome count, no n, no incidence figure of its own. No statement about any gene other than SMN1, GBA1 and an SOD1 microRNA cargo (via the primary).
- It does not say immunosuppression has no effect; it says the effect is partial, undated in durability, and not the root.
- It does not report neonatal or infant dosing, tolerisation, or any human histology.
- It measures no neurofilament or other toxicity biomarker.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

- The primary's immunosuppression was not tapered and no direct T-cell killing was shown.
- Placement of the immune response upstream or downstream of stress needs a promoterless-payload experiment that is not in the primary.

## Reader's caveats (INFERENZA, labelled as such)

- Conflict structure: both authors work in companies whose programmes depend on a transgene-burden mitigation (microRNA de-targeting, regulatory elements). The commentary favours those levers over immunosuppression. That is a reason to read it as an argument, not as a measurement.
- The rebound datum in (3c) is relayed from earlier papers not held here; carry it as *reported by the commentators*.

## Transfer limit to the WWOX-DEE genotype class

Nothing here concerns WWOX. The lesson (an AAV DRG finding has a transgene-burden component and an immune component, and a partial immunosuppression effect is not a bound on risk) transfers only to a CSF-route AAV cassette in a primate-like ganglion; it does not say a WWOX cassette will or will not stress transduced neurons, and no WWOX transgene has been tested for dose-driven cell stress.

## Verdict

**ABSTRACT-SUFFICIENT in scope, read in full as a short commentary: ingest as a counter-reading source, no measurement.** Compared with what is held (see the intake note, comparison section): it adds the withdrawal-rebound datum, the promoterless-payload discriminating experiment, and an explicit authors' statement that clinical DRG experience does not support routine broad immunosuppression; it adds no new measurement.
