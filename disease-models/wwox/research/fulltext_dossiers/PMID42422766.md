## What the paper did

Three experiments with one cargo (AAV9-CAGGs-codon-optimised human *GBA1*, WPRE, AAV2 ITRs):

1. **Mouse, early postnatal.** Unilateral intracerebroventricular (ICV) injection at **P0** in
   *Gba1* D409V+/+ and WT mice, 4 µL, total 1 × 10^9, 7 × 10^9, 1 × 10^10 or 4 × 10^10 genome
   copies; AAV9-YFP control; euthanasia 2 months later.
2. **NHP, intracisterna magna (ICM).** Naive 2–3-year-old cynomolgus, 3/group mixed sex, single
   1 mL bolus of aCSF or **1.0 × 10^13, 3.0 × 10^13 or 1.0 × 10^14 GC/dose**, needle placement
   confirmed by MRI, necropsy day 43.
3. **NHP, intraparenchymal (IPa).** 19–45-month-old cynomolgus, 3/group, MRI-guided
   convection-enhanced delivery bilaterally into putamen (0.15 mL) and/or thalamus (0.125 mL) at
   2.5 × 10^12 (low) or 7.5 × 10^12 (high) GC/mL; necropsy day 43 except the High P+T group.

**No neutralising-antibody pre-screen and no immunosuppression were used** ("we did not pre-screen
for neutralizing antibodies or utilize immunosuppression in the reported results").

## What it measured

Vector genomes (qPCR, GC/µg DNA), bulk tissue h*GBA1* RNA (ddCT vs RPP30), RNAscope ISH, GCase
enzymatic activity (4MUG assay), microglial process perimeter (Iba1/PU.1), in-life clinical and
neurological examination, clinical pathology, CSF analysis, and blinded board-certified
histopathology of brain, spinal cord, DRG, trigeminal ganglia, nerve roots and peripheral nerves.

## What it states

1. **Route, not dose, sorted the safety finding.** After **ICM**, "AAV9-GBA1-related
   histopathology findings were present at all dose levels and affected the DRG, TG, spinal cord,
   cauda equina, dorsal nerve roots, and peripheral nerves" — minimal-to-moderate DRG neuronal
   cell-body degeneration/necrosis with mononuclear infiltration, and minimal-to-marked dorsal
   nerve-root and dorsal-funiculus fibre degeneration. These were classed **adverse** at every
   dose level, including the lowest (1 × 10^13 GC).
2. **ICM gave the toxicity without the brain benefit.** Brain vector genomes ~10^1–10^3 GC/µg
   while spinal cord/DRG/TG were ~10^2–10^4; DRG RNA rose dose-dependently to ~10^6 ddCT; and
   **no significant change in GCase activity was detected in any brain region**. There were "no
   AAV9-GBA1-related microscopic findings in any brain region".
3. **IPa inverted the pattern.** Brain vector ~10^4–10^7 GC/µg with RNA ~10^4–10^6 and GCase
   activity at or above target in most samples; spinal cord, DRG and TG only ~10^2 GC/µg and
   10^1–10^2 ddCT; "no AAV-related DRG toxicity" (abstract). But all three **High P+T** animals
   were euthanised early (days 17–30) for deteriorating neurological condition, with neuropil
   inflammation, neuronal necrosis and perivascular cuffing at the injection sites; one **aCSF
   control** animal had adverse procedure-related brain cavitation.
4. **The neonatal mouse arm was clean.** P0 ICV restored GCase to or above WT level at
   7 × 10^9 GC with no clinical signs, no neuronal pathology and no treatment effect on microglial
   process perimeter; the only histological finding was neuronal vacuolation at the top dose
   (4 × 10^10 GC), judged non-adverse and attributed to over-expression.
5. **Total vector per brain region did not explain the species difference.** NHP IPa vector levels
   (~10^4–10^6 GC/µg) matched the mouse ICV levels, "suggest[ing] that the total amount of vector
   delivered to a brain region is not the primary driver, but perhaps species and/or route of
   administration … played a more critical role".
6. **DRG toxicity is called a class effect of current capsids**, reported across capsids,
   promoters and cargos, "generally … asymptomatic" in NHP, with a single human report of
   transient sensory symptoms after IT AAV in an ALS patient that the authors say cannot be
   definitively attributed.

## What it does NOT state

- Nothing about WWOX, nothing about any DEE.
- **It contains no immunosuppression experiment of its own.** The sentence "data from our group
  suggest that immunosuppression can greatly reduce but not eliminate the severity and/or
  incidence of DRG, spinal cord, and other adverse histopathology findings" is a **citation**
  (ref 42), not a measurement in this paper.
- It did not compare early-postnatal with young-adult **NHP** administration; the age variable is
  confounded with species ("Future experiments directly comparing early postnatal and young adult
  NHP AAV administration would be valuable").
- It did not characterise transduced cell types systematically.
- It reports no functional sensory testing or nerve-conduction measurement: "adverse microscopic
  findings" is a histopathology grade, not a deficit.
- No statistical significance was reached for the IPa GCase increase (small n, high variability).

## Caveats the authors themselves give

Small group sizes and high animal-to-animal variability; codon-optimised cargo, which "some
studies suggest may be linked with untoward outcomes in the clinic"; a lysosomal-enzyme cargo
whose adverse pattern resembles other lysosomal-enzyme AAV studies; procedure-related variability
in the IPa arm (one control animal affected); possible contribution of pre-existing neutralising
antibodies, unscreened.

## Transfer limit

Cynomolgus macaque, young adult, no immunosuppression, no nAb screen; cargo is a **secreted**
lysosomal hydrolase that cross-corrects — WWOX is an intracellular protein with no established
secretory route, so the "limited transduction may still suffice" argument does not transfer.
The mouse arm is a **neonatal** arm in a different species and cannot be read as evidence about a
neonatal primate or human. Nothing here is medical advice.
