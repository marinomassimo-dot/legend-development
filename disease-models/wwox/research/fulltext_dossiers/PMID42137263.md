## What the paper did

A secondary analysis of liver RNA-seq data produced in an earlier toxicology study (the authors say the data were "originally used only for SMN1 transgene expression analysis"). Vector: AAV9-PHP.B carrying CBh-SMN1, given intravenously at 2e13, 5e13 and 1e14 vg/kg to cynomolgus monkeys (two per sex per dose, one male fewer at the mid dose; four liver lobes sequenced per animal) and to male rats (six per dose). Sacrifice 4-5 days after dosing. Differential expression and gene-set enrichment against untreated controls, then correlation of gene expression with transgene expression, dose and day-4 clinical pathology values.

## What it measured

- Monkey liver: number of differentially expressed genes rose with dose in both sexes; rat liver showed far fewer and no dose trend (Figure 1B, inspected).
- High dose: up-regulation of apoptosis, p53/DNA-damage, ER-stress/unfolded-protein-response (PERK arm: EIF2AK3, ATF4, DDIT3, TRIB3; IRE1 arm: MAPK8, MAP3K5; RIDD-sensitive mRNAs down), cytokine, TLR and NF-kB sets; down-regulation of glycine, lipid, fatty-acid and amino-acid metabolism and of hepatocyte-identity genes (Figures 1C-D, 2A, inspected).
- Low dose (monkey): p53/DNA-damage up, interferon/antiviral and T-cell activation up, regeneration signature; no ER-stress signature (Figure 1C-D).
- Rat: type I interferon and cell-cycle-arrest sets, a few p53 targets at the top dose, no UPR.
- Correlations: transgene expression with DDIT3 r = 0.96 and with CES1 r = -0.89 (Figure 2B, inspected; points are lobes within animals, dose and expression move together). Day-4 ALT, AST, D-dimer, PT, APTT correlate positively with stress and immune genes; albumin, fibrinogen, platelets negatively (Figure 3; caption read, panel not retrieved).

## What the authors state

- Toxic-dose liver injury is associated with cell-intrinsic stress (p53 axis, pro-apoptotic UPR) plus innate immune activation; they propose genome presence drives the DNA-damage response and capsid plus high transgene expression drive the UPR, citing another group's empty-capsid and promoterless-vector monkey data (not tested here).
- The earlier study's histology showed "only mild immune cell infiltration", from which the authors infer a vector-specific T-cell response is unlikely at this early time point (Introduction, citing the earlier study).
- Fatal clinical hepatotoxicity usually appears weeks to months after dosing, and the data are "mechanistic insights rather than a direct temporal model of the clinical scenario".
- They float TUDCA-type UPR modulation as untested and conclude "more is not always better" on transgene level.

## What the paper does NOT say

- No intervention arm of any kind: no immunosuppression, no UPR modulator, no empty-capsid or promoterless control, no dose-matched transgene-null group. Mechanism is correlational.
- No cause of death established; no protein-level or histology data here (histology is from the earlier study).
- No CNS, DRG, CSF-route or intrathecal data. No WWOX, no human-genotype relevance.
- ATF6 and IRE1-XBP1 branch absence is "data not shown".
- Whether the early innate signature is capsid-, genome- or transgene-driven is not resolved ("Additional mechanistic studies are needed").

## Caveats the authors give or that the design imposes

- n = 2 per sex per dose; the mid group was excluded from enrichment because the male arm had one animal. Fold changes and p values treat the four lobes of one animal as biological replicates (Figure 1 legend), so the effective n is smaller than the lobe count (INFERENZA: pseudoreplication).
- Dose, transgene level and injury are collinear; the correlation with transgene expression cannot separate them.
- Capsid is PHP.B, selected by the earlier study for DRG transduction; liver injury is IV and early (day 4), a different time course from the weeks-later T-cell pattern the authors themselves describe.
- Rat explanation (about tenfold lower transgene in rat liver) is from the earlier study, not measured here.

## Transfer limit

Liver, systemic IV, PHP.B capsid, SMN1 cargo, monkey, day 4. It says nothing measured about CNS/DRG toxicity after CSF delivery or about any WWOX construct. It bears on the question "is high-dose AAV organ injury only immune?" by showing a monkey liver signature at toxic dose dominated by stress/p53/UPR with only mild immune infiltration reported by the parent study; it does not show immunosuppression would or would not help.
