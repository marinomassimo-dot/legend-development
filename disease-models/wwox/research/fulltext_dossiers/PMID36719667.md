## What it is

An animal study in adult male Lewis rats: blunt head injury (200 g weight drop), tail amputation causing uncontrolled bleeding, or both, followed 15 minutes later by 3 mL of allogeneic rat plasma, lactated Ringer's solution, or nothing. Eight groups of 10 to 16 immediate survivors (97 in all); the outcome that matters is blood shed from the tail over 120 minutes, with haematocrit, lactate, pressure and mortality. WWOX: zero occurrences. No brain endpoint was measured.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** An adult trauma-resuscitation model in normal rats.

## What it reports (DATO)

- In combined-injury rats, bleeding after resuscitation was 5.2 mL with plasma, 2.5 with Ringer's, 1.9 with no fluid (one-way ANOVA P 0.005); mortality 10 of 12, 5 of 12 and 9 of 14 (P 0.106 for plasma against the other two pooled).
- Plasma rats had the lowest haematocrit and a higher lactate peak (not significant); UCHS-only rats showed a non-significant trend to more bleeding with plasma (P 0.254).
- Head injury alone did not change bleeding before resuscitation (P 0.361).

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Nothing about WWOX, a genetic DEE, or a child.** It cannot inform plasma in any human or paediatric setting (H2 questions 1 and 5: not testable here).
2. **No brain outcome.** Despite the title, no brain injury severity, intracranial pressure, histology or neurological score was measured; the "TBI" is an exposure whose severity is unquantified ("we did not assess the degree of brain injury").
3. **"Plasma increases haemorrhage" is one omnibus result.** The P 0.005 is a three-group ANOVA; no pairwise plasma-versus-Ringer's test is reported although the Abstract and Conclusion state the pair. In UCHS-only rats the same comparison is not significant (0.254), yet the Discussion reads "higher volumes ... suggest FFP has a detrimental effect". Many comparisons, no multiplicity control, groups of 10 to 16. INFERENZA.
4. **The sample-size premise fails against the data.** The calculation assumed SD 1 mL; the Table 1 intervals imply SDs of about 2 to 3.4 mL in the combined-injury groups, so the power claimed for 10 per group does not hold. INFERENZA from DATO.
5. **Mortality is not shown to differ.** 83 percent versus 42 percent with Ringer's is nominally striking at n = 12 each but P 0.106 for the pooled comparison; the Conclusion's "very high mortality" is a descriptive statement the authors themselves caution against reading as an effect.
6. **Bleeding volume is entangled with survival time.** Blood shed is summed to 120 minutes; animals that die early stop bleeding, and later means (MAP, lactate, haematocrit) rest on survivors, which the authors state produce "factitious" rises and falls. Bleeding data are missing for 21.6 percent of rats, almost all in unresuscitated groups.
7. **The mechanism is not tested.** "Pop-the-clot" is hypothesised and then doubted by the authors (low pressure throughout). The plasma was allogeneic, pooled or not unstated, and carried citrate (donor blood drawn into CPDA-1); ionised calcium is not reported (only sodium and potassium appear), so a citrate or calcium effect on coagulation cannot be excluded. IPOTESI.
8. **Reporting errors.** Table 1 gives bTBI-only mortality as 0.20 (0.50, 0.52), a lower limit above the estimate; the pre-resuscitation bleeding in the two groups is 2.0 and 1.8 mL with the same interval (1.7, 2.3); Figure 2 shows means without error bars and misspells a group key. Group totals (97) and the pooled mortalities (63.2 and 39.5 percent) do recompute.
9. **Design points not described:** randomisation method, blinding of bleeding and outcome assessment, donor-plasma pooling, and any dose-response; a fixed 3 mL volume is not a pressure-guided resuscitation (stated limitation).
10. **Species and model.** Young adult male rats, an amputation bleed, a fixed small volume, 120-minute horizon; none of it maps to paediatric genetic encephalopathy.

## Design and limits

Small groups, many endpoints, one-way ANOVA across protocols, survivors-only secondary endpoints, no brain endpoint, military-funded laboratory, cited prior model from the same group.
