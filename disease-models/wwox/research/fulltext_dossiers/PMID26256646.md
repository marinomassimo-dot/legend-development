## Scientific verdict

Stable WWOX re-expression in HOS and LM7 osteosarcoma cells reduced wound closure and Matrigel
invasion. In NOD/SCID mice (five per arm), HOS-WWOX delayed intratibial primary-tumour growth and
reduced lung metastasis; intravenous HOS and LM7 experiments likewise showed fewer metastatic lungs.
These are direct rescue-model observations, but ectopic expression in established cell lines is not a
therapeutic intervention.

The title's phrase “by modulating RUNX2 function” is stronger than the experiment. WWOX expression
was associated with lower abundance of several metastasis genes and reported RUNX2 targets, but the
study did not perturb RUNX2, measure RUNX2 protein, run ChIP/reporter assays or demonstrate a direct
WWOX–RUNX2 interaction. The mechanistic link is therefore **INFERENCE**, informed by prior work.

## Internal constraints

- Intratibial HOS-EV mice were sacrificed at about three weeks and HOS-WWOX mice at five weeks to
  reach comparable tumour size, while the Figure 2 caption compresses the lung comparison to five
  weeks. Primary-tumour delay and metastasis suppression cannot be collapsed into one time-matched
  effect.
- LM7 did not form intratibial tumours in the observation period; that model supplies no intratibial
  metastasis comparison.
- Figure 3 prints `p = 0.05` for LM7 although Methods defines significance as `P < 0.05`; without an
  unrounded value this is boundary evidence, not a result safely below the declared threshold.
- Figure 4 shows no visible significance marks and gives no biological replicate count. LM7 integrin
  alpha-2 is not visibly lower, so a blanket “all targets decreased” statement is unsupported.
- Wound closure lacks a named proliferation control.
- Reference 28, PMID 16223882, carries an expression-of-concern hold in the repository and is not
  load-bearing here.
