## What the paper did

Whole-genome short-read sequencing (Illumina PE150, mean about 27x) of 41 samples from 38 common marmosets
(*Callithrix jacchus*) in two captive colonies (one Japanese, one Chinese), each holding an epileptic pedigree.
Three aims: compare chimerism between blood and fingernail DNA, describe colony population structure, and search
for variants associated with epilepsy.

## Unit of analysis and design

- **Unit:** individual animals inside two extended pedigrees; controls are unaffected siblings and unrelated
  unaffected animals (Methods, "treated as controls according to the LAMP manual").
- **Case definition:** at least one witnessed seizure induced by handling; the phenotype resembles generalized
  tonic-clonic seizures; EEG spectra are shown (Figure S1) but **no ictal EEG** was obtained, so the epilepsy type
  is not established (authors).
- **Association set:** 31 fingernail samples only (blood excluded for chimerism; one mis-sampled animal excluded):
  colony 1 11 controls / 9 cases, colony 2 6 controls / 5 cases.
- **Test:** LAMP pedigree linkage-and-association likelihood-ratio test, empirical significance, 8.9 million SNPs;
  suggestive threshold P = 1e-5. Table 1 footnote defines "LOD" as −log10(P), so "LOD > 5" means P < 1e-5, not a
  linkage LOD.
- **CNVs:** Manta and Smoove callers, Fisher's exact test per gene at raw P < 0.05, manual read-depth validation;
  PCR validation for the one validated candidate.

## What it measured about WWOX — and what it did not

1. **SNP association, not deletion.** Seventeen SNPs in WWOX passed the suggestive threshold (Table 1;
   P 2.4e-6 to 9.2e-6). Supplementary Table S6 annotates every one as `intron_variant` in the **last intron
   (8 of 8)** of the marmoset model transcript XM_002761188.5, spanning about 31 kb (chr20 34.231–34.262 Mb). They
   lie on one phased haplotype (Results; Figure 3B–C, inspected), so they are **one signal, not seventeen**.
2. **Carriers (Table 1, control/case, and S6 haplotype sheet):** colony 1 — cases 9/9 heterozygous, controls 1/11
   heterozygous; colony 2 — cases 5/5 carriers (one homozygous), controls **3/6** carriers. In colony 2, two of the
   three carrier controls are flagged "Unrelated" in Table S2, i.e. animals that married into the pedigree; the
   haplotype is therefore common in that colony and present in its asymptomatic founder (pedigree, Figure 1B,
   inspected; S6).
3. **No WWOX copy-number finding.** No WWOX row appears in the CNV raw-test sheet or the verified-candidate sheet
   of Supplementary Table S7; the WWOX transcript ID that S6 annotates does not occur in S7 at all (reader's
   search over the extracted sheet, a post-reading audit, not a method of analysis). The **case-enriched deletion is
   KCTD18-like** (Fisher P = 0.012 in colony 1), which the authors state is "no longer significant after multiple
   testing correction". Figure 4B: decreased read depth in all 9 cases **and 5 of 11 controls**.
4. **No genome-wide significance.** The authors say no signal reached P < 5e-8. Supplementary Figure S8
   (inspected): genomic inflation λ = 0.825, and the observed P-values fall on or below the null line at the tail —
   the top hits are not in excess of chance expectation.
5. **Technical validation only.** Sanger re-sequencing (Table S9, Figure S9) confirms the genotype calls; it says
   nothing about association or function.
6. **Nothing functional.** No expression, splicing, protein, eQTL or phenotype-in-carrier analysis of WWOX.

## What the authors state

- WWOX, PTPN21 and KCTD18-like "may be candidates with pathogenic mutations in epileptic marmosets"
  (Discussion), followed by a literature paragraph on WWOX in epileptic encephalopathy and mouse models.
- Limitations they name: small sample size; no ictal EEG; short reads limit CNV characterisation; chimerism not
  quantifiable with these data; identified loci not claimed to be "the sole cause".

## What the paper does NOT say

- It does not report a WWOX deletion, a WWOX exonic variant, or any WWOX coding change.
- It does not report any marmoset WWOX expression or protein measurement.
- It does not say the WWOX haplotype is causal, nor estimate its penetrance.
- It does not correct the SNP association for relatedness beyond the LAMP model, and does not report a
  permutation within families.

## Reader's caveats (INFERENZA, labelled as such)

- **Identity by descent can produce this pattern.** Colony 1's cases descend from an affected founder pair
  (Figure 1B). Any haplotype carried by that pair and transmitted to the affected line will "associate" with
  epilepsy within one family; with 20 cases in two pedigrees and λ < 1, the WWOX hit is indistinguishable from
  pedigree structure. `PREMISE: INFERENZA`.
- **Intron 8 is most of the gene.** In human WWOX, intron 8 is the giant FRA16D intron (on the order of 70% of the
  1.1 Mb gene; `PREMISE: INFERENZA` from public RefSeq coordinates, not from this paper, and the marmoset
  intron's proportion was not measured); an intragenic signal landing
  there is the expected case, not a coincidence.
- **Chimerism** was handled by an allele-balance filter (< 0.25 excluded) and by using fingernail DNA; the authors
  concede chimerism cannot be quantified here.

## Transfer limit to the WWOX-DEE genotype class

T3 at most. A common, non-coding, heterozygous haplotype in a primate colony, with no functional consequence
shown, models neither a destabilizing SDR missense allele nor a canonical splice-acceptor allele, and says nothing
about biallelic loss. It cannot be read as a non-rodent replication of WWOX-associated epilepsy.

## Verdict

**INGEST (earned near-null).** The paper is read completely; its WWOX content is a suggestive, pedigree-confounded,
intronic haplotype association with no deletion and no function. The brief's premise ("WWOX gene deletion was more
common in epileptic marmosets") is **wrong**: that sentence in the abstract is about KCTD18-like.
