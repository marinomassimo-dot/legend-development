## WWOX content — an earned null, with one citation

The paper performs no WWOX experiment of any kind. The sentence is:

> "the presence of one WW domain may have a chaperone activity for the other, promoting its folding,
> as in the case of WW domains of WWOX68"

Reference 68 is McDonald CB et al., *J Mol Biol* 2012;422:58–74 (PMID 22634283) — a paper this
laboratory already holds with a `complete_fulltext_read` receipt. So the WWOX appearance is a
**citation of a result the corpus already has**, not a new measurement. What this source adds about
WWOX is only that an unrelated structural-biology group treats the WWOX WW1/WW2 relationship as the
textbook example of intra-tandem chaperoning — and that when they tested the same proposal for their
own protein, it failed: "our data demonstrate that WW2 can be folded independently of WW1".

**Transfer limit, stated exactly.** Nothing measured here is about WWOX. No WWOX construct, no WWOX

## What the paper did (the method, which is the reason it was read)

On **purified recombinant protein**, with constructs defined by residue number:

| Technique | What it delivered |
|---|---|
| Solution NMR (triple-resonance assignment, NOESY, structure calculation in ARIA) | Structures of the tandem free and ligand-bound (PDB 8PXW, 8PXX) |
| Isothermal titration calorimetry (MicroCal PEAQ-ITC, 25 °C, 20 mM sodium phosphate pH 6.5, 100 mM NaCl, at least duplicate) | Dissociation constants and stoichiometry per peptide |
| SAXS (SEC-SAXS and batch) | Independent validation of the solution ensemble (χ² = 1.35) |
| Residual dipolar couplings; paramagnetic relaxation enhancement with IPSL spin labels | Interdomain orientation and mobility |
| Site-directed mutagenesis of the autoinhibitory motifs to polyalanine | Causal test of the proposed mechanism |
| GFP-tagged constructs, SILAC immunoprecipitation + quantitative mass spectrometry in HeLa | Whether the in-vitro selectivity holds in cells |

Numbers the paper states in text: the best SF1 16-mer (residues 575–590) binds the tandem at
Kd ≈ 1 µM; other proline-rich 16-mers at Kd ≈ 10 µM and ≈ 20–30 µM; the SF3A1 364–413 region at
Kd ≈ 10 µM; adding the autoinhibitory N-terminal extension moves the high-affinity Kd from 1.4 to
8.8 µM and pushes the weaker ligands into the hundreds of µM; and the field baseline the authors
give for **isolated** WW domains is "typically in the range of hundreds of micromolar", with isolated
PRPF40A WW1 measured by others at 150–600 µM.

## Why this is the assay format the WWOX question lacks

Each element of this battery answers a question that the WWOX literature answers by annotation:

1. **Does the construct boundary matter?** Here it demonstrably did — the previously published tandem
   structure was built on a truncated construct and is inconsistent with the new SAXS and RDC data.
2. **Is the domain pair coupled or independent?** Answered by RDC/PRE/relaxation, not by inspection.
3. **How tight is the interaction, and with what stoichiometry?** Answered by ITC on purified protein.
4. **Is a proposed regulatory mechanism causal?** Answered by mutating the motif and recovering the
   lost affinity.
5. **Does the in-vitro selectivity hold in a cell?** Answered by IP-MS against a mutant control.
6. **Is the interpretation robust?** Two orthogonal solution techniques are required to agree before
   a structure is believed.

## What the paper does NOT state

- It says nothing about WWOX WW1 or WW2 affinities, nothing about the WWOX SDR domain, nothing about
  any oxidoreductase chemistry, nothing about any disease allele, and nothing about any neurological
  phenotype.
- It does not test whether WWOX's own WW tandem is autoinhibited; it names WWOX only as a prior example
  of intra-tandem chaperoning, and lists WW-tandem proteins it considers candidates for autoinhibition
  (PRPF40B, TCERG1, MAGI1-3, HECW1/2, YAP1, BAG3, SAV1, FNBP4, WWP2, ITCH, NEDD4, ARHGAP27, ARHGAP39)
  — **WWOX is not in that list.**
- Its own generalisation is explicitly a proposal: "Whether the internal proline-rich sequences that
  these proteins have, act in a similar way as in PRPF40A still needs further analysis".

## Caveats the authors themselves give

- Backbone assignment of the autoinhibited construct reached only 69% completeness because of sequence
  repetitiveness; methyl groups carried the analysis instead.
- The peptide linker region in the complex "is not well-defined due to the absence of experimental
  restraints", with an exchange process (probably proline cis-trans isomerisation) suggested.
- Multiple binding events on long proline-rich regions make apparent affinities and stoichiometries
  composite rather than single-site.
- The proposed generality across WW-tandem proteins is a hypothesis, not a result.
