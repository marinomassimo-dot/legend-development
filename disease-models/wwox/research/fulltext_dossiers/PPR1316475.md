## 1 · What the study did

Two South African longitudinal birth cohorts (128 + 200 mother–child dyads; heavy prenatal alcohol
exposure vs control) plus a US child-only cohort (n = 429). Genome-wide gene-by-exposure interaction
(GEMMA), gene-based tests (GCTA), meta-analysis (METASOFT), and a haplotype transmission
disequilibrium test (Haplin HTDT) on **256 dyads with complete genotype data**, run on
TOPMed-imputed genotypes. Prioritised genes were then tested for differential expression by
exposure (DESeq2) in placenta and child blood.

## 2 · The WWOX result, exactly as printed

| Item | Value | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Effect | a **maternal** four-SNP haplotype, intronic in WWOX, chr16:78493391 / 78493396 / 78493458 / 78493612 | Table 2, MOTHERS block |
| Outcome | FAS/PFAS diagnosis | Table 2 |
| Risk ratio | **4.07 (95 % CI 2.11–8.11)**, haplotype frequency 0.1170, adjusted p = 0.0002 | Table 2 |
| Multiple testing | frequency-weighted Benjamini–Hochberg (IHW), chosen because overlapping sliding-window haplotypes make unweighted BH "overly conservative" | Methods |
| Found only after imputation | the defining SNPs were not on the genotyping array and "were also absent in the CIFASD dataset, **limiting replication across cohorts**" | Discussion |
| Expression | WWOX **decreased** with exposure: placenta logFC −0.7567, p = 0.0358; child blood logFC −0.8289, p = **0.0500** | Table 3 |

The haplotype is **maternal**, not the child's; the child-side significant haplotype was COP1. The
brief's framing ("a WWOX haplotype associated with FASD diagnosis risk") is true only with that
qualifier.

## 3 · An internal contradiction in the preprint

Results § transcriptomic validation and Table 3 both report WWOX **decreased** in placenta and child
blood. The Discussion states the opposite: *"In our data, placental WWOX expression was increased in
exposed vs. control children."* One of the two is wrong; Table 3 carries the numbers.

## 4 · What the preprint does NOT state

No WWOX protein; no functional test of the haplotype; no mechanism linking the intronic haplotype to
WWOX expression (it is not shown to be an eQTL, and the expression analysis is by **exposure**, not
by genotype); no WWOX coding variant; no epilepsy or DEE outcome; no brain tissue; no replication of
the WWOX haplotype in the second cohort; no child-side WWOX effect. The authors state the sample is
small for genome-wide approaches.

## 5 · What it would take for this to bear on the disease model

The haplotype would have to be shown to change WWOX dose (allele-specific expression or an eQTL in a
relevant tissue), and the expression difference would have to be shown to follow genotype rather
than exposure. Neither is done here. As it stands this is a **common-variant, gene-by-environment
association on a modifier axis**, in a specific admixed population, unreplicated.

## 6 · Transfer limits

Nothing here concerns a WWOX coding or splice allele, a biallelic genotype, or a DEE phenotype;
no transfer to P47T, Q230P, G372R, A141T, P252A or any splice allele. A maternal haplotype effect is
not a child genotype effect. Preprint, not peer reviewed.
