## 1 · What the paper did

Primary mouse embryonic fibroblasts from `Wwox` knockout embryos (~E14.5), serially passaged:
passages 1–4 "early", passages 20–30 (mean 25) "late". Plus WWOX knockdown in human dermal
fibroblasts and in HEK293T. Readouts: growth curves, BrdU/PI flow cytometry, annexin V, caspase-3,
γH2AX immunofluorescence, bleomycin-induced micronuclei, *Hprt* 6-thioguanine mutation assay,
five mononucleotide-repeat microsatellite markers, SA-β-gal, qPCR and western for p16^Ink4a^ /
p19^Arf^ / p21^Cip1/Waf1^ / p27^Kip1^ / p53, luciferase reporters, bisulfite–MALDI-TOF methylation,
ChIP, subcellular fractionation, and sequencing of the entire p53 coding region.

🔴 **Control genotype.** The comparator through most figures is **`Wwox+/−`, not wild type**.
`Wwox+/+` appears only in Figures 2b, 4d, 5g–i and 6a–e. **Nothing in this paper licenses a
statement about heterozygotes versus wild type.**

## 2 · What the paper states

- Late-passage `Wwox−/−` MEFs **failed to enter replicative senescence**: spindle morphology
  retained, SA-β-gal not induced, growth accelerated (S-phase 33.1% vs 14.6%), **and** apoptosis
  simultaneously increased.
- Genome instability accumulated: γH2AX up, bleomycin-induced micronuclei up, *Hprt* mutation
  frequency up, microsatellite instability present.
  > *"was highly expressed in late-passage Wwox −/− MEFs"*
- Mechanism: cytosolic ROS overproduction → p16^Ink4a^ promoter hypermethylation **plus** p53
  destabilisation (and, in one clone, acquired loss-of-function p53 mutations A135P/V213G in the
  DNA-binding domain) → p21 not induced; p27 degraded via Thr187 phosphorylation.
- **Rescue:** the antioxidant N-acetyl-L-cysteine reversed it.
  > *"during the passage culture prevented microsatellite instability and resulted in senescence
  > induction in the late-passage Wwox −/− MEFs"*
- UV-induced premature senescence was likewise blocked by WWOX knockdown in HEK293T and in human
  dermal fibroblasts.

## 3 · The finding that only emerges against another paper in this batch

γH2AX **rises when WWOX is lost** (here, late-passage `Wwox−/−` MEFs) **and rises when WWOX is
added** (PMID 42395553: applied recombinant protein ≥100 ng/mL, and stable overexpression, in
SH-SY5Y and RPE-1).

One marker, two opposite perturbations, the same direction of change. Whatever else that means,
it means **γH2AX cannot serve as a directional readout of WWOX state**: a raised γH2AX is
consistent with too little WWOX and with too much. Neither paper cites the other; the observation
belongs to reading them together.

**§13 classification of γH2AX as a WWOX biomarker: REJECTED.** It is not a gene readout (Tier 1),
it is not specific to any WWOX-proximal pathway (Tier 2) — it is the generic double-strand-break
marker — and it is **non-monotonic in WWOX**, which disqualifies it independently of tier. It is
also not samplable in a living patient for neural tissue.

Likewise rejected, with reasons: **SA-β-gal** (requires cultured cells, generic senescence),
**p16/p21/p27** (generic cell-cycle inhibitors, measured here by western in fibroblasts),
**microsatellite instability** (a cumulative culture artefact at passage 20–30, not a patient
assay), **ROS by DHE** (generic, requires live cells).

## 4 · What the paper does **not** state

- **No neuron, no brain, no in vivo endpoint anywhere.** Every measurement is in MEFs, HEK293T or
  human dermal fibroblasts.
- No WWOX missense or splice allele; the model is a constitutive null.
- No claim that NAC would help any animal or person; the rescue is in culture.
- It does not show that the phenotype arises *in vivo* — the authors ask whether senescence escape
  *"interferes with cell physiology and homeostasis of systemic metabolism ... in patients and
  animals"* and answer *"remains to be verified"*.
- The p53 mutations arose in **one clone** (no. 56) and are explicitly absent from the others.

## 5 · Caveats the authors give themselves

They note that early-passage `Wwox−/−` MEFs showed **no** ROS difference, only induced redox-gene
transcription, and ask openly whether an initial antioxidative response buffers ROS until a
threshold is passed — i.e. the ROS phenotype is a late, culture-accumulated one. They state that
p21 downregulation is **independent** of its own promoter methylation, and that how p16 cooperates
with p53 to regulate p21 *"is unclear"*. They state the mechanism by which WWOX controls ROS
*"needs further investigation"*.

## 6 · Caveats this reading adds

⚠️ **Not independent of PMID 33195192** in this wave: same senior author (Hsu), same institution,
same knockout mouse line.

⚠️ Serial passage to P20–30 is a strong selective regime. A clone that acquires p53 mutations under
it is as much a report about the regime as about WWOX, and the authors' own framing — WWOX loss
*permits* the accumulation — is the careful version.

⚠️ The therapeutic-sounding closing line, *"Targeting WWOX can be exploited in the treatment of
senescence-related diseases"*, is unsupported by anything in the paper and should not be carried.
