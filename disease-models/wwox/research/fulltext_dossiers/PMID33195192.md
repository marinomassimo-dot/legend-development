## 1 · What the paper did

Whole-body constitutive `Wwox−/−` mice, with `Wwox+/−` and `Wwox+/+` littermates carried as
**separate arms throughout**, examined from E-stage and P0 to P21. Plus primary keratinocyte
cultures from those mice, and lentiviral WWOX knockdown in human HaCaT keratinocytes.
Readouts: H&E and Sirius red histology, transmission electron microscopy, immunohistochemistry and
immunofluorescence (K5/K10/K15/CD34/loricrin/Ki67/p63/FABP4/E-cadherin/ERK1/2/pERK), TUNEL, flow
cytometry (PI, BrdU), corneometry, tewametry, fibre-optic skin-surface thermometry, western blot.

## 2 · What the paper states — the WWOX-null phenotype outside the brain

| Domain | Result in `Wwox−/−` |
|---|---|
| Epidermal thickness (P21) | significantly decreased; transiently *increased* at P7 |
| Hydration | significantly reduced; **transepidermal water loss NOT increased** — the lipid barrier held |
| Keratinocyte proliferation | Ki67 down at P20; BrdU down; S and G2/M fractions down; HaCaT growth suppressed on knockdown |
| Differentiation | K10⁺ and K5⁺K10⁺ cells down; keratohyalin granules reduced; stratification domes reduced on knockdown |
| Apoptosis | TUNEL 1.77% vs 0.77% wild type |
| Hair follicles | first coat delayed (P6 vs P4); shorter follicles P3–P21; thinner shafts; catagen entry delayed to ~P20 |
| Stem cells | junctional-zone proliferation **increased** P3–P8, then K15⁺/CD34⁺ bulge stem cells **depleted** by P21 |
| Dermis | collagen content and fibre thickness reduced |
| Subcutaneous fat | **lost at P21**; few shrunken FABP4⁺ adipocytes without lipid droplets |
| Body temperature | **31.3 °C vs 35.8 °C** |

## 3 · The ERK readout — scope stated exactly

> *"keratinocytes expressed significantly reduced levels of pERK and total ERK1/2 protein"*

- **Species/tissue:** mouse epidermal keratinocytes, in situ.
- **Method:** immunohistochemistry (pERK Thr202/Tyr204, Cell Signaling #4370), quantified by ImageJ
  IHC Profiler over 25 regions from 3 mice. **Not a blot.**
- **Genotype:** constitutive whole-body null.
- **Direction:** reduced — both phospho- and total.
- ⚠️ **The total-ERK1/2 result sits in Supplementary Figure S9, which I did not read.** Only the
  running-text assertion is receipted.

### §13 classification of pERK as a candidate

| Criterion | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Measures WWOX functional state? | No — it reports a node many inputs converge on |
| Tier | Would sit at **Tier 2 by position** in the pathway and **fails on specificity**: MEK/ERK phosphorylation is among the least specific readouts in cell biology, and a fall in pERK is the ordinary accompaniment of reduced proliferation, which this tissue also shows |
| Samplable in a living patient? | **Partially — and this is its one genuine merit.** Skin is accessible by punch biopsy, unlike brain. But no CSF, blood or imaging route exists |
| Validated in the disease population? | No. No human WWOX-DEE skin has been assayed for pERK anywhere in this paper or its citations |

🔴 **VERDICT: REJECTED as a disease biomarker** (fails specificity, §13). **RETAINED, with its name
written down, as a possible *pharmacodynamic* readout inside a controlled experimental system** —
e.g. a WWOX-restoration experiment in keratinocytes where the perturbation is known and pERK is
read as a response, not as a diagnosis. That is a different use and must not be conflated with a
patient biomarker.

## 4 · The heterozygote result, which the assignment's genotype-caution rule makes worth naming

`Wwox+/−` mice were carried through hydration, epidermal thickness, Ki67, hair-growth timing,
follicle length, whisker length and body temperature, and **behaved like wild type throughout**.
This is a measured negative for heterozygosity in skin, in one constitutive model. It says nothing
about any human missense allele, and a constitutive null models neither P47T nor Q230P nor a splice
allele.

## 5 · The dose-relevant sentences — this paper's real contribution to question 1

> *"the control of cell growth and apoptosis by WWOX may depend on cell types, such as their tissue
> origin, differentiation state or differences between normal, benign and malignant cells"*

> *"in low-grade invasive HT29 colon adenocarcinoma cells, increased WWOX expression promotes cell
> proliferation but inhibits apoptosis"*  ← **a citation to Nowakowska et al. 2014, not measured
> here.** Carried as a pointer to an unread source.

> *"A certain amount of WWOX expression may be necessary for maintaining normal physiological
> functions in cells."*

Written in 2020, independently of the 2026 preprint, this is the same proposition the preprint
reaches: the quantity matters in both directions.

## 6 · What the paper does **not** state

- It never raises WWOX. There is no overexpression arm. The HT29 statement is a citation.
- No neural tissue, no brain, no seizure, no EEG.
- No human WWOX-DEE material.
- It does not establish that ERK loss *causes* the skin phenotype — the wording is *"partly due
  to"* and *"may be partly through"*.
- It offers no clinical recommendation and names no drug.

## 7 · Caveats the authors give themselves

They state that WWOX has pleiotropic functions; that the mechanism linking WWOX to E-cadherin is
unknown; that whether WWOX controls differentiation *through* p63 is unclear; that whether the
fat loss is a consequence of metabolic dysregulation *"remains to be investigated"*; and that the
K5-Cre conditional knockout of Ferguson 2012 had a morphologically **normal** two-layer epidermis,
which they do not reconcile with their own whole-body findings beyond noting the difference.

## 10 · Earned result

This is a peripheral, non-neural, constitutive-null phenotype. Its transfer to WWOX-DEE is **low**:
nothing about keratinocytes predicts a neuron, and nothing about a constitutive null predicts a
compound heterozygote with residual function. What it contributes is (a) the measured
heterozygote-equals-wild-type negative in skin, (b) a clearly-scoped rejected biomarker candidate,
and (c) the clearest statement in the batch that WWOX's effect is cell-context dependent.

*Nothing here is medical advice.*
