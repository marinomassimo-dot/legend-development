## 🔴 THE FINDING: a figure generated by a language model contradicts the running text, and the framework's figure rule points the wrong way

Section 1 lists **six** categories of neurodevelopmental gene at common fragile sites:

> "synapse organization (GRIK2, GPHN), differ[entiation of olfactory bulb interneurons]
> (POU3F2, SPOCK1), neuron projection development (TIAM1, CTNND2), **glutamate receptor
> signaling pathways (GRID1, GRM5)**, central nervous system (CNS) neuron differentiation
> (FGF13, PPFIA2), and axonogenesis (SLIT2, EPHA2)"

**Figure 2 shows five.** The glutamate-receptor category is gone, **GRID1 does not appear
anywhere in the figure**, and **GRM5 has been moved under "Neuron projection development"**
beside TIAM1 and CTNND2 — a category the text assigns to those two genes alone. The caption
enumerates the same five and also omits glutamate-receptor signalling, so **caption and panel
agree with each other and both differ from the body.**

The caption's last sentence is:

> "This figure was prepared using Gemini."

### Why this is a framework finding and not a complaint about a paper

Rule 5c privileges the panel over the prose, and it earned that: on 2026-08-04 a panel reversed
a conclusion the running text did not contain. **But that rule was written for data panels**,
where the panel is the observation and the prose is the summary of it. This panel is a
*rendering downstream of the prose*, and its own caption says so.

A reader applying the 5c default here would carry away that GRM5 is a neuron-projection gene
and that GRID1 is not a CFS-linked neurodevelopmental gene at all. **Both are wrong, and the
text was right.**

🔴 `PREMISE_TAG` — the load-bearing premise of rule 5c is *a figure is an observation*.
**`PREMISE: DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK`**, and false for this class of figure.

The discriminator is cheap and already present in the document: **the caption declares the
generator.** Four figures, four declarations — FigureLabs (Fig. 1), Gemini (Fig. 2), Biorender
(Fig. 3), Biorender and Figure Labs (Fig. 4). A manifest can be required to classify a
declared-generated figure as a **schematic** surface, where the precedence of panel over text
is reversed and no proposition may rest on it as evidence.

### And it exposes a gap in the vocabulary we adopted today

`text_contradicted_by_panel` **presumes the panel is data.** Figure 3 shows the same problem
from the other side: its caption says the treatment *"suppresses seizure occurrence"* and its
panel says **"No Seizures Occurence"** [sic], with no dose, no vector, no fraction of animals
and no time point — the figure contains no data at all. Against `PMID 42422765` from this same
laboratory, where survival has a threshold between 1.23E11 and 2.63E11 vg and **no treated arm
survives at 4E10 or 8E10**, "No Seizures" with no dose attached is the favourable half of a
dose-dependent result.

Both figure entries are stamped `text_contradicted_by_panel` with `contradicts` pointing at the
text entry, because the relation is real *as a discrepancy*. **It is meaningless as evidence,
and the value carries no way to say which.** That is the gap.

## What changes a decision in this repository

### 🔴 WWOX protein abundance is not a readout of WWOX function — demonstrated in vivo

> "WWOX protein levels in P47T mice remained comparable to wild-type controls"

…while the mutation disrupts the **WW1 domain's PPXY motif**, producing partial loss of
function through *failed interaction*, not through loss of protein. A Tier 1/2 biomarker built
on WWOX abundance would score this allele **normal** in an animal with adult-onset seizures,
cerebellar neurodegeneration and ataxia.

This is the **function** axis failing while **synthesis** and **solubility** pass — the same
five-way separation the 2026-07-12 thyroid-cancer case forced on this repository, now
demonstrated *in vivo for a disease allele*.

**And the bracket closes from the other side.** SCAR12-derived forebrain organoids resemble
healthy controls in morphology, layer markers and E/I ratio while displaying *"minimal WWOX
expression"*. Normal protein with a severe phenotype; near-absent protein with a near-normal
organoid. **WWOX abundance predicts neither the presence nor the absence of the phenotype.**

### 🔴 The three-class genotype–phenotype framework is published and falsified in the same section

The framework: **(1)** biallelic null → severe, early-lethal; **(2)** compound het null/missense
→ intermediate; **(3)** biallelic hypomorphic missense → milder, SCAR12-like. Gene-dosage model,
residual function tracking severity.

The counterexamples **the review itself supplies**:

| case | genotype class | observed |
|---|---|---|
| intron-4 deletion → exon-5 skipping (Oliver 2023) | 2 | severity of class 1 |
| six predominantly missense patients (Havali 2021) | 3 | spans the whole WOREE↔SCAR12 range |
| **homozygous p.Ser304Tyr missense (Feng 2024)** | 3 | **rapid deterioration, death in early infancy** |

> "outlier cases and inconsistencies in clinical studies caution against over-[reliance]"

🔴 **"Missense ⇒ residual function ⇒ milder" is the premise under much of the hypomorph and ASO
reasoning here, and it has a published counterexample that kills in infancy.**
`PREMISE: DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK` — and the textbook is wrong for at least one allele.

### 🔴 A requirement only a challenge reveals

| model | baseline | under cuprizone |
|---|---|---|
| `Olig2-Cre; O-KO` | **"No major defects"** | *"Defective oligodendrocyte differentiation and impaired remyelination"* |

Both rows cite Abudiab et al. 2025, and the review calls the second a **cell-autonomous**
function. **An unchallenged oligodendroglial readout is a designed false negative for this
axis** — the silent, self-reinforcing kind the epistemic-discipline section warns about, with
the trigger now identified: *the phenotype requires a stressor to exist.*

The neuronal side is non-cell-autonomous and stated as such — hypomyelination arises *"even
though WWOX remains intact"* in oligodendrocytes. **WWOX acts on myelination through both
routes, and each is invisible in the assay that isolates the other.**

### A degenerative component inside a neurodevelopmental disorder

Oliver 2023, 13 new + 62 reported: focal seizures 85%, epileptic spasms 77%, tonic 69%, onset
typically within five weeks, mostly drug-resistant. **Repeated scans at different time points
showed *progressive* cerebral atrophy.** A cross-sectional imaging endpoint cannot see that by
construction. And the Kaplan-Meier in the same study — biallelic null with significantly
reduced survival against ≥1 missense allele — means **survival analyses must stratify on allele
class or they measure genotype mix.**

### An unexamined pharmacological handle

Breton 2021, neocortical slices: layer II/III bursts absent in controls, delta and theta power
up, sEPSCs up in frequency *and* amplitude, sIPSCs down in frequency, depolarised RMP, reduced
AP threshold, increased firing probability — and 🔴 **"elevated potassium currents were
observed"**, read as a *maladaptive compensatory response*.

A signed, proximal, druggable node with a mature paediatric-CNS pharmacology, and **nothing in
this repository has looked at it.** `IPOTESI`, and the sign is the whole question: if the
elevation is compensatory, opposing it is harmful.

### Two honest negatives, and one tension the review never reconciles

- **WWOX is not differentially expressed** in AD brain transcriptomics (the vascular claim rests
  on its *co-expression* network in perivascular fibroblasts), nor in two large ASD cortical
  transcriptomic studies. A review from the gene's primary laboratory printing two nulls on its
  own gene is a mark of calibration.
- 🔴 **§3 reports that the substantia nigra "[has] no WWOX despite being rich in such cell
  types"** (human IHC, Nunez 2006). **§5 attributes PD dopaminergic loss to a WWOX–JNK axis.**
  Not formally incompatible — but the review states both and reconciles neither, and the
  expression datum is the more direct measurement. Anything built on the PD axis is
  `PREMISE: INFERENZA` with a human-expression observation pointing the other way.

### Two smaller ones worth carrying

- **Metabolism** (Drosophila, preprint, `ESPANSIONE`): Wwox loss → lactate and LDH up via the
  **ATF4** branch of the UPR, **HIF1α-independent**. Rescue restores lifespan and motor function
  and **does not reduce lactate** — it *"lowers L-methionine and suppresses methionine
  metabolism"*. **The marker that moves with the lesion is not the marker that moves with the
  treatment.**
- **Sleep**: rs16948804 and rs4887991 associated with *"shorter self-reported sleep duration and
  reduced time in bed"* in a Korean GWAS; Drosophila LOF shows fragmented daytime sleep
  **independent of circadian disruption** — which makes *architecture*, not phase, the thing to
  measure.
- **UPD**: homozygous `c.172+1G>C` from **maternal uniparental disomy**, exon 2 skipping
  confirmed. Trio sequencing assuming biparental inheritance can misread this — and it adds a
  second canonical splice-site allele to the class the ASO track is built around, the first
  being `c.517-2A>G` in two independent WOREE iPSC lines (`PMID 42397075`).

## The condition of the surface, recorded because it bounds how much its prose can carry

- A **near-verbatim duplicated statement** in §4: *"recent studies have revealed decreased WWOX
  protein levels in AD patients… associated with enhanced tau phosphorylation in the
  hippocampus"* appears **twice within about 280 characters**, with the same two citations.
- **Table 2 describes the article in the third person, twice** — *"is presented in the review
  as"*, *"because the review also notes important exceptions and outlier cases"*. That is the
  voice of a summary **of** the paper, not of a table **in** it.

The finding is **not** about how the text was produced, which I cannot know. It is that **the
ordinary editorial passes that would catch a duplicated paragraph did not run on this
document** — so its prose is a map of the field's current positions, not a carefully weighed
statement of them. Which is exactly how a review should be used anyway, and rarely is.
