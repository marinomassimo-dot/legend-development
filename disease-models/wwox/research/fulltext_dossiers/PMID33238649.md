## What it is

A retrospective single-unit cohort of 2577 preterm neonates (24+0 to 31+6 weeks) in a Dutch tertiary neonatal unit over 16 years, divided into three epochs by birth date. It describes how often fresh-frozen plasma was given and how often coagulation tests were done, and why. It is a utilisation study: it measures practice, not effect. WWOX: zero occurrences.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** The study concerns prematurity, not a genetic disorder.

## What it reports (DATO)

- Plasma use fell from 47 of 824 (5.7 percent) to 30 of 901 to 17 of 852 (2.0 percent), trend p below 0.001; coagulation testing fell from 24.3 to 14.5 to 8.0 percent.
- Of the 94 plasma recipients, indications were abnormal coagulation tests 56 percent, DIC 20, clinical bleeding 17, not recorded 4.
- The unit's guideline did not change over the period; major haemorrhage did not change significantly (p = 0.204; counts not stated).

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Nothing about WWOX or a genetic DEE.** Plasma in a WWOX-deficient neonate or child is not addressed (H2 questions 1 and 5: not testable here).
2. **No evidence that plasma helps or harms.** It is a utilisation study with no outcome comparison by exposure; the authors say whether plasma corrects coagulation tests or reduces bleeding "is unknown".
3. **"Safe to give less" is not shown.** Haemorrhage was not significantly different across epochs, but it is stated only as p = 0.204 with no counts and no power, and the study was not designed to test it. INFERENZA.
4. **The fall is not attributable to one cause.** Coagulation testing fell by two thirds, and the authors themselves link plasma use to testing (most plasma was for abnormal tests); case mix and morbidity also shifted (Supplementary Figures S1-S2: proven sepsis and PDA fell, NEC rose, caesarean sections rose). The time trend is a before-after description with no adjustment. INFERENZA from DATO.
5. **Internal inconsistencies.** (a) The Abstract gives 30 of 901 for the middle epoch and the text and Figure 1 give 3.7 percent, but 30/901 is 3.3 percent. (b) The epoch III PT prolongation is stated as 30.5 percent of 68 tested infants, against 64.5 and 66.4 percent in the other epochs and 64.2 percent overall; the overall 256 of 399 implies about 59 percent for epoch III. (c) Appendix Table A1 prints the day-1 PT interval as 13.0 (10.6-6.2). None changes the direction of the trend.
6. **"Prophylactic" is an assumption.** The paper assumed that a transfusion recorded for abnormal coagulation followed the guideline in a non-bleeding infant and did not check bleeding status; the 17 percent coded as bleeding are a separate row.
7. **Reference ranges are borrowed.** The interval used (30-36 weeks) is applied below 30 weeks and flags aPTT as prolonged in about 90 percent of tested infants, so "abnormal" is mostly a property of the range.

## Design and limits

Retrospective, single centre, three epochs, descriptive tests only, no individual-level adjustment, indication taken from the record.
