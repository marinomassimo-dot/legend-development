## 1 · What the paper is

A two-author narrative review of HIF-1 alpha in hypoxic brain and adult neurogenesis: induction in stroke and hypoxia, time-dependent dual actions, hippocampal and subventricular-zone neurogenesis, Wnt/beta-catenin, Notch and MAPK (EPO, VEGF) pathways, intermittent hypoxia, and HIF-1 alpha activators (Table 1). No data of its own; no WWOX.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. HIF-1 alpha protein rises in an early phase (hours) and again in a late phase (48 to 72 h) after hypoxia, with mRNA induction only late.
2. Timing reverses the sign of intervention: the HIF-1 alpha inhibitor 2-methoxyestradiol 0.5 h after MCAO lowers apoptosis and infarct size; at 8 h it worsens both.
3. HIF-1 alpha acts differently, often oppositely, in neurons, astrocytes and microglia; the review warns against ignoring this.
4. Wnt/beta-catenin (GSK3 beta destruction complex, DVL) is a key regulator of adult neurogenesis; the HIF-1 alpha link to it in neurogenesis is stated as still needing integration; the authors conclude the Wnt, Notch and MAPK relations are co-regulated rather than linear.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- This is the neural HIF1A biology the audit said was missing, but it is stroke, hypoxia and adult neurogenesis, not WWOX loss and not development in a loss-of-function genotype. Its main usable fact is the sign reversal with timing: any argument that lowering HIF1A in a WWOX-deficient brain would help must specify the window, and a chronic elevation (as WWOX loss would imply) has no measured analogue here.
- WWOX binds DVL and GSK3B is in the Wnt destruction complex: both nodes appear in the review's Wnt description, but the review never mentions WWOX. The overlap is a graph adjacency, not evidence.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Any WWOX statement; any chronic-HIF1A effect in developing brain.
- A linear hierarchy HIF-1 alpha to Wnt, Notch, MAPK (the authors say co-regulated).
