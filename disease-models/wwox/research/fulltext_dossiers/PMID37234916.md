## What it is

A knockout-mouse study: constitutive C3-null and C4B-null female mice against wild type given PTZ at 80 or 50 mg/kg; seizure scores and mortality; cortical mRNA of immediate early genes, BDNF, GABRA2 and cytokines at about 9 minutes; Egr1 protein; rotarod and Barnes maze; serum blots confirming genotype (Supplement). WWOX: zero occurrences.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** Complement-deficient mice are a general demonstration that removing a circulating and local complement component can worsen, not reduce, seizure outcome.

## What it measures (DATO)

C4B-null mice: 6 of 6 died after 80 mg/kg and 3 of 5 after 50 mg/kg PTZ, with scores 5-6, against 0 deaths in wild type; C3-null mice had lower scores (2-3) at 80 mg/kg and no deaths at 50. In C4B-null cortex Egr1 mRNA and protein were low at baseline and nine of ten immediate early genes plus BDNF and three cytokines failed to rise at 9 minutes; both knockouts had higher PSD95 and IL-6 baselines and poorer rotarod learning; C4B-null had worse Barnes-maze performance.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **It is a germline knockout of a complement component in every tissue.** The result cannot say whether the effect arises from circulating C4, from brain-produced C4, from development, or from any one cell type; the authors did not measure activated C4 in brain. It says nothing about a therapeutic lowering of a circulating factor in a later-life setting, nor about plasma products (which would add rather than remove complement).
2. **Survivor-selected expression data.** C4B-null mice die 6-12 minutes after PTZ, and tissue was taken at 9 plus or minus 1 minute only from animals still alive, in a state of severe seizures; failure to induce immediate early genes at that moment may reflect state, timing or imminent death rather than a specific defect. INFERENZA.
3. **Causal chain is a hypothesis.** C4 activation causing immediate early gene induction (Figure 7B) is not tested by rescue, C4 deposition or a CSMD1 experiment; the authors say the Egr1-behaviour link is association only.
4. **Small groups, female only.** n = 5-6 per mortality group, 4 per expression group, 3 for the immunoblot and rotarod, and the Barnes maze analysis treats 4 mice x 3 trials as 12 observations; one sex.
5. **C3-null lower scores are explained by a single mRNA** (GABRA2, 2.8-fold) with "might explain"; C3-null also shows the highest IL-1beta after PTZ (about 6 against 2.5), against the text's grouping of knockouts.
6. **Not WWOX, not a developmental or genetic DEE model, not a plasma intervention.** The paper's own Introduction calls the role of complement in epilepsy "complex and controversial", with C1q-null mice also seizure-prone.

## Design and limits

Constitutive knockouts purchased as B6.129S4 designations (not backcrossing-verified here), single chemoconvulsant, 10-minute scoring window with latency censored at 600 s for non-seizing mice, one tissue (frontal cortex), no protein-level IEG data beyond Egr1.

## Horizontal impact on the disease model

- H2 question 2 answered: complement lowering is not uniformly protective; removing C4B was harmful and removing C3 lowered seizure scores in the same assay, so the sign depends on the component. This is a counter-lesson to complement-inhibition hypotheses (read with the review and the C1q and C3 mouse studies earlier in this wave), not a WWOX statement.
