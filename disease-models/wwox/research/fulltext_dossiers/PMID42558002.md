## 1 · What the paper did

A narrative review of astrocyte phenotypes in monogenic epilepsies (Table 1) and in neurodevelopmental disorders with seizures (Table 2). Inclusion rule: astrocyte function investigated in a model carrying a known pathogenic variant. Each row records the primary astrocyte phenotype, whether the authors judge it reactive or cell-intrinsic, the seizure phenotype, the model, the animal age, the interval between evaluation and seizure onset, the presence of status epilepticus, and any therapeutic implication.

## 2 · The WWOX content, at class level

Four rows and one paragraph. WWOX appears 26 times in the article, 23 of them inside Table 1 rows and the WWOX paragraph; the rest are reference entries.

| Row | Model, as the review records it | Astrocyte phenotype | Reactive or intrinsic? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hussain 2019 | `Wwox−/−` mice, 2 weeks old | astrogliosis, TNF-a and IL-6 up | **Unclear** |

The paragraph's argument: astrogliosis appears in the constitutive null without reported seizures and in the P47T model with spontaneous seizures; the organoid model shows GFAP/S100b increases partially reversed by restoring WWOX; and because the astrocyte conditional knockout shows no gross phenotype while the neuronal conditional knockout does show GFAP upregulation, the review concludes that astrocyte changes in WWOX disease "occur as a result of neuronal dysfunction and seizure activity".

## 3 · What the review does NOT state

- It gives **no new astrocyte measurement** in any WWOX model, and no astrocyte-intrinsic WWOX experiment exists in its tables: every WWOX row is marked **Unclear** on the reactive-versus-intrinsic axis — the only axis that would answer cell autonomy.
- It never discusses oligodendrocytes in the WWOX context, although its own Repudi row lists the Olig2-Cre knockout among the models. (Legitimately: the review is about astrocytes.)
- It reports no calcium, potassium-buffering, glutamate-clearance, gap-junction or metabolic measurement for WWOX — the axes on which it does report data for SCN1A, SCN8A, SLC6A1, MECP2 and ALDH7A1.
- It makes no claim about seizure onset age in any WWOX model; it marks the information as not reported.
- It proposes no therapy for WWOX beyond "Phenotypes reversed by restoring WWOX" copied from the organoid primary.

## 4 · Caveats the authors give themselves

- Conclusion: "Due to the complex and interdependent networks between glial cells and neurons, it can be difficult to establish the relative contribution of astrocytes."
- The review repeatedly marks its own uncertainty in the table ("Unclear", "Not defined", "N/A").

## 5 · Three statements I checked against primaries LEGEND already holds

1. **"iPSCs edited to carry a patient variant" is wrong for the organoid primary.** PMID 34268881's own abstract states brain organoids "from CRISPR-engineered human ES cells **and** from patient-derived iPSCs" — the edited line is an engineered knockout in ES cells, and the patient allele line is derived, not edited. The review's Table 1 row is correct on this; only the narrative sentence conflates the two.
2. **"S100b, GFAP, AQP4 and ALDH1A1 … upregulated by week 24"** — the organoid primary does contain "qPCR analysis of astrocytic markers in COs at week 24", so the sentence is traceable, but the strings `AQP4` and `ALDH1A1` occur **zero times** in the primary's JATS XML, including its figure legends. The marker identities live in the panel image, which I did not inspect. The review's sentence is therefore not checkable against the primary's text, and I do not carry those two markers forward.
3. **The Repudi sentence is faithful, the inference drawn from it is not.** The primary's figure legend reads: deletion "either in oligodendrocytes (O-KO) or in astrocytes (G-KO) does not cause phenotypic abnormalities, such as developmental delay … weight loss … and postnatal lethality". That is a negative on **three gross organismal measures**, measured in the astrocyte *and* oligodendrocyte knockouts; it is not a negative on astrocyte function, seizures or network excitability, none of which the G-KO was tested for in those panels. The review's conclusion ("indicating that changes in astrocyte phenotypes occur **as a result of** neuronal dysfunction") converts that negative plus a correlation (GFAP up in the neuronal knockout) into a causal direction.

## 6 · Two further observations

- **The table hardens an absence of reporting into a measured negative.** The narrative says of the constitutive null "although no seizure activity was reported"; the Table 1 cell for the same row reads **"No seizures"**. A reader who takes the table at face value reads a measured negative where the review means a silent primary.
- **Three of the four WWOX rows come from two laboratories** (Hussain ×2; Steinberg and Repudi from the same group). The review's WWOX section is therefore not independent corroboration across labs, which is exactly what one would want from a review for the neuroinflammation claim.
