## Verdict — a split, and the second half fails

| What PMID 19500159 says, citing this paper | What this paper says |
|---|---|
| *"28-day-old lde/lde rats had normal levels of these constituents"* (total protein, glucose, cholesterol, electrolytes) | ✅ **Confirmed.** Glucose, calcium, Na⁺, K⁺, Cl⁻ and triglycerides are all comparable between normal and mutant. |
| *"Normal levels of serum constituents **clearly excluded** the possibility that these abnormal excitabilities are resulted from a systemic metabolic disorder"* | ⚠️ **Overstated.** BUN is ~3.2–3.5× normal in both sexes and creatinine is significantly raised. "Normal levels of serum constituents" is false as a blanket statement. |
| *"The cause of severe dwarfism … may be related, at least in part, to **lower levels of pituitary growth hormone** (Suzuki et al. 2007)"* | 🔴 **Refuted by its own source.** The GH difference was **not statistically significant**; GH-producing cells were present; and the paper concludes the dwarfism *"cannot be explained solely by low levels of plasma GH"*. |

### 🔴 The GH misattribution, and where it came from

This paper's **abstract** says: *"as well as decreased concentrations of plasma growth
hormone"* — unqualified.

This paper's **body** says: *"although the plasma GH level was slightly lower in mutant than
in normal rats, the difference was not significant, suggesting that the severe type of
dwarfism observed in lde/lde rats **cannot be explained solely by low levels of plasma GH**."*
Figure 6C shows the two pairs of bars with substantially overlapping error bars.

**The abstract overstates its own body text, and the 2009 paper cited the abstract's version.**

This is the third instance today of a citation firming up its source, and the first where the
overstatement originates *inside the source's own abstract*. It is also the cleanest possible
vindication of the rule that stopped this session from closing `FT-041` on the abstract the
operator supplied: had that abstract been accepted as a reading, **"decreased plasma GH" would
have entered the model as a datum, and it is not one.**

## The renal question — resolved, and reopened better

Table 2, read row by row (n = 4 normal, 5 mutant per sex):

| **BUN** (mg/ml) | 12.6 ± 4.3 | **40.3 ± 3.7** *(P<0.05)* | 10.1 ± 2.7 | **35.6 ± 12.8** *(P<0.01)* |

So the rat is **uraemic without being hypoglycaemic, hypocalcaemic or electrolyte-deranged** —
the exact opposite profile from the mouse null, which is *all* of those.

### 🔴 And the authors offer an explanation nobody has tested

> *"Although these are clinical markers of renal insufficiency, our histologic examination did
> not reveal any marked pathologic alteration in mutant kidneys. Furthermore, urostick tests
> and blood examinations did not identify any manifestations of proteinuria and anemia.
> Therefore, if renal excretive function is reduced in the mutant rats, the degree of
> dysfunction may not be severe. **Alternatively, the increases in plasma BUN and CRE may be
> related to the increased production of these compounds.** … the production of urea-nitrogen
> and creatinine may be increased due to **hypercatabolism and muscle disruption**."*

With a named precedent: the **SER strain** shows elevated BUN alongside growth retardation and
motile seizures.

**This directly competes with the renal-tubular-acidosis hypothesis that PMID 19936220 built
for the mouse on the strength of the same marker** — and that paper never considered it. An
animal having repeated tonic–clonic convulsions, severely wasted and ataxic, has an obvious
non-renal route to raised urea and creatinine.

Field density, measured 2026-08-06: `WWOX AND ("blood urea nitrogen" OR creatinine)` returns
**1** record. `WWOX AND (hypercatabolism OR "muscle disruption")` returns **0**. Elevated BUN
is now documented first-hand in **three** places — the mouse null, this rat, and by explicit
cross-citation between them — with **three different implied explanations and zero follow-up.**

The discriminating experiment is named by the authors themselves, along with why they could
not do it: renal clearance testing, impractical because the dwarfism and lethality make urine
collection difficult. In the mouse the same test would need paired creatine-kinase and
muscle-mass measures to separate the two routes.

## The phenotype, first-hand

- **Seizures: 33.8% (22/65) males, 33.9% (19/56) females**, onset **16–63 d**. Each animal
  observed >6 h/day until death. Three patterns: type I wild running → generalised tonic–clonic;
  type II wild running only; type III alternating wild-running-like and clonic convulsions.
- 🔴 **The authors state 33% is a floor, not a rate:** *"A limitation in our ability to detect
  spontaneous seizures and the early death of the mutant rats before seizure detection may
  reduce the apparent incidence of seizures in these rats."* — the identical argument PMID
  19500159 later applies to the mouse, made here about the rat itself.
- **Ataxic gait in 95%** of mutants, **0%** of normals. Near-fully penetrant, cleaner than the
  seizures — and the phenotype the downstream literature does *not* carry.
  🔴 **Not cerebellar:** *"we did not detect any marked pathologic changes in the cerebella"*.
  So the ataxia has no structural explanation in this paper.
- **Survival:** all mutant males dead by 77 d, females by 84 d; normal mortality **1.5%**.
- **Audiogenic already known in 2004:** *"an audio sound at 15 KHz (88 dB) induced convulsions
  in 90% of mutant dwarf rats and 0% of normal rats"*, citing a conference abstract. The
  formal study is `FT-042`, five years later.
- **Vacuoles** in CA1 and amygdala, absent in normals; dentate gyrus and CA3 appear spared in
  the image. Regionally restricted, and *"not been reported in human epileptic disease and
  other animal models of epilepsy"*.
- **Testis:** relative weight 288.1 → 159.4; apoptotic germ cells, no spermatocytes, immature
  spindle-shaped Leydig cells.
- **Brain sparing, again and quantified:** absolute mutant brain >90% of normal, relative
  brain **about twice** normal. In males the absolute reduction is **not** significant. The
  authors note two other dwarf rat strains show the same and argue it is GH-independent.
- **Inheritance:** χ² 0.47, P>0.50 for single autosomal recessive (228 normal / 83 mutant).
  ⚠️ Survivor-selected: mutants appeared in 83 of 254 litters, but only the **33** litters in
  which every low-weight pup survived to 21 d entered the test — the same structural caveat as
  the mouse paper's day-3 χ².
- **Cause of death: explicitly unknown.** *"repeated seizures, small body size, and ataxia may
  be disadvantageous for their survival."*

## What makes this paper unusually credible

It was accepted in **April 2007, two years before the same group mapped `lde` to `Wwox`.**
None of its 35 references is WWOX-related, because none could be. The seizure, dwarfism,
ataxia and hippocampal-vacuolation phenotypes were therefore established **before anyone knew
which gene was involved** — they cannot be a WWOX-motivated group finding a WWOX-shaped result.

It also states its own limitations without being asked: the seizure incidence is a floor, the
renal claim is weak and its test infeasible, the cause of death is unknown, and the dwarfism
is not explained by GH. That is more epistemic hygiene than the 2009 paper that cites it
applied when summarising it.
