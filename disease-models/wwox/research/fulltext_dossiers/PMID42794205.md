## What it is

A retrospective single-centre secondary analysis of 142 outborn neonates with HIE cooled 2014-2022: how many received blood components or coagulation products (52.1 percent; plasma 38.7 percent, prothrombin complex concentrate 20.4, antithrombin 14.1, red cells 10.6, platelets 9.9), what predicted exposure (visible bleeding, respiratory support), and unadjusted MRI findings. WWOX: zero occurrences.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** Acquired perinatal encephalopathy, not a genetic DEE.

## What it measures (DATO)

Transfusion exposure and its clinical correlates within one unit's institutional thresholds (red cells below 10 or 11 g/dL; plasma by INR and fibrinogen; platelets below 25). Transfused infants were sicker (Apgar, base deficit, lactate, respiratory support, seizures).

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No causal effect of transfusion on the brain.** The authors state the MRI comparison is unadjusted and that exposure is a proxy for severity.
2. **The text overstates the MRI contrast.** It says intracranial bleeding and hypoxic-ischaemic injury were more frequent in transfused infants; Table 4 shows intracranial bleeding essentially equal (21.6 versus 22.1 percent) and the difference is in hypoxic-ischaemic injury (28.4 versus 10.3 percent). A panel/table-versus-text contradiction on one of the two named findings.
3. **Figure 1 disagrees with the text on product combinations** (18 percent FFP-only equals 26 infants, the number the text gives for two or more products; the combination sectors sum to about 35 percent). The text and figure cannot both be right; the likely reading is a transposition (INFERENZA).
4. **Wide, fragile estimates.** Visible bleeding aOR 12.95 (1.34-124.97) from 16 infants; a few percentage digits differ between text and tables (respiratory-support interval 7.45 versus 7.48).
5. **Plasma use is described, not justified.** Plasma was the most-used product (38.7 percent) under INR-based thresholds that the authors say are largely extrapolated from general neonatal practice; no outcome benefit of plasma is tested. For H2: this is a use-pattern paper, not an efficacy paper.
6. **No death occurred and no neurodevelopmental follow-up exists** in the dataset, so no outcome is available even for association.

## Design and limits

Retrospective, single-centre, outborn infants (transport confounds timing), coagulopathy not separable from cooling, no adjustment of the MRI comparison.
