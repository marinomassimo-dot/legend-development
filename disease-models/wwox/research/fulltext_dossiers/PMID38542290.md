## 1 · What the paper is

A rat anaphylactic-shock study. The headline is an O-GlcNAcase inhibitor (NButGT) given one hour before shock that adds to epinephrine. The part relevant to group H3 is what the epinephrine-alone arm shows: pressure and survival restored, cardiac mitochondrial respiration still 29 to 40 percent below control, and lactate high.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. Shock alone: blood pressure and cardiac output collapse, rats die within about 20 minutes; complex II respiration down 36 percent at 15 minutes.
2. Epinephrine (infusion titrated to a pressure target, total about 880 micrograms per kg): pressure returned to near target by 20 to 25 minutes, survival to 60 minutes; left-ventricular shortening fraction up to 100 percent; cardiac output only partly improved (15 against 62 mL/min); respiration still impaired at 60 minutes; lactate 4.8 mmol/L against control.
3. The adjuvant, only when given before shock, raised O-GlcNAcylation, restored complexes I and II, lifted cardiac output to 35 mL/min and reduced lactate to 3.6.
4. The authors say epinephrine "might even provoke" cardiomyocyte dysfunction and that the adjuvant lacks evidence for trials.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- No registry record touches epinephrine or lactate-as-drug-effect. The wave-1 reading of the lactate readout in Wwox-null cells and of the Wang 2025 brain-lactate result both warned that lactate has many sources; this paper adds another: epinephrine-treated shock leaves lactate several times control while pressure is restored.
- The comparison cannot separate three sources for the residual respiratory and lactate abnormality: the shock itself, epinephrine, and the anaesthetic and ventilation conditions, because the design omits the groups that would (epinephrine in non-shocked rats; shock alone at 60 minutes, impossible as they die).
- Mitochondrial complex impairment under a catecholamine is a candidate concept for a WWOX-null setting only by analogy; here the respiratory data come from heart muscle in a hypovolaemic-vasoplegic shock.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Any WWOX statement.
- That epinephrine harms cardiac mitochondria: no epinephrine-versus-no-epinephrine contrast under matched shock exists beyond survival.
- That the adjuvant is clinically usable: the authors themselves say not.
- That the shortening fraction measures contractility here: it saturates at 100 percent with a collapsed cavity.
- Any statement about brain, seizures, neonates or a genetic disease; or about the dose of epinephrine in any patient.

## 5 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype (WWOX-DEE)

ESPANSIONE, minimal. System gap: adult rat, anaesthetised, whole-body shock, heart tissue. What transfers is a design warning: an epinephrine arm needs an epinephrine-alone control and a time-matched shock control before any respiratory or lactate change is read as an epinephrine effect.

## 6 · Epistemic tags and reopening

- DATO: epinephrine restored pressure and 60-minute survival but not cardiac respiration or lactate in this model.
- INFERENZA (authors): epinephrine may provoke cardiomyocyte dysfunction. Not testable with this design.
- IPOTESI (ours, not a claim): none for WWOX. PREMISE: DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK (beta-adrenergic stimulation raises glycolytic lactate).
- Rejected: citing this paper as evidence that epinephrine impairs mitochondria. REVIVAL_TRIGGER: an epinephrine-only arm in non-shocked animals with fibre respirometry.
- BLOCK-1 caution: nothing here is dosing guidance; the epinephrine regimen is an experimental infusion in anaesthetised rats.
