## 1 · What the paper is

A narrative review of how seizures reach brainstem cardiorespiratory centres in mice and how this may produce SUDEP: propagation and spreading depression, the autonomic brainstem network, ion-channel genetic mouse models (Table 1), and techniques (EEG, optrodes, imaging, photometry, ECG and plethysmography). It is the SUDEP and autonomic leg of the H3 substitute set.

## 2 · Findings as the source gives them (first pass)

1. SUDEP is defined to exclude status epilepticus; incidence 1 to 5 per 1000 patients per year in drug-resistant epilepsy; most follow convulsive seizures, many in sleep.
2. The common final pathway is postictal brainstem dysfunction (apnea, bradycardia, asystole); spreading depression into the brainstem is the best-characterised mouse mechanism, mostly in channelopathy models.
3. Catecholamines appear three times, each as a single hedged sentence with a cited source: sympathetic tone and neuropeptides can follow seizures; norepinephrine is one of nine respiratory neuromodulators several of which rise during and after seizures; norepinephrine with repeated hypoxia may destabilise pre-Botzinger function.
4. Table 1 and Figure 3 list ion-channel models only; developmental and epileptic encephalopathies are named generically.
5. The human recorded-SUDEP series are biased toward focal presurgical cases.

## 3 · Comparison with the laboratory's records

- The two registry records that mention SUDEP use it only as a reason to keep seizure control. The review adds a definitional point the laboratory should not blur: by this definition a death in status epilepticus is not SUDEP, so the documented status-epilepticus deaths in WWOX cannot be counted as SUDEP.
- No registry record carries an autonomic or catecholamine measure for WWOX; this review shows what such a measurement set would be (ECG, plethysmography, telemetry) but nothing in the repository records says it was ever applied to a Wwox model.
- The review does not weigh catecholamine surge as a SUDEP cause against alternatives; it offers brainstem spreading depression, arrhythmia and drug effects as co-candidates.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT support

- Any WWOX statement; WWOX is not named, and the genetic models are channelopathies.
- A quantitative role for catecholamines in SUDEP: no value, no timing, no causal test appears in the review.
- Any statement about adrenaline or epinephrine given as a drug.
- That rodent SUDEP mechanisms generalise to infants with a developmental and epileptic encephalopathy (the review itself says human data are small and biased).
