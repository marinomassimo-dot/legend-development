## What the paper did

A PRISMA review (PubMed and Scopus, 2000-2020) of monogenic neonatal-onset epilepsy or DE/EE with a movement disorder (MD) at any age: 49 papers, 27-28 genes (the text gives both numbers). Table S1 summarises each gene's included cases.

## What it says about WWOX — and where it comes from

- **Every WWOX statement rests on one source, Piard 2019 (`PAPER 117`; reference 46).** "5/20 cases had neonatal-onset epilepsy with associated MD", one more with suspected paroxysmal non-epileptic events. Table S1 lists six genotype lines; each matches a Piard Supplemental Table 1 patient (1, 2, 3, 4, 9, 11). **No new patient.**
- The review's distinctive claim: the hypokinetic MD from the neonatal period "only with WWOX" (Abstract), "highly suggestive for WWOX-related disorders" (Discussion). Table S1 counts **"Hypokinesia: 5"**.
- **What the primary actually records for those six (read after the first pass, Piard Supplemental Table 1):** in the "Movement disorder" row — patient 1 pedalling and boxing movements possibly non-epileptic; patient 2 **no**; patient 3 dystonic upper-limb movements; patient 4 myoclonus and dystonic upper-limb movements; patient 9 limited voluntary movement; patient 11 brief involuntary and startle movements. In the separate "Poor spontaneous movements" row: yes for 2, 3, 4, 9 and 11, no for 1 — **five**. The only Piard patient whose movement disorder is written "hypokinetic movements" is patient 8, who is **not** among the review's six. The review's "hypokinesia: 5" is therefore the primary's **"poor spontaneous movements"** re-labelled as a movement disorder; Piard reports poor spontaneous movements in 17 of 19 of its whole cohort, neonatal onset or not.
- The authors themselves name the recognition bias ("hyperkinetic MD might be easier to recognize").

## What the paper does NOT say

- It does not define "hypokinetic" for the WWOX cases or distinguish it from the motor poverty of profound developmental impairment.
- It does not test whether poor spontaneous movement is commoner in WWOX than in the other 26 genes' profoundly impaired infants; the comparison is across descriptive vocabularies of different source papers.
- Table S1 also lists hypokinesia or rigidity for GRIA2 and STXBP1 (later onset), consistent with the text.
