## What the paper did

Cell biology of a WWOX/TIAF1/p53 complex: Wwox+/+, +/−, −/− MEF signalling and migration; overexpression in
breast, lung, fibroblast and COS7 cells; tri-molecular FRET; Ras-rescue yeast two-hybrid; SMAD-promoter reporter;
soft agar; and one xenograft experiment — p53-null NCI-H1299 lung-cancer cells transiently transfected with p53
and/or WWOX, injected into nude mice, with western blots of the hosts' brain and lung at day 86.

## What it measured that bears on the brain

- **Only the xenograft experiment (Figure 7).** The mice are nude mice with intact Wwox; the "brain aggregation" is
  in the host brain of a mouse carrying a flank tumour, not in a WWOX-deficient brain.
- **n = 1 per condition, as far as the figure shows.** One lane per group (EGFP, WWOX, p53, WWOX/p53) in every blot
  (Figure 7c, inspected at publisher resolution); the legend says "the mouse harboring WWOX-expressing
  NCI-H1299" and "A representative data is shown from two experiments". Tumour curves carry no error bars.
- **The readout is high-molecular-weight signal on non-reducing gels.** It appears in lane 4 for pERK, BACE, APP,
  Aβ, NFT **and the housekeeping α-tubulin** ("Housekeeping protein α-tubulin was also aggregated"). A smear that
  includes the loading control is as compatible with a lane-4 sample difference as with specific aggregation
  (`PREMISE: INFERENZA`, mine). No loading normalisation, no densitometry, no statistics.
- **Mechanism unmeasured by the authors' own statement:** "For unknown reasons…"; "We believe that cytokines…" (no
  cytokine measured); "How the tumor cells in the flanks control neurodegeneration in the brain is unknown".
  "Inflammation" is inferred from spleen size alone.

## Internal inconsistencies

- Yeast two-hybrid (Figure 5b, Results): *"the SDR domain of WWOX did not bind TIAF1"*; TIAF1 binds the first WW
  domain.
- FRET section, final sentence: *"WWOX joins the complex via its C-terminal SDR domain to stabilize the complex
  triad"*.
- Discussion: WWOX binds p53 and TIAF1 "via its first N-terminal WW domain".
- "p53 does not bind TIAF1" is attributed to ref 42 in Results, ref 40 in Background and ref 53 in Discussion.

## Independence

- 26 of 56 references are the same laboratory (25 with "Chang NS", 1 as "Chang N").
- The Background sentence *"WWOX deficiency leads to rapid protein aggregation to cause neuronal damage and death
  in vivo"* cites refs 3, 22–26 — **all six from this laboratory** (Liu 2018; Lee 2010; Chang 2012; Chang & Chang
  2016; Chang 2015 TRAPPC6AΔ; Lee 2017 Zfra).
- PubMed, 2026-10-02: `WWOX AND TIAF1` = 9 records; `WWOX AND TIAF1 NOT Chang NS[au]` = **0**. The triad has no
  independent replication. The one independent human-genetic TIAF1 signal LEGEND knows of points the other way
  (`FT-100`, a protective-variant report) — noted, not adjudicated here.

## What the paper does NOT say

Nothing on epilepsy, myelin, white matter, neurodevelopment or any WWOX-DEE allele; no measurement in a WWOX-null
brain; no statistics on the brain blots.

## Transfer limit to the WWOX-DEE genotype class

None. Overexpression in cancer lines plus one tumour-bearing mouse with intact Wwox models neither a destabilizing
SDR missense allele nor a splice-acceptor allele, and not biallelic loss. The brain-aggregation statement is a
single-laboratory, single-animal observation.

## Verdict

**INGEST as an earned null for the brain question.** The title's "brain protein aggregation" rests on one xenograft
experiment the authors cannot explain; the paper adds one more node to the same-laboratory chain already
down-weighted in `DL-BIO-004`, and no independent support.
