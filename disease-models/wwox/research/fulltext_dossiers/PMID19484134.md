## What it is

A cell-biology paper from the laboratory that coined WOX1: in human prostate cancer DU145 cells (plus MCF7, SH-SY5Y and a basal cell carcinoma line) purified C1q, or complement-depleted human serum, is reported to change nuclear accumulation of an in-house Tyr33-phosphorylated WOX1 epitope; C1q increases cell-cycle sub-G1 and DNA fragmentation in cells overexpressing EGFP-WOX1 but not vector cells; dominant-negative WOX1 and Y33R block it; serum depleted of C1q or C6 changes p-ERK, WOX2 isoform, p-JNK1; C9 depletion changes p53 localisation; hyaluronan modulates p53 and STAT3; C1q signal is lower in BPH and cancer than normal post-mortem prostate.

## WWOX content

Direct. The system is **gain of WWOX** in cancer cell lines (overexpression), plus endogenous-WWOX immunofluorescence. **There is no WWOX-deficient arm**: no knockdown, knockout or patient cell; so the paper cannot say what happens to C1q signalling when WWOX is lost. It is the only WWOX-direct complement paper in the repository; its relevance to H2 (plasma, complement) is as a mechanistic pointer, not evidence about plasma products in WWOX loss.

## What it measures (DATO, panel level, new in this resume)

- C1q alone gives about 85 percent nuclear p-WOX1 against about 1 percent in medium (Figure 1E, about 100 cells x 3, in-house antibody).
- Apoptosis (sub-G1) rises with C1q only in WOX1-overexpressing cells, from about 13.5 to about 38 percent at 4 ug/mL; WOX1-overexpressing cells already have about twice the vector baseline sub-G1 (Figure 2B, single representative experiment).
- Effects are largest at low to mid WOX1 doses and saturate or vanish at the highest dose in the supplementary dose series (S1, S3, S4B).
- Depletion experiments show C1q- and C6-depleted sera lower p-ERK and the WOX2 isoform and raise p-JNK1; depletion of C7 does not (Figure 8).

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No loss-of-function inference.** Every killing experiment needs electroporated WOX1; vector cells with endogenous WWOX show no C1q-induced death (the authors' own control). A WWOX-null or hypomorphic cell is never tested, so the paper does not show that loss of WWOX changes C1q responses.
2. **The direction of the paper's phenotype is death.** The outputs are apoptosis and adhesion destabilisation, and the Discussion projects C1q-WOX1 killing of neurons in Alzheimer disease. It is not a rescue or protective axis; in a WWOX-deficient neuron the proposed arc would be absent, not "restored" (INFERENZA from the design).
3. **Overexpression itself is toxic and the dose matters.** WOX1 overexpression alone raises sub-G1 and DNA fragmentation (Figures 2B, 5B, S1, S3); C1q adds on top at low to mid doses and adds nothing, or lowers the value, at the top dose (S1, S3, S4B). The DNA-fragmentation increase in WOX1 cells in Figure 5B is about 15 percent from two experiments without error bars, which is less than the text's "induced by C1q".
4. **dn-WOX1 control is weak.** Figure 3 has no WOX1 plus C1q arm in the same experiment and dn-WOX1 itself raises sub-G1 at 20 ug.
5. **Text and panels disagree in places.** The Results sentence that C7, C8 and C9 "did not support" WOX2 and p-ERK reads the opposite way from Figure 8B (delta-C7 keeps p-ERK about 210 percent and WOX2 about 80 percent). Fragmentation gels in Figure 5 and S4 are smears rather than ladders at this reproduction size.
6. **Antibody and specificity.** p-WOX1 is an in-house rabbit antibody; the Y33R mutant tests the construct, not the antibody; WOX1 protein is unchanged (and +25 percent in one blot) while p-WOX1 falls 50 percent.
7. **Complement independence rests on "data not shown"** (serum-free arm) and a heat-inactivated-serum argument; no receptor is named and Hyal-2 binding is untested.
8. **Tissue comparison is confounded by image acquisition.** The legend states longer exposure for BPH pictures; n = 5 post-mortem samples per group.
9. **A contested model.** The paper itself cites a paper arguing the opposite function for WWOX; the Y33 phosphorylation and WOX2 splicing inferences are the authors' hypotheses.

## Design and limits

In vitro cancer lines, overexpression, representative experiments with several bars lacking error bars, in-house antibodies, vendor complement-depleted sera at 1 percent, no WWOX loss.
