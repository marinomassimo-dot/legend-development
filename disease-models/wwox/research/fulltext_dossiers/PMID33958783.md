## 1 · What the paper did

A genome-wide survival study of 11.2 million variants in 3 821 Parkinson's patients across 31 053 longitudinal visits, with progression to Parkinson's disease dementia as the event. Cox proportional-hazards models per variant, a discovery and a replication population, a combined meta-analysis, and linear mixed models on serial MMSE as support. Polygenic progression and susceptibility scores were then built.

## 2 · The WWOX content, exactly

- **One imputed common variant**, rs8050111, chr16:78.28 Mb, risk allele G, frequency 0.066, **HR 2.12 (95 % CI 1.63–2.75)**, discovery p = 1.08 × 10⁻⁶, replication p = 0.01, combined p = 2.37 × 10⁻⁸. Imputation R² 0.961, concordance 98.9 %.
- The authors class it as **suggestive**: genome-wide significance is reached only in the combined analysis.
- **MMSE mixed model:** carriers decline faster, p = 0.009.
- **Extended Data Fig. 3 legend states**, of its own survival curves, that the hazard ratios and p values shown differ from Table 1 because that analysis coded alleles 0/1/2, and that these p values "were not corrected for multiple hypothesis testing".
- The only statement about WWOX biology is one clause citing the SCAR12 primary.
- 688 of 3 821 patients (18 %) carried at least one of the three progression alleles.

## 3 · The panel finding

The Results end: "TMEM108 and WWOX are both expressed in human brain (Extended Data Fig. 4) and **specifically in dopamine and pyramidal neurons** (Extended Data Fig. 5)."

Extended Data Fig. 5, read as pixels, carries three panels on a shared log₁₀(FPKM+0.01) axis over laser-captured substantia nigra dopamine neurons (n = 86), motor-cortex pyramidal neurons (n = 3), temporal-cortex pyramidal neurons (n = 10), peripheral blood mononuclear cells (n = 4) and fibroblasts (n = 3):

| Gene | neuronal medians | PBMC | fibroblast |
|---|---|---|---|
| RIMS2 | ≈ 1.5 to 1.7 | ≈ −1.9 | ≈ 0.45 |
| TMEM108 | ≈ 0.3 to 0.65 | ≈ −1.0 | ≈ −0.9 |
| **WWOX** | ≈ 0.35, 0.2, 0.1 | **≈ 0.5** | **≈ 0.35** |

For RIMS2 and TMEM108 the panel supports the word "specifically" — three to four log units between neurons and blood. **For WWOX it does not**: the two non-neural cell classes sit at or above the neuronal medians. The sentence is true of its two companions and false of WWOX, and it is the only cell-type statement about WWOX in the paper.

This matters beyond this paper, because a WWOX "expressed specifically in neurons" sentence is exactly the kind of claim that travels into reviews without its panel.

## 4 · What the paper does NOT state

- It makes no functional measurement of WWOX: no expression difference by genotype, no eQTL, no fine-mapping, no demonstration that rs8050111 acts through WWOX. "WWOX" is the **nearest gene** column of Table 1.
- It says nothing about biallelic loss of function, about any coding variant, or about the WWOX-DEE phenotype beyond the one SCAR12 citation.
- It does not claim WWOX is a dementia gene; the polygenic progression score is an aggregate over loci, and the susceptibility score is explicitly non-predictive.
- It reports no brain pathology, no protein and no cell-type-resolved genotype effect.

## 5 · Caveats the authors give themselves

- "Two suggestive association signals"; the WWOX locus is offered for prioritisation, not as a discovery — the discovered locus is RIMS2.
- Extended Data Fig. 3's p values are uncorrected and use a different allele coding from the main table.
- Six further loci reached genome-wide significance in discovery and did not replicate.
- They call for larger longitudinal populations to detect small effects.

## 6 · Transfer limit for the WWOX-DEE model

A common non-coding variant near WWOX, imputed, at 6.6 % frequency, with a hazard ratio for dementia conversion in late-onset Parkinson's disease. There is no dose, no allele class, no measured WWOX quantity, and the gene assignment is positional. It transfers nothing to a biallelic loss-of-function genotype class except the weak background observation that common variation at this locus is associated with cognitive trajectory in an adult neurodegenerative disease. What it does supply to LEGEND is **the primary behind the SNP that wave 6 met second-hand**, with its own authors' word "suggestive" attached, and the panel correction in § 3.
