## 1 · What the paper is

A narrative review of intrathecal gene therapy for neurological disease, organised as preclinical
proof-of-concept (mice, rats, cats, pigs, dogs, sheep, NHPs) then clinical application, with one
large index table. The Orchestrator called it "deliberately the weakest member" of group C and said
to drop it first. **That judgement was wrong.** It is the only source in the group that holds the
*human* evidence base, and the human evidence base is what the group's question asks for.

## 2 · The human intrathecal AAV9 record, as this review reports it

| Programme | Dose (IT) | Ages enrolled | What is reported |
|---|---|---|---|
| SMA — STRONG, phase 1/2 | 6.0 × 10¹³ (n = 3), 1.2 × 10¹⁴ (n = 25), 2.4 × 10¹⁴ (n = 4) | 6–<24 months and 24–60 months | all 32 tolerated administration; **all developed adverse events**; one serious transaminase elevation; no deaths; motor gain vs historical control in the older cohort at 1 year |

| SMA — STRENGTH, phase 3b | 1.2 × 10¹⁴ | 2–<5 (n = 42) and 5–<18 (n = 75) | endpoint met; FDA approval of IT administration **November 2025, for individuals 2 years of age and older** |
| GAN | 3.5 × 10¹³ (n=2), 1.2 × 10¹⁴ (n=4), 1.8 × 10¹⁴ (n=5), 3.5 × 10¹⁴ (n=3) | >6 years (table: 3–99) | generally well tolerated, few treatment-related SAEs; **enrolled both AAV9-seropositive (NAb ≥1:5) and seronegative participants** |

### The regulatory fact that governs the whole group

> "Concerns for DRG toxicity identified in NHPs by other investigators prompted the FDA to issue a
> hold on further enrolment. It was subsequently determined that the STRONG study could not be
> modified sufficiently to address the FDA's concerns, and it was stopped before the high dose
> cohort was fully enrolled."

**NHP DRG histopathology stopped enrolment in a human paediatric intrathecal AAV9 trial.** Whatever
one concludes about how well-quantified the NHP DRG finding is (PMID 41210171: not quantified at
all; PMID 41948127: present in 50% of vehicle controls), it has already functioned as a
programme-stopping signal. A WWOX programme would meet the same gate.

### And the human counter-evidence

Human DRG toxicity is **real but mild and mostly reversible** where it has appeared: two sensory
cases in STEER that improved with symptomatic treatment, and one adult ALS patient with imaging and
electrophysiological signs plus pain that improved but did not fully resolve. Against that, CLN7
(four patients, 5 × 10¹⁴–1 × 10¹⁵ vg — the highest doses in the table) and SPG50 (1 × 10¹⁵ vg) both
assessed DRG explicitly, by MRI **and nerve conduction studies**, and found nothing.

**So the human record does not scale with dose**: the highest IT doses reported (1 × 10¹⁵) produced
no detectable DRG signal, while 1.2 × 10¹⁴ in STEER produced two cases. This argues against a simple
dose-driven DRG mechanism in humans and, given that the CLN7 and SPG50 programmes used triple
immunosuppression while SMA used prednisone alone, is at least consistent with an immune-modifiable
component. **Stated as a HYPOTHESIS generated here, not as the review's claim** — the review does
not make this comparison, the cohorts differ in disease, age and cargo, and absence of a signal in
n = 4 and n = 1 is weak evidence.

## 3 · Table 1 — the NHP rows that name the mechanism

The adverse-event column of the review's index table attributes DRG toxicity to the transgene twice:

- **CLN2 (ICM), AAV9, 3.1 × 10¹³ or 1.1 × 10¹⁴ vg — "DRG toxicity due to overexpression of the gene"**
- **SMA, SMN1, scAAV9, 1.2 × 10¹³ / 3 × 10¹³ / 6 × 10¹³ vg, animals aged 13–19 and 25–34 months —
  "DRG toxicity due to overexpression of the gene at 6 weeks"**
- SPG50, AP4M1, scAAV9, 0.36–3.6 × 10¹² vg, 2–4 years — "Minimal-to-mild DRG toxicity" (and the same
  phrase in the rat row of the same programme)

Two things worth holding:

1. The SMA NHP DRG toxicity occurred at **1.2 × 10¹³–6 × 10¹³ vg**, i.e. at and below the human IT
   dose of 1.2 × 10¹⁴ vg, in **juvenile** macaques. The species gap here is not one of dose.
2. A dog row reports **"Encephalitis associated with a T-cell response to the transgene product"**
   after IT AAV9 **carrying GFP** — a transgene-directed, not capsid-directed, immune injury in a
   large animal. This is the clearest statement in the group that *what the vector expresses* can
   itself be the toxic agent, and it is immune-mediated and therefore in principle modifiable.

## 4 · The review's own conclusion on mechanism, and why it is in tension with PMID 41257285

> "One of the main concerns that have to be addressed in future studies is the DRG toxicity mostly
> related to supraphysiological gene expression levels with strong ubiquitous promoters. The use of
> cell-specific or weaker ubiquitous promoters combined with cell specific or cell targeted capsids
> may overcome this issue."
> — Outstanding questions

This agrees with PMID 41257285 that a **working promoter is necessary** (empty capsid and
promoterless vector were non-toxic there). It disagrees on whether expression *magnitude* is the
graded driver: PMID 41257285 found transgene transcript highest in heart and skeletal muscle, the
tissues *without* pathology, while injury tracked vector-genome load in liver and DRG. **The two
sources are not reconciled here and should not be presented as agreeing.** The actionable overlap —
use a weaker or cell-restricted promoter — survives either way.

## 5 · The immunosuppression pattern, and why it matters for WWOX

Two distinct regimens appear:

- **Prednisone alone** (SMA, 1 mg/kg/day ~2 months) — used where the recipient has *some* endogenous
  protein and the transgene is not predicted to be seen as foreign.
- **Prednisolone/prednisone + sirolimus + tacrolimus** (GAN, CLN7, SPG50) — adopted explicitly
  because the recipient was predicted to have **no endogenous expression of the transgene product**
  and therefore to mount an anti-transgene response. The SPG50 protocol says so directly: the
  regimen was used "to mitigate immune responses and promote tolerance to AP4M1, given the predicted
  absence of endogenous expression for this patient". The GAN trial deliberately enrolled
  CRIM-negative participants "and thus at higher risk of developing an immune response to
  transgene-expressed gigaxonin protein".

PMID 41966056's MPS I case used the same triple regimen (methylprednisolone/prednisone + tacrolimus
+ sirolimus, 48 weeks). **Four independent programmes converge on triple immunosuppression where the
recipient is predicted null for the transgene product.**

A WWOX-DEE recipient with a biallelic loss-of-function genotype is, by construction, in that class.
**The relevant precedent for a WWOX restoration programme is the GAN/CLN7/SPG50 regimen, not SMA's
prednisone alone.** This is an inference from genotype class to immunological class; it is stated
as such and it is the most practically transferable conclusion in this dossier.

## 6 · The closest disease analogue in the group

**CLN7** (neuronal ceroid lipofuscinosis type 7, MFSD8): a paediatric, neurodegenerative,
seizure-bearing disorder, dosed intrathecally with scAAV9 at 5 × 10¹⁴–1 × 10¹⁵ vg in patients aged
1–18 years, under triple immunosuppression, with DRG assessed by MRI and nerve conduction studies,
followed >2 years, reporting "maintenance of respiratory function, maintenance of oral feeding, and
relative stability in their seizures" while the disease nonetheless progressed.

In route, capsid, age band, immunosuppression, DRG monitoring and seizure-bearing
neurodegenerative phenotype, this is the nearest available human precedent for a WWOX-DEE
intrathecal programme. **The transfer limit is the gene**: MFSD8 ≠ WWOX, and the endpoint claim
("deviation from natural history") is uncontrolled, in four patients, against a historical course.

## 7 · A passage whose printed numbers are unusable

> "Side–effect profile was favourable and similar to that seen with earlier IV studies (9.3% for the
> OAV101 IT treated patients and 9.8% for the sham-control treated patients hepatotoxicity), in
> comparison to IT delivery (2.7% for the OAV101 IT treated and 2.0% or the sham-control treated
> patients) but with two notable cases of sensory symptoms that are suggestive of DRG toxicity."

As printed, this compares "IT" to "IT", labels both arms of both comparisons, has a sham-control
hepatotoxicity rate (9.8%) *exceeding* the treated rate (9.3%), and contains a typo ("2.0% or").
At least one "IT" must be "IV", and which one cannot be recovered from the text. **None of these
four percentages may be carried.** The same paragraph calls STRENGTH "a single arm study" while
listing sham groups of 29 and 51 for its two cohorts.

## 8 · What it does NOT state

- Nothing about WWOX, and nothing about epileptic encephalopathy as a class apart from the NCL
  entries.
- No dose per brain mass, per CSF volume or per kilogram for any human IT programme — **all human IT
  doses in this review are fixed totals per patient**, across age ranges as wide as 2 to <18 years
  and 1–18 years.
- No durability data for IT AAV9 in humans; the review flags this as open.
- No account of how DRG toxicity was assessed in STEER (the two cases are described as "sensory
  symptoms", with no statement of nerve conduction or MRI).
- It does not quantify, anywhere, the incidence of DRG toxicity in any human cohort.

## 9 · Correcting one inference I formed before reading Table 1

On the prose alone, the approved IT indication (≥2 years) suggested that infants are not dosed by
this route. **Table 1 shows otherwise**: STRONG enrolled from 6 months, a CMT2S trial
(NCT05152823) lists **2 months–14 years**, a Tay-Sachs trial lists 6 months–12 years, CLN7 lists
from 1 year, and CLN6 lists >1 year. The correct statement is: *the approved* intrathecal indication
begins at 2 years, while *trials* dose intrathecally from the first months of life. Recorded here
because the prose and the table give different impressions and the table is the authority.

## 12 · Verdict

**INGEST** — and the Orchestrator's instruction to drop it first should be reversed. It supplies the
human layer that C1–C5 lack: the dose envelope actually used in patients, the regulatory history of
the DRG question, the immunosuppression pattern keyed to predicted-null recipients, and the nearest
disease precedent. It supplies no primary evidence whatever.
