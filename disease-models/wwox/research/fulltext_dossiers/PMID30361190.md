## What the paper did
Two new probands from two unrelated consanguineous families, studied by chromosomal microarray and exome sequencing, plus a hand-built literature table (Table 1) comparing them with earlier reports.

## What it measured
- **Child 1:** homozygous deletion of exons 3-4 (~11 kb, microarray-confirmed); both parents heterozygous for the loss. Hypsarrhythmia with spasms at seven months; spasms resolved with vigabatrin after a partial response to prednisolone; then other seizure types, improved with levetiracetam. Acquired microcephaly (−3.38 SD at six months), optic atrophy, spastic quadriplegia.
- **Child 2:** homozygous canonical splice-acceptor `c.606-1G>A` (intron 6), an allele already reported by another group; both parents heterozygous. Focal seizures from five weeks, modified hypsarrhythmia at four months, partial response to vigabatrin, clustered spasms at 11 months on four drugs, ventilator-dependent after a tracheostomy at four months. Bilateral posterior polymicrogyria. Head circumference −1.62 SD at 11 months, which the paper calls acquired microcephaly.
- Each child had **one** MRI, at two months (Figure 3 legend; Table 1 row 'Age at MRI').
- Metabolic screening normal in both.
- Neither allele's RNA or protein consequence was measured.

## What it states
- Biallelic null genotypes define a recognisable severe CNS disorder, often with West syndrome.
- The DEE argument: impairment preceded the epileptic encephalopathy and did not improve with better seizure control **"in some of these cases"**.
- 23/23 tabulated patients have severe delay/ID and epilepsy; spastic quadriplegia 14/23.
- Two Mignot 2015 siblings (frameshift + missense P47R) are an "intermediate phenotype" without spastic quadriplegia, microcephaly or MRI abnormality.

## What it does NOT state
- Nothing about parental phenotype beyond child 2's "unremarkable family history" and child 1's "four healthy children" (siblings, genotypes not given). The heterozygous parents are not examined.
- No serial imaging of these children: "progressive brain atrophy, suggestive of neurodegeneration" in the abstract is carried from literature rows, not observed here.
- Family 1 has two affected cousins with a similar disease; their genotypes are not reported and they are not counted.
- It does not cite Tarta-Arsene 2017 (PMID 28721938) and does not include that patient in Table 1.

## Table 1 as an aggregation (the provenance question)
- Printed totals: **13 families / 23 cases**, including this paper's own two children; the families column sums to **14** (2+1+1+4+2+1+1+2).
- Footnote `#`: where MR images were available in the cited papers, one author added features "not described in the manuscript or figure legends". Footnote `&`: ages inferred from clues. Table 1 is therefore partly a **re-description**, not a transcription.
- Gribaa 2007 and Mallaret 2014 are merged into one 2-family / 6-case column (no double count there). Valduga's terminated pregnancy is excluded.
