## 3 · The haploinsufficiency argument, source by source

This is the lot's question. The review makes it in three sentences, and each was set against its primary.

| Review says (entry) | Cites | What the primary actually shows | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loss of one allele "appears not to be deleterious **or carcinogenic** in the longer-lived heterozygous mice" (21) | ref 51 | Heterozygotes normal for postnatal growth, survival, blood and bone (22). The comparison pools wild type and heterozygotes, n = 5, against 3 knockouts (24). Its only neoplasia assessment is nine **18-day-old knockouts** (23). **No sentence names heterozygotes and tumours together, and there is no long-term follow-up.** | "Not deleterious": SUPPORTED, narrowly (early postnatal, small n). **"Or carcinogenic in the longer-lived heterozygous mice": UNSUPPORTED by ref 51.** |
| Loss of a single allele "did not have **any** observable phenotypic effect in the mammary gland" (25) | ref 55 | Heterozygote survival 100% (n = 41) and no tumours beyond a year; the authors state disagreement with speculation that haploinsufficiency is tumorigenic (26). Branching morphometry groups are Cre− WT, Cre+ WT and KO: **no heterozygote group.** | Tumour and survival: SUPPORTED. **"Any observable phenotypic effect": NARROWER** in the source, which did not measure branching in heterozygotes. |
| "Increase tumorigenicity was reported when Wwox heterozygous mice are exposed to chemical carcinogens (50, 53) or when backcrossed into a C3H … background (54)" (27) | refs 50, 53, 54 | Ref 50 Table 1, read on the rendered page (28). **Spontaneous** tumours in untreated heterozygotes at 9–18 months: 10/58 (17.2%) vs 2/60 (3.3%), p = 0.03; lung cancer 9/58 vs 2/60, p = 0.028. ENU-treated, separately: 37/46 (80%) vs 20/42 (48%), p = 0.002. | **OMISSION.** The review reports ref 50's heterozygote tumour excess only under carcinogen, and never its untreated spontaneous excess. That excess directly contradicts the review's own "not carcinogenic" generalisation. |

**Conclusion (INFERENZA, typed).** The review is a **faithful conduit for heterozygote normality in early postnatal and mammary phenotypes**, from its own two conditional models. It is **not** a faithful conduit for the general claim that WWOX haploinsufficiency is benign.

- It attributes the "not carcinogenic" half to a paper that contains no such data.
- It omits the one cited primary reporting the opposite in untreated animals.
- Line (conventional vs conditional), background and age (9–18 months vs weaning) could reconcile the primaries. The review offers none of these, because it never reports the discordant datum.

`PREMISE: DATO` (the three primaries, as read). `REVIVAL_TRIGGER`: a long-term spontaneous-tumour study of conditional (EIIA-Cre) heterozygotes, or a replication of ref 50's spontaneous excess on a matched background. Either would decide which generalisation holds.

**What this does and does not bear on.** Every datum here is mouse, heterozygous, and about tumours, growth, blood, bone or mammary gland. **No heterozygote CNS, seizure or neurodevelopmental phenotype is measured in any of the three primaries.** So nothing in this review licenses a haploinsufficiency statement about the brain, in either direction.

## 4 · Other findings of the census

- **Arithmetic that does not reproduce (10, 11).** Table 1, recounted from the XML, has 36 missense rows with Mutation Assessor High 6 + Medium 13 = **19**, not the text's "18 (50%)". So truncating 8 + 19 = **27 of 44 (61%)**, not "59% (26 of 44)". One call apart. The "unlikely to be background" conclusion is an inference from an algorithm, and no functional assay exists.
- **"All" intrinsic subtypes is broader than its figure (6, 7).** Figure 5 has three subtype panels (Luminal A, Luminal B, Basal-like); there is no HER2-enriched or normal-like panel.
- **Figure 6's curves cross after about 80 months (8).** The ovarian survival separation is early rather than sustained, and the text does not say so.
- **Figure 4 prints "p<0.936" and "p<0.706" (4).** A "<" is printed where an equals sign is meant, and no test is named.
- **Figure 11 cannot show what it is cited for (40, 41).** It is cited for knockout seizures and shows two photographs of a rodent: no EEG, no score, no comparator.
- **CoA metabolism appears once in the review's own analysis (14, 15).** It is a co-expression enrichment in breast datasets, under a premise the authors declare themselves ("guilt by association", 13). It is annotation, not measurement.
- **A declared, unresolved conflict (43).** Another group localises WWOX to mitochondria and nucleus; these authors cannot detect it there. Any compartment-based mechanism inherits this conflict.
- **Alternative splice forms have not been shown to make protein (49),** "to the best of our knowledge", on Western non-detection. This bears on any exon-skipping reasoning and is a negative by absence, not by measurement.
- **Brain is the second-highest tissue for WWOX RNA (17, 18).** It rests on a single-dataset ranking with no n and no error bars. `PREMISE: DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK` flagged: body-map datasets having about one sample per tissue was not verified here.

## 7 · Design limits

- A review. Its three analyses are database re-queries with unstated thresholds (Kaplan-Meier quantile), no multiplicity handling, and one arithmetic slip.
- The authors adjudicate a dispute in which they are a party: their conditional models vs a competing laboratory's conventional line.
- No legend for any figure, and one supplement unreachable.
- Two load-bearing restatements were not verified in this reading: ref 49 (hypomorph lifespan) and ref 101 (human families; knockout seizures). Refs 102, 103 and 81 were likewise not opened.

## 8 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype (WWOX-DEE class)

**T3, and specifically not a haploinsufficiency source for the brain.**

- The CNS section restates the human biallelic families (refs 101–102) faithfully, hedges included: G372R's functional consequences are "less clear", and the mechanism "remains to be defined".
