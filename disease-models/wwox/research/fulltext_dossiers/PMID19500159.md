## Verdict

**The premise does not merely lack support at its terminus. Its terminus asserts the
opposite.**

This paper states, in three separate places and once more in a table, that **Wwox knockout
mice show no epilepsy**:

| Where | What it says |
|---|---|
| Introduction, p.2 | *"Although neither epileptic seizures nor abnormal behavior has been reported in Wwox KO (knockout) mice"* |
| Discussion, p.8–9 | *"Although neither abnormal behavior nor impaired motor skill was observed in the Wwox KO mice (Aqeilan et al. 2007), lde/lde rats show ataxic gait and spontaneous epileptic seizures"* |
| Discussion, p.9 | *"Although the reason for no detection of spontaneous epilepsy in the KO mice is unknown … the KO mice may die before they experience epileptic seizure"* |
| **Table 2** | The **Epilepsy** row carries an entry for `lde/lde` **only**. It is empty for both mouse models. |

So the full chain reads:

```
PMID 30290271  "this model develops epileptogenesis"
      ↓ cites
PMID 19936220  does not measure it (no EEG, no seizure, no behaviour, no brain histology)
      ↓ cites
PMID 19500159  "no epileptic seizures have been reported in Wwox KO mice"   ← terminus
```

Two citation hops converted an explicit negative in a **rat** paper into a positive
assertion about a **mouse**.

## What this paper does establish, first-hand

### The allele — and a correction to what I recorded yesterday

On 2026-08-06 I recorded from PMID 19936220 that the rat allele is "a C-terminal frameshift,
**not a null**". That was faithful to how Ludes-Meyers described it, and it is incomplete.
This primary measures the consequence directly:

- mRNA is **normal** in testis and hippocampus (Fig. 4) — no nonsense-mediated decay.
- Protein is **absent**: neither the 47 kDa nor the 42 kDa species is detectable in `lde/lde`
  testis or hippocampus (Fig. 5).
- 🔴 The control that makes this admissible: *"The Wwox epitope bound by the anti-Wwox
  antibody used in this study did not contain the region altered by the lde mutation"* — so
  absence of signal is absence of protein, not a masked epitope.
- Conclusion, verbatim: *"the 13-bp deletion in exon 9 of the Wwox gene is a **functional null
  mutation**"*.

**Corrected formulation:** structurally a C-terminal frameshift, functionally a null at the
protein level in the two tissues assayed. Both halves must travel together — the structural
half explains why tumour suppression may be partially spared, the functional half is why the
neurological phenotype is severe.

### 🔴 A textbook default, in the terminal source itself

The mechanism offered for the protein's disappearance:

> *"C-terminal sequence in lde/lde rats may lead to the aberrant conformation of them, **as
> degenerated by ubiquitin proteasome system**"*

**`PREMISE: DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK`.** No turnover assay, no inhibitor, no route
determination appears anywhere in the paper. This is the same *"misfolded, therefore
proteasome"* default that the 2026-07-12 episode already falsified for a different WWOX
allele, where turnover ran through an **HSC70-associated lysosomal route** and did not
respond to MG-132. The route here has never been measured.
**`REVIVAL_TRIGGER`:** any turnover experiment on lde Wwox protein — cycloheximide chase with
proteasomal versus lysosomal/CMA inhibitors — settles it.

🔴 **A restriction visible only in the figure:** Figure 6's caption specifies *"21- to 33-day-old
lde/lde **females**"*. The headline 95% is a **female-only** cohort. Neither the Results
narrative nor the abstract says so.

### The anatomical lesion

Extracellular vacuoles in the hippocampus of **9/9** affected and the amygdala of 6, with
**0/10** normals. The authors note the regional restriction is itself unusual: *"vacuolization
localized to a specific region, such as the hippocampus or amygdala, has not been reported in
other genetic epilepsy models"* — unlike the widespread spongy degeneration of tremor and SER
rats.

🔴 **Figure 1's caption misassigns its own panels.** The title says normal (a, b) and affected
(c, d). The image shows **a and c clean, b and d vacuolated** — i.e. normal (a, c), affected
(b, d). The caption's own body then contradicts its title by describing "many vacuoles" in
(a, b). It also misspells *size* as *"seize"*. A reader working from the caption alone will
invert which hippocampus is diseased. This is precisely the class of defect the coverage
vocabulary's `captions_only` value exists to make detectable.

## 🔴 The systemic-confounder question — and a tension the operator's abstract exposed

Yesterday's read of PMID 19936220 established that the **mouse** systemic null is
metabolically decompensated at P18 (hypoglycaemia, acidosis, uraemia, hypocalcaemia,
leukopenia, anaemia) — a measured confounder for any brain phenotype in that window.

This paper addresses the same axis for the **rat**, and appears to dissociate it:

> *"Postnatal earlier lethality in the KO mice seems to be related to their severely reduced
> serum levels of total protein, glucose and cholesterol as well as abnormal levels of
> electrolytes … **By contrast, 28-day-old lde/lde rats had normal levels of these
> constituents**"*

and, decisively:

> *"**Normal levels of serum constituents clearly excluded the possibility** that these
> abnormal excitabilities are resulted from a systemic metabolic disorder"*

If that held, the two rodents would form a clean natural dissociation: **seizures without
metabolic crisis** (rat) versus **metabolic crisis without seizures** (mouse), which would
place Wwox-dependent hyperexcitability upstream of systemic illness.

**But the whole statement is cited to `FT-041` (PMID 17803050), which is unread.** On
2026-08-06 the operator supplied that paper's **abstract only** — and it reports:

> *"Mutant rats had significantly increased concentrations of plasma **urea nitrogen,
> creatinine, and inorganic phosphate**, as well as decreased concentrations of plasma growth
> hormone."*

Those are not normal serum constituents. They are a **renal-impairment signature** — and the
same signature measured in the mouse null (BUN 37.25 vs 17.67 mg/dL), and the same one
Ludes-Meyers 2009 explicitly noted as *consistent between the two species*.

The two statements are not strictly contradictory: the 2009 list is protein/glucose/
cholesterol/electrolytes/calcium, while the 2007 abnormalities are BUN/creatinine/phosphate.
**But the word "clearly excluded" is doing work its own source does not license.** A rat with
elevated urea nitrogen and creatinine has a systemic derangement; whether that derangement is
sufficient to drive hyperexcitability is an open question, not an excluded one.

**Status:** `INFERENZA`, flagged, pending `FT-041`. An abstract is not a reading, so this
tension is recorded and not resolved. **This is the single highest-value reason to obtain
`FT-041`.**

## Cross-species comparison (Table 2, read as a table)

| | `Wwox−/−` mouse | `Wwox^gt/gt` mouse | `lde/lde` rat |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mutation | targeted disruption | gene trap | spontaneous |
| Expression | absence | hypomorph | functional loss |
| Viability | 2–3 weeks | 2 years | **3–12 weeks** |
| Tumours | osteosarcoma | B-cell lymphoma | — |
| Metabolic disorder | severe | — | — |
| Vacuolization | — | — | **hippocampus and amygdala** |
| **Epilepsy** | **—** | **—** | **wild running + tonic–clonic** |

Two observations the table forces:

1. The rat lives **3–12 weeks** against the mouse's **2–3**. That is the material basis for
   the authors' explanation — *"the KO mice may die before they experience epileptic
   seizure"* — which is testable and, notably, still untested seventeen years later.
2. The table lists **osteosarcoma** for the mouse knockout, taking Aqeilan at face value.
   PMID 19936220, accepted later in 2009, found **zero** osteosarcoma in nine knockouts by
   four modalities. Suzuki could not have known this. The contested claim propagates here.

## Other findings

- **Heterozygotes:** Wwox protein reduced 20–40%, *"however, +/lde rats are phenotypically
  normal"*. Converges with the mouse — no rodent haploinsufficiency phenotype.
- **Brain sparing again:** *"except for brain weights, the absolute weights of most organs
  were significantly lower"*, and *"the relative weight of brain was significantly larger"*.
  The same pattern as the mouse, and the same trap: it is a denominator effect, not brain
  overgrowth. (Underlying numbers are in the unavailable Table S1.)
- **No tumours in the rat.** The paper's explanation is internally confused: *"an
  undetectable level of smaller Wwox variants lacking exon 9 … can partially compensate"* —
  an undetectable species cannot compensate as written. The intended meaning is presumably
  that variants lacking exon 9 would be spared by the deletion.
- **Bone:** *"slightly lower bone density … (data not shown)"* with **normal plasma calcium** —
  milder than the mouse's severe hypocalcaemic bone disease. Skeletal severity is therefore
  **not** conserved.
- **Growth hormone:** dwarfism attributed to low pituitary GH — again citing unread `FT-041`.

## Field density

`Wwox AND audiogenic` → **2 records**. `Wwox AND "lethal dwarfism"` → **2**. In both cases the
pair is this paper and `FT-041`, from one laboratory, **never independently replicated in
seventeen years**. That is not a criticism of careful work; it is a statement of how thin the
base is under a premise LEGEND has been propagating.

Group note: the laboratory's speciality is deriving and phenotyping spontaneous rat mutants

observed **before** the gene was known, so this cannot be a case of a WWOX-motivated group
finding a WWOX-shaped result. That raises the phenotype's credibility rather than lowering it.
