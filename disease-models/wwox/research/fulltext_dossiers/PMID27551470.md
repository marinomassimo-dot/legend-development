## 0 · What this document is, and the one thing to hold throughout

**It is an editorial, not a review, and it reports no measurement of its own.** The PMC deposit
carries `article-type="editorial"` and subject `Editorial`; PubMed assigns publication type
`Journal Article` and **no `Review` tag**. The dispatch called it a review, and the distinction is
not cosmetic for a repository that weights sources: it is five sections, 13,575 characters and
**17 references**, against the **103** of the Aqeilan-co-authored review (PMID 24510053) it cites
for the same territory.

Everything empirical in it is a restatement of another paper. **Two statements cannot be resolved
at all**: one rests on *"unpublished data"*, and the section *"WWOX promotes apoptosis"* opens with
an *in vitro* and an *in vivo* result carrying **no citation whatsoever** — the section's first
reference appears two sentences later.

So the product of reading it is not biology. It is **what it says, what it selects, and how it
disposes of what it rejects** — and, where this repository holds the cited sources, whether the
restatement survives contact with them.

## 1 · What the piece establishes in its own right

Three things, and they are worth having:

1. **A clean statement of the controversy.** *"The role of CFSs in cancer is debatable and two
   major views dominate the discussion in this regard"* — passenger versus driver, with the
   passenger side's own premise (hemizygosity, *"suggesting, although not proven, that the other
   allele is maintained"*) stated fairly, hedge intact.
2. **One structural argument that actually bears on driver status**, and it is not the Q-value:
   *"WWOX, on the other hand, is a single gene within the deleted peak"*, against PTEN's peak which
   also contains KLLN. A one-gene peak is a genuine argument that the deletion selects for losing
   *that* gene. This is the strongest thing in the document.
3. **Its own best sentence, which concedes both causes:** WWOX is among the most deleted genes
   *"suggesting a selective pressure because of a tumor suppressor activity **and also because of
   the fragility of that locus**"*. The Conclusions then drop the concession.

And one self-limitation, stated once, in the last paragraph, after the conclusion it qualifies:
CFS expression *"is cell-type-specific"*, so behaviour is expected to *"vary… from one cancer type
to another"*. **This is the sentence that most constrains any transfer of the thesis** — to another
tumour, and *a fortiori* to a post-mitotic, non-neoplastic tissue.

## 2 · What it does **not** support — the negative is the product

### 2.1 🔴 The same observation is used on both sides, and the two uses are never reconciled

**Hemizygosity is the premise of the passenger hypothesis in the Introduction** — *"This assumption
led to the hypothesis that alterations in CFSs are secondary events"* — and **three paragraphs
later it is evidence for the driver view**: *"most of the deletions within WWOX are hemizygous
rather than homozygous, suggesting haploinsufficiency."*

No argument is offered for why hemizygosity favours haploinsufficiency over passenger status. Both
uses simply stand in the same article. `entries[2]` against `entries[3]`; both declared `text_only`
on both sides, because this is text against text within one document and nothing here is a panel
reading.

**Field size, measured:** `WWOX AND haploinsufficien*` returns **3 records in all of PubMed**. That
is the entire published field behind an inference this editorial treats as settled.

### 2.2 🔴 The central statistic cannot discriminate the hypothesis it is offered to settle

*"the WWOX locus is the fifth most frequently deleted locus in 10 844 samples from 33 cancer types
(Q-value=1.31E−266; http://www.broadinstitute.org/tcga/home)"*.

**First, the citation.** A specific quantitative claim cited to the **root URL of a project
homepage** — no accession, no dataset, no GISTIC run identifier, no access date. The number is not
reproducible from what the article provides.

**Second, the calibration set, checked both ways before being asserted.**

- *Internally coherent:* by the quoted values WWOX (1.31E⁻²⁶⁶) ranks below PTEN (0) and RB1
  (7.24E⁻²⁷³), consistent with fifth place if two further unnamed loci also outrank it.
- *But the inference fails:* **TP53 — offered here as the calibration for what "significantly
  deleted" means — is quoted at 5.04E⁻²², some 244 orders of magnitude weaker than WWOX.** If this
  metric ranked loci by tumour-suppressor importance, the canonical tumour suppressor could not sit
  that far below WWOX. It does not rank importance. It ranks **recurrent focal deletion** — which
  is precisely what a common fragile site is expected to produce **whether or not the gene inside
  it is a driver**.

**The article's leading evidence for the active hypothesis is a measurement that the passive
hypothesis predicts equally well.** It begs the question the title asks. `entries[4]`, `entries[5]`.

### 2.3 🔴 How it disposes of the one negative it reports: by absorption

McAvoy *et al.* found *"no relationship between alteration of a given CFS and expression of its
associated gene product"* — a null that weakens the deletion→loss-of-function chain the whole
argument needs. It appears **inside a section headed "support its role as a tumor suppressor"**,
glossed as *"suggesting that these alterations are not merely responsible for dysregulation of WWOX
protein expression"* — i.e. converted from a constraint on the mechanism into a call for
*additional* mechanisms.

The negative is neither refuted nor conceded. It is absorbed. **PMID 18000379 is not held in this
ledger at any depth, so the restatement could not be checked against its source** — recorded rather
than glossed.

### 2.4 🔴 The one place it touches the reference genotype, the cited fact runs the other way

> *"cancer usually occurs after the reproductive phase and therefore loss of CFS would not have an
> inherited selective pressure. **In support of this assumption**, germline mutations or loss of
> function of WWOX are associated with neuronal disorders"*

**The second sentence contradicts the first.** A germline loss-of-function phenotype is exactly what
inherited selective pressure on a locus looks like, and it acts **before** reproduction, not after
it. The fact is offered as support for the claim it undercuts.

This needs no external data — the inversion is visible in the article's own two consecutive
sentences, which is why it is asserted at all. `entries[13]`.

The neuronal claim rests on **one** citation, ref 14 (**PMID 25416187**, Tabarki 2015), which
carries **no receipt of any kind** here. The two Aqeilan-authored reviews cited elsewhere cannot
carry it: this repository's complete read of PMID 24510053 measured **zero** occurrences of
*epilepsy, seizure, brain, CNS, WOREE, encephalopathy, ataxia, intellectual, recessive* or
*syndrome* across its 38,845-character body.

### 2.5 It supports nothing about the reference genotype, and that is the whole of it

No patient, no allele, no neurological endpoint, no developmental measurement. One sentence, one
citation, one unread source.

**Ref 9 — faithful datum, stripped inference.** *"Wwox heterozygous mice develop higher incidence of

**Ref 12 — faithful, and half.** Cytoplasmic retention of ΔNp63α and suppressed transactivation are

thereby **increases** ΔNp63α abundance while lowering its activity — the demonstration that
*greater protein abundance can coexist with lower function*. Under a heading *"WWOX promotes
apoptosis"*, only the suppressive half survives. Incomplete rather than false, and incomplete in
the direction that serves the argument.

**Ref 7 — disposal by omission, checked against the very review cited.** The editorial attributes
the *"frequent loss in cancer"* framing to this review. That review, as read here, reports:
*"WWOX loss is not the only direction reported. Some studies document **increased** WWOX in breast,
gastric and prostate carcinomas, which the authors read as complex regulation."* The countervailing
observation is not carried. **The entire selective-pressure argument requires loss to be the rule.**

**Ref 8 — the ITCH step.** *"ITCH mediates K63-linked ubiquitination of WWOX and promotes its
translocation into the nucleus where it activates ATM in a positive feed-forward loop manner"*,
cited to refs 8 and 10. Three facts, and the conclusion is deliberately weaker than they look:

- This repository's reading of PMID 23370280 assigns ITCH-dependent K63 stabilisation of WWOX to a

**And one thing it gets right, said because a fidelity test that only collects errors is not a

### 4.1 🔴 The caption's Δ sits on the element the panel labels *Passenger*

**Caption:** *"Cells with deletions within WWOX-encoding gene **(depicted as Δ)** are **positively
selected** owing to the role of WWOX in DDR"* — positive selection is the **driver** property.

**Panel, read at native resolution and adjudicated at 400 dpi / 300 %:** on the right (*Extensive*)
branch there is **exactly one** open triangle, directly above a **truncated** DNA helix whose
shaded field extends past the drawn duplex — the deletion — and the label beneath that helix reads

**Charitable reconstruction, stated because it must be:** the authors may intend two distinct
deletions, one in the locus and one in the gene. **The panel draws one.**

On an article whose title asks whether the event is passive or active, **the reader who trusts the
caption and the reader who opens the figure get opposite assignments.** `entries[19]`

### 4.2 The panel is less one-sided than the text, and is the better answer

The Conclusions assert WWOX loss is *"not a secondary/passive event"*. The panel does not draw a
choice: it draws **both**, side by side on the same *Extensive* arm — *Passenger* for the locus
break, *Driver* for the loss of the gene product. That is a both-and model, and it answers the
title's question better than the text's either-or. Recorded as a **qualification**, not a
contradiction: the panel does not deny the thesis, it decomposes it. `entries[20]`.

### 4.3 The panel labels the locus **FRA16**, not FRA16D

Verified at 400 dpi and again at 300 % of that render: the glyph sequence is F-R-A-1-6 and the
label ends there. Title, body and caption all say **FRA16D**. FRA16 is not a fragile-site

is ambiguous where the text is specific. **Small, and reported as small: it changes no result.** It
is recorded because it is the article's own title object and because no check that does not open

## 7 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype

> **`ESPANSIONE`, and weaker than that word usually implies.** The document is a citation layer in
> cancer genomics. It contains no patient, no allele, no neurological endpoint and no measurement of
> its own. The single sentence reaching the WWOX-DEE genotype class is an **evolutionary argument
> whose supporting fact contradicts it** (§2.4), resting on one unread citation.

What legitimately crosses is narrow, and it is epistemic rather than mechanistic:

1. **`INFERENZA` — germline WWOX loss of function is evidence of purifying selection on WWOX, and
   this article's own two sentences establish the inversion.** Whatever the fitness cost of a
   fragile FRA16D, it is not licensed by "cancer happens after reproduction" when germline loss of
   the gene inside it produces a severe early phenotype.
2. **`ESPANSIONE` — the single-gene-in-the-peak argument** (§1.2) is the only structural argument in
   the piece that survives §2.2, and it is about the locus, not the protein.
3. **A negative worth keeping:** *"WWOX AND haploinsufficien\*"* = **3 records in all of PubMed**.
   Any statement in this model about WWOX heterozygote dosage rests on a field of three papers.

`PREMISE: DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK` — *"a gene that is recurrently deleted in tumours is thereby a
driver in those tumours."* Tagged because it is the premise this whole article rests on and never
writes down, and because §2.2 shows it is exactly what the cited statistic cannot establish. A
conclusion resting on it is **provisional by construction**.

**`REVIVAL_TRIGGER`:** a measurement — in any tissue — that separates FRA16D fragility from WWOX
protein loss and shows the two dissociating with respect to an outcome. That would convert the
passive/active question from a rhetorical one into a testable one, and PMID 18000379 (McAvoy),
unread here, is where to look first.

## 8 · What a later reader should not take from this dossier

That the editorial is unsound. It is a short opinion piece by the field's principal WWOX
laboratory, it states its case clearly, and its structural argument in §1.2 is good. What this
reading establishes is narrower and more useful: **its restatements must not be used as sources.**
Four of seventeen could be checked, and on all four the check changed something — a stripped
inference, a dropped headline, an omitted counter-direction, and a citation that points where this
repository's record does not. The other thirteen are queued.
