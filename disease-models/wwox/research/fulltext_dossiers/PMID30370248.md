## Figure-surface audit

The direct PMC `/bin/` image URL and two Frontiers legacy image patterns returned HTTP 404.
The Frontiers article PDF was therefore saved as a separate visual source. Before rendering,
`figure_ppi_preflight.py` measured the page-7 raster as 1535×863 pixels placed at an effective
ceiling of 300 ppi. Page 7 was then rendered at exactly 300 ppi and inspected at native pixels.

Figure 1 is a qualitative infographic, not an experiment. It groups phenotypes into flies,
fish and a single **Rodents** box. The rodent box prints postnatal lethality, growth retardation,
metabolic defects, tumour formation, gonadal/bone findings and “Seizures, epilepsy, & ataxia”.
It does not separate mouse from rat, genotype, strain, age or assay. That compression matters:
the body makes a false mouse-specific seizure attribution, while the primary seizure evidence
cited elsewhere in the same review is rat evidence.

## Core scientific verdict

This is the 2018 baseline map against which Steinberg 2021 should be read. It usefully establishes
that WWOX phenotypes depend on genetic background, cell of origin, cooperating lesions and model
construction. It does **not** add a primary DATO. Its tissue-specific mouse repertoire is mainly
mammary epithelium, hepatocyte and osteoblast; it predates the Nestin-, Synapsin-, GFAP- and
Olig2-targeted CNS comparison emphasized by Steinberg 2021. Steinberg is therefore a genuine
cell-type/neurodevelopmental extension of this baseline, not merely a restatement.

## Deep biological dive

### What the review actually synthesizes

- Global Wwox loss produces early postnatal death, growth failure, hypoglycaemia, hypocalcaemia,
  osteopenia, gonadal abnormalities and altered organ weights.
- The review calls the bone differentiation defect cell-autonomous at the osteoblast
  mineralisation stage, but the underlying primary paper carries that evidentiary burden.
- Heterozygous tumour susceptibility is strongly context-dependent: mixed-background mammary
  models show low incidence, whereas C3H-background or chemical-challenge models show much more.
- Mammary-specific Wwox deletion perturbs branching, but several models do not form mammary
  tumours. Hepatocyte loss alters lipid-related readouts and sensitizes carcinogen contexts, yet
  does not establish that every systemic metabolic phenotype is hepatocyte-autonomous.
- Osteoblast-specific loss cooperates with Trp53 deletion; Wwox loss alone and Wwox/Trp53 loss
  are not interchangeable causal interventions.
- The hypomorph's survival is explicitly “presumed” to result from residual expression. That is
  a threshold hypothesis, not a directly tested rescue.
- A reported Drosophila irradiation phenotype was later assigned to a background insertion,
  and the review explicitly turns this into a warning about allele/background control.

### Cross-paper correction exposed by the reference list

The body says that Mallaret et al. showed spontaneous and audiogenic seizures in a short-lived
Wwox-KO mouse. Reference 53 is not a mouse experiment: it is the human SCAR12 paper. The actual
animal seizure citation is reference 104, Suzuki's `lde/lde` rat. This is not merely weak support;
the sentence points to the wrong species and source. The error is repeated in the summary, while
Figure 1 avoids an explicit species claim only by collapsing mouse and rat into “Rodents”.

The already completed primary read of PMID 19500159 makes the conflict sharper: that paper says
Wwox-KO mice had no reported seizures or abnormal behaviour and contrasts them with epileptic
`lde/lde` rats. The review therefore propagates a citation-hardened mouse-seizure claim that the
cited chain does not sustain.

## Transfer and evidentiary boundaries

- **Genotype:** low-to-moderate transfer. Global null, heterozygous, hypomorphic and conditional
  alleles are biologically distinct and cannot be compressed into “WWOX loss”.
- **Tissue:** model-specific. A phenotype measured in liver, bone or blood does not identify the
  tissue in which WWOX is necessary; this review does not perform reciprocal deletion/rescue.
- **Stage:** weak for therapeutic timing. Most severe phenotypes are juvenile and the review
  contains no intervention window.
- **Overall:** high-value synthesis and historical baseline; background evidence, not independent
  replication and not a new datum.

## 🔴 APPEND-ONLY CORRECTION — 2026-09-22, Orchestrator

**The "Cross-paper correction exposed by the reference list" section above is wrong, and it is wrong
in the direction that suppressed a live contradiction. It is corrected here, not edited above.**

That section states:

> *"The body says that Mallaret et al. showed spontaneous and audiogenic seizures in a short-lived
> Wwox-KO mouse. Reference 53 is not a mouse experiment: it is the human SCAR12 paper. … the
> sentence points to the wrong species and source."*

**The premise is true and the conclusion does not follow.** Reference 53 *is* the human SCAR12
paper — and that paper reports a mouse observation, in its own abstract, in the first person.
According to PubMed, PMID 24369382 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/brain/awt338), Mallaret M,
Synofzik M, … **Aldaz CM**, Koenig M, *Brain* 2014;137(Pt 2):411–9):

> *"Moreover, **we observed** that the short-lived Wwox knock-out mouse display spontaneous and
> audiogenic seizures, a phenotype previously observed in the spontaneous Wwox mutant rat
> presenting with ataxia and epilepsy…"*

So the review's citation is **faithful**, not mistaken. The review inherited the mouse-seizure
claim from Mallaret rather than inventing it or misattributing it. What this dossier established —
that reference 53 is a human genetics paper — is correct, and is **not the same thing** as
establishing that no mouse observation stands behind the sentence. The inference from the one to
the other was the error.

**What the corrected state of the question is.** Not *"a review miscited a human paper"*, but:
a 2014 *Brain* paper **co-authored by the maker of the knockout mouse** claims to have observed
spontaneous and audiogenic seizures in it; PMID 19500159 states the opposite three times plus an
empty Table 2 `Epilepsy` row; **nobody in this repository has read Mallaret's body**; and a
canonical prohibition (`CLAIM 005`, `CLAIM 037`) was written as though the matter were settled.

**Why the body is unread, measured rather than assumed.** `get_copyright_status(["24369382"])`
returned `checked_sources: ["pubmed","pmc"]` — PMC *was* consulted — with
`license.is_open_access: false` and *"© The Author (2013) … All rights reserved."* `PMC3914474`
exists as a deposit and is **not** a body. This is a **paywall, not a route failure**, and the
distinction matters: PMID 36779245's Table S1 was the opposite case, open access and unreachable,
which was a route problem. Acquisition of 24369382 is a human action.

**What does not change:** the prohibition stands. An abstract is not a read, and a first-person
sentence in an abstract is exactly what this repository refuses to promote — cf. `LIT-0405`, where
*"decreased plasma GH"* was refused as a reading and the body reported it as not significant.

**What changes:** the prohibition's *status*, from settled to **contested with the contesting
evidence unread and paywalled**, and the question sharpens from *"does the mouse seize?"* to
**"was the mouse ever provoked, EEG'd, or outlived?"** — audiogenic seizure is a *provocation*,
19500159's 95% is a stimulated figure, and the rat's day-16 onset nearly exceeds the mouse null's
whole lifespan.
