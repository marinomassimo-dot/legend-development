## What the paper did

A compassionate-use single-patient report: intravenous AAV9-CAG-hASAH1 at 2.2e14 vg/kg (total about 1.2e16 vg) in an adolescent with advanced SMA with progressive myoclonic epilepsy (acid ceramidase deficiency), no detectable anti-AAV9 neutralising antibodies, under prophylactic prednisolone plus sirolimus begun a week before dosing. Clinical course, serial laboratory, retrospective cytokine and complement panels, autopsy, vector copy number by ddPCR, ASAH1 mRNA and acid ceramidase immunoblots.

## What it measured

- Stable for three days; fever, worsening myoclonus, raised D-dimer and liver enzymes on days 3-4; mild platelet fall and proteinuria; eculizumab on day 5; cardiogenic shock and respiratory failure on day 5 needing catecholamines and ECMO; care withdrawn on day 8 for multi-organ failure and ischaemic neurological injury.
- Complement: soluble C5b-9 above 1,650 ng/mL on day 5 (normal below 300); C3, C4 and CH50 fall by day 7 (CH50 attributed to eculizumab). Cytokine rise from day 2 with a storm before death. vWF rising days 4-8 and hyaluronic acid raised days 7-8 (authors: endothelial injury signal).
- Pre-dose: raised CD40L, IL-18, VEGF-A (normalised on immunosuppression); at day 1 IL-1 alpha about 60-fold, eotaxin 3-fold, IL-17F 2-fold above six healthy donors; low total IgG to AAV9 (1:157) with neutralising antibodies undetectable.
- Autopsy: acute circulatory failure; no myocarditis, no thrombotic microangiopathy; ARDS-type lung; CNS microglial activation and PAS-positive ballooned neurons (disease features).
- Biodistribution (Figure 3B, inspected): liver about 7,700 vg per diploid genome, spleen 2,450, lung 850, kidney 810; CNS 14 to 493 (16- to 550-fold below liver). Protein (Figure 3C, immunoblot): higher than donor controls in liver, spleen, heart, adrenal; at or below endogenous in several CNS regions at day 8.

## What the authors state

- Mechanism proposed: early innate immune and complement-mediated endothelial injury, cytokine-mediated capillary leak, refractory shock and multi-organ failure, distinct from the thrombotic-microangiopathy pattern; direct endothelial transduction may contribute.
- "Multiple factors likely contributed": pre-existing inflammation, dose, AAV9, intravenous route, low pre-existing anti-capsid IgG. "Sirolimus did not appear to mitigate early immune response", consistent with its adaptive-immune action. Complement inhibition or intensified immunosuppression "may be considered" but late initiation after the storm may blunt it. Contributions of disease inflammation and a recent bronchial congestion "remain difficult to disentangle".

## What the paper does NOT say

- No dose-response (one patient, one dose); no counterfactual (no lower-dose or no-immunosuppression comparator); causation of the innate response by vector versus by disease inflammation is open by the authors' own account.
- No DRG, nerve or liver-histology pathology is described beyond the autopsy summary; the DRG lesion of the primate ICM studies is neither sought nor reported here.
- Whether eculizumab earlier, a different immunosuppression, or a lower dose would have altered the outcome is not tested.

## Transfer limit

One adolescent, advanced disease with a pre-existing inflammatory state, intravenous route, AAV9 with a strong ubiquitous promoter, dose 2.2e14 vg/kg (more than ten times the 1e13 threshold the authors cite for high-dose events). A single fatal case cannot give a dose-risk function and does not model a CSF-delivered programme; it is a boundary observation for systemic high-dose AAV9 in an advanced neurogenetic disease.
