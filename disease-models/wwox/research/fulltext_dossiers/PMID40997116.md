## What it measures

Drosophila larval class IV dendritic-arborization neurons: single-cell MARCM clones of a cerS null and a catalytic-dead H215D allele, and whole-larva lipidomics from a weak hypomorph (cerS P61, mRNA down about 60 percent) because null animals die in the first instar. Readouts: dendrite length and terminals, caspase staining, ceramide and long-chain-base species. WWOX: zero occurrences.

## Findings (DATO, from the text and figures read)

- Null and catalytic-dead clones lose about 75 percent of dendrite length and almost 90 percent of terminals; no caspase activation, neurons survive.
- Hypomyelination is not a readout (fly sensory neurons); the defect is cell-autonomous growth, not demyelination.
- Hypomorph lipidome: C18-C24 ceramides down, C12 and C16 not down (possibly diet or gut bacteria, the authors' suggestion), Cer-PE down, (dihydro)sphingosine and its phosphate up.
- Rescue is complete only when long-chain bases are lowered and a C18-C26 ceramide synthase (mouse CerS2 or CerS4) is supplied; mCerS5 (C16) does not rescue and reduces branching on overexpression; dietary C22-ceramide is a mild rescue.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **Nothing about WWOX.** No WWOX measurement, genotype or ceramide readout in a WWOX model.
2. **Not a statement about mammalian disease.** The fly has a single CerS; the lipid phenotype comes from a hypomorph while the neuronal phenotype comes from null and dead-enzyme clones, so the two never come from one allele. N is 4 pooled larval samples for lipidomics and 4-8 clones per genotype.
3. **The mammalian therapeutic route is the authors' stated future test**, not a result.
4. **Chain-length selectivity is inferred by rescue with a transgene panel across species**, not by measuring the produced species in the rescued neurons.
