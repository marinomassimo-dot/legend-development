## What it is

A 15-animal experiment: healthy adult male Wistar rats on an in-house high-fat diet (8.6 to 1 fat to carbohydrate-plus-protein, goat fat based) or standard chow for 60 days, then one end-point blood sample (blood gas, counts, liver and kidney markers, plasma SOD) and liver and kidney malondialdehyde. WWOX: zero occurrences; no epilepsy, no seizure measure, no model of any disease.

## WWOX content

None. **No WWOX-deficient evidence.** Healthy wild-type adult rats; nothing about a developing brain.

## What it measures (DATO)

Ketogenic rats lost about 40 percent of body weight (260 to 157 g), had glucose of 57 mg/dL and ketones near 8 mmol/L, pH 7.36 (control 7.52), lower base excess (1.08 to 0.32 mmol/L), lower haemoglobin (13.76 to 11.98), haematocrit, MCV and MCH with red cell count not significantly lower, higher liver and kidney MDA and about 90 percent lower plasma SOD.

## What it does NOT support (the negative is the product)

1. **No transfer to a child on a clinical ketogenic diet.** A 40 percent weight loss in adult healthy rats on an ad libitum home-made 8.6:1 diet is not a clinical regimen (external note: clinical epilepsy ketogenic diets are usually far lower ratios; that figure is outside this paper). No clinical ketogenic formulation, no growing animal, no epilepsy.
2. **"Metabolic acidosis" is not demonstrated by the numbers.** Ketogenic pH 7.36 is a near-normal value; the control group is alkalotic (7.52, pCO2 27.6). The contrast is driven by an abnormal control. Table 5 base excess is 0.32 versus 1.08 mmol/L, while the abstract states -5.57 mOsm/L (a value and a unit absent from the table; INFERENZA that the abstract and table disagree). pCO2 27.63 versus 35.72 is listed with a p of 1.00.
3. **"Anaemia" rests on indices from one end-point draw.** Haemoglobin is lower (p 0.02), red cell count is not significantly different, no baseline sample, no iron, ferritin, reticulocyte or copper measurement; the copper explanation is cited from children's data, not measured.
4. **Weight loss and energy intake do not reconcile.** Table 3 gives ketogenic rats an equal or higher energy intake than controls (week 8: 98.24 versus 75.57 kcal/day) while they lost 40 percent of body weight; the paper does not discuss it, and unmeasured food spillage, malabsorption or diet palatability are open. The anaemia could therefore reflect starvation or malnutrition rather than ketosis.
5. **Oxidative-stress claims are one-time end-point values with truncated axes.** MDA differences of about 7 and 11 percent look large because of axis cut-offs; plasma SOD (control mean about 33, a wide error bar) falls about 90 percent while the text says about 80 percent.
6. **Small n (8 versus 7), single laboratory, p-value pairs displayed inconsistently** (hematocrit p 0.001 with one asterisk, MCV p 0.001 with two).
7. **Not about transfusion or iron therapy.** It shows a diet can coincide with lower haemoglobin in rats; nothing about correcting it.

## Design and limits

15 rats, in-house diet, one time point, no baseline, no limitations section.
