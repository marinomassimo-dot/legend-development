## What the paper did

Built and tested "Engineered Cross-Correction": AAV.GMU01 (a capsid with a 7-amino-acid insertion
in AAV9) delivered **intra-CSF**, carrying human *FXN* engineered for secretion (an exogenous
N-terminal signal peptide plus a TATk cell-penetrating peptide, placed before the native
mitochondrial targeting sequence) under a muscle-biased **newDesmin** promoter.

- In silico screen of 16 native signal peptides with DeepLoc and SignalP, then 52 engineered
  variants, 21 passing, 5 taken in vivo (SP1–SP5).
- Mouse: route comparison (i.c.v., ICM, lumbar IT, ICM+IT), promoter comparison (hSynapsin vs
  newDesmin, mono- vs bicistronic), and efficacy in **two separate conditional-null strains** —
  PvalbFxn (neuronal) and MckFxn (muscle) — treated intra-CSF at 5 weeks of age.
- NHP: an i.v. biodistribution study; a pilot ICM study of SP1-FXN and SP3-FXN (n = 2); and an
  extended 12-week ICM study of SP1-FXN at **2 × 10^13 vg** in four animals, with histopathology.

## What it measured

Vector genomes per cell; FXN mRNA by transgene-specific qPCR, normalised to VG and to a
housekeeper; ISH and IHC for FXN RNA and protein; capillary western for mitochondrial processing
(mature vs pre-protein); rotarod and a scored ataxia rubric (PvalbFxn); survival (MckFxn); and in
NHP, a grid-based quantification of cardiac FXN signal with a modified quantile-quantile alignment
against buffer-treated animals, plus histopathology of 8 brain regions, 3 spinal levels, 3 DRG
levels (2 ganglia per level) and heart.

## What it states

1. **Route choice is a dose-sparing instrument.** In NHP, i.v. AAV.GMU01 gave 10-fold higher
   cardiac distribution but **25-fold lower** cerebellar dentate and **62-fold lower** DRG
   transduction than ICM; intra-CSF administration "reduced cardiac biodistribution by ~90%
   compared to i.v.".
2. **A secretion tag expands the footprint without expanding transduction.** SP1-, SP3- and
   SP5-FXN produced cells carrying AAV-FXN **protein without AAV-FXN RNA** in cerebellum, and FXN
   protein in spinal motor neurons where no transgene RNA was present; SP2 and SP4 did not.
   SP1-FXN increased cardiac repletion area 2.65-fold over wild-type FXN (p = 0.0593).
3. **The engineered protein still reaches the mitochondrion.** Capillary western detected only the
   mature FXN isoform in NHP heart, and SP1-FXN was processed as efficiently as WT-FXN in mouse
   cerebellum.
4. **Efficacy split by model.** In PvalbFxn mice, all AAV-FXN arms including wild-type FXN rescued
   rotarod performance and reduced maximum ataxia score, and only SP1-FXN delayed ataxia onset
   (p = 0.017 at 11 weeks). In MckFxn mice, wild-type FXN did **not** extend survival (p = 0.108)
   while SP1-FXN (p = 0.012) and SP5-FXN (p = 0.043) did — i.e. cross-correction mattered only
   where transduction was low.
5. **There is a stated therapeutic window in fold-of-endogenous.** 9-fold over endogenous was
   tolerated in prior work, >20-fold impairs mitochondrial function; the authors therefore define
   the window as "60%–900%" and report that no NHP sample reached 20-fold, with liver below
   endogenous.
6. **Clinically framed cardiac repletion.** FXN protein increased by ≥60% in 40%–60% of the heart
   and by ≥30% in >85% of the heart.
7. **DRG findings persisted even in this low-burden design.** In the 12-week, 2 × 10^13 vg ICM
   study: no clinical signs; "no evidence of neuronal degeneration in the brain or spinal cord and
   **minimal to mild degeneration in the DRG**", which the authors call "consistent with prior
   AAV-related findings"; variable axonal degeneration in spinal cord and DRG and increased glial
   cells particularly in lumbar DRG; one animal with minimal mononuclear infiltration in the
   cardiac apex.

## What it does NOT state

- Nothing about WWOX, nothing about any DEE.
- It does **not** claim cross-correction is proven: "our current data collectively is suggestive
  of cross-correction, further confirmatory assays … would strengthen the mechanistic case."
- It does not report a dose–response for DRG findings: the extended NHP study used one dose.
- It does not test immunosuppression, nor report anti-capsid or anti-transgene immunity.
- The two mouse models are each a partial model; "no mouse model accurately replicates both
  nervous system and cardiac dysfunction", so the dual rescue is a rescue in two separate strains,
  not in one animal.
- It does not demonstrate functional benefit in NHP — the NHP endpoint is protein repletion.

## Caveats the authors themselves give

Species-dependent capsid behaviour (AAV.GMU01 spreads less in mouse than in NHP); the mouse
cardiac biodistribution did not translate into a large survival extension; antigenicity risk of
the CPP is argued by analogy, not measured; the route study in mouse "was not powered to detect
statistically significant differences among intra-CSF routes".

## Transfer limit

Cynomolgus macaque and conditional-null mice; cargo is **engineered to be secreted**, which is the
entire mechanism. WWOX is an intracellular protein associated with membranes and mitochondria with
no established secretory route, so the cross-correction trick does not transfer as such; what
transfers is the **design question** — whether therapeutic benefit can be bought at lower vector
burden by promoter selectivity and route choice. The FXN therapeutic-window reasoning
(fold-of-endogenous, with a measured upper toxicity bound) is a method, not a WWOX number.
Nothing here is medical advice.
