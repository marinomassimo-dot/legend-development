## 1 · What this paper establishes

**The cohort — `DATO`.** Six affected individuals in two consanguineous families, every one a
homozygote, read off the pedigree at 600 dpi rather than from prose: Family 1 (Saudi) II.1–II.4
all labelled `47T/47T`; Family 2 (Israeli Palestinian) II.1 and II.2 both `372R/372R`. Nine
genotyped relatives are unaffected — both sets of parents heterozygous, and Family 1's II.5
`47T/N`, II.6 `N/N`, II.7 `47T/N`. Every heterozygote shown is unaffected.

**The variants — `DATO`.** `c.139C>A` / `p.Pro47Thr` in the first WW domain (Family 1) and
`c.1114G>C` / `p.Gly372Arg` in the C-terminal dehydrogenase/reductase domain (Family 2), both
homozygous, both segregating, neither in the population databases the authors queried.

**The phenotype — `DATO` for its presence, and its detail lives elsewhere.** Childhood-onset
recessive cerebellar ataxia with generalized tonic-clonic epilepsy and intellectual disability
in both families, with prominent upper motor neuron involvement (leg spasticity, bilateral
extensor plantar responses) **added by Family 2**, which the authors read as spastic ataxia or
complicated hereditary spastic paraplegia. 🔴 The clinical baseline of Family 1 — MRI,
muscle biopsy, course — is in **Gribaa 2007 (PMID 17470496, resolved this session, unread in
this repository)**, not here.

**The one functional assay — `DATO`, in vitro.** In a peptide pull-down of bacterially expressed
GST fusions against a biotinylated WBP1 PPPY oligopeptide, wild-type WWOX1 and WWOX1–2 were
pulled down and the p.Pro47Thr versions were not. Direction: loss of PPPY-peptide binding.

**The mouse — `DATO`, with denominators that move.** A **full constitutive** *Wwox* knock-out
(BK5-Cre active in the oocyte; the section heading says "conditional" and the Methods say
otherwise) has a few spontaneous seizures from ~2 weeks; on 11–14 kHz exposure at 16 days
**3 of 8** seized; at 20 days **the four survivors** all seized; **n = 8** wild-type littermates
seized zero times. The animals die before 4 weeks of failure to thrive.

**The negatives, which are products and not residue — `DATO`.**
- Screening WWOX's coding exons in **189 unrelated ataxic patients from 182 families** found
  **no further mutation**. The cohort is two families because the search returned nothing.
- **No tumour** in any of the six affected individuals (ages 17–26 in Family 1; 5 and 10 in
  Family 2) nor in any heterozygous carrier — with the authors' own caveat that later risk is
  not excluded.
- The mouse's balance disturbance **could not be attributed to the cerebellum**: the authors
  state that early death prevented them from testing it.

## 2 · What this paper does **not** support

| Statement it cannot carry | Why, from the source |
|---|---|
| A genotype–phenotype correlation | Two families, two variants, six individuals; the authors themselves say larger cohorts "should allow us to … perform genotype–phenotype correlation studies" — i.e. this paper does not |
| Any quantitative claim about WWOX protein abundance | One patient, three passages of one fibroblast culture, four control lines, judged **by visual inspection** (the caption's own words). No densitometry, no replicate quantification, no statistic, no error term anywhere in the paper |
| Anything about abundance for `p.Gly372Arg` | No protein was measured in Family 2 at all. The variant also **could not be modelled** — "moderate conservation … prevented reliable modelling" — so G372R rests on segregation, conservation and database absence, and on no assay |

| That the human ataxia is cerebellar in mechanism | Stated as the paper's own limitation; the mice died before it could be tested |

| Abolition (as opposed to strong reduction) of WW1 binding | 🔴 The panel qualifies the text — see §3, Fig 3 |

## 4 · Design and limits

Two families ascertained through a prior linkage study, plus exome sequencing, plus one in vitro
binding assay, plus one whole-body knock-out mouse. The design's strength is the clinical half:
a disease-primary group (Koenig, `Koenig M[Author] AND (ataxia OR ataxias)` = 147 records
measured this session) reporting segregation in consanguineous families with a 189-patient
negative screen. The molecular half is thinner and its instruments are borrowed from the
gene-primary laboratory (`Aldaz CM[Author] AND WWOX` = 38 against `Koenig M[Author] AND WWOX`
= 3): one peptide pull-down, one homology model, and a mouse that models neither variant. Every
sentence connecting the variants to a mechanism in a patient's brain crosses at least one
system boundary, and the authors' hedges — *presumably*, *most likely*, *suggesting* — mark
them.

**Field density, measured 2026-09-13.** `WWOX AND (ataxia OR SCAR12)` = 34 today against **8**
up to end-2014: this paper opened the literature it is now cited within.
`WWOX AND fibroblast AND "western blot"` = **1** — this paper is the entire intersection, so its
abundance datum has never been replicated because there is no second record to replicate it.
`WWOX AND Pro47Thr` = 1 and `WWOX AND G372R` = 0.

## 5 · The abundance observation, stated exactly as the paper makes it

Asked as a reading question, not against any registry text (that comparison is the M4b note's
job, and it is written after this dossier):

- **How many individuals?** One. Patient II-3 of Family 1, `p.Pro47Thr` homozygous.
- **How many lanes?** Seven human lanes: three passages (P10, P13, P14) of that one culture, and
  four unrelated control fibroblast lines.
- **By what method?** One polyclonal antibody against WWOX residues 1–19 at 1:500, on an
  immunoblot, with an actin loading control — and the comparison declared as **"Based on visual
  inspection"**.
- **What comparator?** Control fibroblasts. **Not** another affected individual, **not** another
  genotype, **not** a severity stratum.
- **What did the authors conclude?** `"suggesting that the mutation does not alter global protein
  levels"` — scope: global protein levels, hedged.
- 🔴 **Where the paper does connect protein to severity, the direction is the opposite of a
  dissociation.** The Discussion explains the *milder* human phenotype **by** residual protein:
  the p.Pro47Thr protein "is still present, at least in human skin fibroblasts", unlike the mouse
  and rat models, and the lde rat — the severe end — has **no detectable WWOX** at all. So this
  paper uses abundance *as* a severity explanation across species, while measuring abundance in
  exactly one human being and in no second genotype.

`INFERENZA` (mine, typed as such): what the panel licenses is *"in this individual's fibroblasts,
the P47T protein is not grossly depleted"* — a statement about one tissue, one individual and one
allele, which is compatible with a fold/function defect at normal abundance and says nothing
about abundance in brain, about G372R, or about any severity comparison between patients.
`PREMISE: DATO` (the caption and the blot). The competing textbook default this refutes for this
allele — *"a destabilising missense must reduce steady-state protein"* — is `DEFAULT_FROM_TEXTBOOK`
and the authors themselves cite PQBP1 Y65C as the precedent against it.

## 6 · Transfer verdict toward the reference genotype (WWOX-DEE genotype class)

**T2, partial, and asymmetric between the two mechanisms.**

- *Transferable:* that biallelic WWOX missense variants cause a recessive neurodevelopmental
  phenotype with tonic-clonic seizures, intellectual disability and ataxia in humans, and that a
  WW1 missense can abolish-to-strongly-reduce PPPY-motif peptide binding without depleting the
  protein in fibroblasts. Both are `DATO` within their stated systems.
- *Not transferable:* any abundance-to-severity relation (one individual, one allele, one
  tissue); any statement about the SDR-domain allele's function (never assayed); any cerebellar
  mechanism (explicitly untested); any dose-response or modifier reasoning (n = 6, two variants).
- *The mouse* transfers to complete loss of function only, and it is short-lived, which the
  authors name as the reason the ataxia could not be characterised.
