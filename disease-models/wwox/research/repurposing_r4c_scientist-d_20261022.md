## 3. Three new precedent and design reports (not in the registry)

Registry query per PMID returned no record and no manifest; all three are lawful open text (CC BY or CC BY-NC).

### 3.1 Thiel 2026, EClinicalMedicine (PMID 42542632): oral zinc in GNAO1-related disorders, an open-label pilot trial

`partial_fulltext_read` (appendix read in full, protocol in its synopsis, endpoint and statistics sections; supplementary declared `captions_only`). 13 enrolled, 12 treated, 11 completed; primary outcomes safety and feasibility; 6 months; 11 variants; all variants zinc-responsive in a post-inclusion in-vitro test.

- **What it measures.** Safety, feasibility (84.6 percent, 11 of 13), and exploratory secondary outcomes with nominal p values and no multiplicity adjustment (protocol and plan: descriptive, no adjustment). Efficacy signals are GMFM-66 (+4.7 points as a difference of means, +2.3 paired), BFMDRS-Disability (-2.0 as means, -1.6 paired), COPM (caregiver-set goals); the blinded movement score did not change.
- **What it does not support.** (a) The headline GMFM-66 and disability numbers in the Summary are differences of means over different numbers of patients (12, 11, 10); the paired six-month changes in appendix Table S2 are about half. (b) The disability scale was rated unblinded. (c) The external comparator is cerebral palsy: published reference centiles (8 treated children) and a propensity-matched cohort (10 treated, 20 matched controls from a rehabilitation programme with intensive training and whole-body vibration), nominal P 0.040, Cohen's d 0.88 (0.05 to 1.71); the authors call it biologically non-equivalent and note a later GNAO1 longitudinal study with no significant motor change. The appendix uses 10 treated where the main text restricts to 8. (d) By baseline severity the GMFM mean rises in the moderate group and barely moves in the severe group (appendix Table S8). (e) Four of twelve exposed participants had a serious adverse event, one had three including asystole, all judged unrelated. (f) The text says no ferritin deficiency was seen and that zinc normalised in all after three months with eight high at six months, while appendix Figure S2 shows one low ferritin at day 90 and 8 high at day 90 and 9 high at day 180. (g) The stool microbiome and the parent movement log are in the protocol's secondary endpoints and absent from the article. (h) Central nervous system exposure was not measured; the one in-vitro responsive participant (P11) showed no obvious improvement; the analysis plan is dated 22 July 2025, after enrolment closed.
- **Card consequence (class level).** A repurposed-drug pilot can show feasibility and safety and can generate effect-size signals against a non-disease comparator, but with the open-label design and caregiver-set goals it cannot separate expectancy, maturation and treatment; its authors themselves list randomised, crossover, delayed-start or N-of-1 designs as the next step.

### 3.2 Aartsma-Rus 2026, Cell Reports Medicine (PMID 42594875): eligibility for individualised antisense treatment in neurodevelopmental diseases, a consensus perspective

`complete_fulltext_read`. Eight voters, a five-finger vote, two consensus statements, five illustrative scenarios.

- **What it says.** Assess the variant, the disease and the patient; the first statement was softened to "as it evolves" evidence with an expert panel estimating where evidence is missing; the second lists considerations for whether a phenotype aspect may be modifiable (functional rather than structural damage, active gene pathways, plasticity, molecular versus secondary progression, and "responsiveness to symptomatic treatment suggests a dynamic and thus potentially modifiable nature").
- **What it does not support.** No new patient data; the criteria are expert consensus with one vote of 3 on statement 2; windows differ by symptom within one disease (Angelman mouse: behaviour only if restored at conception, motor also postnatally) and the Dravet antisense window lost between day 2 and 14 in mice "persisted into adolescence in humans" only preliminarily; the stated effect of antisense in these diseases is partial and seizures return when treatment stops. Development takes 1 to 3 years; the Food and Drug Administration had reviewed 49 individualised applications for 82 patients by November 2025. A knockdown antisense is not an option for loss-of-function variants and might exacerbate them (Scenario 2); structural malformation (lissencephaly) is judged unreachable postnatally.
- **Card consequence.** The "nanorare" rarity criterion is fewer than 30 patients worldwide; the published WWOX-related patients exceed that for the class, so the framework fits a private allele rather than the class (inference). The consensus's "symptomatic response suggests modifiable" rule is an untested heuristic.

### 3.3 Acosta 2026, Neuroscience Applied (PMID 42494992): outcome strategies for trials in neuropaediatric rare diseases, an expert-opinion review

`partial_fulltext_read` (Supplementary Tables 1 and 2 read in full, the poster in part). 91 references.

- **What it says.** A "toolbox": home video, wearables, goal attainment scaling, tailored global impressions, a multi-domain responder index, reliable change, growth scale values, natural-history cohorts as external controls (45 and 47 percent of non-oncology single-arm approvals 2019 to 2022), core outcome sets, caregiver expectancy, a maturity index.
- **What it does not support.** The strategies rest on expert opinion without prospective validation, although the Conclusion says each is in use or validated elsewhere; the supplementary tables list cons the text omits (regulators may not accept per-patient endpoints; retrospective global impression scoring "requires validation and shows a high subjectivity"; reliable-change values for Dravet and Lennox-Gastaut instruments came from post hoc hypothesis-generating studies; composite scores need regulatory agreement long in advance). The article text never uses "disease-modifying", i.e. it does not address separating a symptomatic effect from a change of course; a maturity index (bone age) is proposed without validation. The images attached to its Figure 2 and Figure 3 elements are swapped relative to their legends (the same publisher figure-pairing trap recorded in the wave-4 protocol note). Several authors are employees of outcome-assessment companies.
- **Card consequence.** The one design lesson that stands independent of the authors' opinion: goal-attainment and caregiver-rated endpoints in a single-arm design need an expectancy control, and growth-scale-value forms need an agreed meaningful-change threshold.

## 4. The group's five questions: addenda from this lot (entered in the selection file)

1. *Symptomatic versus modifying:* nothing here adds a design that separates them; the consensus text treats a symptomatic response as a hint of modifiability and the outcome toolbox never names disease modification. The GNAO1 pilot's disability and goal scores moved while the blinded movement score did not.
2. *Soticlestat:* not touched.
3. *External controls:* the GNAO1 pilot shows the limit of a non-disease comparator (cerebral palsy trajectories, 8 versus 10 treated, nominal p), and the CLN2 sensitivity analysis shows a weaker hazard ratio than the main matched analysis; neither is a WWOX comparator and none exists.
4. *N-of-1 and aggregated designs:* the SCN2A antisense reporting summary states two open-label single-patient trials with replication of doses inside patients and no multiplicity correction.
5. *Responsive instruments:* growth scale values lack agreed thresholds; the CDKL5 baseline study judges the 88-item gross motor measure unsuitable and keeps it in the schedule.
