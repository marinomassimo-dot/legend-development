## 2. What the completing reads add to the T-type axis (substance per paper)

- **30175250 (Ogiwara 2018, Scn2a haploinsufficiency).** Full text and supplement read. The thalamus appears in the local-field-potential data of the excitatory-neuron deletion line (ventroposterior thalamus U = 0, P = 0.0286) and not in the global heterozygous knockout (U = 7.5, P > 0.9999); seizures about ten times more frequent in the Emx1-Cre line than in the knock-in heterozygotes; ethosuximide suppression paired t(5) = 5.683 (already in the first read; Figure 5f repaired in the previous task). The Discussion gives two untested routes to a T-type contribution: the authors' earlier Cacna1a layer VI result (T-type current up in thalamic relay neurons) and a possible shift toward inhibition with low-threshold rebound firing "via de-inactivation of T-type calcium currents", closed by "the circuit basis ... requires further study". The paper contains no T-type transcript or current measurement (its only expression assay is a sodium-channel TaqMan panel).
- **27616982 (Aguado 2016, wild-type OF-1 mice).** Cav3.1 protein in the thalamus rises across postnatal development to P60 (about 3 at P0 to about 27); the cerebellar molecular layer peaks at P21. Cav3.2 is moderate in the adult thalamus (Figure 2B, lowest of the plotted regions at about 12) and peaks around P21 in hippocampal subfields and cerebellum. Two anti-Cav3.3 antibodies "failed to provide an acceptable or specific signal": **this paper gives no Cav3.3 result**.
- **32751129 (Bouron 2020, wild-type C57Bl6/J embryonic cortex RNA-seq).** Cacna1g about 15 TPM at E11 to about 28 at PN1, Cacna1h about 4 to about 38 (about tenfold), Cacna1i absent at E11 and E13 and about 8 at PN1 (Figure 1A read as an image). The functional imaging concerned other channels (Chrnb2, Grik5, Orai3, Trpm7), not T-type channels.
- **32678276 (Kam 2020, aged APP/PS1 mice).** Ethosuximide reduced spike-wave discharges only in the mixed behavioural state (waking immobility: about 40 against about 88 events per hour, no star; mixed about 17 against about 108, one star); no drug changed single-event duration; single dose per drug; sleep counts are confounded by sleep spindles (authors' caution). The Introduction cites an earlier preclinical study in which phenytoin was ineffective on these discharges while ethosuximide was effective.
- **34483921 (Iizumi 2021, GluA1 C811S mice).** The Discussion adds the authors' statement that phenytoin and trimethadione may differ because of their targets, and that the AMPA antagonist acts "similar" in both genotypes. Figures 1 and 3 read as images: phenytoin lowers the wild-type score from near 5 to about 2.7 with a mutant group mostly at 5 (no star against mutant vehicle); the AMPA antagonist lowers both genotypes at the two higher doses.

## 3. One new report read: the Cntnap2 reticular-nucleus preprint (PMID 40166234), the only design in hand with a vehicle arm in each genotype

Jang SS, Takahashi F, Huguenard JR, bioRxiv 2025 (version posted 22 March 2025; not peer reviewed; the PMC record is a restricted stub; the full text was read from the bioRxiv PDF). Registry check before reading: `registry_records.py get --pmid 40166234` returned no record, `fulltext_receipts.py status` nothing, claim granted to scientist-b. Depth `partial_fulltext_read` (receipt `FTR-20261022-40166234-01`, staged): abstract, introduction, results, discussion and methods read in full; Figure 3 panels E to H and Figure 6 panels C to G opened as images (rasters extracted from the PDF); the other figures and the supplementary figures are `captions_only` (declared debt). Manifest `PMID40166234.json`, 18 locators, STRICT PASS.

DATO. In male Cntnap2 knockout mice (8 to 14 weeks) the reticular thalamic neurons are hyperexcitable: rebound bursts at minus 60 mV in 15 of 18 knockout against 9 of 18 wild-type cells; peak low-threshold T-type current density about 2.85 against about 1.47 pA/pF at minus 105 mV (two-way ANOVA bracket p = 0.0005; 14 and 15 cells from 3 mice each); synaptic currents unchanged. A single dose of the T-type blocker Z944 (and, separately, a chemogenetic inhibition) was given against vehicle in both genotypes (n = 9 to 13 per cell): in the three-chamber test the knockout vehicle group lost social preference (social index about minus 0.05 against about 0.37 in wild type) and Z944 restored it (about 0.42); wild-type social preference was unchanged; knockout grooming time fell from about 82 to about 40 s; in the reciprocal-interaction test Z944 lowered the wild-type time (about 120 to about 88 s) and raised the knockout time (about 60 to about 95 s), i.e. the two genotypes moved toward a common value. Statistics: two-way ANOVA with Tukey's test; no genotype-by-treatment interaction statistic is printed in the legends.

What it does NOT show (the preprint's own words and my string counts): "likely driven by increased expression of T-type calcium channels" is an inference; the text contains no occurrence of mRNA, qPCR, RNA-seq, Western, in situ or Cacna (counts 0); "the pharmacological approach with Z944 did not direct support a role of RT T channels, per se"; the authors "cannot at this point exclude" effects outside the reticular nucleus; males only; no developmental time course; a seizure assay was run in the knockout (low-dose chemoconvulsant: 6 of 10 progressed against 4 of 11 wild type) but Z944 was not tested on seizures in this preprint. It is a genotype with a T-type function increase and a T-type blocker effect, and its readout is behaviour, not an absence-seizure endpoint. NOT WWOX EVIDENCE.

## 6. What DL-MECH-171 and DL-MECH-172 need in order to be TESTED rather than stated

### DL-MECH-171 (Wwox-deficient brain transcripts: T-type lower or unchanged, no consistent Cacna1g change)

**The discriminating observation.** Cell-type-resolved T-type expression (transcript counts per nucleus or in situ, and ideally current density) in the cell types that carry the absence-type circuit, thalamic reticular and thalamic relay neurons, in a Wwox-deficient animal against a matched control at a fixed postnatal age, with the cell-type composition measured in the same samples. A bulk whole-tissue ratio cannot discriminate between "channel expression changed per neuron" and "the mix of cells changed".

**Where the existing data stand against that observation (DATO).**
- *GSE158681/GSE158682 (mouse, P17, hippocampus and cortex).* Not the right regions (no thalamus, no reticular nucleus), n = 2 to 4, no test, partial transcript mapping; the audit already limited the reading to "Cacna1i group means lower in all four comparisons; Cacna1h ratios 0.47 to 0.92; Cacna1g no consistent direction". The single-nucleus hippocampal deposit GSE158684 (Syn-Cre deletion) can resolve cell types but covers the hippocampus only.
- *GSE156243 (human cerebral organoids, KO against WT, week 15), recomputed rows in `drug_repurposing/gse156243/derived/gene_rows_cpm_t_type_and_composition.tsv`* (counts per million of the file's own library sums, shown for display; no model, no differential test; 4 KO and 2 WT libraries, clone and batch per library unresolved):

  Reading (INFERENZA): the three T-type rows are not among the authors' differentially expressed genes; the KO and WT ranges of CACNA1G and CACNA1H overlap; CACNA1I is higher in each KO library than in either WT library (ratio 1.46) with n = 4 against 2 and no test, the **opposite direction** to the mouse P17 table (Cacna1i lower in all four comparisons) and of a different species, tissue and age. A third unreconciled readout is therefore on disk, not a reconciliation. **WWOX mRNA is not lower in the knockout libraries** (ratio 1.39): the knockout is an exon-1 frameshift verified at protein level in the paper, so mRNA cannot serve as the positive control that the mouse tables had (Wwox falling to 0.06 to 0.32 there).
- *Why composition dominates in these organoids (DATO, same table).* Neuronal and maturation genes are lower in KO (STMN2 ratio 0.75, MAP2 0.65, SYT1 0.70, SLC17A7 0.64, SATB2 0.59; TUBB3 0.85, DCX 0.83), progenitor and radial-glia genes higher (NES 2.51, VIM 1.82, SOX2 1.43), and several regional-identity genes are raised with very uneven spread across libraries (PAX2 1.2 to 69 CPM across the four KO libraries against 0.2 to 2.9 in WT; HOXB2, GATA3, PAX7, OTX2, LHX5, GBX2, GAD1, SLC32A1 similarly, the highest values in one KO library). A T-type ratio from such libraries mixes a genotype effect with a different cell mix and a different maturation stage; in the normal developing mouse cortex Cacna1h itself rises about tenfold between E11 and PN1 and Cacna1i appears only after E13 (32751129), and thalamic Cav3.1 protein keeps rising to P60 (27616982), so developmental stage alone moves these rows.
- *What the new Cntnap2 preprint adds (DATO).* A genetic model in which T-type current density in the reticular nucleus is about 2 times higher (about 2.85 against about 1.47 pA/pF) while **no transcript or protein was measured**: current and transcript are separable questions, and a transcript table would not have decided it. *The tottering abstract* (parked, not evidence) reports T-type transcript elevation in thalamic relay neurons before seizure onset: a positive-control pattern for the right cell type and age.

**Test, as a design fixed before looking (IPOTESI).** (1) In a Wwox-null or neuronal-deletion line, single-nucleus or in situ counts of Cacna1g, Cacna1h, Cacna1i in thalamic relay and reticular neurons at one age before any seizure phenotype, with the cell-type proportions reported; (2) the same genotype's T-type current density in reticular neurons by patch clamp, since the Cntnap2 case shows current can differ with no transcript measurement; (3) a negative control from a comparison already known to move T-type expression (the P/Q-deficient mouse) to show the assay can see a change. A result of "no change per cell type and no change in current" would refute the lead's premise; a change confined to the cell mix would make the bulk readings uninformative about the channel.

### DL-MECH-172 (T-type-class blocker effect by genotype: trimethadione weaker in a hyperexcitable mutant)

**The discriminating observation.** A genotype-by-drug interaction in the same experiment, with a concurrent vehicle arm in every genotype, a pre-specified endpoint that is neither at a ceiling nor at a floor in the vehicle arms, a T-type-class blocker and a sodium-channel comparator, and an interaction term (not only per-genotype brackets). The Wwox three-genotype ethosuximide experiment (32000863, Figure 7) has the genotypes but no interaction term, no concurrent vehicle arm matched to the lithium experiment, and a late-phase effect in six animals (previous note, section 9).

**Where the literature in hand sits against that design (DATO, all NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).**
- 34483921: two arms, interaction F reported for both drugs; effect strong in wild type and weak in the mutant for the T-type-class drug as well as phenytoin; **vehicle ceiling** at stage 5 in both genotypes; printed F for phenytoin does not reproduce (26.22 against 23.69).
- 40166234 (new): vehicle and drug in both genotypes with a T-type blocker; effect present in the knockout for social index, grooming and locomotion, absent for locomotion and social index in wild type, and *reversed in sign* for reciprocal interaction (wild type lowered, knockout raised); behavioural endpoints, one dose, males, two-way ANOVA with no printed interaction statistic.
- 32678276: ethosuximide effect on spike-wave discharges depends on behavioural state (mixed only) in the aged APP/PS1 mouse; one dose; an earlier study in the same two models found phenytoin ineffective (cited, not read).
- 30175250: ethosuximide against vehicle in one genotype only (paired, n = 6): no genotype contrast.

**What this changes in the statement of DL-MECH-172 (INFERENZA).** The two genotype-by-blocker datasets in hand point in different directions (effect lost in the GluA1 mutant, effect present or normalising in the Cntnap2 knockout) on different drugs, different endpoints and different species of mutation; neither is a seizure endpoint with a T-type blocker in a Wwox model. The direction of the lead is therefore not a property of "hyperexcitable genotypes" in general. A test that could separate "T-type-specific" from "any anticonvulsant" needs the sodium-channel comparator in the same design (the phenytoin arm of 34483921 is the only such pair in hand), and an endpoint stratified by state or time (32678276 and the late-phase reading of Figure 7 both show that a pooled endpoint can hide the effect).
