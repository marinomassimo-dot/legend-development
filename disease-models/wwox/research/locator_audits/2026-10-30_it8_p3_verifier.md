# IT8 P3 verifier report: pharmacological qualification of nine cases

Independent verifier of package P3 (producer branch `task/it8-p3`, commits 34e3f399 and 3813ed5e). Non-canonical research material. Not medical advice. A database or enzyme measure is not therapeutic benefit; association is not modulation is not rescue. Reference genotype class: a WWOX-DEE genotype class; no individual-level record is used. No registration, contact, spend or access step was taken. Literature checks are abstract level (PubMed records); no full text was opened and no receipt was staged.

Method: ChEMBL REST re-queried live on 2026-10-29 (molecule-first and target-first, unfiltered by standard type unless stated); GtoPdb from the catalogue cache (release 2026.3.1); openFDA labels re-queried; PubMed records for PMIDs 25449033, 33279441, 30248303, 39769031. Producer tests and checkers re-run.

## 1. Verdict

The decisive numerical claims re-derive. No defect rated HIGH. Four MEDIUM defects concern what the text says beyond the data (a selectivity word, a "listed by id" statement, a type filter that hid one cell-level datum, one over-stated bumetanide consequence). All are repairable by wording or by a small table change; none moves an F/C/P position and none reverses a changed decision. The three changed decisions are right in direction; D3 is over-stated and D1 is slightly over-worded.

## 2. Defect list, severity-ranked

### MEDIUM

**M1. The ChEMBL type filter hid the only cell-level LDHA-pathway datum for stiripentol, and "28 rows" is stated without the filter (decision D1).**
The molecule-first pull is restricted to the types IC50, Ki, Kd, EC50, Kb, Potency, Inhibition (`TYPES` in `pharm_qualify.py`). Unfiltered, the racemate (CHEMBL1983350) has 88 activity rows, not 28. The excluded types include four `FC` rows of document CHEMBL5120856 (2022, "glycolate oxidase and lactate dehydrogenase double inhibitors"): "Inhibition of GO/LDHA in hyperoxaluric primary hepatocyte (Grhpr-knockdown PH2 or Hoga1-knockdown PH3 mouse) assessed as relative oxalate output at 10 uM", values 0.27 and 0.33 (PH2, 24 and 48 h) and 0.70 and 0.49 (PH3). That is a mouse hepatocyte functional readout (oxalate output, a pathway that also runs through glycolate oxidase), assay type B, not LDH potency and not human; it does not make the "no fitted LDH potency" statement false. But the README, the card and the work order say the second half of the question is "closed negative on primary data" and "no LDH potency among its 28 curated rows" without saying that 60 further rows of other types exist and that one document reports an LDHA-pathway cell effect at 10 uM. A reader cannot tell the datum was seen and weighed.
Repair: state the filter ("28 rows under the IC50/Ki/Kd/EC50/Kb/Potency/Inhibition filter; 88 unfiltered"), add the FC rows to `measures_detail.tsv` or name them as a contrary signal with the reading "mouse hepatocyte oxalate output, not enzyme inhibition, not human, mechanism not established", and soften "closed negative" to "no enzyme-level support". The same filter applies to every molecule-first pull in the package (AC50, FC, Activity, ED50 rows are dropped); the Q06 and Q09 NO_ROWS_UNDER_FILTERS wording is correct and should be copied for the others.

**M2. "Selective LDHA tool" is not supported by any datum in the package (D1; card E02 next_measurement; work order NEED-LACTATE-LDH).**
The target-first pull is on human LDHA only; LDHB was never queried. Re-query: the best characterised nanomolar LDHA compound, CHEMBL3335794, has LDHA IC50 2.6 nM and LDHB IC50 43 nM in one document (CHEMBL3763153), about 17 fold, and 1,150 nM LDHB in another (CHEMBL4028893): a potent dual or LDHA-preferring inhibitor, not a selective one. The other ids the decision points to (CHEMBL3335792, IC50 5 nM; CHEMBL3359440, IC50 30 nM) have no LDHB datum at all. The need itself says LDHB was the discordant gene in the P47T transfer test, so isoform selectivity is the point of the arm.
Repair: replace "selective LDHA tool" by "nanomolar human-LDHA inhibitor (selectivity over LDHB to be established per compound)" in the cards, the work order and `comparison.tsv`.

**M3. "Listed by ChEMBL id in measures_detail.tsv" overstates what the table holds (D1, D2).**
`pharm_qualify.py` writes at most 12 molecules per target-first pull (`mols[:12]`), ordered by the id string, not by potency and not by document; there is no row saying that the list is a cap. The README says 134 molecules at or below 100 nM among the retrieved LDHA rows are "listed by ChEMBL id"; 12 are. The same cap applies to PDK1, PDK2 and PDK4. The wording sits in the E02 and E03/I01 `next_measurement` text and in the work order ("listed by id in the cycle record").
Repair: either write all qualifying molecules (a few hundred rows) or write "12 of N, first by id, not ranked" in the table and the text.

**M4. D3 (bumetanide): the consequence "a systemic arm is not a test of the premise" is stronger than the cited data.**
Re-read at abstract level: PMID 25449033 gives the mouse brain-to-plasma ratio of about 0.015 to 0.02 (confirmed). PMID 33279441 states that with the systemic dose used (10 mg/kg intravenous, the only dose tested in the abstract) the modelled free brain concentrations of bumetanide were in the NKCC1-inhibitory range. The card F03 text says "free brain concentration in the NKCC1-inhibitory range only at an intravenous dose far above the human diuretic range": "only at" implies lower doses were tried and failed; the abstract does not say that. PMID 30248303 supports that human diuretic-range doses (0.1 to 0.5 mg/kg) do not reach inhibitory concentrations in neurons, and that a non-NKCC1 derivative reproduces CNS effects (confirmed). Together the data say: a systemic arm is interpretable only with a measured free brain concentration, and a CNS effect is not evidence of NKCC1 action. They do not say a systemic negative is uninformative in every dose regime, and the neonatal age window of the Wwox-null model (blood-brain barrier and efflux maturation) is not addressed. The ex vivo bath arm is the better design and the decision to use it stands.
Repair: "only at" becomes "at the single dose reported, 10 mg/kg intravenous, which is far above the human diuretic range"; the consequence becomes "a systemic arm is not a test of the premise unless the free brain concentration is measured".

### LOW

**L1. README misstates the phenylbutyrate Ki document (Q01).** The README says the parent acid carries Ki 2,800 nM "in a different document". Live ChEMBL and the producer's own `measures_detail.tsv` agree that both Ki values (salt record CHEMBL1746: 6,340 nM; acid CHEMBL1469: 2,800 nM) are rows of one document, CHEMBL1155249, one assay description (HeLa nuclear extract, fluorimetric). The HDAC1 IC50 pair (162,000 nM on the salt, 64,000 nM on the acid) is in different documents (CHEMBL5136998 and CHEMBL1671826) as stated. The substantive point (do not mix salt and parent records) stands and is in fact stronger, because a within-document pair differs 2.3 fold. Label Cmax re-derived: 218 mcg/mL (tablet, single 5 g fasting dose) on the labels re-queried; 218 mcg/mL is about 1.33 mM as the acid (molar basis not stated by the label), so the 5 to 20 fold exceedance of the 64 to 260 uM HDAC IC50s is arithmetic-correct.

**L2. "7 to 9.9 percent inhibition at 0.5 to 2 mM" blends two kinds of row (Q03).** In CHEMBL4311969 the 2 mM rows (LDH5, LDH1) carry the value 7.0 percent, relation "="; the 0.5 mM rows carry no number and the text value "Not active". The 9.9 percent at 500 uM is the independent report (PMID 39769031; the abstract gives about 10 percent for stiripentol at 500 uM; receipt not re-read). The conclusion is unaffected; the sentence should say "7 percent at 2 mM (ChEMBL), 'not active' at 0.5 mM (ChEMBL), about 10 percent at 0.5 mM (independent report)". Rough size: 7 percent at 2 mM and about 10 percent at 0.5 mM imply an IC50 of the order of millimoles or more, one to two orders above the 42 to 500 uM range, so the withdrawal of that range as an exposure target is sound.

**L3. Memantine voltage pair (Q05): the implausibility is not sized and the GluN2B pair is not mentioned.** Re-derived: CHEMBL6083260 (Eur J Med Chem 2024, dizocilpine derivatives, memantine as comparator) holds GluN1/GluN2A IC50 840 nM at -60 mV and 28,070 nM at -40 mV, and GluN1/GluN2B 780 nM and 25,630 nM, all patch clamp in HEK293, all from the same document. For a single-site voltage-dependent block, 20 mV would be expected to change the IC50 by roughly 2 fold; a 33 fold shift needs an apparent electrical distance times valence of about 4.4. The README correctly says the shift is a curated source statement of one document and keeps the item UNVERIFIED, but should state this size (and the identical shift on GluN2B) so the reader sees why the value is not usable as a voltage dependence.

**L4. Nanomolar PDK probe: no compound is identified beyond ids, and a matched same-document comparator was missed (D2).** The README states honestly that availability, exposure and neuronal engagement are not shown. Re-derived: PDK1, PDK2 and PDK4 each hold nanomolar "=" IC50 rows (for example CHEMBL3727577: PDK1 10 and 35 nM, PDK2 65 to 84 nM, PDK3 26 to 40 nM, PDK4 21 to 91 nM across documents, all cell-free, all enzymes "unknown origin" or recombinant; a cell-level PDK1 readout in PC3 cells is 573 nM, about 16 fold weaker than cell-free). The per-isoform lists in the table are separate pulls, so pan versus isoform-selective profile cannot be read from it. The same document that gives dichloroacetate PDK4 108,000 nM (CHEMBL6121009, 2025) also holds this class of nanomolar inhibitor measured in the same assay format, which would have given a matched, single-laboratory potency ratio instead of a cross-laboratory spread. Not a defect of fact; a missed strengthening.

**L5. `selftest` check 1 is weak.** `assert [...] == [500, 500, 200] or len(net.log) >= 4` is satisfied by the second clause alone, so the retry sequence is only loosely tested. The other four checks (permanent 500 is `SERVICE_ERROR` and never `NO_ROWS_UNDER_FILTERS`; transport failure retried; 404 not an error; grouping never merges units, relations or assay types) are sound.

**L6. Row-count drift against the live service (informational).** LDHA target rows are now 1,469 under the filter (producer 1,428); molecules with an "=" value at or below 100 nM are 206 over the full set and 135 in the first 1,000 (producer 134). The declared truncation hides 71 further nanomolar molecules but nothing decision-relevant: existence of nanomolar human-LDHA inhibitors is already established, and the decision does not depend on which one. PDK1 and PDK2 truncations (600 of 1,115 and 600 of 1,393) are likewise not decision-relevant. The increase is a ChEMBL release change, as the README anticipates for changed hashes.

**L7. Species label of one dichloroacetate group.** For the sodium-salt record, two PDK IC50 ">" rows are labelled target organism Homo sapiens while the assay organism is Sus scrofa (porcine enzyme, documents CHEMBL1132309 and CHEMBL1133733). The assay-organism column of `measures_detail.tsv` shows it; `comparison.tsv` does not. Not used in any decision.

## 4. Judgement of the decisions

**Changed.**
- D1 lactate/LDH (Q03): right in direction. Stiripentol as an LDH-engagement probe is not supported by any enzyme-level datum that was found, the 42 to 500 uM range is not supported by the two readable assays, and the label is silent. Slight over-wording ("closed negative on primary data") because the 2015 report is unread, the unfiltered FC rows were not weighed (M1), and "selective" is unsupported (M2). The unchanged first half (is lactate or LDH raised in WWOX-deficient neural cells) is the right verification.
- D2 PDH axis (Q04): right and well hedged. Dichloroacetate inhibits recombinant human PDK isoforms only at about 0.1 to above 5 mM, with large cross-laboratory spread, so a null at cell-attainable concentrations cannot separate "kinase does not matter" from "kinase not inhibited"; the text says availability, brain exposure and neuronal engagement of a nanomolar inhibitor are not shown. Weakness only in L4 and M3 (the "listed by id" wording).
- D3 bumetanide (Q08): the ex vivo bath design is sound and the premise measurement (E_GABA) is untouched; the consequence for systemic arms is over-stated (M4).

**Cannot change (six cases).** Q01, Q02, Q05, Q06, Q07, Q09: the missing-datum statements are specific and the searches recorded. I re-derived Q01, Q05, Q06, Q07 and Q09; Q02 (proteasome class: ChEMBL counts, GtoPdb beta5 pIC50, label, preprint abstract) was not independently re-queried and is not endorsed beyond the producer's record. They are under-stated rather than over-stated where a datum exists: Q07 gives a quantified reason for a potent tool compound (ethosuximide's only fitted T-type value sits inside the labelled plasma range, with no isoform resolved).

**Epistemic checks.** No statement turns a binding or enzyme measure into proof of benefit. No compound is called "the probe" as a named, available product; the nanomolar class is the probe, with availability and exposure marked unknown. Association, modulation and rescue are kept apart (the cards keep P1/F3 positions, the bumetanide card stays an abstention). The phrase "a CNS effect of the drug is not evidence of NKCC1 action" is correctly supported by PMID 30248303.
