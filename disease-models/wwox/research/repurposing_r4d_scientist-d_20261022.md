## 3. What a card for any candidate drawing on these two papers must carry as limits

These are the abstention-gap lessons in the existing cards' style: not a new card, and not a recommendation for any candidate. Each paragraph says what a card would have to state in `evidence_for_and_against.against` and `temporal_or_model_limit` for any candidate that cites the paper.

**42542632 (oral zinc in GNAO1-related disorders, open-label pilot).** A card citing this pilot would have to say that it is a precedent for design, endpoints and comparators and not evidence for any WWOX target: it is a different gene, with an in-vitro zinc-responsiveness assay that every enrolled participant passed, so the assay's predictive value could not be tested and the test was done after inclusion; it had no randomisation, no placebo and no masking, the one blinded rater's movement score showed no clear change over six months, and the disability score was rated by an unblinded rater. The headline gross-motor means (baseline 33.1 to 37.8 at six months) are differences of means over 12, 11 and 10 patients, and the paired six-month change in the appendix is 2.3 points (95 percent interval 0.8 to 3.8), about half the printed difference; the exploratory secondary outcomes carry nominal p values with no multiplicity adjustment and the protocol itself declares all analyses descriptive. The propensity-matched comparison rests on 10 treated participants against a control pool of 137 taken from a neuromuscular rehabilitation programme, which the authors call biologically non-equivalent; the main text and the appendix use different treated subsets (8 and 10). Central nervous system exposure was not measured, seizure frequency was reported unchanged (few participants had active seizures, observation was short), four of twelve exposed participants had a serious adverse event (judged unrelated to zinc), and the protocol lists secondary end points (stool microbiome, a parent movement log) that the article never reports. A card must therefore mark any "reported no change" cell as a measured null that is not comparable, any gain as unmasked and uncontrolled, and the target, endpoint and genotype comparisons as UNRESOLVED; it must not convert a pilot's mean change into a candidate-level effect.

**42594875 (eligibility for individualised antisense treatment in neurodevelopmental disease, consensus perspective).** A card citing this paper would have to say that it carries no patient data: it records the opinions of a workshop whose statements were voted on a five-finger scale by voters who voted only when they felt expert (statement 2 was voted by eight participants), the evidence standard of the first statement was softened during voting ("definitive data" became "evidence", "evidence-based estimate" became "estimate"), and the authors say the criteria rest on current understanding and may change. Eligibility is not efficacy: the paper's mechanism rule is that a knockdown antisense is not an option for loss-of-function variants and might exacerbate symptoms, so whether a given WWOX allele class falls inside or outside that rule is a mechanism statement a card must source separately and must not infer from this paper (I make no claim here about splice-modulating or expression-raising antisense, which the quoted sentence does not address). The paper's own supports for modifiability are weak or conditional: responsiveness of a symptom to symptomatic treatment is listed as a consideration suggesting a dynamic, potentially modifiable symptom, seizures are described as returning when treatment stops (so the effect depends on continued treatment), and it is stated to be unclear whether the developmental window fully closes or differs by aetiology; the two cited mouse windows differ by symptom within one disease (the Angelman reinstatement study) and between mouse and human (the Dravet antisense, which the authors call preliminary). Structural malformation is stated not to be reversed by postnatal treatment, individualised development takes one to three years during which benefit must still be expected, the nanorare cut (under 30 patients worldwide) fits private alleles rather than a genotype class, and the 49 reviewed applications for 82 patients are a count of applications, not of outcomes. A card must mark scope of benefit (symptom versus disease modification) as an open discussion item of the paper's own Figure 1, not a finding, and must keep "eligible" and "expected to benefit" in separate fields.
