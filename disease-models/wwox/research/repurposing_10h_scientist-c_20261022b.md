## 2 · First-pass observations (each paper ends with its negative)

**29163040 (THZ1 in neurons; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** The covalent CDK7 inhibitor lowers phosphorylation of RNA polymerase II C-terminal serines 2, 5 and 7 in cultured P1 cortical neurons: 10 nM does nothing, 50 nM already about one third of control, 250 to 1000 nM a plateau (Fig 2B), and four hours of exposure do not recover for 8 h after washout. It lowers immediate-early gene mRNAs at baseline and, strongly, after bicuculline (Npas4 about 59-fold and c-Fos about 46-fold over vehicle, about 8 and 4-fold with the inhibitor). In slices it spares early LTP and lowers late LTP. A single intracerebroventricular injection of 20 micrograms per mouse lowered whole-brain polymerase phosphorylation to about 62, 66 and 49 percent of vehicle at 6 h, spared 2-h object and cued-fear memory and impaired the 24-h versions (n = 10 per group). Cdk7 protein in cortex is about 20 percent of its adult level from E14.5 to P7 and rises at P30 (Fig 1B). Two printed inconsistencies: the slice pre-incubation is 2 h in the Results and 4 h in Methods and legend; bicuculline is 25 micromolar in Methods and 20 in Results. **Negative:** no seizure, EEG or excitability measurement exists; the injection bypasses the blood-brain barrier, so it is a pharmacodynamic proof in brain, not an exposure from a systemic dose; one dose, adult males, whole-brain lysate; the authors' own seizure remark is a hypothesis taken from a bromodomain precedent, and they advise caution about neurological side effects.

**35501379 (maladaptive myelination; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** In two absence-epilepsy models callosal oligodendrogenesis and myelin thickness rise only after seizure onset (Wag/Rij rat mean g-ratio 0.7368 to 0.6862; Scn8a mouse oligodendrocytes plus 88 percent at P45). Ethosuximide (about 300 mg/kg/day in drinking water, plasma 101.3 microgram per mL) abolished seizures on EEG and normalised g-ratio without changing it in controls: **this is an EEG-and-myelin pairing after a drug**. Genetic blockade of activity-dependent myelination (TrkB deleted in OPCs) cut seizures per hour by 51 percent at 4 months and 70 percent at 6 months, and lowered ictal theta coherence (P = 0.047, n = 6 and 7, overlapping dots, Fig 4i). The pan-HDAC inhibitor trichostatin A (10 mg/kg intraperitoneally daily, P28 to P45) lowered mature oligodendrocytes to the wild-type level and seizures at P45 by about 35 percent (64 against 42, n = 5-6 mutants per arm). In the same reading Fig 1g and 1h carry colour keys opposite to the caption (red marked control). **Negative:** the models are absence epilepsies, not a DEE; the g-ratio differences are about 0.05 and n is 3 to 4 animals per myelin group; seizure counts come from 3 to 4 hours of EEG; trichostatin A is pleiotropic, so its seizure effect is not proven to run through myelin; no myelin-promoting drug was tested, so the paper says nothing about clemastine or similar; the authors write that more myelin is not necessarily better and do not propose any myelin-lowering therapy. Extended data figures and supplements were not opened.

**29940337 (GSK-3 beta inhibition versus Wnt3A, preterm rabbit; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** After glycerol-induced IVH, activated beta-catenin, TCF4 and Axin2 are lower and GSK-3 beta mRNA higher; the inhibitor AR-A014418 (20 mg/kg intramuscularly twice daily for 7 days from day 1, the paper's dose) raised the Wnt readouts, raised MBP (stereology P = 0.001) and gait scores, lowered microglia and IL-1 beta at day 3 and Notch intracellular domain, with no side effect reported. Recombinant Wnt3A given into the ventricle (500 ng at days 1, 3, 6) raised the same Wnt readouts but not MBP or behaviour and raised IL-1 beta, IL-6 and Notch. **So two ways of raising Wnt readouts in a newborn brain separated on myelin and inflammation.** **Negative:** g-ratio unchanged and the rise in myelinated axons to about 150 percent not significant; the no-IVH control result is "data not shown", so the safety side is undocumented; no EEG or seizure measure; 14-day window; figures unread (captions only).

**28504693 (THZ1 in glioma; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** Eleven patient-derived lines: IC50 under 100 nM. Normal fibroblasts and marrow stromal cells are described as dying at 7 days but not 3 days; Supplementary Fig 1c-e shows BJ viability about 55 to 65 percent at 3 days and about 10 percent at 7 days (IC50 roughly 30 to 50 nM), HS5 about 0 percent by 100 nM, so **no selectivity window for normal cells is shown**. Mice with intracranial xenografts given 10 mg/kg every second day for two weeks had overlapping survival curves (Supplementary Fig 9, about six per arm), from which the authors conclude THZ1 does not cross the blood-brain barrier. **Negative:** that is an inference from an absent survival effect; no drug level was measured. The main-text Methods give no dose or n.

**28434841 (THZ1 in DIPG; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** The same compound, measured by LC-MS/MS: brain parenchymal concentration about 13.7 ng/g (about 24 nM) after intraperitoneal dosing and about 600 nM after intravenous dosing (10 mg/kg, five days a week). Median 72-h IC50 in eight DIPG cultures 56 nM (range 28 to 533). Intraperitoneal dosing gave about 20 percent longer median survival in one aggressive xenograft; intravenous dosing was limited to one week by tail-vein sclerosis. **Negative:** n, time point and tissue matrix of the concentration are in a panel not available to me; no normal-brain, neuronal or behavioural safety endpoint; a tumour paper.

**31331032 (roscovitine after status epilepticus; NOT WWOX EVIDENCE).** Roscovitine, a multi-kinase CDK inhibitor (the paper lists CDK5, Cdc2, CDK2, CDK9 and protein kinase A; CDK7 is not named), infused into the lateral ventricle (100 micromolar in the pump, 7 days) lowered CD68 amoeboid cells, microglial MCP-1, LAMP1 and p38 phosphorylation in the cortex after pilocarpine status epilepticus (n = 7). The authors state, without data, that the infusion did not change seizure susceptibility in normal animals. The text says "about 38 percent" fewer CD68 amoeboid cells; the bars in Fig 1C read about 32 and 12, a fall of about 62 percent (the figures agree only if 38 percent is the remaining fraction). **Negative:** ventricular infusion bypasses the barrier; the half-life statement is cited, not measured; no EEG or seizure-severity endpoint after the drug; adult male rats; one marker family.

## 4 · What the readings change in the five questions of group R3

**Q1, brain exposure and a neural readout of the CDK7/9 and CK1-alpha class (partly answered; none for the compound itself).**
- For THZ1, the only brain-exposure statement from a systemic dose is **measured** in 28434841 (about 24 nM after intraperitoneal and about 600 nM after intravenous dosing) and **inferred in the opposite direction** in 28504693 (no survival effect, so "does not cross"). The two do not contradict as data: the same compound reaches brain at levels that straddle the in-vitro range in other papers.
- Neural readout: 29163040 supplies the only direct one, with an injection that bypasses the barrier: lower brain RNA polymerase II phosphorylation and a selective loss of long-term memory in adult male mice. **(inference, audit owed)** In neuron cultures 10 nM did nothing and 50 nM acted (29163040 Fig 2B); the intraperitoneal brain level of about 24 nM (28434841) lies between those points and the intravenous level of about 600 nM lies above the 500 nM that gave the plateau. Different systems, labs, and the culture concentration is in medium, not tissue; this is a juxtaposition, not a measured dose-response in brain.
- **Still unanswered for BTX A51 itself:** no brain level, no neural readout, no seizure or EEG endpoint, no young-animal test. The missing measurement is unchanged: a brain-region concentration or CSF level plus a developmental and electrographic readout in a young animal at more than one dose.
- The multi-CDK inhibitor roscovitine has one neural readout (cortical microglia after status epilepticus) and an unmeasured claim of no change in seizure susceptibility.
- Cdk7 protein is low in cortex until P7 (29163040 Fig 1B): this argues that a CDK7 arm may act differently in a neonatal cortex than in an adult, but no experiment here tests it.

**Q2, a safe Wnt suppression window (still none; one new, opposite-direction datum).** No paper measured Wnt suppression in a developing brain with a seizure or EEG endpoint. The only developing-brain pharmacology read here raises Wnt readouts (GSK-3 beta inhibitor in preterm rabbits, with no side effect reported over 14 days and a "data not shown" safety control), and it shows that raising Wnt readouts by ligand and by kinase inhibition are **not equivalent** on myelin and inflammation. That is a caution against reading "Wnt up" or "Wnt down" as one direction. The measurement owed is unchanged: a dose-by-timing series of Wnt/GSK3/CK1 modulation in developing cortex with progenitor, myelin and electrographic endpoints. The tideglusib juvenile Cdkl5 paper (29603837) would be the nearest, and is parked.

**Q3, EEG paired with myelin after a drug (one found, with limits; not a uniqueness claim).** 35501379, the only one found in the files searched (the blind audit searched the 832 full-text XML files on disk for EEG together with g-ratio and found only this paper; PDFs, supplements and the literature off disk were not searched, and my own PubMed title/abstract queries found no other). In an absence-epilepsy rat, ethosuximide removed the seizures and the excess callosal myelin; in a mouse model a pan-HDAC inhibitor lowered oligodendrogenesis and seizures; genetic blockade lowered seizures and ictal coherence. It shows that, in these models, more myelin followed seizures and that blocking its formation lowered seizure counts. **Consequence for the scaffold, a caution not a verdict:** a pro-myelination card must not assume that more myelin is protective in a hyperexcitable network; this is the second counterweight beside the clemastine harm in healthy pups, and it comes from absence epilepsy, not from a DEE or a hypomyelinating one.
