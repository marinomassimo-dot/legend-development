## 0 - The premises being qualified (read first, from the three recovered dossiers)

The dossiers sit under `vps_recovery_20260925/residue_q230p_20260922/`; their headers declare `UNREAD_PREMISE` and historic status. Nothing in them was relied on as evidence here. The premises each one carries are, in my own words:

| Id | Premise (what a design depends on) | Dossier |
|---|---|---|
| **PA** | A published "normal transcript, absent protein" for a missense allele may be an extraction artefact: the protein may be present but outside the mild-lysis soluble fraction (insoluble, or invisible to the antibody), so a three-fraction split (soluble S, strong-denaturant pellet P, spin-free total T) plus an orthogonal non-antibody read is the minimum architecture that separates "absent" from "absent from S". Five branches are enumerated: not synthesised (H1), synthesised then cleared (H2), synthesised and insoluble (H3), soluble but epitope-invisible (H4), truncated or cleaved (H5). | fractionation and orthogonal detection |
| **PB** | The deficit can be placed before, during or after the measurable mature pool; a cycloheximide chase started from an undetectable baseline has almost no discriminating power, so the order is inhibitor arm first, chase second; a two-epitope puromycin-PLA ratio is the one instrument that separates "during" from "before" and "after"; the soluble/insoluble split and an inhibitor arm are complements, not alternatives. | nascent-synthesis discriminator |
| **PC** | A systemic rescue premise (neuron-directed replacement versus peripheral phenotypes) that a proteostasis modulator is a rescue route whose site of action matters. R1 papers can speak to it only through exposure and brain-penetrance facts of the classes they test; it is not otherwise addressable by this group and is reported as such. | systemic rescue |

Verdicts (KEEP / DISCARD / INCONCLUSIVE, per proposal) are collected in section 3 after the readings; each carries what would reopen it.

### 30266908 - Guiberson 2018, Munc18-1 missense destabilisation and chemical chaperones - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-30266908-01` (partial_fulltext_read: supplementary figures are captions_only except two pages opened as images; all eight main figures opened as images), manifest PASS, 16 locators. Primary, the nearest published precedent for the fractionation premise.

- **What it establishes (DATO, in this system).** Five missense variants of Munc18-1 (STXBP1) give total protein below 10 percent of wild type for four of five in primary Munc18-1-null neurons (Figure 3a, read from the image: P335L about 65, the other four about 4 to 8), with the transgene mRNA unchanged (Supplementary Figure 4a-c, opened). Turnover is faster by cycloheximide chase and by Dendra2 photoconversion; the 0.1 percent Triton-soluble fraction falls from about 80 to 25-45 percent (Figure 4a); aggregates are visible, not cleared at the soluble rate and not toxic.
- **The fractionation lane exists and works.** The soluble/insoluble split runs a 13,000 g spin and loads the pellet beside the supernatant at equal volume; total is a sonicated, boiled Laemmli lysate. This is an S / P / T arrangement in a missense protein, and the totals are low in the direct lysate: the loss is **not** merely an S-fraction artefact here.
- **Bearing on PA.** The branch that dominates in Munc18-1 is H2-like (fast turnover, low total) with a minority H3 pool. It is not a precedent for "protein present, only invisible in S". Munc18-1 mutant protein is also hard to see in neurons: the authors state that detectable protein for the mutants "were very low in neurons" and moved the chase to HEK293T and Neuro2a cells, which is the regime problem PB names (a chase needs a detectable baseline) and the cost of solving it (overexpressed cDNA in non-neuronal cells).
- **Bearing on PB.** No proteasome or lysosome inhibitor arm is reported (0 occurrences of MG132 or bafilomycin in the article XML; the only chloroquine is the transfection step). So the paper measures turnover but not its route, and cannot place the loss on the proteasome or the lysosome. Its own reference list carries a Munc18-1 C180Y proteasomal-degradation paper that does (carried to the adjacent-search reserve).
- **Quantity versus function: measured together, and not in proportion.** Figure 6a normalises each variant's treated bars to that variant's **own** untreated control (control fixed at 100): chaperones raise total mutant protein about 1.1 to 1.7 fold from a baseline below 10 percent of wild type, and the soluble fraction rises (Figure 6b, for example G544D from about 30 to about 68 percent with one compound), while the vesicle-recycling read-out is reported restored to wild-type level (Figure 6d: G544D about 33 untreated to about 75 treated against untreated WT about 55). Function recovered to wild-type level while total protein, on the arithmetic of these two figures, remains a small fraction of wild type (INFERENZA: the figure does not plot treated mutant as a fraction of wild type). Either the assay saturates well below wild-type protein, or the soluble pool rather than the total is what counts; the paper cannot tell which. It also means the quantity read-out alone would have under-called the functional rescue.
- **Chaperones are not mutant-specific.** The same screen raised wild-type Munc18-1 (Supplementary Figure 12a, opened: wild-type to about 180 percent with the phenylbutyrate class and about 195 with sodium butyrate). Both are HDAC-inhibitor-class compounds acting on a transfected cDNA, and no mRNA was measured under treatment, so a transcriptional contribution is not excluded here (the SLC6A1 paper in this lot, 42157447, tests exactly this).
- **What it does not support.** Nothing about an enzyme, a recessive homozygous allele, a protein expressed from its endogenous promoter, or an exposure window in a mammal: the rescue is cell culture and worms fed the compounds for two to three generations. The dominant-negative half (wild type co-aggregated, solubility down about 25 percent) is specific to a hetero-oligomeric client and does not transfer to a monomeric SDR enzyme.
- **Transfer verdict (class level).** For PA: partial support for the S/P/T architecture as a workable assay, and a measured case in which the pellet is a minority fate; for PB: supports "chase needs a detectable baseline" and "route needs an inhibitor arm" by showing the paper that skipped it. NOT WWOX EVIDENCE.

### 29785012 - Matreyek 2018, VAMP-seq abundance of every missense variant of PTEN and TPMT - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-29785012-01` (partial_fulltext_read: all six figures opened as images; the two Word supplements, a Supplementary Note and a Reporting Summary in binary .doc, were retrieved but cannot be read with the tools on this host, so supplementary is `not_read`), manifest PASS, 14 locators. This pays the queue-entry FT-113 debt for this paper ("not read").

- **What it measures (DATO).** The steady-state abundance of one variant per cell, as an N-terminal EGFP fusion integrated at one Tet-on landing pad in HEK293T with mCherry as an expression control; four FACS bins; a score from 0 (nonsense) to 1 (wild-type synonym). No antibody is involved. For Q230P-class work this is the antibody-independent abundance read the fractionation dossier's reagent finding (no C-terminal-only anti-WWOX antibody) asks for, **with three costs the paper states or shows**: the construct is an EGFP fusion on a heterologous promoter in a non-neuronal line; single-variant scores are noisy (replicate r 0.63 for PTEN, 0.73 for TPMT, eight replicate sorts needed); and the method "cannot yield insight into" activity-, localisation- or splicing-driven pathogenicity.
- **Abundance is not function, in the same paper.** Of 25 ClinVar-pathogenic PTEN missense variants with a score, 16 were low abundance; four of the rest are active-site variants "known to be inactive without loss of abundance" (Figure 4b, opened: a group of pathogenic variants sits at wild-type-range abundance). Known dominant-negative variants have wild-type-like or higher abundance. So a normal VAMP-seq score would not clear a Q230P-class variant, and a low score would not by itself show the function lost is the quantity lost.
- **Where abundance does track function.** For the enzyme TPMT, a clinical function surrogate (mean tolerated dose intensity in a published leukaemia cohort) correlates with the abundance score across six variants (r 0.99, Figure 5b, read against its axes). Six points including wild type is thin evidence and the surrogate is pharmacological, not catalytic.
- **Baseline stability sets the missense fraction.** More PTEN than TPMT variants are low abundance, attributed to PTEN's lower thermostability (Tm 40.3 versus about 60 degrees C). The WWOX SDR-domain Tm is a missing measurement, so "most missense variants destabilise" cannot be assumed for it from this paper. The "as much as 75 percent" figure is cited in the Introduction as "thought to", not measured here (the primary on this is Pandey 2024, 38396641, in this lot).
- **Chaperones.** Proposed, not done: "Combining VAMP-seq with small molecule modulators of chaperone or protein degradation machinery may even reveal variant-specific treatments". No such arm is reported.
- **Applicability.** Not every protein works: BRCA1 and LMNA gave low signal or no wild-type/low-abundance difference (Figure 6, opened); complexes, secreted and marginally stable proteins are listed as unsuitable.
- **Bearing on PA / PB.** Supports a design element (an antibody-independent, ratio-controlled abundance read of one defined missense) and states its limits; does not test degradation route, solubility, or rescue. NOT WWOX EVIDENCE.

### 34649609 - Matreyek 2021, PTEN abundance paired with activity for thousands of variants - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-34649609-01` (partial_fulltext_read: all four figures opened; the supplementary Word file and score table not read), manifest PASS, 13 locators. This is the direct test of "quantity of protein is not function" for missense variants, on one protein, in two assays.

- **What it establishes (DATO, PTEN).** Of 2,377 variants scored in both a human-cell abundance assay (VAMP-seq) and a published yeast lipid-phosphatase activity assay, 51 percent are wild-type-like on both, 21 percent lose both, **22 percent lose abundance only and 6 percent lose activity only** (Figure 2A counts read from the panel: 1,215, 502, 512, 148). Both discordant classes exist, in both directions.
- **"Less protein, activity retained" is partly an assay-range artefact, and the paper says so.** In a 20-variant panel activity fell only when abundance was down at least 5-fold (Figure 2B, opened), so a mildly reduced abundance does not register in the yeast activity read, and the comparison crosses systems (human cell abundance, yeast activity, both overexpressed). The authors list noise, insensitivity to subtle activity loss and "alterations in function not captured by the yeast activity assay" as reasons the loss-of-abundance-only pathogenic variants might look discordant, while noting some may be pathogenic through lowered abundance alone.
- **"More protein, no function" is real and some of it acts dominantly.** Abundant-but-inactive variants are numerous; two of four tested raised pAkt in cells that also carried wild-type PTEN (Figure 4B, opened), and five did not. The authors state the datasets alone could not call a dominant negative; cancer-genomics incidence was needed.
- **Selection patterns bear on dose.** Loss-of-abundance-only variants are depleted in tumours (retained partial activity is thought to suffice) and the authors cite literature in which partial protein loss associates with milder neurodevelopmental presentations and gross activity loss with cancer and severe disease. That is a literature statement in the Discussion, not a result here (labelled as such in the manifest).
- **Method detail relevant to PA.** The abundance Western read is a cleared Triton supernatant (21,100 g, supernatant kept); the VAMP-seq fluorescence is whole-cell. INFERENZA, not stated in the paper: an EGFP fusion that aggregates may still fluoresce, so VAMP-seq abundance would be total rather than soluble abundance. If true, VAMP-seq cannot see the S/P split PA needs; the paper does not measure it.
- **Answers to R1 question 2 in part:** yes, a published precedent where the two came apart exists, with the direction and the assay-range caveat above. NOT WWOX EVIDENCE.

### 38378758 - Clausen 2024, a VAMP-seq abundance atlas of Parkin - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-38378758-01` (partial_fulltext_read: all six figures opened as images; the three supplementary PDFs and the source-data workbook retrieved, not read), manifest PASS, 16 locators. Pays the FT-113 debt for this paper: the queue states that the published negative below "may not be used" until a reading carries a receipt, and that a delegate's earlier read left none. This is that reading, from the XML and the images, not from a delegate's summary.

- **What it measures (DATO, Parkin).** 9,219 of 9,300 possible single-site variants as N-terminal GFP fusions with IRES-mCherry at one Tet-on landing pad in HEK293T; four biological replicates, replicate correlations 0.96 to 0.99. Endogenous Parkin is below detection in these cells, so everything rests on overexpressed fusions, which the authors state as a limit.
- **Route of loss (library-wide, measured).** Bortezomib or knock-down of a proteasome subunit lifts the low-abundance shoulder (visible in the zoom insets, small beside the wild-type peak, not quantified in text); chloroquine does not (Figure 3A-C). Low temperature removes the low-abundance peak almost entirely (Figure 3E), but the authors themselves say that reflects both more stable variants and generally slower turnover, which confirms in a primary the point the nascent-synthesis dossier makes against a permissive-temperature arm (it perturbs two variables). Of 63 low-abundance variants at exposed, predicted-stable positions, 28 gain a predicted quality-control degron, i.e. some loss is neo-degron, not folding.
- **THE PUBLISHED NEGATIVE (now read from the source).** A small-molecule positive modulator of wild-type Parkin activity, applied to the whole library, "did not confer any substantial differences in Parkin abundance" (Figure 3F: the treated and untreated histograms overlie). Scope, stated by the authors and confirmed on the image: it is one compound, one concentration, one 24 h exposure, on a library dominated by wild-type-like variants, and "might only stabilize variants localized to the region or domain where the compound binds". So the negative is "this activity modulator is not a global abundance stabiliser", not "small molecules cannot stabilise destabilised variants". It must not be generalised to pharmacological chaperones or proteostasis regulators, none of which was tested. NOT WWOX EVIDENCE.
- **Quantity versus function, the Parkin version.** The destabilised R42P variant is about ten-fold down but active when overexpressed (mitophagy reporter), so for that variant the lesion is quantity; yet half of the 12 disease-linked missense variants have wild-type-range abundance (Figure 6B) and the abundance score separates pathogenic from benign worse (AUC 0.69) than conservation or stability predictions (0.79). The authors: "high abundance is a necessary, but not sufficient criterion for a variant to be functional".
- **Shares of the stability mechanism.** The Introduction cites "as much as 60%" of pathogenic missense variants as abundance-loss (and 50-75% elsewhere), as estimates from other work. In Parkin, thermodynamic stability explains abundance only moderately (Spearman -0.46 for all variants, -0.55 for residue medians, both read from the text and Figure 5A).
- **Bearing on PA / PB.** (1) For PB, a proteasome-versus-autophagy inhibitor arm on a library is feasible and was informative; the clear lysosome-inhibitor null is a measured case. (2) The inhibitor result is a flow shift, not a protein recovery measure (no immunoblot of recovered variant). (3) For R1 question 2, Parkin supplies a case where the quantity-raising agent did nothing, and a case (R42P) where quantity is the lesion.

### 33914734 - Gersing 2021, degradation pathway of a stability-normal aspartoacylase variant (C152W) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-33914734-01` (partial_fulltext_read: all seven main figures opened as images; eight supplementary figures, a workbook and a Word file retrieved, not read), manifest PASS, 16 locators. The closest enzyme-class precedent in the lot to the "protein made, then lost, native state intact" branch.

- **What it establishes (DATO, ASPA, overexpressed fusions in yeast and in human U2OS cells).** C152W has low protein, a native state that molecular dynamics at 310, 350 and 400 K does not distinguish from wild type, and (from earlier work cited) wild-type-like thermal stability with very low or undetectable activity. Proteasome inhibition restores the band in yeast (Figure 2C, opened); in human cells the loss is proteasomal, ubiquitin-dependent and strongly Hsp70-dependent (Figure 6C-D).
- **When in the life of the protein is it lost? A direct measure.** In a temperature-sensitive Hsp110 strain, C152W accumulates at the restrictive temperature, but degradation after translation is blocked does not differ between permissive and restrictive temperature, from which the authors conclude Hsp70 and Hsp110 "are mainly required for the degradation of newly synthesized ASPA". This is a cycloheximide chase used as an inhibitor-versus-chase discriminator, and it places the loss at de novo folding rather than in the mature pool, the very distinction PB asks for ("before, during or after"). It is a single protein in yeast and the chase is read on a Western blot of an overexpressed fusion that is detectable at baseline.
- **Quantity is not function, in the strongest form in the lot.** Stabilising C152W through the Hsp110 axis yields protein that is insoluble (yeast Figure 4E; human Figure 6F, read from the image: soluble lanes empty, pellet band faint with control siRNA and stronger with the Hsp110 knock-down). No enzyme activity could be measured in this study or was found in earlier mammalian-cell work. The authors say targeting Hsp110 "might still be a relevant target for other ASPA variants that are at least partially soluble and contain residual activity", i.e. not for this one.
- **A precedent for the pellet lane.** In human cells the detectable C152W protein sits in the pellet, so a soluble-only lysate reads "absent" while a pellet lane reads "present". This is a worked example of the PA branch H3 (synthesised, insoluble) in an enzyme, in an overexpression system; the authors do not state the soluble negative as an artefact, they report both fractions with fraction-specific loading controls (tubulin and Pma1 in yeast; GAPDH and Na/K ATPase in human cells). It does not show it for a protein expressed from its own promoter.
- **What transfers and what does not.** Yeast Hsp104 has no metazoan homologue and lowered, rather than raised, C152W in human cells, so the disaggregase arm is not a rescue route. The proposed pairing of an abundance map with a function assay (to find the variants for which targeting quality control is relevant) is the same design this lot keeps arriving at, proposed here, not done.
- **Bearing on PA / PB.** Supports PA's pellet lane as informative; supports PB's "chase only means something from a detectable baseline" (here the baseline is the overexpressed fusion) and shows a chase paired with a chaperone-function perturbation can place loss at synthesis. NOT WWOX EVIDENCE.

### 31402619 - Abbink 2019, vanishing white matter, polysomal profiling and an eIF2B-activator-class trial in missense knock-in mice - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-31402619-01` (partial_fulltext_read: seven main figures opened as images; the article body has no Methods section, so the Methods were read from the supplementary Word file; supplementary figures and data files not opened), manifest PASS, 14 locators. The group selection file asks of this paper: does polysomal profiling separate altered translation from degradation in a missense model, and does a translation-state modulator normalise white-matter pathology?

- **What polysome profiling did and did not separate (DATO, VWM mice, forebrain).** It identified which mRNAs sit on ribosomes in knock-in versus wild-type brain (176 differ; 17 of 33 initially differential ones are ATF4 targets). It did **not** read the mutant protein: no eIF2B subunit abundance or solubility is measured anywhere in the article (0 hits for "abundance" or "eIF2B protein" in the XML). And on its own the method did not separate translational from transcript-level regulation: most altered mRNAs were up in monosome **and** polysome fractions, which the authors read as transcript-level change. For PB this is a measured limit: a polysome read tells where an mRNA is, not what happens to the protein after it is made, and it works on a cleared lysate (a spin removes "nuclei, membranes and debris") that discards any insoluble pool by design.
- **A modulator with function endpoints and no quantity endpoint.** The eIF2B activator improved balance-beam slips, gait, astrocyte maturation and (in one genotype) myelin transcripts and MOG staining (Figures 5 and 6, opened). Protein recovery is not measured, so this paper cannot be counted among the studies that measure both quantity and function; it measures function and pathway state only.
- **The response depends on the allele.** Benefit is larger in the 2b4ho2b5he (delta Arg484Trp) than in the 2b5ho (epsilon Arg191His) mice on gait, myelin and the pathway markers, and astrocyte sensitivity to the compound differs by genotype in vitro (Figure 7C-D). The authors tie this to where the mutation sits relative to the compound's binding interface and to whether the mutation destabilises the complex ("may preferentially impact eIF2B with complex-destabilizing mutations"), and say patient-cell testing may be needed. It is a primary-literature example of a missense-specific response to a small molecule, in two alleles of one complex, with exposure measured in blood and brain and above the reported potency in all genotypes (so the difference is not exposure).
- **Not a full correction.** eIF2alpha phosphorylation is unchanged by treatment and the authors state that it "does not fully normalize eIF2B activity".
- **Limits.** Mouse, chronic daily injection from young age, a compound with low solubility and unestablished human bioavailability (authors' words); the first author's group declares a pending patent on a different compound in the same disease. A repurposing card would have to record that this is a modulator of a pathway downstream of a missense lesion, not a proteostasis rescue of the mutant protein.
- **Bearing on PA / PB.** Offers no support to the fractionation premise (no pellet, no solubility), and a caution for the nascent-synthesis premise (polysome association is not protein synthesis and does not separate transcript from translation without the monosome ratio). NOT WWOX EVIDENCE.

### 42268630 - Frick 2026, 4-phenylbutyrate and AI-selected analogues in stxbp1 zebrafish ("argues against repurposing") - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-42268630-01` (partial_fulltext_read: three figures opened as images and Table 1 read; the supplementary statistics workbook not read), manifest PASS, 14 locators. A failed-repurposing check that the group selection includes so that the set is not only supportive precedents.

- **What it shows (DATO, zebrafish).** In larvae homozygous for frameshift loss-of-function alleles, 4-phenylbutyrate and 16 other compounds did not rescue the stxbp1a movement deficit, and 4-phenylbutyrate did not change the frequency of ictal-like events in nine stxbp1b larvae over a one-hour bath exposure (p 0.8593, Figure 1G read from the image; a wide interval on the difference). After Bonferroni correction no compound changed locomotion in either genotype, and every stxbp1a comparison had p of at least 0.18.
- **What it cannot show, by the authors' own account.** (1) The lines are nulls: the authors say 4-phenylbutyrate "may lack a molecular target" in a null allele and the findings "may not exclude" a role for patients with specific missense variants. So this is a test of the null/haploinsufficiency case, and it does not test pharmacochaperoning of a missense protein, the question the Munc18-1 worm paper (30266908) answered positively. (2) No protein was measured: no validated antibodies exist for the paralogs, so quantity and solubility are both unknown, before and after treatment. (3) The bath concentration was bounded by larval toxicity (Figure 1C: 100 percent survival at 10 and 25 micromolar, falling above 50), about a hundredth of the worm study's concentration, so a concentration-dependent effect could not be tested. (4) Assay quality: the paper reports a Z-prime of -5.936 and calls it satisfactory against published larval screens; by the usual screening convention (external to the paper, labelled) a negative value means the control separation does not beat the noise, so the assay's power to detect a modest rescue is unestablished. (5) The text and Methods disagree on exposure (24 h in Results, 48 h in the Locomotion Assay Methods).
- **Verdict for the card.** The title's "argues against" outruns the design. For a missense, protein-expressing allele this paper is INCONCLUSIVE; for a null allele it is a weak negative with the power limits above. A repurposing card for a destabilised-missense lesion should cite it only as "a null-allele zebrafish negative at non-toxic bath concentrations, protein not measured", and should not use it to lower the prior on pharmacochaperoning of a missense protein.
- **Bearing on PA / PB.** None directly: no protein quantity or fractionation. It supports the R1 theme that a missense-specific premise needs a protein-expressing model and a protein read-out before a negative or positive means anything. NOT WWOX EVIDENCE.

### 42157447 - DeLeeuw 2026, phenylbutyrate in SLC6A1 variants: pharmacochaperoning or HDAC inhibition - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-42157447-01` (**complete_fulltext_read**: all eight figures opened as images and paired to captions with `figure_legend_pairs.py`, because the image file numbers are not the article's figure numbers, for example file g001 is article Figure 6; the article lists no supplement), manifest PASS, 20 locators. Measures quantity and function together, in cells and two knock-in mouse lines, and compares four mechanism classes. Three text-versus-image discrepancies are recorded (below).

- **What it establishes (DATO, GAT-1 transporter, HEK293T and two heterozygous knock-in lines).** All 32 variants lose [3H]GABA uptake; the 1:1 "heterozygous" condition keeps 56.5 to 80.2 percent of wild type (Figure 1C, opened). The phenylbutyrate class raises uptake by 19 to 39 percent over each variant's own baseline (Figure 2B, opened), a bile-acid chaperone by 16 to 32 percent, two HDAC inhibitors by 7 to 18 percent (butyrate) or by about nothing (SAHA, Figure 3, opened). In four variants total GAT-1 protein rises from about 0.1 to 0.17 of wild type to about 0.7 to 1.4 with the phenylbutyrate class (Figure 4G, opened).
- **Quantity and function again do not move in proportion.** Protein rises roughly six- to nine-fold (Figure 4G) while uptake rises 20 to 40 percent over a baseline that is already 55 to 80 percent of wild type; and the wild-type bar rises about 15 percent too (Figure 2B), so the treatment is not variant-specific. By the arithmetic of Figures 1C and 2B (INFERENZA: two experiments, controls shared across experiments per the Methods) the treated variants sit at about 0.7 to 1.1 of untreated wild type, not uniformly "to or above WT levels" as the Figure 2 legend says. Butyrate raised the immature, ER-retained GAT-1 pool (Figure 5F-G) without function: the same paper holds a quantity-up, function-flat case.
- **Mechanism (answer to R1 question 3, in this system only).** The evidence that phenylbutyrate acts as a pharmacochaperone rather than an HDAC inhibitor is inferential: (i) two HDAC inhibitors rescue little in cells and butyrate does not rescue synaptosomal uptake and worsens 4-8 Hz EEG events in one line; (ii) a bile acid without HDAC activity gives a similar variant-by-variant pattern; (iii) phenylbutyrate raises the mature-to-immature ratio, which butyrate mostly does not. No direct binding, thermal-stability or folding measurement is reported, and an eIF2alpha-dephosphorylation inhibitor with no chaperoning role gave the strongest uptake gain for some variants (Figure 4A, S295L about 2.5-fold), so stress-pathway modulation is an equal candidate for part of the effect. The butyrate S295L maturation ratio rises about 2.7-fold (Figure 5G, four asterisks) and the text paragraph does not list it. Overexpressed BiP or calnexin raise wild-type and variant protein alike.
- **Three text-versus-image discrepancies, checked on the images.** (1) Figure 6: the panel letters printed on the image are A = PBA, B = butyrate (A288V) and C = PBA, D = butyrate (S295L), the opposite of the text and legend; the conclusions agree with the image. (2) Figure 8: the p values printed on panels C and F are 0.0004 and 0.7252, not 0.0119 and 0.1046 as stated in the text and legend; direction and conclusion are unchanged (butyrate worse in S295L, no benefit in A288V). (3) The Figure 8 legend places the representative traces at B (A288V) and D (S295L); on the image the traces are B (S295L) and E (A288V). None changes the finding; all are logged so a later reader does not quote the text p values.
