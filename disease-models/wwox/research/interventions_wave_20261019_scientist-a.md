**Standing statement for every row below.** There is no WWOX-deficient evidence on any of these interventions (transfusion, iron, plasma, FFP, plasma exchange, albumin, IVIG). Every paper in this note is a substitute: adjacent biology, a different disease class, or a clinical-practice paper in another population. None is WWOX evidence, and a substitute is read for what transfers and what does not. Caution (BLOCK-1): nothing here is a recommendation for or against any intervention in a child with WWOX-related disease.

## Per-paper readings (appended in reading order)

### 42712566 Sinha 2026 (H1, tier 1) - a PROTOCOL, not a review
Measures nothing: it is a plan for a systematic review of iron supplementation, myelination and neurodevelopment in children 0-3 years. The selection file calls it "a systematic review"; the title and body say protocol. It changes nothing in the model. Two negatives matter: (1) it cannot answer H1 question 2 (is WWOX-null hypomyelination modifiable by iron?), and (2) its eligibility text excludes participants with significant congenital neurological abnormalities, so a DEE cohort would probably not be in the eventual review. Revival trigger: publication of the review's results, if they include a myelin-imaging endpoint. Depth: complete (protocol has no results or figures). No commit candidate.

### 37124220 Jin 2023 (H1, tier 2) - ferroptosis inhibitors in epilepsy, review
Measures nothing new (narrative review of chemoconvulsant rodent models: kainic acid, pilocarpine, PTZ, FeCl3; HT22/SH-SY5Y cell stress; one cited paediatric blood study and one cited patient-cell study with EPI-743). WWOX absent. Changes nothing: the model has no iron or ferroptosis line and this review does not supply a WWOX datum to compare with. Negatives: cannot say whether WWOX loss changes neuronal ferroptosis susceptibility; internal inconsistency (conclusion calls ferrostatin an iron chelator, its own Table 1 lists GPX4/P38 MAPK); the oligodendrocyte question is open in the review itself. Figures 1-2 opened as images. Depth: complete. No commit candidate.

### 39239479 Porras 2024 (H1, tier 2) - Irp2-null mouse behaviour (iron misregulation comparator)
Measures a constitutive Irp2-null mouse battery (rotarod about 40 percent shorter latency, lighter animals with weight-covariate-dependent hang-time result, reduced thermal nociception, reversal-specific learning deficit, n = 6 per group in the Barnes maze). Does not measure haemoglobin, iron content, seizures or any intervention; the human anaemia and epilepsy of IREB2 disease are cited context. By the authors' own statement the mouse is much milder than the human disease. For the model: nothing changes; it is a severity-discordance comparator, not WWOX evidence (the Wwox-null mouse is the severe side of that discordance). Figures 1, 8, 11 opened; supplement legends only (declared debt: eight TIFFs and the peer-review PDF). Depth: partial. No commit candidate.

### 34909297 Padda 2021 (H1, tier 2) - anaemia, epilepsy and antiseizure drugs, narrative review
Says (cited, nothing new): anaemia meets epilepsy through sickle cell disease, iron deficiency (contradictory for febrile seizures) and antiseizure-drug marrow, immune, oxidative and folate/B12 mechanisms; management is stop the drug, count monitoring, folic acid, transfusion where haemolysis is severe. Does not support: any disease-versus-drug separation in a DEE cohort, any WWOX or genetic-epilepsy anaemia datum, any effect of transfusion on seizures. Its two seizure-threshold statements contradict each other. Changes nothing in the model; H1 question 5 answered only as "both occur, in other populations". Figure 1 (a lifestyle graphic) opened. Depth: complete. No commit candidate.

### 41293173 Garcia-Delgado 2025 (H1, tier 2) - deferasirox pre/post in transfusion-dependent MDS
Measures leukocyte flow-cytometry readouts (ferritin, ROS, glutathione, Rhodamine-123 MFI, adhesion markers, endothelial progenitors, cytokines) before and after deferasirox in 23 adults with low-risk MDS; uncontrolled, no transfusion record, haemoglobin unchanged. Does not support: that transfusional iron damages mitochondria (the paper's Figure 5 is a model; deferasirox acts "beyond iron removal" by the authors' own words), anything about brain, children or WWOX. Found while reading: Figure 2 contradicts two statements in its own text (baseline EPC/EC "reduced" versus healthy controls drawn lower; healthy EC 0.30 versus a box near 0.12; image supplied at 649 px), and Supplementary Table S1's BH and Holm values do not reproduce from its own inputs (recomputed: BH 0.000106 for all 17 tied outcomes, Holm 0.0018; the table prints a ladder). H1 question 4 answered: the overlap with WWOX respiratory findings is unmeasured. All five figures opened; supplement Word files read as derived text. Depth: complete. No commit candidate.

### 42794294 Fitz 2026 (H1, tier 2) - iron supplementation and infant neurodevelopment, review
Says (cited, nothing new): supplementation benefits iron-deficient or high-risk infants, not iron-replete healthy term infants, with possible long-term harm in replete infants from one Chilean cohort lineage (a U-shaped risk). Does not support: iron modifying WWOX-null hypomyelination (H1 question 2 answered: nothing tests it; the single DTI-type study cited has no untreated arm and no motor difference at 6 months, though the Discussion says "reversible"), anything about a genetic encephalopathy. Found while reading: the 7840 total is the arithmetic sum of Table 2 sample sizes and counts a 412-child subsample of a 3300-child trial twice; the PRISMA figure (last search 15 May 2025; different exclusion categories) contradicts the Methods (search December 2025 to April 2026); registered as an undergraduate thesis. All three figures opened (webp, viewing conversion). Depth: complete. No commit candidate.

### 37733345 Chock 2023 (H1, tier 3) - NIRS tissue oxygenation after transfusion in extremely preterm infants (TOP NIRS)
Measures: cerebral saturation rises about 2.7-4.8 points and mesenteric 5.6-6.7 after transfusion with no change in peripheral saturation, no difference between haemoglobin-threshold groups; exploratory association of transfusions at pre-transfusion Csat below 50 percent with death or impairment (OR 2.41, P = .03). H1 question 3 answered for the readout only (cerebral NIRS saturation/extraction moves after transfusion in preterm infants). Does not support: benefit of higher saturation, any WWOX or genetic-DEE brain (such infants were excluded), or a mitochondrially stressed brain behaving the same (NIRS confounded by cardiac output, carbon dioxide, ductus). Outcome analysis exploratory, tree leaf of 10 infants, attrition 140 to 101 to 80. Figures 1-3 opened; protocol supplement unread (partial depth). No commit candidate.

### 42313692 Bailey 2026 (H1, tier 3) - liberal versus restrictive haemoglobin thresholds and cerebral oxygenation, meta-analysis
Pools seven NIRS studies: crSO2 rises 6.40 points (restrictive) versus 2.75 (liberal), cFTOE falls 0.08 versus 0.03, certainty very low (I-squared 0.93). Does not support a threshold effect: the one randomised within-trial contrast (TOP arms) is 4.48 versus 4.84; the pooled gap is between-study (small-variance observational studies on one side, two liberal studies one with baseline crSO2 77-78 on the other); Aktas is classed restrictive with week-1 and week-2 thresholds equal to the TOP high-threshold values (cross-document reading with the TOP NIRS supplement); certainty stated as low in Results and very low in the Limitations and GRADE table; no outcome benefit shown; rise may be transient. H1 question 3: readout answered, no WWOX bearing. Figures 1-3, supplementary risk-of-bias images and GRADE table read. Depth: complete. No commit candidate.

### 38463073 Shafique 2024 (H1, tier 3) - neonatal transfusion strategies, narrative review
Says (cited): trial and guideline overview, with a lean to restrictive thresholds; transfusion complications in neonates (hyperkalaemia, hypothermia, glucose fluctuation both directions, 2,3-DPG depletion in stored red cells shifts the oxygen curve left for hours). Does not support a direction: its Abstract, Highlights, Advantages section and Conclusion point different ways; "RBC trials" Table 4 is mostly observational cohorts; no methods. Nothing about WWOX or a chronic-encephalopathy child. One class-level observation touching an existing model strand: transfusion is associated with neonatal hypo- and hyperglycaemia (confounded by indication), relevant only as a reason to read glucose beside any blood-product event in the hypoglycaemia-prone Wwox-null frame (CLAIM 036), not WWOX evidence. No figures. Depth: complete. No commit candidate.

### 41904698 Nguyen 2026 (H1, tier 3) - physiology-guided red-cell transfusion in adult critical illness, expert perspective
Says (cited, nothing new): haemoglobin alone does not track tissue oxygenation; restrictive thresholds are broadly safe at population level with a subgroup signal in type 1 myocardial infarction; markers at global, regional and cellular level; cut-offs are the authors' own and explicitly unvalidated. Relevance to H1 question 3: one cited adult-ICU study found mitochondrial oxygen tension and consumption did not change consistently after transfusion, with many patients starting normal. Does not support: any statement about a mitochondrially stressed brain, a child, or WWOX (zero pediatric content; the "mitochondrial failure" of the abstract rests on that one study of patients mostly without cellular oxygen deficit). A conceptual principle only (INFERENZA): if oxygen use rather than supply were limiting, extra carrying capacity adds little; nothing in the repository measures WWOX tissue oxygen delivery or extraction. Tables read from JATS; table images not opened. Depth: complete. No commit candidate.

### 42794205 De Rose 2026 (H1 tier 3; also H2-relevant) - blood-product burden in cooled HIE neonates
Measures (single NICU, 142 neonates): 52.1 percent received a product, plasma 38.7 percent (most used), prothrombin complex 20.4, antithrombin 14.1, red cells 10.6, platelets 9.9; predictors visible bleeding (aOR 12.95, CI 1.34-124.97 from 16 infants) and respiratory support. Does not support: a causal effect of transfusion on the brain (authors: unadjusted, proxy for severity), any efficacy of plasma (use-pattern paper), anything about a genetic DEE. Found while reading: the text says intracranial bleeding was more frequent in transfused infants, Table 4 shows 21.6 versus 22.1 percent (the difference is hypoxic-ischaemic injury 28.4 versus 10.3 percent); Figure 1 disagrees with the single versus multi-product counts in the text (likely transposed). Figure 1 opened. Depth: complete. No commit candidate.

### 39712732 Jadhav 2024 (H1, tier 3) - iron deficiency anaemia and first febrile seizure, case-control (Cureus)
Measures (150 children, one hospital): cases had lower haemoglobin, MCV, ferritin and higher RDW and TIBC; anaemia in 96 percent of cases and 85 percent of febrile controls. Does not support: causality or "correctable risk factor" (single admission draw, no intervention), anaemic versus healthy (both groups anaemic), an interval-bounded fourfold odds (recomputed OR 4.24, 95 percent CI 0.93-19.3), anything about DEE (developmental delay excluded). Confounding unaddressed (age, weight, temperature, fever-raised ferritin). No figures. Depth: complete. No commit candidate.

### 32685205 Arsyad 2020 (H1, tier 3) - long-term home-made ketogenic diet in healthy rats: acidosis, anaemia, oxidative stress
Measures (15 rats, one end-point sample): about 40 percent weight loss, glucose 57 mg/dL, ketones near 8 mmol/L, pH 7.36 versus control 7.52, haemoglobin 13.76 to 11.98 with red-cell count not significantly lower, tissue MDA up, plasma SOD down. Does not support: a clinical ketogenic diet in a child (8.6 to 1 home-made, adult healthy rats), "metabolic acidosis" (control is alkalotic, ketogenic pH is near normal; abstract base excess -5.57 disagrees with Table 5, 0.32 versus 1.08), "anaemia" as diet effect (no baseline, no iron or copper data; energy intake equal or higher in Table 3 despite 40 percent weight loss, so malnutrition is open), or any WWOX or epilepsy statement. Truncated MDA axes. CLAIM 042 (ketogenic responders) is neither moved nor tested; glucose caution is class-level only. Figures 1-3 opened. Depth: complete. No commit candidate.
