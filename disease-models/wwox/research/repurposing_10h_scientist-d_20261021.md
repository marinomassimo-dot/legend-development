### 33180985 - Kotulska 2021, EPISTOP (tuberous sclerosis, pre-symptomatic antiseizure treatment) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-33180985-01` (complete, staged) - manifest PASS, 19 locators (17 body, 2 figure attestations). Figures 1 and 2 opened as page renders after `paper_packet.py rank` flagged the Figure 2 page as likely vector.

- **Design (DATO).** Infants with definite TSC and no seizure history, video-EEG monthly; a drug was started either at first epileptiform EEG activity (preventive) or after the first seizure (conventional). Randomised at 6 sites (13 versus 14); at 4 sites ethics boards refused randomisation, so a parallel **non-randomised open-label** group was enrolled (12 versus 15). Caregivers and treating physicians were blinded to the EEG data; EEG readers and psychologists blinded to allocation.
- **Endpoint type.** Primary: **time from birth to first clinical seizure** (event-time count outcome). Secondary at 24 months: drug-resistant epilepsy, infantile spasms, diary-derived proportion of days with seizures (count-derived), and **instrument endpoints** Bayley-III cognitive score below 70 and ADOS-2.
- **Symptomatic versus modifying.** Not separated. The "modification" claim is a seizure-endpoint claim ("natural history of seizures"). The instrument endpoint (neurodevelopmental delay) was **not significantly different** (RCT 33% versus 50%; text and Table 3 disagree on the open-label figures, 20/30% in text, 17/25% in Table 3). The authors give three reasons (small n, follow-up to 2 years, benefit of intensive monitoring in the control arm) and add that processes before the surrogate-triggered start may already shape outcome.
- **Control.** Conventional (post-seizure) treatment; randomised in half the infants only. **Both arms received the surveillance**, so the contrast is timing of the drug, not drug versus nothing; the authors name two interventions bundled in the strategy.
- **Conditioning.** Infants with a seizure before the EEG trigger (22 of 94) were excluded from the primary and secondary analyses; the analysed set is conditional on the surrogate appearing first. The abstract's medians (364 versus 124 days) are for patients **who developed seizures**.
- **Statistical fragility visible in the tables (DATO).** In the randomised part alone, clinical seizures at 24 months p 0.202, infantile spasms p 0.096; drug-resistant epilepsy p 0.047; significance of several headline results comes from **pooling randomised and non-randomised** infants. Text reports p 0.011 for the randomised seizure-free proportions but the randomised panel of Figure 2 prints log-rank p 0.009 and the 0.011 sits on the open-label panel (panel versus text, recorded as two entries). Table 2 prints a pooled median age "9996" (typographical artefact, not interpreted).
- **Follow-up.** 24 months; no effect on development at that time; a pilot's school-age follow-up is cited by the authors, not read here.
- **What it gives a card.** A surrogate-triggered pre-symptomatic start needs a **validated trigger**; WOREE has none (no qEEG, no fluid marker, no pharmacodynamic readout). A seizure-time endpoint can look significant while the developmental instrument does not move: the same dissociation the WOREE diet series prints.
- **What it cannot claim.** Disease modification of development. Class-level inference only; no transfer of any regimen.

### 39625912 - Wied 2024, secondary analysis and methodological lessons from EPISTOP - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-39625912-01` (staged; supplementary declared `captions_only`: S1, the 56-page trial protocol, was read in its summary, objectives, blinding, early-stopping and statistics sections from an extracted text layer, not end to end) - manifest PASS, 24 locators. Figures 1 and 2 (Kaplan-Meier) opened as the embedded 302 ppi rasters of the PDF page.

- **What it is.** A non-pre-planned re-analysis of the 27 randomised infants with a selection-bias term, plus a methods discussion. It tests **one** bias model on a reconstructed allocation sequence ("due to missing information") and finds the hazard ratio essentially unchanged (2.91 to 2.82).
- **The abstract and the table disagree on a label (DATO).** The abstract calls hazard ratio 2.89 "bias-corrected"; Results and Table 3 give 2.89 as the uncorrected study-entry model (IIa) and 2.82 and 2.77 as the corrected ones. Cite the table, not the abstract.
- **A developmental instrument again did not move; one sub-scale did, in a direction the text reads the other way (DATO).** Bayley-III cognitive: mean difference 4.90, interval from minus 5.58 to 15.38, p 0.34. Only the social-emotional composite reached p below 0.05 (nine Bayley endpoints in two models each, no multiplicity correction stated); Table 4 gives preventive 78.78 versus conventional 100.00 and the footnote defines the difference as conventional minus preventive, so the preventive arm scored **lower**, while the text calls it an advantage of preventive treatment. Table 5 also repeats the identical corrected row for two different endpoints. An evaluation card should treat a sub-scale at p 0.04 among eighteen tests as noise unless pre-specified.
- **Event counts do not reconcile with the primary report (DATO).** This reanalysis counts 8 of 13 preventive infants with a seizure; the primary report's Table 3 gives 7 of 13. Medians also differ. The reconciliation is not in either paper.
- **Pre-specification, from the protocol (S1, version 05, March 2016).** The protocol's **primary endpoint is biomarker collection**; the **key secondary** endpoint is "better epilepsy outcome" (the summary words it "better neurodevelopmental outcome"), with time to seizure onset one of several listed parameters beside a neuropsychological battery; the sample size is 60 randomised infants powered on clinical seizure development; early stopping for benefit at an interim analysis (p 0.01). The 2021 paper designates time to first clinical seizure as primary. **What cannot be said:** whether an earlier protocol version fixed that designation, since only version 05 was read and it is dated two years after the first recruitment the 2021 paper reports. The faithful statement is that the endpoint hierarchy in the document available differs from the one published.
- **Blinding design worth copying as a design fact.** A central randomiser receives the EEG report and returns only "epilepsy or no epilepsy" (treat or not) to the treating neurologist; the clinician therefore never sees the EEG that triggers allocation.
- **Rare-disease operating facts (DATO, quoted).** Only 11.5% of registered trials concern rare diseases and about 30% of those are uncompleted or unpublished at four years; ethics committees at four sites split on randomisation for opposite reasons (preventive treatment already accepted at two, conventional-only accepted at two); a regulator pilot supports repurposing of established medicines by non-profit and academic groups.
- **Follow-up and replication.** Same 24 months, same data; nothing here is an independent replication.
- **Card consequence.** Declare the endpoint hierarchy before enrolment, state which endpoint is primary, and carry multiplicity for instrument sub-scales.

### 34270884 - French 2021, antiepileptogenesis and disease modification: clinical and regulatory issues (2018 NINDS workshop report) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-34270884-01` (complete, staged) - manifest PASS, 18 locators. A narrative workshop report: no Methods or Results section, no figure, no table, no supplement (element counts in the JATS are zero). Its authors include investigators of the trial it summarises, which is a conflict a reader should weigh, not a flaw of the quotation.

- **Vocabulary a card can use (DATO, quoted).** Epilepsy disease modification "will prevent or retard progression in the seriousness of epilepsy ... or lessen or eliminate the manifestations of epilepsy, once it has been established"; a drug that prevents a comorbidity "would not be called epilepsy disease modification unless it also impacted seizures". 🔴 **For WOREE this has a consequence the paper does not draw:** in the one WOREE response series the developmental row is flat while seizure control is reported, and the deficit precedes seizures; a drug that moved development without moving seizures would fall outside this definition of *epilepsy* disease modification, and one that moved seizures only would not touch development. Both could be real and neither would be "modification" of the whole disease, so a card needs a disease-level modification definition of its own (INFERENZA, premise: the paper's own definition).
- **What the standard drug-trial design cannot show (DATO, quoted).** Randomised placebo-controlled add-on trials followed by open-label extension "would not be able to demonstrate disease modification". The authors' sketched modification design instead uses a baseline period, treatment, **an outcome period with the agent withdrawn** so that an antiseizure effect is not counted, patient as own control, dose chosen by **target engagement on a biomarker**, and a post-treatment durability window whose length "will need further clarification".
- **Endpoint expectation.** The regulator's traditional requirement is an objective clinical endpoint such as seizure incidence; a validated biomarker could later replace the event. Multiple sclerosis is cited as the precedent in which a community-accepted progression scale made disease-modification claims possible.
- **Biomarker bar.** A prevention biomarker "need[s] to properly reflect the epileptogenesis process rather than the injury or recovery process"; prevention trials with standard antiseizure drugs failed "at least partly" because patients of different risk were pooled, and trials are "too expensive ... without predictive biomarkers". WOREE has neither a risk biomarker nor a pharmacodynamic one (CLAIM 046).
- **Tension inside the paper (DATO).** The report states that currently available drugs "none is disease-modifying or antiepileptogenic, whether introduced before or after clinical seizures", and in the same section presents the EPISTOP result as documenting lower risk of seizures, drug resistance and infantile spasms; the trial's developmental endpoint is not mentioned. The authors' generalisation that TSC lessons "are likely to be applicable in other epileptic encephalopathies" is ESPANSIONE and carries no data in this paper.
- **Replication / follow-up.** The report cites two randomised vigabatrin trials (the one read here and a US trial, PreVeNT, named but not described) and a planned comparison of a second drug class. It gives no follow-up requirement beyond "long enough ... for the objective outcome event to be seen".

### 38959712 - Downs 2024, ganaxolone non-seizure outcomes in CDKL5 deficiency disorder (randomised placebo-controlled trial) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-38959712-01` (complete, staged) - manifest PASS, 20 locators (13 body or table, 3 figure attestations). The Europe PMC route returned HTTP 500 for this record; the NCBI efetch JATS of the author manuscript was used. The three figures were opened as the native 300 ppi JPEGs. The primary seizure paper is paywalled (R4 BLOCKED), so the seizure result below is **as reported by this secondary analysis**, not read at its source.

- **Design (DATO).** Double-blind, placebo-controlled, 1:1, adjunctive to existing medicines, 17 weeks, 101 children aged 2 to 19 years with at least 16 major motor seizures per month; analysed as randomised; missing data by full-information maximum likelihood.
- **Endpoint type.** Primary (in the parent trial): percentage change in the 28-day count of major motor seizures; powered on that alone (92% power for a 30% difference). The **non-seizure outcomes are parent- or proxy-reported questionnaires**: ADAMS (five behaviour subscales), a sleepiness subscale, and QI-Disability (six domains and a total). **Development was not an outcome**: a study-modified 15-item score from routine clinical examinations served only as a baseline covariate, and was imbalanced between arms (median 2 against 4).
- **Result and how far it carries (DATO).** Two of the five behaviour subscales reached p 0.025 and 0.046 (adjusted differences minus 1.27 and minus 0.58); the lower of the two has its upper interval limit at the zero line in Figure 2; sleepiness and all quality-of-life intervals include zero (Figure 3, total 2.64, interval minus 1.74 to 7.02). Raw means fell in **both** arms (manic/hyperactive 7.0 to 4.8 on active drug, 6.3 to 5.2 on placebo): a placebo response in a parent-reported scale is visible in the table. The authors write that the upper limits "were close to the line of no effect", that the sample "may be inadequate" for these outcomes, that they "do not know what differences ... are clinically meaningful", and that quality of life may need longer than 17 weeks. Twelve tests are reported without a multiplicity statement, and the article does not state whether they were pre-specified.
- **Separating symptomatic from modifying.** Not attempted; 17 weeks on drug, no off-drug period. The seizure link was explored only by within-arm correlation (compulsive-behaviour correlation +0.33 on drug and minus 0.35 on placebo, opposite signs), because mediation was underpowered.
- **What it gives a card.** The only randomised DEE trial in the group with non-seizure outcomes shows what a symptomatic-plus-behaviour package looks like: proxy-reported, small, unadjusted for multiplicity, short. **It contains no developmental instrument as an outcome.**
- **What it cannot claim.** That the behaviour effect is independent of seizure change; that anything persists after exposure; anything about development.

### 42246937 - Correale 2026, cognitive and behavioural clinical outcome assessments in DEE (instrument review) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-42246937-01` (complete, staged) - manifest PASS, 23 locators. A narrative review: no Methods, Results or supplement; its single figure is a one-child bar chart, opened as a 400 dpi page render. Several authors list industry advisory roles; the instrument facts are checkable against the instruments, not against the authors.

- **The instrument menu (DATO, from Tables 3 and 4).** Cognition and development: Wechsler scales (lowest standard score 40, floor effects, unsuitable for severe or profound disability), Bayley-IV (0 to 42 months, no composite score), Griffiths III (to 72 months), Mullen (to 68 months), Developmental Profile 4 (caregiver report). Adaptive behaviour: Vineland-III (standard-score floor 20) and ABAS-3 (floor 40). Behaviour: checklists normed on typically developing children are not suited to moderate to profound disability; the review points to the Developmental Behaviour Checklist, Aberrant Behaviour Checklist and Behaviour Problems Inventory as ID-normed.
- **Responsiveness is the central finding (DATO, quoted).** Standard scores floor out; **raw scores, age equivalents and growth scale values** show within-patient change that the standard score hides. Figure 1 is a single child with a HUWE1 variant, interpersonal-relationships domain, 38 to 44 months: raw score 9 to 14, age equivalent 4 to 7 months, growth scale value 30 to 46.7, standard score 2 to 2 (n = 1; illustrative, no comparator, the child was also on an investigational antiseizure treatment per the text).
- **Responder definition.** Fixed thresholds on standard scores are "insufficient"; responders may be defined by stabilisation, re-emergence of micro-skills or modest participation shifts anchored to the individual; goal attainment scaling is offered, with the stated need for rigorous goal-setting; the review adds that **caregivers may value stability**, which is a claim about meaningfulness and not about modification.
- **Follow-up (DATO, quoted).** Where natural history is thin, 12 months or more; 1 to 3 Vineland-III raw-score points (about 2 to 3 growth-scale-value points) "may be considered meaningful"; in a Dravet cohort adaptive functioning over 12 to 24 months changes "small and domain-specific".
- **Control.** The review proposes a child serving as their own control "to evaluate treatment impact on developmental progress, decline, or maintenance" - which presupposes more than one pre-treatment time point; none exists for WOREE (INFERENZA, premise: the only WOREE series reports one timepoint per patient).
- **The paper's own limit (DATO).** "There remains also a clear need for further validation work to determine how well existing cognitive and behavioural measures perform in children with DEEs." None of these instruments is validated in WOREE; a search of this repository found none applied (early-benefit note, section 1.5).
- **Answer to R4 question 5 in part.** The responsive instruments are the ones scored in raw, age-equivalent or growth-scale form; the standard-score forms most trials report are the ones that floor out.

### 41784472 - Sullivan 2026, SKYLINE: soticlestat in Dravet syndrome, phase 3 (non-replication of a phase 2 signal) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-41784472-01` (complete, staged) - manifest PASS, 23 locators (15 body, 3 supplement text, 7 figure attestations including supplementary Figures S1 and S2). Data figures opened as 400 dpi page renders; the supplementary docx was read as text and its two images opened. Sponsor-funded; authors include sponsor employees and stockholders; the sponsor does not plan to share data (all quoted).

- **Design (DATO).** Randomised 1:1, double-blind, placebo-controlled, add-on, 16 weeks (4 titration, 12 maintenance); 144 randomised (71 and 73); stratified by age and region; 21 discontinuations (8 and 13; adverse events 4 and 11).
- **Endpoint type.** Primary: percentage change from baseline in the **28-day count of convulsive seizures** (a diary count, agreed with epileptologists before the start). Key secondaries, in a **hierarchical gatekeeping order**: responder rate (50 percent or more reduction) first, then caregiver and clinician **global impression of improvement** (7-point scales), a clinician non-seizure-symptoms global scale, a quality-of-life inventory, and a seizure intensity-and-duration global scale.
- **The non-replication (DATO).** Primary difference minus 15.64 percent, 95% interval minus 31.30 to 0.24, p 0.061 (maintenance period p 0.089); the phase 2 trial had reported a placebo-adjusted median reduction of 50.0 percent in its Dravet subgroup (interval minus 74.2 to minus 24.7, p 0.0002). The sensitivity analyses give p 0.053 to 0.090. The authors name a higher placebo response and a more refractory population as contributors, and in the same Discussion say the miss is "unlikely to be due to a high placebo response" because the rate matched other approved-drug trials; both sentences are in the paper. An SCN1A-positive subgroup (exploratory, nominal p 0.045) and a post hoc quartile split (the most resistant quartile, +15.0 percent, unfavourable) are offered as explanations; they are post hoc and were not multiplicity-controlled.
- **What the secondary endpoints show about the *kind* of endpoint (DATO, Figure 4).** The caregiver and clinician global impressions favoured the drug (Care GI-I 60.6 versus 35.3 percent; CGI-I 52.1 versus 26.8) **and the intensity-and-duration scale** (58.5 versus 24.6), but the three non-seizure sub-items did not (alertness 25.4 versus 25.4; communication 31.0 versus 32.4; disruptive behaviours 23.9 versus 19.7) and the quality-of-life change was numerically **worse** on drug (minus 1.17 versus plus 1.86, difference minus 3.03, interval minus 7.59 to 1.52). **A third to over a half of placebo-arm caregivers reported improvement.** Global impression scales are seizure-contaminated and placebo-sensitive; the non-seizure items are the ones that carry information about anything beyond seizures, and they were flat. The authors, after the primary missed, write that "positive outcomes cannot be declared for secondary endpoints" and also conclude that "multiple secondary endpoints showed clinically meaningful efficacy" (Conclusions).
- **🔴 The pharmacodynamic point that matters most for us (DATO).** The supplement shows the target-engagement biomarker did what a pharmacodynamic readout should: median plasma 24-hydroxycholesterol fell from 92.3 to 18.2 ng/mL on drug (about 80 percent) against 92.6 to 83.3 on placebo, in about 60 per arm at each visit. **A clear pharmacodynamic effect and a failed clinical endpoint coexisted.** Pharmacodynamic movement is necessary to show the drug acts on its target; it is not evidence of benefit. For WOREE the situation is worse in both directions: there is no pharmacodynamic readout at all (CLAIM 046), so neither the necessary nor the insufficient condition can be shown.
- **Follow-up.** 16 weeks on drug, with an open-label extension that the paper does not report on; nothing about durability, development or modification.
- **Card consequence.** (a) Score a mechanism-matched candidate's phase-2 signal against the replication rate of seizure-count signals: here a subgroup estimate of 50 percent became 16 percent. (b) Require a pre-registered hierarchy with the non-seizure item high in the order if early benefit beyond seizures is the claim. (c) Treat global impressions as placebo-sensitive and seizure-coupled.

### 41891667 - Guerrini 2026, SKYWAY: soticlestat in Lennox-Gastaut syndrome, phase 3 (second non-replication) - NOT WWOX EVIDENCE

Receipt `FTR-20261021-41891667-01` (complete, staged) - manifest PASS, 25 locators (16 text, 2 supplement text, 8 figure attestations incl. Figures S1-S4). Same sponsor, same conflict structure and data-sharing statement as the Dravet report.

- **Design (DATO).** Randomised 1:1, double-blind, placebo-controlled, add-on, 16 weeks (4 titration, 12 maintenance), 270 randomised (136 and 134), age 2 to 55 years; discontinuations 10 versus 20, adverse events 5 versus 19 (Figure 1).
- **How the phase 3 endpoint was chosen (DATO, quoted) - the most transferable fact in the group.** The phase 2 LGS subgroup's drop-seizure result was non-significant (placebo-adjusted 17.08% p 0.116 maintenance; 14.81% p 0.128 full); "an additional post hoc analysis" of seizures that led to or would have led to a fall "showed a larger signal" (minus 30.6%, interval minus 82.18 to 14.44, which includes zero), and "based on these data ... the primary endpoint was changed" to major motor drop seizures. **The phase 3 result on the endpoint selected from a post hoc subgroup signal was minus 1.17 percent, p 0.785** (Figure 2 raw medians minus 6.69 and minus 6.11). A post hoc, interval-includes-zero estimate was used to pick a primary endpoint and a 270-person trial; it did not replicate.
- **Other phase 2 to 3 differences the authors name (DATO).** Shorter treatment (16 versus 20 weeks), wider age range (to 55 years versus 17), older mean age; they report that neither a placebo response nor compliance nor adult inclusion explains the result, and the adult-versus-paediatric subgroup data are "not shown".
- **The pharmacodynamic biomarker again (DATO).** Plasma 24-hydroxycholesterol fell by about 80 percent on drug (Figure S4: 83.55 to 17.20 ng/mL; placebo 70.60 to 69.45; baseline differed between arms) with no clinical effect. The authors infer from this that the drug was "sufficiently dosed" and conclude "targeting the CH24H mechanism of action is not a suitable pharmacotherapeutic strategy for the treatment of LGS" - an inference at mechanism level from a single trial in one syndrome (INFERENZA, author), set against the same mechanism's partial numerical signal in Dravet syndrome.
- **Secondary endpoints, by hierarchy (DATO).** Responder rates 16.4 versus 9.6 percent (nominal p 0.081); the global impression scales numerically favoured the drug with a placebo arm already at 30 to 39 percent; the non-seizure sub-items 31.8 versus 21.8 (alertness, communication) and 18.2 versus 9.8 (disruptive behaviour, nominal p 0.032, which the abstract highlights); quality of life changed minus 2.39 on placebo and minus 1.86 on drug (Figure 3C prints a mean difference under an "odds ratio" label). The authors' own words: effects "small, making it difficult to draw meaningful and clinically relevant conclusions".
- **Smaller observations.** The planned sample was about 117 per arm; 270 were randomised and Figure S1 says N approximately 234; the reason is not given.
- **Follow-up.** 16 weeks; open-label extension not reported; no durability or development data.
- **Card consequence.** A candidate whose support is a phase 2 subgroup result chosen post hoc is a prior with a measured replication failure behind it; record the endpoint selection history on the card; require that the pharmacodynamic readout be reported separately from, and never as support for, benefit.
