# Meta — Human Spectrum

## Scope
This meta stabilizes the human layer of the WWOX system:
- phenotype spectrum
- genotype-phenotype logic
- natural history / survival
- longitudinal trajectory

It is not an exhaustive review, but an operational synthesis meant to connect:
- claim layer
- working model
- research lines
- counseling logic

## Core question
Is human WWOX disease a single undifferentiated block, or is there a readable clinical-genotypic spectrum with real value for interpreting a case?

## Current answer
The convergence of human data supports a strong but prudent reading:
- **DATO**: WWOX disease spans a spectrum from severe WOREE/WWOX-DEE to milder SCAR12-like phenotypes
- **DATO**: null/null genotypes carry substantially worse survival than genotypes with at least one missense variant
- **DATO**: selected patients survive into adolescence/adulthood with severe disability and possible later regression
- **DATO**: exon 6 splice and Q230P compound-context logic are clinically meaningful for genotype interpretation

## Block A — Core severe syndrome (WOREE / WWOX-DEE)
Across human series and cohorts, the severe pole is characterized by:
- very early seizure onset (often days/weeks to early months)
- multiple seizure types
- drug resistance
- profound developmental impairment
- severe motor / feeding / visual burden
- movement disorders and abnormal tone
- high mortality

This is the backbone clinical syndrome and remains the default high-risk frame.

## Block B — Genotype stratification
Human data support genotype-aware stratification.

### Null/null
- highest severity
- highest mortality risk
- poorer 5-year and 10-year survival

### At least one missense
- still severe in many cases
- but survival probability is significantly better than null/null
- does not imply mild disease
- does support a less catastrophic class than double null

For a compound-heterozygous case, this matters because the working interpretation is closer to **N/M** logic than **N/N**.

## Block C — Specific genotype logic (worked examples)
### Exon 6 splice (c.517-2A>G)
- supported as pathogenic / exon-skipping / null-like

### Q230P
- cannot be treated as a weak missense by default
- sits in severe human compound contexts
- must be interpreted in compound-genotype logic, not in isolation

This makes such a compound genotype clinically interpretable within a severe but not automatically double-null frame.

## Block D — Longitudinal trajectory
The human literature does not support a purely binary prognosis.
Instead, it supports:
- high early mortality in the most severe genotypes
- survival into later childhood / adulthood in selected cases
- persistent profound disability
- possible late regression / domain-specific worsening

This matters for expectation-setting and long-horizon surveillance.

## Mortality frame
Main mortality drivers in the literature include:
- respiratory complications
- severe systemic intercurrent illness
- epilepsy-related death / possible SUDEP in some cases

This supports vigilance, especially during infections or respiratory compromise.

## Claim impact
This meta primarily supports:
- **CLAIM 017** — human spectrum from severe WOREE to milder SCAR12-like phenotypes
- **CLAIM 018** — exon 6 splice pathogenicity in humans
- **CLAIM 019** — Q230P severe compound-context logic
- **CLAIM 020** — survival into adulthood in selected severe trajectories
- **CLAIM 021** — null/null survival disadvantage versus ≥1 missense

## Working model impact
This meta strengthens:
- genotype-aware reading of a case
- survival / severity interpretation
- long-horizon counseling logic

This meta does **not** justify:
- reduction in vigilance because a case is likely N/M
- direct therapeutic changes
- extrapolation from isolated case reports to deterministic prognosis

## Research impact
This meta keeps active:
- **RL-HUM-001** — Human natural history / trajectory model
- **RL-HUM-002** — Genotype → severity / survival gradient

## Boundaries
This meta must not be used to claim that:
- a case will follow any single published trajectory
- N/M is mild
- Q230P guarantees a specific outcome
- all human WWOX cases are directly comparable

## Current synthesis sentence
The human WWOX spectrum is severe but not uniform: genotype matters, especially for survival, and a compound-heterozygous case is best interpreted within a severe N/M genotype-aware frame rather than a double-null frame, without any relaxation of clinical vigilance.

## Meta update — Commit 181–220: human severe spectrum refinement

Recent papers (corpus 181–220) support the following refinements to the severe human spectrum:

### Microcephaly is not mandatory
Severe WWOX encephalopathy can occur without microcephaly. Electroclinical severity and progressive structural deterioration may be more informative than cranial size alone.

### Null-severe timing begins earlier than often assumed
In null-severe genotypes, abnormalities may already be detectable prenatally (paper 216; CLAIM 022). This shifts the severe human spectrum away from a purely postnatal seizure-driven model and toward a developmental-disorganization model that may begin in utero.

### Practical implication
When assessing severe human WWOX cases, reasoning should prioritize:
- genotype architecture
- developmental timing
- electroclinical trajectory
- progressive structural brain involvement

rather than relying on single phenotypic gates such as microcephaly or postnatal seizure onset.
