# Meta — Prenatal Structure

## Scope
This meta stabilizes the structural/prenatal block of the WWOX-DEE model.
It is not a general review, but an operational cross-paper synthesis meant to connect:
- paper registry
- claim layer
- working model
- research lines

## Core question
Does a relevant share of the WWOX-DEE phenotype arise on an already-disassembled prenatal/early structural substrate, or is the epilepsy primarily a postnatal network problem?

## Current answer
The current convergence supports a strong but prudent reading:
- **DATO**: WWOX loss perturbs prenatal cortical development, migration and cortical maturation across species
- **INFERENZA strongly supported**: part of WWOX-DEE likely arises on a structurally misassembled prenatal cortical substrate
- **INFERENZA prudente**: GSK3β acts more plausibly as an amplifier-node inside a vulnerable system than as a single upstream driver

## Structural block consolidation note
The prenatal/structural block now rests on a three-paper core:
- **Iacomino 2020** → migration, cortical mislayering, cytoskeleton/microtubule program disruption across human fetal tissue, rat and hNPC
- **Cheng 2020** → neurodevelopmental/degenerative phenotype with GSK3β-linked seizure susceptibility in severe Wwox deficiency
- **Kumada 2019** → cortical development failure with hypomyelination and impaired oligodendroglial/glial maturation

This convergence makes the structural axis no longer merely possible, but one of the central explanatory levels of the WWOX-DEE model.

## Evidence anchors

### 1. Migration / cortical layering
- WWOX loss perturbs neuronal migration, especially late-born cortical neurons
- altered cortical layering and cortical misassembly are repeatedly observed
- cytoskeleton / microtubule programs are dysregulated in developmental models

### 2. Cortical maturation
- the phenotype is not limited to migration delay
- cortical maturation appears incomplete/abnormal
- severe early developmental organization may already be compromised before the mature epilepsy phenotype is fully expressed

### 3. Myelin / white matter / glia bridge
- hypomyelination and oligodendroglial maturation failure connect the prenatal structural axis to P4
- this suggests that white matter failure may be part of the developmental program, not only a secondary downstream effect
- bridge with `meta_network_myelin_glia_current.md` is required, but the structural driver remains distinct

### 4. GSK3β node
- Cheng 2020 introduces GSK3β as a seizure-susceptibility node in severe Wwox deficiency
- within this meta, GSK3β is retained as an **amplifier-node**
- it should not be read as a standalone therapeutic target

## Claim impact
This meta primarily supports:

- **CLAIM 014** — WWOX loss perturbs prenatal cortical development, neuronal migration and cortical maturation across species
- **CLAIM 015** — Part of WWOX-related epileptic encephalopathy likely arises on a structurally misassembled prenatal cortical substrate
- **CLAIM 016** — GSK3β hyperactivation may contribute to seizure susceptibility in WWOX deficiency

## Working model impact
This meta strengthens:
- the structural reading of EEG severity
- the interpretation that early MRI may underestimate burden
- the logic that P3 is a core axis, not a decorative one

This meta does **not** justify:
- direct therapeutic change
- automatic extrapolation from null/null severe models to a milder compound-heterozygous case
- treating GSK3β as an operational target

## Research impact
This meta keeps active:
- **RL-007** — Prenatal cortical development / migration axis
- **RL-008** — WWOX → GSK3β seizure axis

## Boundaries
This meta must not be used to claim that:
- every WWOX case has the same malformation burden
- a compound-heterozygous patient necessarily has the same cortical structural damage seen in severe null models
- prenatal structure explains the whole phenotype

## Current synthesis sentence
WWOX-DEE should currently be read as a disorder in which part of the epileptic and developmental phenotype is likely pre-wired by abnormal prenatal cortical assembly, with downstream bridges to network hyperexcitability, myelination failure and glial vulnerability.

## Update — Commit 181–220: prenatal null-severe onset confirmed in human case

Paper 216 (corpus 181–220) adds direct human evidence: homozygous deletion of the first six exons of WWOX was identified in a fetus with detectable prenatal brain abnormalities. This confirms that at least a subset of severe WWOX-null disease reflects developmental architecture failure active in utero, not only postnatal epileptic deterioration.

This update strengthens CLAIM 022 and reinforces the current architecture of this meta without requiring structural reorganization.

### Operational implication
For null-severe cases, prenatal developmental disruption should be considered a major explanatory layer alongside postnatal hyperexcitability, myelination defects, and secondary inflammatory/metabolic stress.

### Status
- Prenatal structural branch: active / confirmed by human case data
- Evidence level: human phenotype refinement — strong but still limited in case number
- No change to the case-level implications (N/M ≠ null-null severe)
