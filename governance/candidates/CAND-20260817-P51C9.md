### What F-3 actually was, and why the repair is not only a list swap

Revision 1 cited `f7a00498`, `6c2ab4f1`, `f3bef292`, `93a443b8`. All four **resolve as objects**
and **none is an ancestor of the branch tip**: they are the pre-rebase forms of rows 1–4, orphaned
when the branch was realigned onto `main`.

The lesson, which the manifest now carries structurally: **a full 40-character oid proves object
identity and proves nothing about reachability.** RC-6 introduced full oids on the reasoning that
one *cannot be mistyped into a valid-looking prefix* — correct, and it does not survive a history
rewrite. An orphaned oid is worse than an abbreviated one, because `git cat-file -e` returns
success and only `merge-base --is-ancestor` fails, so a check that the object exists reports green
over a broken chain.

Hence the ancestry column: **every source commit records the verification, not merely the id.**
And the rule the SLR states: after any operation that rewrites history, previously recorded oids
must be **re-derived, never carried forward**. The rebase was legitimate; recording pre-rebase
values after it was not.

**This remediation does not reproduce the defect it repairs.** The split was performed by a
forward commit that restores `deployment/deployment_profile.md`, not by rebase, amend or force
push. Rows 1–5 keep the oids revision 1 should have had, and they remain valid.
