## 3 · Failure patterns observed

Each entry separates what was **observed in durable state** from what was **reported**. The two are
not interchangeable, and keeping them apart is the same discipline Decision 4 legislated.

### A · Transport ambiguity

#### A.1 · Placeholder transport — REPORTED; and the search is now contaminated by its own report

```
AS REPORTED   a dispatch was blocked because a required field, DEC_DRAFT_PATH, remained an
              unfilled placeholder
AS MEASURED   `DEC_DRAFT_PATH` occurs in the repository EXACTLY ONCE, across all 46 refs
              (32 local heads): inside learning/mirror/SLR-mirror-ORCHSURF-R4-GOVERNED-
              TRANSCRIPTION.md — the prior artefact that reports the token's absence — plus once
              in that record's own commit message. Excluding learning/mirror/, the count is ZERO
STATUS        NOT VERIFIED. NOT DENIED. Unverifiable at this surface
```

**Failure mechanism.** A blocked dispatch produces no commit by construction, so the absence of a
trace is evidence of nothing either way. The object that would make the event checkable — the
dispatch itself — is not persisted.

🔴 **A second-order observation the prior record could not have made.** When it swept for
`DEC_DRAFT_PATH` it measured zero. It then wrote the token into the repository in the act of
reporting the zero. **The token's only durable existence is the report of its non-existence**, and
a later actor running the same sweep gets a hit that means the opposite of what it appears to mean.
Recording the absence of an identifier creates the identifier.

**Reusable lesson.** An observation whose sole evidence is conversation cannot be promoted to fact
by being written down carefully — and a negative measurement over a corpus that includes one's own
record is not repeatable by anyone who reads it afterward. Where a negative result matters, the
population it was taken over has to be stated, and it has to exclude the record stating it.

#### A.2 · Byte / verbatim transport — OBSERVED, and worse than the "whitespace" framing

```
AS REPORTED   a transport step relied on copied textual content where whitespace fidelity became
              ambiguous
AS MEASURED   the durable instance is NOT whitespace. It is a CARRIED COLUMN OF LINE NUMBERS, and
              its consequence was measured rather than feared
```

`REV-ORCHSURF-ADD-002-PATCH-SPECIFICATION` § 2, `OBS-3`, read at blob `ab3a946c` (sha256
`758b45bf…2f63b`, recomputed here), convicting its own table:

> *"as first written, this table's middle column was carried across from the first, and the
> sentence above it denied doing exactly that."*

```
CLAIMED at 7469f4e1        L-2 135–138        L-3 978–979     (the FIRST column's numbers)
MEASURED at 7469f4e1       L-2 opens 158      L-3 opens 1055
WHAT 135–138 ACTUALLY IS   the LINT_RESULT and PUBLICATION_GATE manifest rows
WHAT 978–979 ACTUALLY IS   a table row about the T10 enumeration method
```

A second instance of the same family, different mechanism — `OBS-2`, § 10 of the same
specification: `candidate_content_hash.py` exists as two different blobs on different surfaces, and
the stale one reads its rule from the invoking checkout rather than from the tip being hashed. One
script name, two objects, two answers, and neither surface announcing which it held.

**Failure mechanism.** The defect is not byte fidelity. It is that **a measurement was carried
where a re-measurement was owed**. Carried line numbers stay well-formed while becoming false: they
land on real lines in a real manifest, so the damage reads as correct. The specification's own words
on what a transcriber following that table would have done: *"would have destroyed four unrelated
manifest lines and a findings-table row, and the result would still have looked like a manifest."*

**Reusable lesson.** Prefer operations, hashes, blobs and explicit transformations over copied
fragile content — and note *why* the preference is not merely about fidelity: a hash that is carried
wrongly fails loudly at the next comparison, whereas a line number that is carried wrongly succeeds
into the wrong place. **The argument for immutable references is that their failure mode is
detection, and copied content's failure mode is plausibility.**

### B · Verdict provenance

```
AS MEASURED   DEC-20260821 Decision 4, verbatim at blob 42d26de3:
              Q: "Can a reviewer conclusion existing only in conversation history act as durable
                  procedural evidence?"      RULING: "No."
              REQUIREMENT: the Mirror compliance validation "must be persisted by Mirror itself",
              be "authored by Mirror", exist "on the Mirror-controlled branch", and bind to a
              named revision.
              PROHIBITION: "No third party may transcribe a reviewer verdict into an artefact and
              represent it as Mirror evidence."
```

**The ruling had a subject, and the subject was missing.** When Mirror went to look, stage 2 § 1
records the result of searching rather than assuming:

> *"A prior Mirror assessment of this object does not exist in durable state, and this record does
> not claim to persist one. […] This record is therefore a first-instance validation performed now,
> not a receipt for an earlier one."*

**Failure mechanism.** A conclusion held in conversation is indistinguishable, from the outside,
from a conclusion never reached. Every participant believed a Mirror assessment existed. The belief
was uniform, load-bearing, and false — and nothing in the process would have surfaced that before
Decision 4 converted the belief into a checkable proposition.

**The rule then survived contact with the actor who made it.** Stage 6 exists for the same reason,
applied upward: *"a conclusion held only in conversation is not procedural evidence […] and it
applies to an operator's approval exactly as it applies to a reviewer's verdict."* A rule that
exempts its author is a different rule; this one did not.

**Reusable lesson.** A conclusion existing in conversation is not durable procedural evidence, and
the actor producing the conclusion must persist it. The sharper form the evidence supports: an
unpersisted conclusion is not merely uncitable — **it is not reliably distinguishable from one that
was never reached**, including by the actor who believes they reached it.

### C · Surface ambiguity

```
AS MEASURED   UNRESOLVED-C, at the patch specification § 8: the candidate exists at one blob on
              `orchestrator-surface` while the binding names a different CONTENT_TIP. Patching at
              the branch tip leaves the corrected bytes absent from CONTENT_TIP; patching at
              CONTENT_TIP would be a re-bind, which ADD-002 does not authorize. Two surfaces were

  -> DEC Decision 3 answered with a THIRD surface class: "the transcribing actor's working
     branch", explicitly "distinct from the two surfaces enumerated at the question's source"
```

Decision 3 also separated two values that had been travelling as one:

> *"CONTENT_TIP identifies the reviewed object identity. CONTENT_TIP does not define the writable
> transcription surface."*

and it named what a dispatch must therefore pin: the responsible branch tip recorded, the exact
candidate blob recorded, the three loci **re-derived on that pinned baseline**, and *"no locus may
be inherited from CONTENT_TIP unless independently verified on the transcription surface."*

**Failure mechanism.** An identity and a destination are different questions that a single
identifier appears to answer. Where no writable surface is designated, an actor must infer one, and
every available inference source (the tip they are on, the tip they were given, the directory they
are in) can be individually plausible and mutually inconsistent.

**Reusable lesson.** Every dispatch requiring writes must explicitly identify **branch**, **base
commit** and **writable surface** — three fields, not one, because in this cycle they resolved to
three different things and the governing decision had to invent a surface class to say so.

🔴 **New as a first-class pattern. The prior artefact records the instance; this measures the
population and reaches the binding field.**

**The concrete instance, re-run this session.** `git worktree list` reports:

**The population, which the prior record does not measure.** `git worktree list` registers **14
worktrees**; 13 have a branch checked out and one is on a detached HEAD. Of those 13, **8 have a
directory name that differs from the branch they hold** and 5 agree. **The mismatch is the majority

**And `evidence-index` is the worst of the eight, for a reason none of the others share.** Seven of

**Four distinct things bear the name `evidence-index` or are addressed by it, and the one that was
actually written to is none of them.** The transcription ran in Plan's contractually declared home,

**Failure mechanism, and why it is structural rather than incidental.** `roles/plan.md` binds an
actor to a **worktree name**. A worktree name does not determine which branch is checked out in it,
and nothing verifies the correspondence. So the role contract's own identity field is one of the
ambiguous identifiers. My own identity check at § 0 passed because `roles/mirror.md` says
`worktree: mirror`, the directory is `mirror`, and the branch is `mirror` — three names agreeing.

**The countermeasure that did operate, in the imperative, from Plan's own commit message:**

> *"BRANCH plan-orchsurf-r4-transcription, created at aa49df9 from the SHA named in the dispatch.
> NOT inferred from cwd, worktree name or session name — the worktree this ran in is on a different

noticed. Neither was warned by a mechanism.

**Reusable lesson.** Physical execution location does not establish actor identity. A session has at
least three identifiers — directory name, branch name, session name — and each can disagree with the
other two while all three look authoritative; the role contract's `worktree:` field is a fourth, and
it names the ambiguous one. Identity has to be established from a contract cross-checked against a
ref, and the writable surface has to be established from the ref, never from the location.

## 4 · Mechanisms that worked

Recorded as observed mechanisms, each with the object that evidences it.

**Fail-closed behaviour produced routed output rather than a stall.** Every unanswerable question was
carried forward *named* — `UNRESOLVED-{A,B,C,D}` — rather than silently resolved by whoever hit it.
Four entered stage 1; three were routed by an authority with standing; **`UNRESOLVED-D` remains open
and is stated as open in all six records.** The path completed with a known open item rather than a
closed one that was actually open.

**`BLOCKED` reports instead of forced execution.** The two questions Mirror could not settle —
`UNRESOLVED-A` (may a `MIRROR_REVIEW` value fall outside D.2's enumeration) and `F-3` (is `REQUEST
