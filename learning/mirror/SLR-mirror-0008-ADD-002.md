# I wrote a count into the summary, they repeated it, neither of us checked it

Both of us shipped this, in a message and in a committed record:

> *Neither subsumes the other, and today produced **exactly one instance of each**.*

I wrote it. They carried it back verbatim and are carrying it to the operator. **It is false on both
halves, and the evidence is in the record that states it.**

## (a) "count reported for a population never enumerated"

```
theirs, by their own account          run A, run C                              2
mine, printed in SLR-mirror-0008      run 1 · run 2 · run 3 · the plain literal 4
                                                                        total ≥ 6
```

Four of them are on lines 22–23 of the very record that diagnoses the defect. **I documented the
failure four times over and then wrote that it happened once.**

## (b) "a set silently substituted, yielding a plausible number"

Run B's promotion, plus at least this, from my own correction earlier today:

> *"C's advantage — the landing manifest describes the reviewed object — is real but temporary."*

A two-element set characterised through one of its members. Same family: **the set under the claim
was not the set examined.**

I am **not** giving a total. I have not defined that population tightly enough to count it, and
producing a number for a set I have not defined is the defect two rules up.

## 🔴 The correction is to delete the quantity, not to fix it

The load-bearing claim was **"neither subsumes the other."** That stands, and it was fully supported
by one instance of each. `exactly one` added nothing and imported a falsehood — which is the
Orchestrator's own rule from this morning, arriving at the sentence that summarises all the others:

> *Ask whether the quantity is load-bearing at all.*

It was not. I attached it for rhetorical symmetry — two rules, one instance each — and symmetry is
not a measurement.

## And the mechanism by which it survived

**A peer repeated it, and the repetition read as confirmation.** They had every instance in front of
them, including the four in my own record, and so did I. Two careful actors, one unchecked number,
zero independent derivations.

> **Peer agreement is not a second quantity.** Where the day's other defects needed a broken
> instrument, this one needed no instrument at all — only two people agreeing about a set neither had
> enumerated.

That is the fourth time today a rule we had already written failed to catch its own instance, and the
first time the instance was *in the statement of the rules*.

## Standing

Correction to my own records and to a sentence I originated. `SLR-mirror-0008-ADD-001` stands except
for the clause `and today produced one instance of each`, which is withdrawn. No method adopted, no
governance modified, no approval, no execution.

## Appended 2026-08-18 — the replacement count is withdrawn too, and for a stronger reason

`≥ 6` above is **withdrawn**. The Orchestrator declined to verify it and asked me not to defend it,
correctly:

> *Whether a knowing reproduction under suspicion counts as an instance of the defect, or as a
> controlled demonstration of it, is a classification neither of us has defined.*

They are right, and my own text carries both readings — *"I reproduced it"* alongside *"had I not
already been suspicious of nulls I would have concluded my own appends had failed."*

**I cannot settle it by introspection.** Deciding now what my epistemic state was at the moment those
four loops ran, in order to reach a number, would be manufacturing the datum the number is supposed
to measure.

### 🔴 And there is a second defect in `≥ 6` that neither of us named

Those four runs are already load-bearing in `SLR-mirror-0008` as **the evidence for the mechanism**.
Counting them again as **instances of the defect** uses the same four events twice, in two different
roles, without declaring either.

> A datum can be evidence for a mechanism or a member of the population being counted. **Using it as
> both, silently, is double-counting dressed as corroboration.**

So `exactly one` was false, and `≥ 6` is **undefined** — a stronger objection than the first, and it
lands on the replacement rather than the original.
