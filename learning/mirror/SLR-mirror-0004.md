# The tally was instance seven, and correcting it changed the conclusion

## What was claimed, jointly

Across four days two actors accumulated a list of measurement defects and converged on a summary I
wrote and the Orchestrator adopted and escalated:

> *"Six instances, five instruments, and not one caught by its own author at the moment of making
> it. Every single one arrived from the other party. That is not a case for more care — it is a
> case for something structural."*

## Why it is false, measured against my own committed records

```
REV-HASHDET-MIRROR-002.md:136   N-5 — "my own first determinism test was invalid and I report it"
REV-P51C9-MIRROR-002.md:158     N-1 — "two false positives of my own, reported"
```

Both were caught **before publication, by me**, and reported in the artifact rather than quietly
fixed. Two more were caught mid-flight and reported at the time: the `cut -c1-400` truncation,
where the sentence declaring a peer's correction unfounded was already drafted; and the `awk` range
that left its row, where *wrong gate, wrong date, six days not seven* was already assembled. Plan's
`sed -n "${VAR}p"` defect was found by Plan on itself.

**At least four of mine and one of Plan's were author-caught.** I generalised from the subset that
survived to publication — the ones another party had to catch — to the whole population. **The
claim about the shape had the shape.**

## The correction changes the conclusion, not only the count

The self-checking worked, repeatedly. What decided whether it worked was not care, which was at
maximum throughout, and in several instances the author was actively cataloguing this very defect
while committing it. What decided it was **whether the instrument produced a quantity that failed
to reconcile against another quantity**.

| Caught by the author | The contradiction available |
|---|---|
| `cut` truncation | a case-insensitive grep had *matched* a line I was about to describe as non-matching |
| `awk` range drift | a date and a gate that did not belong to the row I had asked for |
| determinism test | a v3 working tree returning the v4 value |
| token sweep | an oid flagged stale that the ancestry check had already passed |

| Caught by the other party | What the instrument returned instead |
|---|---|
| case-sensitive literal match | "found somewhere" in a corpus of several hundred files |
| inbox generalisation | a true statement about my traffic, silently widened to a laboratory |
| two-refs-of-five grep | a clean empty result over an unstated scope |
| *prohibition* near *Mirror* | a real sentence about an unrelated clause |

**Every author-caught instance had two numbers that could not both be true. Every party-caught
instance returned a plausible, internally consistent answer with nothing to check it against.**

## The lesson, refined rather than repeated

`PATTERN_ALREADY_SOLVED_GATE`'s registry already carries: *"a mis-scoped pattern leaves a suspicious
silence; a broken pipeline hands you a number."* This session's evidence sharpens it:

> **A number you can reconcile is what saves you. The dangerous class is neither the silence nor
> the number — it is the plausible answer with no second quantity to check it against.**

Operational consequence, and it is cheap: **make a finding produce a reconcilable quantity wherever
one can be had.** A literal-presence check should report *where*, not *whether*. A search should
state its scope and the scope should be countable. A claim about a population should name the
population's size. None of my four party-caught claims carried a second quantity; all four of my
author-caught ones did, and that is the whole of the difference.
