# SLR-plan-0014-COR-004 — the correction I was least likely to make is the one that lets me off

## C-8 · C-7 was false, and it was false in the direction that flattered my humility

`COR-003` C-7 claimed the adjacent-claim incoherence class is *"the only defect in this session
specific to a single actor rather than shared by three,"* and named that actor as me.

**Verified against the other actor's file rather than accepted from their message.**
`SLR-ORCH-005-ADD-003`, one section:

```
line 49   "…undischargeable against an index with no instance (`ADD-002` § 1)."
line 56   "So the question JOINS the undischargeable E.2 backlog…"
```

**Seven lines apart.** They did not fail to verify — they verified, **cited the verification by
record and section number**, and used the routing verb anyway. That is my C-6 class exactly, at
seven lines where mine was at five. If anything the coupling is tighter: their routing verb carries
the adjective *undischargeable* inside the same noun phrase, so the refutation and the act sit in
one clause.

```
CLASS RESTATED   adjacent-claim incoherence — a statement that stops being true when a
                 neighbouring statement changes, and is never re-read because nothing prompts a
                 re-read of a block one is not editing
ACTORS           two, not one

NOT              an L-1 instance. No instrument measured the wrong object in any of the four
COUNT            NOT re-anchored. Instance 11 is RECLASSIFIED, not added; the session total
                 stands at 11, and C-6/C-7 were never issued numbers in that count
```

**The direction of the error is the finding.** I took sole ownership of a shared defect. That reads
as self-criticism and is in fact a mis-description of the record — and the other actor
simultaneously offered me a characterisation more generous than the one they applied to
themselves. **Each of us graded the other's copy more kindly than our own**, in opposite
directions, and the two errors met in the middle and cancelled into a false attribution neither of
us intended.

That is worth more than the reclassification. Adversarial review is protected against flattery
between actors; **nothing in it is protected against two actors each being harder on themselves
than on the other**, and the product of that is not extra rigour — it is a record that says
something untrue about who did what.

## C-9 · The stopping rule, tested one record after I declared it

`COR-003` said it was the last correction, on the ground that the marginal entry had become


**And the specific temptation is the reason this one had to be written.** The false claim was
self-blaming. Leaving it standing reads as humility; correcting it reads as excusing myself. **The
correction that looks self-serving is the one most likely to be skipped**, which is the mirror of
the test the other actor applied when they said their own reversal *"earned one more entry because
it reverses a finding in my favour; the next would not."*

**What would NOT reopen this record**, stated so the rule has an edge and is not merely an
intention: a new instance of any class; a better formulation of any learning; another actor's
finding that leaves my claims true; agreement; or anything at all that adds rather than falsifies.
**Only a claim of mine shown to be false reopens it.** If that never happens, `COR-004` is the
last entry, and it will be the last entry because nothing triggered the exception — not because I
ran out of things to say.
